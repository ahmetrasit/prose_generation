[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p146#2/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ثعلب | reports | Thaʿlab transmits that khāmir can mean one who conceals testimony; the root sense is covering, also reflected in the Prophetic instruction to cover vessels.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p146#2/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Khamr can mean a place or thing that conceals someone, such as trees or a mound; khāmiri umm ʿāmir means go into hiding.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p146#2/r7] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ابن الأنباري | reports | Some say it is named for being covered until maturity; Ibn al-Anbari derives the name from mixing with the intellect, illustrated by poems about illness mixing with the heart.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p146#2/r8] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | The mixing derivation returns to covering, since what mixes with something becomes like a veil.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p146#2/r9] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · الثوري وأبو حنيفة وأكثر أهل الرأي | reports | Al-Thawri, Abu Hanifa, and most jurists restrict khamr to grape or date juice that ferments naturally without fire; other drinks are not khamr.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj argues by analogy that anything producing wine’s effect should be called wine and share its prohibition; consensus forbids all gambling though the verse names the camel-lottery form.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says every intoxicant that mingles with and veils reason counts as wine; the Prophet’s hadith lists drink sources and bans every intoxicant.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet lists intoxicants made from dates, grapes, raisins, wheat, barley, and corn, then prohibits every intoxicant.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ابن عباس | reports | Maysir means gambling; Ibn Abbas says a pre-Islamic man wagered his family and property, which the loser surrendered.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r5] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Maysir is a mafʿil from yasara, to become due; yāsir is one whose lot or wager becomes due, then the terms meant gambling and gambler.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r6] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · طرفة | reports | Ṭarafa’s verse uses aysār as the plural of yusr for participants in the lot-based camel gamble.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r7] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · ابن الأعرابي | reports | Ibn al-Aʿrabi explains yasara al-yāsir as bringing one’s arrow to gamble.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r8] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | reports | Another derivation links maysir to division and sharing; yasaru means to divide, illustrated by a poem about dividing the speaker like a camel’s limbs.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p149/r9] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Arabs slaughtered a camel, divided it into shares, and gambled over them using arrows.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p151/r1] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · النضر | reports | Al-Nadr says yāsir means butcher and yasarat al-nāqa means she divided the camel’s meat; al-Aʿsha uses yāsir for butcher.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p151/r10] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · حمزة والكسائي | reports | Hamza and al-Kisaʾi read kathīr with thāʾ; the other readers read kabīr with bāʾ.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p151/r11] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | prefers | The bāʾ reading is preferable because kabīr contrasts with small and other verses call grave sins kabāʾir; wine and gambling are major sins. | mentions 54:53, 53:32, 4:31
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p151/r2] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · مقاتل | reports | A further derivation takes maysir from ease because people shared a camel to make its cost easier; Muqatil says they asked to split its price.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p151/r3] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | This verse is not the prohibition of wine and gambling; the prohibiting verse is in al-Maʾida. | mentions 5:90
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p151/r4] WAHIDI-BASIT, d. 468 · author | holds | Their sin includes quarrels, insults, obscene and false speech, loss of reason, and missing prayer; gambling breeds hostility when one takes another’s money without compensation.
