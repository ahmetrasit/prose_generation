[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi’s position is that abrogation is rationally possible and scripturally established, contrary to Jews who either deny its possibility or accept possibility but deny its scriptural occurrence.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r10] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | If an earlier law was expressly declared abrogable, that qualification should have been transmitted with the law; a major matter would be widely transmitted, and Jews’ and Christians’ united denial shows it was not.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعض المسلمين | reports | Some Muslims are also reported to deny that abrogation has occurred.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · جمهور المسلمين | reports | The Muslim majority argues that Muhammad’s proven prophethood requires accepting that a prior religious law was abrogated.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi challenges the Jews with the Torah’s report that God permitted Noah and his descendants every animal except blood, while later forbidding many animals to Moses and the Israelites.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | He also cites the early permission for Adam to marry his sister, later forbidden in the law given to Moses.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · منكرو النسخ | reports | Opponents answer that Moses and Jesus could have commanded their laws only until Muhammad appeared, after which people had a new duty; this would be an expiry, not abrogation. | mentions 2:187
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · منكرو النسخ | reports | Muslims denying abrogation likewise point to Torah and Gospel predictions of Muhammad and say the obligation to follow him at his appearance does not prove a prior law was abrogated.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · منكرو النسخ | reports | They argue that a law’s wording either promises permanence, denies permanence, or says neither; if it promises permanence, its later cessation would make the original statement false and leave no certainty that the current law is permanent.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p206/r9] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Razi rejects pairing a declaration of permanence with a declaration of later abrogation as contradictory, frivolous speech.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p207/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · منكرو النسخ | reports | If Moses’s law explicitly indicated a limited duration, opponents say that endpoint should have been universally known; in any case, its expiry would not be abrogation.

[RAZI-FULL:v3p207/r11] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Another reply says copying the Qur’an from the Preserved Tablet would concern all of it, whereas the verse’s abrogation concerns only some; a critic can object that ‘verse’ need not mean Qur’an alone.
[RAZI-FULL:v3p207/r12] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · معترض | reports | A critic counters that āya is general for all signs and that the verse may mean transferring from the Preserved Tablet, then bringing something better afterward.
