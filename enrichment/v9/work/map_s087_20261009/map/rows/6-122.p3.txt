[RAZI-FULL:v13p139#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | After saying that idolaters dispute with believers about religion, the passage gives a parable contrasting guided believers with lost unbelievers. | mentions 6:121
[RAZI-FULL:v13p139#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | The guided believer is like someone made alive and given light to find his interests; the unbeliever remains immersed in darkness, endlessly bewildered.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p139#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أصحابنا | holds | Razi's school says God is the one who adorns unbelievers' deeds: action depends on a motivating judgment of greater benefit, and God creates that motive.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p139#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · المعتزلة والحسن | reports | The Mu'tazila say Satan adorns the deeds; they quote al-Hasan as saying that the one who adorned them was Satan.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p139#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | rejects | Razi rejects Satan as the sole adornment: his prior proof points to God; assigning Satan's unbelief to another satan causes an infinite regress; and 6:108 and 6:123 explicitly attribute adornment or placement of criminals to God. | mentions 6:108, 6:123
[RAZI-FULL:v13p139#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · نافع | reports | Nafiʿ reads ميتا with a doubled ya; the other readers lighten it, and language scholars say both forms have the same meaning.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p140/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Other verses call unbelievers dead and contrast life with death (16:21, 36:70, 27:80, 35:19, 35:22); Razi says disbelief is death because ignorance causes perplexity and stillness, while guidance is knowledge and insight leading to salvation. | mentions 16:21, 36:70, 27:80, 35:19, 35:22
[RAZI-FULL:v13p140/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Because جعلنا له نورا is coordinated with فأحييناه, the light must signify something distinct from the life.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p140/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi models knowledge in four soul-stages: initial capacity to receive knowledge, primary universal axioms, acquired theoretical knowledge available for recall, and sacred insights present and illuminating the spirit; the first stage is called death.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p141#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · مقاتل | reports | Muqatil says the verse concerns Muhammad and Abu Jahl, who said he would not believe unless revelation came to him as it came to the Prophet; Razi gives no grading.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p141#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · عكرمة والكلبي | reports | Ikrima and al-Kalbi say the verse was revealed about Ammar ibn Yasir and Abu Jahl; Razi gives no grading.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p141#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says the verse was revealed about Umar ibn al-Khattab and Abu Jahl; Razi gives no grading.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p141#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | Razi prefers a general application to every believer and unbeliever because the meaning fits everyone; since the sura descended all at once, particular occasions are difficult to assert without a prophetic statement specifying an individual.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p141#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi argues that faith and disbelief come from God: life and light mean knowledge and guidance, motives are divinely created, and a person seeking knowledge but getting ignorance must have had the opposite produced in him; blaming error on mistaking it for knowledge regresses to prior ignorance unless some is created externally.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p141/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | In Razi's four-stage model, primary universal knowledge is life, reasoning from axioms to theory is the light, and actively present sacred insights are walking by it; this completes the soul's happiness.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p141/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A further reading calls life the soul's readiness and light the arrival of revelation: as sight needs a sound sense and sunlight, spiritual vision needs sound reason and revelation.
