[ELMALILI:2:185/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hz. Peygamber | reports | A Prophetic report describes Ramadan’s beginning as mercy, middle as forgiveness, and end as release from the Fire; the month’s most blessed night also begins the Qur’an’s descent.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Al-Qur’an is God’s speech revealed specifically to Muḥammad, transmitted to us by mass transmission, and written between the two covers of the codices.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Ibn Kathīr reads Qur’an without hamza in pause and connection, Ḥamza without hamza only in connection, and the remaining readers with hamza; the forms are linked respectively to qarn and qirāʾa.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Shahr derives from publicity or manifestation: the crescent first appears, and the month is named for the interval from one crescent’s appearance to its next appearance, normally twenty-nine or thirty days.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | With hamza, Qur’an means that which is recited; without hamza, it derives from qarn and denotes parts and contents joined together, mutually confirming and coherent.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r21] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmam Şâfiî | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī says the unhamzated Qur’an is, like Torah and Gospel, an un-derived proper name for God’s scripture.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r22] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Katâde, Atâ | reports | Qatāda and ʿAṭāʾ say the earlier counted days were an initial fast of three days each month, perhaps with ʿĀshūrāʾ; they differ over whether it began voluntary or obligatory, but agree Ramadan later abrogated it completely.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r23] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hazret-i Mu‘âz | reports | The same earlier-fast account is also attributed to Muʿādh; on it, Ramadan’s obligation is established by this verse’s command rather than by the earlier fasting passage.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r24] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · cumhûr | reports | The majority hold that the earlier fast was established by Sunna and Ramadan’s obligation began with the earlier command; the counted days mean Ramadan, which this verse explains.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r25] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The counted days prescribed for fasting are the blessed month of Ramadan, in which the Qur’an was sent as guidance for all humanity, with clear proofs of guidance and the Criterion.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Astronomers define the month as the interval between two solar conjunctions, but that specialist definition differs from the common lexical sense, measured between the appearances of crescents.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Mücâhid | reports | Mujāhid reports that Ramadan is one of God’s names, making shahr Ramadan mean God’s month.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hz. Peygamber | reports | A Prophetic report cautions against saying Ramadan came or went, instructing people to say the month of Ramadan; al-Bayhaqī grades this report weak.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Beyhâkî | reports | Al-Bayhaqī considers weak the report that Ramadan is one of God’s names and that one should say the month of Ramadan.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | A second opinion takes Ramadan, like Rajab and Shaʿbān, as the proper name of a particular month rather than a divine name.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | If Ramadan is a divine name, the full phrase shahr Ramadan is a proper name; if Ramadan names the month, the phrase is an explanatory genitive of a general term to a specific one.
[ELMALILI:2:185/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Ramadan, Rabīʿ al-Awwal, and Rabīʿ al-Thānī are properly named with shahr, though it is sometimes omitted for brevity; omission for Ramadan is mildly disfavored by Imam Muḥammad but allowed without risk of confusion.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | على originally signals elevation, then figuratively indicates being in a state; Arabic usage makes على سفر explicit for actually traveling, not merely intending to travel. | mentions 2:5
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r10] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The wording عدّة, rather than صيام, explicitly requires a number of make-up fasting days equal to the days missed through illness or travel.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r11] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | من is partitive if الأيام means days of the year or era; alternatively it classifies the count, as in five thousand angels in 3:125. | mentions 3:125
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The wording means a traveler breaks the fast only after beginning the journey, rather than on mere intention; whether one who travels after dawn may break that day is disputed.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas intended to travel in Ramadan, had his mount readied and wore travel clothes near sunset, ate, then mounted and called this the Sunna; al-Daraqutni transmits the report, and al-Hasan al-Basri shares this view.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الزهري ومالك والشافعي والأوزاعي وأبو حنيفة وأبو ثور | reports | These authorities say that someone who began the day resident cannot break the fast after traveling later that day; if he does, he owes make-up days but no expiation.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن كنانة والمخزومي | reports | Some Malikis, including Ibn Kinanah and al-Makhzumi, require expiation if a person breaks the fast after traveling later in a day begun at home; Ibn al-Arabi chose this view.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أبو عمر بن عبد البر | reports | Ibn Abd al-Barr rejects that expiation view: God explicitly allowed the traveler to break the fast; Ibn Ashur says Ibn Abd al-Barr was right.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أحمد وإسحاق والشعبي | reports | Ahmad, Ishaq, and al-Sha'bi allow a person who travels after dawn to break that day's fast; they also transmit this from Ibn Umar.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reports in Bukhari and Muslim that the Prophet traveled from Medina to Mecca fasting, then at Usfan called for water, displayed it, broke the fast, and remained unfasted until Mecca.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p163/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | prefers | Ibn Ashur favors allowing a traveler to break the fast after departing after dawn, supported by Ibn Abbas's report; al-Qurtubi calls that report explicit and says the contrary view falls away.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p164/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | أخر is the plural of أخرى, matching the plural days because Arabic treats every plural as feminine; the author connects it with أياما معدودات earlier in the passage.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p164/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أبو حيان | reports | Abu Hayyan says the plural أخر was selected instead of أخرى to prevent readers taking it as an adjective of عدّة.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p164/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Ibn Ashur says Abu Hayyan's concern about confusion is unconvincing; عدّة الأيام already means days, so plural agreement and lighter wording better explain the form.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p164/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · جمهور النحويين | reports | Most grammarians explain أخر as diptote because it is an adjective and is deflected from the masculine singular form expected of an unqualified أفعل; Ibn Ashur questions aspects of their account.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p164/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The verse leaves three make-up rules unspecified: whether days must be consecutive, whether make-up must begin promptly, and whether deliberate interruption incurs expiation; unrestricted wording competes with analogy to the original worship.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p164/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | Al-Daraqutni transmits with a sound chain Aisha's report that متتابعات was revealed after the make-up-days phrase and later dropped; she means it was abrogated.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p164/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الأئمة الأربعة | reports | The four imams and Companions Abu Hurayra, Abu Ubayda, Muadh ibn Jabal, and Ibn Abbas hold that make-up days need not be consecutive; the unrestricted wording is a divine concession, unlike expiation fasts specified as consecutive.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p164/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن عمر | reports | Ibn Umar says make-up days for Ramadan missed through illness or travel should be consecutive; al-Baji says he may intend obligation or recommendation.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p165/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Neither Qur'an nor Sunna requires prompt make-up; the command does not entail immediacy, and practice allows make-up until Sha'ban before the next Ramadan.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p165/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عائشة | reports | Aisha says she could not make up her missed Ramadan fasts until Sha'ban; Ibn Ashur treats this as clear evidence against an immediate duty and notes the majority agree.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p165/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · داود الظاهري | reports | Dawud al-Zahiri dissents from the majority and says make-up should begin on the day after the Eid of Ramadan.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p165/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الجمهور | reports | The majority say deliberately breaking a make-up fast incurs no expiation, since expiation protects Ramadan's sanctity and make-up days do not share that sanctity.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p165/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · قتادة | reports | Qatada requires expiation for deliberately breaking a make-up fast, reasoning that the replacement worship has the same rule as its original.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p168/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | These verses continue the preceding fasting verses, and there is no abrogation among them.
