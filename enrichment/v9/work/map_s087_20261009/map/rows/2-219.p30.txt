[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p352/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · قيس بن سعد | reports | Qays ibn Saʿd says the verse concerns obligatory zakat; al- then refers to externally defined growth at the zakat threshold.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p352/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الجمهور | reports | The majority read al-afw accusative as object of an understood verb, treating dha after interrogative ma as canceled and ma as yunfiqun's fronted object.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v2p352/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن كثير وأبو عمرو ويعقوب | reports | Ibn Kathir in one transmission, Abu Amr, and Yaqub read al-afw nominative as predicate of huwa, taking dha as a relative and matching the answer's case to its construction.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#2/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The impairment of perception and intellect during intoxication and afterward is undeniable, is wine’s greatest sin and corruption, and generates its other corruptions.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Islamic law protects sound reason and strongly forbids what disables it, including wine, gambling, fraud, and lies; wine and falsehood especially defeat reason’s governance.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Actions and policies built on intoxication and lies threaten humanity and undermine happiness; a law grounded in reason and barring desire that corrupts it is a source of honor.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Because sensual habits spread quickly and are hard to abandon, God legislates gradually and gently; wine prohibition is one example of that progressive approach.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#3/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Meccan 16:67 mentions intoxicating drink and good provision; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats this as an early, non-explicit stage in wine’s gradual prohibition. | mentions 16:67
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#3/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Medinan 4:43 bars drinking or drunkenness at prayer, limiting it at the best time and place; it precedes al-Baqara and al-Māʾida, since a specific ban after a universal one would reverse the gradual move from ease to severity. | mentions 4:43, 5:90, 5:91
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#3/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Al-Baqara 2:219 came after 4:43 and establishes prohibition by decisively calling wine a great sin, against the background of the earlier Meccan prohibition of sin. | mentions 4:43, 7:33
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#3/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعض المفسرين | rejects | Some exegetes argue that this verse is not explicit in prohibition: sin means harm, and an item with both harm and benefit is not thereby forbidden; they say Companions differed, some abstaining and others continuing while avoiding harm, until al-Māʾida’s explicit ban. | mentions 5:90, 5:91
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#3/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | First, sin does not simply mean harm; that definition fails for Qur’anic uses of ithm concerning associating partners, a sinful heart, liability for another’s sin, and acquired sins. | mentions 4:48, 2:283, 5:29, 24:11, 4:111
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#4/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Kibr compares to size what abundance compares to number; greatness and smallness are relational opposites first understood physically, then extended metaphorically to abstract matters.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#4/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The author points to shirk as another sin called great, using the Qur’an’s description in 4:48 when arguing that wine’s sin is especially grave. | mentions 4:48
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#4/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The verse does not explain the ruling merely by harm; even on that assumption, it explicitly says the sin outweighs the benefit, so appeal to independent Companion reasoning contradicts the text.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#4/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Even if 2:219 alone were insufficient to establish prohibition, its explicit naming of sin follows 7:33’s earlier general prohibition; wine is a complete instance of great sin. | mentions 7:33, 4:48
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#4/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Qur’an calls killing, concealing testimony, and false accusation sin, but does not call these great sins; it calls wine’s sin great and describes shirk as a great sin. | mentions 4:48
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#4/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | After al-Baqara, al-Māʾida 5:90–91 calls wine and gambling satanic pollution and commands avoidance; its closing question shows Muslims had not fully ceased drinking before then. | mentions 5:90, 5:91
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#4/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Gambling’s social harms and its destruction of the structure of life are visible directly; the author defers fuller discussion to Sūrat al-Māʾida. | mentions 5:90, 5:91
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#5/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Benefit and harm describe things sought or avoided for something else, unlike good and evil, which describe things sought or avoided for their own sake.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#5/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The benefits people seek in wine and gambling are monetary gains from sale, purchase, and work, along with amusement and pleasure.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#5/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Although benefits is plural earlier, the comparison says benefit in the singular: plurality does not affect greatness, so the quantity of benefits is left out of the comparison.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#5/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib derives ʿafw from aiming at something to take it, then notes its later senses of forgiveness, effacing traces, and moderation in spending; moderation is intended here.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#5/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The intended ʿafw here is the moderate or surplus amount to spend; he adds “God knows best.”
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#5/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The question and answer about what to spend match the earlier question answered by saying that whatever good is spent goes to parents and relatives. | mentions 2:215
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#6/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The invitation to reflection encompasses realities of existence, God and return, nature, society, ethics, and individual and communal life, namely all knowledge tied to human happiness and misery.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#6/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Although the Qur’an commands full obedience to God and His Messenger, it does not approve receiving its rulings and teachings through blind rigidity without thought and reason.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220#6/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Tabyīn here means uncovering the reasons behind rulings and clarifying the foundations of teachings and knowledge.
