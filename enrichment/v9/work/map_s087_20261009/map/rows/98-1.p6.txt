[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r11] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the People of Scripture as Yathrib’s Jews—Qurayẓa, al-Naḍīr, and Banū Qaynuqāʿ—and the polytheists as those around Mecca and Medina.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r12] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · مجاهد وغيره | reports | Mujāhid and others understand that they persisted in unbelief and error until the clear proof came.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r13] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الفراء وغيره | reports | Al-Farrāʾ and others say they kept recognizing Muhammad’s prophethood as true and awaiting his command until the proof came, whereupon they split.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r14] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says both groups had claimed before Muhammad’s mission that they would keep their religions until the promised prophet foretold in Torah and Gospel came; God recounts their claim.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r15] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya proposes that people remain under God’s command, power, and care until He sends a warning messenger to establish proof and complete favor for believers; they are not left purposeless.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r16] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | An unnamed view takes the phrase to mean that they would not cease living and die until the proof came.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r17] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | prefers | The apparent sense is that the groups did not separate from one another: each accepted the other’s chosen belief, and their affection and unity lasted until the proof came.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r18] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قيل | reports | Another view reads munfakkīn as perishing, drawing on an expression for a part separating during childbirth; it means they were not punished or destroyed before proof through messengers and books.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r19] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Abū Ḥayyān parses munfakkīn as the active participle of complete intransitive infakka, not the defective verb taking a subject and predicate.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن الزبير وعطاء بن يسار | reports | Ibn al-Zubayr and ʿAṭāʾ ibn Yasār say the sūra is Medinan, as Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r20] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · بعض النحاة | reports | Some grammarians treat infakka as defective and supply an omitted predicate such as knowing Muhammad’s affair.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r21] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān rejects that parse because, according to his school, the predicate of kāna and its sisters cannot be omitted either by ellipsis or abbreviation.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r22] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | He notes a poetic example where the predicate in ḥīna laysa mujīr is understood as fī al-dunyā, but says this deletion is forced by poetic necessity.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r23] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Al-bayyina means a manifest, decisive proof.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r3] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · كتاب التحرير | reports | The book al-Taḥrīr calls the sūra Medinan and says this is the majority view.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Abū Ṣāliḥ transmits Ibn ʿAbbās’s view that the sūra is Makkan.
[ABUHAYYAN:98:1/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · يحيى بن سلام | reports | Yaḥyā ibn Sallām chose the reported Makkan classification.
