[TABATABAI:23:1-11/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The traits in the opening passage show faith as living and active, producing its effects so that the believer reaches the intended end, success.
[TABATABAI:23:1-11/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Religion is a social way of life based on beliefs about the universe and humanity; social customs and laws therefore vary with those beliefs.
[TABATABAI:23:1-11/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Those who believe in a Lord as the universe's source and return, and in enduring human life, shape conduct toward lasting happiness in the afterlife.
[TABATABAI:23:1-11/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Those who affirm deities governing through favor and displeasure but no return to God organize life to please them and gain worldly goods and desires.
[TABATABAI:23:1-11/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Materialists, who disregard lordship and deny lasting human life, base social laws on enjoyment of the finite life ending at death.
[TABATABAI:23:1-11/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Theoretical knowledge about God and humanity does not itself direct action; faith is practical knowledge or judgment that one must follow what that knowledge entails.
[TABATABAI:23:1-11/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Religious preaching addresses practical commitment to true belief in God, messengers, the Last Day, and revelation, including worship and conduct for happiness in both lives.
[TABATABAI:23:1/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Meccan sura calls people to believe in God and the Last Day, contrasts believers’ fine servitude with disbelievers’ vices, then gives glad tidings and warnings including the annihilation of denying peoples from Noah through Jesus.
[TABATABAI:23:1/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib says falh means a cleft or splitting, illustrated by iron splitting iron; falah then means success and attainment of a goal.
[TABATABAI:23:1/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Success has worldly and afterlife forms: worldly happiness includes long life, wealth, and honor; the afterlife brings permanence, freedom from poverty, freedom from disgrace, and knowledge without ignorance.
[TABATABAI:23:1/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Calling attainment falah evokes splitting through the obstacle and uncovering the face of one’s desired goal.
[TABATABAI:23:1/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Iman is affirming something while committing to its implications; Qur’anic faith in God affirms His oneness, messengers, the Last Day, and their message, with general obedience.
[TABATABAI:23:1/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Qur’an regularly pairs believers’ praise and reward with righteous action, as in its promise of a good life to anyone who acts righteously while believing and its praise of believers who do good works. | mentions 16:97, 13:29
