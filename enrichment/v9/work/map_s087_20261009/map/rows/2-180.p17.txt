[TABATABAI:2:180-182/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The words ‘written’ and ‘a right’ signal obligation, but limiting the right to the pious weakens a strict duty, which would more naturally be imposed on believers.
[TABATABAI:2:180-182/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Al-Sadiq describes a right placed by God in people’s wealth for ‘the possessor of this matter,’ bounded from one-sixth to one-third; al-Saduq also transmits this in al-Faqih.
[TABATABAI:2:180-182/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | Some say the inheritance verse abrogated this command; if so, only its obligatory force was abrogated, while recommendation and desirability remain, perhaps signaled by ‘upon the pious.’
[TABATABAI:2:180-182/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | ‘Good’ means substantial wealth, not a negligible amount.
[TABATABAI:2:180-182/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | ‘In a fair manner’ means the customary form of beneficence and kindness.
[TABATABAI:2:180-182/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Asked whether a bequest may be made to an heir, al-Sadiq answered yes and recited this verse; reported by Muhammad ibn Muslim in al-Kafi, al-Tahdhib, and al-Ayyashi’s tafsir.
[TABATABAI:2:180-182/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · علي عليه السلام | reports | Ali says that one who dies without bequeathing to non-inheriting relatives has ended his work in disobedience.

[ASAD-NOTES:2:180#n152/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · A'ishah and Ali ibn Abi Talib | reports | Sayings of A’ishah and Ali ibn Abi Talib, both referring to this verse, support reading khayr as much wealth.

[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p120/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | Al-Baqillani left later inimitability writers a rich rhetorical vocabulary; the Sakaki school inherited his method of placing Qur'anic examples beside human speech without distinction.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p120/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الجرجاني | reports | Al-Jurjani's fifth-century treatise addresses theological disputes over inimitability and counters those who divert the explanation from rhetoric, especially proponents of ṣarfa.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p120/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الجرجاني | reports | For al-Jurjani, the challenge was to compose on any chosen subject at a level equal or close to the Qur'an's excellence; isolated words, lexical meanings, and mere matching of meter and rhyme were not the challenge.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p120/r4] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · الجرجاني | reports | Al-Jurjani defines composition as pursuing grammatical meanings and rules in the relations among words; the author identifies this nazm and arrangement as the remaining site of inimitability.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Before Islam, sons inherited all property when present; otherwise it went to the nearest male relatives, from closest outward.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r10] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Dahhâk, Tâvûs, Taberî | reports | Under this view, the amount bequeathed is left to customary practice and fairness.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r11] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | An orphaned grandchild whose father died before the grandfather can be excluded because the living paternal uncle is nearer; Islamic law has no rule making the child inherit in the father’s place.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r12] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Some contemporary Muslim countries used the non-heir bequest reasoning to provide inheritance shares to orphaned grandchildren.

[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r15] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · some müctehidler | reports | Some jurists treat a bequest exceeding one-third as invalid, citing the relevant hadith.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r16] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müctehidler çoğunluğu | reports | Most jurists, including Hanafis, consider an over-one-third bequest valid if the heirs consent.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r17] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A bequest is a legal disposition whose execution is left until after death.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r18] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | It is obligatory to bequeath that debts or outstanding rights be settled from the estate after death.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r19] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Where no such debt or right exists, bequest is recommended rather than obligatory when circumstances allow; hadiths encouraging bequests are read this way.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Pre-Islamic Arabs sometimes also bequeathed property to children, relatives, or friends.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The verse initially required bequests to all relatives, male and female, especially parents, preparing believers for the later inheritance rules.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The inheritance passage in Nisa included female relatives among heirs and, with related verses and hadiths, set fixed, unalterable inheritance shares. | mentions 4:11
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | At the Farewell Pilgrimage, the Prophet said each rightful claimant had received their due and there was no bequest for an heir, barring bequests to relatives with fixed inheritance rights.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | When Saʿd ibn Abi Waqqas proposed bequeathing all his property, the Prophet told him to limit it to one-third and said leaving relatives wealthy is better than leaving them destitute and begging.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The Saʿd report shows that a person with heirs should not bequeath all their property.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müctehidler çoğunluğu | reports | Most jurists, including the four imams, say bequest was first obligatory but inheritance verses partly changed the rule, leaving bequests recommended rather than obligatory.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:180/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Dahhâk, Tâvûs, Taberî | reports | Dahhak, Tawus, and Tabari hold that the bequest verse was not abrogated; it applies to relatives excluded from inheritance, such as parents of another religion or enslaved relatives.
