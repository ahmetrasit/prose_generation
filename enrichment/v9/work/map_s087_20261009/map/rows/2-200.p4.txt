[RAZI-FULL:v5p157/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعضهم | reports | One interpretation makes the remembrance the invocation said over the sacrificial animal.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p157/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعضهم | reports | Another interpretation is the takbir after prayers on the Day of Sacrifice and the Tashriq days, since no other specific remembrance follows Hajj; scholars differ on its time.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p157/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعضهم | reports | Another interpretation redirects people from their customary post-Hajj boasting about ancestors to remembrance of God; without the verse, they would have continued that blameworthy practice.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p157/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعضهم | reports | Another view turns pilgrims after Hajj to supplication, humility, and abundant seeking of forgiveness in response to leaving home, spending wealth, and enduring hardship; this resembles supplication after prayer.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p157/r9] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | An inward reading sees Hajj rites as subduing the lower self and erasing its false impressions; afterward the heart is illumined by God’s remembrance, first negating all but the Real, then affirming Him.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158#2/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | prefers | Al-Razi deems the first interpretation decisive; all the readings converge on constant remembrance, honoring God, seeking needs from Him, and turning away from all else.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158#2/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | “Ashadd” may be genitive, governed by the comparison kāf, or accusative, governed by “remember”: remember God like the ancestors, and remember Him more intensely than them.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158#2/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | holds | Here “or” means “rather, more”: ancestors’ merits are limited, while God’s perfection attributes are endless, so remembering Him should be more intense.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · القفال | reports | Al-Qaffal explains this comparative phrasing as ordinary Arabic usage for moving from an initial term to something sooner, not expressing uncertainty, as in “do this by a month or sooner.”
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158/r1] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · جمهور المفسرين | reports | The majority say to devote as much effort to praising God and recounting His favors as Arabs devoted to ancestors; ancestor boasts are falsehood deserving punishment or truthful pride and arrogance, so God’s remembrance is superior, or at least equal.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158/r2] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · الضحاك والربيع | reports | Al-Dahhak and al-Rabiʿ compare remembrance of God to a child’s constant calling for father and mother; “fathers” can stand for both parents, as in 16:81. | mentions 16:81
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158/r3] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · أبو مسلم | reports | Abu Muslim takes ancestors as an example of constant remembrance: just as a person does not forget his father, he should not neglect remembrance of God.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbari says Arabs commonly swore by their fathers in pre-Islamic times, so the command redirects their reverence toward God.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · بعض المذكورين | reports | Another interpretation says to affirm God’s oneness as people affirm their ancestors’ oneness; indeed, monotheistic insistence is even more fitting for God.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A further analogy is the child who turns to his father for every need and remembers him reverently; believers should relate to God’s remembrance in the same way.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | Another view says people invoked ancestors as intermediaries for answered prayers, but their deeds were invalidated by shirk; instead recount God’s favors and praise Him for future blessings.
[RAZI-FULL:v5p158/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | holds | The Prophet forbids swearing by one’s ancestors, instructing anyone who swears to swear by God or remain silent; God alone deserves exaltation.
