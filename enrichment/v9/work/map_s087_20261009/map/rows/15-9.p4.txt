[TABATABAI:15:1-9#13/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Al-Tabataba'i rejects hadith scholars' claim that variant corruption reports remain probative because they agree on the basic claim: their discrepancies weaken the evidence and undermine one another.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#13/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Insertion and fabrication are widespread in transmitted reports, as seen in accounts of creation, prophets, earlier peoples, Qur'anic exegesis, and early Islamic events.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#13/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Qur'an is the secure foundation of religious knowledge and the enduring proof of prophethood, so enemies of religion had strong reason to target it.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#13/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | If the Qur'an's authority is voided, prophethood and religious teachings lose their proof; the claim that only communal consensus remains cannot rescue them without a further basis for consensus.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#13/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The plausible, evidence-supported possibility that reports were interpolated defeats their legal and rational authority, even when their chains are sound, since sound chains cannot exclude insertion into source books.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#14/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Preserving Muslim unity cannot explain rejecting Ali's collection only after the community had settled on its own codex; the alleged motive would apply after public distribution, not during collection.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#14/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | It is implausible that thousands of omitted verses all concerned Ali's authority or were hidden from most Muslims, given the community's desire to learn revelation and the Prophet's effort to teach it.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#14/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · الروايات | reports | Some reports claim over a third of the Qur'an, more than two thousand verses, fell between two passages of al-Nisa; Sunni reports also say al-Tawba once had a basmala and matched al-Baqara, and al-Ahzab was longer than al-Baqara by two hundred lost verses.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#14/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The appeal to abrogation of recitation does not resolve the claims: the interpreters distinguish recitation-and-ruling abrogation from recitation-only abrogation, such as the stoning verse, while other supposedly abrogated rulings remain in the Qur'an.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#14/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | If recitation was abrogated because the passages lacked divine qualities, their removal conflicts with the Qur'an's descriptions of itself as preserved, mighty, free of falsehood, decisive, guidance, light, and a criterion.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#14/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The broad, unqualified contexts of these descriptions do not permit restricting protection, decisiveness, guidance, light, and miraculousness to only the verses still recited.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#14/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Making useful speech permanently unrecited and forgotten renders it void and abandoned, which is incompatible with the Qur'an being a reminder.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#14/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Al-Tabataba'i concludes that corruption reports transmitted by both Muslim groups, as well as reports that Qur'anic recitation was abrogated, categorically contradict the Qur'an.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#15/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Reports comparing this Muslim community's events with those of Israel are well established, but the analogy is not in every respect; reason and necessity rule out that reading.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#15/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The analogy concerns shared outcomes and effects; it can refer to sectarian division into 73 Muslim sects, compared with 72 Christian and 71 Jewish sects, a report some call recurrently transmitted.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#15/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The community's division can mean distortion of interpretation: rival groups all rely on the same Book but misplace its words, interpret by opinion, or trust exegesis reports without checking them against scripture.
[TABATABAI:15:1-9#15/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The analogy reports do not prove textual corruption; the subset that explicitly describes alteration or omission is unsound and contradicts the Qur'an.
