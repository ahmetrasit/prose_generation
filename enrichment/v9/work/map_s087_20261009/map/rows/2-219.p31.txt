[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Khamr linguistically means any liquid made to intoxicate; its root sense is covering, since it veils the mind and its ability to distinguish good from evil.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r10] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Wine degrades character and leads to obscenity, violence, crimes, murder, divulging secrets, violating rights, and destroying human laws of life, especially protection of honor, life, and property.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The related words khimār, khammartu, and akhmartu refer respectively to a woman’s head covering, covering a vessel, and putting yeast into dough to make it rise.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Arabs originally knew wine made from grapes, dates, and barley; later varieties multiplied and all intoxicating kinds, across levels of potency, are khamr.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Maysir means gambling and yāsir means gambler; its root connotes ease because it acquires another’s property without the toil of earning.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | A common Arabian form of gambling used arrows or lots to divide a slaughtered camel into twenty-eight shares among ten men; seven winning arrows awarded one to seven shares, while three losing arrows had no share and their holders paid the camel’s cost.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The ten arrows were al-fadhdh, al-tawʾam, al-raqīb, al-ḥils, al-nāfis, al-musbِل, al-muʿallā, al-manīḥ, al-sanīḥ, and al-raghd; the first seven drew assigned shares and the last three drew none.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | A variant reading has kathīr, “much sin,” with thāʾ instead of the written reading kabīr, “great sin.”
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Ithm is close to sin and describes a condition in an act or mind that slows a person from attaining good; wine and gambling have this quality.
[TABATABAI:2:219-220/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Wine causes grave medical harms to the stomach, intestines, liver, lungs, nerves, arteries, heart, and senses, as physicians’ writings and statistics document.
[ASAD-NOTES:2:219#n204/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Ithm literally means sin or anything conducive to sinning; because it is contrasted with benefits, Asad considers “evil” a suitable rendering.
[ASAD-NOTES:2:219#n204/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Razi | reports | Razi and other classical commentators point out that ithm is used as the antithesis of manafiʿ, “benefits.”
[ASAD-NOTES:2:219#n205/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Although this verse says their evil outweighs their benefit, Asad points to the clear prohibition of intoxicants and games of chance in 5:90–91. | mentions 5:90, 5:91
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The prohibition unfolded in stages: an early Meccan notice of fruit benefits, then a ban on praying while intoxicated, this comparison of harms and benefits, and finally categorical prohibition. | mentions 16:67, 4:43, 5:90
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r10] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Wine’s benefits are commercial and economic returns and temporary pleasure for the user.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r11] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Islam forbids intoxicating drinks and drugs with comparable or greater effects because their harms outweigh limited benefits that can be obtained elsewhere.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r12] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Medina mainly used date-based wine, imported grape wine from Yemen, Taif, and Syria, and also consumed intoxicating raisin/date infusions and drinks made from honey, corn, or barley; intoxicating varieties were called khamr.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r13] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | In Arabic, khamr commonly names all these intoxicating drinks.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r14] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bazı lugatçılar | reports | Some lexicographers restrict khamr to wine made from unboiled grape juice.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r15] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bazı fıkıhçılar | reports | Some jurists, following the narrow definition of khamr, held that every amount of grape wine is forbidden but other intoxicants are forbidden only in an intoxicating quantity.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r16] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | After later assessment ended the disagreement, all recognized Islamic legal schools agreed that every amount of any intoxicant is forbidden and may not be consumed.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r17] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | A cited hadith says that a small quantity is forbidden when a large quantity of the substance intoxicates; no grading is stated.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r18] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | A cited hadith says khamr can be made from grape juice, raisins, dates, wheat, barley, or corn, and describes it as what clouds reason and intoxicates; no grading is stated.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r19] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | A cited hadith states that every intoxicant is khamr and every intoxicant is forbidden; no grading is stated.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:219/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Ömer ve birçok sahâbî | reports | Umar and many Companions repeatedly asked for clarification about wine and gambling; their requests are cited among the occasions for the successive explanatory and prohibitory revelations. | mentions 5:90
