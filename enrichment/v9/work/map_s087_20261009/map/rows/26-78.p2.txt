[IBNASHUR-FULL:v19p142/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The present tense يهدين conveys guidance as a continuing, renewed act.

[IBNASHUR-FULL:v19p143/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Repeating the relative pronoun rather than coordinating all clauses directly emphasizes the possessor of these three magnificent divine attributes.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v19p143/r9] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | By naming actions in the sensible world that are evident to everyone, Ibrahim’s descriptions of God aim to lead the idolaters to faith if they seek guidance.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v19p144/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The five divine acts span bodily and intellectual creation, food and water, illness and healing, death, and resurrection; together they trace physical life and the second life as gifts.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v19p144/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | These descriptions also prepare for spiritual perfection: death looks like a blessing only after reflection, while the life after it is plainly a blessing for whoever wills it.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v19p144/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The first-person yāʾ is omitted from four verbs for lightness and rhyme at pauses, since most endings in this surah use a still nūn. | mentions 26:14
[TABATABAI:26:74-78/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Against the view that “who created me” begins an insignificant new passage, the author argues that Abraham begins with creation to infer that God also governs his affairs.
[TABATABAI:26:74-78/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | In gradual bodily existence, creation and governance cannot be separated; because God creates, he also manages, and the fāʾ in “then guides me” makes guidance follow from creation.
[TABATABAI:26:74-78/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The unqualified “guides me” means guidance to all beneficial ends, worldly or otherworldly; its present tense conveys ongoing guidance from creation onward and forever.
[TABATABAI:26:79-81/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The guidance given after creation can mean universal direction to each creature's benefits, as in Moses's description of God, and echoes the surah's sign in the growth of plants. | mentions 20:50, 26:7
[TABATABAI:26:79-81/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | On the universal reading, feeding, drinking, and healing are particular instances of general guidance; if guidance means religious guidance, these qualities stand separately and guidance precedes other gifts as the best blessing after existence.
