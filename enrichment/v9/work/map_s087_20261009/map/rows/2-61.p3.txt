[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p80/r2] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | The ḍamma reading of both letters preserves their original vowels: the pronoun was originally همو before the preposition.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p80/r3] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | A kasra on hāʾ with ḍamma on mīm combines yāʾ proximity with the mīm's original ḍamma, needed to avoid two sukūns.
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p80/r4] IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA, d. 370 · author | holds | Al-Kisāʾī matches Ḥamza here but differs in أنعمت عليهم: there mīm stays still, avoiding a shift from yāʾ to ḍamma; here hāʾ returns to its original vowel before mīm is moved. | mentions 1:7
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p110/r1] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Abu ʿAmr kasras the pronoun mīm because its underlying connected form ended in yāʾ; after deletion, the mīm resumes that original vowel when moved. | mentions 36:14
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p110/r2] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | He treats Abu ʿAmr’s kasra like the original ḍamma of Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, and Ibn ʿĀmir, preferring an original vowel to an added one. | mentions 36:14
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p110/r3] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh’s vocative truncation of Rād retains its original vowel at a consonant meeting, an analogy al-Fārisī uses for Abu ʿAmr.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p110/r4] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Vowel-following is not always productive analogy: some forms are transmitted, while it is regular in رد، عض، فر and apparently in مردفين.

[FARISI-HUJJA:v1p110/r6] FARISI-HUJJA, d. 377 · author | holds | Arabic allows vowel-following across an intervening consonant, as in أجوءك for أجيئك and منتن.
