[TABRISI:2:61#4/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | He applies the same parsing to dhālika bimā ʿaṣaw, with mā and its clause interpreted as a verbal noun.

[TABRISI:2:61#4/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A further poetic example makes tafdi jussive with the implied sense li-tafdi.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | After listing God's favors to the Israelites, the passage recounts their ingratitude and disobedient choice.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One view says their request was no sin because they asked for another permissible food; Ṭabrisī prefers the view that it was sinful because they rejected God's choice and were reproached.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | The more apt view is that the request was sinful because they were reproached for refusing what God chose.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن والربيع | reports | Al-Hasan and al-Rabiʿ say the requested Egypt is Pharaoh's Egypt from which the Israelites departed.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو مسلم وابن زيد | reports | Abu Muslim, with a report also attributed to Ibn Zayd, identifies Egypt as Jerusalem.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · قتادة والسدي ومجاهد | reports | Qatada, al-Suddi, and Mujahid take miṣr as any city: the requested crops are found in towns, not in the wilderness.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r15] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | On this reading, the speech ends with the promise that the produce they requested would be available in a settled city; the verse then begins a new account of Sabbath violators and prophet-killers.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The addressees of the recollection are the Israelites' forebears.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | They say they cannot endure one food: manna and quails are called one because the same daily food repeated from day to day, even if it offered varieties.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | An alternative account says only manna first descended; they tired of it and then quails were sent.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · قتادة | reports | Qatada says the people were shaded by clouds and given manna and quails in the wilderness, tired of them, recalled life in Egypt, and asked Moses for its produce.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another explanation says they sought produce so the poor could assist the rich, rather than having everyone equally rich and independent.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One view makes Moses ask whether they exchange the better food God chose for what is lower and worse; another makes God say it.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A further interpretation contrasts farmed and crafted food with manna and quails given freely.
[TABRISI:2:61#5/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another reading of adnā is the lower-priced choice in place of the more valuable, tasty one.
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن وقتادة | reports | Al-Hasan and Qatada interpret the humiliation as the subjection expressed in paying jizya under Quran 9:29. | mentions 9:29
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · آخرون | reports | Others take the wrath as punishment for their sins in the afterlife.
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Dhālika points back to the wrath, humiliation, and poverty already mentioned as caused by their disbelief in God's signs.
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One explanation takes God's signs to mean the Gospel and Quran, making the first wrath for rejecting Jesus and the Gospel and a second for rejecting Muhammad and the Quran; another takes them as Muhammad's attributes.
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Killing the prophets without right means without any offense, as in the killings of Zechariah and John.
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The qualifier 'without right' does not imply that prophets could ever be killed justly; it emphasizes that their killing was sheer injustice, like the cited Quranic and poetic examples. | mentions 23:117
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r15] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Dhālika bimā ʿaṣaw refers to their Sabbath violation and killing prophets, or to breaking the covenant and assaulting prophets; God acted against them for disobedience and transgression.
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r16] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | God allowed prophets to be killed so they could attain ranks unavailable otherwise; allowing murder of believers and righteous people likewise does not mean God has abandoned them.
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r17] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says God never ordered a prophet to fight and then be killed in battle; those killed were killed outside combat.
[TABRISI:2:61#6/r18] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | Ṭabrisī's preferred view is that God may not allow a prophet's death before he completes his assigned revelation, since that would undermine the obligations and their benefits; once he has delivered it, God may allow his killers and need not prevent it.

[TABRISI:2:61#6/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Maskana means the appearance of poverty; even their wealthy people feign destitution lest the tax be doubled.

[TABRISI:2:61#6/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet is reported to say that true wealth is wealth of the soul.
