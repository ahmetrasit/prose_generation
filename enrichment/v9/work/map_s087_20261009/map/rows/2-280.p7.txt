[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#3/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مجاهد وأبو رجاء والحسن | reports | The standard reading has nathira with a kasra; Mujahid, Abu Raja, and Hasan read it with a silent dh, a Tamimi dialect form comparable to their pronunciations of karam and kabid.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#3/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · نافع | reports | Nafi alone reads maysara with a damma on the sin; the majority read it with a fatha.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#3/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مجاهد وعطاء | reports | Mujahid and Ata are reported to read “then defer it to the matter until ease,” with a damma on the sin, kasra on the ra, and the y retained in connected speech.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#3/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو حاتم | rejects | Abu Hatim rejects the reading “fanazira,” which he says belongs only in 27:32 where a woman speaks of herself; here the word means deferment from the verb “to postpone.” | mentions 27:32
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#4/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A report promises one daily charity for each day a creditor gives an insolvent debtor respite before maturity, and double that daily reward for respite after maturity.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#4/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو مسعود | reports | The Prophet recounts a wealthy man whose only good deed was ordering his servants to overlook insolvent debtors; God says He is more entitled to do so and pardons him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#4/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو قتادة | reports | After finding a debtor who had hidden, Abu Qatada heard the Prophet promise rescue from Resurrection's distress to whoever relieves an insolvent person or remits the debt.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#4/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو اليسر | reports | The Prophet says that whoever gives an insolvent debtor respite or remits the debt will be shaded by God in His shade.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#4/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | These reports explicitly encourage giving insolvent debtors relief.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#4/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Abu Qatada's report establishes that a creditor who knows or suspects the debtor's insolvency may not demand payment, even without a judge's finding of insolvency.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#4/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Giving respite means delaying collection until the debtor becomes solvent; remission means cancelling the debt from the debtor's liability.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280#4/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Abu al-Yusr combined respite and remission: he erased the debt record and told the debtor to repay if able, otherwise he was released from the debt.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | When Thaqif demanded principal from Banu al-Mughira, the latter pleaded poverty and asked to wait until their harvest; this verse was revealed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Together with 2:279, the verse establishes a creditor’s right to demand principal and take the debtor’s property without consent; the debtor must therefore repay when able. | mentions 2:279
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · بعض العلماء ومكي وابن عطية | reports | Some scholars say the verse abrogated the pre-Islamic practice of selling insolvent debtors; Makki says the Prophet ordered that practice early in Islam, and Ibn Atiyya says abrogation follows only if that Prophetic act is established.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الطحاوي | reports | Al-Tahawi says a free person who lacked property could be sold for debt in early Islam until this verse abrogated the practice.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | A report used as evidence says a debtor was brought to the Prophet, who found no property and sold him for the debt; al-Bazzar transmits a longer version, but Muslim ibn Khalid and Ibn al-Baylamani are not reliable proof.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو هريرة والحسن وعامة الفقهاء | reports | Abu Hurayra, Hasan, and most jurists apply the deferment rule generally to every insolvent debtor.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النحاس وعطاء والضحاك والربيع بن خثيم | prefers | Al-Nahhas prefers the view of Ata, al-Dahhak, and al-Rabi ibn Khuthaym: every insolvent person is granted time in usury and all debts; this combines the positions, and the nominative reading supports generality.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:280/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس وشريح | reports | Ibn Abbas and Shurayh limit the deferment rule to usury; for other debts and transactions, the debtor must pay or may be imprisoned until payment.
[QURTUBI-FULL:4:6/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عطاء والزهري | reports | Ata, al-Zuhri, and Kufan jurists say a wife cannot seek separation for her husband's poverty and should await payment as a court debt; they cite the respite for an insolvent debtor and the encouragement to marry the poor. | mentions 2:280, 24:32

[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:279-280/r14] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · النبي | reports | The source cites a Prophetic report that a Muslim creditor who postpones a due debt receives charity for every day of delay.

[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:279-280/r9] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | A reading can say that the creditor is waiting for payment or is the one granting respite; another is an imperative to grant respite.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p135#2/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | If a debtor is insolvent, the ruling or command is to grant time until solvency; maysara has two language forms, one read by Nafi'.

[NASAFI-FULL:v1p135#2/r4] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | Another interpretation takes charity to mean granting the insolvent debtor more time; a Prophetic report says delaying an owed debt earns charity for every day.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p135#2/r5] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | If people know this is better for them and fail to act, they are treated as if they do not know.
