4/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عباس | reports | A second interpretation of الفوم is wheat, supported by a verse of poetry Ibn Abbas recited about farming فوم.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو إسحاق الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says أدنى derives from دنو, nearness; it can be lower in value and baseness, or nearer to them in this world, while the better food brings afterlife benefit through patience.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | On the near-in-this-world interpretation, the superior food's benefit is obtained in the afterlife through patience.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r15] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · علي بن سليمان الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash derives أدنى from أدنأ, the lowly or base thing, with hamza softened; the poem is cited as a witness.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Alternatively it may be an accusative circumstantial phrase describing the omitted pronoun returning to ما; من remains explanatory.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | بقل is all plant growth without a stem, and its plural is بقول.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | قثاء is the familiar cucumber; its singular is قثاءة, following the قمح/قمحة pattern.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · قرئ | reports | The usual pronunciation of قثاء has kasra on qāf, and another reading uses ḍamma.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The hamza in قثاء is original, as in أقثأت الأرض when cucumbers become abundant; its pattern is فعال.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | قثاء may have the plural قثائي, like علباء and علابي.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · بعضهم | reports | Some derive قثاء from a waw-root and cite أقثأت القوم and فثأت القدر; the author calls this a gross error because the expected forms would instead preserve the waw-derived pattern, with yāʾ substitution in the four-letter verb.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#4/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects that waw-root argument: it should produce أقثيت from أقثوت and فثوت القدر, and the poem should say نفثوها, not نفثؤها.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The poem fragment is cited with al-Akhfash's proposal that أدنى originated as أدنأ with hamza and underwent hamza easing.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | In the basic language, المصر is the boundary between two things; people of Hajar used مصورها to mean a property's boundaries, and a poem calls the sun a boundary between day and night.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · زهير الفرقبي | reports | Zuhayr al-Firqabi reads أدنأ with hamza, supporting the derivation from دنأ and dناءة.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · قيل | reports | A third etymology takes أدنى from أدون, the bad or inferior thing; the middle radical is moved after the final radical, producing أَدْنَو, then undergoes weak-letter change, on pattern أفلع.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | أدنى is the predicate of هو, while هو خير is likewise a relative clause with a resumptive element.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · قرأ الجمهور | reports | The majority read مصر with nunation, matching the written muṣḥaf; one explanation is that they were sent to any Egyptian city, so the word is declinable.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · قيل | reports | They may instead have been sent to Egypt itself, which is declinable because its middle consonant is silent and the name is light like هند and دعد; a poem illustrates the forms, or the declension may reflect treating it as a place name.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحسن وغيره | reports | Al-Hasan and others read مصر without nunation; the same form occurs in some Uthman and Ubayy codices, as if a particular place were intended.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says the name is an Arabicized Persian form of مصرائيم; if it names a specific place, its foreignness means it should be indeclinable, like ماه and جور and حمص. | mentions 12:99
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#5/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The majority unanimously prevent declension for مصر in يوسف 12:99. | mentions 12:99
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ما is an accusative noun of إن, whose predicate is لكم; it means that which, with its resumptive pronoun omitted: الذي سألتموه.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib says the mīm in مسكنة is extra in the stronger view; citing the dispute also suggests the nūn is extra and the root is مسك.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The alif in باء is from wāw, as shown by باء يبوء and the pattern قال يقول; باء means to return, and the prophetic wording أبوء بنعمتك علي illustrates it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | A poem's آبوا is wrongly offered as evidence for باء: it belongs to آَبَ يَؤوب, with radicals hamza-waw-bāʾ, unlike باء يبوء; reversing the roots is unattested.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa regards the indefinite-relative analysis as weak because the request refers to a specific thing, not an unspecified one.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · قرئ | reports | A reading سلتم, like بعتم, is derived from the long-alif verb سال.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Hassan's poem سالت هذيل رسول الله witnesses the long-alif form; the origin of its alif is disputed as yāʾ, waw, or hamza, with details deferred to 70:1. | mentions 70:1
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ضربت is passive, with الذلة as its نائب الفاعل.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ضربت means imposed or decreed upon them, by analogy with erecting tents; al-Farazdaq's poem to Jarir uses ضربت عليك.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | الذلة means humiliation; ذل with ḍamma is subjection imposed by coercion, while ذل with kasra follows resistance without coercion, as al-Raghib says.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | مسكنة is on the pattern مفعلة from stillness, since poverty leaves a person with little movement; مسكين follows مفعيل.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#6/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The mīm recurs in تمسكن and متمسكن, but this does not make it a root letter: derivation shows it is extra, as with تمندل and تمذرع.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | البواء can mean return in retaliation, and people may be بواء, equal in a matter; the poem لا يبوء الدم بالدم means blood is not repaid with blood in retribution.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · قيل | reports | An alternative attaches من الله directly to the verb, understanding رجعوا من الله بغضب; the author considers this weak.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | باء بكذا can also mean acknowledge or admit; the cited prophetic report means accepting the favor and binding oneself to it, and poetry uses بؤت بحقها.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib says البواء originally means equal parts sharing one place, contrasted with النبوة, the parts' incompatibility.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib explains باؤوا بغضب as settling in a place with wrath; باء signals that their fitting place carries God's anger, and he compares the ironic announcement in 3:21. | mentions 3:21
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib says interpreting بؤت بحقها as I admitted its right does not follow the word's lexical implication.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · خلف الأحمر | reports | Khalaf al-Ahmar says حياك الله وبياك originally was بوأك and changed for sound-pattern matching.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | بغضب is a circumstantial phrase describing those who returned: they returned under wrath, not with wrath as a direct object.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari reads باء فلان بفلان as deserving to be killed in retaliation for him, hence becoming deserving God's wrath; this makes the bāʾ phrase unlike a circumstantial phrase.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#7/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | من الله most naturally modifies غضب as an adjective, with an implied كائن; من marks metaphorical origin.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#8/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The demonstrative ذلك points back to the humiliation, poverty, and incurring of wrath; the clause بأنهم is its predicate and explains the cause of that fate.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#8/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيد | reports | Abu Ubayd says nearly all readers, ordinary speakers, and most Arabs omit the hamza, and relates a report in which the Prophet corrects a man who says يا نبيء الله.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#8/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu Ubayda says Arabs replace hamza in النبي، البرية، and الخابية; he adds الذرية from ذرأ, while Sibawayh's تنبأ مسيلمة shows that the hamza is original.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#8/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الحاكم | reports | Al-Hakim grades the correction report authentic according to the conditions of the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections, though neither collection includes it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#8/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya argues the report is weak because the Prophet did not object when al-Abbas recited يا خاتم النبآء, and the singular should not differ from the plural.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:61#8/r14] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The author will not reject Nafi's canonical reading on the basis of a speculative report; if the report is sound, يا نبيء الله may have been stopped because it suggested an expelled person, not because hamza itself was wrong.
