[TABATABAI:55:31-78/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | A report through Abu Musa assigns two gardens of gold to those brought near and two gardens of silver to the companions of the right.
[TABATABAI:55:56-74/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | The two gardens are lower in rank, grace, and honor than the preceding pair, though similar in their blessings; they belong to believers below the sincere God-fearing, namely the people of the right.
[TABATABAI:55:56-74/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | An alternative takes the phrase to mean near the first two gardens, suggesting the later gardens also belong to their inhabitants.
[TABATABAI:55:56-74/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعضهم | reports | Some claim these two gardens are better than the preceding pair and that their descriptions are more laudatory.
[TABATABAI:55:56-74/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | The lower-rank reading is stronger in light of the distinction between the sincere, near-to-God and the people of the right.
[ASAD-NOTES:55:62/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Besides the two gardens already described, there are two further gardens.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p110/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The sūrah distinguishes the characteristics of Paradise for the muqarrabīn from those for the asḥāb al-yamīn.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p130/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Min dunihima may mean besides, in contrast with, or lesser than the first two gardens; the refrain signals that further gardens and favours remain.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:55:46/r17] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | In light of the meanings of dun and related reports, the verse can mean either two gardens at a lower rank than the first two or two more gardens beyond them.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:55:46/r18] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Ibn Atiyya characterizes these deductions about rank as uncertain inferences.
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r7#8/r14] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · author | holds | Al-Raḥmān describes two levels of gardens, presenting the higher pair first and then explicitly labeling the other pair as lower in rank.
