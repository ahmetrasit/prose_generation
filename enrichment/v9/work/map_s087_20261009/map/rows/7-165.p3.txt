[TABRISI:7:165-166/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو زيد | reports | Abū Zayd distinguishes bāʾusa for being mighty or brave from biʾsa in poverty; baʾsāʾ is a noun for hardship or poverty.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | They abandoned the admonition and continued fishing; God rescued those forbidding evil and punished the wrongdoers for disobedience.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī says the severe punishment struck them before they were transformed into apes.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r15] TABRISI, d. 548 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddī says the admonishers and silent observers were saved while the fishers perished.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Noncanonical readings include bayiʾs from Ibn ʿAbbās; biʾs from Zayd b. Thābit, Yaḥyā, al-Sulamī, and al-Ḥasan; bīs from Ṭalḥa and reportedly Nāfiʿ; bāʾis from Mujāhid.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Baʾīs can be an adjective on faʿīl from bāʾusa, meaning severe, or a maṣdar used adjectivally, as in al-nadhīr and al-nakīr.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The biʾs reading treats the normally verbal form as a noun and adjective, like qīl and qāl.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Bayyiʾs is analyzed as fayʿal, analogous to ḍaygham and ḥaydar; this pattern is normally for a weak second radical, so this form is judged an error.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن جني | reports | Ibn Jinnī explains the hamza in the variant by its resemblance to the two weak letters.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Biʾs on faʿl may suggest biʾsa al-rajulu baʾsatan, “the man was courageous,” implying punishment came promptly; it may instead be shortened from baʾīs.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Bīs is explained as a softened hamza in biʾs, moved toward yāʾ; Ibn Mayyāda illustrates the softened pronunciation of yawmaʾidhin.
[TABRISI:7:165-166/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو حاتم | rejects | Abū Ḥātim rejects al-Ḥasan’s biʾs reading, arguing it would require mā after it, as in niʿma mā.
