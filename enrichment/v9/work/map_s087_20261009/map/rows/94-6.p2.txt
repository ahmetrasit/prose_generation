[RAZI:94:6/r4] RAZI, d. 606 · الفراء والزجاج | reports | Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj take definite al-ʿusr as generic and thus one hardship in both clauses, while the indefinite yusr denotes two distinct eases.
[RAZI:94:6/r5] RAZI, d. 606 · الجرجاني | rejects | Al-Jurjānī rejects that grammatical inference: repeating a phrase about one knight and a sword does not entail two swords.
[RAZI:94:6/r6] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The second clause may repeat the first to establish its meaning firmly, as the repeated warning in 77:15 does. | mentions 77:15
[RAZI:94:6/r7] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | On this reading, the two eases are worldly success in opening lands and the afterlife's reward in Paradise, as in the two good outcomes of 9:52. | mentions 9:52
[RAZI:94:6/r8] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The indefinite form of yusr magnifies it: the promise is of an immense, remarkable ease.
[RAZI:94:6/r9] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Although hardship and ease are opposites, ease follows hardship after a short interval so certainly that it is treated as accompanying it.
[QURTUBI:94:6/r1] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The hardship is constriction and difficulty; the ease is spaciousness and wealth.
[QURTUBI:94:6/r10] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The worldly promise was fulfilled before the Prophet died: he conquered the Hijaz and Yemen, gained wealth, gave lavish gifts including 200 camels, and kept a year's provisions for his family.
[QURTUBI:94:6/r11] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Although the first worldly favor was specific to the Prophet, some of his community may also receive it, God willing.
[QURTUBI:94:6/r12] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The second clause begins a new promise because it has no coordinating particle such as fa or waw to mark it as joined to the first.
[QURTUBI:94:6/r13] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The second promise is for all believers: hardship in this world necessarily accompanies ease in the afterlife, though worldly and afterlife ease may also coincide.
[QURTUBI:94:6/r14] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | The report that one hardship cannot overcome two eases means worldly ease might be overcome, but afterlife ease is certain and nothing can overcome it.
[QURTUBI:94:6/r15] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | reports | Another interpretation makes the hardship the Prophet's expulsion from Mecca and the ease his honorable return at its conquest with ten thousand men.
[QURTUBI:94:6/r2] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الفراء | reports | The repeated clause emphasizes and magnifies the promise, like repeated commands, repeated answers, Qur'an 102:3, and the poet's repeated appeal. | mentions 102:3
[QURTUBI:94:6/r3] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ثعلب | reports | In Arab usage, repeating a definite noun refers to the same thing, while repeating an indefinite noun refers to another; thus there are two eases, strengthening hope and patience.
