[BINTSHATI:v1p106/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Al-ʿādiyāt occurs only here in the Qur'an; the root conveys distance and exceeding, reflected in swift running beyond ordinary pace, enmity's distance, and transgression beyond right.
[BINTSHATI:v1p106/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Though horsemen can be called al-ʿādiyāt, ḍabḥ identifies horses themselves because it is their breathing sound when they run fast.
[BINTSHATI:v1p106/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | Ḍabḥan may be a cognate accusative with an omitted verb or a circumstantial adjective; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers the verbal noun for its absolute, unrestricted sense.
[BINTSHATI:v1p106/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The fāʾ linking mūriyāt to al-ʿādiyāt suggests causation: sparks result from hard running as the horses' hooves strike stones.
[BINTSHATI:v1p119/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | She finds a striking, previously unnoticed connection between the opening violent raid and the closing scene of graves and hearts laid bare.
[BINTSHATI:v1p119/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | The surah opens by evoking the familiar sudden raid at dawn, before its targets notice the raiders have entered and scattered their gathering in dust.
[BINTSHATI:v1p119/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | A familiar raid's panic provides an image for the unseen resurrection: people are scattered from graves, depart in groups, and face rapid, linked events culminating before the All-Aware. | mentions 99:6

[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p415/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-ʿādiyāt means running and describes war horses, since the four following adjectives suit them; the camels of Muzdalifah interpretation has no corroboration and does not fit those adjectives.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p415/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | The alternative that the word means warriors' horses is unsupported, especially because it leaves the oath unrelated to verse 6's assertion that man is ungrateful to God. | mentions 100:6
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p415/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | Both alternative readings wrongly assume that oath objects must be sacred; Islahi rejects that assumption and says the real point is their testimony.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p416/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The oath sworn by horses is evidence for the later premise that man is ungrateful to his Lord.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p416/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Ḍabḥ is the sound from panting horses' nostrils; their exhausted state shows dutiful obedience and spending their last energy on their master's task.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p418/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Horses are singled out because clans needed high-pedigree warhorses for attack, defense, and family protection; their importance also made them common in classical poetry.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p418/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The author cites a Ḥamāsī poet's verse about spending on a thoroughbred horse as a house sentinel and keeping a servant for it, illustrating horses' esteem.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:100:1/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The oath depicts horses charging the enemy in battle, then swears by them because after humans they were the most important element of old warfare. It points to God as the giver of these useful, much-loved possessions and uses what people valued to stress the truth and importance of the following message.
[STUDYQURAN:100:1/r1] STUDYQURAN · al-Bayḍāwī | reports | Al-Bayḍāwī allegorizes the chargers as souls transmitting holiness, kindling spiritual knowledge, attacking caprice, raising desire for God, and entering the highest Garden. | mentions 83:18, 83:19
[STUDYQURAN:100:1/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The oaths can refer to battle horses or pilgrimage camels, and to both since each performs a function worthy of God's oath.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r1] STUDYQURAN · most commentators | reports | Most commentators date the surah early Makkan, likely after al-Qāriʿah; others place it early Madinan.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The Makkan attribution draws on stylistic similarity; the Madinan view draws on some interpretations of the subject.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | One occasion report describes al-Mundhir ibn ʿAmr's expedition to Banū Kinānah: after news was delayed and hypocrites said the men had been killed, the verse announced its horses.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r4] STUDYQURAN · others | reports | Others connect the verses to Badr; a report attributed to ʿAlī says only two horses were there and the other mounts were camels.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r5] STUDYQURAN · ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib | reports | Another account attributed to ʿAlī sees the oaths as pilgrims rushing from ʿArafāt to Muzdalifah and Minā.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The first five verses swear by chargers; 6–8 discuss human ingratitude, while 9–11 warn of judgment and disclosure of hearts.
