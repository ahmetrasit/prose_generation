[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · بعض القصاص | reports | Some storytellers take it as people gathering with the believers morning and evening; Ibn al-Musayyib, ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Abi ʿAmra, and others object that the verse concerns congregational prayers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبو جعفر | reports | Abu Jaʿfar interprets it as reciting and learning the Qur'an.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says it means worship; Ibrahim in another report says remembrance of God; al-Zajjaj says monotheistic, sincere supplication and worship.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبو زيد | reports | Abu Zayd attests غدوة for the morning of the current day; Sibawayh and al-Khalil also report a dialect that declines it with tanwin.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says the statement answers attacks on poor believers' sincerity: the Prophet must judge by their outward conduct, and each person bears their own account, as in the rule that no bearer bears another's burden. | mentions 6:164
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | The author rejects al-Zamakhshari's hypothetical that their inner state might be blameworthy, since God has already testified to their sincere intentions.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | The author rebukes Abu ʿUbayd for ignorance of the dialect and reading transmission, defending the readers' competence and the principle that recitation is transmitted orally.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Al-ʿAshiyy is apparently synonymous with al-ʿashiyya, though it may be a collective; the cited verse uses it for late afternoon. | mentions 38:31
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُ means they make their intentions sincere for God in worship; 'face' can denote a thing's essence and reality.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas explains seeking God's face as seeking His reward.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The phrase يريدون وجهه is circumstantial; theologians who use 'His face' to prove a bodily member are mistaken.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan and the majority understand 'their reckoning' and 'your reckoning' as the reckoning of deeds.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p522/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari transmits an interpretation of reckoning as worldly provision: the Prophet neither provides for them nor they for him.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523#2/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The first من is partitive and circumstantial: if it followed شيء it would be adjectival; عليک is the predicate under the Hijazi analysis, or the subject predicate otherwise.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523#2/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Making the second من حسابك circumstantial is weak because a semantic عامل cannot normally have its حال advanced before both itself and its possessor; other predicate analyses are also weak because عليهم carries the main information.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523#2/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The two clauses honor the Prophet by placing him first both times, although the second could have been phrased with the believers first; this is reciprocal framing (رد العجز على الصدر).
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523#2/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The apparent فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ responds to the negation: since their reckoning is not the Prophet's concern, expulsion cannot follow from it; the first possible sense of ما تأتينا فتحدثنا does not fit.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523#2/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | فتكون is coordinated with فتطردهم, reporting the nonoccurrence of both expulsion and its consequence, wrongdoing.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523#2/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Another parsing makes فتكون a response to the prohibition لا تطرد, with the two account clauses interrupting between prohibition and result.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says the paired clauses together mean that neither side is held to the other's reckoning, echoing the rule that no soul bears another's burden. | mentions 6:164

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | The apparent pronouns throughout the two clauses refer to the poor believers who call on God.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari allows the pronouns to refer to the polytheists, making the clauses parenthetical: the Prophet's account is not theirs, so their belief should not lead him to expel believers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise allows the pronouns to mean the disbelievers seeking the poor believers' expulsion, while تطردهم refers to the weak believers; the following ف supports a causal link.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p523/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari says reckoning here concerns worldly provision, in which case all pronouns refer to the believers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p524/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | On the prohibitive parsing, the account clauses and the response to the first are parenthetical before the result of the ban on expelling them.
