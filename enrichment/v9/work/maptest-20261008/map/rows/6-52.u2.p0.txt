## THE CURRENT MAP
6:52/q01 What prompted the prohibition on expelling the worshippers? [وَلَا تَطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · reports] (turns on: Which proposal the reports describe, and whether later Medinan incidents are causes or applications of a Meccan verse.)
  6:52/q01/p1 Quraysh or other Meccan notables demanded removal of poor believers from the Prophet's company as a condition of meeting or following him. — reasons: Ibn Masud, Qatada and many later transmitters name Suhayb, Ammar, Bilal, Khabbab and other poor followers; chiefs scorned their low status, dress or alleged dependence. Accounts vary on names, whether the Prophet refused, considered separate times, or a written pledge was begun. Ibn al-Anbari, Razi and Qurtubi say the Prophet hoped to win converts, not disparage believers. Reports of Sa'd put six companions there; lists differ. Khabbab also describes morning and evening teaching of Qur'an, religion and the afterlife. Ibn Zayd adds a man ashamed to be seen with Salman and Bilal, after which the Prophet was told to greet them with mercy; Baghawi transmits Mujahid's naming of Bilal and Ibn Umm Abd. Ibn Ishaq, as reported by Ibn Hisham, names Abu Fakihah Yasar alongside Khabbab, Ammar and Suhayb, and says Quraysh mocked the poor as unworthy of receiving guidance first; he connects the response to 6:52–54.
  6:52/q01/p2 Al-Aqra ibn Habis and Uyayna ibn Hisn requested an exclusive audience away from poor companions, sometimes before visiting Arab delegations; Gabriel's revelation interrupted a proposed written agreement. — reasons: Khabbab and Salman transmit this version. Accounts describe their embarrassment at wool clothes or slaves, the Prophet dropping or tearing the page, recalling the believers, sitting knee to knee, and staying until they rose; 18:28 follows in some accounts. Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ashur question its use as a Meccan revelation occasion because these men appeared in Medina; Ibn Ashur allows a later analogous request. Khabbab's report in Ibn Majah says Gabriel revealed 6:52 when al-Aqra and Uyayna sought private access and dismissal of the poor; it also links 6:53–54 and 18:28 to their favor and continued companionship.
  6:52/q01/p3 The nobles requested that poor worshippers move behind them in the prayer rows, rather than leave the Prophet's gathering. — reasons: Ibn Abbas's report makes precedence in prayer the dispute; Tabari expressly contrasts this with expulsion from the gathering. Reports differ about whether nobles promised belief.
  6:52/q01/p4 Abu Talib was approached by nobles, or himself proposed dismissing poor followers, and Umar suggested trying the request before revelation. — reasons: Muqatil attributes the suggestion to Abu Talib; Ikrima's account has Banu Abd Manaf petition him. Several reports say Umar apologized after the verse; names of freedmen and allies vary. Baydawi adds Umar's temporary suggestion and the arrival of Ali and a document just before revelation.
  6:52/q01/p5 The report about al-Aqra and Uyayna is chronologically doubtful as the verse is Meccan, though a similar incident may have happened later. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and Ibn Kathir note those men became known after the Hijra; Ibn Ashur says the original verse could later answer an analogous request. Tabataba'i treats the similar stories as applications, citing the sura's single Meccan descent and unreliable occasion reports; Kuranyolu records commentators' like objection and retains the universal lesson.
  6:52/q01/p6 A proposal to expel the Companions of the Suffa occasioned the verse. — reasons: Tabataba'i rejects it as a specific occasion: the sura was revealed in Mecca before the Hijra.
6:52/q02 What does ṭard mean here? [تَطْرُدِ · meaning]
  6:52/q02/p1 It means to drive away or exclude, with a humiliating or disparaging force. — reasons: Jishumi gives banishment and expulsion; Abu Hayyan adds humiliation and related Arabic usage; Islahi says the harsh verb fits Quraysh's proposed contempt.
6:52/q03 Who are the people whom the Prophet must not drive away? [ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · referent] (turns on: Whether the verse is limited to the original companions or its wording applies to other believers.)
  6:52/q03/p1 The poor Muslim believers in the Prophet's company. — reasons: Mujahid names Bilal and Ibn Umm Abd, with one account saying Ibn Umm Ma'bad or Ibn Umm Maktum; other reports name Salman, Khabbab, Suhayb, Ammar, Ibn Masud, Sa'd, al-Miqdad, freedmen and allies. Hasan singles out congregational worshippers; Abu Ja'far describes Quran learners. Ibn Ashur says their public monotheism showed faith before Medinan hypocrisy emerged. Ibn Ishaq, as reported by Ibn Hisham, additionally names Abu Fakihah Yasar. Itqan's list explicitly includes Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Salman al-Farisi among Suhayb, Bilal, Ammar, Khabbab and Ibn Masud. Khabbab's Ibn Majah report identifies the poor believers whom the chiefs wanted dismissed.
  6:52/q03/p2 More broadly, they include anyone who believes in God and seeks His grace, even without full Qur'anic belief. — reasons: Asad distinguishes the verse's enduring wording from its historical occasion, links 6:51 and 6:53, and counsels patient explanation rather than repulsion.
6:52/q04 What does calling upon their Lord morning and evening denote? [يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether الدعاء names prescribed prayer, particular prayers, remembrance, Quran, supplication, or comprehensive worship.)
  6:52/q04/p1 The prescribed prayers, sometimes all five. — reasons: Ibn Abbas, Ibrahim, Mujahid, Hasan, Qatada, and others say obligatory prayer; some specify congregation. Ibrahim and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib oppose making it post-prayer storytelling. A minority identifies dawn and afternoon specifically; Muqatil and Hasan describe two Meccan daily prayers before five were prescribed; Amr ibn Shuaib identifies the evening as isha. These time-specific details remain distinct in the following positions.
  6:52/q04/p2 Specifically the dawn and afternoon prayers. — reasons: Mujahid, Qatada and Ibn Abbas identify the two times. Alusi says these prayers are singled out for their honor.
  6:52/q04/p3 The two daily prayers then practiced in Mecca, before five prayers were prescribed. — reasons: Muqatil specifies two units at dawn and two at evening; Abu Hayyan attributes this to Hasan and Muqatil.
  6:52/q04/p4 The evening reference is the isha prayer. — reasons: Amr ibn Shuaib gives this identification.
  6:52/q04/p5 Remembrance of God or supplication. — reasons: Ibrahim gives remembrance; some describe literal supplication. Abu Hayyan emphasizes asking, refuge and desire; al-Zajjaj includes monotheism and sincerity. Mujahid sought a post-dawn remembrance gathering, but Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib rejected using this verse for storytellers.
  6:52/q04/p6 Quran recitation, learning or honoring the Quran. — reasons: Abu Ja'far describes the Prophet teaching them and their reciting for God's face; Khabbab's report adds instruction in Paradise, Hell, death and resurrection.
  6:52/q04/p7 Worship in a comprehensive sense, including prayer, remembrance, praise, and required and voluntary acts. — reasons: Tabari declines to narrow الدعاء: 40:60 calls worship supplication; al-Dahhak cites 40:43. Ibn Kathir likewise joins worship and asking. Several commentators allow all practices together.
  6:52/q04/p8 Inwardly, it is constant presence and orientation to God in every act. — reasons: Qashani speaks of presence of heart, spiritual witnessing and love without seeking reward; Tustari includes every motion and rest; Sulami's authorities urge constant remembrance and pure intention.
6:52/q05 What do morning and evening contribute to the description? [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the temporal pair is literal, exemplary of constant practice, or chosen because worship then is especially significant.)
  6:52/q05/p1 They express continual devotion across the day. — reasons: Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi and others read the endpoints as continuity. Ibn Jawzi says morning stands for day and evening for night; Qushayri distinguishes limited outward acts from unbroken inward desire; Ibn Ashur derives all-day practice from generic time words and the temporal ba. Bursevi says constant remembrance keeps one with God.
  6:52/q05/p2 They mark especially telling devotion during busy hours, or honor the prayers at those times. — reasons: Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan say people are ordinarily occupied then, so their worship is stronger evidence of devotion; Biqai says constancy proves sincerity rather than ostentation. Alusi says dawn and afternoon prayers are singled out for honor.
  6:52/q05/p3 They name the exact morning and evening acts or prayer times. — reasons: Ibn Ashur notes that prayer can stand for its time or place; Qiraat-ER contrasts an instance morning ghudwa with general ghadah, affecting a specific versus habitual sense.
6:52/q06 How is the morning expression read? [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ · readings] (turns on: Whether the attested time noun can take the definite article, and the authority of oral transmission versus spelling.)
  6:52/q06/p1 The majority read بالغداة, al-ghadāh, with alif and an open ghayn. — reasons: Abu Ali favors this common reading because ghadāh can receive the article; ghudwa is usually a proper time name. Sibawayh, Yunus and Farra are cited about definiteness. Written waw does not establish sound: الصلاة and الزكاة also have written waw. Qiraat-ER distinguishes general morning from an instance.
  6:52/q06/p2 Ibn Amir and other transmitted readers read بالغدوة, al-ghudwa, with waw and a rounded ghayn. — reasons: The reading occurs also at 18:28. Al-Khalil and Sibawayh attest indefinite ghudwa, which can take the article, or a temporal adverb like duhwa; Abu Ali and al-Wahidi explain generic use of a proper name, citing Ibn Mayada.
