## THE CURRENT MAP
12:49/q01 What does ذَٰلِكَ refer to? [ذَٰلِكَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the demonstrative resumes the numbered years, whose grammatical form can take masculine agreement, or the drought itself.)
  12:49/q01/p1 The seven preceding years. — reasons: Al-Kalbī explains the masculine demonstrative by the form of سبع, which lacks a feminine marker; Ibn al-Qāsim gives the same grammatical account, and al-Kalbī cites a parallel verse in which masculine agreement follows such a feminine noun (al-Wāḥidī; Ibn al-Jawzī).
  12:49/q01/p2 The drought. — reasons: Muqātil identifies the antecedent as the drought rather than the years (al-Wāḥidī; Ibn al-Jawzī).
12:49/q02 What does the choice of عَامٌ convey? [عَامٌ · rhetoric]
  12:49/q02/p1 It marks a full twelve-month year of relief under God's control. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says the word designates a complete twelve months and underscores that God alone knows and controls the coming relief.
  12:49/q02/p2 It suggests ease and fertility after the hard years. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī contrasts عام, often associated with ease and fertility, with سنة, often associated with hardship and famine; both mean year.
12:49/q03 How did Joseph know the details of the year after the famine? [ثُمَّ يَأْتِى مِنۢ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ عَامٌۭ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the dream or the normal end of drought establishes only general relief, or also establishes the precise rain, produce, and timing promised here.)
  12:49/q03/p1 God disclosed an additional, unasked-for year to Joseph by revelation or prophetic knowledge. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī, al-Manār as quoted by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Kuranyolu say it was outside the king's dream; Qatāda says God taught Joseph the unasked year (ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Durr). Al-Ṭabarī and al-Ṭabrisī call it a sign of prophethood and truthfulness; al-Qurṭubī calls it evidence of merit. Al-Wāḥidī uses the detailed forecast to support direct revelation over taught interpretation; al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī distinguish the general end of famine from the precise rain and pressing, which require revelation. Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes interpretation, practical advice, and an unseen report; al-Ālūsī treats revelation as apparent. Ibn ʿAbbās says Joseph volunteered the rain and pressing details (al-Durr).
  12:49/q03/p2 The dream or the end of the seven hard years itself implied a return to abundance. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the dream's interpretation already implied that hardship ends after seven years (reported by Abū Ḥayyān). Al-Bayḍāwī allows inference from the end of drought or God's customary relief after hardship, as well as revelation; the dream's cows and ears symbolize the fertile and famine years and consumed stores (also al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī quoting him). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī derives the final year from the repeated seven and the end of the need to manage sowing and reserves. Al-Ālūsī rejects a merely natural inference as too vague for the specific promised relief.
12:49/q04 What does يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ mean? [يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ · meaning] (turns on: The rain derivation from ghayth, the relief derivation from ghawth, or abundance as the practical form of aid; the following pressing clause and the forms of the Arabic roots are cited.)
  12:49/q04/p1 People receive rain. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba equates ghayth with rain; Ibn al-Sikkīt describes rain falling on the land (al-Wāḥidī; al-Rāzī). Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and Qatāda are cited for rain (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Durr, al-Ālūsī). Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes forms for rain, help, and seeking aid; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call the rain derivation triliteral, and al-Samīn quotes a Bedouin woman's غثنا for obtaining rain. Al-Tabāṭabāʾī prefers rain because pressing follows; he says Nile floods depend on rain upstream in Sudan and cites Lisān al-ʿArab for ghayth as vegetation grown from rainwater. Asad likewise notes Egypt's Nile floods depend on upstream rain. Al-Ṣādiq takes the word from rain in reports collected by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī; al-Ṭabrisī says ghayth is needed rain.
  12:49/q04/p2 God relieves and rescues people from famine and distress. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya connect the word with ghawth, aid or relief; al-Ṭabrisī defines it as urgently needed help that removes harm. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī distinguish the quadriliteral relief derivation from the triliteral rain derivation; al-Biqāʿī notes the forms. Al-Baghawī invokes the idiom of seeking and receiving help; al-Nasafī mentions answered pleas. Asad allows relief alongside rain. Al-Iṣlāḥī prefers help as the literal force and says drought drives people to plead before rain comes; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds rain more apt in this context, except under a passive rain reading of the next verb.
  12:49/q04/p3 People receive abundant produce. — reasons: Ibn ʿĪsā gives this as an explanation in al-Māwardī; Ibn al-Jawzī attributes relief through abundance to al-Māwardī.
12:49/q05 How is يَعْصِرُونَ read? [يَعْصِرُونَ · readings] (turns on: The subject or addressee, active versus passive vocalization, and whether the verb denotes pressing, deliverance, or rain; al-Ṭabarī weighs established regional transmission against a reported rain reading.)
  12:49/q05/p1 Active third-person yaʿṣirūn: they press. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī and others assign yāʾ to Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim, and Ibn ʿĀmir; it resumes الناس (Ibn Khālawayh, al-Fārisī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn). Al-Ṭabarī accepts it as an established regional reading equal in meaning to the address form; Qiraat-Er calls it standard active Form IV. ʿAlī is reported to object to active pressing of wine and direct the reciter toward rain (al-Ṭabrisī; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī).
  12:49/q05/p2 Active second-person taʿṣirūn: you press. — reasons: Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read tāʾ, according to Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, al-Thaʿlabī, and others; it addresses Joseph's questioners and continues the earlier direct address, including قدمتم (al-Fārisī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī). Ibn Khālawayh says it singles them out; al-Ṭabrisī says it may include others. Al-Ṭabarī accepts this established reading too, and Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with a second-person form tied to milking (al-Durr).
  12:49/q05/p3 Passive third-person yuʿṣarūn: they are relieved or rained upon. — reasons: Al-Aʿraj, Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad, and ʿĪsā are named by Ibn Jinnī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī. Some explain rescue; others rain from rain-bearing clouds in 78:14 (Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Zajjāj, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī). Saʿīd b. Jubayr is also cited for a passive reading; al-Ṣādiq and ʿAlī favor rain in reports collected by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. Al-Ṭabarī rejects a non-established yāʾ rain reading against the regional readers.
  12:49/q05/p4 Passive second-person taʿṣarūn: you are rescued or rained upon. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports a second reading from ʿĪsā meaning rescue; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī also assign him a passive address form. Al-Qurṭubī reports ʿĪsā's tāʾ with open ṣād for rain, alongside a kasra variant of the same sense; al-Durr mentions a second-person reciter and 78:14.
  12:49/q05/p5 An intensified or assimilated form is read. — reasons: Al-Naqqāsh transmits a doubled form, described as intensive by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī; their descriptions vary as to vocalization and passive form. Zayd b. ʿAlī reads a form from taʿtaṣirūn with assimilation, which may mean squeezing or rescue (Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī). Al-Naqqāsh also records a reading about squeezing moisture (Ibn ʿAṭiyya).
12:49/q06 What do people do in يَعْصِرُونَ? [يَعْصِرُونَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the verb is physical squeezing, a related action such as milking, an Arabic rescue or prosperity idiom, or a rain expression; cited readings, poetry, and early authorities supply the competing evidence.)
  12:49/q06/p1 They press fruit and plants for juice or oil. — reasons: The lists vary: grapes, olives, sesame, other fruit, sugarcane, radish, oil, wine, syrup, or water (Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, Mujāhid, Ibn Jurayj and many commentators). Al-Qurṭubī cites al-Bukhārī for Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Wāḥidī relates sesame oil, grape wine, and olive oil and points to 12:36. Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī report fourteen years without pressing oil or grapes; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report that nothing was pressed during drought. Al-Samīn calls this the clearest sense; al-Biqāʿī says pressing expresses the transition to fertility. Al-Ṭabarī rejects the rescue derivation because Companion and Successor interpretation favors pressing; al-Wāḥidī reports al-Fārisī favoring both pressing and rescue.
  12:49/q06/p2 They milk livestock. — reasons: Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās through several transmissions (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr, al-Ālūsī); al-Durr also links it to the second-person reading. Al-Wāḥidī names al-Wālibī and Aḥmad b. ʿUbayd; Ibn al-Jawzī cites a poem using the verb for milking. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī explain that squeezing an udder is itself pressing; al-Qurṭubī says milk points to rich vegetation. Al-Ṭabarī rejects a report through al-Faraj b. Faḍāla from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭalḥa as contrary to known Arabic and Ibn ʿAbbās's usage.
