 ‘Ammar, al-Miqdad, and Bilal; revelation answered their demand.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | ‘You are not responsible for their account’ means their judgment and provision belong to God, as do the Prophet’s; the first min is partitive and the second emphatic extra.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Since God alone provides and judges, the Prophet must receive and sit with them; expelling them to accommodate people of lesser faith would be injustice.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The warning is instruction for others, not evidence that the Prophet actually expelled anyone; it resembles the conditional warning against shirk though he would never commit it. | mentions 39:65
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The syntax makes ‘then you would expel them’ a result of the preceding negation and ‘so you would be unjust’ a result of the prohibition; the clauses can also be reordered.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The verse and report forbid elevating people for status or fine clothing and despising those who are obscure or poorly dressed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · المشركون | reports | The polytheists objected to sitting with poor believers such as Salman, Suhayb, Bilal, and Khabbab, and asked that they be dismissed; the Prophet considered it but did not expel them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The Prophet inclined toward their request only in hope of converting them and their people, thinking it would neither harm nor diminish the poor companions; revelation forbade the contemplated expulsion.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · سعد بن أبي وقاص | reports | In Muslim’s report, Sa‘d and five other companions were present when the polytheists asked the Prophet to dismiss them, and revelation came after he reflected on the request.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن | reports | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and al-Hasan interpret morning and evening invocation as keeping the obligatory prayers in congregation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Other explanations take the invocation as remembrance and Qur’an recitation, or as prayers at daybreak and evening for guidance and forgiveness.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | ‘Seeking His face’ can mean sincerely seeking God’s obedience, or seeking God Himself, who is described as possessing the Face. | mentions 55:27, 13:22
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Morning and evening are specified because people are usually occupied then, so one devoted to worship during busy hours is still more devoted in free time.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The Prophet later obeyed the command in 18:28 to remain with these worshippers, not looking away to seek worldly display. | mentions 18:28
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r1] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | After commanding warning for the heedless, God commands honoring and drawing near the devout, rather than expelling them to appease Quraysh.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r2] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · قريش | reports | Quraysh offered to sit and talk with the Prophet if he expelled the poor Muslim worshippers, including Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, and Salman.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r3] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet first said he would not drive away believers; when the request was repeated, he agreed to make them rise when Quraysh arrived.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r4] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · عمر | reports | Umar asked the Prophet to do this temporarily so they could see what the visitors would do; as a document and Ali were brought to write, the verse was revealed.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r5] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Mentioning morning and evening can mean continuous worship, or the dawn and afternoon prayers; Ibn Amir reads ghudwah here and in al-Kahf.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r6] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | The worshippers seek God's face sincerely; making sincerity the qualification shows its centrality, and the prohibition consequently requires honoring them.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r7] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | The accounting clause may mean the Prophet is not responsible for their faith or inner sincerity, even if their inner state is as the pagans alleged; their account is theirs and his is his.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r8] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Another view takes their account as their livelihood and poverty; a further view takes the pronouns to refer to the pagans, who are not accountable for the Prophet's account or vice versa.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:6:52/r9] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Expelling the worshippers would make the Prophet one of the wrongdoers; the final clause answers the prohibition or follows from expelling as its consequence.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | After commanding warning of the non-pious, God commands drawing the pious near and forbids their dismissal.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r2] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Calling on God morning and evening means continuous worship, or specifically dawn and afternoon prayers, or all five prayers; the Shami reading has the stated form of 'morning.'
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r3] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Seeking God's face means sincerity; 'face' can denote a thing's essence and reality.

The Prophet threw away the paper and embraced them.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r5] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The believers' account is theirs alone and cannot transfer to the Prophet; the source compares the statement with a verse about Noah's reckoning. | mentions 26:113
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r6] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The consequence clause answers the negation, while the next clause answers the prohibition; alternatively, being unjust is caused by dismissing them.
[QASHANI:6:52-53/r1] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The dawn and dusk callers are complete people of unity; warnings benefit neither them, lost in God, nor those whose hearts are hardened.
[QASHANI:6:52-53/r2] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Their constant worship is presence of heart, witnessing of spirit, and orientation of the secret to God alone through eternal love, without seeking reward or fearing punishment.
[QASHANI:6:52-53/r3] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | They seek God's Essence rather than changing attributes or intermediaries, having witnessed the annihilation of means and even of their own selves.
