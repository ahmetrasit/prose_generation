  12:49/q06/p4 They receive rain, expressed through clouds being pressed or releasing water. — reasons: ʿĪsā and other passive readers are given this sense (al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī); 78:14 is repeatedly cited for rain-bearing clouds.   12:49/q06/p5 They prosper or receive abundant produce. — reasons: Al-Māwardī cites a poem using يعصر فينا for treating people well; Ibn al-Jawzī reports grammarians who take the clause as abundant produce making life prosperous. Al-Naqqāsh's intensive reading can convey abundance (al-Ālūsī).
  12:49/q06/p6 They obtain what they desire. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayd cites Ibn Aḥmar's poem about taking from life's branches (al-Wāḥidī; Ibn al-Jawzī).
  12:49/q06/p7 They give generously and grant gifts. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī cites a language scholar through Ibn al-Anbārī and says al-Azharī reports the sense from Abū ʿUbayd and Abū ʿUbayda.
  12:49/q06/p8 They bring livestock from abundant pasture. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this from Ibn ʿAbbās.
  12:49/q06/p9 They exploit or extract value from produce. — reasons: Abū al-Ghawth derives it from pressing grapes and compares iʿtaṣartu mālahu, extracting wealth from someone's hand (al-Qurṭubī).
12:49/q07 Why mention pressing produce in the promised year? [وَفِيهِ يَعْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
  12:49/q07/p1 Pressing marks an exceptional return of fertility and specific crops after famine. — reasons: Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī report that no oil or grapes had been pressed for fourteen years; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say nothing was pressed during drought. Al-Biqāʿī treats pressing as a transition marker. Al-Ālūsī says rain alone does not guarantee grapes and oil crops as directly as grain, so the detail adds good news. Study Quran sees oil and wine as signs of sufficient food; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees blessing for people and livestock.
  12:49/q07/p2 The detail suits the king's wine-server or the imagery of the dream. — reasons: Al-Iṣlāḥī says grape pressing subtly fits the king's wine-server; al-Ālūsī says the news suits the king's circumstances and second-person reading. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says rain, fodder, and milk best fit the fat and lean cow imagery.
12:49/q08 Why repeat فِيهِ? [فِيهِ وَفِيهِ · rhetoric]
  12:49/q08/p1 It distinguishes divine rain and relief from people's pressing. — reasons: Al-Bursawī says the first action belongs to God and the second to people.
  12:49/q08/p2 It enumerates or emphasizes distinct benefits of the year. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī allows different times or types of benefit, enumerated blessings, or emphasis that earlier drought rain was negligible beside this year's; fronting focuses the phrase and rhyme may account for the second occurrence.
12:49/q09 Why shift from direct address to ٱلنَّاسُ and يَعْصِرُونَ? [ٱلنَّاسُ يَعْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
  12:49/q09/p1 The wider phrasing signals that people can again live without managing famine reserves. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts Joseph's earlier direct address to the group with his reference here to people receiving rain or aid and pressing produce.
  12:49/q09/p2 The third-person verb resumes الناس; the second-person reading addresses Joseph's petitioners. — reasons: Ibn Khālawayh, al-Fārisī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn, and Qiraat-Er explain the pronouns this way; al-Ṭabrisī says the address can include others.
12:49/q10 How does the promised year relate to the king's dream and Joseph's interpretation? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the earlier seven fertile years are dream symbols being decoded, or Joseph's agricultural policy in response to a foreseen famine.)
  12:49/q10/p1 It is an additional year after seven fertile and seven famine years. — reasons: Qatāda calls it an unasked-for eighth year after the fourteen years (ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān). Fat cows and green ears signify fertile years; lean cows and dry ears signify famine, and stored abundance is consumed (al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, Ibn ʿĀshūr). Study Quran counts fifteen years in the interpretation. Al-Qummī's report likewise has plenty, famine, then rain.
  12:49/q10/p2 Joseph gave a survival policy for the expected famine, rather than deriving the fertile years from the dream. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects commentators' claim that the dream forecasts seven fertile followed by seven barren years; he reads Joseph's directions as the necessary policy for surviving anticipated famine.
12:49/q11 Why does Joseph end with this good news? [* · rhetoric]
  12:49/q11/p1 The placement brings hope after a discouraging forecast and shows Joseph's excellence. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says relief after hardship comforts the hearers; al-Ālūsī contrasts the placement with Joseph's answer to the prisoners, where prediction came first, and says the final glad news highlights his excellence.
12:49/q12 What inward state does the year of relief signify? [* · inward]
  12:49/q12/p1 The soul enjoys fruition after complete tranquility and security. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī reads the year of relief and pressing as the soul's enjoyment after attaining security.
12:49/q13 What happened to Egyptian people and property during Joseph's famine administration? [* · reports]
  12:49/q13/p1 Joseph sold stored food for successive classes of property, then freed people and restored their possessions. — reasons: A report from al-Riḍā says the sequence was money, valuables, livestock, servants, property, land, then personal freedom; Joseph later freed Egyptians and restored possessions and kingship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he omits many similar economic reports because they do not directly explain these verses.
12:49/q14 How did Joseph recognize the famine's first day? [* · reports]
  12:49/q14/p1 A man ate both of two food portions Joseph prepared for him. — reasons: Zayd b. Aslam's report, quoted by al-Ālūsī, says Joseph called that the first day of the hard years.
12:49/q15 Does the counselors' dismissal of the dream challenge the rule that a dream follows its first interpretation? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether calling a dream meaningless is an interpretation, and whether the rule applies to dreams with several admissible meanings.)
  12:49/q15/p1 The dismissal challenges that rule. — reasons: Al-Balkhī argues that the counselors called the king's vision confused dreams, yet that assessment did not determine its outcome (reported by al-Ālūsī).
  12:49/q15/p2 A dismissal as meaningless may not count as an interpretation. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī replies with the hadith that a dream hangs on a bird's wing until interpreted, and says the counselors' words may not have been an interpretation.
  12:49/q15/p3 The rule applies to dreams with several possible meanings, once one interpretation is made. — reasons: Ibn al-ʿArabī gives this restriction, reported by al-Ālūsī.
12:49/q16 What does the king's dream show about true dreams and their interpretation? [* · interpretation]
  12:49/q16/p1 An unbeliever can have a true dream. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this use of the passage; he also finds some associated claims opaque, including advice against seeking interpretation at sunrise, sunset, or night and a claim that a sleeping dream interprets itself.
  12:49/q16/p2 Dreams should be interpreted by qualified people. — reasons: Kuranyolu draws this lesson from Joseph's disclosed forecast and cites 12:4–6.
12:49/q17 What does the account teach about God's direction of events? [* · interpretation]
  12:49/q17/p1 God directs events beyond human understanding, including things people wrongly desire or dislike. — reasons: Asad contrasts the Qur'anic account with the Genesis account of Joseph's fortunes and invokes 2:216.
12:49/q18 How do people respond to severe hardship in the promised relief? [يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ · interpretation]
  12:49/q18/p1 They cry to God for help until their pleas are answered. — reasons: Al-Iṣlāḥī treats asking for aid as the word's literal force and says drought exposes its full effects; even extreme polytheists and disbelievers forget their imagined deities and call on the one God, as the Qur'an says.
12:49/q19 What did Joseph's guidance through the drought accomplish? [* · interpretation]
  12:49/q19/p1 It earned admiration from the king and ordinary Egyptians. — reasons: Al-Iṣlāḥī says Joseph's management of the drought won broad admiration.
12:49/q20 Who are ٱلنَّاسُ in the promised year? [ٱلنَّاسُ · referent]
  12:49/q20/p1 The people of Noah's ark, in Noah's time after Adam. — reasons: Al-Dāmghānī groups the people mentioned in 12:49 with the people of Noah's ark and specifies Noah's time after Adam.
  12:49/q20/p2 The people of Egypt. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī says the people in Joseph's appeal to return to them (12:50) and in this promise are Egyptians.
