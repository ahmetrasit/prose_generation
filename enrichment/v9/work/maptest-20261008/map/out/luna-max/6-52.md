# 6:52: 33 questions

## 6:52/q01 What request prompted the prohibition on expelling the worshippers?  [وَلَا تُطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the demand was to exclude the poor from the gathering or merely to move them in the prayer line, and how the several reports describe the leaders’ proposed arrangement.
- 6:52/q01/p1 Meccan notables asked Muhammad to remove poor, socially low-status believers from his company, promising that they would follow, meet with, or believe in him if he did. — Reports variously name Quraysh leaders, Banū ʿAbd Manāf, or al-Aqraʿ and ʿUyayna; they cite the poor companions’ status, clothing, or presence as the obstacle. Accounts name Bilāl, Ṣuhayb, ʿAmmār, Khabbāb, Salmān, Ibn Masʿūd and others. Some say the Prophet refused outright; others say he considered or briefly agreed to separate access, hoping to win the leaders, before revelation forbade it. Khabbāb’s reports add a written pledge, Gabriel’s intervention, the Prophet putting aside the document, welcoming the believers, and sitting or waiting with them. The sources also report Abu Ṭālib as intermediary and ʿUmar as suggesting a trial of the request, followed by his apology. Al-Tabari, al-Zajjāj, al-Ṭabarī, and others compare the demand with Noah’s people objecting to his low-status followers. - 6:52/q01/p2 Some accounts say the nobles asked only that poor believers be moved behind them in the prayer rows, not expelled from the Prophet’s gathering. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited for nobles taking the front places; Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes this report from the others’ request to remove believers from the gathering. ## 6:52/q02 Who are the people described as calling on their Lord?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · referent]
- 6:52/q02/p1 They are poor, socially weak Muslim believers who kept close to the Prophet; reports identify them by several overlapping lists of early Companions. — Mujāhid names Bilāl and Ibn Umm ʿAbd (also rendered Ibn Umm Maʿbad or Ibn Umm Maktūm in reports). Other reports identify six including Saʿd, Ibn Masʿūd, Ṣuhayb, ʿAmmār, al-Miqdād, and Bilāl; another list names Bilāl, Ṣuhayb, Khabbāb, ʿAmmār, Salmān, Mihja, ʿĀmir ibn Fuhayra, and Sālim. Reports also name Murthid, Subayḥ, Dhū al-Shimālayn, Wāqid, and ʿAmr, and some say Salmān and Khabbāb in particular. The sources describe them as early arrivals, listeners, students of Qurʾan and religion, people in congregational prayer, or believers whose worship was publicly evident. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others distinguish the common gathering account from the report about moving them in the prayer row. Asad extends the wording to all who believe in God and seek His grace, even if their Qurʾanic understanding is incomplete. ## 6:52/q03 What does calling on their Lord mean here?  [يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether الدعاء is specified as the obligatory prayer or taken inclusively for the worship and remembrance performed at those times; the storyteller application is explicitly rejected by some.
- 6:52/q03/p1 It refers to prayer, especially the prescribed prayers; reports specify the dawn and afternoon prayers, all five prayers, or the two daily prayers practiced in Mecca. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Ḥasan, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and others identify obligatory prayer; several specify dawn and afternoon, while another report includes all five. Muqātil says the dawn and evening prayers were then two units each, before the five were prescribed. Some reports identify congregational prayer. Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib and others reject using the verse for post-prayer storytelling, saying it refers to the prayer just completed. [48 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس,الضحاك,سعيد بن المسيب,عامر,عبد الرحمن بن أبي عمرة,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI(أبو جعفر,ابن عباس,الحسن,سعيد بن المسيب,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(إبراهيم,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن المسيب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو جعفر,إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عمر وابن عباس,الزجاج,الضحاك,مجاهد,مجاهد وقتادة,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر,ابن عمر,الحسن,الضحاك,بعض القصاص,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب ومجاهد والحسن وقتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 6:52/q03/p2 It can mean worship and supplication broadly, including remembrance, glorification, praise, Qurʾan recitation, and required or voluntary devotion; no single narrower sense is established as superior. — Al-Ṭabarī says الدعاء includes spoken remembrance, praise, obligatory and voluntary acts because God calls worship دعاء; al-Ḍaḥḥāk cites 40:43 for worship. Other reports say remembrance, sincere monotheistic supplication, reciting or learning Qurʾan, or seeking refuge. Some take the phrase as gathering for remembrance, but this is disputed. [24 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(أبو جعفر,إبراهيم,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(إبراهيم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو جعفر,إبراهيم النخعي,الزجاج,الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر,الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 6:52/q04 What do morning and evening signify?  [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the time expressions denote continuous devotion, customary time-specific worship, the two prayers named, or by synecdoche all five prayers.
- 6:52/q04/p1 They name the times of devotion, and can stand for worship throughout the day and night or for habitual, continuous remembrance. — The terms may be generic temporal expressions for all days and times; morning can run from dawn prayer to sunrise and evening to the day’s end. Some say morning represents the day and evening the night, with nighttime devotion purer; others single out busy hours to show devotion despite occupation. Al-ʿAshiyy may be singular, a synonym of ʿashiyyah, or a collective/plural, with the singular favored by some from 38:31. [13 notes: TUSTARI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; QIRAAT-ER]
- 6:52/q04/p2 The phrase specifies the two appointed prayer times, commonly dawn and afternoon; another report identifies dawn and ʿIshāʾ. — Mujāhid and Qatāda specify dawn and afternoon; reports also identify the morning prayer and evening prayer separately, with ʿAmr ibn Shuʿayb naming ʿIshāʾ. The two ends of the day may stand for all five prayers. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(عمرو بن شعيب,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عمر,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:52/q05 What readings of بالغداة are reported, and what supports them?  [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted reading and dialect/grammatical status of ghudwah: whether a proper time-name can be generic or take the article, and whether script alone establishes pronunciation.
- 6:52/q05/p1 The common reading is بالغداة (al-ghadāh), with alif and fatḥa on ghayn; most readers use it. — Abū ʿAlī prefers it because ghadāh is an indefinite noun that accepts the article, whereas ghudwah is ordinarily a proper name; Sibawayh, Yūnus, al-Farrāʾ and other grammarians are cited for forms and declension. Some argue the written wāw proves nothing, as in ṣalāh and zakāh. One distinction makes ghadāh a general morning and ghudwah an instance or specific morning. [23 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,ابن عامر); TABRISI(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي,سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيد,ابن عامر,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيد,أبو علي الفارسي,الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); QIRAAT-ER; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:52/q05/p2 Ibn ʿĀmir reads بالغدوة (al-ghudwah), with wāw and ḍamma on ghayn; Ibn Abī ʿAbla reads plural بالغدوات والعشيات, and other variant forms are reported. — Ibn ʿĀmir’s reading is transmitted, also in al-Kahf. Al-Khalīl permits ghudwah as a temporal adverb like ḍuḥwah and grammarians report dialects in which it is generic or indefinite and can take the article. Several authors reject Abū ʿUbayd’s charge that it merely follows spelling, since recitation rests on oral transmission and reputable readers are not presumed mistaken. Ibn Abī ʿAbla has plural forms; another report has بالغدو with doubled wāw and no tāʾ. [28 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,ابن عامر); JISHUMI(ابن أبي عبلة,ابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عامر,الخليل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو زيد,ابن عامر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر النحاس,أبو عبيد,أبو علي الفارسي,الفراء,سيبويه والخليل,مكي بن أبي طالب); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيد)-]

## 6:52/q06 What does seeking His face mean?  [يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ‘face’ denotes reward, pleasure, obedience or the commanded path, the divine Essence itself, or a literal face; the notes also dispute whether it can be willed in strict theology.
- 6:52/q06/p1 It means sincerity in seeking God’s pleasure, reward, approval, or obedience, with no worldly aim or association. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it as reward; other explanations say obedience, the commanded path, or God’s pleasure. Al-Zajjāj treats it as evidence of sincere intention. Many authors say face is a metonym for God’s essence or reality, and the clause attests to pure devotion rather than worldly advantage. Rāzī also explains it through love and desire for the beloved’s face; Qushayrī describes longing that robs the seeker of rest. The phrase is sometimes read as a circumstantial qualification of their invocation. [32 notes: TUSTARI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الزجاج,المشهور,بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(IK, R,R)]
- 6:52/q06/p2 It means seeking God Himself or His Essence, with ‘face’ understood as the divine face rather than a metaphor for reward or obedience. — Some reports say the worshippers seek God alone; the common interpretation takes face as His essence. Qushayrī says this claim is witnessed by God, while Qashānī interprets the sought object as the Essence beyond attributes and intermediaries. Rāzī rejects anthropomorphic inference from the phrase by appeal to divine oneness. Al-Tabāṭabāʾī denies bodily face or material direction and treats ‘face’ as the aspect through which God is encountered, including names, attributes, religion, righteous works, and means of nearness. Study Quran records a literal hope of seeing God’s Face. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(المجسمة); QASHANI; QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL(المشهور); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 6:52/q07 How does يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُ relate grammatically to the invocation?  [يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the implicit subject of the circumstantial phrase is the callers or those called upon; Samīn prefers the callers.
- 6:52/q07/p1 The phrase is circumstantial (ḥāl), most naturally describing the callers as seeking God’s face through their invocation. — Qushayrī calls the phrase circumstantial; Abū Ḥayyān and others also treat it as ḥāl. Samīn al-Ḥalabī reports a subject or object analysis for the omitted subject relation, preferring the subject: ‘they seek His face by their supplication at these times.’ [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 6:52/q08 What does ḥisāb mean in the mutual-accountability clauses?  [مِنْ حِسَابِهِم مِنْ حِسَابِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ḥisāb denotes moral accounting for deeds or provision/sufficiency; the provision reading is supported by lexical usage and parallel verses.
- 6:52/q08/p1 It is the reckoning or judgment of deeds: neither party answers for the other, and God alone judges each person. — Al-Ḥasan and the majority take the account as deeds; some say reward and punishment. The wording is related to the principle that no bearer bears another’s burden and to Noah’s statement that their reckoning belongs to God. In this reading the Prophet judges outward signs and leaves hidden faith to God; he is not responsible for the believers’ inner sincerity or the polytheists’ refusal. Al-Tabāṭabāʾī traces ḥisāb from numerical reckoning to measuring deeds for recompense. [24 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(one view); STUDYQURAN(R); QASHANI]
- 6:52/q08/p2 It means provision or sufficiency: neither the Prophet nor the poor believers is responsible for maintaining the other, since God provides for both. — Ibn Baḥr and other interpreters take the account as provision or sufficiency; al-Ṭabarī reports this sense. Al-Biqāʿī adduces 78:36, 2:212, lexicons, the verb for feeding someone until satisfied, and a report about a host’s sufficiency. The point is that poverty is no reason to tire of or expel the worshippers. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); BIQAI-FULL(أبو عبيد الهروي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(another interpretation)]

## 6:52/q09 Who do the pronouns in the accountability clauses refer to?  [حِسَابِهِم حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِم · referent]
turns on: Whether the first two pronouns refer to the poor worshippers or to the polytheists, and whether the final object refers to the believers; the apparent reading keeps the pronouns with the worshippers.
- 6:52/q09/p1 The pronouns refer to the poor worshippers throughout: their account is not the Prophet’s, and his account is not theirs. — This is the apparent and predominant reading for al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and many others; it fits the explicit object of expelling them and the nearby pronouns. Some construe the account as deeds, some as provision. The clause can reassure the Prophet that accusations about the believers do not bind him and that each bears their own account. [20 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(المهدوي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); SAMIN-DURR(الطبري); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد,عطاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(ağırlıklı görüş)+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; STUDYQURAN(Z)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 6:52/q09/p2 The first pronouns refer to the polytheists, while the pronoun in ‘then you would expel them’ refers to the poor believers; in one parsing the clauses are parenthetical. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited for the Prophet and polytheists not answering for one another; al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and others allow the first clauses to refer to the requesters, so conversion concern cannot prompt expulsion of the poor. Some readings refer both ‘their account’ and ‘upon them’ to polytheists, while another makes the first two pronouns polytheists and the final object the believers. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري وابن عطية,الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(some interpreters); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)+]

## 6:52/q10 Why are the two accountability clauses paired and ordered this way?  [مَا عَلَيْكَ مِنْ حِسَابِهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ وَمَا مِنْ حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second clause is required for reciprocity or is emphatic, and whether fronting marks honor, emphasis, or restriction.
- 6:52/q10/p1 The reciprocal clause completes and emphasizes the point that neither party is responsible for the other; the Prophet is named first in both clauses to honor him and to close off any rationale for yielding to the chiefs. — Al-Zajjāj says the repetition confirms parallel phrasing though the second could be omitted. Rāzī says both clauses are needed for reciprocity; al-Zamakhsharī compares them with ‘no bearer bears another’s burden.’ Other authors describe reciprocal framing (رد العجز على الصدر), completion of the rationale, emphasis, or five accumulated emphases, including min, fronting, restrictive wording, and reciprocal negation. Some say the first clause receives priority because its application to the Prophet and requesters is central. [23 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); BURSEVI(أبو السعود); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:52/q11 How are the accountability clauses grammatically parsed?  [مَا عَلَيْكَ مِنْ حِسَابِهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ وَمَا مِنْ حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic role of من حسابهم and من شيء, and whether min is partitive, circumstantial, emphatic, or explanatory; the second clause constrains the possible fronting analysis.
- 6:52/q11/p1 The first min is partitive or circumstantial and the second min is an added/emphatic marker under negation; the clauses have several analyses for the predicate and fronted phrases. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the first min is partitive and the second emphatic. Abū Ḥayyān analyzes min ḥisābihim as a partitive ḥāl modifying shayʾ, dependent on the implicit ‘being’ in ʿalayka; alternatively shayʾ is a subject raised by ʿalayka under negation, with min ḥisābihim circumstantial, or the predicate with ʿalayka circumstantial. Samīn lists ما as governing an accusative predicate or being inactive with a fronted predicate and subject. In the parallel clause, min ḥisābika cannot readily be a ḥāl advanced before its governor and possessor. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats min as emphatic and عَلَيْكَ as fronted predicate, with negation and indefiniteness licensing the order. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:52/q12 How do فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ and فَتَكُونَ relate to the preceding clauses?  [فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ فَتَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the subjunctive clauses answer the negation and prohibition separately, or are coordinated as expulsion and its consequence; some readings treat the intervening account clauses as parenthetical.
- 6:52/q12/p1 فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ answers the negation about mutual accounting, while فَتَكُونَ answers the prohibition against expelling them. — Al-Akhfash, al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭabarīs, and others parse the two subjunctives respectively as the result of the negation and the prohibition. Some treat the account clauses as parenthetical between the ban and its consequence; Abu Muslim links both fa-verbs to the prohibition. [28 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو حيان,مكي والواحدي وأبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(الدر المصون,عطاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو السعود)]
- 6:52/q12/p2 The two fa-verbs are coordinated as the denied expulsion and its consequence: expelling them would make the Prophet unjust. — Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn prefer coordination; al-Zamakhsharī also allows it, and the injustice follows from the expulsion itself. Al-ʿĀlūsī argues that injustice ceases when expulsion is negated and rejects a reading in which the second negation would make expulsion proper if the Prophet were accountable for them. Another parsing makes the final clause the result of the prohibition, with the repeated expulsion as a resumptive link. [11 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,مكي والواحدي وأبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام والزمخشري,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 6:52/q13 What makes expelling the worshippers an act of injustice?  [مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the threatened injustice is directed at the believers or at the Prophet himself; the sources also gloss it as misplacing or diminishing what is due.
- 6:52/q13/p1 It would wrong the believers, who deserve welcome and honor, by putting people or things in the wrong place. — Ibn Zayd says they did not deserve expulsion; al-Ṭabarīs and others say it harms the believers. The poor are entitled to company and rights, and the Qurʾanic prohibition answers social contempt, poverty, or rank. ظلم can mean diminution in Arabic and Qurʾanic usage; some say it is wrongdoing by misplacement, while others include wronging oneself or being characterized by injustice. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد)+]

## 6:52/q14 What does the verse teach about status and keeping company with the worshippers?  [وَلَا تُطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · interpretation]
- 6:52/q14/p1 The Prophet must keep the sincere worshippers near, honor them, and not privilege wealthy or powerful people over them for status, clothing, or hoped-for converts. — The verse prioritizes religion and sincerity over worldly rank. Sources say the Prophet should look past ragged appearance, judge outward piety rather than hidden motives, and welcome the poor; it also forbids humiliating those valued by God and teaches that human worth rests on faith and reverence, not wealth, lineage, or social position. Some read the instruction as a universal ethical rule for Qurʾan followers, including patient teaching of people with incomplete understanding. [32 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري,أنس,عائذ بن عمرو); QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 6:52/q15 Did the Prophet actually expel the believers, and does the warning imply a sin?  [فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ فَتَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the warning describes an act that occurred or a contemplated/hypothetical action, and whether ‘wrongdoing’ means sin or leaving the more fitting course.
- 6:52/q15/p1 The verse warns against a contemplated or hypothetical expulsion; it does not establish that the Prophet actually expelled them or sinned. — Reports say he refused outright or considered temporary separation to attract Quraysh, intending good rather than contempt for the poor. Al-Qurṭubī calls the warning instruction for others, like a conditional warning against shirk; al-Rāzī says any mistaken judgment was forgiven and that wrongdoing means leaving the better course, not violating a duty. Al-Ālūsī similarly says the verse does not prove actual expulsion. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the warning with a conditional address about associationism. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); TABRISI(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(عبدالله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي)-]
- 6:52/q15/p2 Critics cite the incident and the warning as evidence that the Prophet committed a sin by expelling or preparing to expel the poor; Rāzī answers that the contemplated action was not contempt and was an excusable judgment. — The critics invoke the prohibition, the threat of being among wrongdoers, Noah’s refusal to expel believers, and other passages. The source reports Rāzī’s response and explains that the warning concerns what was more fitting. Al-Qurṭubī explicitly rejects reading the verse as proof of an actual expulsion. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الطاعنون في عصمة الأنبياء); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); QURTUBI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 6:52/q16 How do sources assess the reported occasion and its chronology?  [وَلَا تُطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ · reports]
turns on: Whether later reports identify the original Meccan occasion or a later analogous request, and whether occasion reports are reliable and exclusive.
- 6:52/q16/p1 Reports connect the verse with the Meccan demand to remove poor believers, but some commentators doubt that the named later visitors were the original cause because the sūrah is Meccan and was revealed as a whole. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn Kathīr call the report involving al-Aqraʿ and ʿUyayna strange or doubtful because those men became prominent later in Medina. Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise questions the Ibn Mājah attribution; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a similar later request answered by the earlier verse. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats similar stories as applications or repeated proposals rather than exclusive occasions, given the sūrah’s single-period Meccan descent. Some commentators deny the incident as occasion while retaining its universal ethical teaching. [14 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية,ابن ماجة); TABATABAI(عمر بن عبد الله بن المهاجر); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirlerin çoğu)+; SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن كثير)+; TABATABAI+]

## 6:52/q17 How does this verse connect with neighboring verses and other passages?  [* · links]
- 6:52/q17/p1 The command continues the warning in 6:51 by requiring the Prophet to remain with those who fear resurrection, and it anticipates 6:53–55 and the test of the wealthy through the poor. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the command to 6:51 and its renewed description of callers to God; other notes relate the next verse’s question about divine favor to the believers’ precedence and the test between classes. Reports connect 18:28 with staying patiently among the worshippers, and 80:1–10 is cited as a related incident. The sources also compare Noah’s people demanding the expulsion of low-status followers (11:27–29; 26:111–114), the no-bearer-bears-another principle (6:164; 17:15; 35:18; 39:7; 53:38), and other cited verses on prayer, provision, and divine face. [34 notes: ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, W); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Jesus (sws)); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(عبد الله بن مسعود)]

## 6:52/q18 What inward or spiritual readings do the sources give?  [* · inward]
- 6:52/q18/p1 The verse describes continual remembrance, sincere seeking, and the seeker’s movement toward God; some distinguish the striving seeker from the one whom God enables and raises through stations. — Al-Tustarī says seeking God’s face is seeking approval and never being absent from Him; every movement and stillness may be directed to God. He distinguishes the seeker, who exerts intention and effort, from the one sought, who receives enabling and rises through stations, citing the angels’ known stations. Al-Sulamī describes continual remembrance, pure inner secret, and seekers adorned with servanthood. Al-Qushayrī portrays longing and divine care; al-Qāshānī reads the callers as people of unity absorbed in presence, love, and the Essence, beyond reward, fear, or intermediaries. Bursawī adds that continual remembrance keeps one with God and that sincere intention gives worship its value. Other Tustarī aphorisms praise pure food, obedience, sincerity toward creatures, and a life not squandered. [37 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو بكر الفارسي,أبو عثمان,النهرجوري,بعضهم); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(السري السقطي,النبي,بهلول)]

## 6:52/q19 What does طرد mean in the prohibition?  [تَطْرُدِ · meaning]
- 6:52/q19/p1 It means to drive away, banish, exclude, or remove, with an implication of humiliating disregard in some usage. — Jishumī glosses ṭard as banishment, exclusion, or expulsion; Abū Ḥayyān says it includes expulsion accompanied by humiliation and notes ṭarīd for an expelled person. Islahi says the verb is harsh and matches the disparaging treatment Quraysh wanted. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:52/q20 What readings or theological claims about the face are rejected?  [وَجْهَهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether ‘face’ can be taken literally or must be understood without bodily composition or material direction.
- 6:52/q20/p1 The phrase does not establish a bodily part or material direction in God; face can be an honorific or metaphorical expression, and divine oneness rules out composition. — Rāzī answers anthropomorphists by citing God’s oneness in 112:1. Abū Ḥayyān rejects using the phrase as proof of a bodily member. Al-Ālūsī notes the theological problem in willing an eternal essence; al-Tabāṭabāʾī denies bodily face or material direction and explains the face as a relational aspect through which God is encountered. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(المجسمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(المشهور); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)-]

## 6:52/q21 How do sources explain the test mentioned in the continuation?  [* · links]
- 6:52/q21/p1 God tests people through one another by giving poor believers precedence in faith, which challenges the pride of wealthy leaders who do not want to be preceded by them. — Qatāda says God tried them through one another. Al-Ṭabarīs explain that the test may mean God treats people as a tester would, or the difficult obligation on Arab nobles to believe and honor earlier believers; the question about God’s favor is confirming. Bursawī says the poor have religious precedence despite worldly poverty, and Elmalılı describes the challenge to worldly pride and status. [8 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TABRISI(الجبائي); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); THALABI]

## 6:52/q22 What other theological issue appears in the surrounding notes?  [* · theology]
- 6:52/q22/p1 A report about the community’s ‘Magians’ is read as condemning denial of God’s relation to human acts; the Muʿtazilite counter-reading targets those who affirm decree, and a combined manuscript wording is rejected as corrupt. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī interprets the report as opposing independent human creation of acts, compares it with Zoroastrian dualism, records the Muʿtazilite reply, and rejects a manuscript reading that combines denial of decree with denial of human will because the claims contradict one another. [4 notes: TABATABAI(المعتزلة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 6:52/q23 What report is given about the poor Companions’ rank and the Prophet’s conduct toward them?  [وَلَا تُطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ · reports]
- 6:52/q23/p1 Reports say the Prophet sat close with the poor worshippers, waited for them to rise, and promised or affirmed their honor and divine care. — Khabbāb’s accounts say the Prophet welcomed the believers after revelation, sat knee-to-knee or close to them, and waited so as not to hurt them; the later command in 18:28 is linked to remaining with them. Anas reports the assembly as one of prophets and righteous people; a report from Abū Saʿīd says poor migrants listening to Qurʾan would enter Paradise five hundred years before wealthy believers. Another report warns that upsetting Salmān, Ṣuhayb, and Bilāl upsets God, and they forgave Abū Bakr. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(خباب,خباب بن الأرت); THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري,أنس,عائذ بن عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(النبي); RAZI-FULL(سلمان وخباب); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); ALUSI-FULL(خباب); ELMALILI(Selmân ve Habbâb); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Habbâb); IBNKATHIR-FULL(خباب)]

## 6:52/q24 How does the accountability clause answer accusations of hidden insincerity?  [مَا عَلَيْكَ مِنْ حِسَابِهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ وَمَا مِنْ حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause answers doubts about inner faith or concerns accountability for deeds or provision.
- 6:52/q24/p1 The Prophet judges outward conduct and leaves hidden motives and each person’s deeds to God; accusations that the poor seek food, clothing, or worldly support do not justify expelling them. — Abū Ḥayyān rejects the hypothetical that their inward state is blameworthy because God has testified to their sincerity. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects an unsupported accusation of hypocrisy; Study Quran reports the material-motive allegation and says the Prophet need not assess hidden motives. Ibn Zayd and others also link the clause with provision, while the reciprocal-account reading leaves each person answerable for themselves. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators); TABRISI]

## 6:52/q25 What alternative parses connect the result clauses to the prohibition?  [فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ فَتَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fa-clauses attach to the prohibition or the preceding negation, and whether the account clauses are parenthetical.
- 6:52/q25/p1 Both result verbs may be linked to the prohibition, with the intervening account clauses treated as parenthetical; alternatively the second verb coordinates with the first as its consequence. — Al-Akhfash reports the prohibitive parse; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn favor coordination for فتكون. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the first fa-clause may explain or invalidate the proposed rationale for expulsion. Samīn says the clauses can be parenthetical regardless of the referent of the pronouns. [6 notes: AKHFASH; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 6:52/q26 How is wajh used in Arabic, and what poetic image supports sincerity?  [وَجْهَهُۥ · meaning]
- 6:52/q26/p1 Wajh can denote the bodily face or a thing’s essence or aspect; sincere worship is likened to a grain with no kernel when intention is absent. — Jishumī notes the familiar bodily sense and the broader sense of essence or aspect, as in ‘the face of an opinion.’ Bursawī cites a Persian couplet comparing worship without sincerity to a grain without its kernel. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI]

## 6:52/q27 What does calling on their Lord mean here?  [يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · interpretation]
- 6:52/q27/p1 Calling on God includes remembrance and continual devotion, and the public invocation professes faith in Him alone. — Al-Nasafī includes continuous worship alongside dawn and afternoon or all five prayers. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the invocation publicly professes faith and implies conviction; other sources include remembrance, Qurʾan, prayer, and worship. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:52/q28 What do morning and evening signify?  [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ · interpretation]
- 6:52/q28/p1 The terms are derived from time-names and designate the periods of morning and evening devotion. — Al-Ālūsī derives al-ghadāh from ghudwah by changing wāw to alif and al-ʿashiyy from ashwa by changing wāw to yāʾ and assimilating; he notes that some treat the latter as plural. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:52/q29 What makes expelling the worshippers an act of injustice?  [مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · interpretation]
- 6:52/q29/p1 Expulsion would wrong the believers and threaten wrongdoing because the Prophet had considered giving the leaders precedence over the weak. — Ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd says expelling the believers would itself make the Prophet one of the wrongdoers. Ibn al-Anbārī connects the severe warning to the Prophet’s consideration of giving wealthy leaders precedence over the weak. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)]

## 6:52/q30 What does the verse teach about status and keeping company with the worshippers?  [وَلَا تُطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · interpretation]
- 6:52/q30/p1 The devout should be drawn near and honored; the Prophet’s address indirectly rebukes the arrogant requesters and exposes their status-consciousness. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the verse turns from warning the heedless to honoring the devout. Islahi says addressing Muhammad rather than Quraysh strikes at their arrogance and implies that they are unworthy of direct address. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:52/q31 How does this verse connect with neighboring verses and other passages?  [* · links]
- 6:52/q31/p1 The surrounding context describes believers as grateful and the poor worshippers as the objects of the demand to expel; similar demands are associated with Noah’s community. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the context indicates Meccan polytheists asked for the poor believers’ expulsion and compares it with the elites of Noah’s community. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the renewed description to the following rationale and related passages. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:52/q32 What inward or spiritual readings do the sources give?  [* · inward]
- 6:52/q32/p1 Sincere intention gives worship its value and is the basis for seeking God’s pleasure. — Bursawī’s couplet says worship is good when intention is sincere and compares worship without it to a grain without a kernel. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 6:52/q33 What request prompted the prohibition on expelling the worshippers?  [وَلَا تُطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ · reports]
- 6:52/q33/p1 The Prophet’s closeness to the poor believers after revelation affirmed his commitment to remain with them. — Al-Baghawī reports the Prophet saying he was grateful to have lived to receive the command to remain patient with members of his community, and that his life and death were with them. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]
