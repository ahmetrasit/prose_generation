# 12:49: 12 questions

## 12:49/q01 What does ذَٰلِكَ refer to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether ذَٰلِكَ points back to the seven years or to the drought; the seven-year explanation also accounts for the masculine form.
- 12:49/q01/p1 The seven years just mentioned. — Al-Kalbī takes it as the seven years, noting that سبع has no feminine marker; Ibn al-Qāsim likewise explains the masculine form by the number's lack of a feminine marker, and al-Kalbī cites a parallel in which masculine grammar follows a feminine noun without that marker. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن القاسم,الكلبي)]
- 12:49/q01/p2 The drought. — Muqātil explicitly refers ذَٰلِكَ to the drought; Ibn al-Jawzī also reports this explanation. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 12:49/q02 What nuance does عَامٌۭ convey here?  [عَامٌۭ · meaning]
- 12:49/q02/p1 A full year marked by ease and fertility after the famine years. — Al-Biqāʿī says ʿām signals a complete twelve-month period whose coming relief is known and controlled by God. Al-Ālūsī contrasts عام with سنة: both mean year, but عام often suggests ease and fertility, while سنة often suggests hardship and famine, so the positive term signals changed conditions. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:49/q03 What does يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ mean in this year?  [يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ · meaning]
turns on: The derivation and contextual force: ghayth can mean rain, while ghawth means aid or deliverance; a further gloss takes the result as abundant vegetation or produce.
- 12:49/q03/p1 The people receive rain. — Ibn Qutayba says yughāthu means that people receive rain and ghayth is rain; Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, and most exegetes also gloss the year as rain. Ibn al-Sikkīt describes rain falling on the land. Al-Ṭabarī says rainfall brings relief. Al-Zajjāj and other commentators relate the rain sense to water poured from clouds in 78:14. Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes the related forms for aid, relief, and rainfall; al-Sāmin and al-Ālūsī explain the triliteral derivation from ghayth, while al-Abū Ḥayyān contrasts that root with the quadriliteral relief derivation. A Bedouin woman's phrase غثنا ما شئنا means that they got whatever rain they wished. Al-Biqāʿī and Bursevī explain the alternative root forms and the alif's relation to the root vowel. Rain also fits the following pressing of crops: al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Durr, and others connect it with renewed fertility. Ibn al-Jawzī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's rain gloss, and al-Thaʿlabī reports rain or relief. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the next phrase about pressing makes rain more apt, rejects the objection that Egyptian fertility depends on the Nile because its floodwaters are supplied by Sudanese rain, and cites the lexicon's sense of rain-grown vegetation. Asad notes Egyptian crops depend on Nile floods fed by upstream rain. Al-Islāḥī reports the rain interpretation, and the Kuranyolu commentary describes abundant rain and harvest. [47 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عيسى بن عمر,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن السكيت); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير,ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور,الجوهري); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,جعفر الصادق,علي بن معمر,علي بن معمر عن أبيه عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام,لسان العرب); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 12:49/q03/p2 The people are succored or relieved from distress. — Al-Wāḥidī reports the alternative that the people are rescued from famine. Al-Ṭabrisī defines ghawth as urgent help that removes harm; al-Ālūsī derives the phrase from the quadriliteral form of غوث, God succoring them by lifting hardship. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Abū Ḥayyān, al-Sāmin, Bursevī, Asad, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report relief or rescue alongside rain. Al-Islāḥī prefers the literal sense of seeking help: people cry out to God until their pleas are answered, with rain a consequential result rather than the word's literal meaning. The cited distinction is between ghayth, rain, and ghawth, aid or deliverance. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Thaʿlabī also report relief as an interpretation. [24 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن السكيت); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL; THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; TABATABAI-]
- 12:49/q03/p3 The people receive abundant produce or rain-grown vegetation. — Ibn ʿĪsā, as cited by al-Māwardī, takes the phrase as people receiving abundant produce. Ibn al-Jawzī reports al-Māwardī's relief-through-abundance gloss. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes that ghayth can mean vegetation grown from rainwater, which suits the following mention of pressing. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); TABATABAI(لسان العرب)]

## 12:49/q04 What does يَعْصِرُونَ mean?  [يَعْصِرُونَ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical derivation and vocalization: pressing produce is the majority gloss; reports also take the word as milking, refuge or rescue, rain received through clouds, obtaining desires, or prosperity and gifts.
- 12:49/q04/p1 They press grapes, olives, sesame, and other produce for juice, wine, oil, or similar liquids. — Ibn Qutayba, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, and most exegetes explain the verb as pressing produce. Reports list grapes, olives, sesame, sugarcane, radish, fruits, water, oils, wine, juice, and other extractable liquids; al-Naqqāsh's reading is described as squeezing moisture. Al-Ṭabarī says this is the unanimous interpretation of Companions and Successors and rejects the refuge derivation; another report says nothing could be pressed in drought years, and some say they had not pressed oil or wine for fourteen years. Al-Wāḥidī says abundance made pressing possible after famine. Al-Zamakhsharī also reports an active sense of people rescuing themselves, but gives pressing as a principal sense. Al-Qurṭubī records Abū al-Ghawth's exploiting produce gloss from pressing grapes and the idiom of extracting wealth from someone's hand. Al-Biqāʿī says pressing extracts juices and essences and marks the transition from drought to fertility. Al-Ālūsī says explicit mention suits crops not ensured by rain as directly as grain, or the king's circumstances; al-Islāḥī notes the grape harvest and wine-server. Ibn Kathīr says people press their usual products, including oil and sweet syrup. [46 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); FARISI-HUJJA; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور,النقاش); TABRISI(ابن عباس، مجاهد، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس وقتادة والجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الغوث,ابن جريج,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(النقاش); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R); SAMIN-DURR+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)+; TAB-FULL-]
- 12:49/q04/p2 They milk livestock, understood as squeezing the udders and a sign of abundant pasture. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to gloss the verb as milking; al-Wālibī transmits this from him, and Aḥmad ibn ʿUbayd links it to abundant livestock and fertile years. Al-Māwardī reports bringing livestock from abundant pasture. Al-Ālūsī explains milking as pressing the udder to release milk. Other reports say pressing includes milking. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طلحة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وقتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 12:49/q04/p3 They are delivered from famine or take refuge from distress. — Abū ʿUbayda and others derive the sense from ʿaṣra/ʿuṣra, refuge or relief; Abū Isḥāq agrees. Arabic poetry by Labīd, Abū Zubayd, ʿAdī ibn Zayd, Ibn Muqbil, Ibn Aḥmar, and other poets uses related forms for rescue, relief, or refuge, and Zajjāj notes that a person may be in ʿaṣr inside a stronghold beyond reach. Al-Ṭabarī rejects this derivation, saying poetry cannot outweigh the unanimous Companion and Successor interpretation and that a transmitted reading conflicts with Arabic and Ibn ʿAbbās's usage. Al-Ṭabrisī cites thirst and reliance on water; al-Ālūsī cites Abū Zubayd's refuge image. The rescue sense also appears as a passive reading elsewhere. [34 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة,شاعر); ZAJJAJ(عدي بن زيد); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس,عيسى والأعرج وجعفر بن محمد); THALABI(أبو عبيدة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة والزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبيدة وغيره,الطبري); TABRISI(ابن مقبل، أبو زبيد الطائي، أبو عبيدة، لبيد,علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة وغيره,جعفر بن محمد، والأعرج، وعيسى البصرة); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR+; TAB-FULL-]
- 12:49/q04/p4 They receive rain, as if a cloud is squeezed or releases its water over them. — Al-Zajjāj records a passive yuʿṣarūn meaning that they receive rain; Ibn ʿUmar's recitation is glossed as being given rain. Al-Naqqāsh and other reports connect the cloud image with the rain-bearing clouds of 78:14; al-Mustanīr explains the action as figuratively attributed to the people, and al-Jawharī says ʿaṣara al-qawmu means a people received rain without a verbal supplement. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Bayḍāwī report this reading and analogy. ʿAlī rejects the active pressing interpretation in this context and directs the reader to a form meaning they are rained upon, with 78:14 as evidence. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عيسى بن عمر); MAWARDI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر الثقفي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعرج وعيسى وجعفر بن محمد); TABRISI(علي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عيسى); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن المستنير); SAMIN-DURR(قطرب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير,الجوهري); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام,جعفر الصادق,علي بن معمر,علي بن معمر عن أبيه عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 12:49/q04/p5 They obtain what they desire. — Abū ʿUbayd reports this sense, supported by Ibn Aḥmar's verse about taking from life's branches; Ibn al-Jawzī also records it. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة عن ابن أحمر)]
- 12:49/q04/p6 They prosper, are treated well, or are given abundant gifts and produce. — Al-Māwardī cites poetic usage يعصر فينا for treating people well. A grammarian cited by Ibn al-Anbārī glosses the verb as giving generously and granting gifts; al-Azharī reports this from Abū ʿUbayd and Abū ʿUbayda. Ibn al-Anbārī also reports abundant produce as making people's lives prosperous. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)]

## 12:49/q05 How is يَعْصِرُونَ read?  [يَعْصِرُونَ · readings]
turns on: The reading traditions differ in person (third or second), voice (active or passive), and form (including doubled or assimilated consonants); the second-person form is tied to the surrounding address.
- 12:49/q05/p1 Active third-person plural يَعْصِرُونَ, with yāʾ, referring to the people previously mentioned. — The standard regional reading uses yāʾ in the third person; Ibn Khalawayh says its subject is al-nās. Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jushamī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Sāmin, al-Ṭabrisī, and Qiraat-ER list the readers who use this form. It refers to the people already mentioned. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(أهل الكوفة); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخوان); SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI; QIRAAT-ER; ZAJJAJ]
- 12:49/q05/p2 Active second-person plural تَعْصِرُونَ, addressing the interlocutors. — Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī use tāʾ; the address continues the surrounding direct speech and can single out the questioners, the king's questioner, or the people addressed. Al-Fārisī notes its parallel with قدمتم; Ibn Khalawayh says the form distinguishes addressees from other people. Several reports say the other readers use yāʾ for the people. Zajjāj also allows this form to mean pressing or escaping distress. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(أهل الكوفة); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخوان); SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عيسى بن عبيد الثقفي); QIRAAT-ER]
- 12:49/q05/p3 Passive third-person yuʿṣarūn, meaning they are rained upon or delivered. — ʿĪsā, al-Aʿraj, Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Ibn ʿUmar, and other transmitters report passive yāʾ forms. ## 12:49/q06 Why is فِيهِ repeated?  [فِيهِ يَغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ وَفِيهِ يَعْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Al-Ālūsī reports several possible rhetorical effects: differentiation, enumeration or emphasis, focus, and rhyme.
- 12:49/q06/p1 It separates God's acts of rain and relief from the people's act of pressing. — Bursevī says the repeated فيه distinguishes rain and relief, acts of God, from pressing, which people perform. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 12:49/q06/p2 It enumerates distinct times or kinds of benefit and the year's blessings. — Al-Ālūsī says the repetition may signal different times or kinds of benefit and enumerate the year's blessings. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/q06/p3 It emphasizes that this year's rain is much greater than the negligible rain during the preceding drought. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as another explanation for the repetition. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/q06/p4 The fronted phrase marks the focus, and the second occurrence may also serve the rhyme. — Al-Ālūsī notes focus from fronting and allows rhyme as a reason for the second occurrence. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:49/q07 Was the final year's detailed forecast part of the king's dream, and how did Joseph know it?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the dream only implied the end of hardship or supplied the specific forecast; most cited authorities attribute the unasked-for detail to divine disclosure, while some allow inference from the dream or the expected cycle.
- 12:49/q07/p1 The details were not in the dream; God disclosed the additional year's unseen abundance to Joseph, often identified as revelation. — Muqātil says the statement is Joseph's, not part of the dream. Qatāda repeatedly says God gave Joseph knowledge of an unasked-for additional year. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Durr, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, al-Manār as cited by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Kuranyolu say the extra detail was disclosed by God or revelation. Al-Kashshāf says the dream implied some fertility after famine but not this detailed promise. Al-Rāzī distinguishes the dream's shift from drought to fertility from the detailed relief and pressing forecast. Abū Ḥayyān says revelation specified the eighth year's abundance, already expected after seven severe years. Al-Ālūsī rejects inference from natural cycles or God's custom because the prophecy would be vague, especially where mutual relief is described. Al-Wāḥidī says the unasked-for prediction supports direct revelation; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the specificity as requiring revelation. [31 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة وابن عباس); TABATABAI(صاحب المنار); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+]
- 12:49/q07/p2 The dream implied hardship would end and fertility return, so the final abundance could be inferred rather than known as new revelation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the dream's interpretation itself implies hardship ends after seven years, followed by abundance. Al-Bayḍāwī allows revelation, inference from the expected end of drought, or God's custom of relief after hardship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports al-Bayḍāwī's alternatives and says the final year can be inferred from the repeated seven, since after seven hard years people need not exert themselves to manage sowing and reserves. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI(البيضاوي); NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; KASHSHAF-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-; TABATABAI(صاحب المنار)-]

## 12:49/q08 How does this forecast complete the king's dream and Joseph's reply?  [* · links]
turns on: The common explanation treats the dream as seven fertile years followed by seven famine years and this as the following good year; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī instead reads Joseph's words as survival policy rather than that dream forecast.
- 12:49/q08/p1 After seven fertile and seven famine years, an eighth year brings rain, renewed fertility, and pressing produce, completing the agricultural cycle and ending with good news. — The cow and grain symbols are explained as fertile and famine years; stored abundance is consumed during famine. The final year follows those fourteen years and brings rain, flourishing crops, and ordinary production. Al-Bayḍāwī says fat cows and green ears mean fertility, lean cows and dry ears famine, and the fat cows' being swallowed means stored abundance will be consumed. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the promise brings comfort and hope after the discouraging forecast. Abū Ḥayyān says Joseph combines interpretation, practical advice, and a report of the unseen future; al-Ālūsī says ending with glad tidings highlights his excellence. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the king's dream and Joseph's interpretation as seven plentiful years, seven famine years, then rain; he says rain, fodder, or milking fits the fat and lean cows. StudyQuran calls it a fifteen-year agricultural cycle. Al-Islāḥī adds answered pleas, abundant rain, and a large grape harvest, and says grape pressing subtly suits the king's wine-server. ## 12:49/q09 What does the promise teach about relief and Joseph's role?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:49/q09/p1 Hardship can turn people toward God, and God's direction can bring ease after adversity; Joseph's guidance helps Egypt through the famine and gives the people hope. — Al-Islāḥī says people cry out to God in the extraordinary year, and hardship drives even extreme polytheists and disbelievers to call on the one God and forget imagined deities. He says Joseph guided Egypt through drought and won admiration from the king to ordinary people. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees comfort and hope in the promise and reflects God's pattern of ease after hardship. Asad says the Joseph story illustrates God's unfathomable direction of human affairs: people may hate what is good for them or love what is bad for them, as in 2:216. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 12:49/q10 What reports describe Joseph's preparations or conduct during the famine?  [* · reports]
- 12:49/q10/p1 Joseph tested the onset of famine by giving each man two portions; when one man ate both, Joseph identified it as the first day of the hard years. — Zayd ibn Aslam reports this test and Joseph's statement identifying the first hard day. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]
- 12:49/q10/p2 Joseph stored harvests and sold food progressively, then freed the Egyptians and restored their possessions and kingship. — A report from al-Riḍā describes staged sales for money, valuables, livestock, servants, property, and land, then people's freedom, followed by Joseph freeing the Egyptians and restoring their possessions and kingship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he omits many similar famine-economy reports because most do not directly serve interpretation of these verses. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام)]

## 12:49/q11 What does this passage establish about interpreting dreams?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether a dream's fulfillment depends on its first interpretation, and how broadly the related reports apply.
- 12:49/q11/p1 A dream may come to pass according to its first interpretation, though some restrict this rule to visions with several possible meanings. — Al-Balkhī uses the counselors' dismissal of the king's dream as confused dreams to challenge the rule; al-Ālūsī replies that dismissing it as meaningless may not count as an interpretation and cites the report that a dream hangs on a bird's wing until interpreted. Ibn al-ʿArabī restricts the rule to visions admitting several possible meanings, where one interpretation is made and then comes to pass. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن العربي,البلخي)]
- 12:49/q11/p2 The passage is cited as evidence that unbelievers can have true dreams, but other rules about dream interpretation are judged opaque. — Al-Ālūsī reports the argument that the passage shows an unbeliever may have a true dream, advice not to ask at sunrise, sunset, or night, and the claim that a dream seen in sleep is its own interpretation; he finds many such claims unclear. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:49/q12 What inward meaning is given to the fruitful year?  [* · inward]
- 12:49/q12/p1 The year of relief and pressing signifies the soul's enjoyment after attaining complete tranquility and security. — Al-Qāshānī interprets the fruitful year of relief and pressing as the soul's enjoyment following complete tranquility and security. [1 notes: QASHANI]
