# 12:49: 16 questions

## 12:49/q01 What does 'that' (dhālika) refer to, and why is it masculine?  [ذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the masculine demonstrative can point back to the feminine years (because sabʿ lacks a feminine marker) or points instead to the drought.
- 12:49/q01/p1 It refers to the seven years; the masculine demonstrative is used because the number sabʿ has no feminine marker and resembles a masculine form. — Al-Kalbī (Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī): sabʿ has no feminine marker in its form; Ibn al-Qāsim (Ibn al-Jawzī): the number resembles a masculine form; al-Kalbī supports it with a verse where masculine grammar follows a feminine noun lacking a feminine marker. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن القاسم,الكلبي)]
- 12:49/q01/p2 It refers to the drought, not to the years. — Muqātil (reported by Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī). [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 12:49/q02 Why is the coming year called ʿām?  [عَامٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q02/p1 ʿĀm often suggests ease and fertility, whereas sana often suggests hardship and famine; the positive term signals the change from the preceding years. — Ālūsī: both mean year, but differ in connotation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/q02/p2 It is a full twelve months, and calling it ʿām signals that its coming relief is known and controlled by God alone. — Biqāʿī. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 12:49/q03 Why is 'in it' (fīhi) repeated and fronted?  [فِيهِ · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q03/p1 The repetition distinguishes rain and relief, which are acts of God, from pressing, which is an act people perform. — Bursevi. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 12:49/q03/p2 The repetition signals different times or kinds of benefit, enumerates the year's blessings, or stresses that the drought's rain was negligible beside this year's; fronting marks the focus. — Ālūsī; he adds that rhyme is also possible as a reason for the second occurrence. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:49/q04 What does yughāthu al-nās mean: that people receive rain or that they are relieved?  [يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ · meaning]
turns on: Derivation from triliteral ghāth (ghayth, rain) or quadriliteral aghātha (ghawth, relief); fit with the following mention of pressing (Ṭabāṭabāʾī: aid is more natural only under the passive reading yuʿṣarūn); literal versus consequential sense (Iṣlāḥī).

## 12:49/q05 Why does Joseph speak of 'the people' in the third person here?  [ٱلنَّاسُ · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q05/p1 Joseph shifts from addressing the group directly to saying people will receive rain or aid and press produce, indicating they will no longer need to manage their livelihoods. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 12:49/q07 How is yaʿṣirūn read?  [يَعْصِرُونَ · readings]

## 12:49/q08 Why does the verse mention pressing, and what does it signify?  [يَعْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q08/p1 Pressing signals that the famine has ended and prosperity returned, since nothing was pressed during the drought years. — Fārisī (also via Wāḥidī): some say they had pressed neither oil nor grapes for fourteen years, no oil or wine being pressed in famine years (Ṭabrisī likewise); Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān: it is reported they pressed nothing in the drought years; Ibn ʿAbbās (Wāḥidī): pressing signals famine's end; Biqāʿī: pressing marks the transition from drought to fertility; Qurṭubī: milking would indicate abundant vegetation; Study Quran: pressing oil and wine signaled food was plentiful again. [9 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 12:49/q08/p2 The mention suits the king's circumstances: the special good news is apt for him, and mentioning grape pressing subtly suits the king's wine-server. — Ālūsī: the good news suits the king's own circumstances, making the second-person reading apt; Iṣlāḥī: grape pressing subtly suits the king's wine-server. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:49/q08/p3 It is mentioned explicitly because rain does not ensure these pressed crops as directly as it ensures grain. — Ālūsī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/q08/p4 Rain, fodder growth and milking livestock fit the imagery of the fat and lean cows, explaining the specific mention of rain and pressing. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 12:49/q09 How did Joseph know of this year: was it part of the dream's interpretation or revealed knowledge of the unseen?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the specific details of the year (rain, relief, pressing) go beyond what the dream implies, or whether the end of hardship after seven years and God's custom of ease after hardship suffice to infer it.
- 12:49/q09/p1 The year was not in the king's dream nor asked about; God gave Joseph this unseen knowledge by revelation, as a sign of his prophethood. — Qatāda (ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Durr, Ālūsī): God gave Joseph knowledge of an extra, unasked year; Muqātil: it is Joseph's statement, not part of the dream; Ibn ʿAbbās (Durr, Ālūsī): God taught Joseph to volunteer the forecast; Ṭabarī, Ṭabrisī: a sign of prophethood and truthfulness; Wāḥidī: exegetes debate whether the interpretation came from knowledge God taught him or direct revelation, and the unasked year supports direct revelation, which the exegetes say is its only source; Zamakhsharī, Rāzī: the end of famine implies fertility in general, but the detailed promise of relief and pressing needs revelation (Abū Ḥayyān likewise: abundance was expected after seven severe years, revelation specified it); the Manār author (Ṭabāṭabāʾī): the unusually specific forecast is not entailed by the dream; Ālūsī: revelation is apparent, and mere inference would make the prophecy vague, especially in readings describing mutual relief; Abū Ḥayyān: Joseph's reply combines interpretation by meaning, practical advice and a report of the unseen eighth year; Māwardī: possibly prophetic information independent of the dream; Bayḍāwī (and Ṭabāṭabāʾī citing him): revelation is one possibility; Nasafī, Bursevi: by revelation, not from the dream; Kuranyolu: not signaled in the dream. - 12:49/q09/p2 Joseph could infer it: the dream's interpretation implied hardship would end after seven years, and God's custom is to send ease after hardship. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya (Abū Ḥayyān): it need not be unseen knowledge, since the interpretation implied abundance after seven years; Bayḍāwī (and Ṭabāṭabāʾī citing him): the expected end of drought or God's custom of relief after hardship; Ṭabāṭabāʾī: the final year is inferred from the repeated seven, after which people need not exert themselves to manage sowing and reserves. ## 12:49/q10 Why does Joseph add, and end with, the good news of this year?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:49/q10/p1 It displays Joseph's merit, knowledge and excellence, as a sign of his prophethood. — Ṭabarī, Ṭabrisī: unseen knowledge given as a sign of prophethood and truthfulness; Qurṭubī (with Qatāda): it displays his merit and knowledge; Ālūsī: ending his answer to the king with the glad news, unlike his answer to the prisoners where the prediction came first, highlights his excellence. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/q10/p2 It is glad tidings bringing comfort and hope after the discouraging forecast of hardship. — Ibn ʿĀshūr: reflects God's pattern of ease after hardship; Bayḍāwī and Bursevi call the final year good news / glad tidings. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 12:49/q11 How do the dream's symbols map onto the years Joseph foretells, and where does this year fall?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Joseph's words read a sequence of years from the dream's symbols or state a policy for the famine (Ṭabāṭabāʾī).
- 12:49/q11/p1 Fat cows and green ears signify fertile years, lean cows and dry ears famine years, the swallowing the consumption of stored abundance; this year of relief follows the fourteen years. — Rāzī: first seven fertile, next seven drought, as the dream indicates; Bayḍāwī, Bursevi: the swallowing means stored abundance is consumed; Nasafī: the abundant year comes after the fourteen years, a blessed eighth year (Abū Ḥayyān likewise for the symbols); Ibn ʿĀshūr: cows are agricultural years, fatness fertility, leanness drought, ears provisions; al-Qummī (Ṭabāṭabāʾī): seven plentiful years, seven famine years, then rain; Study Quran: the interpretation covers the next fifteen years. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI-]
- 12:49/q11/p2 The dream is not a forecast of seven fertile then seven barren years; Joseph states the policy required to survive the anticipated famine. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī, who says interpreters wrongly treat it as such a forecast. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 12:49/q12 Does a dream come to pass according to its first interpretation, given the counselors' dismissal of the king's dream?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether dismissing a dream as meaningless counts as an interpretation.
- 12:49/q12/p1 Yes: a dream hangs on a bird's wing until interpreted; dismissing it as confused dreams may not count as an interpretation. — Ālūsī, citing the hadith, in reply to al-Balkhī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+]
- 12:49/q12/p2 The rule applies only to visions admitting several possible meanings: one interpretation is made and then comes to pass. — Ibn al-ʿArabī (Ālūsī). [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 12:49/q12/p3 The counselors' dismissal of the king's dream as confused dreams challenges the rule that dreams occur according to their first interpretation. — Al-Balkhī (reported and answered by Ālūsī). [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)-]

## 12:49/q13 What else does the passage teach about dreams and their interpretation?  [* · theology]
- 12:49/q13/p1 An unbeliever can have a true dream; interpreters should not be asked at sunrise, sunset or night; some say a dream seen in sleep is its own interpretation. — Reported by Ālūsī, who finds many such claims opaque. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/q13/p2 Dreams should be interpreted by qualified people. — Kuranyolu, since this good year was not signaled in the dream but revealed by God (with reference to 12:4-6). [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

- 12:49/q14/p3 Joseph guided Egypt through the drought, winning the admiration of everyone from the king to ordinary people. — Iṣlāḥī. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
