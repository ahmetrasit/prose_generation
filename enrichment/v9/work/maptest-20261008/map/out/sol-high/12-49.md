# 12:49: 19 questions

## 12:49/q01 What does ذَٰلِكَ refer to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative resumes the numbered years, whose grammatical form can take masculine agreement, or the drought itself.
- 12:49/q01/p1 The seven preceding years. — Al-Kalbī explains the masculine demonstrative by the form of سبع, which lacks a feminine marker; Ibn al-Qāsim gives the same grammatical account, and al-Kalbī cites a parallel verse in which masculine agreement follows such a feminine noun (al-Wāḥidī; Ibn al-Jawzī). [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن القاسم,الكلبي)]
- 12:49/q01/p2 The drought. — Muqātil identifies the antecedent as the drought rather than the years (al-Wāḥidī; Ibn al-Jawzī). [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 12:49/q02 What does the choice of عَامٌ convey?  [عَامٌ · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q02/p1 It marks a full twelve-month year of relief under God's control. — Al-Biqāʿī says the word designates a complete twelve months and underscores that God alone knows and controls the coming relief. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 12:49/q02/p2 It suggests ease and fertility after the hard years. — Al-Ālūsī contrasts عام, often associated with ease and fertility, with سنة, often associated with hardship and famine; both mean year. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:49/q03 How did Joseph know the details of the year after the famine?  [ثُمَّ يَأْتِى مِنۢ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ عَامٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the dream or the normal end of drought establishes only general relief, or also establishes the precise rain, produce, and timing promised here.
- 12:49/q03/p1 God disclosed an additional, unasked-for year to Joseph by revelation or prophetic knowledge. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī, al-Manār as quoted by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Kuranyolu say it was outside the king's dream; Qatāda says God taught Joseph the unasked year (ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Durr). Al-Ṭabarī and al-Ṭabrisī call it a sign of prophethood and truthfulness; al-Qurṭubī calls it evidence of merit. Al-Wāḥidī uses the detailed forecast to support direct revelation over taught interpretation; al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī distinguish the general end of famine from the precise rain and pressing, which require revelation. Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes interpretation, practical advice, and an unseen report; al-Ālūsī treats revelation as apparent. Ibn ʿAbbās says Joseph volunteered the rain and pressing details (al-Durr). [30 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(صاحب المنار); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة وابن عباس)+]
- 12:49/q03/p2 The dream or the end of the seven hard years itself implied a return to abundance. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the dream's interpretation already implied that hardship ends after seven years (reported by Abū Ḥayyān). Al-Bayḍāwī allows inference from the end of drought or God's customary relief after hardship, as well as revelation; the dream's cows and ears symbolize the fertile and famine years and consumed stores (also al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī quoting him). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī derives the final year from the repeated seven and the end of the need to manage sowing and reserves. Al-Ālūsī rejects a merely natural inference as too vague for the specific promised relief. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI(البيضاوي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 12:49/q04 What does يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ mean?  [يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ · meaning]
turns on: The rain derivation from ghayth, the relief derivation from ghawth, or abundance as the practical form of aid; the following pressing clause and the forms of the Arabic roots are cited.
- 12:49/q04/p1 People receive rain. — Ibn Qutayba equates ghayth with rain; Ibn al-Sikkīt describes rain falling on the land (al-Wāḥidī; al-Rāzī). Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and Qatāda are cited for rain (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Durr, al-Ālūsī). Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes forms for rain, help, and seeking aid; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call the rain derivation triliteral, and al-Samīn quotes a Bedouin woman's غثنا for obtaining rain. Al-Tabāṭabāʾī prefers rain because pressing follows; he says Nile floods depend on rain upstream in Sudan and cites Lisān al-ʿArab for ghayth as vegetation grown from rainwater. Asad likewise notes Egypt's Nile floods depend on upstream rain. Al-Ṣādiq takes the word from rain in reports collected by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī; al-Ṭabrisī says ghayth is needed rain. [44 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن السكيت); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,عيسى بن عبيد الثقفي,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,علي بن معمر عن أبيه عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام,لسان العرب); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people); TABATABAI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 12:49/q04/p2 God relieves and rescues people from famine and distress. — Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya connect the word with ghawth, aid or relief; al-Ṭabrisī defines it as urgently needed help that removes harm. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī distinguish the quadriliteral relief derivation from the triliteral rain derivation; al-Biqāʿī notes the forms. Al-Baghawī invokes the idiom of seeking and receiving help; al-Nasafī mentions answered pleas. Asad allows relief alongside rain. Al-Iṣlāḥī prefers help as the literal force and says drought drives people to plead before rain comes; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds rain more apt in this context, except under a passive rain reading of the next verb. [25 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن السكيت); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; TABATABAI-]
- 12:49/q04/p3 People receive abundant produce. — Ibn ʿĪsā gives this as an explanation in al-Māwardī; Ibn al-Jawzī attributes relief through abundance to al-Māwardī. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي)]

## 12:49/q05 How is يَعْصِرُونَ read?  [يَعْصِرُونَ · readings]
turns on: The subject or addressee, active versus passive vocalization, and whether the verb denotes pressing, deliverance, or rain; al-Ṭabarī weighs established regional transmission against a reported rain reading.
- 12:49/q05/p1 Active third-person yaʿṣirūn: they press. — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī and others assign yāʾ to Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim, and Ibn ʿĀmir; it resumes الناس (Ibn Khālawayh, al-Fārisī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn). Al-Ṭabarī accepts it as an established regional reading equal in meaning to the address form; Qiraat-Er calls it standard active Form IV. ʿAlī is reported to object to active pressing of wine and direct the reciter toward rain (al-Ṭabrisī; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī). [13 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر); THALABI(أهل الكوفة); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخوان); SAMIN-DURR; QIRAAT-ER; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI(علي)-; TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام)-]
- 12:49/q05/p2 Active second-person taʿṣirūn: you press. — Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read tāʾ, according to Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, al-Thaʿlabī, and others; it addresses Joseph's questioners and continues the earlier direct address, including قدمتم (al-Fārisī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī). Ibn Khālawayh says it singles them out; al-Ṭabrisī says it may include others. Al-Ṭabarī accepts this established reading too, and Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with a second-person form tied to milking (al-Durr). [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); THALABI(أهل الكوفة); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخوان); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); QIRAAT-ER; TAB-FULL+]
- 12:49/q05/p3 Passive third-person yuʿṣarūn: they are relieved or rained upon. — Al-Aʿraj, Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad, and ʿĪsā are named by Ibn Jinnī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī. Some explain rescue; others rain from rain-bearing clouds in 78:14 (Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Zajjāj, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī). Saʿīd b. Jubayr is also cited for a passive reading; al-Ṣādiq and ʿAlī favor rain in reports collected by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. Al-Ṭabarī rejects a non-established yāʾ rain reading against the regional readers. [22 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عيسى بن عمر); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(عيسى والأعرج وجعفر بن محمد); THALABI(عيسى بن الأعرج); MAWARDI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر الثقفي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعرج وعيسى وجعفر بن محمد); TABRISI(جعفر بن محمد، الأعرج، عيسى بن عمر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير والزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عيسى); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جعفر بن محمد، والأعرج، وعيسى البصرة); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,قطرب); ALUSI-FULL(جعفر بن محمد والأعرج وعيسى البصرة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,جعفر الصادق,علي بن معمر); TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام)+; TABRISI(علي)+; TAB-FULL-]
- 12:49/q05/p4 Passive second-person taʿṣarūn: you are rescued or rained upon. — Abū Ḥayyān reports a second reading from ʿĪsā meaning rescue; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī also assign him a passive address form. Al-Qurṭubī reports ʿĪsā's tāʾ with open ṣād for rain, alongside a kasra variant of the same sense; al-Durr mentions a second-person reciter and 78:14. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عيسى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(جعفر بن محمد والأعرج وعيسى البصرة); DURR-FULL(عيسى بن عبيد الثقفي)]
- 12:49/q05/p5 An intensified or assimilated form is read. — Al-Naqqāsh transmits a doubled form, described as intensive by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī; their descriptions vary as to vocalization and passive form. Zayd b. ʿAlī reads a form from taʿtaṣirūn with assimilation, which may mean squeezing or rescue (Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī). Al-Naqqāsh also records a reading about squeezing moisture (Ibn ʿAṭiyya). [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش,زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(النقاش); ALUSI-FULL(النقاش,زيد بن علي)]

## 12:49/q06 What do people do in يَعْصِرُونَ?  [يَعْصِرُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb is physical squeezing, a related action such as milking, an Arabic rescue or prosperity idiom, or a rain expression; cited readings, poetry, and early authorities supply the competing evidence.
- 12:49/q06/p1 They press fruit and plants for juice or oil. — The lists vary: grapes, olives, sesame, other fruit, sugarcane, radish, oil, wine, syrup, or water (Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, Mujāhid, Ibn Jurayj and many commentators). Al-Qurṭubī cites al-Bukhārī for Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Wāḥidī relates sesame oil, grape wine, and olive oil and points to 12:36. Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī report fourteen years without pressing oil or grapes; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report that nothing was pressed during drought. Al-Samīn calls this the clearest sense; al-Biqāʿī says pressing expresses the transition to fertility. Al-Ṭabarī rejects the rescue derivation because Companion and Successor interpretation favors pressing; al-Wāḥidī reports al-Fārisī favoring both pressing and rescue. [46 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); FARISI-HUJJA; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(ابن عباس، مجاهد، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس وقتادة والجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R); SAMIN-DURR+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)+; TABRISI(علي)-; TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام)-]
- 12:49/q06/p2 They milk livestock. — Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās through several transmissions (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr, al-Ālūsī); al-Durr also links it to the second-person reading. Al-Wāḥidī names al-Wālibī and Aḥmad b. ʿUbayd; Ibn al-Jawzī cites a poem using the verb for milking. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī explain that squeezing an udder is itself pressing; al-Qurṭubī says milk points to rich vegetation. Al-Ṭabarī rejects a report through al-Faraj b. Faḍāla from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭalḥa as contrary to known Arabic and Ibn ʿAbbās's usage. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طلحة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وقتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; TAB-FULL-]
- 12:49/q06/p3 They escape drought or are rescued and take refuge. — Abū ʿUbayda derives ʿaṣr, ʿaṣra, or muʿtaṣar from rescue or refuge (Ibn Qutayba, al-Fārisī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī). Al-Samīn prefers this for its fit with يُغاث; al-Wāḥidī reports al-Fārisī favoring it alongside pressing. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says al-Ṭabarī offered no proof for his rejection; al-Ṭabarī argues poetry cannot override the early authorities' consensus. - 12:49/q06/p4 They receive rain, expressed through clouds being pressed or releasing water. — ʿĪsā and other passive readers are given this sense (al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī); 78:14 is repeatedly cited for rain-bearing clouds. Al-Jawharī's عصر القوم directly means that people received rain. Al-Ṭabarī rejects a reported rain reading against the established readings; ʿAlī and al-Ṣādiq are reported to favor the rain sense over pressing wine. - 12:49/q06/p6 They obtain what they desire. — Abū ʿUbayd cites Ibn Aḥmar's poem about taking from life's branches (al-Wāḥidī; Ibn al-Jawzī). [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة عن ابن أحمر)]
- 12:49/q06/p7 They give generously and grant gifts. — Al-Wāḥidī cites a language scholar through Ibn al-Anbārī and says al-Azharī reports the sense from Abū ʿUbayd and Abū ʿUbayda. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)]
- 12:49/q06/p8 They bring livestock from abundant pasture. — Al-Māwardī reports this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 12:49/q06/p9 They exploit or extract value from produce. — Abū al-Ghawth derives it from pressing grapes and compares iʿtaṣartu mālahu, extracting wealth from someone's hand (al-Qurṭubī). [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الغوث)]

## 12:49/q07 Why mention pressing produce in the promised year?  [وَفِيهِ يَعْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q07/p1 Pressing marks an exceptional return of fertility and specific crops after famine. — Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī report that no oil or grapes had been pressed for fourteen years; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say nothing was pressed during drought. Al-Biqāʿī treats pressing as a transition marker. Al-Ālūsī says rain alone does not guarantee grapes and oil crops as directly as grain, so the detail adds good news. Study Quran sees oil and wine as signs of sufficient food; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees blessing for people and livestock. [10 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 12:49/q07/p2 The detail suits the king's wine-server or the imagery of the dream. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says grape pressing subtly fits the king's wine-server; al-Ālūsī says the news suits the king's circumstances and second-person reading. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says rain, fodder, and milk best fit the fat and lean cow imagery. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 12:49/q08 Why repeat فِيهِ?  [فِيهِ وَفِيهِ · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q08/p1 It distinguishes divine rain and relief from people's pressing. — Al-Bursawī says the first action belongs to God and the second to people. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 12:49/q08/p2 It enumerates or emphasizes distinct benefits of the year. — Al-Ālūsī allows different times or types of benefit, enumerated blessings, or emphasis that earlier drought rain was negligible beside this year's; fronting focuses the phrase and rhyme may account for the second occurrence. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:49/q09 Why shift from direct address to ٱلنَّاسُ and يَعْصِرُونَ?  [ٱلنَّاسُ يَعْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q09/p1 The wider phrasing signals that people can again live without managing famine reserves. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts Joseph's earlier direct address to the group with his reference here to people receiving rain or aid and pressing produce. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 12:49/q09/p2 The third-person verb resumes الناس; the second-person reading addresses Joseph's petitioners. — Ibn Khālawayh, al-Fārisī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn, and Qiraat-Er explain the pronouns this way; al-Ṭabrisī says the address can include others. [8 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر,حمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; QIRAAT-ER]

## 12:49/q10 How does the promised year relate to the king's dream and Joseph's interpretation?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the earlier seven fertile years are dream symbols being decoded, or Joseph's agricultural policy in response to a foreseen famine.
- 12:49/q10/p1 It is an additional year after seven fertile and seven famine years. — Qatāda calls it an unasked-for eighth year after the fourteen years (ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān). Fat cows and green ears signify fertile years; lean cows and dry ears signify famine, and stored abundance is consumed (al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, Ibn ʿĀshūr). Study Quran counts fifteen years in the interpretation. Al-Qummī's report likewise has plenty, famine, then rain. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); STUDYQURAN]
- 12:49/q10/p2 Joseph gave a survival policy for the expected famine, rather than deriving the fertile years from the dream. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects commentators' claim that the dream forecasts seven fertile followed by seven barren years; he reads Joseph's directions as the necessary policy for surviving anticipated famine. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 12:49/q11 Why does Joseph end with this good news?  [* · rhetoric]
- 12:49/q11/p1 The placement brings hope after a discouraging forecast and shows Joseph's excellence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says relief after hardship comforts the hearers; al-Ālūsī contrasts the placement with Joseph's answer to the prisoners, where prediction came first, and says the final glad news highlights his excellence. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:49/q12 What inward state does the year of relief signify?  [* · inward]
- 12:49/q12/p1 The soul enjoys fruition after complete tranquility and security. — Al-Qāshānī reads the year of relief and pressing as the soul's enjoyment after attaining security. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 12:49/q13 What happened to Egyptian people and property during Joseph's famine administration?  [* · reports]
- 12:49/q13/p1 Joseph sold stored food for successive classes of property, then freed people and restored their possessions. — A report from al-Riḍā says the sequence was money, valuables, livestock, servants, property, land, then personal freedom; Joseph later freed Egyptians and restored possessions and kingship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he omits many similar economic reports because they do not directly explain these verses. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام)]

## 12:49/q14 How did Joseph recognize the famine's first day?  [* · reports]
- 12:49/q14/p1 A man ate both of two food portions Joseph prepared for him. — Zayd b. Aslam's report, quoted by al-Ālūsī, says Joseph called that the first day of the hard years. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]

## 12:49/q15 Does the counselors' dismissal of the dream challenge the rule that a dream follows its first interpretation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether calling a dream meaningless is an interpretation, and whether the rule applies to dreams with several admissible meanings.
- 12:49/q15/p1 The dismissal challenges that rule. — Al-Balkhī argues that the counselors called the king's vision confused dreams, yet that assessment did not determine its outcome (reported by al-Ālūsī). [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)]
- 12:49/q15/p2 A dismissal as meaningless may not count as an interpretation. — Al-Ālūsī replies with the hadith that a dream hangs on a bird's wing until interpreted, and says the counselors' words may not have been an interpretation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)]
- 12:49/q15/p3 The rule applies to dreams with several possible meanings, once one interpretation is made. — Ibn al-ʿArabī gives this restriction, reported by al-Ālūsī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 12:49/q16 What does the king's dream show about true dreams and their interpretation?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:49/q16/p1 An unbeliever can have a true dream. — Al-Ālūsī reports this use of the passage; he also finds some associated claims opaque, including advice against seeking interpretation at sunrise, sunset, or night and a claim that a sleeping dream interprets itself. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/q16/p2 Dreams should be interpreted by qualified people. — Kuranyolu draws this lesson from Joseph's disclosed forecast and cites 12:4–6. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 12:49/q17 What does the account teach about God's direction of events?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:49/q17/p1 God directs events beyond human understanding, including things people wrongly desire or dislike. — Asad contrasts the Qur'anic account with the Genesis account of Joseph's fortunes and invokes 2:216. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 12:49/q18 How do people respond to severe hardship in the promised relief?  [يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ · interpretation]
- 12:49/q18/p1 They cry to God for help until their pleas are answered. — Al-Iṣlāḥī treats asking for aid as the word's literal force and says drought exposes its full effects; even extreme polytheists and disbelievers forget their imagined deities and call on the one God, as the Qur'an says. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 12:49/q19 What did Joseph's guidance through the drought accomplish?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:49/q19/p1 It earned admiration from the king and ordinary Egyptians. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says Joseph's management of the drought won broad admiration. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
