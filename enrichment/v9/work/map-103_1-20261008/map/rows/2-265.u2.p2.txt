2:265/q20 What happens if only طل reaches the garden? [فَإِن لَّمْ يُصِبْهَا وَابِلٌۭ فَطَلٌّۭ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether light moisture preserves the same multiplied crop or produces a smaller yet reliable one.)
  2:265/q20/p1 The fertile garden still gives a full or doubled yield; little moisture suffices. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf invokes fertile soil; al-Baghawi says steady light dew can do a downpour's work. Razi and Abu Hayyan offer full-yield possibilities; al-Wahidi and al-Nasafi say the crop remains abundant. Ibn Qutayba expressly says lighter rain does not diminish its yield compared with heavy rain.
  2:265/q20/p2 The garden yields less than under heavy rain, but still yields something beneficial. — reasons: Al-Mawardi argues dew-fed crops are weaker than rain-fed crops; Razi allows this alternative; Ibn Ashur links varying yield to varying sincerity.
2:265/q21 What does the orchard teach about sincere giving? [* · interpretation]
  2:265/q21/p1 Sincere charity or a believer's deed remains fruitful and multiplied, whether the gift is large or small. — reasons: Qatada says God's promise never fails; al-Suddi connects doubled fruit to doubled reward. Al-Mawardi urges giving even a little. Razi, al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur say degree depends on sincerity, effort, attachment to wealth, recipient need and accompanying circumstances. Al-Qummi's lawful-date report and al-Qurtubi's Abu Hurayra report depict God nurturing a date to mountain size. Jishumi cites al-Hasan and Qatada: God multiplies small and large gifts and never withholds their recompense; he says the promise encourages charity.
2:265/q22 What does God's seeing deeds convey? [وَٱللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ · interpretation]
  2:265/q22/p1 God knows outward deeds and inward motives, records and recompenses them; the close promises reward and warns against display. — reasons: Al-Tabari distinguishes sincere from hurtful giving and warns against neglected obligations; Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi and others see both promise and warning. Razi notes amount and manner; al-Biqa'i urges improvement of inward and outward work. Al-Khattabi says basir's form functions as an active participle. Jishumi says God rewards each according to spending and intention, and reports Abu Muslim's distinction between ostentation and sincerity.
2:265/q23 How is تعملون read, and whom does it address? [تَعْمَلُونَ · readings] (turns on: A change in grammatical person and the antecedent of the third-person pronoun.)
  2:265/q23/p1 Second-person تعملون addresses the audience directly. — reasons: The majority reading shifts from third-person narration to direct address, making God's knowledge a promise and warning.
  2:265/q23/p2 Third-person يعملون refers to all people or specifically the donors; one report also relates it to hypocrites. — reasons: Al-Zuhri is named for the variant. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi say a donor-only reference makes it pure promise; Abu Hayyan also mentions hypocrites.
2:265/q24 How does the verse connect with the surrounding spending parables and passage? [* · links] (turns on: Which earlier image supplies the main comparison: the barren rock or the multiplying seed.)
  2:265/q24/p1 It contrasts accepted, fertile giving with showy or harmful giving that the preceding rock image leaves barren. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan contrast 2:264; Ibn Ashur says contrast clarifies rank and renews praise. Al-Tabataba'i treats the linked verses as a unit addressing purpose, manner, good wealth, needy recipients and reward. Jishumi contrasts the two traits of sound charity with the two faults of reminder and harm in 2:264, reporting the Qadi's formulation.
  2:265/q24/p2 It develops the earlier seed image: the lasting orchard pictures sustained generosity and further multiplication. — reasons: Al-Biqa'i says the grain represents spending in God's path while the orchard covers every good path. Ibn Ashur says the simpler seed image quickly pictures multiplication; this one has several favorable features. Elmalili says double does not reduce the earlier seven-hundredfold promise.
2:265/q25 What inward lesson is drawn from the garden and rain? [* · inward] (turns on: The image's inward analogue: a lasting disposition of generosity or purification of intention.)
  2:265/q25/p1 Giving fixes generosity in the soul; the ridge signifies higher rank, and stronger or weaker rain signifies different shares of mercy. — reasons: Al-Qashani contrasts the enduring orchard with a grain to express an acquired lasting quality; he calls generosity a divine attribute.
  2:265/q25/p2 Sincere devotion removes hidden idolatry and makes deeds grow; insincere work cannot produce good fruit. — reasons: Bursevi cites Sa'di's thorns-and-flowers analogy, and says pure work seeks no praise. He also assigns symbolic meanings to charity's three letters.
2:265/q26 What reports about charity's effects are cited with this verse? [* · reports] (turns on: Different transmitted statements about charity's effect.)
  2:265/q26/p1 A lawfully earned date given in charity is nurtured by God until it becomes mountain-sized. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi cites Abu Hurayra's Prophetic report through Muslim and the Muwatta; Study Quran also invokes it.
  2:265/q26/p2 Charity itself speaks of five benefits: growth, enlargement, turning an enemy friendly, preservation and protection. — reasons: Bursevi transmits the report through Ali from the Prophet.
  2:265/q26/p3 Hell prostrates in gratitude when a believer gives charity, because it frees someone from punishment. — reasons: Bursevi reports Makhul's saying.
2:265/q27 How do other cited verses about riba and giving relate to this verse's reward contrast? [* · links]
  2:265/q27/p1 Gift-riba seeking a larger return earns no divine reward, unlike sincere charity; prohibited exchange-riba is distinct. — reasons: Al-Farisi distinguishes unlawful excess in like weighed or measured goods from disliked gift-riba, citing 2:278 and 30:39. He gives conditional and relative parses of 30:39, its omitted resumptive and shift of person, and discusses 74:6: Ikrima forbids seeking more back, while al-Farisi says a jussive parse lacks semantic fit.
2:265/q28 What adjacent grammatical example is discussed alongside this verse? [* · links]
  2:265/q28/p1 In the bird passage, فصرهن can mean cut them or incline them, with different attachments of إليك. — reasons: Al-Farisi records both interpretations of 2:260; Sibawayh attaches إليك to the nearer turning verb, while attachment to خذ also permits an implied later object.
2:265/q29 What do ابتغاء and مرضات mean? [ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ · meaning]
  2:265/q29/p1 ابتغاء means seeking, and مرضات names satisfaction or approval. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi derives marḍāt as the verbal noun of raḍiya. Jishumi derives ibtigha from ibtaghaytu and notes related forms for seeking something or helping another seek it.
2:265/q30 What verbal sense underlies تثبيتا? [وَتَثْبِيتًۭا · grammar]
  2:265/q30/p1 Causative ثبّت means to make firm or establish, distinct from intransitive ثبت, to become firm. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and al-Samin's al-Shaykh insist on this distinction, opposing replacement by the intransitive verbal noun unless a licensing verb precedes it. Jishumi illustrates the root's firmness by a steadfast person who does not waver in battle; illness can likewise keep a person from leaving.
2:265/q31 How are جنة and ربوة pronounced when pausing? [جَنَّةٍۭ بِرَبْوَةٍ · readings]
  2:265/q31/p1 Their feminine tāʾ is pronounced hāʾ at pause according to written form and practice. — reasons: Al-Nashr states the pause rule for singular feminine nouns without open ta, using janna bi-rabwa as an example; it also contrasts indefinite nonfeminine accusatives and rejects Ibn Jabbara's alleged hāʾ stops at other words as contrary to the script and performance practice.
2:265/q32 How should ربوة be classified when distinguishing a word's forms and meanings? [بِرَبْوَةٍ · meaning]
  2:265/q32/p1 Its occurrence should not be inflated into a distinct sense of riba merely because the forms are related. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi rejects a grouping that makes the garden-on-a-height expression a separate riba sense. He defines wujuh as meanings and naza'ir as repeated forms, criticizes unexamined copying and maximal category counts, and surveys earlier compilers. His separate ifk entry illustrates how one root can have genuinely different contextual senses, from lie and diversion to overturning, sorcery, slander and idols; Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Faris and Abu Ubayda connect the root to turning or diversion.
2:265/q33 What wider revelation setting of al-Baqara is noted alongside this verse? [* · sciences]
  2:265/q33/p1 Ibn Kathir situates al-Baqara within staged revelation and discusses the Prophet's Meccan and Medinan periods and their signs. — reasons: Ibn Kathir grades Ibn Abbas's report of the Qur'an's descent to the lowest heaven on Laylat al-Qadr and subsequent twenty-year revelation authentic. He discusses the Prophet's years in Mecca and Medina, possible reconciliations of their counts, an Ahmad report about Mika'il and Gabriel, Ramadan's honor and increased recitation, the before-or-after-Hijra definition, disputed surah criteria, disjoint letters, believer-address, and Medinan al-Baqara examples of people-address.
