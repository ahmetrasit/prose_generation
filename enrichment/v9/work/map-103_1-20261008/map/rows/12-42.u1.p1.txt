12:42/q12 What range does biḍʿ denote? [بِضْعَ · meaning] (turns on: Reported Arabic ranges differ in lower and upper bounds, and some notes address compound-number usage rather than this verse's exact duration.)
  12:42/q12/p1 Three through nine, or generally below ten. — reasons: Mujahid, Qatada, al-Asma'i and many others give 3–9; al-Zajjaj calls it correct and derives the word from a cut portion. Al-Tabari appeals to Arabic usage. Al-Farra contrasts نيف for one or two; reports on Abu Bakr's Roman wager support the upper bound, though one version says 7–9.
  12:42/q12/p2 Three through seven. — reasons: Abu Bakr and Qutrub are cited; a wager report has Abu Bakr answer 3–7. Some reports ascribe this narrower range to Mujahid or al-Farra.
  12:42/q12/p3 Three through five. — reasons: Abu Ubayda is quoted for this by al-Tha'labi, al-Jishumi and Ibn al-Jawzi; other sources attribute it to al-Zajjaj or unnamed authorities.
  12:42/q12/p4 One through four, below half a decade. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba quotes Abu Ubayda; al-Wahidi and Ibn Atiyya also report him. Al-Qurtubi dismisses this definition.
  12:42/q12/p5 One through ten, including the wider lower range. — reasons: Al-Akhfash and a report of the Prophet's question to companions give 1–10; Ibn Abbas is cited for the same. Abu Ubayd's wording is internally awkward in some transmissions.
  12:42/q12/p6 Three through ten. — reasons: Reports from Ibn Abbas give this inclusive upper end; Ibn Atiyya connects it to Malik's rules for claims and oaths.
  12:42/q12/p7 A single seven-year span, or about seven. — reasons: Ikrima is quoted with seven; al-Tha'labi says most commentators use seven in this passage, a contextual gloss rather than a general numerical boundary.
  12:42/q12/p8 Five through seven. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi transmits al-Hasan's narrower range.
  12:42/q12/p9 Four through nine. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi transmits this variant from Mujahid.
  12:42/q12/p10 Seven through nine. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi cites a report of the Prophet to Ibn Abbas in the Abu Bakr Roman wager story.
  12:42/q12/p11 Five through twelve. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi transmits this from Abdullah ibn Rashid al-Basri through Sa'id ibn Abi Aruba.
  12:42/q12/p12 Twelve years in one transmitted explanation of this occurrence. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi attributes it to al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas; al-Alusi calls a fourteen-year explanation uncommon.
  12:42/q12/p13 The root means a cut-off piece or portion, without fixing a numerical boundary. — reasons: Al-Jishumi compares a portion of flesh or property; al-Samin cites بضع اللحم, بضاعة and مبضع. Al-Raghib offers either 3–10 or more than five but below ten.
  12:42/q12/p14 With compound numbers it accompanies tens up to ninety, not hundreds or thousands. — reasons: Al-Farra gives the Arabic usage, repeated by later grammarians; al-Qurtubi notes variant vowelings and a general span of time.
12:42/q13 How is “a few years” constructed grammatically? [بِضْعَ سِنِينَ · grammar]
  12:42/q13/p1 It functions as a temporal adverb specifying the duration of Joseph's stay. — reasons: Al-Samin reports this i'rab and notes disagreements over the lexical range of بضع.
12:42/q14 How long was Joseph imprisoned, and which part of the term followed the request? [فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ بِضْعَ سِنِينَ · reports] (turns on: Whether biḍʿ counts the entire confinement or only the period after Joseph's request, and how competing numerical reports are assessed.)
  12:42/q14/p1 Seven years in all. — reasons: Qatada, Wahb and many others report seven; Wahb compares Job's affliction and Nebuchadnezzar's beastly punishment. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi read the verse as possibly giving the entire term; al-Alusi reports two years after the request within seven.
  12:42/q14/p2 Twelve years total: five before the request and seven afterward. — reasons: Al-Kalbi and many commentators give the sequence; a Prophetic report in al-Baydawi specifies it. Al-Qurtubi treats the penalty years as distinct from total imprisonment. Al-Alusi rejects the precise wording of the seven-additional-years report.
  12:42/q14/p3 Twelve years total: seven before the request and five afterward. — reasons: Muqatil reverses the periods and relates the extra years to 12:35.
  12:42/q14/p4 Twelve years total, without a specified division. — reasons: Ibn Abbas is quoted for twelve, though other reports divide it into five and seven.
  12:42/q14/p5 Fourteen years in all. — reasons: Tawus and al-Dahhak transmit fourteen; al-Mawardi cautions the period of punishment could be only part of the total, and al-Alusi calls applying fourteen to biḍʿ unusual.
  12:42/q14/p6 Five years in all, or five years plus an unspecified biḍʿ. — reasons: Kur'an Yolu lists five among reported totals; al-Qurtubi reports a formula of five years and a further biḍʿ.
  12:42/q14/p7 Two years were added after Joseph's request. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya records this as an alternative to seven additional years; Abu Hayyan also lists two, and al-Alusi reports a total of seven with two after the request.
  12:42/q14/p8 Seven or nine penalty years were added after five already served. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi leaves the exact penalty span open between seven and nine.
  12:42/q14/p9 The exact duration should be left unspecified. — reasons: Al-Alusi rejects firm numbering because reports diverge and precise additional-seven wording is not established; Abu Hayyan notes that the verse itself leaves the figure vague.
12:42/q15 What reports describe a divine or angelic rebuke to Joseph? [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ · reports] (turns on: The identity of the rebuking speaker and the details and reliability of the transmitted stories.)
  12:42/q15/p1 Gabriel enumerated God's past favors and asked why Joseph sought human aid. — reasons: Ka'b and other reports list creation, father's love, rescue from the well, protection from women, and dream interpretation; the lists vary. Joseph answers God at each step; in some versions he asks mercy or accepts confinement if God is pleased.
  12:42/q15/p2 God directly reproached Joseph for making the cupbearer an intermediary and announced longer confinement. — reasons: Malik ibn Dinar and Hasan report Joseph wept, explaining that anguish distracted his heart and an utterance escaped; variants name a divine oath or promise not to repeat the lapse.
  12:42/q15/p3 A report says Joseph prayed in prison for relief through God. — reasons: Abu al-Malih invokes Jacob's face and asks for relief, a way out, and unexpected provision; another report says Gabriel taught a prayer for relief, forgiveness, firm hope in God, and detachment from others.
12:42/q16 Which reports connect Joseph's request to other alleged lapses? [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · reports] (turns on: Whether the source links the request to the woman episode or to Joseph's later statement about betrayal.)
  12:42/q16/p1 Ibn Abbas reports three lapses: the episode with the woman, the request to the cupbearer, and calling the brothers thieves. — reasons: The later taunt concerned Benjamin; Ibn Atiyya cites 12:24, 12:70 and 12:77. Al-Tabataba'i rejects this as inconsistent with divine election, purification and Joseph's truthfulness.
  12:42/q16/p2 Another three-lapse report lists the request, the theft accusation, and Joseph's claim that he had not betrayed the Aziz. — reasons: Gabriel is said to remind Joseph of his desire, after which he says he does not absolve himself. Al-Tabataba'i rejects the implied lies; transmitted verse references vary.
12:42/q17 What does the Torah account add around the prison episode? [* · reports]
  12:42/q17/p1 It narrates Joseph's sale, temptation, imprisonment, the prisoners' dreams, their outcomes, and the cupbearer's failure to remember him. — reasons: Al-Biqa'i quotes Midianites and Potiphar; Joseph refuses the wife and is falsely accused, then prospers under the jailer. The vine's three branches and baker's three baskets mean three days; Pharaoh restores one, executes the other, and the cupbearer forgets.
12:42/q18 How does this verse connect to Joseph's later release and the sura's wider lesson? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the later recollection, Joseph's changed conduct, or the broader sura explains this scene.)
  12:42/q18/p1 The saved man's recollection in 12:45 confirms that he had forgotten Joseph. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi, al-Alusi, al-Tabataba'i and Kur'an Yolu use the later verse to support the cupbearer reading; al-Qurtubi adds that prophets may forget ordinary matters, while revelation is protected.
  12:42/q18/p2 Joseph's eventual refusal to answer the king immediately may repair his earlier appeal for royal help. — reasons: Al-Razi says Joseph put vindication of his name before favor from the king.
  12:42/q18/p3 The sura illustrates God's hidden direction of human affairs. — reasons: Asad contrasts Genesis' romantic account and cites 2:216: people may dislike what benefits them and like what harms them.
12:42/q19 Can prophets forget ordinary matters? [فَأَنسَىٰهُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · theology] (turns on: Whether the reported forgetting is ordinary and permissible or entails blameworthy satanic influence.)
  12:42/q19/p1 Yes; protection concerns conveying revelation, not every ordinary lapse. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi cites 12:45, 17:65 and hadith that Adam and the Prophet forgot; he uses this to answer an objection to the cupbearer reading.
