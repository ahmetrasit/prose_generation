18:25/q08 Why is the unit 'years' stated after three hundred? [سِنِينَ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the explicit plural primarily identifies the unit or adds emphasis.)
  18:25/q08/p1 It removes uncertainty over whether the three hundred were years, months, days, weeks or hours. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba contrasts bare 300 with measured dirhams. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil say the first revelation gave 300, listeners asked its unit, then Gabriel or revelation supplied 'years and nine more'; al-Wāḥidī notes Ibn Mujāhid permitted a pause after 300 because of the staged descent, but prefers no pause grammatically.
  18:25/q08/p2 The plural can magnify the long duration. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests the plural wording emphasizes the length while remaining appositional.
18:25/q09 What does 'nine' count? [تِسْعًۭا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the established unit carries into the following number, and whose act of adding is described.)
  18:25/q09/p1 Nine further years. — reasons: Ibn ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr explicitly supplies years; al-Zajjāj and al-Qushayrī rule out nights or hours because the prior classifier carries forward, as in 100 dirhams and five. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn and others identify an omitted noun; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī reject ambiguity after the specified count. Abū al-ʿAbbās uses 2:234 and Arab counting of day-night periods by nights; he says the feminine form excludes months.
  18:25/q09/p2 Its unit is left uncertain or might be months, days or hours. — reasons: Al-Ḍaḥḥāk is reported to say people did not know the nine's unit, and al-Qāshānī takes it as gestation months; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī argue that the previous years supply the unit.
  18:25/q09/p3 On the quoted-speech reading, the nine is an additional claim by some disputants. — reasons: Al-Khafājī assigns 'they added' to the People of the Book, though al-Ālūsī rejects it as weak.
18:25/q10 Why are nine added to the three hundred? [وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًۭا · interpretation] (turns on: The notes offer a calendar conversion, an actual extension of sleep, a return to sleep, emphatic wording, or an allegorical monthly unit.)
  18:25/q10/p1 The extra nine convert 300 solar years into approximately 309 lunar years. — reasons: Al-Naqqāsh gives about one lunar year per 33⅓ solar years; Ibn Kathīr and al-Bursawī use roughly three per century. Al-Biqāʿī says 300 follows Jewish reckoning and nine the Arab reckoning. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the two-calendar precision a scientific aspect of inimitability; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī answers arithmetic objections by noting an error of under three months, while al-Bursawī cites al-Kāshifī's 309 years, two months, nine days. Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī raise astronomical objections; al-Khafājī says a fraction under half a year is rounded.
  18:25/q10/p2 Their sleep genuinely continued nine more years after 300. — reasons: Al-Rāzī tentatively suggests circumstances prolonged it; al-Ṭībī says they nearly woke at 300 but something kept them asleep. Al-Ālūsī objects that this would require an unexpressed increase in their sleep.
  18:25/q10/p3 They woke briefly and then slept again, adding nine years. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī attributes this to Qatāda and considers it closer than the solar-lunar account.
  18:25/q10/p4 The separate nine emphatically confirms and heightens the long duration. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī compares the confirming exception in Noah's thousand years at 29:14. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the earlier passage minimizes the period while this one stresses 'not merely 300 but nine more'.
  18:25/q10/p5 Nine signifies gestation months after 300 monthly cycles. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says the 300 lunar cycles yield 25 years before waking; al-Ālūsī rejects this allegorical reduction as baseless and contrary to the possible outward duration.
18:25/q11 How is tisʿan pronounced? [تِسْعًۭا · readings] (turns on: The recorded vowel of the first letter differs.)
  18:25/q11/p1 The common form has kasra on the tāʾ. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī contrasts it with Abū ʿAmr's reported fatḥa; the latter is described as dialectal.
  18:25/q11/p2 Al-Ḥasan and, in one report, Abū ʿAmr open the tāʾ. — reasons: The sources compare the pronunciation to ʿashr; al-Ālūsī names the al-Luʾluʾī transmission and calls it dialectal.
18:25/q12 How does azdādū govern 'nine'? [وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًۭا · grammar] (turns on: The syntactic object is agreed in the main parse; the alternative concerns the pronoun's referent.)
  18:25/q12/p1 'Nine' is its direct object; the underlying noun for additional stay or years is omitted. — reasons: Abū ʿAlī denies an adverbial time parse. Al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say the derived verb takes one object though its base takes two; al-Samīn explains iftiʿāl with tāʾ assimilated to dāl after zāy and cites 18:13, while al-Ālūsī cites al-Rāghib and notes Abū al-Baqāʾ's contrary two-object claim.
  18:25/q12/p2 The pronoun can refer to People of the Book who added nine to a 300-year claim. — reasons: Al-Khafājī proposes this under quoted speech; al-Ālūsī rejects it.
18:25/q13 How does the stated duration connect to the surrounding passage? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the clause attaches to quoted guesses, resumes the divine story, or attaches to the victorious party determines its relation to 18:26.)
  18:25/q13/p1 It specifies the previously summarized years of sleep. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect the exact count to God sealing their hearing for years at 18:11; Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the sleepers' discussion at 18:19.
  18:25/q13/p2 It continues the earlier catalogue of human guesses. — reasons: Qatāda attaches it to 18:22, and al-Wāḥidī reports the preceding 'they will say' as a frame. Asad sees idle guesses refuted by 18:22 and 18:26; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes Ibn Masʿūd's explicit 'they said'.
  18:25/q13/p3 It resumes God's narrative after the digression about their number. — reasons: Al-Rāzī points to the intervening command against disputation; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the narrative continuation possible. Iṣlāḥī says the parenthetical discussion in 18:22–24 ends here, returning to the earlier estimates of their number.
  18:25/q13/p4 It is linked to the group who proposed building a mosque over the sleepers. — reasons: Elmalılı reports this connection to the preceding victorious party, then prefers a fresh statement about that party's long hidden period.
  18:25/q13/p5 The next verse reserves the later or disputed interval to God's knowledge. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Thaʿlabī distinguish known sleep from unknown time after discovery or death; the rival view takes 18:26 as refuting the quoted count. Al-Baghawī says God can state the true count and still proclaim that He knows best.
18:25/q14 What does the long stay demonstrate? [* · theology] (turns on: The notes offer theological and philosophical explanations of the possibility and significance of extraordinary sleep.)
  18:25/q14/p1 God can preserve sleepers and raise the dead; the duration is a sign of divine power. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī says making the count only a human claim weakens the passage's proof. Al-Rāzī argues from God's power over possibilities and knowledge of particulars; if a day's sleep is possible, so is 300 years. He compares the bodily night journey and Jesus' fatherless birth in adjacent sūras and cites Avicenna's report of an Aristotelian parallel; al-Ālūsī likewise cites the parallel.
  18:25/q14/p2 Rare celestial conditions could also permit extraordinary preservation. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports philosophers allowing unusual heavenly configurations to affect sublunary matter.
  18:25/q14/p3 The sleep represents an intermediate state between life and death. — reasons: The Study Quran analogizes the centuries-long sleep to the interval between individual death and universal resurrection.
