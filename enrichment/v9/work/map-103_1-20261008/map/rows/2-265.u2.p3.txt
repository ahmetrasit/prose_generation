2:265/q35 How do modern discussions explain the guiding purpose of scriptural parables and stories? [وَمَثَلُ · rhetoric]
  2:265/q35/p1 Their intended lesson can be moral guidance and reflection, with later writers debating the relation of that lesson to historical detail. — reasons: Rifai reports Taha Husayn's caution about historical inference from shared names; Abduh's account of Qur'anic stories as admonition rather than detailed chronicle; Khalaf Allah's symbolic account likened to parables and the criticism it received; and Iqbal's symbolic reading of Adam. The author calls Khalaf Allah's book pioneering but largely unacceptable and says current studies remain bound by inherited methods instead of al-Khuli's proposed modern perspective. These notes discuss stories generally, not a specific reading of 2:265.
2:265/q36 What role does reflection on natural signs have in modern discussion of Qur'anic interpretation? [جَنَّةٍۭ بِرَبْوَةٍ وَابِلٌۭ طَلٌّۭ · interpretation]
  2:265/q36/p1 Abduh and al-Khuli urge rational reflection, moral learning, and critical examination of inherited interpretations. — reasons: Ykhuli places al-Khuli in Abduh's renewal tradition. Abduh says cosmic signs invite moral lesson, gratitude and thought rather than fixed natural laws; he emphasizes inquiry, reason, evolving beliefs, and practical responses to new cases. Both criticize uncritical imitation; al-Khuli says each generation belongs to its own time, while the author contrasts their judgments on imitators and reports Abduh's list of social and intellectual harms. These notes state general interpretive principles rather than an explicit exposition of 2:265's garden.
