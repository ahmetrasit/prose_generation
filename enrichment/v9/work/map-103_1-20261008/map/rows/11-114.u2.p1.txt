11:114/q12 What are the good deeds that remove bad deeds? [ٱلْحَسَنَٰتِ · referent] (turns on: Whether the definite good deeds refer to the preceding prayer, particular devotions, or the general class of obedience.)
  11:114/q12/p1 The five prescribed prayers, especially with ablution. — reasons: Tabari prefers this from context and recurrent sound prophetic reports; Ibn al-Jawzi prefers the majority and a connected report. Ka'b, Salman and the Prophet compare prayer to washing dirt or shaking leaves; Uthman says each prayer expiates the interval since the prior prayer.
  11:114/q12/p2 All acts of obedience or accepted good deeds, with prayer as a leading example. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan treat specific deeds as examples of general wording. Alusi adduces reports on voluntary affirmation, fasting Arafat, and gratitude. Qushayri lists nearness, remorse, tears, kindness, generosity, good opinion, sincerity and truthfulness as remedies for particular faults. Bursevi ranks knowledge of God, monotheism and resisting the lower self highly.
  11:114/q12/p3 Remembrance formulas: glorification, praise, testimony of divine unity, and magnification. — reasons: Most reports attribute these to Mujahid, with slight variation: Ibn Abi Hatim and Abu Hayyan include reliance on God; Ata calls them the enduring good works. Abu Dharr's report calls the testimony the best good deed; Bursevi transmits the same answer from the Prophet. Al-Itqan cites Abu Dharr's report naming la ilaha illa Allah as the best good deed.
  11:114/q12/p4 Repentance and return to obedience. — reasons: Jishumi prefers Abu Ali's reading: unbelievers can do good without erasure, so repentance is essential; Tabrisi says repentance certainly cancels punishment.
  11:114/q12/p5 The Prophet's and believers' good actions that overcome polytheists' evil actions. — reasons: This interpretation makes the verse a promise of religious victory; Abu Muslim links it to truth crushing falsehood in 21:18.
11:114/q13 How do good deeds remove bad deeds? [إِنَّ ٱلْحَسَنَٰتِ يُذْهِبْنَ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether removal means retrospective expiation, prevention of future wrongdoing, or spiritual transformation.)
  11:114/q13/p1 They expiate the guilt, punishment, or effects of earlier sins. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and Bursevi distinguish expiation from making a past act never occur. Alusi says liability is removed; another explanation has records erased. Tabatabai speaks of traces in believers' souls. Ibn Kathir cites ablution and two-unit prayer reports.
  11:114/q13/p2 Regular good deeds help a person stop committing sins. — reasons: Al-Asamm, Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi cite prayer restraining indecency at 29:45; Elmalili adds that regular prayer cultivates remorse for major sins. Ibn Ashur allows both prevention and expiation.
  11:114/q13/p3 God turns sins into good deeds after covering and forgiving them. — reasons: Yahya ibn Muadh cites 25:70.
  11:114/q13/p4 Only good can remove evil; an evil deed cannot. — reasons: The Prophet says illicit wealth is not accepted as charity; Abu Abdillah rejects charity and pilgrimage funded by officials' illicit earnings.
  11:114/q13/p5 Inward light from prayer and remembrance dispels darkness from the senses and bodily life. — reasons: Qashani describes prayer's light removing sensory residue. Bursevi compares sacred works nurturing spirit to a watered seed multiplied seven hundredfold (2:261); Wasiti uses the light-and-darkness image.
  11:114/q13/p6 Recent good deeds overtake older sins. — reasons: Hasan urges meeting old sins with new deeds; Abu Ubayda says even vast sins can be overcome by a good deed. Al-Itqan records a weak Ibn Abbas report in which the Prophet calls a new good deed after an old sin exceptionally quick and effective.
  11:114/q13/p7 Follow a bad deed with a good one as a general moral instruction. — reasons: The Prophet gives this counsel to Muadh and Abu Dharr, alongside fearing God and treating people well. Al-Itqan cites Abu Dharr's report of the Prophet's instruction to follow a bad deed with an erasing good deed.
11:114/q14 Which sins are removed, and on what conditions? [ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · interpretation] (turns on: How to read the condition about major sins and the scope of divine expiation.)
  11:114/q14/p1 Minor sins are expiated if major sins are avoided. — reasons: The prayer report conditions expiation on avoiding majors. Ibn Ashur cites 53:32 and 4:31. Sunni majority make avoidance a condition of the whole promise, while another group treats majors as excluded from what is erased; Alusi prefers that distinction and argues prayers erase minors even when majors exist. Al-Damghani gives minor sins as a sense of sayyi'at that good deeds remove and cites 46:16 for overlooking them.
  11:114/q14/p2 Minor sins require repentance and nonpersistence for erasure. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya invokes sins falling with ablution water and expert legal theorists; Abu Hayyan concurs. Alusi records disagreement and stresses that expiation does not end the duty to repent.
  11:114/q14/p3 Repentance can expiate major sins. — reasons: Jishumi favors Abu Ali's reading; Tabrisi says punishment is cancelled by repentance without dispute. Al-Subki reports debate over whether forgiveness after repentance for sins is certain or presumed.
  11:114/q14/p4 Some good deeds can expiate major as well as minor sins. — reasons: Al-Sadiq says a later good deed can remove a forgotten grave sin. Al-Dhakha'ir cites promises of forgiveness of past and future sins; Ibn al-Mundhir inclines to this. Alusi answers critics who invoke Murji'ism by retaining the duty of repentance.
  11:114/q14/p5 Faith erases unbelief and at least removes eternal punishment, so believers' sins cannot ultimately harm them. — reasons: Razi reports this argument by its proponents.
  11:114/q14/p6 A major sin may be lightened when an expiatory act finds no minor sins. — reasons: Al-Nawawi presents this as a hope.
  11:114/q14/p7 Avoidance of major sins can itself expiate minor sins at each time, when restraint is intentional. — reasons: Al-Balqini reads 4:31 over a lifetime; Alusi rejects that reading with al-Qadi and Ghazali. Ghazali requires ability and desire to commit the major sin.
  11:114/q14/p8 Punishment for minor sins remains possible despite avoidance of majors. — reasons: Alusi reports Sunni authorities citing 4:48 and 18:49; Mu'tazila deny this from 4:31, which may concern unbelief.
11:114/q15 What happened in the incident linked to this verse? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether the reports describe new revelation or application of an existing verse, and the varying details of the man's act and remedy.)
  11:114/q15/p1 A man had illicit physical contact with a woman short of intercourse, confessed, and the Prophet applied the verse and prayer as expiation. — reasons: Accounts vary: a date seller lured a woman home, a man kissed or touched her backside, or a woman by a pool occasioned temptation. Some say the Prophet waited until revelation; others say Gabriel brought it after Asr. Abu Bakr and Umar advised concealment and repentance. Some reports prescribe thorough ablution and two units, others prayer with the Prophet. Tirmidhi grades Ibn Masud's reports hasan sahih, Abu al-Yusr's hasan gharib with a weak transmitter; Ibn Ashur says Muadh and Abu al-Yusr routes are weak. Ibn Abi Hatim and Durr preserve other versions. Al-Burhan reports a date seller who tried to seduce a buyer; its Ibn Mas'ud report in both Sahihs describes a kiss followed by the Prophet's application of this verse.
  11:114/q15/p2 The pool encounter ended in remorse, with forgiveness sought and four prayer units prescribed. — reasons: Ibn Kathir calls Yahya ibn Ja'da's account mursal. The reports differ on whether the man found a woman by a pool or approached her while she bathed, and whether impotence stopped him.
  11:114/q15/p3 The verse already existed, and the Prophet applied its general rule to the penitent. — reasons: Ibn Ashur cites a Tirmidhi transmission saying the Prophet recited the verse without saying it newly descended; 'revealed' can mean applied to the case.
  11:114/q15/p4 The verse was newly revealed in response to the confession. — reasons: The Prophet's silence or delay until revelation is a recurrent detail; Ibn Ashur notes this wording would make the verse Medinan.
  11:114/q15/p5 The verse descended again when a new incident arose, recalling its existing ruling. — reasons: Al-Burhan says an already revealed verse may recur when a new case prompts it; this explains a report of revelation without requiring the first descent then.
11:114/q16 Who was the man in the confession report? [* · reports] (turns on: Variant transmission of the man's name, kunya, and tribe.)
  11:114/q16/p1 Abu al-Yusr, the Ansari date seller; names given include Amr ibn Ghaziya and Ka'b ibn Amr. — reasons: Mawardi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn Kathir preserve divergent personal names for Abu al-Yusr. Tirmidhi's Abu al-Yusr transmission is reported as hasan gharib, with Qays ibn al-Rabi weakened. Al-Burhan says al-Tirmidhi or another source identifies the confessor as Abu al-Yusr.
