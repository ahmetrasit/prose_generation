## THE CURRENT MAP
35:37/q01 Who are the people crying in the Fire? [وَهُمْ · referent]
  35:37/q01/p1 The disbelievers condemned to Hell, continuing the people of 35:36. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the pronoun to the disbelievers of 35:36 and coordinates this clause with their having the Fire, rather than making it circumstantial; Study Quran likewise describes disbelievers facing the judgment they denied.
35:37/q02 What does يَصْطَرِخُونَ mean? [يَصْطَرِخُونَ · meaning]
  35:37/q02/p1 They cry loudly and intensely for help or rescue in Hell. — reasons: Qatāda and Ibn Jurayj gloss it as seeking help. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bayḍāwī connect the plea to ṣarākh, a loud cry; al-Thaʿlabī says ṣārikh can name either supplicant or helper, while al-Qurṭubī distinguishes ṣārikh from muṣrikh and cites a poem about a frightened caller and hoofbeats. Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the verb's intensity and the sufferers' helplessness. Al-Bursawī also says it is used for seeking intercession. Their following plea explains the cry, according to al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī.
35:37/q03 How is the form يَصْطَرِخُونَ derived? [يَصْطَرِخُونَ · grammar]
  35:37/q03/p1 It is an intensive iftiʿāl form from ṣarākh or ṣurākh, with the form's tāʾ changed to ṭāʾ near emphatic ṣād. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya explain the nearby articulation and emphatic matching; al-Wāḥidī cites iṣṭarakha and taṣārakhū. Al-Bursawī describes the inserted emphatic letter as strengthening the cry. Al-Samīn's note describes the consonant change differently, saying tāʾ changes to ṣād before ṭāʾ. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the intensive form with yaṣrukhūn.
35:37/q04 To whom are their cries for help directed? [يَصْطَرِخُونَ فِيهَا · referent] (turns on: Whether the cry is addressed to God, as the ensuing rabbana indicates, or exchanged among the damned.)
  35:37/q04/p1 They call on God to rescue them. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports Qatāda's account and rejects the suggestion that they call to one another in bewilderment; the address rabbana supports their appeal to God.
  35:37/q04/p2 They call to one another in bewilderment. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī records this suggestion only to reject it.
35:37/q05 What do the condemned ask to do if brought out? [رَبَّنَآ أَخْرِجْنَا نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا · interpretation]
  35:37/q05/p1 They ask to leave Hell, return to worldly life, and replace their former conduct with faith and righteous obedience. — reasons: Muqātil specifies abandoning association of partners; Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the promised deed as professing lā ilāha illā Allāh; al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī specify belief for disbelief and obedience for sin. Al-Bursawī says deeds require faith; al-Qurṭubī adds following messengers. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the promise belated repentance, with an implicit condition of release. Al-Rāzī says a captive progresses from hope, to request, to a promise of reform. Study Quran relates the plea to 32:12, 19:38, 67:11, 40:11, 6:27, 7:53 and 26:102.
35:37/q06 How is the plea beginning رَبَّنَآ attached syntactically? [رَبَّنَآ أَخْرِجْنَا · grammar]
  35:37/q06/p1 An implied verb of saying introduces the quoted plea; it can explain the shouting, stand independently, or describe their state. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies omitted 'they say'; al-Samīn allows it as specification of what is shouted or a circumstantial phrase. Al-Ālūsī also reports coordination with prior speech or an independent explanatory sentence.
35:37/q07 How does نَعْمَلْ depend on أَخْرِجْنَا? [أَخْرِجْنَا نَعْمَلْ · grammar]
  35:37/q07/p1 The jussive 'we shall do' answers the request, implying 'if You bring us out, we shall do good.' — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the jussive as the answer to the plea and the understood condition of release.
35:37/q08 How can صَٰلِحًا غَيْرَ ٱلَّذِى كُنَّا نَعْمَلُ be parsed? [صَٰلِحًا غَيْرَ ٱلَّذِى كُنَّا نَعْمَلُ · grammar] (turns on: Which omitted noun the two accusatives describe, or whether ghayra is the direct object.)
  35:37/q08/p1 Both ṣāliḥan and ghayra qualify an omitted verbal noun or omitted object. — reasons: Al-Samīn gives the two ellipsis options, and al-Ālūsī transmits Abū al-Baqāʾ's parallel parses.
  35:37/q08/p2 Ṣāliḥan qualifies an omitted verbal noun, while ghayra is the object of 'we do.' — reasons: Al-Samīn and Abū al-Baqāʾ, as reported by al-Ālūsī, offer this alternative.
35:37/q09 What does 'other than what we used to do' add to their plea? [غَيْرَ ٱلَّذِى كُنَّا نَعْمَلُ · rhetoric]
  35:37/q09/p1 It heightens their regret and admits that their former deeds were corrupt, even though they once mistook those deeds for good. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn citing al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī explain that the apparent suggestion of other righteous deeds disappears once their mistaken self-assessment is recognized; al-Samīn cites 32:12 and 18:104. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they now know their past deeds were false and harmful. Al-Rāzī says the qualification exposes their former corruption, while al-Biqāʿī allows admission of error or regretful recognition.
35:37/q10 What is the force of the question أَوَلَمْ نُعَمِّرْكُم? [أَوَلَمْ نُعَمِّرْكُم · rhetoric]
  35:37/q10/p1 It answers and rebukes their plea by confirming that God gave them ample time to reflect, repent, and heed warning. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda calls it rhetorical confirmation, not information-seeking; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it censure. Al-Ṭabarī addresses the rebuke to Quraysh; al-Biqāʿī adds delayed punishment and reason, with the past tense closing the register of reflection at Resurrection. Al-Nasafī says longer lives intensify the rebuke. Abū Ḥayyān reports confirmation and reminder, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says taste and no-helper follow from this answer. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr stress that the time allowed any willing person to benefit.
35:37/q11 What does نُعَمِّرْكُم contribute? [نُعَمِّرْكُم · meaning]
  35:37/q11/p1 God prolonged their worldly life enough for reflection and amendment. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr defines taʿmīr as prolonging life, citing 2:96 and 35:11; al-Bursawī says reflection is opportunity to consider one's condition and amend it; al-Jishumī contrasts this worldly opportunity with its absence after death.
35:37/q12 How should مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ be construed? [مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether fīhi requires mā to be a noun, can refer to an implied age, or permits a temporal verbal noun; the negative parse is rejected on wording and sense.)
  35:37/q12/p1 Mā is an indefinite noun meaning a span or age, qualified by the clause 'in which one remembers.' — reasons: Al-Samīn prefers this because fīhi can refer back to a noun. Al-Ālūsī takes it as the years in which anyone willing can reflect; al-Bursawī allows a period or time generally, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause describes adequate time for instruction.
  35:37/q12/p2 Mā is a temporal verbal noun or temporal adverbial, expressing the duration for reflection. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, the Shaykh cited by al-Samīn, and Abū Ḥayyān as reported by al-Ālūsī give this construal; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the related phrase a masdar-based temporal adverb. Al-Samīn objects that fīhi lacks a nominal antecedent, except on al-Akhfash's and Ibn al-Sarrāj's allowance; al-Ālūsī says it may instead refer to implied 'age.'
  35:37/q12/p3 Mā is negative. — reasons: Ibn al-Ḥājib, cited by al-Ālūsī, calls this untenable in both wording and meaning.
35:37/q13 What age or span counts as enough life to remember? [نُعَمِّرْكُم مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ مَن تَذَكَّرَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the phrase identifies a fixed age, the start of mature responsibility, a final age before decline, or a sufficient opportunity; reports differ in numbers, and al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr assess the sixty-year transmissions differently.)
  35:37/q13/p1 Sixty years is sufficient and removes a person's excuse. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī and many others transmit sixty; the Prophet is reported to say God has left no excuse after sixty. Al-Wāḥidī prefers it on Abū Hurayra's report; Ibn Kathīr prefers its sounder Ibn ʿAbbās route and Bukhārī's authentication against al-Ṭabarī's reservation. Reports describe a Resurrection caller asking for sixty-year-olds, though Ibn Kathīr questions one route through Ibrāhīm ibn al-Faḍl. Al-Khaṭṭābī explains sixty as near death and suited to repentance. Sources also give sixty or seventy in a Prophetic wording; al-Bukhārī titles a chapter for sixty. Al-Biqāʿī cites al-Bukhārī's opening of al-Riqāq and Aḥmad ibn Manīʿ; al-Ālūsī cites al-Ḥākim and Sahl ibn Saʿd.
  35:37/q13/p2 Forty years is the significant threshold for mature reflection. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī prefers forty because a sixty-year transmission has a transmitter requiring caution and understanding matures at forty, diminishing before and after. Al-Qurṭubī calls it sound and cites 46:15. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a good view and cites reports about reaching forty without repentance and a poem on forty without modesty. Masrūq urges caution then; Medina worshippers and Mālik's townspeople are reported to shift toward worship at forty. Al-Ḥasan and al-Kalbī also specify this age.
  35:37/q13/p3 Eighteen years suffices for the rebuke. — reasons: Qatāda says an eighteen-year-old was included when the verse was revealed; ʿAṭāʾ, Wahb, Abū al-ʿĀliya, al-Kalbī and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās or al-Bāqir are also cited. Al-Bursawī says duty intensifies around eighteen or twenty.
  35:37/q13/p4 The threshold begins at puberty or mature discernment. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan calls puberty the first period of mature recollection; al-Māwardī and Abū Ḥayyān similarly call it the start of reflection. Elmalılı applies the term to anyone dying after puberty, and Iṣlāḥī says those dying before maturity receive more lenient treatment.
