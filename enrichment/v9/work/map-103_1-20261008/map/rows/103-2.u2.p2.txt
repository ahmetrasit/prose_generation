  103:2/q13/p1 It is Meccan, probably after Sūrah 94. — reasons: Abu Hayyan reports Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr and the majority as Meccan; Ibn Kathir and Qutb affirm it. Tabatabai favors Meccan despite allowing either; Asad and Study Quran place it shortly after al-Sharh.

103:2/q14 What reports describe the sūra's recitation or reception? [* · reports] (turns on: Different reports concern distinct acts of recitation and reception.)
  103:2/q14/p1 Two Companions recited it to one another before parting and then exchanged greetings. — reasons: Reported by Tabarani through Abu Madina in Ibn Kathir and repeated by Elmalili, Qutb and Study Quran; Qutb takes the practice as renewing commitment to its guidance.
  103:2/q14/p2 The Prophet interpreted the sūra's phrases by naming Abu Jahl and four Companions. — reasons: Thalabi transmits Ubayy's recitation to the Prophet and question for interpretation; its reported reply identifies loss with Abu Jahl and belief, deeds, truth and patience with Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali. A sermon attributed to Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Abbas repeats the assignments.
  103:2/q14/p3 Musaylima answered a recitation of al-ʿAsr with an unconvincing parody. — reasons: Ibn Kathir reports Amr ibn al-As reciting it before conversion; Musaylima's response invoked al-wabr, a catlike small animal with notable ears. Ibn Kathir says even an idolater found the parody incoherent and notes al-Khara'iti's version.
103:2/q15 What textual or translation details are noted for the verse? [* · sciences] (turns on: The notes report independent editorial details.)
  103:2/q15/p1 One verse division joins the oath verse to verse 2. — reasons: Spitaler reports that Medina II counts the opening oath and its answer together.
  103:2/q15/p2 The cited German rendering follows Zirker. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum credits Zirker for its translation of verse 2.
103:2/q16 How does affliction relate to human spiritual progress? [* · inward]
  103:2/q16/p1 God uses affliction to draw servants toward Himself, with the best tested first. — reasons: Qashani calls affliction God's whip and ranks those afflicted from prophets through saints and then other excellent people.
103:2/q17 What historical era does the oath and warning evoke? [* · interpretation]
  103:2/q17/p1 The era of Muhammad's mission opens a transformation from widespread ignorance and loss toward guidance. — reasons: Elmalili describes religious disorder and injustice before the mission, the Qur'an's first iqra revelation on the Night of Power, and the upright religion calling people to reform, Paradise and divine approval. He links this transition to al-Bayyina and reads the oath before the warning as meaningful.
103:2/q18 How does al-insān differ from other words for humankind? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · meaning]
  103:2/q18/p1 Al-insān can denote human beings in contrast with wild creatures, while al-nās denotes humanity broadly. — reasons: Bint al-Shati compares Qur'anic use of al-nas with al-ins in contexts paired with jinn, citing 49:13, 13:17 and 59:21.
103:2/q19 What contextual reading of the opening oath is reported? [* · readings]
  103:2/q19/p1 Al-ʿaṣr is read with kasra on the ṣād. — reasons: Qurtubi attributes this vocalization to Salam.
103:2/q20 What claim did opponents make that this verse reverses? [* · reports]
  103:2/q20/p1 Opponents claimed Muhammad was in loss; the divine oath declares the opposite. — reasons: Razi transmits the report as an explanation of the oath's rebuttal.
103:2/q21 How does this verse illustrate Ibn Ashur's account of Qur'anic style? [* · rhetoric]
  103:2/q21/p1 Its broad declaration is precisely qualified by the following exception for believers. — reasons: Ibn Ashur cites 103:2–3 as an example of carefully delimited Qur'anic propositions, unlike loose generalities that may overlook rare cases. He places this in a wider catalogue of Qur'anic innovations: a preserved recited book distinct from poetry and oratory, systematic topics, vivid narratives, reshaped reported speech, and adapted narrative names. Those other items describe his surrounding stylistic account rather than further claims about this verse.
