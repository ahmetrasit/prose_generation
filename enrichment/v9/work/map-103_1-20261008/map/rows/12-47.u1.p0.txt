## THE CURRENT MAP
12:47/q01 What do the seven years of sowing signify in the king's dream? [تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the dream's images primarily forecast agricultural conditions or prescribe the work and storage needed for famine.)
  12:47/q01/p1 Seven fertile years, followed by seven years of drought or famine. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran identify fat cows and green ears with fertile years, lean cows and dry ears with famine. Ibn Kathīr adds rain and explains that cows signify years because they plow fields. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates fatness to fertility, leanness to drought, and ears to provisions; he takes each green ear as one year's fresh food. Al-Qummī's report adds rain after the famine. Al-Biqāʿī connects this interpretation to the two dreams in 12:36 and 12:43.
  12:47/q01/p2 The dream depicts the practical measures needed to survive famine: repeated planting, storing grain in ears, and rationing. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues that fat cows picture plentiful harvests from sustained planting, lean cows shortages that consume saved food, green ears stores, and dry ears unthreshed preservation; the food exception permits necessary consumption rather than interpreting a dream symbol. He expressly rejects reducing the dream to a forecast of fertile and barren years.
12:47/q02 Is تزرعون a prediction or an instruction to sow? [تَزْرَعُونَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the declarative predicts ordinary cultivation or casts cultivation as an emphatic command; the force of the following فذروه is disputed.)
  12:47/q02/p1 It is a command expressed as a declarative statement. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī's Ṣāḥib al-Naẓm, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn's report of al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ālūsī's reported view, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī cite the following explicit command فذروه; al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī say report form makes compliance emphatic or certain. Al-Rāzī adduces 2:228 and 2:233; al-Kashshāf and Ṭabāṭabāʾī adduce 61:11. Al-Wāḥidī compares prayer in present form at 23:118. Al-Ālūsī reports a grammatical objection to coordinating an imperative harvest clause with a declarative forecast and replies that both clauses may be read as guidance.
  12:47/q02/p2 It predicts their customary seven years of farming; the storage instruction then gives new advice. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān prefers the apparent prediction, mentioning irrigation, and denies that the later imperative proves an earlier command. Al-Samīn says sowing is already habitual but storing in ears needs instruction; he reports al-Shaykh's same objection. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the familiar farming forecast prepares for the counsel. Al-Kashf, preferred by al-Ālūsī, reads the storage clause as urgent advice interrupting a forecast; al-Ālūsī also reports answers to the coordination objection: independent advice, response to an implied condition, or an imperative understood as report.
12:47/q03 How do زرع and حرث differ? [تَزْرَعُونَ · meaning]
  12:47/q03/p1 حرث is sowing and preparing soil; زرع covers tending and growing a crop and can also include sowing. — reasons: Bursevī says زرع is broader, while God causes growth, citing 56:64.
12:47/q04 Why can Joseph announce the seven years without saying 'if God wills'? [تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ · theology] (turns on: Whether his speech is divinely certain, implicitly qualified, or an instruction.)
  12:47/q04/p1 He knew it by revelation. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī gives this as one answer; al-Wāḥidī reports direct revelation as a possible source for Joseph's interpretation.
  12:47/q04/p2 The interpretation God taught him was certainly true, leaving no doubt. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī gives certainty from God-taught interpretation as a second answer; al-Wāḥidī records the knowledge-versus-revelation question.
  12:47/q04/p3 He intended an unspoken exception. — reasons: Ibn al-Anbārī, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, compares Joseph's brothers' promise to bring their brother.
  12:47/q04/p4 His wording is a command rather than an uncertain forecast. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī gives the imperative force of 'cultivate for seven years' as a fourth answer.
12:47/q05 What does دأبا mean here? [دَأَبًۭا · meaning] (turns on: Whether the word emphasizes habitual continuity, labor, or succession of the years; many notes combine these senses.)
  12:47/q05/p1 Continuous cultivation according to habitual practice. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr combine continuity with customary action. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya cite Imruʾ al-Qays's verse about habitual conduct; al-Qurṭubī cites ka-daʾbika. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects usage to 3:11; al-Rāghib, reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, starts from continued travel and cites 14:33 and 3:11. Al-Samīn points to his treatment of 3:11. Ibn ʿAbbās and others stress successive years.
  12:47/q05/p2 Persistent, industrious effort at sowing. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī give diligent work alongside customary practice. Ibn Khalawayh cites al-Kumayt's line about persistent travel; al-Zajjāj links the word with perseverance. Al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report effort as an alternative nuance; Bursevī joins diligence to continuous farming.
  12:47/q05/p3 Seven consecutive years of planting. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly interprets it as consecutive years; al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī transmit this application, with al-Ṭabrisī adding the people's usual agricultural practice.
12:47/q06 How is دأبا read? [دَأَبًۭا · readings] (turns on: Which transmitted vocalization or recitation context is followed, and whether fatḥa is explained by dialect or guttural easing.)
  12:47/q06/p1 With a quiescent hamza. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī and Ibn al-Jawzī list Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim; al-Fārisī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Rāzī call it commoner. Ibn Khalawayh prefers it by analogy with the reading about Pharaoh's people at 8:54. He distinguishes a verbal noun with sukūn from a noun with fatḥa; other sources say the two are dialectal or both verbal nouns. Qiraat-ER says the vowel change leaves meaning intact.
  12:47/q06/p2 With fatḥa on the hamza. — reasons: Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim is repeatedly named; Ibn Mujāhid also reports Mūsā al-Zābī transmitting fatḥa, and al-Fārisī a fatḥa transmission from ʿĀṣim. Al-Qurṭubī reports Abū Ḥātim's attribution to Yaʿqūb and derivation from daʾiba, alongside al-Naḥḥās's objection that linguists know only daʾb. Al-Farrāʾ and reported Kufans permit vowel opening with a guttural, while al-Zajjāj reports Basrans limiting this to attested forms. Al-Fārisī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Rāzī call it a dialect form like shamʿ and nahr variants; Ibn Khalawayh suggests easing a guttural environment. Qiraat-ER records full vocalization and tanwīn.
  12:47/q06/p3 Abū ʿAmr drops or eases the hamza in connected recitation. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī give this connected-flow detail; Qiraat-ER calls the hamza-less variant phonological simplification.
  12:47/q06/p4 At a pause it is pronounced dābā, resuming as daʾban. — reasons: Qiraat-ER specifies the pausal and resumed forms.
12:47/q07 How is دأبا governed grammatically? [دَأَبًۭا · grammar] (turns on: What accusative construction is supplied or understood, and whether it describes the work, cultivators, or years.)
  12:47/q07/p1 It is an accusative verbal noun governed by an omitted verb meaning 'work persistently'. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, Sībawayh and Abū Ḥayyān supply تدأبون, prompted by تزرعون; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī allow an understood verb. Al-Qurṭubī calls the usage figurative because sowing entails habitual perseverance.
  12:47/q07/p2 It is a verbal noun attached to تزرعون through the sense of diligent work. — reasons: Al-Mubarrad, reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān, makes the farming verb govern it by meaning. Al-Samīn objects that this is not a specific manner like sitting القرفصاء; al-Ālūsī likewise rejects al-Mubarrad's analogy.
  12:47/q07/p3 It is a circumstantial or adverbial expression describing the cultivators' persistent or customary work. — reasons: Other grammarians in al-Wāḥidī, al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give this parse. Al-Kashshāf allows verbal noun used adverbially or as ḥāl. Al-Samīn lists an intensive absolute noun, an adjective slot, omitted دائبين, or metaphorical identity as analyses of the ḥāl.
  12:47/q07/p4 It qualifies the seven years as continuous. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports this alternative adjective analysis of the seven years.
12:47/q08 What can ما in فما حصدتم be? [فَمَا حَصَدتُّمْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether ما sets a condition or refers to the harvested grain.)
  12:47/q08/p1 A conditional particle: whatever you harvest, leave it. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this parse.
  12:47/q08/p2 A relative pronoun: what you have harvested, leave it. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this alternative.
12:47/q09 Why leave the harvest في سنبله? [فَذَرُوهُ فِى سُنۢبُلِهِۦٓ · interpretation]
  12:47/q09/p1 Storing unthreshed grain in its ear preserves it longer, especially from insects and spoilage. — reasons: Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās name weevils or pests; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and many later commentators give preservation as the purpose. Ibn Zayd says grain still in its ear is not eaten. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say Egyptian wheat otherwise will not last two years and advise using older stores first. Al-Ṭabrisī reports that insects avoid grain in its ear. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī suggest the dream's green ears prompted the method; Iṣlāḥī specifies termites. The report of al-Ṣādiq prescribes seven years in the ears.
