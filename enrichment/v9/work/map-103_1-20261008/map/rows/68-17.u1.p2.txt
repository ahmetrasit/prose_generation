68:17/q22 What became of the owners afterward? [* · reports] (turns on: Reports state different degrees of restoration or leave the final outcome unanswered.)
  68:17/q22/p1 They repented and were given a better garden. — reasons: Mujāhid reports this; al-Qushayrī says another account has God replace the garden, followed by a renewed secret harvest plan.
  68:17/q22/p2 They became sincere and received al-Ḥayawān, with grape clusters requiring a donkey to carry. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī transmits this from Ibn Masʿūd.
  68:17/q22/p3 Their ultimate destination is left unsettled. — reasons: When asked whether they were of Paradise or Hell, Qatāda said the question burdened him.
68:17/q23 How does this parable connect to its sūra and nearby verses? [* · links] (turns on: The sources relate the parable to nearby deniers, shared responsibility and ultimate punishment.)
  68:17/q23/p1 It continues the warning against proud, wealthy deniers while consoling the Prophet and urging patience. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr links the new parable to wealth and children in 68:14; Elmalılı links the oath to the earlier frequent swearer. Ṭabāṭabāʾī summarizes the sūra's reply to accusations of madness, praise of the Prophet's character, rejection of compromise and command to await God's judgment. The Cave and Qarun stories supply parallels.
  68:17/q23/p2 The plot shows the shared wealth and joint responsibility implied by plural owners. — reasons: Elmalılı contrasts the single owner in al-Kahf 18:35 and treats the oath as the founding agreement of a communal enterprise.
  68:17/q23/p3 The worldly punishment foreshadows a greater punishment in the afterlife. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī makes the explicit comparison.
68:17/q24 When were the garden verses revealed? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether verses 17–33 share the sūra's early Meccan setting and how that bears on Badr.)
  68:17/q24/p1 The sūra is early Meccan, but the garden passage may have been revealed separately in Medina. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds the sūra's overall Meccan setting clear but judges a Medinan origin for the seventeen garden verses plausible. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ excepts 17–33 and 48–50 from the early Meccan sūra and notes the traditional placement after al-ʿAlaq (96:1), despite ordering disputes.
  68:17/q24/p2 The garden warning can be read with the preceding passage and Badr as a later worldly demonstration. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes this conditional on joint revelation; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects reading a Badr event into the passage because Badr came about five years afterward.
68:17/q25 How does the preceding threat of a mark relate to the parable's warning? [* · links]
  68:17/q25/p1 A report sees the mark on the nose as humiliation on Judgment Day for one who showed arrogance. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this reading and mentions 3:106; the report belongs to the preceding threat, which the orchard warning follows.
