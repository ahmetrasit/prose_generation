  103:1/q14/p1 Two Companions recited it to one another on meeting and exchanged greetings before parting. — reasons: Tabarani's report via Abu Madina is cited by Ibn Kathir; Alusi, Elmalılı, Qutb, Kur'an Yolu and Study Quran repeat it. Qutb says it renewed commitment to the guidance.
  103:1/q14/p2 Before Islam, ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ recited the brief sūra to Musaylima, whose parody failed. — reasons: Ibn Kathir describes al-wabr as a small catlike animal with notable ears, says the parody impressed no one, and notes al-Khara'iti transmitted a close version.

103:1/q15 Who do a reported interpretation and pulpit recitation identify with the sūra's clauses? [* · reports]
  103:1/q15/p1 Abu Jahl is the person in loss; Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali correspond successively to faith, good works, truth and patience. — reasons: Tha'labi attributes the identification to the Prophet and reports Ali ibn Abdullah ibn Abbas repeating it from the Prophet's pulpit.
103:1/q16 What does the sūra's opening suggest about its literary form? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the oath's prayer-time association produces a hymn-like effect.)
  103:1/q16/p1 It is a single-member oath followed by a rebuke, with three rhymes. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum describes the structure; Neuwirth supports keeping the oath as an independent verse. Al-Zarkashi groups it with fifteen oath openings; al-Suyuti describes the specific oath-plus-human-condition pattern. Al-Zarkashi also notes the short opening's rhyme analogy with al-Fajr and al-Ḍuḥā.
  103:1/q16/p2 Oaths by prayer times preserve liturgical experience and give a hymn-like tone. — reasons: Neuwirth proposes this reading.
  103:1/q16/p3 The sūra has no hymn-like resonance. — reasons: The Corpus Coranicum commentator objects to Neuwirth and refers to critical discussion at 93:1–2.
  103:1/q16/p4 Across its three verses, the sūra uniquely has ten wāws among three-verse sūras. — reasons: Al-Suyuti cites Ibn Khalawayh's letter-count observation through the end of al-ʿAṣr.
103:1/q17 What does the sūra's basmala imply? [* · interpretation]
  103:1/q17/p1 Everything perishes except God, whose mercy reaches all and whose complete gifts mark His chosen allies. — reasons: Biqai gives this reading in two parallel entries.
103:1/q18 Why is the loss described as grave? [* · interpretation]
  103:1/q18/p1 The servant sins against the Lord despite immense benefaction; the indefinite form can mark a distinctive loss. — reasons: Bursevi offers both the moral and rhetorical explanations.
103:1/q19 Does the sūra contain both abrogating and abrogated material? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether the four-part classification of sūras by abrogation is sound.)
  103:1/q19/p1 It is listed among sūras containing both abrogating and abrogated material. — reasons: Al-Zarkashi reports al-ʿAṣr in a list of thirty-one such sūras.
  103:1/q19/p2 The four-part classification behind this list is open to question. — reasons: Al-Suyuti questions that classification and points to later discussion, without specifying an abrogating or abrogated passage here.
103:1/q20 How is the transition from al-ʿAṣr to the next sūra recited? [* · readings] (turns on: The transmitted practice of a reader who ordinarily joins or pauses between sūras.)
  103:1/q20/p1 Some readers who generally join sūras make a brief pause at this transition. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari reports a special sakt among several Hamza, Abu ʿAmr, Ibn ʿAmir, Yaʿqub and Warsh readers. He explains that separating the ending wa-tawāṣaw bi-l-ṣabr from the next opening wayl avoids a jarring joined phrase; special sakt presupposes joining elsewhere.
  103:1/q20/p2 Some readers who generally pause insert the basmala at this transition. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari reports this practice from Ibn Ghalbun, the Hidaya author, Makki and others; it separates the same jarring phrase, and the special basmala presupposes pausing elsewhere.
  103:1/q20/p3 Most authorities give this transition no special treatment, and joiners do not insert an untransmitted basmala. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari names Faris ibn Ahmad, Ibn Sufyan, Abu al-Tahir, Iraqi authorities and al-Dani among those making no distinction. He says an optional basmala cannot override explicit transmission for readers who join and that special treatments follow the reader's general joining or pausing practice.
103:1/q21 Where does al-ʿAṣr stand in the reported codex order of Ibn Masʿūd? [* · sciences]
  103:1/q21/p1 It appears among the mufassal sūras after al-Fajr. — reasons: Al-Suyuti reports this order for Ibn Masʿūd's codex.
103:1/q22 What principle determines whether the opening of al-ʿAṣr is a separate āya? [وَٱلْعَصْرِ · sciences] (turns on: Whether verse boundaries can be inferred by analogy and form or require transmitted specification.)
  103:1/q22/p1 An āya is a marked, separate unit of Qur'anic speech, understood through its boundaries or function as a sign. — reasons: Al-Suyuti relays al-Jaʿbari's sentence-based definition, another scholar's separated-portion definition, and explanations that the name āya marks a break or signifies proof of the messenger's truth.
  103:1/q22/p2 The actual verse boundaries are fixed by revelation and prophetic transmission, not inferred from definitions or analogy. — reasons: Al-Wahidi says a general definition could include less than a full verse but revelation fixed the divisions; others and al-Zamakhshari insist on transmitted specification. Al-Suyuti also cites a report about thirty verses in al-Ahqaf as evidence used in verse-counting discussion.
  103:1/q22/p3 Rhyme and analogy with other one-word openings support counting wa-l-ʿaṣr separately. — reasons: Al-Zarkashi compares it with al-Fajr and al-Ḍuḥā despite their differing lengths; he also notes that a word can be a complete single utterance even when short.
103:1/q23 What does the general day-night pattern of revelation say about this sūra's timing? [* · sciences]
  103:1/q23/p1 Most Qur'anic revelation occurred by day; this report gives no specific time of day for al-ʿAṣr. — reasons: Al-Suyuti relays Ibn Habib's general statement and notes that examples of night revelation were collected separately.
