0/p1 It follows al-Layl's most God-fearing figure with the Prophet and God's favors to him. — reasons: Jishumi links earlier reward to the Prophet's resurrection honor; Abu Hayyan and Alusi cite 92:17. Biqa'i makes enduring favor through perfected qualities the theme and daylight its sign.
  93:1/q20/p2 Sūras 93 and 94 are a pair moving from opposition toward success. — reasons: Islahi distinguishes intense Meccan opposition from emerging success; Study Quran distinguishes external needs here from spiritual relief in 94, both recalling favor and duties.
  93:1/q20/p3 Al-Layl concerns Abu Bakr; this sūra concerns the Messenger, with no intervening sūra. — reasons: Alusi reports the Imam's inference of closeness, while denying precedence implies superiority; he rejects the Imam's flower metaphor.
  93:1/q20/p4 Sūras 93 and 94 may once have been a single sūra. — reasons: Study Quran reports Companions reciting them in one prayer cycle without an intervening basmala.
93:1/q21 What is the sūra's central assurance? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether it is framed as specific consolation or a broad account of perfected piety.)
  93:1/q21/p1 God has not abandoned the Prophet and will keep favoring and consoling him. — reasons: Qutb describes hope, closeness and calm amid persecution, future reward, and past care as proof. Study Quran adds care for the vulnerable and proclamation of God's gifts. Ibn Ashur says past care proves continuing care; Islahi traces better times and duties; Jishumi includes honor at resurrection. Itqan says the day-and-night oath attests God's favor and honor of the Prophet, and thereby affirms his prophethood, resurrection, and recompense.
  93:1/q21/p2 The most God-fearing remains in divine favor through perfected qualities. — reasons: Biqa'i identifies this purpose and reads the daylight title as spiritual light of perfected character.
93:1/q22 What reading and wording are reported in the following reassurance? [* · readings] (turns on: Whether the verb is doubled or lightly pronounced; the light verb's rarity is relevant.)
  93:1/q22/p1 The standard recitation doubles the consonant in mā waddaʿaka. — reasons: Tabrisi sets it against a noncanonical light-consonant reading.
  93:1/q22/p2 A noncanonical reading lightens the consonant in mā wadaʿaka. — reasons: Tabrisi attributes it to the Prophet and Urwa. Ibn Jinni calls the light verb rare; Sibawayh says Arabic ordinarily uses taraka and cites a line by Abu al-Aswad.
93:1/q23 How short is this verse among Qur'anic verses? [* · sciences]
  93:1/q23/p1 It is a one-word verse and among the shortest verses. — reasons: Burhan defines a word as one utterance of two to ten letters and counts al-Duha, like al-Fajr and al-Asr, as a one-word verse. He ranks al-Duha and then al-Fajr as shortest, rejecting shorter claims for mudhammatan and thumma nazar on their letter or word counts; he contrasts the 128-word, 540-letter debt verse as longest.
