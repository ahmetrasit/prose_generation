## THE CURRENT MAP
12:36/q01 How does the verse indicate Joseph's imprisonment? [وَدَخَلَ ٱلسِّجْنَ · rhetoric]
  12:36/q01/p1 It leaves his imprisonment unstated and implies it through the youths' entry with him. — reasons: Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Razi, Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi identify the omitted prior event, 'they imprisoned him'; Ibn al-Jawzi relays al-Zajjaj.
  12:36/q01/p2 The wording itself shows Joseph was already imprisoned. — reasons: Al-Zajjaj says entry into prison with him establishes his imprisonment; al-Wahidi reports the inference.
12:36/q02 What does 'with him' say about when the youths entered? [مَعَهُ · grammar] (turns on: Whether maʿa requires concurrence when the entry began.)
  12:36/q02/p1 They entered prison together with Joseph at the same time. — reasons: Al-Zamakhshari and al-Nasafi take maʿa as newly begun companionship; Abu Hayyan says it denotes a new shared event; al-Biqaʿi cites him. Al-Alusi prefers concurrence as the original sense, while comparing 27:44.
  12:36/q02/p2 The wording allows different times; it only establishes shared confinement. — reasons: Ibn ʿAtiyya allows simultaneous or separate admission. Al-Qurtubi allows before, after or together and says the verse only requires the same room.
12:36/q03 Why place 'with him' before 'prison,' and what might the wording imply? [مَعَهُ ٱلسِّجْنَ · rhetoric] (turns on: The force of maʿa and the emphasis conveyed by word order.)
  12:36/q03/p1 The order emphasizes companionship, with Joseph as the focus. — reasons: Al-Alusi says the object precedes the subject for emphasis and companionship precedes prison because it matters more; he cites 18:62. He distinguishes ongoing Islam from punctual entry and invokes 27:44.
  12:36/q03/p2 Their entry with him may have made imprisonment easier through his presence. — reasons: Al-Alusi reports this possibility.
  12:36/q03/p3 The expression associates Joseph with their prison state while leaving his choice of it unstated. — reasons: Al-Alusi reports this explanation and observes that maʿa normally implies concurrence from the action's beginning.
  12:36/q03/p4 'With' can express joining another in obedience rather than exact simultaneous action. — reasons: Al-Kashf's comparison is the Queen's 'I submitted with Solomon' (27:44): she enters submission to God and the Messenger following prophetic guidance, despite earlier religious self-understanding.
12:36/q04 How is 'prison' pronounced and understood here? [ٱلسِّجْنَ · readings] (turns on: The vocalization and whether the noun denotes place or act.)
  12:36/q04/p1 With kasra, it means the place of confinement. — reasons: Ibn ʿAshur says all readers use kasra because one enters a place, not the act of imprisoning.
  12:36/q04/p2 With fatḥa, al-sajn denotes the prison location. — reasons: Al-Alusi gives this vocalization in explaining the clause after implied 'they imprisoned him.'
12:36/q05 Why are the prisoners called 'two youths'? [فَتَيَانِ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the designation concerns age, service status or their mutual conduct.)
  12:36/q05/p1 Fatā can designate an enslaved man or servant regardless of age. — reasons: Al-Zajjaj allows young or old slaves; al-Mawardi says slaves are so called; al-Qushayri also permits free servants; Ibn ʿAtiyya allows literal youth, slave, or free servant. Ibn al-Anbari ties it to being owned. Al-Tabataba'i takes them as young male slaves.
  12:36/q05/p2 They are called youths because neither tried to outdo the other's claim and each deferred to his companion. — reasons: Al-Tustari gives this ethical explanation.
12:36/q06 What is the form of 'two youths'? [فَتَيَانِ · grammar]
  12:36/q06/p1 It is the dual of fatā, a weak-y noun. — reasons: Al-Nahhas identifies the dual; al-Qurtubi notes the irregular form al-futu.
12:36/q07 Who were the two prisoners and whom did they serve? [فَتَيَانِ · referent] (turns on: Whether external reports are used to fill the Quran's unnamed roles.)
  12:36/q07/p1 They were the Egyptian king's baker or food steward and cupbearer or wine steward. — reasons: Qatada, Ibn Abbas, al-Suddi and many commentators assign food and drink duties; the wine vision belongs to the cupbearer and bread vision to the baker. Ibn ʿAshur says the Quran leaves offences unspecified; Asad cites the Bible for the offices; Islahi stresses that the Quran does not identify them. Al-Razi says Joseph could infer royal service from their later use of rabb for their master in 12:41-42.
  12:36/q07/p2 The Quran leaves their identities unnamed. — reasons: Islahi states this limitation explicitly; Asad says the Bible identifies the offices without conflict with the Quran.
12:36/q08 What names are reported for the king and the two servants? [فَتَيَانِ · reports] (turns on: The transmitted personal names differ beyond spelling variants.)
  12:36/q08/p1 Ibn Ishaq's pair is Majlath or Mujlith and Nabu or Nabwa; the latter serves drink. — reasons: The names vary in spelling: Mujlith/Majlath/Mujlab and Nabu/Nabwa/Banu/Bunū. King-name order varies: al-Rayyan ibn al-Walid or al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan; Baghawi adds ibn Sharwan. Al-Suhayli could not retain the baker's name.
  12:36/q08/p2 The servants were Sharham Aqam and Sharham Asham. — reasons: Muqatil gives these fuller names.
  12:36/q08/p3 The cupbearer was Manja and the baker Majlath. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi cites Kaʿb via al-Thaʿlabi.
  12:36/q08/p4 They were Sharham and Sarham. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi cites al-Naqqash.
12:36/q09 Why were the royal servants imprisoned? [وَدَخَلَ مَعَهُ ٱلسِّجْنَ فَتَيَانِ · reports] (turns on: Whether the report describes suspicion alone, an actual poisoned meal, or reciprocal accusation.)
  12:36/q09/p1 The baker was suspected of trying to poison the king, and the cupbearer of complicity. — reasons: Al-Suddi reports suspicion, not proven execution; several later accounts repeat it. Asad says the Quran does not specify their offences; Ibn ʿAshur notes royal anger and an unnamed poisoning report.
  12:36/q09/p2 Both were implicated in a planned poisoning, but the cupbearer withdrew while the baker accepted and poisoned food. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi and Baghawi describe bribery, warning, animal test and the poisoned meal; al-Qurtubi and Bursevi relay the same basic split.
  12:36/q09/p3 Conspirators pushed both to poison food and drink and the two accused one another. — reasons: Kuranyolu gives this report.
12:36/q10 What happened to Joseph on entering prison? [وَدَخَلَ مَعَهُ ٱلسِّجْنَ · reports] (turns on: Different reports emphasize public punishment or Joseph's reception inside prison.)
  12:36/q10/p1 He was paraded in chains on a donkey and chose this hardship over Hell. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi attributes the report to Wahb and others; it frames the punishment as a consequence of refusing his mistress.
  12:36/q10/p2 He encouraged hopeless prisoners, won their affection, and was offered favorable quarters. — reasons: Qatada and later reports describe counsel about patience, reward, purification and hope; the keeper could improve confinement but not free him. Al-Durr also reports a severe reply from Joseph to the keeper about the Fire's mountains.
12:36/q11 What did Joseph say when people in prison loved him? [فَتَيَانِ · reports]
  12:36/q11/p1 He warned the two men or jailer that love for him had previously brought hardship. — reasons: Versions name his aunt's love and theft accusation, his father's love and the well, and the Aziz's wife's love and prison; some omit the aunt. The men nevertheless persisted; al-Mawardi adds that Joseph wanted only his Lord's love.
12:36/q12 How did Joseph identify himself in prison? [* · reports]
  12:36/q12/p1 He named his lineage through Jacob, Isaac, and Abraham. — reasons: Qatada's account, al-Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan and al-Durr preserve the lineage; Abu Hayyan adds epithets 'God's chosen,' 'sacrificed one' and 'friend.' The keeper offered what treatment he could.
12:36/q13 How did the two men come to ask Joseph for interpretation? [قَالَ أَحَدُهُمَآ نَبِّئْنَا · reports] (turns on: Whether their approach followed a deliberate test or genuine distress and observed trust.)
  12:36/q13/p1 Joseph had announced or demonstrated that he could interpret dreams, and they tested him. — reasons: Al-Suddi, Ibn Masud and others say the men feigned dreams; al-Tabari says they first asked about his knowledge. Abu Hayyan says he told prisoners his skill, while al-Razi allows prior demonstration. Study Quran reports they had seen him interpret others.
  12:36/q13/p2 They were troubled or curious about unusual dreams and trusted his knowledge and piety. — reasons: Ibn Abbas reports troubled waking; al-Razi says they may have mentioned their sadness; Islahi says they had observed his knowledge and piety. Ibn ʿAshur says prisoners exchanged dreams for conversation and hoped for signs of release; Egyptian interpretation was familiar.
12:36/q14 Did both prisoners actually have the dreams they recounted? [إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰنِىٓ وَقَالَ ٱلْءَاخَرُ إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰنِىٓ · reports] (turns on: The transmitted accounts conflict; requests for interpretation and arānī are offered as evidence for genuine dreams.)
  12:36/q14/p1 Both truly dreamed. — reasons: Ibn Ishaq and Mujahid report actual dreams, and Ibn Abbas describes troubled waking; al-Zajjaj and al-Wahidi argue that requesting interpretation implies dreams because waking sight needs none. Al-Razi adds that arānī would otherwise be unnecessary. Ibn Kathir prefers this as the majority view.

Al-Durr gives the subsequent interpretations and request to remember Joseph. Baghawi notes contrary reports.
