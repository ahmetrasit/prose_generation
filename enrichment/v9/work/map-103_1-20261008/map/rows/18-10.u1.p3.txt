18:10/q31 Why was the Cave story related to the Prophet? [* · reports]
  18:10/q31/p1 Quraysh tested him with questions obtained from Jewish scholars. — reasons: Abu Hayyan names al-Nadr and Uqba and questions about the youths, a world traveler and the spirit; another report says answering two while withholding one would confirm prophethood. Kuranyolu says Meccans heard the story from Jewish scholars in Medina. Al-Sadiq in Tabatabai says three envoys went to Najran and asked also about Moses' companion, Dhul-Qarnayn and the Hour.
18:10/q32 What larger themes does the account serve? [* · links] (turns on: The story bears on resurrection, faith under persecution and the sura's presentation of revelation.)
  18:10/q32/p1 It demonstrates resurrection and the coming Hour. — reasons: Kuranyolu says long sleep and awakening make these ideas easier to grasp; the townspeople's recognition is part of the instructive narrative.
  18:10/q32/p2 It supports faith and places this sign within the sura's larger praise of revelation. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says protection supports their faith; Abu Hayyan links this sura's praise of the upright warning Quran, its warning against claims of a divine son and its promise to believers, with the preceding sura's truth of revelation and humility at 17:105.
18:10/q33 How should the many historical reports about the cave be treated? [* · reports]
  18:10/q33/p1 Their particulars diverge, while the Quran selects the details needed for guidance. — reasons: Tabatabai lists disagreements on rulers, date, number, dog, names and titles; he attributes them to widespread Christian and Jewish storytelling and Muslim collection of outside reports via converts such as Wahb and Ka'b, later elevated to prophetic reports. The Quran omits ancestry, occupations, locations and names because guidance does not depend on them. Kuranyolu calls number guesses baseless; Christian reports differ on number, dog and sleep duration, and parallels occur in Indian, Talmudic and Christian stories.
18:10/q34 What is the adjacent verse's “raqim”? [* · links] (turns on: The word may derive from writing, but a transmitted uncertainty leaves a building possible.)
  18:10/q34/p1 It is something written or inscribed, perhaps a book or a stone tablet of the youths' story. — reasons: Ibn Kathir prefers Ibn Jarir's derivation from inscription, comparing qatil and jarih. Ibn Abbas and Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd say book, the latter citing 83:9; Sa'id ibn Jubayr describes a stone tablet at the cave entrance.
  18:10/q34/p2 It may be a building rather than a book; its meaning was uncertain to Ibn Abbas. — reasons: Ibn Abbas says he knew the Quran except hannan, awwah and raqim, and through Ikrima says he cannot decide between book and building.
18:10/q35 What does “cave” mean and who are its companions? [ٱلْكَهْفِ · meaning]
  18:10/q35/p1 It is a large, broad mountain cave, associated with the long-sleeping youths. — reasons: Kuranyolu supplies the lexical size and says the title refers to people said to sleep there for years and awaken; Ibn Ashur says their refuge became their dwelling.
18:10/q36 What effect does dropping the hamza after “when” have? [إِذْ · readings]
  18:10/q36/p1 It smooths liaison without changing the meaning. — reasons: Qiraat-ER describes elision after the dhāl of idh.
18:10/q37 What is the status of the report about reciting Surat al-Kahf? [* · reports]
  18:10/q37/p1 A report promises light for reciting its beginning and end, and greater light for the whole sura. — reasons: Thalabi transmits it from Muadh as a saying of the Prophet: head-to-foot light for the ends and earth-to-heaven light for the whole.
