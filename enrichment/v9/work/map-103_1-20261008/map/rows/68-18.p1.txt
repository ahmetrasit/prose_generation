[TABATABAI:68:17-21/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The garden owners agreed at night to harvest in the morning; not making an exception means they relied on themselves and outward causes without acknowledging God's will.
[TABATABAI:68:20-28/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Another possible sense of not making an exception is that they set aside no share of the fruit for the poor.
[ASAD-NOTES:68:14#n12/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | They planned without saying 'if God wills'; this is the parable's first lesson and links it to the question about their wealth in vv.14-15. | mentions 68:14, 68:15
[BINTSHATI:v2p63/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | The apparent story is that the garden owners planned to harvest everything at dawn and leave nothing; the author rejects the gloss that they merely omitted saying in shāʾ Allāh.
[BINTSHATI:v2p63/r9] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | reports | Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p432/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | Although exegetes unanimously take the phrase to mean they omitted ‘if God wills,’ Islahi rejects that reading: the construction is unsuitable, and istithnāʾ is not explicit enough without contextual evidence.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p432/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | prefers | He reads istithnāʾ literally: they swore to take all the fruit and leave none for the poor, rejecting a customary charitable gleaning practice also stated in Leviticus.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p432/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The oath about leaving nothing emphasizes the same stinginess as Quraysh leaders’ forbidding good, especially Abū Lahab and his associates.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:68:17/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu | reports | Most commentators say the garden owners resolved what they would do the next day without saying 'if God wills.'
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:68:17/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bazı yorumlar | reports | An alternative interpretation is that they made no provision for the poor's share.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:68:17/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | When intending future action, people should say inshaallah because human will and power alone cannot make an event occur; God's will is also necessary. | mentions 18:23, 18:24, 31:34
[STUDYQURAN:68:17/r2] STUDYQURAN · Bg, IK, Z | reports | “Made no exception” can mean the brothers did not say “If God wills,” showing they relied on themselves rather than God.
[STUDYQURAN:68:17/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The brothers’ failure to make an exception indicates self-reliance rather than trust in God.
[STUDYQURAN:68:17/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Alternatively, “made no exception” can mean that they left no part of the harvest for the poor.
