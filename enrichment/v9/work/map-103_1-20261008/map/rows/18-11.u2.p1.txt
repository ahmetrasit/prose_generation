18:11/q09 Where was the cave thought to be? [ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports] (turns on: Whether archaeology and geography can identify a proposed cave.)
  18:11/q09/p1 Rujayb in Jordan is the strongest proposed fit, though no cave is conclusively identified. — reasons: Tabataba'i favors the excavated Rujayb site for its signs and some Muslim reports, while also saying none is proved. He notes Amman occupied ancient Philadelphia under Roman control until the Muslim conquest.
  18:11/q09/p2 Other proposals include caves near Damascus, Petra, Scandinavia, and Nakhchivan. — reasons: Tabataba'i lists Mount Qasiyun, Petra, a Scandinavian cave with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and Nakhchivan. He says Scandinavian and Caucasus proposals conflict with a Roman setting and the limits of Roman power.
18:11/q10 Were the youths alive during their long sleep? [فَضَرَبْنَا سِنِينَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the condition is ordinary sleep, a sleep-like death, or an intermediate state.)
  18:11/q10/p1 They remained alive, asleep, and protected; they had not died. — reasons: Mawardi says the stopped hearing indicates sleep rather than death. Tabrisi says they slept safely after their prayer. Razi says they survived 309 years unharmed and shielded from sun, citing 18:17–18. Tabataba'i argues that a small counted span fits the passage because the sleepers were not dead. Study Quran attributes the same sleep-not-death conclusion to Tabarsi.
  18:11/q10/p2 God had taken their souls in a death resembling sleep. — reasons: Tha'labi says this while recounting the king's plan to seal them in alive.
  18:11/q10/p3 Their centuries-long sleep represents an intermediate realm between life and death. — reasons: Study Quran compares it with the state between individual death and universal resurrection.
18:11/q11 How long did they sleep? [سِنِينَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the notes state only a counted span or identify it with 309 years in 18:25.)
  18:11/q11/p1 The stay lasted 309 years. — reasons: Muqatil and Mujahid give 309; Razi calls it 309 years of protected sleep. Qurtubi reports reading 18:25's 300 plus nine as specifying this verse's duration. Bursevi and Study Quran also connect the span with 309; Study Quran notes 18:19 says they felt it as a day or part of one.
  18:11/q11/p2 The verse states a counted span of years without specifying its number here. — reasons: Yahya and Ibn Kathir say counted years; Wahidi and others describe the duration here as counted. Wahidi says the cave identifies place and the years identify duration. Baydawi calls both adverbial complements. Ibn Qutayba glosses al-amad in the following context as the limit or endpoint.
18:11/q12 How does 'years' function syntactically? [سِنِينَ · grammar]
  18:11/q12/p1 It is an accusative time adverb governed by the action of striking or sleeping. — reasons: Nahhas notes its nūn may also take declension and says the ear noun is feminine with a heavy damma omitted. Wahidi says the cave phrase gives the place and years the duration. Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi, Alusi, and Tabataba'i agree on the time complement; Alusi says a temporal and a spatial adverb may combine without difficulty.
18:11/q13 How is 'ʿadadan' parsed? [عَدَدًۭا · grammar] (turns on: Whether it modifies 'years' as an adjective or verbal noun, or acts as an independent cognate accusative.)
  18:11/q13/p1 It modifies 'years' as counted or possessing a number. — reasons: Farra, Zajjaj, Tha'labi, Wahidi, Razi, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, and Samin give the adjectival analysis. Nahhas distinguishes Farra's 'counted' and the Basrans' 'possessing a number.' Wahidi and Razi report omitted noun or passive verbal noun analyses. Abu Hayyan allows a verbal noun used adjectivally or an omitted preposition; Samin allows ellipsis of dhawat. Tabataba'i allows an elided genitive complement.
  18:11/q13/p2 It is an accusative verbal noun or cognate expression: the years were counted with counting. — reasons: Zajjaj, Nahhas, Abu Ubayda, Wahidi, Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Qurtubi, Tabrisi, Samin, Alusi, and Tabataba'i allow this. Samin says it may intensify the count or be governed by an implied taʿudd; Tabrisi also treats 'years' as a time expression.
18:11/q14 What does calling the years 'counted' imply? [سِنِينَ عَدَدًۭا · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether explicit counting evokes abundance, brevity, either scale, complete years, or an unspecified length.)
  18:11/q14/p1 It suggests many years, since a small quantity needs no counting. — reasons: Zajjaj states the principle; Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Biqa'i, and Tabataba'i's report of Zajjaj repeat it. Aisha's report about the Prophet's many nights at Hira illustrates the usage for Ibn Ashur. Alusi says abundance displays divine power; al-Kashf connects it to plural addressees.
  18:11/q14/p2 It can suggest a small or readily numbered span. — reasons: Zamakhshari argues even long human time is small beside God; Tabataba'i prefers scarcity as fitting 18:9's denial that this story is uniquely astonishing, comparing 12:20. Alusi says it suits the same point, and al-Kashf links it to a singular addressee. Abu Hayyan rejects comparison with 23:113, which concerns the sight of promised punishment. Al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam considered scarcity impossible here, though Alusi disputes him.
  18:11/q14/p3 The number can suggest either abundance or scarcity according to the point of comparison. — reasons: Kashshaf gives both; Baydawi makes it depend on reference point. Alusi reports al-Raghib and others and notes Ibn Hajar's discussion, which he finds unpersuasive. Study Quran reports this two-way sense from Zamakhshari.
  18:11/q14/p4 It denotes complete years without fractions of months or days. — reasons: Mawardi gives this as an alternative to enumerated years.
  18:11/q14/p5 The indefinite count leaves the duration unspecified in the wording. — reasons: Biqa'i links that indefiniteness to the indeterminate age of polytheism. Bagawi reports that counting may simply emphasize number or a long period.
18:11/q15 What does the prolonged sleep show about extraordinary divine acts? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether its delayed verification makes it a saintly gift or a prophetic miracle.)
  18:11/q15/p1 It is a gift to the youths as saints, not a prophet's miracle. — reasons: Razi says the sleep itself was ordinary; the extraordinary fact could be confirmed only after the same youths were recognized 309 years later. He also notes their unharmed survival and shielding from sun in 18:17–18.
  18:11/q15/p2 An extraordinary sleep would require a prophet in that era to count as a prophetic miracle. — reasons: Al-Qadi's objection, reported by Razi, rests on a prophet's presence for a prophetic miracle; Razi answers that this event could not serve that purpose.
18:11/q16 How are these verses related to the surrounding story? [* · links]
  18:11/q16/p1 They summarize the flight and prayer, years of sleep, and awakening before the detailed account. — reasons: Tabataba'i assigns one element to each opening verse and says later verses add the public discovery at 18:21. Islahi says advance summary keeps the purpose visible and prevents secondary details from obscuring it, a Qur'anic narrative pattern.
18:11/q17 Why is hearing the beloved's story from God especially valued? [* · interpretation]
  18:11/q17/p1 The beloved's own account is the highest source and deepens longing. — reasons: Qushayri cites a poem asking Sa'd to repeat the beloved's story because hearing it increases longing.
18:11/q18 What can be learned from the varying historical accounts of the cave story? [* · reports]
  18:11/q18/p1 Reports diverge greatly; the Qur'an selects what serves guidance and omits many historical particulars. — reasons: Tabataba'i lists disagreement over dates, kings, lineage, looks, names, and the title People of the Inscription. He traces divergence to Christian and other People of the Book traditions passed to Quraysh, monuments and images, and Muslims' broad collection of outside material through converts such as Wahb and Ka'b; later transmitters treated some as prophetic. Names, occupations, places, rulers, dates, and the dog are omitted because guidance does not depend on them. He notes historians often place the event between Jesus and Muhammad; the two Testaments lack it, Christian sources preserve it, and Christian versions differ over sleeper count, dog, and sleep length.
18:11/q19 How does Ibn Qutayba approach the figurative wording of this verse amid criticism of Qur'anic style? [فَضَرَبْنَا ءَاذَانِهِمْ · rhetoric]
  18:11/q19/p1 He explains its metaphor through tafsir, Arab language, and possible readings while resisting unsupported personal interpretation and later charges against Qur'anic style. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba says literal translation of the ear expression obscures its sense. He describes later accusations of contradiction, impossibility, faulty grammar, and disorder, unlike early opponents' charges of magic, soothsaying, or old tales; he says he gathered tafsir, expanded it, and used Arab usage to explain metaphor and possible readings without imposing a personal interpretation. He mentions 3:7.
