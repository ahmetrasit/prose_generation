## THE CURRENT MAP
92:1/q01 What does the night signify here? [ٱلَّيْلِ · referent] (turns on: Whether the night is read as the physical phenomenon, the natural self, or an inward cosmic experience.)
  92:1/q01/p1 The actual recurring night, marked by darkness, obscurity, and concealment. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says it gradually obscures what daylight showed and suits secrecy; Elmalılı allows the whole class of nights, and says its darkness follows existing light rather than primordial nonexistence. He also allows a particular terrifying night, literally or figuratively.
  92:1/q01/p2 The natural self. — reasons: This is Sahl al-Tustarī's inward reading.
  92:1/q01/p3 Cosmic and inward darkness that overwhelms perception and may bring the soul from multiplicity into annihilating unity. — reasons: Elmalılı describes movement from differentiation to unity, multiplicity to solitude, and witnessing to annihilation; he relates passing away to 55:26–27 and 28:88 and an ensuing abiding witness.
92:1/q02 What does the definite article in ٱلَّيْلِ specify? [ٱلَّيْلِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the article denotes the class or a specific night.)
  92:1/q02/p1 The whole kind of night. — reasons: Elmalılı offers generic reference.
  92:1/q02/p2 A particular terrifying night, literally or figuratively. — reasons: Elmalılı offers particular reference.
92:1/q03 What does يَغْشَىٰ mean? [يَغْشَىٰ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the phrase is glossed by covering and concealment or by its resulting darkness.)
  92:1/q03/p1 It covers, veils, or enshrouds, so darkness conceals what was visible. — reasons: Ibn Jubayr and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr explain covering and concealment; Qatādah describes creatures encompassed and filled. Al-Naḥḥās compares darkness to a garment and cites al-Nābigha's poem of night pursuing and catching a fugitive. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces gh-sh-y through veils, sleep, fainting, dulled sight and insight, rejection in 71:7 and 11:5, the lote tree in 53:16, coverings of senses in 3:154, 8:11, 33:19, 47:20, 2:7 and 45:23, and judgment or punishment in 88:1, 44:10–11, 29:55, 14:50 and 7:41. She notes terror and darkness in 24:40, 10:27, 31:32, 20:78, 13:3, 7:54 and 91:4, and understands this night as deep darkness. Al-Jishumī contrasts ghishāʾ, covering, with tajallī, clarity and illumination.
  92:1/q03/p2 It means that night darkens. — reasons: Mujāhid gives this concise gloss; the physical effect is the night removing daylight's light.
92:1/q04 What does the night cover? [ٱلَّيْلِ يَغْشَىٰ · referent] (turns on: Which implicit object is supplied for the verb, or how broadly its covering is understood.)
  92:1/q04/p1 The day or daylight. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī says night removes daylight's light until day clears and reveals what was hidden; al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī cite 13:3; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān cite 7:54; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 13:3; al-Qummī also calls this an oath. Elmalılı includes daylight among the possible objects.
  92:1/q04/p2 The sun. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī invoke 91:4; al-Nasafī points to the sun in the preceding oath; Abū Ḥayyān and Elmalılı likewise connect the possibility with 91:4. Al-Ālūsī says the sworn object is the entire night whichever object is supplied.
  92:1/q04/p3 Everything hidden by darkness, including all creatures or all creation. — reasons: Qatādah says night encompasses and fills all creatures. Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī cite 113:3; al-Nasafī cites the nightfall verse 113:3. Al-Ālūsī says the object may be whatever night veils in general, while the oath is by the whole night. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes universal coverage the lesson of the intransitive use.
  92:1/q04/p4 The earth and what is on it. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī gives earth as one possible object; Abū Ḥayyān says earth and all it contains; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran also offer earth.
  92:1/q04/p5 The horizons, or the horizon and everything between heaven and earth. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī specifies the horizon under darkness; Ibn al-Jawzī reports al-Zajjāj's wider horizon-to-earth account; Elmalılı includes all horizons.
  92:1/q04/p6 The world. — reasons: Study Quran reports this as a possible unexpressed object; Quṭb depicts the world as enveloped and hidden.
  92:1/q04/p7 Grief or people's eyes, hearts, and faculties through sleep and heedlessness. — reasons: Elmalılı extends the physical image to affect and perception, citing 6:60.
92:1/q05 Why is the object of يَغْشَىٰ left unexpressed? [يَغْشَىٰ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the missing object is recoverable, intentionally left wide, or absent because the verb is used intransitively.)
  92:1/q05/p1 The listener can infer the object from context. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say it is understood; al-Nasafī takes the preceding mention of the sun as contextual evidence, citing 91:4.
  92:1/q05/p2 Leaving it open broadens the image to every possible object. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās says the omission may deliberately leave the object vague. Elmalılı says it broadens the meaning and heightens terror, while pointing back to 91:4; al-Sāmarrāʾī says it keeps all possible objects in view.
  92:1/q05/p3 The verb functions intransitively here to teach that night covers everything. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly explains the omission as intransitive use and draws the universal lesson.
92:1/q06 How does إِذَا describe the time of the covering? [إِذَا · grammar]
  92:1/q06/p1 It presents the covering as a realized event that may occur now or later. — reasons: Elmalılı says conditional idhā asserts realization and the imperfect verb can indicate present or future time.
92:1/q07 How is the case of إِذَا governed in the coordinated oaths? [إِذَا · grammar] (turns on: Whether the coordinating wāws themselves must govern two cases, or coordination joins paired constituents under the first oath governor.)
  92:1/q07/p1 The construction poses a governance difficulty: coordination appears to make the wāws govern both genitive and accusative, or the oath wāws create a disputed construction. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's account and objects that coordinating wāws are not themselves governors; he says al-Zamakhsharī merely calls the disputed construction disliked where al-Khalīl's wording prohibits it.
  92:1/q07/p2 The later wāws coordinate genitive-and-accusative pairs; the first oath particle supplies governance rather than each coordinating wāw governing two cases. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān gives مررت بزيد أمس وعمرو اليوم as a parallel and cites Sībawayh's poetic coordination of a genitive and nominative in the same pattern. He reports al-Khalīl that the first wāw acts like oath bāʾ or tāʾ, while the later wāws join nouns by ordinary coordination; he reads al-Khalīl as prohibiting the competing account.
92:1/q08 Why is يَغْشَىٰ imperfect while the following daylight verb is perfect? [يَغْشَىٰ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the tense contrast chiefly depicts the different pace of night and day or creates immediate presence through a past form.)
  92:1/q08/p1 Night's covering unfolds gradually, whereas daylight appears at once at sunrise. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the paired verbs in al-Shams, 91:3–4; he explains the imperfect for the continuing covering and the perfect for sudden manifestation.
  92:1/q08/p2 A past form can make a present event vivid and immediate by placing the listener at the event. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī explains that Arabic past verbs have more temporal uses than simple past reference; he illustrates the shift with the Prophet turning toward heaven in 2:144 and the reproach for killing prophets in 3:21.
92:1/q09 Why is yaghshā apt here while 93:2 uses sajā? [يَغْشَىٰ · rhetoric]
  92:1/q09/p1 Yaghshā suggests movement and covering; sajā gives both stillness and darkness suited to the pause in revelation. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says sajā fits revelation's suspension and loss of light, whereas yaghshā and yasrī imply movement; he compares 92:1 and 89:4.
92:1/q10 How do night and day function in creation and human life? [ٱلَّيْلِ يَغْشَىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Which aspect of the paired phenomena carries the argument: daily benefit, divine governance, human divergence, the Hereafter, or secrecy.)
  92:1/q10/p1 They alternate as divine signs and provide complementary times for shelter, sleep, activity, and livelihood. — reasons: Qatādah calls them two great signs God rotates over creation. Al-Rāzī describes animals sheltered and creatures stilled for restorative sleep, then people, birds and other creatures moving for livelihood, citing 25:62 and 14:33. Abū Ḥayyān contrasts creatures' night shelter with daytime dispersal. Quṭb stresses their opposed appearance, qualities, effects, and recurring cycle.
  92:1/q10/p2 Their precise alternation points to a power governing the cosmos and purposeful human life. — reasons: Quṭb says the broad change touches the soul, evokes human inability to control the mysterious universe, and shows another hand ordering the heavens and directing lives; instinct and reflection recognize this director even amid denial. Küran Yolu sees the worth of creatures and greatness of their Creator in the oaths.
  92:1/q10/p3 Their contrast provides a cosmic frame or matrix for divergent human deeds and recompense. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr pairs night darkness and day brightness with the created male and female, citing 78:8 and 51:49. Quṭb relates the two opposed outcomes to night/day and male/female. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces the contrast to fire for the denier and satisfaction for the pious giver. Corpus Coranicum says the two cosmic oppositions frame varied human aims and their recompense, with God later speaking in 92:7–10; Neuwirth calls the elemental pairs a structural matrix. Study Quran likewise links the polarity with opposed ends of deeds.
  92:1/q10/p4 Their complementary pairing argues that creation has a completing counterpart, ultimately the Hereafter. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says night/day and male/female fill one another's void
