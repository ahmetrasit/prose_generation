## THE CURRENT MAP
11:114/q01 What does it mean to establish prayer? [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
  11:114/q01/p1 Perform the prescribed prayers fully, with their required acts and at their appointed times. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi and Ibn al-Jawzi specify complete bowing and prostration. Alusi includes regular observance or prayer at the beginning of its time. Jishumi says the verse fixes times generally while the Prophet explained details; Asad and Qurtubi likewise refer forms and exact times to prophetic teaching.
  11:114/q01/p2 Maintain prayer continuously, evenly, or at its best times. — reasons: These are alternative glosses reported by al-Tabrisi and Abu Hayyan; al-Alusi also reports regular maintenance and beginning-of-time performance.
  11:114/q01/p3 Fill one's time with obligatory and voluntary worship or remembrance. — reasons: Sufi authorities take the command to cover all time. Asad extends its stress on prayer to remembrance throughout waking life. Bursevi allows work, human needs, and restorative sleep.
  11:114/q01/p4 The five prayers close the five sensory doors and restore presence with God. — reasons: Qashani says the senses veil the heart; praying at day edges preserves gathered light through intervening times (23:9), while night wakefulness refines powers drawn toward sleep.
  11:114/q01/p5 Prayer is the foremost worship after faith and a refuge in hardship. — reasons: Razi relates its placement after steadfastness to its rank; Biqai calls it the broadest aid to uprightness; Qurtubi reports the Prophet turned to prayer when troubled.
11:114/q02 Which prayers are at the two ends of the day? [طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ · referent] (turns on: Whether dawn and sunset count within daytime, and whether the latter half of the day can contain both Zuhr and Asr as an end.)
  11:114/q02/p1 Fajr at the first end and Zuhr and Asr at the second. — reasons: Wahidi prefers Zajjaj: Maghrib belongs to night. Zamakhshari calls Zuhr and Asr evening prayers; Elmalili calls them the after-noon division. Tabatabai says the five-prayer report points to before and after noon. Qurtubi reports Ibn Atiyya's choice. Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Dahhak accepts all three second-end views. Al-Damghani also identifies dawn, noon, and afternoon and notes 20:130.
  11:114/q02/p2 Fajr and Asr. — reasons: Razi says sunrise and sunset mark opposite edges, leaving Maghrib to night; he parallels 20:130. Abu Hayyan says each boundary belongs to what it bounds and cites fasting from dawn. Biqai notes morning and afternoon prayer before the Night Journey. Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Dahhak accepts all three second-end views. Razi says Abu Hanifa inferred later performance of Fajr and Asr near the boundaries.
  11:114/q02/p3 Fajr and Maghrib. — reasons: Tabari argues that all agree on Fajr and that Maghrib alone supplies the opposite end; Wahidi says the apparent wording supports it. Tabrisi says Zuhr and Asr are obvious daytime prayers or specified by 17:78. Qurtubi defends dawn as daytime from fasting law but retains the sunset objection. Kuranyolu defines day as dawn to sunset. Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Dahhak accepts all three second-end views.
  11:114/q02/p4 Zuhr and Asr are the two boundaries; Fajr is grouped with night prayers. — reasons: Some connect this grouping with quiet versus loud recitation. Elmalili argues dawn is the day's root and noon and afternoon its two prominent parts; the night set of Maghrib, Isha and Fajr gives five prayers, with 17:78 and 20:130.
  11:114/q02/p5 Fajr and Isha. — reasons: Ibn Abi Hatim reports this from Mujahid.
  11:114/q02/p6 Fajr and Zuhr at one end, Asr and Maghrib at the other. — reasons: Wahidi and Baghawi attribute this division to Muqatil.
  11:114/q02/p7 The first end is Fajr, while the evening end remains disputed. — reasons: Tabari says all agree on Fajr but differ about evening; Ibn al-Jawzi reports the majority agrees, but notes Ibn Jarir gives Zuhr instead. Qurtubi transmits Mawardi's claimed agreement and qualifies it by the Zuhr/Asr view. Asad gives the literal wording, 'the two ends of the day.'
11:114/q03 Why is the yāʾ of طرفي pronounced with a vowel? [طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ · grammar]
  11:114/q03/p1 It meets a following still consonant and takes the usual kasra. — reasons: Akhfash compares صاحبي السجن at 12:39.
11:114/q04 Does avoiding major sins already require performing the five prayers? [ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · law]
  11:114/q04/p1 Yes, because abandoning prayer is a major sin. — reasons: Ibn Hajar argues this; al-Alusi says it merits examination.
11:114/q05 How does al-Tirmidhi grade the principal confession reports? [* · reports]
  11:114/q05/p1 The Ibn Masud account is hasan sahih; the Muadh and Abu al-Yusr routes are weak. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reports al-Tirmidhi's distinctions.
11:114/q06 Can night prayer erase sins committed during the day? [زُلَفًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · interpretation]
  11:114/q06/p1 Yes, a believer's night prayer removes daytime sins. — reasons: Tabatabai cites Abu Abdillah and notes parallel transmissions in several collections.
11:114/q07 What does زلفا mean? [زُلَفًۭا · meaning]
  11:114/q07/p1 Near or successive hours, stages, or portions of the night; singular zulfa. — reasons: Akhfash lists zulfat and zulf as other plurals. Layth and Thalab specify the first night hours; Abu Ubayda, Akhfash and Ibn Qutayba allow any hour. Al-Ajjaj's poem uses successive zulf. Ibn Qutayba and Abu Hayyan derive nearness; Tabari and Mawardi give Muzdalifa etymologies, while Raghib includes rank, favor and euphemistic punishment. Zajjaj rejects an unattested night meaning for zalif. Al-Samarra'i stresses gradual approach and concludes that zulfa conveys drawing near generally.
11:114/q08 How is زلفا read? [زُلَفًۭا · readings] (turns on: The vowel on lam and whether the form is a plural, collective, or singular.)
  11:114/q08/p1 Zulafan, with damma on zay and fatha on lam, a plural of zulfa. — reasons: Tabari prefers this because Isha follows portions of the night; Sam in calls it the common reading, and Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan the majority reading. Ibn al-Jazari says the fatha form, like the damma form, is an attested dialectal plural of zulfa, comparing zulam/zulma and yusr/yusra.
  11:114/q08/p2 Zulufan, with damma on both letters. — reasons: Attributed to Medinan readers, Abu Jafar, Ibn Abi Ishaq, Talha and others. Analyses include a plural with vowel harmony, plural of zalif, or dialectal singular; Qiraat-ER says the plural pattern changes without changing meaning. Ibn al-Jazari names Abu Ja'far, Talha, Shayba, Isa ibn Amr and transmitters from Abu Amr for the damma on lam; he treats both vowel forms as dialectal plurals of zulfa.
  11:114/q08/p3 Zulfan, with silent lam. — reasons: Attributed to some Meccan readers and to Ibn Muhaysin and Mujahid. It may shorten the vowelled form or function as a collective singular.
  11:114/q08/p4 Zulfa or zulfa, a singular near interval or rank. — reasons: Mujahid and Ibn Muhaysin transmit the feminine fuʿla form; Kashshaf compares qurba; Sam in calls it a singular adjective for a near rank or hour.
11:114/q09 How do طرفي النهار and زلفا function grammatically? [طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ زُلَفًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether زلفا names a time coordinate with the day ends or a prayer coordinate with الصلاة.)
  11:114/q09/p1 Both are accusative time expressions governed by establish; زلفا is coordinated with طرفي. — reasons: Kashshaf, Nasafi and Sam in say taraf becomes temporal through annexation to a time, as do 'beginning of day' and 'half of night'; Sam in prefers governance by أقم.
  11:114/q09/p2 The night expressions are prayers that draw one near to God and coordinate with الصلاة. — reasons: Kashshaf supplies the alternative parsing as devotional night prayers; Sam in and Abu Hayyan report it.
  11:114/q09/p3 من الليل modifies زلفا as an adjective. — reasons: Sam in explicitly parses the prepositional phrase as a qualifier.
11:114/q10 Which prayers are meant by the night intervals? [زُلَفًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ · referent] (turns on: Which hours count as night approaches and whether the plural requires at least three prayers.)
  11:114/q10/p1 Maghrib and Isha. — reasons: Hasan transmits this as the Prophet's description of two night intervals; Tabari lists early authorities. Zajjaj and Wahidi put sunset within night. Al-Samarra'i reports that many commentators identify Maghrib and Isha because their night portions are nearest daytime.
  11:114/q10/p2 Isha alone, in a later portion of night. — reasons: Mujahid calls it an hour at Isha. Ibn Abbas cited the phrase in favor of delaying Isha; Tabari says Isha follows portions of night.
  11:114/q10/p3 Voluntary night vigil or tahajjud. — reasons: Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn Abbas's voluntary-night gloss. Ibn Kathir describes the earlier obligation of night vigil and its later abrogation; Bursevi cites 17:79 for the Prophet.
  11:114/q10/p4 Maghrib, Isha, and Fajr. — reasons: The dawn-as-night classification may follow loud recitation; Elmalili argues this yields five prayers.
  11:114/q10/p5 Maghrib, Isha, and witr or night vigil; or Isha, witr, and tahajjud. — reasons: Razi and Elmalili infer at least three from the broken plural; Razi argues witr obligation using 34:20, while Elmalili describes a possible sixth prayer and 17:79. Bursevi reports Abu Hanifa's witr view.
  11:114/q10/p6 Night prayer without specifying which one. — reasons: Qurtubi attributes this to Akhfash. Al-Samarra'i reports an unrestricted reading of night prayer as drawing near to God.
