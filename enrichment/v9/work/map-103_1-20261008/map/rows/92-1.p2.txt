AHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p343/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Sūrah al-Layl pairs with al-Shams; their central themes and inner and outer features correspond so closely that even a general reader can sense the harmony.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p343/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-Layl develops the preceding sūrah’s claim that the soul succeeds through purification by identifying what corrupts it and how it can be protected and purified. | mentions 91:9, 91:10
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p343/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verses 1–4 use evidence from the outer and inner worlds to establish that Judgment must come: the Hereafter is this world’s counterpart and makes it meaningful.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p345/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Oaths by natural phenomena in the Qur’an are intended to establish a premise.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p346/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Night and day, like male and female, are complementary pairs whose members fill one another’s void and together serve their created purpose; without a counterpart, their capacities and uses make no sense.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p346/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The harmony of opposites argues for monotheism and, here, for the Hereafter; 51:49 calls pairs a reminder that this world has a counterpart completing its purpose. | mentions 51:49
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:92:1/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | These oaths show the worth of the created things and the greatness of their Creator, while directing attention to the coming subject.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p361/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Using a past verb for a present event makes it vivid and immediate; the author illustrates this with the Prophet's turning toward heaven and the reproach for killing prophets. | mentions 2:144, 3:21
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p361/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Arabic grammarians describe this vivid tense shift as transporting the listener to the event or bringing the event to the listener; past verbs have more temporal uses than the single schoolbook past.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p361/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Contrast makes meanings clearer and more visible; in the opening oath, night covers while day becomes manifest, and the object of يغشى is omitted to keep every possible object in view.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p362/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Night's covering unfolds gradually, so يغشى is present tense; daylight appears at once at sunrise, so تجلى is past tense, as in the parallel verbs in al-Shams. | mentions 91:3, 91:4
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p410/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Sajā brings together stillness and darkness, matching revelation's suspension and loss of light; yaghshā and yasri imply movement and therefore fit less well. | mentions 92:1, 89:4
