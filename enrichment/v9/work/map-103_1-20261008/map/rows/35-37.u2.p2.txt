35:37/q20 Is there a variant reading of ٱلنَّذِيرُ? [ٱلنَّذِيرُ · readings] (turns on: Singular versus plural wording and, in al-Qurṭubī's form, feminine plural agreement.)
  35:37/q20/p1 The verse is read with singular al-nadhīr. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says the singular suits the immediate point.
  35:37/q20/p2 An alternate reading has plural al-nudhur, 'warners.' — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī transmits the feminine plural verbal agreement wajāʾatkum al-nudhur; al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān also report the plural, which supports a generic prophetic referent.
35:37/q21 What does فَذُوقُوا۟ command, and why does it follow? [فَذُوقُوا۟ · interpretation]
  35:37/q21/p1 They must taste the punishment of Hell, with remorse, as the consequence of ignored lifetime and warning. — reasons: Al-Rāzī calls the command humiliating and signifying lasting punishment; al-Qurṭubī links it to failure to reflect. Ibn Kathīr links it to disobedience to prophets. Ibn ʿĀshūr says fāʾ marks consequence, 'punishment' is the omitted object, and the imperative conveys continuity and inability to escape. Al-Biqāʿī cites 17:56 and 77:36 for their helplessness.
35:37/q22 Who are ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ here? [ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · referent] (turns on: Whether 'wrongdoers' is limited in context to the disbelievers in Hell or expresses a rule about all injustice.)
  35:37/q22/p1 The wrongdoers are the unbelievers or polytheists, who wronged themselves through disbelief and idolatry. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī make disbelief explicit; Yaḥyā calls them polytheists. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls shirk the gravest injustice, violating God's oneness and harming the self, citing 31:13.
  35:37/q22/p2 The wording reaches every wrongdoer whenever wrongdoing is established. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says wrongdoing weakens as truth strengthens; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the general statement includes these disbelievers and all wrongdoers.
  35:37/q22/p3 A wrongdoer can be one with compounded ignorance who mistakes falsehood for truth. — reasons: Al-Rāzī offers this as a possible explanation of why such a person lacks beneficial knowledge in the afterlife.
35:37/q23 What does having no helper mean? [فَمَا لِلظَّٰلِمِينَ مِن نَّصِيرٍ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether helper covers every possible rescuer, specifically intercession, or even beneficial knowledge.)
  35:37/q23/p1 No one can protect or rescue them from God's punishment. — reasons: Al-Rāzī's broad preferred sense excludes help from God and every other helper; he says 'sultan' names proof because authority or power aids. Ibn Kathīr mentions torment, restraints and chains. Study Quran says they turned away from God and the help He sent in this life, citing 2:270, 3:192, 5:72 and 22:71. Al-Ālūsī treats the denial as the cause for tasting punishment; al-Biqāʿī applies it to eternal torment.
  35:37/q23/p2 There is no intercession for major sinners. — reasons: Al-Jishumī draws this theological conclusion from the denial of a helper.
  35:37/q23/p3 No beneficial knowledge remains to help someone who mistook falsehood for truth. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports this as a possible narrower sense of helper.
35:37/q24 Why is 'helper' singular here? [نَّصِيرٍ · rhetoric]
  35:37/q24/p1 At this stage in Hell they have despaired of human allies and idols and hope only for God; elsewhere expected idol-help is still in view. — reasons: Al-Rāzī compares 3:192 and distinguishes their actual state in Hell from warnings in worldly life or early gathering.
35:37/q25 How is مِن نَّصِيرٍ parsed? [مِن نَّصِيرٍ · grammar] (turns on: The syntactic status of the preposition and the nominal subject.)
  35:37/q25/p1 The noun can be a subject governed by the preposition. — reasons: Al-Samīn gives this parse.
  35:37/q25/p2 The noun can be the topic, with the preceding prepositional phrase as predicate. — reasons: Al-Samīn gives this alternative.
35:37/q26 Who hears the final no-helper statement? [فَمَا لِلظَّٰلِمِينَ مِن نَّصِيرٍ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the sentence continues God's address to the damned or turns outward into a general conclusion.)
  35:37/q26/p1 It continues the rebuke to the condemned, ending their hope of rescue and challenging their imagined allies. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this reading; al-Ālūsī says naming them wrongdoers itself rebukes them.
  35:37/q26/p2 It is a separate conclusion addressed to listeners, applying to every wrongdoer. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this alternative, including the disbelievers just described in 35:36.
35:37/q27 What does the verse teach about accountability and reform? [* · theology]
  35:37/q27/p1 Worldly life gives people choice and opportunity to amend themselves; after death the chance ends and prior warning removes excuses. — reasons: Al-Jishumī argues from the interrogation and lifetime as evidence for human choice and action. Qatāda calls long life proof against people. Al-Rāzī says the cry cannot reduce punishment, and that this is punishment rather than correction, for a corrected pupil would be released. Al-Biqāʿī adds that messengers and books supplied what reason alone could not. Iṣlāḥī distinguishes more lenient treatment for those who die before maturity; Study Quran cites 4:18 against repentance at death. Al-Bursawī's inward reading says heedless people awaken fully only after death.
35:37/q28 Why is their request to return refused? [رَبَّنَآ أَخْرِجْنَا · interpretation]
  35:37/q28/p1 The requested return is impossible, and if returned they would repeat their sins. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr cites 6:28 and 40:11–12; Study Quran cites 23:100 and 6:28. Al-Rāzī says they fail to recognize how remote and impossible the return is, invokes 17:72 for continuing loss, and contrasts punitive confinement with correction. Kuran Yolu compares their ineffective cries with 23:107–108.
35:37/q29 When do Hell's people utter these words? [وَهُمْ يَصْطَرِخُونَ فِيهَا · links] (turns on: The notes give two distinct timing details rather than competing chronologies.)
  35:37/q29/p1 They speak before the command in 23:108 that they be despised and speak no more. — reasons: Qatāda gives this ordering for all speech attributed to the people of Hell.
  35:37/q29/p2 They receive God's answer after an interval equal to worldly life. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports this timing.
35:37/q30 What does their vow reveal about reliance on God? [نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا · theology]
  35:37/q30/p1 Their unconditional promise reveals self-reliance and failure to honor God's majesty. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says they omit reliance on God and cannot produce works proportionate to His majesty. He contrasts believers, who confess that their deeds are small beside divine grace, ask for forgiveness more than reward, and praise God's guidance and blessings beyond expectation.
35:37/q31 What obstacles to faith does the answer rule out? [أَوَلَمْ نُعَمِّرْكُم مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ مَن تَذَكَّرَ وَجَآءَكُمُ ٱلنَّذِيرُ · interpretation]
  35:37/q31/p1 Neither inadequate opportunity to reflect nor failure of guidance explains their disbelief; they had time and a messenger. — reasons: Al-Rāzī identifies the two possible impediments as the people's failure to reflect on revelation and a guide's failure to recite it. God's answer removes both excuses; Qatāda names both lifetime and messengers as proofs.
35:37/q32 How does the passage connect with other afterlife discourse? [* · links]
  35:37/q32/p1 This exchange belongs to a wider dialogue between God and Hell's people about their futile plea and judgment. — reasons: Study Quran compares 40:11–12; its other cited pleas are 32:12, 19:38, 67:11, 6:27, 7:53 and 26:102. Kuran Yolu compares 23:107–108.
  35:37/q32/p2 The sūra contrasts believers' enduring reward with the ruin and helplessness of disbelievers, then moves to God's power and idols. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says wealth and children without faith and good deeds increase ruin and remain exposed to danger, while the faithful's chambers give enduring safety and reward; ḍiʿf may mean equal or unbounded multiples, not only double. Study Quran describes verses 29–39 as the long comparison that leads to God's power, idols and the outcomes of unbelief.
35:37/q33 What inward lesson is drawn from the condemned person's late awakening? [فَذُوقُوا۟ · inward]
  35:37/q33/p1 Those asleep in worldly heedlessness awaken fully after death and resurrection, when punishment becomes directly felt. — reasons: Al-Bursawī gives this ishārī reading. He also transmits ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlānī's addresses to young and elderly penitents: God preserves and draws the young back; after pardon and delay through youth and adulthood, the estranged elder can still return. His Persian couplet contrasts youthful spiritual haste with old age's coldness.
35:37/q34 What practical duty does a clear warning create for delayed Hajj? [وَجَآءَكُمُ ٱلنَّذِيرُ · law]
  35:37/q34/p1 Hajj may be delayed while completion remains likely; clear warning that delay may prevent it makes postponement impermissible. — reasons: Al-Bursawī attributes the sounder view to Muḥammad al-Shaybānī. Sudden death after delay entails no sin under this account, but clear warning changes the duty.
