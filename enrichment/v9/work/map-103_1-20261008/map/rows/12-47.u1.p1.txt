e and relation of فذروه to the sowing clause? [فَذَرُوهُ · grammar] (turns on: How an imperative harvest clause is joined to an apparently declarative sowing forecast.)
  12:47/q10/p1 It is an explicit instruction to store the grain; the preceding report may also function as an instruction. — reasons: Al-Māwardī contrasts the imperative storage clause with the apparent indicative sowing clause, then reports that the first may also command. Al-Qurṭubī agrees. Al-Ālūsī reports that on the command reading both clauses prescribe action; some regard storage as part of dream interpretation and some as advice outside it.
  12:47/q10/p2 It is new, independent advice after a prediction of ordinary sowing. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Shaykh say the command does not turn the preceding verb into an imperative. Al-Kashf calls the interruption urgent. Al-Ālūsī answers the coordination objection by treating storage as independent advice or as the answer to an implied condition.
12:47/q11 What is the lexical peculiarity of فذروه? [فَذَرُوهُ · meaning]
  12:47/q11/p1 ذر, like دع, has no past-tense form in use because ترك supplies it. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī makes this lexical observation.
12:47/q12 How much of the harvest may be used, and is seed included? [إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا مِّمَّا تَأْكُلُونَ · interpretation]
  12:47/q12/p1 Use only the small amount needed for food and save the rest; seed for further planting is understood. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās says thresh only what is eaten. Al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others stress restraint or gradual use through the prosperous years. Al-Ālūsī says seed need not be separately excepted because seven years of cultivation presuppose retaining it; Kuranyolu explicitly mentions food and seed set aside. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the exception permission for needed food.
12:47/q13 Is the eater addressed directly in مما تأكلون? [مِّمَّا تَأْكُلُونَ · readings] (turns on: Whether the verb is read with tāʾ or yāʾ and whether the third person names people or shifts address.)
  12:47/q13/p1 The standard reading addresses 'you' who eat. — reasons: Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī contrast the second person wording with a reported third person reading.
  12:47/q13/p2 Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān or al-Sulamī reads يأكلون, 'they eat'. — reasons: Al-Samīn explains 'they' as people or a shift of address; al-Ālūsī says it refers to what people eat.
12:47/q14 How does Joseph's reply combine interpretation and advice? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the agricultural directions extend Joseph's interpretation or are themselves what the dream depicts.)
  12:47/q14/p1 It interprets the dream and adds practical storage guidance; the later abundant year is an unseen report. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes interpretation by meaning, counsel, and a future report. Ibn Zayd says Joseph did more than interpret; al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda stress reserves. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the guidance valuable to Egypt's king and views the dream as divine kindness, perhaps preparing the king for faith and reform. Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran likewise describe a drought strategy. Al-Ālūsī reports that even on an imperative reading some separate the storage advice from interpretation.
  12:47/q14/p2 The dream itself reveals the practical planting, storage, and rationing policy. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the cows and ears as depicting required agricultural measures, while allowing the small eating exception as permission rather than dream interpretation. Al-Ālūsī reports that some, on the imperative reading, count the storage instruction within the interpretation.
12:47/q15 How was the further abundant year known? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the surplus forecast derives from direct revelation or already taught interpretation.)
  12:47/q15/p1 God disclosed it by revelation or gave Joseph knowledge of that year. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī cites the unasked-for year in 12:49 as evidence of revelation. Al-Kashshāf calls it a blessed eighth year known through revelation; Qatāda says God gave Joseph knowledge of it, as transmitted by al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī. Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes the unseen report from the interpretation.
  12:47/q15/p2 It followed from the dream interpretation God had taught Joseph. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī records the alternative that his knowledge was taught by God, rather than direct revelation; Ibn al-Jawzī says that interpretation was certainly true.
12:47/q16 What related variant wording is reported in the provisioning account? [* · readings]
  12:47/q16/p1 A reading has ما قربتم, explained as 'what you bring near for them'. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī attributes ما قربتم to Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports al-Ṣādiq's wording and a gloss about bringing the harvest near in revelation's ordering. The notes do not place the wording in this verse.
12:47/q17 What report gives Joseph's age and years away from home? [* · reports]
  12:47/q17/p1 He left at seventeen, spent seven years in the well and seven in prison, and stored food for seven years. — reasons: Al-Durr transmits Qatāda's report; its transmitters think Joseph met his father at that time.
12:47/q18 What report illustrates Joseph's patience when summoned? [* · reports]
  12:47/q18/p1 He did not hurry out after interpreting the cows, wanting his innocence established first. — reasons: Al-Durr transmits ʿIkrima's Prophetic report, which marvels at Joseph's patience and generosity.
12:47/q19 What report marks the start of the hard years? [* · reports]
  12:47/q19/p1 A man who usually left half of a two-person serving uneaten ate it all, and Joseph recognized the first hard year. — reasons: Al-Durr transmits this from Zayd ibn Aslam.
12:47/q20 How does the reported famine economy unfold? [* · reports]
  12:47/q20/p1 Joseph stockpiles harvests, then sells food for successive classes of assets and finally restores Egyptians' freedom and possessions. — reasons: The report from al-Riḍā lists money, valuables, livestock, servants, property, land and people's freedom before Joseph frees them and restores possessions and kingship. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he omits many similar reports because most do not directly interpret these verses.
12:47/q21 What does the al-Qummī report recount about the dream? [* · reports]
  12:47/q21/p1 The cupbearer recalled the king's cows and ears dream after a time; Joseph interpreted plenty, famine, then rain. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits al-Qummī's report of seven fat cows eaten by seven lean cows and seven green ears with dry ones.
12:47/q22 What legal principle does the storage policy illustrate? [* · law]
  12:47/q22/p1 Public benefit preserves religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property; preventing their loss is also benefit. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī derives these five interests from the verse, treating their preservation as benefit and their loss as harm.
12:47/q23 Why do revealed laws address worldly interests? [* · theology]
  12:47/q23/p1 Worldly guidance enables knowledge and worship of God and happiness in the afterlife; it is divine grace. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī attributes to Sunni authorities the qualification that God grants this care by grace, not by an obligation owed by God.
12:47/q24 What larger lesson does the Joseph story illustrate? [* · links]
  12:47/q24/p1 God directs human affairs in ways people cannot foresee; disliked events may prove good and liked events bad. — reasons: Asad contrasts the Qur'anic lesson with Genesis' account of Joseph's fortunes and invokes 2:216.
12:47/q25 To whom did the messenger hope to return? [* · referent] (turns on: Whether 'the people' includes the king's community or is an honorific for the king alone.)
  12:47/q25/p1 The king and his people. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this as the sense of 'the people' in the messenger's earlier speech.
  12:47/q25/p2 The king alone, honorifically called 'people'. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this respectful alternative.
12:47/q26 Why did the messenger say 'perhaps they will know'? [* · interpretation]
  12:47/q26/p1 He hoped they would understand and accept the interpretation, while fearing rejection or a false dream. — reasons: Al-Māwardī says the concern is about their response and confirmation by events, not doubt in Joseph.
