79:46/q24 What do the accompanying rhetorical notes say about harmony and roughness of wording? [* · rhetoric]
  79:46/q24/p1 Balanced letters can make speech easy and pleasing, while celebrated poetry can contain harsh or obscure passages. — reasons: Al-Rummani and reported al-Khalil distinguish degrees of letter harmony, pronunciation and reception, with the Qur'an placed at the highest level. Al-Baqillani adduces rough passages from Abu Nuwas, al-Aʿsha, Zuhayr and al-Tirmah, then reports a critic's defense of even speech and praise for al-Buhturi and Abu Nuwas. The excerpts make general rhetorical claims, without analyzing this verse's sounds.
79:46/q25 What general rules of large assimilation are recorded in the recitation notes? [* · readings]
  79:46/q25/p1 Large assimilation merges two moving related letters; Abu ʿAmr is especially associated with it, though transmission manuals differ. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari defines large and small assimilation, explains proposed names and ease of articulation, cites Abu ʿAmr and poetic Arabic evidence, and records both omission and inclusion by earlier manuals. These notes give no specific assimilated reading of 79:46.
79:46/q26 What other lexical sense list appears alongside the note on ḍuḥā? [ضُحَىٰهَا · meaning]
  79:46/q26/p1 The accompanying lexicon also lists seven senses for ḍarar. — reasons: Al-Damghani lists affliction, drought, terrors, illness, decrease, hunger, and harm itself. The list does not gloss ḍuḥā in this verse.
79:46/q27 What special-property report uses the verse's brief-time expression? [* · reports]
  79:46/q27/p1 A stopped report from Ibn Abbas prescribes writing the verse with 46:35 for difficult childbirth and giving the writing to drink. — reasons: Al-Suyuti transmits the Ibn Abbas report and says his collection presents non-fabricated reports and reports from Companions and Successors that he found concerning Qur'anic special properties.
