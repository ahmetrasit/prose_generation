[TABRISI:11:114#2/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي | reports | Ali reports that the Prophet called the prayer command in this verse the most hopeful verse in the Book.
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي | reports | The Prophet compares the five daily prayers to bathing five times in a river: prayer removes sins as washing removes bodily dirt.
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One interpretation is that persistence in good deeds leads a person to abandon evil deeds, as though the good deeds remove them.
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A second interpretation identifies the good deeds as repentance, which removes sins by cancelling their punishment; the author says there is no disagreement that repentance cancels punishment.

[TABRISI:11:114/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Other glosses say to perform prayer evenly or to keep performing it continuously.
[TABRISI:11:114/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس، ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zayd identify the two ends of day as dawn and sunset prayers and the night intervals as the first hours of night, including the final evening prayer.

[TABRISI:11:114/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن | reports | Hasan reports the Prophet's wording that Maghrib and Isha are the two night intervals called zulf.
[TABRISI:11:114/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | A further view identifies the two ends as the dawn and afternoon prayers.
[TABRISI:11:114/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس وأكثر المفسرين | reports | Ibn Abbas and most commentators say the five daily prayers expiate sins committed between them, taking the definite good deeds as referring back to prayer.
