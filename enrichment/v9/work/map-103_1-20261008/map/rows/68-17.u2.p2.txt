68:17/q24 When were the garden verses revealed? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether verses 17–33 share the sūra's early Meccan setting and how that bears on Badr.)
  68:17/q24/p1 The sūra is early Meccan, but the garden passage may have been revealed separately in Medina. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds the sūra's overall Meccan setting clear but judges a Medinan origin for the seventeen garden verses plausible. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ excepts 17–33 and 48–50 from the early Meccan sūra and notes the traditional placement after al-ʿAlaq (96:1), despite ordering disputes. Al-Sakhāwī, as cited in al-Itqān, specifically lists the stretch from 68:17 through 68:33 as Medinan, alongside another passage in the sūra.
  68:17/q24/p2 The garden warning can be read with the preceding passage and Badr as a later worldly demonstration. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes this conditional on joint revelation; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects reading a Badr event into the passage because Badr came about five years afterward.
  68:17/q24/p3 This verse is Meccan; it does not refer to ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām. — reasons: Al-Shaʿbī, as cited in al-Itqān, explicitly rejects the proposed referent and calls the verse Meccan.

68:17/q27 How do composition and story selection give this garden account instructive force? [* · rhetoric]
  68:17/q27/p1 Qur'anic stories select useful material and place it in a fitting context to teach, admonish, and provide proof. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says stories serve higher purposes than amusement, select material for lessons and communal benefit, and gain force as proof and explanation in their sūra contexts; knowledge of earlier peoples also challenged People of the Book. 68:17/q28 What does it mean to call the garden account a qiṣṣa? [* · meaning]
  68:17/q28/p1 Qiṣṣa is a report of an event absent from the reporter; qiṣaṣ is its plural, while qaṣaṣ names the narrated report. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes a story from reports of events contemporaneous with revelation, such as Muslim battles.
