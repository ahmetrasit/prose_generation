12:42/q20 What inward reading is given to Joseph's request and continued imprisonment? [* · inward] (turns on: Which spiritual faculty or stage is represented by the forgotten messenger.)
  12:42/q20/p1 The request represents seeking stable spiritual existence through love, with a remnant of self that prolongs the journey. — reasons: Al-Qashani maps intoxicated love, spirit and heart toward unity, yet sees veiling rather than annihilation; ego or illusion can persist, and prison marks seclusion until subsistence through God begins.
  12:42/q20/p2 The alternative forgetting is the lover's loss of the love-messenger in bewildered witnessing. — reasons: Al-Qashani says unity makes the lover forget creation and self until annihilation ends and divine life restores distinction.
12:42/q21 What institutional lesson is drawn from Joseph's long imprisonment? [* · law]
  12:42/q21/p1 Prisons need regular judicial oversight so detainees do not disappear from official attention. — reasons: Ibn Ashur infers that the prison lacked a reliable register or periodic royal report, then says Islamic law requires a judge to review prisoners daily.
12:42/q22 What did Joseph's patience after imprisonment show? [* · reports]
  12:42/q22/p1 He interpreted the king's dream before asking for release and sought vindication before leaving prison. — reasons: Abd al-Razzaq transmits a Prophetic expression of admiration for his patience and generosity, while connecting the earlier request to longer confinement.
12:42/q23 How is the term of imprisonment connected to an earlier decree? [فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ · links]
  12:42/q23/p1 The added years fulfill the earlier decision to imprison Joseph in 12:35. — reasons: Muqatil says the initial seven years and five later punitive years made twelve in all.
12:42/q24 What did Ali say about disciplining the soul in relation to this episode? [* · inward]
  12:42/q24/p1 The soul repeatedly scatters from discipline like a flock moving away from the shepherd. — reasons: Al-Tustari transmits Ali's comparison as an inward reflection on attention and self-discipline.

  12:42/q26/p1 Some scholars read Joseph's words without blame. — reasons: Al-Alusi reports this interpretive possibility, which accords with lawful use of means discussed elsewhere in the notes.
