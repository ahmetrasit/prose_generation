[IBNASHUR:93:1/r1] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Most muṣḥafs, many tafsīrs, and al-Tirmidhī title it Sūrat al-Ḍuḥā without the wāw; many tafsīrs and Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī call it Sūrat Wa-l-Ḍuḥā. No sound Companion report on its name reached the author.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r10] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Forenoon, when sunlight spreads, suggests revelation and its guidance; night suggests the Prophet's Qurʾān recitation, heard by nearby polytheists.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r11] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The restriction of the night by idhā sajā may point to the time the Prophet prayed at night, as described in al-Muzzammil. | mentions 73:2, 73:3
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r12] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The author refers readers to his explanation of al-ḍuḥā in the opening of Sūrat al-Shams. | mentions 91:1
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r13] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The muṣḥaf writes the final alif of al-ḍuḥā in yāʾ shape despite its wāw origin, for visual harmony with the sūra's mostly yāʾ-derived endings and to accommodate imāla by harmony.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r14] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ابن مالك | reports | Ibn Mālik cites imāla in dialects that incline an alif normally not inclined when it occurs beside an alif that is inclined, describing this in his commentary on the Kāfiya.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r15] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Sajā means that the night stretches and its darkness lasts; it is also likened to covering the whole body with a blanket, and its root is wāwī.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r2] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The sūra is Meccan by agreement.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · جندب بن سفيان البجلي | reports | In the Ṣaḥīḥayn account, Jundub reports that the Prophet injured his finger and was unwell and did not rise at night for two or three nights; Umm Jamīl, identified through Ibn ʿAbbās by Ibn ʿAṭiyya as Abū Lahab's wife, taunted him that his devil had left, and the verses were revealed. | mentions 93:2, 93:3
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r4] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · جندب البجلي | reports | In al-Tirmidhī's report, Jundub recalls being with the Prophet in a cave when his finger bled; after Gabriel's delay, polytheists said the Prophet had been abandoned. Al-Tirmidhī grades the report ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. | mentions 93:3
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r5] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn ʿĀshūr doubts that Jundub heard Umm Jamīl's taunt: Jundub was a young Companion and perhaps converted after the Hijra; Ibn ʿUyayna may have combined two reports.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r6] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | One proposed solution is that fī ghār, 'in a cave,' is a scribal error for kunta ghāziyan, 'you were on campaign'; that would still require combining two reports.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r7] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | It is the eleventh sūra in revelation order, after al-Fajr and before al-Inshirāḥ; it has eleven verses and begins the short al-Mufaṣṣal sūras.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r8] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Its purposes are to refute the claim that revelation ceased, promise the Prophet a better Hereafter and what pleases God, remind him of past care through childhood, youth, and maturity, and direct gratitude through beneficial service and praise.
[IBNASHUR:93:1/r9] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The oath strengthens the report against the polytheists' allegation that revelation stopped when the Prophet missed night prayer for a few nights; its emphasis indirectly rebukes them, since the Prophet did not doubt God's report.
