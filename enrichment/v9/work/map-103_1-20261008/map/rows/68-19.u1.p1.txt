  68:19/q11/p2 The loss punished their failure to give thanks for the garden. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr explains God's sending of the destruction this way.
  68:19/q11/p3 The loss was a trial that could serve as grace by bringing repentance. — reasons: Al-Jishumī offers punishment and trial as alternatives, with repentance explaining how the latter was a grace.
68:19/q12 What lesson does the garden's loss teach? [* · interpretation] (turns on: The sources draw consequences for provision, giving to the poor, and the Prophet's opponents from the same loss.)
  68:19/q12/p1 Sin can deprive people of provision already prepared for them. — reasons: Ibn Masʿūd transmits a Prophetic warning applying loss of provision to the owners' crop. Another report in al-Durr also names lost knowledge and missed night prayer before applying it to the garden.
  68:19/q12/p2 The passage warns against withholding wealth but does not establish that the owners withheld obligatory zakat. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the broad admonition from proof of a particular legal obligation.
  68:19/q12/p3 The episode warns people who feel safe and call the Prophet possessed that punishment can suddenly ruin firm plans. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī connects the parable to Quraysh's apparent ease and their accusation against the Prophet; the orchard was destroyed in a blink before its owners could enact their pledges.
