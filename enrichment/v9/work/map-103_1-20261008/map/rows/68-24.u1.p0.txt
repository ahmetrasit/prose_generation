## THE CURRENT MAP
68:24/q01 How is أن لا يدخلنها connected to their whispering? [أَن لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا · grammar] (turns on: Whether أن explains an implied utterance or introduces a verbal noun, and whether the reading without it requires an implied قول or lets يتخافتون act as speech.)
  68:24/q01/p1 أن introduces an explanation of the speech implicit in their whispering. — reasons: Naḥḥās says the answer lies in the earlier أن and that يتخافتون already implies secret speech. Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr take the clause as explanatory; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses that تخافت implies speech without quoting it.
  68:24/q01/p2 أن introduces a verbal-noun clause conveying the content or purpose of their whispering. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn allow this alongside the explanatory parse. Ālūsī says an omitted ب is understood before the verbal noun.
  68:24/q01/p3 Without أن, an unspoken verb of saying or قول introduces the quoted prohibition. — reasons: Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī supply a speech verb for the reading without أن. Abū Ḥayyān also gives this analysis; Samīn calls it the Basran analysis, and Ālūsī reports the supplied قول.
  68:24/q01/p4 Without أن, يتخافتون itself governs the quoted prohibition because it conveys speech. — reasons: Naḥḥās says the particle may be left out because يتخافتون means speaking secretly. Abū Ḥayyān offers this alternative; Samīn identifies it as Kufan analysis, also reported by Ālūsī.
68:24/q02 How is the opening of the prohibition read? [أَن لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا · readings] (turns on: The reported forms differ in the presence of أن; Samīn's quoted form also lacks the emphatic ن found in Ibn ʿAṭiyya's and Abū Ḥayyān's quoted form.)
  68:24/q02/p1 The cited verse reads أن لا يدخلنها, with أن and the emphatic ن. — reasons: The wording quoted by the verse and discussed by Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī has أن; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that the emphatic ن reinforces the owners' oath.
  68:24/q02/p2 Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh and Ibn Abī ʿAbla are reported to read لا يدخلنها without أن. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī name both readers; Rāzī names Ibn Masʿūd. Ālūsī says the omission supports the explanatory parse of the wording with أن.
  68:24/q02/p3 Samīn reports Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh and Ibn Abī ʿAbla reading لا يدخلها, omitting both أن and the emphatic ن. — reasons: Samīn gives the wording لا يدخلها and offers Basran ellipsis of قول or Kufan treatment of يتخافتون as speech.
68:24/q03 What did the owners resolve by saying no poor person should enter? [لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا ٱلْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكُم مِّسْكِينٌۭ · interpretation]
  68:24/q03/p1 They meant to keep poor people out of the garden that day and deny them any of its harvest. — reasons: Mujāhid, Muqātil, Ibn Qutayba, Yaḥyā ibn Sallām in Māwardī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Ṭabrisī give the exclusion as their private resolve. Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, Elmalılı and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say secrecy kept the poor from hearing or noticing and claiming fruit. Elmalılı says they intended to finish the harvest and leave nothing. Biqāʿī says they planned to search through the day so no poor person could reach fruit or an opening. Bursevī says they barred the poor.
68:24/q04 Why is the ban phrased as a poor person's not entering? [لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا عَلَيْكُم · rhetoric]
  68:24/q04/p1 The phrasing emphatically orders the owners themselves to admit no poor person, rather than addressing the absent poor. — reasons: Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain the intended prohibition as not letting anyone in. Bayḍāwī calls this hyperbolic; Ālūsī compares لا أرينك ههنا. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the emphatic ن reinforces their oath and each brother is to admit none.
68:24/q05 What does miskīn denote here? [مِّسْكِينٌۭ · meaning]
  68:24/q05/p1 A person with nothing, more destitute than a faqīr. — reasons: Bursevī supplies this lexical distinction while explaining whom the owners excluded.
68:24/q06 What claim did poor people have on this harvest? [مِّسْكِينٌۭ · law] (turns on: Jishumī transmits two specifications of the poor person's due, a tenth or fruit falling during harvest; other notes establish a share without fixing its amount.)
  68:24/q06/p1 They had a rightful share of the harvest, without a quantity fixed in these notes. — reasons: Jishumī calls it an enforceable due. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, says the father gave every rightful person a share and his sons withheld an abundant harvest from Muslim poor. Asad cites the long-standing understanding of a harvest share and 6:141. Study Quran cites both ancient Near Eastern gleaning custom and the Quranic command to give the harvest due on harvest day (6:141). Elmalılı says God and needy servants had rights in the orchard.
  68:24/q06/p2 The poor person's due was a tenth of the harvest. — reasons: Jishumī reports this as one identification of their enforceable due.
  68:24/q06/p3 The poor person's due was fruit that fell during picking. — reasons: Jishumī reports this as another identification of their enforceable due; Study Quran separately notes a custom of gleaning.
68:24/q07 Why did the owners whisper their plan? [* · interpretation]
  68:24/q07/p1 They wanted to keep the poor or other listeners from hearing the plan and claiming fruit. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba and Baghawī describe private whispering. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn Kathīr explicitly say the poor or anyone else were not to hear; Elmalılı likewise says no one was to hear. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they consulted quietly lest the poor notice and claim a share. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ explains تخافت as whispering to one another so outsiders would not hear.
68:24/q08 What do reports say about the garden's owners and their decision? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports vary in the owners' provenance, the number and conduct of the sons, and whether the place is specified.)
  68:24/q08/p1 They were Ethiopian sons who called their charitable father foolish and vowed to exclude the poor after his death. — reasons: ʿIkrima, reported by Ṭabarī, says their father fed the poor from his garden. The Ethiopian identification varies from reports that specify another provenance or none.
  68:24/q08/p2 They were sons of a charitable old man who opposed his charity and excluded the poor after he died. — reasons: Qatāda, reported by Ṭabarī, gives this account without naming the place or number of sons.
  68:24/q08/p3 Five sons inherited from a father who gave everyone their due; four planned to deny Muslim poor an unusually abundant crop, and the moderate brother objected. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, gives the number, the unusual abundance, the intended recipients and the dissent.
  68:24/q08/p4 Exegetical elaboration makes the garden the estate of a righteous man left to his people and town. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this elaboration and notes variant provenance reports: near Ṣanʿāʾ in Yemen, Abyssinia, or among People of Scripture.
68:24/q09 Where do reports place the garden or its people? [* · reports] (turns on: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports alternative provenance traditions; ʿIkrima specifically calls the owners Ethiopians.)
  68:24/q09/p1 The owners were Ethiopians or the garden was in Abyssinia. — reasons: ʿIkrima in Ṭabarī calls the owners Ethiopians; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports Abyssinia as one proposed setting.
  68:24/q09/p2 The garden was in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this as one proposed setting.
  68:24/q09/p3 The garden's people belonged to the People of Scripture. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this as another provenance account.
68:24/q10 How does the next verse's ḥard characterize their plan? [* · links] (turns on: Qushayrī reports ability and anger as alternatives; Bursevī brings both into his account of ḥard.)
  68:24/q10/p1 They thought themselves capable of carrying out the exclusion. — reasons: Qushayrī offers ability as one interpretation of ḥard. Bursevī says they thought they could deny the poor their share, yet achieved only their own deprivation.
  68:24/q10/p2 They approached the poor with angry, forceful prevention. — reasons: Qushayrī offers anger at the poor as another interpretation. Bursevī defines ḥard as forceful prevention accompanied by anger.
68:24/q11 What should the owners have remembered instead of excluding the poor? [* · interpretation] (turns on: These notes emphasize different neglected duties: invoking God, honoring needy people's rights, and preserving mutual trust.)
  68:24/q11/p1 They should have made an exception by saying إن شاء الله and remembered God. — reasons: Farrāʾ explains the justest brother's later call to tasbīḥ this way, invoking the instruction at 18:24 to remember God after forgetting.
  68:24/q11/p2 They should have remembered God's gift and protection of the orchard and the rights of God and needy people in it. — reasons: Elmalılı says God gave and guarded the orchard while they slept; gratitude called for generosity, sharing the crop and cultivating beneficiaries and well-wishers rather than enemies through greed and despotism.
  68:24/q11/p3 They should have remembered their own reliance on oaths and mutual trust the previous day. — reasons: Elmalılı says they had needed oaths to reassure each other and establish their company; the yield of the orchard would likewise depend on trust.
68:24/q12 What does their exclusion teach about its consequences and social wrong? [* · interpretation] (turns on: The notes draw complementary lessons about the owners' ensuing deprivation and the social wrong of withholding the poor's due.)
  68:24/q12/p1 Their attempt to deprive the poor turned into their own loss and deprivation. — reasons: Bursevī says they thought they could refuse the poor their share but only deprived themselves. Al-Kashshāf, reported by Bursevī, says they intended to distress the poor although able to benefit them, then lost their wealth and suffered deprivation.
