## THE CURRENT MAP
12:45/q01 Who is the one who survived from the two? [ٱلَّذِى نَجَا مِنْهُمَا · referent]
  12:45/q01/p1 The surviving man is the king's cupbearer or wine server, Joseph's former fellow prisoner. — reasons: Ibn Abbas identifies him as the servant who dreamed of pressing wine (Wahidi); Baghawi says he survived execution, and Tabari's Ibn Ishaq account and Ibn Abi Hatim's Mujahid name him Nabu. Others identify him through his earlier dream and Joseph's correct interpretation.
12:45/q02 What brought the survivor to remember Joseph, and what did he recall? [وَٱدَّكَرَ · reports]
  12:45/q02/p1 The failed attempt to explain the king's dream prompted the cupbearer to recall Joseph, his reliable interpretations, and his request to be mentioned. — reasons: Muqatil, Mujahid (Ibn Abi Hatim), Kalbi (Wahidi), Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi's Kashifi, Ibn Ashur, and Study Quran recall the accurate interpretations of the cupbearer and baker's dreams. Qurtubi and Study Quran connect the request to 12:42. The recollection occurs during the court discussion.
  12:45/q02/p2 The verse simply states that the survivor remembered Joseph or his request after a lapse, without a specified immediate trigger. — reasons: Ibn Kathir, Baydawi, Alusi and others give the delayed recollection as the event itself; Tabari's Ibn Ishaq account names Nabu.
12:45/q03 Whose earlier forgetting does this recollection indicate? [وَٱدَّكَرَ · links] (turns on: Whether the form ادكر itself entails earlier forgetfulness and how 12:42 is read.)
  12:45/q03/p1 The cupbearer forgot Joseph or forgot to mention him to the king. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya calls the verb evidence, while leaving the issue open and comparing 18:63. Tabatabai says 12:45 confirms the cupbearer as the forgetter in 12:42. Tabatabai and Islahi specify forgetting to mention Joseph; Ibn Ashur says calling the period an ummah magnifies this delay.
  12:45/q03/p2 The verb can mean remembering or recounting without establishing prior forgetting by the cupbearer. — reasons: Ibn al-Anbari, reported by Wahidi and Razi, compares reflexive verbs with simple ones, including the use of اعتدى in 2:194. Ibn Jawzi notes that this allows the earlier forgetter to be Joseph. Islahi calls the words for remembrance synonymous.
12:45/q04 Why did mention of Joseph wait until this moment? [وَٱدَّكَرَ بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · reports] (turns on: Whether he forgot, deliberately withheld the information from fear, or both men forgot.)
  12:45/q04/p1 The cupbearer actually forgot; Joseph's continued imprisonment was God's decree. — reasons: Wahidi's Kalbi and Qurtubi report divine decree behind the delay. Tabatabai says he failed to mention Joseph to his lord for years; Ibn Ashur portrays a prolonged forgetting.
  12:45/q04/p2 He remembered but concealed Joseph because mentioning the prison offense might remind the king of his own offense. — reasons: Wahidi's Kalbi reporting Ibn Abbas, Ibn Jawzi's Abu Salih reporting Ibn Abbas, Razi, and Qurtubi give this motive.
  12:45/q04/p3 Both Joseph and the cupbearer forgot. — reasons: Razi transmits this as an alternative to the fear of recalling the cupbearer's crime.
12:45/q05 What does وَٱدَّكَرَ mean, and how is its form derived? [وَٱدَّكَرَ · grammar]
  12:45/q05/p1 It means he remembered; the form develops from a root of remembrance through substitution and assimilation ending in doubled dāl. — reasons: Majaz derives it from dhakartu. Akhfash explains the underlying اذتكر and preservation of voicing; Nahhas and Qurtubi say voiced dhāl cannot assimilate to unvoiced tāʾ, so tāʾ changes to dāl. Zajjaj, Tabrisi, Ibn Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Ibn Ashur detail the sequence. Ibn Ashur calls dāl lighter and more eloquent; Ibn Atiyya compares other Arabic forms and 54:15. The Prophet's recitation of مدكر at 54:15 is reported from the Sahih by Ibn Ashur. Wāhidi refers to that same passage. Bursevi gives idh-tadhakkara, and Islahi calls the remembrance forms synonymous.
  12:45/q05/p2 It can mean he remembered and told or reported the matter, without implying an earlier lapse. — reasons: Ibn al-Anbari, cited by Wahidi and Razi, argues from Arabic reflexive forms that can equal simple verbs, with 2:194 as an example. Biqai parses it as seeking remembrance.
  12:45/q05/p3 A reported analysis begins by replacing tāʾ with dhāl before assimilation. — reasons: Akhfash reports this alternative morphological route; his comparison with forms at 4:128 concerns the voicing and emphatic sound that govern assimilation.
12:45/q06 How is وَٱدَّكَرَ read? [وَٱدَّكَرَ · readings] (turns on: The consonant assimilation and the reported reciters' forms.)
  12:45/q06/p1 The prevalent dāl reading is وَٱدَّكَرَ. — reasons: Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi and Alusi explicitly favor its eloquence; Samīn calls it common and compares مدكر at 54:15. The assimilation is described by Zajjaj and Ibn Ashur.
  12:45/q06/p2 Al-Hasan reads a form with dhāl rather than the usual dāl. — reasons: Kashshaf links wa-dhakara to original tadhakkara; Ibn Jawzi calls it a doubled dhāl. Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn, Razi and Alusi give variant vocalizations of the form. Alusi and others regard the dāl form as more eloquent.
  12:45/q06/p3 Mujahid read the verb with a jussive ending and softened consonant. — reasons: Durr preserves this distinct report without further explanation.
12:45/q07 How is the recollection clause connected syntactically? [وَٱدَّكَرَ بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the remembrance supplies circumstance or another description of the survivor.)
  12:45/q07/p1 The recollection clause is circumstantial to the survivor, with بعد governed by ادكر. — reasons: Samīn prefers attachment to the relative pronoun or its referent, the subject of نجا. Abu Hayyan and Bursevi call the clause circumstantial; Samīn explicitly makes ادكر govern بعد. Alusi allows a circumstantial analysis with an understood قد.
  12:45/q07/p2 It may coordinate with نجا in the relative clause. — reasons: Samīn offers this as an alternative. Alusi rejects joining the later recollection to the relative clause, since it is new information rather than a known description.
12:45/q08 What does بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ mean, including its reported readings? [بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · meaning] (turns on: The transmitted vowel and consonant forms support time, forgetting, a community, or a favor; the favor's recipient is disputed.)
  12:45/q08/p1 It means after a period, usually a long span or years. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba and Bursevi specify seven years; Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn Abbas, Dahhak, Saʿid ibn Jubayr and Mujahid, with Dahhak leaving the term to God. Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas, Hasan, Mujahid, the majority, Ata and language scholars; its large-group sense underlies a span of time. Ibn Durustawayh requires an omitted noun, and Akhfash says ummah is formally singular but collective, with a dog-community hadith. Ibn Ashur explains a generation-length span and cites 3:110; Bursevi compares a collective of days and hours. Qurtubi mentions 11:8.
  12:45/q08/p2 It means after forgetfulness, read with an open or light form, sometimes with hāʾ and sometimes a still mīm. — reasons: Farra, Majaz and Ibn Qutayba report the reading; Ibn Jinni distinguishes أمه from إمة, while Thaʿlabi's Abu Ubayda cites a poem with amihat. Jishumi's Abu Ubayd also cites poetry; Nahhas gives أمه يأمه, 'to forget.' Reports attribute variants to Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, Ikrima, Mujahid, Dahhak, Qatada, Zayd ibn Ali, Abu Raja, Shibl and others. Alusi rejects dismissal of the reading.
  12:45/q08/p3 It means after a group or community of people had passed. — reasons: Al-Hasan's reading is transmitted by Ibn Abi Hatim, Mawardi and Durr. Tabrisi also gives a group united in purpose as a sense of ummah; Qurtubi's Akhfash and the dog hadith illustrate ummah as a community or species.
  12:45/q08/p4 With a kasra reading, إمة means after a blessing or favor. — reasons: Ibn Jinni contrasts it with forgetting. Al-Ashhab al-Uqayli is named by Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn and Alusi. Kashshaf, Razi and Samīn cite ʿAdī ibn Zayd's poetry; Abu Hayyan also reports an open-hamza form for a favorable condition. Interpretations disagree on who received the favor.
12:45/q09 Is the reading of forgetfulness with a still mīm accepted? [أُمَّةٍ · readings] (turns on: The legitimacy of the transmitted still-mīm form versus the proposed regular verbal noun.)
  12:45/q09/p1 The still-mīm form is a transmitted dialectal or irregular verbal noun for forgetting. — reasons: Thaʿlabi's Mujahid, Jishumi's Mujahid, Ibn Atiyya's Mujahid and Shibl, Abu Hayyan's Ikrima/Mujahid/Shibl and Samīn's reports attest it. Jishumi's Abu al-Haytham prefers it and calls the mīm with fatḥa erroneous. Abu Hayyan criticizes dismissing readings; Samīn says an authentic reading cannot be wrong; Alusi likewise dismisses critics.
  12:45/q09/p2 The still-mīm form is erroneous; the verbal noun has a vowel on mīm. — reasons: Zajjaj rejects the still-mīm form attributed to Abu Ubayda and requires fatḥa. Kashshaf calls still mīm an error; Abu Hayyan and Samīn discuss that judgment. Another grammarian in Jishumi accepts the open-mīm form, while Abu al-Haytham rejects it.
12:45/q10 How long was the interval before the cupbearer remembered? [بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · reports] (turns on: The sources give different figures or leave the length unspecified.)
  12:45/q10/p1 The interval was seven years. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba and Bursevi state seven; Baghawi says Joseph had remained in prison seven years; Study Quran connects the seven years with 12:42.
  12:45/q10/p2 The interval was four years after the two prisoners separated. — reasons: Mawardi transmits this from Ibn Abbas along with the report that the prison lay outside the city.
  12:45/q10/p3 The interval was two years. — reasons: Ibn Ashur attributes this figure to the Torah while explaining the rhetorical use of ummah.
