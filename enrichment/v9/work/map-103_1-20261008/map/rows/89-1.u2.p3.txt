Suyuti lists extra alifs after waw and certain hamzas, extra ya in specified phrases, and extra waw in ulu and related forms; he reports al-Kirmani's older-script explanation.
89:1/q28 Why are Quranic passages called ayat in the discussion that cites al-Fajr? [* · sciences]
  89:1/q28/p1 An ayah is a transmitted Quranic unit and a sign of its inimitable revelation. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says a Quranic portion can be explicitly complete, understood by ellipsis, or complete by inclusion; he calls ayah a Quranic designation grounded in the text's eloquence and challenge. He distinguishes Torah and Gospel clauses, explaining a narrator's 'ayah of stoning' as analogy, and notes kitab can mean the Quranic verses.
89:1/q29 How are disjoint-letter sura openings explained in the related opening-form discussion? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether a later explicit mention of the Quran is needed.)
  89:1/q29/p1 Their relation to later Quran references reflects the audience's attention and the weight of revelation. — reasons: Bint al-Shati reports al-Razi's explanations that mentioning the Quran draws attention to all of it and that some openings reflect its obligations; she finds these explanations forced. Al-Zarkashi asks about exceptions to the usual later Quran mention.
  89:1/q29/p2 Every disjoint-letter sura contains a defense of the Quran and its inimitability. — reasons: Bint al-Shati reports Ibn Kathir's inductive explanation for the apparent exceptions.
