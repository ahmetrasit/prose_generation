  18:11/q21/p1 No; recitation must retain its Arabic form, in and out of prayer, even for someone unable to read Arabic. — reasons: Al-Burhan holds that the Qur'an's inimitability is bound to its Arabic form and reports consensus on retaining that form: translation loses expressive precision, and even Arabic paraphrase cannot replace the challenged wording. It reports al-Qaffal's view that a complete Persian translation is impossible, although tafsir can convey some of the meaning.
  18:11/q21/p2 Persian recitation is allowed without qualification, according to a view attributed to Abu Hanifa. — reasons: Al-Burhan reports this attribution to Abu Hanifa but says his retraction is authentic.

18:11/q22 What does the divine plural in فَضَرَبْنَا convey? [فَضَرَبْنَا · rhetoric]
  18:11/q22/p1 It conveys divine majesty and abundance; a following singular indication prevents reading the plural as multiple gods. — reasons: Al-Samarrai says the divine plural appears in contexts of greatness and abundance, while singular forms occur elsewhere, citing 94:1 and 74:11. He says a singular indicator always follows a divine plural to rule out plurality for an audience steeped in shirk, citing 108:1, 97:1, and 48:1.
