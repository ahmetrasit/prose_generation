78:14/q07 Can the verb behind thajjāj be transitive or intransitive? [ثَجَّاجًۭا · grammar] (turns on: Whether the flowing water is the verb's subject or something poured by an agent; al-Alusi says the intransitive is more common.)
  78:14/q07/p1 It can be intransitive: the water flows or pours itself. — reasons: Al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Biqai and Elmalili acknowledge this use. Al-Zajjaj's gloss makes the water pour itself. Al-Alusi prefers it as the more common construction.
  78:14/q07/p2 It can be transitive: someone pours the water. — reasons: Al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Biqai and Elmalili acknowledge this construction; al-Alusi attributes it to al-Zajjaj while preferring the intransitive sense.
78:14/q08 What variant form is reported for thajjāj? [ثَجَّاجًۭا · readings] (turns on: The reported final consonant or form varies, while the notes connect the forms with flowing water.)
  78:14/q08/p1 Al-Aʿraj reads thajjāḥan with ḥāʾ in place of jīm. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and al-Samin report the reading; al-Alusi relates it to mathājiḥ, places where water gathers or runs together. Al-Samin reports al-Zamakhshari's mathājih for spillways and yantajihu for water entering a valley.
  78:14/q08/p2 The form thajjāj and the reported mathājij both denote outflows. — reasons: Al-Baydawi reports both forms in his explanation of pouring and the pilgrimage saying.
78:14/q09 What does this rain sign establish in the surah? [* · interpretation] (turns on: The notes draw different lessons from rain: provision and resurrection, a reproductive image of creation, or a condition concerning the Hour.)
  78:14/q09/p1 Rain and its resulting growth show divine provision, power, and the certainty of resurrection and judgment. — reasons: Al-Biqai says cooling rain yields food and drink; Islahi says sky-earth harmony displays monotheism, providence and accountability, while the signs of mercy, wisdom and power require a fixed reckoning. Kur'an Yolu links the seven heavens, sun, rain clouds and growth to God's power to create the afterlife, citing 2:29 and 67:3. Ibn Ashur says the surah uses creation and humanity's first creation to prove resurrection, warn of punishment and contrast believers' reward; Muqatil says each sign is more astonishing than resurrection.
  78:14/q09/p2 Sky, earth, water, and vegetation form a reproductive analogy. — reasons: Al-Biqai likens sky to male, earth to female, water to semen, and plants to offspring; he says springs and pools are implied.
  78:14/q09/p3 The Hour cannot come while any water remains. — reasons: Muqatil adds this consequence while explaining the rain from the lowest heaven's sea of provisions and cites 51:22.
78:14/q10 How does this surah connect with al-Mursalāt? [* · links]
  78:14/q10/p1 Both use signs of providence and pointed questions to establish judgment and address deniers. — reasons: Islahi says the two surahs use providence across heaven and earth to establish a purposeful world's judgment, rewarding the God-fearing and punishing rebels. Their affirmative-question style reproaches deniers while glad tidings for believers are understood against the warning.
78:14/q11 What names of this surah are reported? [* · sciences]
  78:14/q11/p1 Five names: al-Nabaʾ, ʿAmma yatasaʾalūn, ʿAmma, al-Tasāʾul, and al-Muʿṣirāt. — reasons: Ibn Ashur lists these five and says al-Itqan lists four.
78:14/q12 Where and when was this surah revealed? [* · sciences]
  78:14/q12/p1 It is Meccan; Jabir ibn Zayd's order places it after al-Maʿārij and before al-Nāziʿāt. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reports the place and relative revelation order.
78:14/q13 How many verses are counted in this surah? [* · sciences] (turns on: Regional verse-counting traditions differ.)
  78:14/q13/p1 Forty verses in Medinan, Syrian, and Basran counts. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reports the regional counts.
  78:14/q13/p2 Forty-one verses in Meccan and Kufan counts. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reports the regional counts.
78:14/q14 What does māʾ mean here, and what is its lexical derivation? [مَآءًۭ · meaning]
  78:14/q14/p1 It is rain in this verse; māʾ denotes life-sustaining flowing water and derives from m-w-h. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī defines water as a flowing substance sustaining animals and quenching thirst, and reports a definition calling it subtle, porous, and naturally seeking rest. He gives the root موه, diminutive مويه, plurals مياه and أمواه, and relational forms مائي and ماوي. He reports exegetes' identification of the water sent down from clouds here as rain, comparing 15:22 and 25:48.
78:14/q15 How are ellipsis and case understood in poetic citations using al-muʿṣirāt? [ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the cited noun is accusative under an omitted verb or nominative in an identifying nominal clause.)
  78:14/q15/p1 An accusative noun may be governed by an omitted verb understood from context, such as 'I recall' before 'dwellings'. — reasons: Sibawayh supplies أذكر in Dhu al-Rumma's verse about Mayya's dwellings and rain clouds; he contrasts accusative الديار with the nominative and adduces omitted verbs in ولا زعماتك, familiar prohibitions, poetic accusatives, and formulas such as انتهوا خيرا لكم (4:171). He reports al-Khalīl's explanation of the latter as an understood second action, and notes that a parallel imperative can be explicit. Al-Jurjānī likewise supplies أذكر before accusative ديار and says omission can heighten eloquence.
  78:14/q15/p2 A nominative noun may form an identifying nominal clause, with a demonstrative, subject, or predicate supplied from context. — reasons: Sibawayh reads nominative الديار as identification of Mayya's dwellings and reports Tamim speakers' supplied predicates in كلاهما وتمرا and كل شيء. Al-Jurjānī supplies ذاك or هو in ربع قواء أذاع المعصرات به and تلك before دار in another line; he notes the common omission of a subject at a fresh start. His teacher rejects taking الربع as a substitute for الطلل because a substitute must equal or exceed its antecedent, whereas a quarter is larger than a ruin trace.
78:14/q16 What other Quranic uses share the root of al-muʿṣirāt? [ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ · meaning]
  78:14/q16/p1 This form is unique in the Quran, while related forms appear in al-ʿAsr, Yusuf's dream and harvest year, and the whirlwind in al-Baqara. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes the unique form and compares 103:1, 12:36, 12:49, and 2:266, along with the following growth verses 78:15–16.
