## THE CURRENT MAP
68:26/q01 Who owned the garden before these people? [رَأَوْهَا · reports]
  68:26/q01/p1 It belonged to a righteous man, who left it to his people and town. — reasons: Bint al-Shati reports this as part of the exegetes' elaboration of the garden story.
68:26/q02 Where or among whom do reports place the garden? [رَأَوْهَا · reports] (turns on: The reported settings differ; the note gives no grounds for choosing among them.)
  68:26/q02/p1 In Yemen near Sana'a. — reasons: Bint al-Shati lists this among the reported settings, alongside Abyssinia and the People of Scripture.
  68:26/q02/p2 In Abyssinia. — reasons: Bint al-Shati lists this as another reported setting.
  68:26/q02/p3 Among the People of Scripture. — reasons: Bint al-Shati lists this as another reported association.
68:26/q03 How soon did they speak after seeing it? [فَلَمَّا رَأَوْهَا قَالُوٓا۟ · grammar]
  68:26/q03/p1 They spoke immediately and instinctively on seeing it. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the answer to لما follows its condition immediately.
68:26/q04 What does the plural attribution of their words show? [قَالُوٓا۟ · rhetoric]
  68:26/q04/p1 All the owners agreed about the cause of their misfortune. — reasons: Ibn Ashur draws this from the plural attribution and relates it to the owners in 68:17.
68:26/q05 What did they mean by 'We are astray'? [إِنَّا لَضَآلُّونَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the destroyed orchard made them think they had taken a wrong road, or they already recognized moral error; the moral readings differ over excluding the poor, straying from truth, and ingratitude.)
  68:26/q05/p1 They thought they had lost the road and arrived somewhere other than their garden. — reasons: Muqatil, Qatada as reported by Abd al-Razzaq, al-Tabari, al-Jishumi, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi, and Ibn Abbas as reported by Ibn Kathir and al-Durr give the wrong-road sense. Al-Farra and al-Tabari say another brother recognized the garden. Al-Zajjaj, al-Nahhas, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi connect their mistake to its burned, blackened or ruined condition. Al-Mawardi calls it bare; al-Biqa'i says there was no trace of crop or fruit and it no longer resembled their orchard. Al-Baydawi and al-Islahi stress their initial failure to recognize it. Al-Baghawi adds that they had denied the poor and omitted the exception; Ibn Atiyya and al-Zajjaj say they later recognized God's affliction, and al-Razi relates the loss to their miserly resolve. Elmalili says the deprivation exceeded what they had planned for the poor and led to despair of present and future benefit. Ibn Ashur says بل in the next verse corrects their mistaken identification of the site. Al-Tabatabai reports this view but adopts the moral reading; the Study Quran reports it as an alternative and also uses it in its allusion to Muhammad's opponents.
  68:26/q05/p2 They realized that trying to exclude the poor was a moral error and had brought punishment or loss upon them. — reasons: Al-Jishumi reports a reading that they strayed by withholding the poor's due. Al-Razi says they expected to profit from barring the poor but discovered that they themselves were deprived. Al-Qurtubi says they recognized their wrongful dawn plan and the destruction as punishment. Abu Hayyan says their treachery rebounded on them. Al-Tabatabai says they had strayed from right conduct in trying to harvest while excluding the poor and then acknowledged the lost crop. Al-Alusi reports this interpretation but finds it unpersuasive.
  68:26/q05/p3 They recognized that they had strayed from truth or done wrong and were being punished. — reasons: Al-Jishumi reports straying from the truth and being punished for their conduct as an alternative to the specific interpretation about withholding the poor's due. The Study Quran also reports recognition of wrongdoing without specifying its form.
  68:26/q05/p4 They acknowledged straying from gratitude and understood the disaster as punishment for ingratitude. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the moral use of 'astray' is metaphorical and signals recognition of their failure to thank God.
68:26/q06 Why is their declaration emphatic? [إِنَّا لَضَآلُّونَ · rhetoric]
  68:26/q06/p1 Its emphasis expresses grief and regret at their newly recognized heedlessness. — reasons: Ibn Ashur connects the emphatic form with the freshness of their recognition.
68:26/q07 How did their next words correct this first statement? [لَضَآلُّونَ · links] (turns on: Whether the first words mistook the garden's location or confessed their wrong plan, and thus what the next declaration corrects.)
  68:26/q07/p1 After initially mistaking the ruined garden for another place, they recognized it and said that they had been deprived of its produce and blessing. — reasons: Al-Farra and al-Tabari describe a brother recognizing the garden. Qatada as reported by Abd al-Razzaq and Abu Hayyan, and Ibn Abbas as reported by Ibn Kathir, give the progression from wrong path to deprivation. Al-Zajjaj and Ibn al-Jawzi say they recognized punishment; al-Baghawi attributes it to denying the poor and omitting the exception. Ibn Atiyya specifies lost yield and blessing, while al-Razi says their miserly resolve brought the loss. Al-Nasafi says they recognized the garden after looking closely. Elmalili says they found deprivation greater than they intended for the poor, with despair of present and future benefit. Ibn Ashur says بل corrects the location mistake: this was their garden, destroyed and stripped of produce. Al-Islahi says they soon realized their prized possession was gone.
  68:26/q07/p2 After acknowledging their wrong plan, they corrected themselves by saying that they were deprived of the crop. — reasons: Al-Razi says they had expected benefit despite their resolve to bar the poor. Abu Hayyan says 'we are deprived' recognizes that their treachery rebounded on them. Al-Tabatabai says their first words confess straying from right conduct, while the correction names the actual loss.
68:26/q08 What lesson does their reaction to the ruined garden convey? [* · interpretation] (turns on: The notes draw different lessons from the same reaction: repentance through trial, the loss caused by ingratitude, and an analogy to Muhammad's opponents.)
  68:26/q08/p1 A trial can awaken people to their fault and turn them back. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the owners woke from heedlessness, blamed their pride and failure to thank God, and fulfilled the lesson of 7:168; their quick regret and hope for a better replacement urge repentance.
  68:26/q08/p2 Ingratitude exposes blessings to loss, while gratitude preserves them. — reasons: Ibn Ashur links their lost garden to failure to thank God and quotes Ibn Ata Allah al-Iskandari's maxim that gratitude binds blessings fast.
  68:26/q08/p3 Muhammad's opponents, though thinking themselves guided, will recognize their error and should repent promptly. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the story urges idolaters to repent as quickly as the owners regretted. The Study Quran relates their initial mistake and eventual recognition to the opponents' mistaken confidence, citing 68:5-7.
