## THE CURRENT MAP
12:42/q01 Who expected one prisoner to survive, and how certain was that expectation? [ظَنَّ أَنَّهُۥ نَاجٍۢ مِّنْهُمَا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether ظن names Joseph's certainty or inference, or the prisoner's response to Joseph's revealed interpretation.)
  12:42/q01/p1 Joseph knew the cupbearer would survive; “thought” expresses certain knowledge or conviction. — reasons: Al-Tabari says prophetic prediction cannot be falsely certain; Ibn Atiyya and al-Tabataba'i cite قضي الأمر; al-Tabrisi cites 69:20 and al-Alusi and al-Tabataba'i cite 2:46. Al-Biqa'i says human knowledge is modest beside God's; al-Alusi notes the imperfect prepares Joseph's request. Al-Samarra'i calls zann the highest degree of knowledge, an apprehension reaching certainty, and cites 69:20 and 2:249.
  12:42/q01/p2 Joseph inferred the cupbearer's survival with ordinary, fallible conjecture. — reasons: Qatada says dream interpretation is conjectural; al-Wahidi adds that God may confirm or annul it; al-Mawardi proposes Joseph doubted whether the prisoners told true dreams. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan object that قضي الأمر sounds decisive unless it merely ends the interpretation. Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, and al-Samin tie this to interpretation by reasoning.
  12:42/q01/p3 The saved prisoner himself expected release. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf and followers propose this if Joseph interpreted through revelation: Joseph knew, while the hearer only expected, since the men had not accepted his prophethood (al-Razi). Ibn Atiyya says joy raised the prisoner's expectation.
12:42/q02 How does “among the two” attach grammatically? [مِّنْهُمَا نَاجٍۢ · grammar]
  12:42/q02/p1 It may describe the saved one or be circumstantial from the relative pronoun, but it is not a complement meaning escape from both men. — reasons: Al-Samin records Abu al-Baqa's objection to taking the preposition as a complement of ناج; al-Alusi gives the same constraint.
12:42/q03 Who is the “lord” in Joseph's request? [رَبِّكَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the report's identified master is the reigning king or the Aziz.)
  12:42/q03/p1 The cupbearer's master is the king of Egypt. — reasons: Al-Mawardi and Ibn al-Jawzi name him al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan. Ibn Qutayba and al-Qurtubi cite al-A'sha's poetry for rabb meaning master; al-Qurtubi cites 12:23 and ordinary managerial usage. Al-Razi answers the objection that this would make Joseph affirm another god. Al-Suyuti identifies the king as Rayyan ibn al-Walid.
  12:42/q03/p2 The master meant in one transmitted report is the Aziz. — reasons: Al-Tabataba'i quotes a report in al-Ayyashi from Sama'a.
12:42/q04 What did Joseph ask the cupbearer to tell his master, and why? [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether Joseph's desired audience with the king chiefly served release, testimony to his qualities, or religious guidance.)
  12:42/q04/p1 Tell the king of Joseph's wrongful imprisonment and innocence so he could be released. — reasons: Al-Tabari says he had committed no crime; al-Razi includes his brothers' sale and the accusation that led to prison. Ibn Kathir says Joseph spoke privately so the baker would not learn his fate. Al-Iṣlāḥī says other paths to justice had closed. Al-Damghani and Ibn al-Jawzi gloss dhikr here as telling the king Joseph's case; al-Suyuti likewise says to speak to the king about Joseph's situation.
  12:42/q04/p2 Tell the king Joseph's knowledge, dream interpretation, virtue, and standing. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan allow the qualities, the wrongful detention, or both; al-Qurtubi combines interpretation and injustice; al-Biqa'i stresses clean character against the charge; al-Iṣlāḥī stresses wisdom and dream interpretation.
  12:42/q04/p3 Use the request to guide the king to faith. — reasons: Abu Hayyan points to Joseph's earlier guidance of both prisoners. Al-Alusi calls it fitting to Joseph's rank but contrary to the apparent sense and reports.
12:42/q05 What happened when Joseph made the request? [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · reports]
  12:42/q05/p1 At or near the prison gate, the released man asked what Joseph wanted, and Joseph asked to be mentioned to the king. — reasons: Ibrahim al-Taymi gives a prison-door or escort setting; al-Alusi specifies the third day. The accounts differ slightly in setting.
  12:42/q05/p2 Joseph asked the surviving prisoner privately to avoid revealing the baker's impending execution. — reasons: Ibn Kathir infers the private setting from Joseph's concern for the other prisoner.
12:42/q06 Whom did Satan cause to forget, and what was forgotten? [فَأَنسَىٰهُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ ذِكْرَ رَبِّهِۦ · referent] (turns on: The antecedents of the two pronouns, the needed ellipsis, Joseph's prophetic protection, and the later recollection in 12:45.)
  12:42/q06/p1 The freed cupbearer forgot to mention Joseph to his master, the king. — reasons: Ibn Ishaq and others give this reading. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi say distraction and palace duties account for the lapse; 12:45 says the man later remembered (al-Qurtubi, al-Alusi, al-Tabataba'i, Kur'an Yolu). Ibn Kathir rejects the alternative reports as weak; al-Tabataba'i invokes Joseph's protection from Satan. Al-Zarkashi allows dhikr as a reminder or verbal noun of mentioning, with the mention attached to the lord by semantic affinity.
  12:42/q06/p2 Joseph forgot to invoke or rely on God and sought help from a human. — reasons: Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Tabrisi and al-Razi favor it; Ibn al-Anbari says grammar favors Joseph because the other reading needs an omitted 'mention him.' Forgetting can mean deliberate leaving of the better course rather than involuntary lapse (Ibn al-Anbari, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Alusi). Al-Razi says the literal phrasing favors it. Al-Zarkashi reports the interpretation that both pronouns point to Joseph: after asking the rescued man to tell the king his case, Joseph was made to forget his Lord.
  12:42/q06/p3 Both forgettings are intended: Joseph neglected appeal to God, causing the cupbearer to forget him. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the indirect phrasing tactfully reproaches Joseph while also narrating the cupbearer's lapse. Ibn Ashur says both dhikr and rabb carry two meanings in this prison verse, allowing both accounts of the forgotten remembrance and its lord.
12:42/q07 How does “remembrance of his lord” work grammatically? [ذِكْرَ رَبِّهِۦ · grammar] (turns on: Whether 'his lord' is Joseph's God or the cupbearer's king.)
  12:42/q07/p1 On the cupbearer reading, the phrase means mentioning Joseph to his master by association or ellipsis. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf licenses the possessive through association with the king or omission of 'reports'; al-Baydawi proposes mentioning the lord's affairs; Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi explain the loose genitive, while al-Iṣlāḥī compares another annexation. Ibn al-Anbari objects that omission is required. Al-Zarkashi explains the attachment of 'mention' to the lord through semantic affinity.
  12:42/q07/p2 On the Joseph reading, the possessive directly names God's remembrance. — reasons: Al-Razi and al-Tabari take the wording as a reason to favor Joseph, since the other reading needs an omitted object.
12:42/q08 How can Satan cause forgetfulness? [فَأَنسَىٰهُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · theology]
  12:42/q08/p1 Satan distracts and whispers, while God alone directly effects loss of recall. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf compares the attribution of forgetting and mentions 2:106; al-Razi says Satan cannot erase knowledge from a heart, but can occupy it with other activity. Abu Hayyan says the cupbearer was distracted; al-Iṣlāḥī says Satan makes people forget virtuous deeds. Citing al-Dahhak, Ibn Kathir says remembering God drives Satan from the heart as the call to prayer does; a good deed removes sin and with it a cause of forgetting.
12:42/q09 Why did Joseph remain in prison after the request? [فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether prophetic reports of punishment are accepted, and whether requesting lawful help breaches Joseph's prophetic station.)
  12:42/q09/p1 God lengthened his confinement as reproach or punishment for seeking a human intermediary while forgetting the higher reliance on God. — reasons: Prophetic reports say he would have left sooner without the request; Gabriel and divine-rebuke stories amplify the lesson. Al-Razi compares Abraham in the fire. Some describe a lapse of heedlessness or an elite's departure from the better course. Al-Wahidi says release was near.
  12:42/q09/p2 The cupbearer's forgetting prolonged Joseph's stay as part of God's wise plan; the trial could increase his reward. — reasons: Abu Hayyan holds that the lapse was Satan's stratagem but God used the delay to increase Joseph's reward; al-Iṣlāḥī says plans succeed only by God's wisdom; al-Biqa'i says means have no independent effect. Al-Tabataba'i rejects punishment for lawful use of means.
  12:42/q09/p3 The text leaves the reason for the extra time open, and an exact blame narrative is not established. — reasons: Ibn Kathir calls the Ibn Abbas report very weak due to Sufyan ibn Waki and Ibrahim al-Khuzi, and rejects the mursal Hasan and Qatada versions here. Al-Tabataba'i says the alleged lapses conflict with God's praise and protection of Joseph.
12:42/q10 What does the request teach about seeking help from people? [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · law] (turns on: How lawful practical means relate to trust, prophetic rank, and the attributed cause of Joseph's delay.)
  12:42/q10/p1 Seeking lawful human help is permitted, sometimes required, while prophets or spiritual elect may choose fuller reliance on God. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf cites 5:2, 3:52 and reports of mutual help; al-Tabrisi cites the Prophet's help from the Emigrants and Helpers; Abu Hayyan cites Jesus' helpers and the Prophet's guard. Al-Razi distinguishes legal permission from spiritual rank; al-Alusi calls the higher resolve fitting to prophets.
