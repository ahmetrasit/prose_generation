12:45/q24 How is Joseph characterized in the account that follows? [* · links]
  12:45/q24/p1 Joseph is exceptionally truthful, pious, and skilled in interpreting dreams. — reasons: Razi says the cupbearer praises Joseph's virtue, piety, learning and the accuracy of both prison interpretations. Tabrisi gives ṣiddīq as strongly affirming truth or especially truthful; Abu Hayyan calls it an intensive form grounded in long acquaintance and tested interpretations. Qurtubi notes the singular form.
12:45/q25 What other reports connect this scene to Joseph's imprisonment and life? [* · reports]
  12:45/q25/p1 Gabriel told Joseph he would leave prison the next day and described Jacob's grief and reward. — reasons: Muqatil says Joseph asked after Jacob, whose grief had blinded him, and Gabriel said Jacob's reward equaled one hundred martyrs.
  12:45/q25/p2 Joseph entered the well at seventeen and later lived through eighty years of servitude, prison and kingship, then twenty-three more years after reunion. — reasons: Mawardi transmits this chronology from al-Hasan.
12:45/q26 What broader point is drawn from Joseph's story? [* · interpretation]
  12:45/q26/p1 God directs human affairs in ways people cannot foresee; what they dislike may benefit them and what they favor may harm them. — reasons: Asad contrasts this Qur'anic point with Genesis' romantic account and connects it to 2:216.
