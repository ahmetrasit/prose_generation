93:1/q10 Why does this sūra place day before night? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the order emphasizes daylight's honor, the duration of joy and sorrow, or moral stages.)
  93:1/q10/p1 Brief joy is set against much longer sorrow. — reasons: Bursawi equates one forenoon hour to the whole night as Muhammad equals all prophets. He cites a cloud raining grief for 300 years versus a cloud raining joy for an hour. Bint al-Shati reports the Razi/Nisaburi prophet comparison.
  93:1/q10/p2 Day leads here for its inherent and contextual honor. — reasons: Bursawi says night precedes in al-Layl because darkness is the original state; sunlight makes day, then air returns to darkness at sunset. He cites 6:1.
  93:1/q10/p3 Life is preferred before warning of despair and divine plotting. — reasons: Bint al-Shati reports an interpretation giving this staged moral order.
93:1/q11 What interrupted revelation before this sūra? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports name different circumstances before revelation resumed.)
  93:1/q11/p1 Illness prevented night prayer for one or two, or two or three, nights. — reasons: Jundub's versions vary. Ibn Ashur understands the taunt as a reaction to absent nighttime Quran recitation.
  93:1/q11/p2 Gabriel or revelation paused, with no precipitating cause specified. — reasons: Tabari, Ibn Khalawayh, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Suyuti, Qutb and Study Quran relate pause and ensuing taunt. Qutb says Gabriel's visits sustained the Prophet through rejection and persecution, so absence distressed him.
  93:1/q11/p3 A puppy or dead puppy was in the Prophet's house. — reasons: Zayd ibn Aslam says Gabriel explained angels avoid a house with a dog or image. Khawla and Umm Hafs locate a dead puppy under the bed and give four days. Ibn Hajar says a puppy-related delay is known, but rejects this as the cause of the sūra because it conflicts with sound reports.
  93:1/q11/p4 The Prophet promised answers to Jewish questions without saying 'if God wills'. — reasons: Tha'labi, Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi name Dhu al-Qarnayn, the Cave Companions and the Spirit.
  93:1/q11/p5 A stone injured the Prophet's finger, after which he missed night prayer. — reasons: A second Jundub version gives two or three nights. Ibn Kathir calls the injury authentic but the causal link unusual; Durr cites Tirmidhi's authentication.
  93:1/q11/p6 The Prophet or companions neglected grooming. — reasons: Tha'labi reports the Prophet referring to unclean spaces between fingers and untrimmed nails; Ibn al-Jawzi lists a companion's lack of cleanliness.
93:1/q12 How long was the reported interruption? [* · reports] (turns on: The transmitted numerical counts differ.)
  93:1/q12/p1 Forty days or mornings. — reasons: Muqatil gives forty days; Ibn Khalawayh cites forty mornings; Baghawi and Study Quran include forty.
  93:1/q12/p2 Twelve days. — reasons: Ibn Jurayj gives twelve; Baghawi repeats it and Study Quran includes it.
  93:1/q12/p3 Four days. — reasons: Umm Hafs and Khawla give four days in the dead-puppy account.
  93:1/q12/p4 Fifteen days. — reasons: Ibn Abbas gives fifteen; Baghawi repeats it.
  93:1/q12/p5 Twenty-five days. — reasons: Tha'labi transmits this number without a named transmitter.
  93:1/q12/p6 Fifty days. — reasons: Study Quran includes fifty among the reports.
93:1/q13 Who voiced the claim that the Prophet had been abandoned? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether the speaker was hostile, compassionate, or identified as a cousin's daughter.)
  93:1/q13/p1 Polytheists or Quraysh collectively. — reasons: Their taunt follows Gabriel's delay; Razi/Nisaburi say they lacked proof, and Ibn Ashur ties their insinuation to missed nighttime recitation.
  93:1/q13/p2 A woman, identified in some accounts as Umm Jamil, Abu Lahab's wife. — reasons: Jundub calls her a woman. Tha'labi, Baghawi, Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Kathir, Ibn al-Jawzi and Zayd ibn Arqam name Umm Jamil. She calls his companion a devil or Satan. Durr places her confrontation after Sūrat Abī Lahab. Ibn Hajar says her remark was spiteful.
  93:1/q13/p3 Khadija spoke with concern over apparent rejection. — reasons: Abdullah ibn Shaddad and Urwa transmit her remark after a pause or distress. Ibn Hajar distinguishes compassion from Umm Jamil's spite. Tha'labi relates this to the first pause after al-Alaq. The later reports are mursal.
  93:1/q13/p4 A cousin's daughter said his companion had left him. — reasons: Durr's Jundub report gives this identification.
93:1/q14 What followed Gabriel's return and reassurance? [* · reports] (turns on: The accounts describe different responses to Gabriel's return.)
  93:1/q14/p1 The Prophet and Gabriel expressed mutual longing; Gabriel said he only descends by God's command. — reasons: Tha'labi and Baghawi transmit the dialogue, and Baghawi relates it to 19:64.
  93:1/q14/p2 The Prophet rejoiced and pronounced takbīr. — reasons: Ibn Khalawayh links the greeting, joy and takbīr to the forty-morning pause and the reassuring revelation.
93:1/q15 How should the following phrase about night be understood beside this oath? [* · links] (turns on: Whether sajā means arrival, departure or stillness.)
  93:1/q15/p1 Night settles, darkens, or grows still among its people. — reasons: Tabrisi cites poetry about a calm sea and night; Ibn Kathir adds darkness. Durr reports Qatada's quiet people. Bint al-Shati reports Tabari, Zamakhshari and Abduh on stillness.
  93:1/q15/p2 Night arrives. — reasons: Durr gives Ibn Abbas's gloss; Bint al-Shati includes this among Tabari's reported alternatives.
  93:1/q15/p3 Night departs. — reasons: Bint al-Shati lists this among Tabari's reported alternatives.
93:1/q16 Where and when in the revelation sequence was this sūra revealed? [* · sciences] (turns on: The notes preserve different revelation-order lists and first-complete-sūra claims.)
  93:1/q16/p1 It is Meccan. — reasons: Baghawi, Abu Hayyan, Durr, Ibn Abbas, Alusi and Bint al-Shati affirm Meccan origin; Study Quran calls it early Meccan. Bint al-Shati says there is no disagreement.
  93:1/q16/p2 It followed al-ʿAlaq after Gabriel's first pause. — reasons: Abdullah ibn Shaddad gives this order and Khadija's concern.
  93:1/q16/p3 It followed 'Read', al-Muddaththir and al-Qalam through 68:4. — reasons: Tha'labi gives this sequence.
  93:1/q16/p4 It is commonly eleventh, after al-Fajr. — reasons: Bint al-Shati gives this revelation rank.
  93:1/q16/p5 It is fourth in one list or seventh in another. — reasons: Study Quran reports both rankings, which vary in the note.
  93:1/q16/p6 It may be the first sūra revealed whole. — reasons: Study Quran reports this and the competing assignment to al-Fatiha.
93:1/q17 How many verses does the sūra have? [* · sciences] (turns on: Different verse counts.)
  93:1/q17/p1 Eleven verses. — reasons: Durr gives eleven; Alusi calls eleven unanimous.
  93:1/q17/p2 Ten verses. — reasons: Baghawi gives ten.
93:1/q18 How is takbīr recited from this sūra onward? [* · readings]
  93:1/q18/p1 Say Allāhu akbar at each sūra ending from al-Ḍuḥā through the Qur'an's end. — reasons: Ibn Kathir's practice includes the basmala; Durr reports a Prophetic instruction. Ibn Khalawayh links the practice with the Prophet's joy when Gabriel returned.
93:1/q19 What reward is reported for reciting the sūra? [* · sciences]
  93:1/q19/p1 Intercession and reward for every orphan and asker. — reasons: Jishumi transmits a Prophetic report through Ubayy scaling the reward to every such person.
