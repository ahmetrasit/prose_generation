18:16/q33 What pause treatments are given for the hamza in and around yuhayyiʾ? [وَيُهَيِّئْ · readings] (turns on: The hamza's written seat, preceding letter, vowel, and whether its sukun is obligatory.)
  18:16/q33/p1 The written seat governs the pause: wāw or yāʾ permits eased rūm, while alif calls for substitution. — reasons: Some readers distinguish these cases, and Makki and Ibn Shurayḥ choose the distinction.
  18:16/q33/p2 Eased rūm applies after an alif, but not after a moving letter. — reasons: Ibn Balima draws this restriction.
  18:16/q33/p3 Eased rūm extends through the three hamza vowels, with two options after a moving letter for ḍamma and kasra. — reasons: Ibn al-Faḥḥām gives these options; Ibn Sawwar agrees for hamza after alif.
  18:16/q33/p4 Eased rūm is allowed for every hamza vowel after either alif or another letter, even with fatḥa. — reasons: Some give this exceptional option; al-Dani records it without claiming to have received it, and Ibn Ghalbun records but disapproves of it.
  18:16/q33/p5 At pause, a necessarily silent hamza is replaced by a long-vowel letter that jussive syntax cannot remove. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari applies this to yuhayyiʾ and rejects both full hamza articulation and deletion of the substitute at pause.
  18:16/q33/p6 For a ḍamma hamza after kasra, five transmitted pause faces include yāʾ substitution, rūm or ishām, and two eased articulations. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari lists these for yunsʾi and similar forms, attributing one yāʾ form to al-Akhfash and a wāw-like easing on rūm to Sibawayh.
18:16/q34 What length follows when a final hamza after alif is substituted at a pause? [وَيُهَيِّئْ · readings] (turns on: Whether one alif is deleted and, if so, which one.)
  18:16/q34/p1 Deleting one of the meeting alifs gives shortness if the first is removed, or short and long measures if the second is removed. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari distinguishes the two deletions; Makki permits length on deleting the second and shortness on deleting the first.
  18:16/q34/p2 Both alifs can remain at pause, with a medium measure also allowed by cited authorities. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari says pause permits the two alifs to remain, and he reports authorities who allow a medium measure in that case.
18:16/q35 How is rāʾ thickened or thinned in mirfaq and related recitation rules? [مِّرْفَقًۭا · readings] (turns on: Whether mīm's kasra is necessary or incidental, and whether tafkhīm is the default for rāʾ.)
  18:16/q35/p1 The rāʾ of mirfaq is thinned because the preceding mīm's kasra is necessary. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazari prefers tarqīq even though mīm is an added letter, since removing it damages the word; he compares Warsh's al-miḥrāb and ikhrājan.
  18:16/q35/p2 The rāʾ of mirfaq is thickened because the added mīm's kasra is incidental. — reasons: Some authorities thicken it on this analysis; another view calls every kasra on an added letter incidental.
  18:16/q35/p3 In general, tafkhīm is the rāʾ's default and tarqīq needs a cause; rāʾ after ḍamma or before an istiʿlāʾ letter stays thick. — reasons: The readers agree on those thickening cases, rejecting a contrary attribution to Warsh; the majority and Makki treat thinning as the restricted case.
  18:16/q35/p4 Rāʾ is neutral between thick and thin, its vowel influence being especially strong. — reasons: Other authorities cite the tongue's position near the upper palate and rāʾ's repeated articulation.
