12:35/q08 Why did they want Joseph imprisoned? [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: The sources attribute overlapping aims to different actors: rulers' public reputation, public inference of guilt, the wife's desire to control Joseph, or a provisional wait.)
  12:35/q08/p1 To quiet gossip, conceal the scandal, protect the household's reputation, and separate Joseph from the wife for a time. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi prefers this account; al-Razi says the Aziz sought to quiet scandal; Ibn Kathir calls it a conjecture and al-Suddi says her behavior would otherwise be exposed while Joseph's name was cleared. The wife feared discussion after the women dispersed, according to Ibn Ashur; Islahi says confinement could reduce her infatuation. Al-Tabataba'i also suggests avoiding disorder or wider infatuation with Joseph's beauty. Al-Kuranyolu says the household sought to conceal the affair.
  12:35/q08/p2 To make the public believe Joseph was guilty because criminals are imprisoned. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi and al-Baydawi give the appearance of guilt as a motive; Bursavi assigns it especially to the wife. Ibn Ashur says the wife meant to conceal Joseph's innocence, and Islahi says people would infer he was punished. A report from Ikrima says an adviser expected blame to shift to the household if Joseph spoke freely.
  12:35/q08/p3 The wife hoped prison would humble, punish, or coerce Joseph into yielding to her. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf gives both her possible loss of hope and her hope for submission; al-Qurtubi reports concealment and renewed desire or a plot to wear him down. Al-Nasafi, Bursavi, al-Alusi, al-Tabataba'i, Asad and Kuranyolu state variants of her coercive purpose. Al-Tabrisi reports the prison was near her so she could see him from her window.
  12:35/q08/p4 They meant to observe Joseph's behavior and decide their next course later. — reasons: Al-Tabari says the decision was provisional; al-Baydawi says they would see what happened; al-Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan connect the term to what the wife would see Joseph do.
12:35/q09 How does 'they will surely imprison him' fit grammatically after 'badā'? [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the oath force is supplied by implicit speech or an interrogative setting, and whether emphatic nūn can occur after a question.)
  12:35/q09/p1 It is the response to an unspoken oath, introduced through an implicit statement of their resolve. — reasons: Al-Farra says the lām works after expressions of speech, opinion, or knowledge and cites 12:26, 12:36, 12:80. Al-Tabari's rejecting grammarians say emphatic nūn does not follow a plain question. The Kufan explanation lets badā carry 'they said/decided'; al-Samin says the majority posit implied speech. Al-Alusi allows an implied saying or explanatory clause. Al-Tabataba'i calls the lām an oath of firm resolve, and Ibn Ashur treats the oath clause as suspended from badā.
  12:35/q09/p2 An interrogative environment licenses the emphatic nūn and lām. — reasons: Al-Akhfash says the clause can host a question like 'which of them will take someone'; a Basran explanation makes an interrogative with ay implicit. The Kufan grammarian allows speech after badā to introduce either an oath or question. Other grammarians object that emphatic nūn would not follow a question such as 'will you stand?'.
  12:35/q09/p3 The heavy emphatic nūn belongs to this form, with different pause and connected forms. — reasons: Al-Jishumi contrasts the heavy nūn in la-yasjunanna with the light or heavy emphatic nūn in la-yakūnan, and compares the pause form with la-nasfaʿan.
12:35/q10 How is the verb 'they will imprison him' alternatively read? [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · readings] (turns on: Whether the resolve is narrated in the third person or voiced as a direct address to the ruler or his group.)
  12:35/q10/p1 With third-person yāʾ: 'they will surely imprison him.' — reasons: Al-Wahidi says the third person follows the unseen subject; the written verse has this form. Al-Hasan's alternative addresses the Aziz or his circle.
  12:35/q10/p2 Al-Hasan reads with address tāʾ: 'you will surely imprison him.' — reasons: Al-Kashshaf says the address may target the Aziz and those around him, or the Aziz alone deferentially. Al-Baydawi allows people addressing the ruler honorifically or the ruler addressing attendants; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin also give singular or respectful plural possibilities.
12:35/q11 How is 'ḥattā' alternatively read? [حَتَّىٰ · readings] (turns on: Dialectal substitution of ʿayn for ḥāʾ and Umar's preference for Quraysh recitation.)
  12:35/q11/p1 The ordinary form is ḥattā with ḥāʾ, identified as Quraysh usage. — reasons: The report of Umar's letter instructs Ibn Masud to teach Quraysh Arabic after a Hudhayli recitation. Ibn Jinni says the common form is preferable although the other is permissible, since ḥāʾ and ʿayn have close articulation points. Al-Samin says ḥattā marks the endpoint of what precedes.
  12:35/q11/p2 Ibn Masud's Hudhayl form reads ʿattā with ʿayn. — reasons: Umar heard this form, asked who had taught it, and wrote Ibn Masud to teach Quraysh usage; al-Durr adds that the Qur'an was revealed in clear Quraysh Arabic. Ibn Jinni explains the consonant exchange by proximity of articulation and allows the variant; al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi identify it as Hudhayl speech.
12:35/q12 What does 'ḥīn' mean as a word? [حِينٍۢ · meaning] (turns on: Whether ḥīn is taken in its unrestricted lexical range or assigned a length from a particular usage or report.)
  12:35/q12/p1 An indefinite span of time, short or long; the word itself fixes no length. — reasons: The linguists in al-Wahidi and al-Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Bursavi and al-Alusi distinguish lexical indefiniteness from estimates of Joseph's actual confinement. Ibn Atiyya says Qur'anic usage supports short and long spans; Abu Hayyan says any specific duration comes from the prison report, not the lexeme. Bursavi says a juristic unqualified oath of 'a while' defaults to six months unless a particular period was meant.
  12:35/q12/p2 It can denote a morning or evening. — reasons: Ibn Abbas is reported with this lexical possibility.
  12:35/q12/p3 It can denote six months. — reasons: Sa'id ibn Jubayr gives this meaning, with Tariq reported to agree; Bursavi says unqualified juristic oaths use six months unless a specific duration was intended.
  12:35/q12/p4 It can denote seven years. — reasons: Ikrima is reported with this lexical interpretation.
12:35/q13 What period or endpoint was intended by 'until a time'? [حَتَّىٰ حِينٍۢ · reports] (turns on: The verse gives no number; sources differ between the rulers' intended endpoint and reports estimating Joseph's actual stay. Transmissions even vary for Ikrima, al-Kalbi and Muqatil.)
  12:35/q13/p1 No fixed length was stated; they meant an open-ended period, and the exact stay is unknown or only known to have been long. — reasons: Many commentators leave it unspecified. Ibn Jawzi prefers this because the jailers set no fixed term, while later commentators estimate Joseph's stay. Ibn Ashur says the authorities' words may be open-ended or the narrator omits an irrelevant duration. Al-Razi cites 12:45 for a long imprisonment while declining exactness; al-Alusi similarly avoids choosing a number. Ibn al-Shaykh in Bursavi says the verse only indicates a long stay. Ibn al-Anbari says withholding the period directs people to scholars; Ibn Abi Hatim's report describes the Qur'anic term as unbounded. Al-Aṣamm calls it a long period.
  12:35/q13/p2 Until city gossip and scandal faded or Joseph's news was forgotten. — reasons: Ibn Abbas is reported by al-Wahidi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi and al-Alusi; Ata appears in al-Baghawi and Ibn Jawzi. Al-Jubba'i adds that Joseph's news faded. The wife's request and the ruler's apparent goal explain the endpoint.
  12:35/q13/p3 Until the rulers decided what to do with Joseph or observed how he behaved. — reasons: Al-Tabari gives a provisional term; Abu Ali reports a decision point; al-Kashshaf, al-Baydawi and Abu Hayyan connect it to watching Joseph's reaction.
  12:35/q13/p4 One year or thirteen months. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Jawzi report one year, while al-Kiya gives thirteen months; these are distinct estimates of actual duration. Ibn Atiyya relates the estimates to disclosures during Joseph's confinement. Ikrima's vow report gives one year of imprisonment for an unqualified period, but also disallows the promised hand cutting.
  12:35/q13/p5 Six months. — reasons: Sa'id ibn Jubayr is named in Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi, with Tariq agreeing in Ibn Abi Hatim.
  12:35/q13/p6 Five years. — reasons: Al-Kalbi is named by al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi and al-Qurtubi; Ibn Abbas is also reported with five years by al-Wahidi and Ibn Jawzi. Al-Razi, Bursavi and al-Alusi include it among competing estimates.
  12:35/q13/p7 Seven years. — reasons: Al-Tabari and Ibn Abi Hatim report Ikrima; al-Wahidi also reports Sufyan, while al-Qurtubi attributes seven to Muqatil. Al-Baydawi states seven; al-Razi, al-Biqa'i, Bursavi and al-Alusi report it among estimates.
  12:35/q13/p8 Nine years. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi and al-Qurtubi attribute this estimate to Ikrima, although other sources attribute seven years to him.
  12:35/q13/p9 Ten years. — reasons: Al-Jishumi reports this from al-Kalbi, while other transmitters give him five years.
  12:35/q13/p10 Twelve years. — reasons: Al-Wahidi attributes twelve to Muqatil; al-Qurtubi attributes it to Wahb. Al-Razi, Bursavi and al-Alusi include twelve among estimates, and Bursavi says it is the most famous account.
