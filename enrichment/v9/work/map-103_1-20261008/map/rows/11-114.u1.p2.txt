  11:114/q19/p1 It is a verbal noun ending in feminine alif, with other attested forms and a plural. — reasons: Abu Ali compares العدوى, الدعوى and شورى; Sibawayh records الذكر with dal and cites Ibn Muqbil's line, alongside 12:45. Qurtubi calls it a verbal noun.
11:114/q20 To what does ذلك point? [ذَٰلِكَ · referent] (turns on: Which antecedent the demonstrative resumes.)
  11:114/q20/p1 The preceding counsel and commands, including steadfastness and prayer. — reasons: Kashshaf starts at the uprightness command; Tabari includes this surah's commands and prohibitions. Ibn Atiyya and Ibn al-Jawzi mention avoiding transgression and unjust rulers; Biqai includes warning and consolation.
  11:114/q20/p2 The command to establish prayer at these times. — reasons: Abu Hayyan prefers the nearest antecedent; Ibn Atiyya says prayer supplies a cause and setting for remembrance.
  11:114/q20/p3 The statement or promise that good deeds erase sins. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya says this encourages good deeds; Jishumi calls it the promise itself.
  11:114/q20/p4 The Qur'an. — reasons: Wahidi says it is an admonition for one who remembers the Quran.
  11:114/q20/p5 This, meaning the present admonition. — reasons: Ibn Abbas takes ذلك in the sense of هذا.
11:114/q21 Who benefits from the reminder, and what kind of reminder is it? [ذِكْرَىٰ لِلذَّٰكِرِينَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the reminder is admonition, repentance, doctrinal strengthening, or inward recollection.)
  11:114/q21/p1 It is admonition for people who remember God and heed it. — reasons: Tabari contrasts responsive people with sealed hearts and mentions hope for reward and fear of warning. Hasan describes remembering in prosperity, adversity, health and illness; Mujahid adds standing, sitting and lying. Alusi and Qurtubi say those who heed are named because they benefit.
  11:114/q21/p2 It is a call to repentance or recollection of one's own sin. — reasons: Al-Kalbi calls it repentance; Yazid ibn Ruman and Ibn Jurayj say the penitent man continued to remember his deed.
  11:114/q21/p3 It strengthens believers in religion. — reasons: Abu Muslim explains the reminder's effect this way.
  11:114/q21/p4 It recalls spiritual clarity, gathered attention, intimacy and taste. — reasons: Both connect prayer times and sins' removal to inward recollection.
11:114/q22 How does the verse connect to nearby commands and other verses? [* · links]
  11:114/q22/p1 Prayer follows the command to remain upright as the foremost aid to steadfastness, and its expiatory power explains the command. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says 'indeed' emphasizes the causal explanation and links 29:45; Biqai says prayer cures the weakness behind lesser sins.
  11:114/q22/p2 Other verses supply or parallel these prayer times. — reasons: Muqatil recalls Moses' prayer for remembrance at 20:14; Hasan invokes 17:78; Razi and Elmalili cite 20:130; Bursevi adds 50:39–40. Study Quran compares disagreement over the middle prayer at 2:238.
  11:114/q22/p3 The verse's direct address to the Prophet marks positive commands, while prohibitions address the community. — reasons: Elmalili says this shift implies transgression and inclination to oppressors are conceivable for the community, not the Prophet.
11:114/q23 Was this verse revealed in Mecca or Medina? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether 'revealed' in the confession account denotes first revelation or application of an earlier verse.)
  11:114/q23/p1 It was revealed in Medina as an exception within the surah. — reasons: Ibn Abbas and Qatada infer this from the confession report's 'revealed to him' wording; many commentators list verses 12, 17 and 114 as possible exceptions.
  11:114/q23/p2 It is Meccan along with the whole surah; the Prophet later applied it to the penitent. — reasons: Rashid Rida rejects Medina dating as unconvincing. Ibn Kathir suggests it may predate the five prayers of the Night Journey, when two daily prayers and night vigil were required.
  11:114/q23/p3 If newly revealed in Medina, it was placed here for coherence with steadfastness and patience. — reasons: Ibn Ashur points to the adjacent commands at 11:112 and 11:115.
11:114/q24 What distinct reports illustrate the verse's hope and expiation? [* · reports]
  11:114/q24/p1 The five prayers wash away sins like repeated bathing in a river or leaves falling from a branch. — reasons: Salman shakes a dry branch; the Prophet's river image asks whether dirt could remain after five baths. Tabatabai lists parallel Sunni transmissions.
  11:114/q24/p2 It is the most hopeful verse, according to a report from Ali and the Prophet. — reasons: Ali rejects proposed alternatives at 4:48 and 4:110 before identifying this prayer command.
  11:114/q24/p3 God's mercy to a believer exceeds a mother bird's care for her chick. — reasons: The Prophet uses the bird's entrusted chick as the comparison.
  11:114/q24/p4 A worshiper who makes people despair of mercy is himself made to despair after death. — reasons: Zayd ibn Aslam transmits the warning.
  11:114/q24/p5 Ablution itself purifies and draws immense reward. — reasons: Bursevi notes jurists who treated used ablution cloth as impure because sins wash off; the Moses report promises a garden per drop, and Hafiz praises prayer purified by tears.
  11:114/q24/p6 Faith and lawful deeds matter: illicit wealth is not blessed or accepted as charity. — reasons: The Prophet says faith is given to those God loves and requires a surrendered heart and tongue and a safe neighbor.
  11:114/q24/p7 Testimony of faith can erase sins, though some specific routes are weak or unique. — reasons: Ibn Kathir notes Uthman al-Waqqasi is weak in one Anas route and another route is unique through Mastur.
  11:114/q24/p8 Every deed is recorded, so one should not belittle a sin or a good deed. — reasons: Al-Sadiq warns of angels recording public and hidden acts.
11:114/q25 What inward reading does the verse support? [* · inward]
  11:114/q25/p1 Prayer and remembrance bring light that clears sensory darkness and sustains presence. — reasons: Qashani relates five prayers to five senses and night vigil to refining natural powers; Bursevi describes the luminous spirit nourished by good works, and Wasiti contrasts obedience's light with sin's darkness. The Study Quran reports a heart polished by remembrance (13:28).
