[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأسماءالأرضين)#541/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil contrasts اللسان, established as feminine in that dialect and therefore indeclinable, with قباء and حواء, which are not fixed as feminine.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأسماءالأرضين)#541/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A foreign three-letter name is indeclinable even when light; examples include حمص and جور, as with longer feminine names.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأسماءالأرضين)#541/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | واسط is usually masculine and declinable because it lies between Basra and Kufa; some Arabs instead make it feminine and indeclinable.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأسماءالأرضين)#541/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Masculine declension is best for دابق, though it can be feminine and indeclinable; Ghaylān’s verse illustrates masculine use.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأسماءالأرضين)#541/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | منى is best treated as masculine and declinable, but feminine indeclinable use is also possible.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأسماءالأرضين)#541/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | هجر is used as masculine or feminine; al-Farazdaq’s verse supplies feminine use, and Sibawayh also reports spoken usage.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأسماءالأرضين)#541/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | حجر اليمامة is masculine and declinable, though some treat حجر as feminine and indeclinable, like a woman named عمر.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابأسماءالأرضين)#541/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Land names vary in gender: عمان, الزاب, and إراب are feminine only, فلج masculine only, and adjectives can become place names.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباستفعلت)#687/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Istafʿala can mean finding or judging its object to have a quality, as in istajadtuhu, ‘I found it good.’
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباستفعلت)#687/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Istafʿala can express seeking or requesting something, including a gift, pardon, or information.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذاباباستفعلت)#687/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Tafaʿʿala can mean that someone puts himself into a quality; taḥallama seeks to become forbearing, unlike tajāhala.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىمافيهالزوائدمنبناتالحرفين)#569/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A name ذو مال takes nisba as ذووي by restoring its base; similarly ذاه becomes ذووي, فو زيد is treated as فم, and شاء gives شاوي.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىمافيهالزوائدمنبناتالحرفين)#569/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | بنت and ابنة are originally فعل like أخت; أخوك and the reported plural آخاء support that analysis.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىمافيهالزوائدمنبناتالحرفين)#569/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nisba forms from ذية and ذيت are ذيوي because tā is retained; هنت and است likewise have a vowelled middle radical, as their plurals show.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىمافيهالزوائدمنبناتالحرفين)#569/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | سه, ابن/بنون, and اثنتان/اثناء support a vowelled middle radical; هنت follows the common pattern, while ذيت is the exceptional non-declinable form.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىمافيهالزوائدمنبناتالحرفين)#569/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | كلتا reflects a vowelled middle radical in كلا; an alternative treats its alif as feminine, and as a proper name it is diptote with tā like the wāw of شروى.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىمافيهالزوائدمنبناتالحرفين)#569/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In nisba, a deleted element is treated as absent from the base because the nisba yā follows the completed stem; thus ذيت is treated as though it had no tā and is lengthened.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىمافيهالزوائدمنبناتالحرفين)#569/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | فم derives from فوه, with mīm replacing wāw to create an ordinary-looking noun; the mīm behaves as the middle radical across cases.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىمافيهالزوائدمنبناتالحرفين)#569/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Al-Farazdaq’s line هما نفثا في في من فمويهما attests restoration of the original form of فم in inflection.
