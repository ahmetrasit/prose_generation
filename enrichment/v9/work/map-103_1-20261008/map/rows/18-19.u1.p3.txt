18:19/q34 What happened when the envoy reached the changed city? [إِلَى ٱلْمَدِينَةِ · reports] (turns on: Reports agree on a changed era and an old coin, but differ in officials, discovery details and the cave's outcome.)
  18:19/q34/p1 His ancient coin led sellers to suspect hidden treasure and take him before the authorities. — reasons: Al-Tabari names governors Arius/Areus and Astius; al-Tha'labi calls the first Armos. They bound or threatened him; no one knew him or his father. He asked about Decianus, learned he had died centuries earlier, and revealed companions in the cave. Ibn Abbas's report adds a baker and a scholar matching names to an old tablet.
  18:19/q34/p2 A righteous king or governors recognized the youths as a divine sign and went to the cave. — reasons: Wahb and Ikrima name a just or Muslim king; al-Tabari's narrative has Areus, Astius and the people accompany Yemliha; al-Tha'labi has Armos; reports differ whether terror prevented entry, whether they found preserved bodies, and whether a mosque or church was built (18:21).
  18:19/q34/p3 He saw public Christian signs and heard Jesus invoked, unlike the persecution he remembered. — reasons: Al-Tabari and al-Tha'labi describe his bewilderment and fear of madness; Ibn Atiyya says landmarks and clothing had changed. Al-Sadiq adds mutually unintelligible speech.
  18:19/q34/p4 The verse leaves the exact route of discovery unspecified. — reasons: Islahi says investigation eventually exposed their origins, perhaps through the market; Tabatabai says the old coin is a narrative key.
18:19/q35 Why did God awaken the youths in that era according to the reports? [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ · reports] (turns on: The reports supply a specific royal crisis behind the broader resurrection lesson.)
  18:19/q35/p1 Their awakening was a sign of resurrection and God's protection, whether or not a king had prayed. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi likens it to resurrection; Kashshaf, Baydawi, Biqai, Ibn Ashur, Study Quran and Kuranyolu emphasize divine power. Tabatabai says persecution had passed and monotheists could receive the sign.
  18:19/q35/p2 Their appearance answered a dispute over resurrection of body and soul. — reasons: Ikrima says the community was divided; Ibn Ishaq names Theodosius, whose 68-year reign saw deniers of bodily resurrection gain ground. The king donned sackcloth, sat in ashes and prayed. God made the youths a sign confirming the Hour and bodily resurrection, preserving the king and believers. Al-Kashifi's version names Tindrous.
18:19/q36 What happened in the report of Muhammad's message to the sleepers? [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ · reports] (turns on: The later visitation reports differ in visitor and timing.)
  18:19/q36/p1 Four eminent companions carried Muhammad's greeting; the revived youths accepted his prophethood and returned to sleep. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi reports Gabriel instructing Muhammad to send Abu Bakr, Umar, Ali and Abu Dharr on a cloth carried by Solomon's gentle wind. The dog greeted them; God restored the youths' souls; they answered, praised God, accepted Islam and sent greetings. A separate variant gives the Mahdi a later greeting. The Prophet prayed for loved ones and companions.
  18:19/q36/p2 The Mahdi will greet them, after which they will sleep until resurrection. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi presents this as a variant to the four-companion narrative.
18:19/q37 What legal rule about sending an agent is drawn from the errand? [فَٱبْعَثُوٓا۟ أَحَدَكُم · law] (turns on: Jurists agree on delegation for need but differ over an unexcused, present principal.)
  18:19/q37/p1 Agency is valid for delegable, lawful matters, including buying food. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya cites Ali appointing Aqil before Uthman; al-Qurtubi adds 9:60, 12:93, Urwa al-Bariqi, Jabir collecting fifteen wasqs at Khaybar, and Bukhari's Abd al-Rahman–Umayya protection contract. Ibn al-Arabi calls this passage the strongest proof. Al-Qurtubi bars agency for usurpation or unlawful acts.
  18:19/q37/p2 Even a healthy present person may appoint an agent. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi says most permit this and cites the Prophet delegating repayment of a camel debt with a better camel; the creditor praised the repayment.
  18:19/q37/p3 A present person without an excuse may not appoint an agent. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi reports Abu Hanifa and Sahnun; Ibn al-Arabi speculates Sahnun used the restriction against oppressors. Al-Qurtubi and the prophetic camel report oppose it.
  18:19/q37/p4 The youths' fear of discovery was an excuse for delegation. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi says all scholars agree an excused person may appoint an agent, even where they dispute healthy present persons.
18:19/q38 Does the shared silver support pooling money and food? [بِوَرِقِكُمْ هَٰذِهِۦٓ · law] (turns on: Whether the plural possessive establishes shared ownership or merely a pooled errand.)
  18:19/q38/p1 Yes: companions may pool costs, buy jointly and eat together despite unequal appetites. — reasons: Al-Mawardi calls this munahada; Ibn Khuwayz Mindad cites this verse and 2:220; al-Qurtubi cites school permission to mix donated meals and the orphan and shared eating verses (2:220, cited as 24:16). Al-Kiya permits an eater to pay extra for a portion.
  18:19/q38/p2 Their communal purse expresses mutual care and rebukes habitual begging. — reasons: Alusi says one youth may have had no money, perhaps a shepherd, yet the group calls it theirs; Tabatabai sees equal delegation and mutual concern.
  18:19/q38/p3 The verse alone does not prove a partnership, since each might have handed over money separately. — reasons: Ibn al-Arabi relies instead on hadiths. Ibn Umar's two-dates prohibition concerns companions' permission; al-Qurtubi says the army-of-khabt report may reflect equal distribution, so its joint-eating proof is weaker.
18:19/q39 What does their carrying provisions teach about trust in God? [بِوَرِقِكُمْ · theology] (turns on: The dispute concerns ordinary means versus exceptional abandonment by those held to have special certainty.)
  18:19/q39/p1 Prudent provisioning and work are compatible with reliance on God. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf cites Aisha on a pilgrim's money belt and an ascetic scholar's refusal of patrons; al-Razi and Ibn Kathir agree. Bursevi cites the Mathnawi: cultivate before relying; Alusi invokes tying the camel. Abu Hayyan and al-Nasafi repeat the money belt maxim.
  18:19/q39/p2 Those especially firm in certainty may relinquish ordinary means. — reasons: Alusi records eminent scholars who cite Khalid drinking poison and other extraordinary reports; Ahmad, Ishaq and others permit desert travel without supplies or foregoing earning and treatment. Abraham's refusal of Gabriel's help illustrates direct reliance. Alusi rejects attributing this only to Sufis.
18:19/q40 What does the brief time estimate teach about judgment? [لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · law] (turns on: The first position concerns permissibility of probable judgment; the second explains its limit.)
  18:19/q40/p1 Reasoned conjecture can be acted upon and is not a lie if later shown mistaken. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf derives permission for ijtihad; al-Nasafi, al-Biqai, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi say their mistaken estimate remained sincere. Tabatabai distinguishes judgment about duration from blameworthy dissension in action.
  18:19/q40/p2 An exact factual judgment is unavailable from signs alone, so knowledge returns to God. — reasons: Tabatabai says sun, light and shadow cannot determine a full day; humans infer through signs while God knows each event directly.
18:19/q41 How is this scene linked to resurrection and the perception of time? [لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · links] (turns on: The parallel may serve bodily resurrection, Barzakh perception, or a wider lesson on the nature of time.)
  18:19/q41/p1 The youths' brief estimate parallels the revived passerby and people at resurrection or in Barzakh. — reasons: Elmalili cites 2:259; Study Quran lists 2:259, 10:45, 17:52, 20:104, 23:112–13, 30:55, 46:35 and 79:46. Asad connects 2:259 and creation-life-resurrection passages (3:27, 6:95, 10:31, 30:19). Islahi takes it as a preview of perceived Barzakh time; Tabatabai likens worldly life to a brief dream and quotes Ali that people awaken on dying.
  18:19/q41/p2 The youths' answer discloses the deception of earthly time perception. — reasons: Asad says an earthbound conception of time is deceptive; Qushayri's inward reading says days of union feel short, citing poems.
  18:19/q41/p3 The story shows truth survives long spans despite apparent triumph of coercive falsehood. — reasons: Tabatabai compares the century-old revival at 2:259 and says time neither destroys truth nor revives falsehood.
18:19/q42 What inward reading is given to their duration and awakening? [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · inward] (turns on: The notes read the temporal disorientation as a spiritual state.)
  18:19/q42/p1 Spiritual awakening follows absorption; apparent time vanishes in love or union. — reasons: Qashani reads the youths as faculties awakening to discuss latent truths; al-Qushayri says self-absorption removes detailed awareness and cites poetry on short-feeling union; Ibn Ata says longing makes any union seem short. Alusi describes sobriety after intoxication and cites a poem comparing a life of union to uncounted nights.
  18:19/q42/p2 Return to embodied sobriety makes food an immediate concern. — reasons: Al-Qushayri
