## THE CURRENT MAP
18:25/q01 Who states the three hundred and nine year duration? [وَلَبِثُوا۟ ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍۢ سِنِينَ وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًۭا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the clause continues earlier quoted claims or resumes God's narrative; the variant with 'they said' and the force of 18:26 are disputed.)
  18:25/q01/p1 God states the sleepers' true duration. — reasons: Mujāhid and Ibn ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr take the count literally. Al-Ṭabarī says unmarked quotation needs proof; al-Zajjāj calls the number a stronger sign. Al-Baghawī says God's subsequent claim to know best confirms disclosed knowledge. Al-Rāzī cites the intervening ban on dispute; al-Ṭabrisī says an unmarked quotation would leave the proof of divine power incomplete. Ibn Kathīr says the People of the Book gave 300 solar years without the nine and the Ibn Masʿūd report is anomalous with disconnected transmission. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī appeals to passage structure and says their reported figure was 200 or less. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a possible continuation of the narrative; al-Kuranyolu presents it as interpretation B.
  18:25/q01/p2 The number quotes the People of the Book or other disputants, whose claim God refers back to His own knowledge. — reasons: Qatāda connects it to the earlier guesses of 18:22; Maṭar al-Warrāq names Jews, while other accounts name Jews and Christians of Najrān. Ibn Masʿūd's reading inserts 'and they said'; Ibn ʿAbbās and others argue 18:26 would be strange after an exact divine statement. Al-Rāzī and Asad cite the following reply; al-Qāshānī says the duration is not established. Al-Kuranyolu prefers this by verse order and links the sleepers' admission in 18:19. Al-Ālūsī challenges use of 'God knows best' as decisive because it also follows a count Ibn ʿAbbās accepted at 18:22. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī demand evidence for an unmarked quotation.
  18:25/q01/p3 Some People of the Book said 300 years and others added nine. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Kuranyolu describe variant traditional counts. Al-Khafājī takes the pronoun of 'they added' as the People of the Book; al-Ālūsī finds this weak. Al-Kalbī says Najrān Christians knew 300 but not the extra nine, occasioning revelation of the divine answer.
  18:25/q01/p4 The victorious Cave party stayed hidden for 309 years. — reasons: Elmalılı reads the clause as a fresh resumption about the party whose cause prevailed; he links its emergence to Christians overcoming idolatrous Romans in the early fourth century and sees a foreshadowing of Islam's faster victory after the Hijra.
18:25/q02 What interval does their stay in the cave measure? [وَلَبِثُوا۟ فِى كَهْفِهِمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the duration belongs to the first sleep, a later interval, the party's concealment, or an allegorical reckoning.)
  18:25/q02/p1 Their life and sleep from entering the cave until God woke and disclosed them. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī ties this to awakening and discovery in 18:19, 18:21; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī connect the detailed count to the earlier sealed hearing in 18:11. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the first interval before awakening and notes Christian historians claimed 240 years. Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Ṭabarī distinguishing this from the unknown period after discovery; Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise distinguishes the two uses of 'remained'. Mujāhid, as transmitted by al-Suyūṭī, calls 300 plus nine the count of their stay.
  18:25/q02/p2 The count runs from entry until their final deaths. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this as a form of direct divine account, distinct from the awakening endpoint.
  18:25/q02/p3 The known sleep is followed by another, unknown period after discovery or death. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī says 18:26 concerns the span from their souls being taken to the present. Al-Baghawī contrasts the People of the Book's count up to their own era with what only God knows after death. Mujāhid says the later period reached the Qurʾān's revelation; al-Ḍaḥḥāk says until death. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports a proposed reading of 309 years from final death until revelation.
  18:25/q02/p4 The count denotes a hidden party's period before its victory. — reasons: Elmalılı connects 309 years with Christian victory over idolatrous Roman rule.
  18:25/q02/p5 The count can mean 25 years plus nine months. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī takes 300 lunar cycles as 25 years and the nine as gestation; he appeals to possible ambiguity in the unit, while al-Ālūsī rejects reducing Quranic years to months as baseless.
18:25/q03 What cave or place is associated with their stay? [كَهْفِهِمْ · referent] (turns on: The location reports and archaeological signs do not establish identity conclusively.)
  18:25/q03/p1 Their cave lay between two mountains. — reasons: Mujāhid supplies the physical description.
  18:25/q03/p2 Rujayb near Amman best fits the cave, though identification is unproved. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Jordanian excavation, archaeological signs and some Muslim reports, and identifies nearby Amman with Roman Philadelphia; he also admits no proposed cave is demonstrably the Qurʾānic cave.
  18:25/q03/p3 Other proposed caves lie at Qāsiyūn, Petra, Scandinavia, or Nakhchivan. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the Scandinavian and Caucasus proposals clash with the Roman setting and reach of Roman power.
  18:25/q03/p4 A Roman cave was shown as the sleepers' site, but its identity is uncertain. — reasons: Al-Khwārizmī reports al-Wāthiq sent him to inspect it; he saw hair on displayed bodies, suspected contrivance and possible preservation with aloe or other drying medicines. Al-Qaffāl says Roman identification proves nothing, and al-Rāzī says era and location require unavailable revelation.
  18:25/q03/p5 The cave's exact location cannot be established. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says reason cannot determine it without a revealed text; al-Qaffāl rejects proof from Roman identification. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says none of the proposed sites has decisive evidence.
18:25/q04 When did the sleepers live? [وَلَبِثُوا۟ · reports] (turns on: The reports disagree on dates and the available revelation does not specify them.)
  18:25/q04/p1 They entered after Jesus, with some disciples still alive. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī infer an early post-Jesus date; al-Qaffāl transmits Ibn Isḥāq's post-Jesus view. Most historians place the episode between Jesus and Muḥammad, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares Christian dates under Decius ending in 425, 437, or 439 CE with shorter Christian estimates.
  18:25/q04/p2 They entered before Jesus and awoke between Jesus and Muhammad. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports this chronology, in which Jesus related their story.
  18:25/q04/p3 They lived before Moses. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports a claim that Moses mentioned them in the Torah, explaining Jewish interest.
  18:25/q04/p4 Their precise era cannot be established. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says reason cannot settle the date, and no revealed text is available; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī observes divergent dates and rulers in transmitted accounts.
18:25/q05 What was the sleepers' physical state during and after the interval? [وَلَبِثُوا۟ · reports] (turns on: Accounts differ about what happened after the awakening.)
  18:25/q05/p1 They remained alive and asleep throughout the stated cave interval. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī explicitly say alive asleep; al-Nasafī ties it to sealed hearing; Muqātil says colors, hair and clothes were unchanged. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes divine care and awakening after 309 years.
  18:25/q05/p2 They never died and will live until the Resurrection. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports this claim from some people.
  18:25/q05/p3 Reports differ on whether they died or slept again after discovery. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records the divergence; al-Thaʿlabī and the Study Quran explain God's knowledge as concerning the time after death.
18:25/q06 How is the phrase three hundred years read? [ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍۢ سِنِينَ · readings] (turns on: Whether miʾa is nunated, annexed to sinīn, or replaced by a singular or nominative variant; grammarians dispute the plural after a hundred.)
  18:25/q06/p1 The majority read miʾa with tanwīn, then sinīn separately. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim and Ibn ʿĀmir; al-Ṭabarī names Medinans, Basrans and some Kufans and prefers it because hundreds usually take singular specifications. Al-Jishumī prefers its Arabic. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Naḥḥās criticize the annexed alternative; ʿUthmān is reported to recite sinīn with nunation. Al-Nashr says all remaining readers pronounce tanwīn, after naming Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Khalaf as the readers without it.
  18:25/q06/p2 Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read without tanwīn, annexing miʾa to genitive sinīn. — reasons: Other reported readers include Yaḥyā, Ṭalḥa, al-Aʿmash, al-Ḥasan and Khalaf; al-Nasafī also names ʿĀṣim. Abū al-Ḥasan says the plural construction is rare but allowed, Abū ʿAlī adduces poetry, and al-Ālūsī takes the canonical reading itself as Arabic evidence. Abū Ḥātim censured it; al-Samīn rejects that censure. Qirāʾāt ER calls the annexation a tighter unit.
  18:25/q06/p3 Ubayy and Ibn Masʿūd have the singular sana after three hundred. — reasons: The singular is the usual complement of hundreds; Ibn Masʿūd's codex and Ubayy's reading are both cited.
  18:25/q06/p4 Al-Ḍaḥḥāk reads nominative sunun. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī supply an omitted subject: 'they are years'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk's plural variant.
  18:25/q06/p5 A dialectal miyatin omits the hamza. — reasons: Qirāʾāt ER says the pronunciation changes no meaning.
18:25/q07 How does sinīn function grammatically? [سِنِينَ · grammar] (turns on: The grammatical status of plural sinīn after a hundred and whether it clarifies the count, gives its time unit, modifies it, or belongs to an annexed construction.)
  18:25/q07/p1 It is an apposition or clarifying substitute for the numbered duration. — reasons: Al-Akhfash allows substitution for three or hundred with c
