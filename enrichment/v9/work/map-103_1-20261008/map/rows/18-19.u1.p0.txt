## THE CURRENT MAP
18:19/q01 What does 'thus' compare the awakening with? [وَكَذَٰلِكَ · links] (turns on: Whether the demonstrative recalls the preceding sleep or heightens the new event.)
  18:19/q01/p1 God's preceding sleep and preservation of the youths, equally wondrous signs of His power. — reasons: Al-Wahidi links sleep, increased guidance and turning (18:11,13,18); al-Zajjaj and al-Zamakhshari compare the sleep; al-Baghawi and al-Tabrisi stress preservation from decay; Abu Hayyan sees the earlier compressed account; Alusi says both display overwhelming power.
  18:19/q01/p2 The awakening is likened to itself to magnify its wonder. — reasons: Ibn Ashur compares the rhetorical usage at 2:143.
18:19/q02 What does 'We raised them' mean and how were they raised? [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ · meaning] (turns on: Whether baʿth is read as bodily awakening in the account or as an inward state.)
  18:19/q02/p1 God woke or revived them from a deathlike long sleep, with their bodies and condition preserved. — reasons: Abu Ubayda relates baʿth to resurrection; Ibn Qutayba and al-Wahidi compare slumber to death; Jishumi gives its root as stirring and its other uses as sending messengers or raising the dead; al-Qurtubi cites a poet's waking usage. Ibn Kathir says skin and hair were unchanged after 309 years; al-Asamm and al-Qurtubi say they woke as they slept.
  18:19/q02/p2 The awakening is an inward return to wakefulness or sobriety. — reasons: Qashani calls it the Sufi first awakening, in which faculties discuss latent meanings; Alusi reports the return from spiritual intoxication.
18:19/q03 Why were they awakened to question one another? [لِيَتَسَآءَلُوا۟ بَيْنَهُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the lām marks a direct purpose or a consequence, and which wisdom the dialogue serves.)
  18:19/q03/p1 Their questioning was a purpose of the awakening, revealing God's power and the duration's mystery. — reasons: Muqatil and Jishumi take the stated end directly; al-Razi says disclosure of divine power is desirable itself; al-Samin prefers causal purpose; Tabatabai ties it to 18:12 and rejects reducing it to a minor consequence; Ibn Ashur says it benefits the youths by direct knowledge of resurrection, like Abraham in 2:260. Al-Biqai adds faith and certainty; al-Tabrisi adds recognition of the Creator and gratitude.
  18:19/q03/p2 Questioning was the resulting event, while the awakening chiefly displayed divine power. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi read the lām as consequence, citing 28:8; al-Baghawi calls questioning an outcome; al-Khafaji prefers this because divine power is the purpose; Asad renders it 'and they began to ask.' Al-Samin and Tabatabai argue against this reduction.
  18:19/q03/p3 They questioned one another to determine their condition, reflect, grow certain and give thanks. — reasons: Al-Tabari says the long sleep seemed strange; al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi, and al-Baydawi treat the event as a sign of sleep and resurrection; Elmalili says self-knowledge is a primary concern.
18:19/q04 How is the questioning dialogue introduced? [قَالَ قَآئِلٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · grammar]
  18:19/q04/p1 The quoted question explains the preceding statement that they questioned one another. — reasons: Alusi calls the introductory qāla a resumption; Ibn Ashur calls the ensuing dialogue an explanation of tasāʾul, mutual questioning.
18:19/q05 Who asked 'How long did you stay?' [قَآئِلٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · referent] (turns on: The narrative does not name the questioner; transmitted identifications differ.)
  18:19/q05/p1 Their leader Maksalmina, also spelled Miksalmina, asked. — reasons: Muqatil calls him eldest; al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, Bursevi and others call him chief; Alusi and Abu Hayyan preserve alternatives.
  18:19/q05/p2 An unnamed member asked, apparently sensing an unusual interval. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya cites signs of an extraordinary sleep; Tabatabai suggests its heaviness; al-Biqai suggests appearance or other signs.
  18:19/q05/p3 Tamlikha or Yamlikha, the youth who managed or carried the provisions, asked. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit this alternative; the name has spelling variants.
18:19/q06 Why did they ask how long they had stayed? [كَمْ لَبِثْتُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: The notes give different motives and scope for the question.)
  18:19/q06/p1 They noticed something unusual and sought to gauge the sleep's duration. — reasons: Al-Tabari says it seemed strange; Ibn Atiyya says conditions looked similar enough that a sudden change was unlikely; al-Biqai points to appearance or other signs, Tabatabai to the heaviness of sleep; al-Tha'labi notes their surprise.
  18:19/q06/p2 They feared they had missed a prayer. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi and Alusi record the concern.
  18:19/q06/p3 The question asks how long since they entered the cave. — reasons: Ibn Abbas states this construal.
18:19/q07 How is 'how long' parsed? [كَمْ لَبِثْتُمْ · grammar]
  18:19/q07/p1 Kam is an accusative time expression with 'days' understood. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi supplies the omitted counted unit from the answer; al-Samin calls it adverbial.
18:19/q08 How is labithtum pronounced? [لَبِثْتُمْ · readings] (turns on: Whether thāʾ remains distinct or assimilates into the following consonant.)
  18:19/q08/p1 The other four readers assimilate thāʾ; proximity of articulation permits the form. — reasons: Ibn Mujahid reports the four; al-Nahhas explains that the articulation points are close.
  18:19/q08/p2 Ibn Kathir, Nafi and Asim pronounce thāʾ distinctly. — reasons: Ibn Mujahid names the three readers and says this occurs in each form of the word.
18:19/q09 How should 'a day or part of a day' be understood? [لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether aw expresses one uncertain estimate, a correction, or different speakers; one report gives longer guesses.)
  18:19/q09/p1 The group gave an uncertain estimate, first thinking a day and then part of one. — reasons: Morning entry and remaining sunlight explain the revision for Muqatil, Yahya, al-Mawardi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi and others. Alusi favors plain doubt over a correction; Tabatabai says one speaker's uncertain estimate and rejects distributed speakers. Ibn Kathir says they later deferred to God.
  18:19/q09/p2 Different youths supplied the two estimates: some said a day, others part of one. — reasons: Al-Nahhas transmits Ibn Abbas with separate youths; Abu Hayyan allows aw to distribute the answers; Elmalili reads the two groups similarly; Ibn Ashur allows it. Alusi calls distribution unlikely; Tabatabai rejects it.
  18:19/q09/p3 The report has one day, two days, or longer. — reasons: Ibn Abbas report in al-Durr and Tabatabai; Tabatabai objects that two days or more conflicts with the Quranic wording and ordinary evidence.
18:19/q10 Why did their estimate seem so short, and what later made them doubt it? [يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · interpretation] (turns on: The sources differ on the immediate cue for the short guess and on the later evidence of elapsed time.)
  18:19/q10/p1 They slept from morning until later daylight, so the visible sun made a day or part of one seem plausible. — reasons: Yahya, Muqatil, al-Wahidi and many others describe morning entry and late waking; al-Kashifi places waking around afternoon, which Bursevi favors because a shopping round trip needed daylight. Alusi says sleep haze prevented checking signs.
  18:19/q10/p2 Their grown hair and nails, or altered faces, suggested a much longer sleep. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Razi, al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi and Study Quran mention the signs; Ibn Jawzi reports them as an alternative. Kashshaf juxtaposes these signs with the morning account.
  18:19/q10/p3 Sleep and inward absorption disrupt the ability to measure elapsed time. — reasons: Al-Baydawi says sleepers cannot measure duration; al-Qushayri says absorption removed self-awareness; Alusi says sleep haze; Tabatabai notes shadows and sunlight cannot fix an exact day and external events require indicators.
18:19/q11 Who said that their Lord knew best how long they had stayed? [قَالُوا۟ رَبُّكُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا لَبِثْتُمْ · referent] (turns on: The repeated qālū may introduce another group, the whole group, a named leader, or divine speech.)
  18:19/q11/p1 A different group of youths corrected those who estimated a day. — reasons: Al-Tabari says the others deferred; al-Zamakhshari says evidence or inspiration showed them a long stay; Abu Hayyan prefers two groups from the dialogue. Alusi says this fits its question-and-answer structure; Tabatabai says they had deeper monotheistic insight, distinct from the factions of 18:12.
  18:19/q11/p2 Their leader Maksalmina, the eldest, stopped the dispute. — reasons: Muqatil names him; al-Mawardi cites Ibn Abbas; al-Tha'labi and al-Baghawi transmit this explanation; Ibn Jawzi records the name as Miksalmina.
  18:19/q11/p3 Tamlikha or Yamlikha, described as their leader, gave the answer. — reasons: Al-Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas; al-Tabrisi and al-Razi cite him as the leader, with al-Razi saying altered hair, nails and faces led him to infer a long period. Ibn Jawzi records a competing name.
  18:19/q11/p4 The whole group agreed to defer to God, or one person's accepted answer was attributed to all. — reasons: Ibn Ashur gives both possibilities; al-Baghawi reports the group statement; al-Biqai sees affectionate agreement. Alusi disfavors the same speaker giving both answers.
  18:19/q11/p5 It is God's own statement about their duration. — reasons: Al-Mawardi records this alternative to speech by the youths.
18:19/q12 What does deferring their duration to God express? [رَبُّكُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا لَبِثْتُمْ · theology] (turns on: The ordinary explanation concerns epistemic humility; the inward account treats time itself differently.)
  18:19/q12/p1 They acknowledge that only God knows the exact, long duration, and end an unresolvable dispute. — reasons: Muqatil
