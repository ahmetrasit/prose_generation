2:265/q24 How does the verse connect with the surrounding spending parables and passage? [* · links] (turns on: Which earlier image supplies the main comparison: the barren rock or the multiplying seed.)
  2:265/q24/p1 It contrasts accepted, fertile giving with showy or harmful giving that the preceding rock image leaves barren. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan contrast 2:264; Ibn Ashur says contrast clarifies rank and renews praise. Al-Tabataba'i treats the linked verses as a unit addressing purpose, manner, good wealth, needy recipients and reward.
  2:265/q24/p2 It develops the earlier seed image: the lasting orchard pictures sustained generosity and further multiplication. — reasons: Al-Biqa'i says the grain represents spending in God's path while the orchard covers every good path. Ibn Ashur says the simpler seed image quickly pictures multiplication; this one has several favorable features. Elmalili says double does not reduce the earlier seven-hundredfold promise.
2:265/q25 What inward lesson is drawn from the garden and rain? [* · inward] (turns on: The image's inward analogue: a lasting disposition of generosity or purification of intention.)
  2:265/q25/p1 Giving fixes generosity in the soul; the ridge signifies higher rank, and stronger or weaker rain signifies different shares of mercy. — reasons: Al-Qashani contrasts the enduring orchard with a grain to express an acquired lasting quality; he calls generosity a divine attribute.
  2:265/q25/p2 Sincere devotion removes hidden idolatry and makes deeds grow; insincere work cannot produce good fruit. — reasons: Bursevi cites Sa'di's thorns-and-flowers analogy, and says pure work seeks no praise. He also assigns symbolic meanings to charity's three letters.
2:265/q26 What reports about charity's effects are cited with this verse? [* · reports] (turns on: Different transmitted statements about charity's effect.)
  2:265/q26/p1 A lawfully earned date given in charity is nurtured by God until it becomes mountain-sized. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi cites Abu Hurayra's Prophetic report through Muslim and the Muwatta; Study Quran also invokes it.
  2:265/q26/p2 Charity itself speaks of five benefits: growth, enlargement, turning an enemy friendly, preservation and protection. — reasons: Bursevi transmits the report through Ali from the Prophet.
  2:265/q26/p3 Hell prostrates in gratitude when a believer gives charity, because it frees someone from punishment. — reasons: Bursevi reports Makhul's saying.
2:265/q27 How do other cited verses about riba and giving relate to this verse's reward contrast? [* · links]
  2:265/q27/p1 Gift-riba seeking a larger return earns no divine reward, unlike sincere charity; prohibited exchange-riba is distinct. — reasons: Al-Farisi distinguishes unlawful excess in like weighed or measured goods from disliked gift-riba, citing 2:278 and 30:39. He gives conditional and relative parses of 30:39, its omitted resumptive and shift of person, and discusses 74:6: Ikrima forbids seeking more back, while al-Farisi says a jussive parse lacks semantic fit.
2:265/q28 What adjacent grammatical example is discussed alongside this verse? [* · links]
  2:265/q28/p1 In the bird passage, فصرهن can mean cut them or incline them, with different attachments of إليك. — reasons: Al-Farisi records both interpretations of 2:260; Sibawayh attaches إليك to the nearer turning verb, while attachment to خذ also permits an implied later object.
2:265/q29 What do ابتغاء and مرضات mean? [ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ · meaning]
  2:265/q29/p1 ابتغاء means seeking, and مرضات names satisfaction or approval. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi derives marḍāt as the verbal noun of raḍiya.
2:265/q30 What verbal sense underlies تثبيتا? [وَتَثْبِيتًۭا · grammar]
  2:265/q30/p1 Causative ثبّت means to make firm or establish, distinct from intransitive ثبت, to become firm. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and al-Samin's al-Shaykh insist on this distinction, opposing replacement by the intransitive verbal noun unless a licensing verb precedes it.
