## THE CURRENT MAP
18:16/q01 What does withdrawing (iʿtizāl) mean here? [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ · meaning]
  18:16/q01/p1 It is separation from the people and their worship, inwardly in belief and outwardly by leaving them. — reasons: Jishumi defines withdrawal from an affair and cites a poem on avoiding a house for fear of enemies; Tabrisi cites taʿazzul in poetry and Alusi a line about ʿAtika's house for bodily or inward avoidance. Abu Hayyan and Bursevi distinguish belief from physical departure; Tabatabai glosses withdrawal. Jishumi compares this usage of iʿtizal with Abraham's withdrawal in 19:48.
18:16/q02 What does their separation from the people's worship show about that community? [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ · interpretation]
  18:16/q02/p1 Their community practiced polytheism. — reasons: Biqai infers this from the separate mention of the people and their worship.
18:16/q03 Who speaks the advice to withdraw and enter the cave? [وَإِذِ ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ فَأْوُۥٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · referent] (turns on: Whether the certainty of the promised mercy is fitting for the youths' speech, and how verse 16 resumes the speech of verse 14.)
  18:16/q03/p1 The youths advise one another after deciding to flee. — reasons: Muqatil, Yahya, Tabari, Thalabi, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, and others present it as mutual counsel. Ibn Atiyya prefers this; if 18:14 is an audience with the king, an omitted 'some said to others' is needed. Nahhas says one youth addresses the others. Qurtubi and Alusi cite the possibility. The advice was fulfilled, says Alusi.
  18:16/q03/p2 Their leader Tamlīkha or Yamlīkha speaks to them. — reasons: Ibn Abbas names him through Tabrisi; Ibn Jawzi calls him Yamlīkha, Wahidi calls Temlikha their leader, and Kashifi places his speech on the road after Decianus gave respite. Study Quran notes variant names Tamlīkha, Yamlīkha, Iamblichus. Qurtubi attributes this to Ibn Atiyya.
  18:16/q03/p3 Their leader Maksalmina urges the retreat. — reasons: Al-Ghaznawi's name is reported by Qurtubi.
  18:16/q03/p4 God instructs them to seek the cave. — reasons: Qurtubi records this option. Islahi sees the certain promise as divine speech and the cave guidance as inspiration comparable to God's instruction to Moses' mother; he objects to treating the promise as the youths' own confident words.
18:16/q04 What prompted the youths' withdrawal and how did they leave? [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ · reports] (turns on: The reports differ over prior concealment, public confession, and whether the youths coordinated before leaving or recognized each other only in the wilderness.)
  18:16/q04/p1 They fled persecution and forced idolatry, acting together or resolving to leave together. — reasons: Thalabi says they feared Daqyanus and took provisions; Ibn Abbas says seven fled by night after an idol-worship summons. Biqai says they lacked force against many opponents. Ibn Kathir, Tabatabai, Islahi, and Kuran Yolu cite threats to faith, stoning, or forced conversion. Tabatabai prefers a public confession at an enforcing assembly, citing a report that six were royal advisers, yet allows earlier concealed belief. Islahi sees imminent stoning and a chosen cave.
  18:16/q04/p2 They left separately, unaware of one another's faith, and pledged common belief when they met outside the city. — reasons: Reports of al-Sadiq and Ibn Abbas, cited by Tabatabai, describe separate departures and cautious exchange in the wilderness; Ibn Abbas says God brought them together unexpectedly. Tabatabai notes traditions disagree over planning versus chance meeting.
  18:16/q04/p3 They concealed belief and outwardly conformed before departure. — reasons: Al-Sadiq says God rewarded them twice for concealed belief and outward disbelief; other reports mention zunnār belts and festivals. Tabatabai argues their later public declaration or private resolve does not exclude earlier taqiyya.
18:16/q05 Who accompanied them to the cave? [إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports] (turns on: Whether reports add a shepherd or field worker to the dog that accompanied the youths.)
  18:16/q05/p1 A dog followed or entered with the youths. — reasons: Thalabi says their dog followed their remaining provisions; Kaʿb depicts it repeatedly following, professing love for God's beloved, and offering to guard them; Ubayd ibn Umayr calls it a hunting dog. Kuran Yolu also mentions the dog.
  18:16/q05/p2 A believing shepherd or field worker joined, bringing his dog. — reasons: Ibn Abbas reports the shepherd and his dog joining the seven; Wahb reports a fellow believer working in a field and his dog joining the pursued youths at night. Tabatabai notes this is one disputed report variant.
  18:16/q05/p3 Only a dog, rather than a shepherd, joined them. — reasons: Tabatabai records this alternative among the divergent accounts.
18:16/q06 What happened as they settled in the cave and after they disappeared? [إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports] (turns on: Reports disagree whether the fugitives remained hidden, were sealed in, or met the king; Ibn Kathir tests the sealing story against the sunlight of 18:17.)
  18:16/q06/p1 They lived there briefly with provisions, then God put them to sleep. — reasons: Thalabi says they took remaining supplies; Ibn Abbas says they prayed and praised God while Tamlikha secretly bought food, disguised in poor clothes, and checked news. Ubayd ibn Umayr says they slept 309 years; Wahb says God put them to sleep. Kuran Yolu says God accepted their plea through a long sleep; Elmalili links sleep to 18:10–11 and protection from distress.
  18:16/q06/p2 The ruler searched but could not find them. — reasons: Ibn Kathir compares their concealment to the Prophet and Abu Bakr in Thawr and cites 9:40. Tabatabai records this as one version.
  18:16/q06/p3 Their people found the sleepers and sealed the cave entrance. — reasons: Ibn Kathir rejects this because 18:17 describes sunlight entering morning and evening; other accounts add a shepherd later breaking the wall. Elmalili mentions a walled entrance as one possibility.
  18:16/q06/p4 They met the king and died, or died before he arrived and were then hidden. — reasons: Tabatabai lists both variants and says a mosque was later built.
  18:16/q06/p5 The youths' parents said they had spent their money and withdrawn to Mount Yanjalus; the ruler released the parents. — reasons: Thalabi gives this account.
18:16/q07 Where was the cave? [ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports] (turns on: Archaeological signs, Muslim reports, the story's Roman setting, and the lack of decisive identification.)
  18:16/q07/p1 The cave was at Rujayb near modern Amman. — reasons: Tabatabai prefers this candidate after Jordanian excavation, using archaeological signs and some Muslim reports; he notes Amman occupies ancient Philadelphia, a Roman city.
  18:16/q07/p2 The cave was called Hayyum. — reasons: Abu Jaʿfar also says the youths were polishers; Qurtubi reports him.
  18:16/q07/p3 The cave was near Mount Yanjalus. — reasons: Thalabi reports the parents' statement to the ruler.
  18:16/q07/p4 It lay between two mountains. — reasons: Al-Suddi's report through Durr is not involved; Durr here cites Mujahid.
  18:16/q07/p5 A cave near Damascus, Petra, Scandinavia, or Nakhchivan is proposed. — reasons: Tabatabai lists Mount Qāsiyūn, Petra, a Scandinavian cave with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and a cave near Nakhchivan; he says no site is securely identified and the Scandinavian and Caucasus claims conflict with a Roman setting and Roman power's reach.
18:16/q08 How does 'what they worship' relate grammatically to 'you withdrew from them'? [وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether mā names objects, makes worship a verbal noun, or negates a parenthetical clause.)
  18:16/q08/p1 Mā is a relative pronoun in the accusative, coordinated with the object pronoun: they left the people and their worshipped objects. — reasons: Tabari, Zajjaj, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Atiyya as cited by Qurtubi, and Samin explain coordination; Samin notes an omitted object in the relative clause and that the exception can attach to mā or its object pronoun. Burhan includes this mā among relative mā clauses, alongside what is harvested, prepared, or married.
  18:16/q08/p2 Mā is infinitival: they left the people's worship, with an implied 'worship' supplying the exception. — reasons: Baydawi, Samin, Bursevi, and Alusi give this alternative; Alusi requires the omitted act of worship for a connected exception. Samin says the two possibilities for the exception remain.
  18:16/q08/p3 Mā is negative, making a parenthetical declaration that the youths worship none but God. — reasons: Zamakhshari, Samin, Abu Hayyan, Baydawi, Nasafi, Kashshaf, and Alusi give this divine parenthesis; illā is an unrestricted exception. Alusi says al-Kashf relates it to the following clause. Samin criticizes Abu al-Baqa for wrongly tying connected and disconnected exceptions solely to the negative analysis. Itqan also classifies mā before illā here under the general negative reading.
18:16/q09 What does 'except God' imply about the people's worship? [إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the idolaters also worshipped God; Tabatabai and Alusi cite the surrounding passage against that inference.)
  18:16/q09/p1 The exception is connected: the people acknowledged or worshipped God alongside idols, and the youths abandoned only the idols. — reasons: Muqatil says they retain knowledge of their Lord and Creator. Baghawi explicitly says the common reading implies worship of God and idols. Ata al-Khurasani and Farra report such shared worship; Kashshaf compares Meccans. Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi, and Alusi make this conditional on the people's practice.
  18:16/q09/p2 The exception is disconnected: the people worshipped only idols, so God was never among the objects abandoned. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Samin, Bursevi, and Alusi
