12:45/q11 What is the syntactic role of بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ in the sentence? [بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · grammar]
  12:45/q11/p1 It is part of an intervening or circumstantial recollection clause between 'said' and the cupbearer's quoted speech. — reasons: Baydawi calls the clause intervening. Abu Hayyan and Bursevi call it circumstantial; Alusi calls it parenthetical and allows a circumstantial construction with understood قد; Samīn makes بعد dependent on ادكر.
  12:45/q11/p2 The time sense can be understood through an ellipsis such as 'a span of time'. — reasons: Ibn Durustawayh, reported by Qurtubi, argues this because ummah also means a large community.
12:45/q12 How is أَنَا۠ recited here? [أَنَا۠ · readings] (turns on: The transmitted length of the final vowel.)
  12:45/q12/p1 A canonical form has the short final vowel. — reasons: Qiraat-ER identifies this as the standard canonical recitation.
  12:45/q12/p2 A canonical option extends the final ā with madd. — reasons: Qiraat-ER records this as another canonical option.
12:45/q13 Why does the cupbearer begin his offer with 'I'? [أَنَا۠ · rhetoric]
  12:45/q13/p1 Putting himself forward catches the king's attention and strengthens his surprising claim to explain the baffling dream. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the king would be surprised that this man could explain what had baffled the court.
12:45/q14 What does the cupbearer offer to do in أُنَبِّئُكُم بِتَأْوِيلِهِۦ? [أُنَبِّئُكُم بِتَأْوِيلِهِۦ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the verb is 'inform' or the reported 'bring,' and whether his agency is direct or through Joseph.)
  12:45/q14/p1 He will report the king's dream interpretation obtained from Joseph, who possesses the knowledge. — reasons: Tabari, Mawardi, Kashshaf, Ibn Jawzi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Tabatabai and Ibn Ashur distinguish his report from Joseph's knowledge. Ibn Ashur calls attribution to himself figurative because he causes the explanation. Razi says he wants the court to see Joseph's learning and speaks cautiously after the others failed; Biqai instead describes his offer as confident. Wahidi's Hasan intended not to say it came from Joseph.
  12:45/q14/p2 In the variant wording, he says he will bring them the interpretation. — reasons: Al-Hasan and Ubayy transmit آتيكم; al-Hasan asks whether Joseph was their prophet or whether the cupbearer had already informed them, defending the sense of bringing back information. Zajjaj objects that it differs from the consonantal codex; Samīn calls its meaning close to 'inform'.
12:45/q15 Which wording of 'I will inform you' is read? [أُنَبِّئُكُم · readings] (turns on: The transmitted verb and its conformity to the written codex.)
  12:45/q15/p1 The majority wording is أُنَبِّئُكُم, 'I will inform you'. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya calls it the majority reading; Nahhas, cited by Qurtubi, says it means 'I will tell you when asked.' Zajjaj opposes the alternative because it departs from the written codex.
  12:45/q15/p2 Al-Hasan and Ubayy have آتيكم, 'I will bring you'. — reasons: Ibn Abi Hatim gives Hasan's challenge, 'Was Joseph their prophet?' Wahidi says he meant to report the answer without identifying Joseph. Ibn Atiyya and Alusi connect Ubayy's codex with the reading; Abu Hayyan also reports it as in the codex. Durr preserves Hasan's defense and another reader's similar question. Samīn says the meanings are near.
12:45/q16 Why does he speak as though he himself can supply the interpretation? [أُنَبِّئُكُم · rhetoric]
  12:45/q16/p1 He is the cause of obtaining Joseph's answer; speaking in the first person draws attention and can display Joseph's learning. — reasons: Ibn Ashur calls the claim figurative because he causes the explanation and says 'I' arouses surprise. Razi says he hoped people would recognize Joseph's learning, and cautioned his promise because court interpreters had failed.
  12:45/q16/p2 He confidently claims that he can report the answer once sent. — reasons: Biqai describes his assurance; the cupbearer has previously seen Joseph's successful interpretation, as Razi and Study Quran note.
12:45/q17 Why does the cupbearer leave Joseph unnamed in his offer? [أُنَبِّئُكُم بِتَأْوِيلِهِۦ · rhetoric] (turns on: The notes offer surprise and fear as motives for withholding Joseph's name.)
  12:45/q17/p1 He leaves the source unnamed so the prisoner's unexpected skill and the explanation will surprise the court. — reasons: Ibn Ashur gives the effect of delaying Joseph's identification; Mawardi observes that he initially promises to identify someone who knows, without naming him.
  12:45/q17/p2 He avoids saying the information comes from Joseph. — reasons: Wahidi explains al-Hasan's variant this way; reports of fear of reopening the cupbearer's imprisonment provide a separate motive for withholding Joseph's story.
12:45/q18 What does the cupbearer ask with فَأَرْسِلُونِ? [فَأَرْسِلُونِ · interpretation]
  12:45/q18/p1 He asks permission to go to Joseph, in prison, obtain the interpretation, and return with it. — reasons: Tabari and Thaʿlabi note that the dispatch and journey are omitted. Baghawi supplies 'send me to him'; Ibn Atiyya calls it permission to go; Baydawi allows 'to the knowledgeable person' or 'to prison.' Qurtubi says his return may be to explain the dream or display Joseph's knowledge and obtain his release. Ibn Ashur says he seeks leave from the king's presence; Alusi transmits a report that he knelt to ask. Abu Hayyan reports Ubayy's codex adding فأرسلون with this sense.
  12:45/q18/p2 He asks the king's party to authorize him to find the knowledgeable interpreter, while initially leaving that person unnamed. — reasons: Mawardi stresses that Joseph was initially unnamed. Razi says the cupbearer speaks cautiously because the court has failed.
12:45/q19 Who is addressed by the plural forms in 'inform you' and 'send me'? [أُنَبِّئُكُم فَأَرْسِلُونِ · referent] (turns on: Whether the court is addressed with the king or the plural honors the king alone.)
  12:45/q19/p1 The plural addresses the king and the assembled courtiers or officials. — reasons: Wahidi's Abu Bakr, Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Tabatabai and Asad allow or prefer this. Tabatabai says officials managed state affairs and cites the later return to 'the people' at 12:46; Asad says the whole assembly.
  12:45/q19/p2 It addresses the king alone as an honorific plural. — reasons: Wahidi's Abu Bakr explains that companions share the king's view and command. Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Qurtubi and Alusi allow the singular royal addressee; Ibn Ashur chooses it and compares Q 23:99.
12:45/q20 How is the final first-person ending of فَأَرْسِلُونِ read? [فَأَرْسِلُونِ · readings] (turns on: Whether the first-person yāʾ is retained, especially in pause.)
  12:45/q20/p1 The seven-reader or standard form omits the final first-person yāʾ at pause. — reasons: Qiraat-ER records elision in pause.
  12:45/q20/p2 Yaʿqub and the ten-reader form retain the full first-person yāʾ. — reasons: Ibn Jawzi reports Yaʿqub keeps the yāʾ both connected and paused, paralleling two other verses; Nasafi reports his form. Qiraat-ER describes the ten-reader option.
12:45/q21 Where was Joseph's prison relative to the king's city? [فَأَرْسِلُونِ · reports] (turns on: The transmitted location reports differ; Ibn Ashur argues separately from what the court would have known.)
  12:45/q21/p1 It lay outside the king's city. — reasons: Ibn Abbas is cited by Tabari, Mawardi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi; Ibn Abi Hatim's Suddi also says so. This explains why the cupbearer sought dispatch. Mawardi adds a four-year separation.
  12:45/q21/p2 It lay within the city. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit this alternative without a stated argument.
  12:45/q21/p3 A place beside the Nile, eight miles from Fustat, was identified as the prison in the commentators' day. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan describe this contemporary identification; Alusi leaves the actual location to God.
  12:45/q21/p4 The cupbearer's certainty Joseph remained there suggests a private prison. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reasons that a release or death from another sort of prison would have reached the king and court.
12:45/q22 What action is left unstated between this request and Joseph's reply? [فَأَرْسِلُونِ · links]
  12:45/q22/p1 The king or court sent the cupbearer to Joseph; he traveled to the prison and then relayed or received the interpretation. — reasons: Tabari and Thaʿlabi identify the ellipsis; Abu Hayyan specifies the dispatch and arrival, and Ibn Kathir says the king sent him. Kuranyolu says he got permission and brought the answer back.
  12:45/q22/p2 Joseph received the dream from the former prisoner without reproaching his failure to remember or demanding release first. — reasons: Ibn Kathir records this narrative detail after the dispatch.
