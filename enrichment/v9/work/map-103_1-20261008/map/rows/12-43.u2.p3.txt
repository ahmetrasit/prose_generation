han hulm or ahlam? [رُءْيَٰىَ لِلرُّءْيَا · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether dictionary synonymy permits substitution in the Qur'an's distinct singular and plural usage.)
  12:43/q41/p1 The terms can be glossed as equivalent. — reasons: Bint al-Shati reports dictionaries explaining hulm by ru'ya, but rejects natural substitution between them in Qur'anic usage.
  12:43/q41/p2 Ru'ya marks a singular, true, clear vision; plural ahlam denotes confused and mixed dreams. — reasons: Bint al-Shati says ahlam occurs three times and is always plural, including 21:5 and 12:44, whereas ru'ya occurs seven times and is always singular. She says the king's fulfilled vision is called ru'ya twice here because it was clear to him, despite the courtiers calling it confused dreams.
