18:25/q15 What report describes attempts to inspect the sleepers? [كَهْفِهِمْ · reports] (turns on: The two reports concern different visits and different kinds of evidence.)
  18:25/q15/p1 Muʿāwiya sought to uncover them; a divinely sent wind burned the men sent in. — reasons: Al-Rāzī transmits that Muʿāwiya passed the cave on a Roman campaign; Ibn ʿAbbās warned that God had forbidden inspection to someone better than Muʿāwiya, but he sent men anyway.
  18:25/q15/p2 Al-Wāthiq sent al-Khwārizmī to a purported Roman cave; the bodies appeared preserved by human means. — reasons: Al-Khwārizmī saw chest hair, suspected a staged display and suggested drying medicines such as aloe; al-Qaffāl says neither the site nor Roman identification is certain.
18:25/q16 How reliable are detailed reports about the cave story? [* · reports] (turns on: Accounts vary widely and no decisive text or physical identification resolves several historical particulars.)
  18:25/q16/p1 Many transmitted details conflict; the shared core is monotheists fleeing a coercive polytheist king and sleeping in a cave. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists reports from al-Qummī, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Ibn Isḥāq, Wahb and al-Nuʿmān; they differ on death, dates, rulers, lineage, looks, names and al-Raqīm. He traces divergence to widespread Christian stories and images, Quraysh's exposure, and Muslim collection of outside traditions through converts. Christian accounts diverge from Muslim reports on number, dog and duration; one Christian chronology names Decius and a fifth-century awakening.
  18:25/q16/p2 Reason and purported site identifications cannot settle the era or location. — reasons: Al-Rāzī requires an unavailable revealed text; al-Qaffāl says Roman site identification is no proof, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds no decisive site evidence.
18:25/q17 Why does the Quran omit many historical details of the sleepers? [* · interpretation]
  18:25/q17/p1 It selects what serves guidance rather than giving a complete history. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says names, family, occupations, status, dates, rulers, cities, persecutors and even the dog's identity do not bear on guidance; later historians filled the omissions with divergent reports.
18:25/q18 Is an inward reading of the duration offered? [* · inward] (turns on: The allegories assign nonliteral measures to the years, which al-Ālūsī contests.)
  18:25/q18/p1 The numbers can allegorize a 25-year cycle and nine months of gestation. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī reads 300 lunar cycles as 25 years and nine as pregnancy, arguing that the verse leaves the count indefinite; al-Ālūsī condemns this reduction of years to months.
  18:25/q18/p2 Spiritual training may take years directly but only days with a guide. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports the allegory and replies that God can guide without intermediaries in hours.
18:25/q19 What does sinīn mean? [سِنِينَ · meaning]
  18:25/q19/p1 It means ordinary years. — reasons: Al-Dāmghānī glosses the phrase as three hundred years and mentions 30:4.
