# 18:19: 51 questions

## 18:19/q01 What does 'thus' compare the awakening with?  [وَكَذَٰلِكَ · links]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative recalls the preceding sleep or heightens the new event.
- 18:19/q01/p1 God's preceding sleep and preservation of the youths, equally wondrous signs of His power. — Al-Wahidi links sleep, increased guidance and turning (18:11,13,18); al-Zajjaj and al-Zamakhshari compare the sleep; al-Baghawi and al-Tabrisi stress preservation from decay; Abu Hayyan sees the earlier compressed account; Alusi says both display overwhelming power. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزجاج والزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج والزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:19/q01/p2 The awakening is likened to itself to magnify its wonder. — Ibn Ashur compares the rhetorical usage at 2:143. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:19/q02 What does 'We raised them' mean and how were they raised?  [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether baʿth is read as bodily awakening in the account or as an inward state.
- 18:19/q02/p1 God woke or revived them from a deathlike long sleep, with their bodies and condition preserved. — Abu Ubayda relates baʿth to resurrection; Ibn Qutayba and al-Wahidi compare slumber to death; Jishumi gives its root as stirring and its other uses as sending messengers or raising the dead; al-Qurtubi cites a poet's waking usage. Ibn Kathir says skin and hair were unchanged after 309 years; al-Asamm and al-Qurtubi say they woke as they slept. [21 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(شاعر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:19/q02/p2 The awakening is an inward return to wakefulness or sobriety. — Qashani calls it the Sufi first awakening, in which faculties discuss latent meanings; Alusi reports the return from spiritual intoxication. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:19/q03 Why were they awakened to question one another?  [لِيَتَسَآءَلُوا۟ بَيْنَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the lām marks a direct purpose or a consequence, and which wisdom the dialogue serves.
- 18:19/q03/p1 Their questioning was a purpose of the awakening, revealing God's power and the duration's mystery. — Muqatil and Jishumi take the stated end directly; al-Razi says disclosure of divine power is desirable itself; al-Samin prefers causal purpose; Tabatabai ties it to 18:12 and rejects reducing it to a minor consequence; Ibn Ashur says it benefits the youths by direct knowledge of resurrection, like Abraham in 2:260. Al-Biqai adds faith and certainty; al-Tabrisi adds recognition of the Creator and gratitude. [21 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الجرجاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(most commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:19/q03/p2 Questioning was the resulting event, while the awakening chiefly displayed divine power. — Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi read the lām as consequence, citing 28:8; al-Baghawi calls questioning an outcome; al-Khafaji prefers this because divine power is the purpose; Asad renders it 'and they began to ask.' Al-Samin and Tabatabai argue against this reduction. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)+; ASAD-NOTES+; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-; TABATABAI-]
- 18:19/q03/p3 They questioned one another to determine their condition, reflect, grow certain and give thanks. — Al-Tabari says the long sleep seemed strange; al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi, and al-Baydawi treat the event as a sign of sleep and resurrection; Elmalili says self-knowledge is a primary concern. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:19/q04 How is the questioning dialogue introduced?  [قَالَ قَآئِلٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · grammar]
- 18:19/q04/p1 The quoted question explains the preceding statement that they questioned one another. — Alusi calls the introductory qāla a resumption; Ibn Ashur calls the ensuing dialogue an explanation of tasāʾul, mutual questioning. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 18:19/q05 Who asked 'How long did you stay?'  [قَآئِلٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: The narrative does not name the questioner; transmitted identifications differ.
- 18:19/q05/p1 Their leader Maksalmina, also spelled Miksalmina, asked. — Muqatil calls him eldest; al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, Bursevi and others call him chief; Alusi and Abu Hayyan preserve alternatives. [7 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:19/q05/p2 An unnamed member asked, apparently sensing an unusual interval. — Ibn Atiyya cites signs of an extraordinary sleep; Tabatabai suggests its heaviness; al-Biqai suggests appearance or other signs. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:19/q05/p3 Tamlikha or Yamlikha, the youth who managed or carried the provisions, asked. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit this alternative; the name has spelling variants. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:19/q06 Why did they ask how long they had stayed?  [كَمْ لَبِثْتُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give different motives and scope for the question.
- 18:19/q06/p1 They noticed something unusual and sought to gauge the sleep's duration. — Al-Tabari says it seemed strange; Ibn Atiyya says conditions looked similar enough that a sudden change was unlikely; al-Biqai points to appearance or other signs, Tabatabai to the heaviness of sleep; al-Tha'labi notes their surprise. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; THALABI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان)]
- 18:19/q06/p2 They feared they had missed a prayer. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi and Alusi record the concern. [3 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:19/q06/p3 The question asks how long since they entered the cave. — Ibn Abbas states this construal. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 18:19/q07 How is 'how long' parsed?  [كَمْ لَبِثْتُمْ · grammar]
- 18:19/q07/p1 Kam is an accusative time expression with 'days' understood. — Al-Tabrisi supplies the omitted counted unit from the answer; al-Samin calls it adverbial. [3 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 18:19/q08 How is labithtum pronounced?  [لَبِثْتُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether thāʾ remains distinct or assimilates into the following consonant.
- 18:19/q08/p1 The other four readers assimilate thāʾ; proximity of articulation permits the form. — Ibn Mujahid reports the four; al-Nahhas explains that the articulation points are close. [2 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم); NAHHAS]
- 18:19/q08/p2 Ibn Kathir, Nafi and Asim pronounce thāʾ distinctly. — Ibn Mujahid names the three readers and says this occurs in each form of the word. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم)]

## 18:19/q09 How should 'a day or part of a day' be understood?  [لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether aw expresses one uncertain estimate, a correction, or different speakers; one report gives longer guesses.
- 18:19/q09/p1 The group gave an uncertain estimate, first thinking a day and then part of one. — Morning entry and remaining sunlight explain the revision for Muqatil, Yahya, al-Mawardi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi and others. Alusi favors plain doubt over a correction; Tabatabai says one speaker's uncertain estimate and rejects distributed speakers. Ibn Kathir says they later deferred to God. [36 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(رُوي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ, Z); BURSEVI+; TABATABAI+]
- 18:19/q09/p2 Different youths supplied the two estimates: some said a day, others part of one. — Al-Nahhas transmits Ibn Abbas with separate youths; Abu Hayyan allows aw to distribute the answers; Elmalili reads the two groups similarly; Ibn Ashur allows it. Alusi calls distribution unlikely; Tabatabai rejects it. [5 notes: NAHHAS(سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-; TABATABAI-]
- 18:19/q09/p3 The report has one day, two days, or longer. — Ibn Abbas report in al-Durr and Tabatabai; Tabatabai objects that two days or more conflicts with the Quranic wording and ordinary evidence. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI-]

## 18:19/q10 Why did their estimate seem so short, and what later made them doubt it?  [يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources differ on the immediate cue for the short guess and on the later evidence of elapsed time.
- 18:19/q10/p1 They slept from morning until later daylight, so the visible sun made a day or part of one seem plausible. — Yahya, Muqatil, al-Wahidi and many others describe morning entry and late waking; al-Kashifi places waking around afternoon, which Bursevi favors because a shopping round trip needed daylight. Alusi says sleep haze prevented checking signs. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(رُوي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ, Z)]
- 18:19/q10/p2 Their grown hair and nails, or altered faces, suggested a much longer sleep. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Razi, al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi and Study Quran mention the signs; Ibn Jawzi reports them as an alternative. Kashshaf juxtaposes these signs with the morning account. [9 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(رُوي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); STUDYQURAN(R, Z)]
- 18:19/q10/p3 Sleep and inward absorption disrupt the ability to measure elapsed time. — Al-Baydawi says sleepers cannot measure duration; al-Qushayri says absorption removed self-awareness; Alusi says sleep haze; Tabatabai notes shadows and sunlight cannot fix an exact day and external events require indicators. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); TABATABAI]

## 18:19/q11 Who said that their Lord knew best how long they had stayed?  [قَالُوا۟ رَبُّكُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا لَبِثْتُمْ · referent]
turns on: The repeated qālū may introduce another group, the whole group, a named leader, or divine speech.
- 18:19/q11/p1 A different group of youths corrected those who estimated a day. — Al-Tabari says the others deferred; al-Zamakhshari says evidence or inspiration showed them a long stay; Abu Hayyan prefers two groups from the dialogue. Alusi says this fits its question-and-answer structure; Tabatabai says they had deeper monotheistic insight, distinct from the factions of 18:12. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 18:19/q11/p2 Their leader Maksalmina, the eldest, stopped the dispute. — Muqatil names him; al-Mawardi cites Ibn Abbas; al-Tha'labi and al-Baghawi transmit this explanation; Ibn Jawzi records the name as Miksalmina. [6 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:19/q11/p3 Tamlikha or Yamlikha, described as their leader, gave the answer. — Al-Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas; al-Tabrisi and al-Razi cite him as the leader, with al-Razi saying altered hair, nails and faces led him to infer a long period. Ibn Jawzi records a competing name. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:19/q11/p4 The whole group agreed to defer to God, or one person's accepted answer was attributed to all. — Ibn Ashur gives both possibilities; al-Baghawi reports the group statement; al-Biqai sees affectionate agreement. Alusi disfavors the same speaker giving both answers. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:19/q11/p5 It is God's own statement about their duration. — Al-Mawardi records this alternative to speech by the youths. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 18:19/q12 What does deferring their duration to God express?  [رَبُّكُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا لَبِثْتُمْ · theology]
turns on: The ordinary explanation concerns epistemic humility; the inward account treats time itself differently.
- 18:19/q12/p1 They acknowledge that only God knows the exact, long duration, and end an unresolvable dispute. — Muqatil says Maksalmina returns the matter to God; al-Qushayri says God placed them in that state; al-Kashshaf says signs or inspiration may have exposed their ignorance; al-Biqai calls it faithful fellowship; Tabatabai contrasts human inference from indicators with God's direct encompassing knowledge (96:5; 2:32). [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 18:19/q12/p2 A leader urged them to stop disagreement because it can ruin a community. — Al-Nahhas transmits Ibn Abbas through Sa'id b. Jubayr; al-Durr repeats the leader's warning. [2 notes: NAHHAS(سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 18:19/q12/p3 Some spiritually realized sleepers understood that the interval had no independent reality. — Asad connects this to 18:12 and death and resurrection. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 18:19/q13 How many youths does the dialogue imply?  [قَالَ قَآئِلٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ قَالُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 18:19/q13/p1 At least seven: one asks and two plural replies require three speakers each. — Al-Nasafi attributes the inference to Ibn Abbas; Tabatabai gives the same count from the singular and two plurals. [2 notes: TABATABAI; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس)+]

## 18:19/q14 Why do they turn from duration to sending for food?  [فَٱبْعَثُوٓا۟ أَحَدَكُم · interpretation]
turns on: Both hunger and fear shape the errand.
- 18:19/q14/p1 They woke hungry and redirected an unknowable discussion to an immediate need. — Al-Tabari says they needed food; al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur call the pivot wiser than continuing debate; al-Qushayri says hunger followed a period without bodily appetites; Alusi points to fa as marking the redirect. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL(الغزنوي)]
- 18:19/q14/p2 The errand also let the envoy learn news about their pursuers. — Al-Tabari and al-Tha'labi report instructions to buy more food and investigate; they still believed Decianus sought them. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; TABATABAI]

## 18:19/q15 Who was sent to the city?  [أَحَدَكُم · referent]
turns on: Narrative reports name the envoy; the verse leaves him unnamed.
- 18:19/q15/p1 Yamlikha, also called Tamlikha, was the envoy. — Muqatil names Yamliha; al-Tabari transmits Muqatil and narrative forms; al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Nasafi and others give variants. Al-Ghaznawi calls him the youngest; al-Wahidi has their leader Tamlikha speak earlier. [43 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير,عمرو,مقاتل,وهب بن منبه); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الغزنوي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler); STUDYQURAN]
- 18:19/q15/p2 An unnamed member of their group went; the command need not identify him. — Ibn Ashur says the group may address an unspecified delegate or omit the known name; the Quran's 'one of you' emphasizes membership. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:19/q15/p3 The wording 'one of you' avoids suggesting a specially honored chief. — Ibn al-Anbari contrasts aḥad with wāḥid; Alusi says the latter might imply their chief. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:19/q16 How does 'with your silver' attach to the envoy?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ · grammar]
- 18:19/q16/p1 It is circumstantial to 'one of you': the envoy goes carrying the silver. — Al-Samin describes the prepositional phrase as a circumstantial qualifier. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 18:19/q17 What occupations do reports give the youths?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ · reports]
- 18:19/q17/p1 They were money changers. — Abu Ja'far's report calls them money changers; al-Sadiq distinguishes changers in speech from dealers in coins. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 18:19/q18 What is 'your silver'?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the noun denotes silver generally or minted coins in its lexical scope.
- 18:19/q18/p1 Silver, whether struck as dirhams or unminted; in context it was their money. — Ibn Qutayba and later authors cite the Arfaja silver-nose report to show unminted silver is included. Al-Asma'i's paper-leaf explanation is dismissed by Alusi. Al-Mawardi distinguishes a fatḥa livestock sense attested in poetry. Biqai develops a lexical family involving foliage, beauty, wealth and gathering; Islahi accordingly translates 'currency.' [38 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS(نحوي غير مسمى); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الأزهري); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الأزهري); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:19/q18/p2 Specifically coined silver dirhams. — The wording and old coin reports support a struck currency; Ibn Ashur calls it a minted coin; several authors describe the king's stamped image. Alusi reports this narrower common explanation. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 18:19/q19 How is bi-wariqikum read?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ · readings]
turns on: The variants concern vowel easing and the qāf-kāf contact; sources disagree on which assimilations are sound.
- 18:19/q19/p1 Fatḥa on wāw and sukūn on rāʾ lightens pronunciation. — Al-Farra names Asim and al-A'mash; most Kufans/Basrans, Abu Amr, Hamza and Abu Bakr are often named. Ibn Khalawayh, al-Nahhas and Qiraat-Er explain avoiding a heavy sequence of vowels. [20 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(أهل الكوفة وأبو عمرو); IBNMUJAHID; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); THALABI(أبو عمرو وحمزة وخلف); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر والحسن والأعمش واليزيدي ويعقوب وخلف وأبو عبيد وابن سعدان); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; JISHUMI(أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم)]
- 18:19/q19/p2 Assimilation of qāf into kāf, sometimes with a still rāʾ, is transmitted. — Al-Tabari reports a Meccan form; al-Zajjaj permits it and al-Farisi explains movement from the rear qāf to nearer kāf. Abu Hatim transmits Ibn Muhaysin; Ibn Jinni says possible, yet calls Abu Raja's form concealed articulation rather than full assimilation because full assimilation transfers the vowel. Abu Hayyan challenges a reported attribution to Ibn Kathir. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أهل المدينة); FARISI-HUJJA; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو حاتم); THALABI(أهل مكة); TABRISI(ابن جني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو رجاء,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو رجاء وابن محيصن); ALUSI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن محيصن)-; RAZI-FULL(ابن محيصن)-]
- 18:19/q19/p3 Fatḥa on wāw and kasra on rāʾ, with qāf pronounced, is the common or original reading. — Al-Tabari prefers the original kasra pattern but accepts alternatives; Ibn Khalawayh calls kasra original. Ibn Mujahid, al-Farisi, al-Qurtubi and others name Ibn Kathir, Nafi, Ibn Amir, al-Kisa'i and Hafs; al-Tabari lists Medinan and some Iraqi readers. The forms are Arabic dialect variants with the same sense. - 18:19/q19/p5 Ali reads bawāriqikum, a collective form relating to silver. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin describe its pattern; Alusi mentions it. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); SAMIN-DURR(أمير المؤمنين); ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:19/q20 What does the demonstrative 'this' add to the silver?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ هَٰذِهِۦٓ · rhetoric]
- 18:19/q20/p1 It points to money at hand, perhaps held out or handed to the envoy. — Bursevi infers a companion physically handed it over; Alusi says the speaker held it out. Tabatabai says the unusually emphatic mention foreshadows how the aged coin exposed them. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 18:19/q21 What were the coins like and where had they come from?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ هَٰذِهِۦٓ · reports]
turns on: Narratives add the old ruler's imprint to the verse's bare mention of silver.
- 18:19/q21/p1 They carried old coins from the reign they fled, often bearing the persecuting king's image. — Al-Wahidi and Ibn Abbas say the stamped image was the old king's; al-Tabari dates the coins 309 years earlier; al-Tha'labi says Daqyanus' seal. Ibn Kathir says they brought and spent money when fleeing; Abu Hayyan says they brought it for expenses. Ibn Abbas likens the currency to camel hooves; later reports call the ruler Decius. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير,عمرو,وهب بن منبه); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler); STUDYQURAN]
- 18:19/q21/p2 Their coin was silver but its exact minting is not specified by the verse. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Kashshaf and al-Razi allow coined and uncoined silver; al-Wahidi cites the old stamped-coin narrative as a separate report. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 18:19/q22 Which city was meant?  [ٱلْمَدِينَةِ · referent]
turns on: Reports vary in names and whether they denote one city over time.
- 18:19/q22/p1 Their former city was Ephesus, also reported as Daqsus or Daphsus and later called Tarsus. — Al-Tabari calls it Ephesus; al-Wahidi calls it Daqsus and present Tarsus; al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi reconcile Ephesus as the former name with Tarsus as later name. Alusi says it was familiar home; Ibn Ashur calls it Ephesus. Al-Tha'labi's story spells Daphsus. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(عمرو); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:19/q22/p2 Tarsus is the city's name. — Several sources state this directly without reconciling the older name. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 18:19/q22/p3 It was the city from which they fled; its specific name is unnecessary to the phrase. — Al-Tabrisi and Ibn Kathir identify it by relation to the youths; the Quran leaves the proper name unstated. [3 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:19/q23 Who or what does 'which of it' refer to?  [أَيُّهَآ · referent]
turns on: Whether the city's people, its locations, or its foods are supplied to resolve the pronoun.
- 18:19/q23/p1 The city's inhabitants or vendors are understood: which person has the relevant food. — Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj and al-Nahhas supply 'people'; al-Kashshaf compares 'ask the town' at 12:82; al-Nasafi, al-Samin, Bursevi and Tabatabai agree. Al-Tabari notes listeners understand food belongs to people. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 18:19/q23/p2 The pronoun can point directly to foods understood from context. — Al-Nahhas allows 'which food'; al-Samin says no elision is required; Abu Hayyan and Alusi present this alternative. [4 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:19/q23/p3 It can refer grammatically to the city itself with an understood person or place. — Al-Tabari says the syntax attaches it to 'city' although the sense is inhabitants; Alusi lists city, residents, or food. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:19/q23/p4 It means which place in the city, such as a market stall. — Ibn Ashur makes place the referent and ṭaʿāman the specification of the comparative. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:19/q24 How is 'let him look which has purer food' parsed?  [فَلْيَنظُرْ أَيُّهَآ أَزْكَىٰ طَعَامًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical choice is interrogative versus relative ayyu and whether naẓar governs it or its clause.
- 18:19/q24/p1 Ayyuhā is an interrogative subject, azkā its predicate, and ṭaʿāman a specification. — Al-Zajjaj gives this parse; al-Wahidi cites Abu Ishaq; al-Razi and al-Nasafi follow; al-Samin and Alusi allow it. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:19/q24/p2 Ayy may be relative and function as the object, with an omitted predicate. — Abu Hayyan attributes this to Sibawayh; al-Samin and Alusi record the interrogative/relative options. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:19/q24/p3 The interrogative clause is what 'let him look' investigates. — Al-Akhfash says naẓar can be followed by a question without directly governing ayyu, illustrated with 'look, is Zayd more generous or Amr?'; al-Tabrisi calls the whole clause the object. [2 notes: AKHFASH; TABRISI]
- 18:19/q24/p4 Naẓar can mean seeing or mental consideration. — Abu Hayyan states both senses. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 18:19/q25 What does 'purest food' mean?  [أَزْكَىٰ طَعَامًۭا · meaning]

- 18:19/q25/p1 Lawful, pure food, especially properly slaughtered meat from believers and free of idolatrous sacrifice. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibn Abbas are repeatedly cited; al-Farra and al-Zajjaj explain that many townspeople were Magians, while hidden believers could slaughter lawfully. - 18:19/q25/p2 The more abundant food, or the vendor with the most food. — Ikrima gives the gloss; Abu Ubayda cites the seven-versus-three tribes line, repeated by al-Tha'labi; Ibn Qutayba derives it from increase. ## 18:19/q26 What specific food did they seek or buy?  [طَعَامًۭا · reports]
turns on: Reports name different foods while the verse keeps the noun general.
- 18:19/q26/p1 Lawfully slaughtered meat, perhaps from a concealed believer. — Ibn Abbas, Sa'id and al-Farra point to Magian or idolatrous slaughter; al-Kashifi says hidden believers could supply meat. ## 18:19/q27 What does 'bring you provision from it' mean?  [فَلْيَأْتِكُم بِرِزْقٍۢ مِّنْهُ · interpretation]
turns on: The antecedent of minhu may be selected food or the coins; both yield provisions.
- 18:19/q27/p1 Bring them some of the selected food for them to eat. — Al-Tabari's report and al-Mawardi give food; al-Tha'labi, al-Nasafi and Ibn Ashur call it sustenance. Tabatabai prefers the antecedent 'food' because silver would require an extra omitted pronoun. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 18:19/q27/p2 Bring lawful food for them to eat. — Al-Mawardi records a more specific gloss; Alusi says the provision should be lawful on either pronoun analysis. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:19/q27/p3 The pronoun 'from it' can refer to their silver, the source or substitute for the provision. — Alusi allows min to be partitive, source marking or substitution; Tabatabai calls this remote because an additional pronoun must be assumed. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 18:19/q28 What does 'let him act gently' require?  [وَلْيَتَلَطَّفْ · interpretation]
turns on: The verb's gentleness is applied to concealment, buying, seller relations, or the food's delicacy.
- 18:19/q28/p1 Move, shop and return discreetly, carefully and courteously so their presence stays hidden. — Muqatil and Yahya stress secrecy; al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Razi and Ibn Kathir specify roads, entry and purchases. Abu Hayyan prefers concealment from context; Tabatabai specifies smooth dealing to prevent quarrels and rejects generic transaction caution. Alusi includes courtesy; al-Qushayri emphasizes kind dealing with the seller. [32 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)+; TABATABAI+]
- 18:19/q28/p2 Bargain or inspect carefully to avoid a high price or being cheated. — Al-Mawardi gives low-price bargaining; al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi and Baydawi give avoiding deception. Tabatabai argues this broad transactional sense lacks support from the following secrecy clause. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 18:19/q28/p3 Deal gently with the seller without haggling or quarrelling. — Al-Tabrisi offers this alternative; al-Qushayri and Alusi stress courtesy, and Tabatabai says a quarrel could betray them. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:19/q28/p4 Choose delicate food suited to recently fasting bodies or refined spiritual people. — Alusi records the bodily and spiritual explanations but rejects making delicate food a rule, citing simple diet and clothing of the Prophet and major Companions. [4 notes: SULAMI(يوسف بن الحسين); QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل,يوسف بن الحسين); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 18:19/q29 How are the commands 'let him look' and 'let him act gently' read?  [فَلْيَنظُرْ وَلْيَتَلَطَّفْ · readings]
turns on: The variation is vowel on the imperative lām and an attested passive verb.
- 18:19/q29/p1 The command lām is quiescent in the common reading; kasra preserves its original vowel. — Al-Zajjaj allows kasra in falyatikum; al-Nahhas notes it in li-ya'tikum and li-yatalaṭṭaf; Ibn Atiyya reports al-Hasan's kasra in falyanẓur and yatalattaf; al-Samin calls it original. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 18:19/q29/p2 Qutayba al-Mayyal reads yutalaṭṭaf passively, with ḍamma on yāʾ. — Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and Alusi report it. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتيبة الميال); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قتيبة الميال)]

## 18:19/q30 What does 'let him not alert anyone to you' mean?  [وَلَا يُشْعِرَنَّ بِكُمْ أَحَدًا · meaning]
turns on: The warning's scope differs when the envoy is captured.
- 18:19/q30/p1 He must tell no one about the group or its location and avoid conduct that reveals them. — Abu Ubayda and Ibn Qutayba derive shaʿara as knowing and connect 'poet' to perception; Ibn Ashur explains causative ishʿār and an omitted object that covers their existence, number and location; the emphatic nūn signals the danger. Al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi, Bursevi and al-Biqai include unintended disclosure. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 18:19/q30/p2 If he is caught, he must not expose his companions. — Al-Zajjaj, al-Tha'labi and Abu Ishaq construe the warning as protection of the others after the envoy is discovered. Al-Wahidi prefers that he not disclose them voluntarily, but reports a contrary oath if captured. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي وغيره)-]
- 18:19/q30/p3 If captured, he should guide captors to the other youths so they share martyrdom. — Al-Suddi and others report their pledge not to keep martyrdom to one person; al-Wahidi finds this more fitting than Abu Ishaq's reading while preferring voluntary secrecy. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي وغيره); WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 18:19/q31 How is 'let no one know' read grammatically?  [وَلَا يُشْعِرَنَّ بِكُمْ أَحَدًا · readings]
turns on: The variant changes the verb's subject and case of aḥad.
- 18:19/q31/p1 Abu Salih, Yazid ibn al-Qa'qa', Qutayba and Abu Ja'far read an active form with aḥad nominative as subject. — Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and Alusi report this reading. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو صالح ويزيد بن القعقاع وقتيبة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر وأبو صالح وقتيبة); ALUSI-FULL(قتيبة، أبو صالح، يزيد بن القعقاع)]
- 18:19/q31/p2 The regular construction forbids the envoy to make anyone aware of them. — Ibn Ashur calls ishʿār a causative verb taking one object and notes the emphatic nūn; al-Kashshaf says the envoy can cause awareness even unintentionally. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:19/q32 How did the cave open and the sleepers awaken in the narrative reports?  [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ over how the sealed entrance was opened and include a separate later revival story.
- 18:19/q32/p1 A shepherd opened the cave as shelter for sheep; the youths awoke the following morning. — Al-Tabari attributes this to Wahb; Study Quran transmits it from al-Tabari; Ibn Atiyya records a shepherd as one account. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 18:19/q32/p2 The entrance had been sealed, collapsed, or later unsealed. — Al-Tha'labi says Decianus sealed it and Tamlikha noticed the stones removed; Ibn Atiyya says it may have collapsed, alternatively been opened by a shepherd. [3 notes: THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 18:19/q32/p3 They awoke after the persecuting king and city had disappeared. — Al-Sadiq's account says the dog slept with them; al-Mawardi also reports Muqatil's statement that the dog slept. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 18:19/q32/p4 Olyas removed stones to make a sheep pen; the revived youths greeted each other and prayed. — Al-Tabari says terror kept others at a distance while they revived unchanged; the account gives a named agent of the opening. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 18:19/q32/p5 God restored their souls for a later greeting to Muhammad's companions, then they returned to sleep. — Al-Tha'labi reports the four Companions brought by Solomon's wind; a variant says the Mahdi will later greet and revive them. [6 notes: THALABI(أصحاب الكهف,أصحاب النبي,جبريل)]

## 18:19/q33 What did the youths say and fear immediately after awakening?  [لِيَتَسَآءَلُوا۟ بَيْنَهُمْ · reports]
turns on: Narratives differ in post-awakening details and the state in which the youths were later found.
- 18:19/q33/p1 They simply estimated their sleep and made a food errand under a later just or Muslim king. — Abd Allah ibn Ubayd ibn Umayr and Ibn Abbas reports describe a just or Muslim ruler already reigning. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 18:19/q33/p2 They prayed and thought Decianus still sought them; some urged steadfastness even unto death. — Al-Tabari's account says Yemliha warned of forced sacrifice or death and Maksalimina vowed fidelity; al-Tha'labi reports the same with Tamlikha. Their pledge held them together in life and death. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(مكسلمينا); THALABI(مكسلمينا)]
- 18:19/q33/p3 One report has the sleepers' souls gone when the king found their bodies; another says bones had vanished. — Qatada reports bodies without souls; Ibn Abbas says their bones had vanished after more than 300 years. Study Quran reports perfectly preserved bodies after they fell asleep again. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 18:19/q34 What happened when the envoy reached the changed city?  [إِلَى ٱلْمَدِينَةِ · reports]
turns on: Reports agree on a changed era and an old coin, but differ in officials, discovery details and the cave's outcome.
- 18:19/q34/p1 His ancient coin led sellers to suspect hidden treasure and take him before the authorities. — Al-Tabari names governors Arius/Areus and Astius; al-Tha'labi calls the first Armos. They bound or threatened him; no one knew him or his father. He asked about Decianus, learned he had died centuries earlier, and revealed companions in the cave. Ibn Abbas's report adds a baker and a scholar matching names to an old tablet. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير,عكرمة,عمرو,وهب بن منبه); THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler); STUDYQURAN]
- 18:19/q34/p2 A righteous king or governors recognized the youths as a divine sign and went to the cave. — Wahb and Ikrima name a just or Muslim king; al-Tabari's narrative has Areus, Astius and the people accompany Yemliha; al-Tha'labi has Armos; reports differ whether terror prevented entry, whether they found preserved bodies, and whether a mosque or church was built (18:21). [17 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير,عكرمة,قتادة,وهب بن منبه); THALABI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler)]
- 18:19/q34/p3 He saw public Christian signs and heard Jesus invoked, unlike the persecution he remembered. — Al-Tabari and al-Tha'labi describe his bewilderment and fear of madness; Ibn Atiyya says landmarks and clothing had changed. Al-Sadiq adds mutually unintelligible speech. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 18:19/q34/p4 The verse leaves the exact route of discovery unspecified. — Islahi says investigation eventually exposed their origins, perhaps through the market; Tabatabai says the old coin is a narrative key. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 18:19/q35 Why did God awaken the youths in that era according to the reports?  [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports supply a specific royal crisis behind the broader resurrection lesson.
- 18:19/q35/p1 Their awakening was a sign of resurrection and God's protection, whether or not a king had prayed. — Al-Tabrisi likens it to resurrection; Kashshaf, Baydawi, Biqai, Ibn Ashur, Study Quran and Kuranyolu emphasize divine power. Tabatabai says persecution had passed and monotheists could receive the sign. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:19/q35/p2 Their appearance answered a dispute over resurrection of body and soul. — Ikrima says the community was divided; Ibn Ishaq names Theodosius, whose 68-year reign saw deniers of bodily resurrection gain ground. The king donned sackcloth, sat in ashes and prayed. God made the youths a sign confirming the Hour and bodily resurrection, preserving the king and believers. Al-Kashifi's version names Tindrous. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,عكرمة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); TABATABAI]

## 18:19/q36 What happened in the report of Muhammad's message to the sleepers?  [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The later visitation reports differ in visitor and timing.
- 18:19/q36/p1 Four eminent companions carried Muhammad's greeting; the revived youths accepted his prophethood and returned to sleep. — Al-Tha'labi reports Gabriel instructing Muhammad to send Abu Bakr, Umar, Ali and Abu Dharr on a cloth carried by Solomon's gentle wind. The dog greeted them; God restored the youths' souls; they answered, praised God, accepted Islam and sent greetings. A separate variant gives the Mahdi a later greeting. The Prophet prayed for loved ones and companions. [7 notes: THALABI(أصحاب الكهف,أصحاب النبي,جبريل,رسول الله)]
- 18:19/q36/p2 The Mahdi will greet them, after which they will sleep until resurrection. — Al-Tha'labi presents this as a variant to the four-companion narrative. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 18:19/q37 What legal rule about sending an agent is drawn from the errand?  [فَٱبْعَثُوٓا۟ أَحَدَكُم · law]
turns on: Jurists agree on delegation for need but differ over an unexcused, present principal.
- 18:19/q37/p1 Agency is valid for delegable, lawful matters, including buying food. — Ibn Atiyya cites Ali appointing Aqil before Uthman; al-Qurtubi adds 9:60, 12:93, Urwa al-Bariqi, Jabir collecting fifteen wasqs at Khaybar, and Bukhari's Abd al-Rahman–Umayya protection contract. Ibn al-Arabi calls this passage the strongest proof. Al-Qurtubi bars agency for usurpation or unlawful acts. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,جابر بن عبد الله,عبد الرحمن بن عوف); ALUSI-FULL(ابن العربي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 18:19/q37/p2 Even a healthy present person may appoint an agent. — Al-Qurtubi says most permit this and cites the Prophet delegating repayment of a camel debt with a better camel; the creditor praised the repayment. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي ﷺ); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 18:19/q37/p3 A present person without an excuse may not appoint an agent. — Al-Qurtubi reports Abu Hanifa and Sahnun; Ibn al-Arabi speculates Sahnun used the restriction against oppressors. Al-Qurtubi and the prophetic camel report oppose it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة وسحنون); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 18:19/q37/p4 The youths' fear of discovery was an excuse for delegation. — Al-Qurtubi says all scholars agree an excused person may appoint an agent, even where they dispute healthy present persons. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 18:19/q38 Does the shared silver support pooling money and food?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ هَٰذِهِۦٓ · law]
turns on: Whether the plural possessive establishes shared ownership or merely a pooled errand.
- 18:19/q38/p1 Yes: companions may pool costs, buy jointly and eat together despite unequal appetites. — Al-Mawardi calls this munahada; Ibn Khuwayz Mindad cites this verse and 2:220; al-Qurtubi cites school permission to mix donated meals and the orphan and shared eating verses (2:220, cited as 24:16). Al-Kiya permits an eater to pay extra for a portion. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن العربي); TABATABAI]
- 18:19/q38/p2 Their communal purse expresses mutual care and rebukes habitual begging. — Alusi says one youth may have had no money, perhaps a shepherd, yet the group calls it theirs; Tabatabai sees equal delegation and mutual concern. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:19/q38/p3 The verse alone does not prove a partnership, since each might have handed over money separately. — Ibn al-Arabi relies instead on hadiths. Ibn Umar's two-dates prohibition concerns companions' permission; al-Qurtubi says the army-of-khabt report may reflect equal distribution, so its joint-eating proof is weaker. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد)-]

## 18:19/q39 What does their carrying provisions teach about trust in God?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ · theology]
turns on: The dispute concerns ordinary means versus exceptional abandonment by those held to have special certainty.
- 18:19/q39/p1 Prudent provisioning and work are compatible with reliance on God. — Al-Kashshaf cites Aisha on a pilgrim's money belt and an ascetic scholar's refusal of patrons; al-Razi and Ibn Kathir agree. Bursevi cites the Mathnawi: cultivate before relying; Alusi invokes tying the camel. Abu Hayyan and al-Nasafi repeat the money belt maxim. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(عائشة رضي الله عنها); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(مولانا); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:19/q39/p2 Those especially firm in certainty may relinquish ordinary means. — Alusi records eminent scholars who cite Khalid drinking poison and other extraordinary reports; Ahmad, Ishaq and others permit desert travel without supplies or foregoing earning and treatment. Abraham's refusal of Gabriel's help illustrates direct reliance. Alusi rejects attributing this only to Sufis. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أحمد، إسحق,إبراهيم,بعض الأجلة); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 18:19/q40 What does the brief time estimate teach about judgment?  [لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · law]
turns on: The first position concerns permissibility of probable judgment; the second explains its limit.
- 18:19/q40/p1 Reasoned conjecture can be acted upon and is not a lie if later shown mistaken. — Al-Kashshaf derives permission for ijtihad; al-Nasafi, al-Biqai, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi say their mistaken estimate remained sincere. Tabatabai distinguishes judgment about duration from blameworthy dissension in action. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 18:19/q40/p2 An exact factual judgment is unavailable from signs alone, so knowledge returns to God. — Tabatabai says sun, light and shadow cannot determine a full day; humans infer through signs while God knows each event directly. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:19/q41 How is this scene linked to resurrection and the perception of time?  [لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · links]
turns on: The parallel may serve bodily resurrection, Barzakh perception, or a wider lesson on the nature of time.
- 18:19/q41/p1 The youths' brief estimate parallels the revived passerby and people at resurrection or in Barzakh. — Elmalili cites 2:259; Study Quran lists 2:259, 10:45, 17:52, 20:104, 23:112–13, 30:55, 46:35 and 79:46. Asad connects 2:259 and creation-life-resurrection passages (3:27, 6:95, 10:31, 30:19). Islahi takes it as a preview of perceived Barzakh time; Tabatabai likens worldly life to a brief dream and quotes Ali that people awaken on dying. [12 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:19/q41/p2 The youths' answer discloses the deception of earthly time perception. — Asad says an earthbound conception of time is deceptive; Qushayri's inward reading says days of union feel short, citing poems. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:19/q41/p3 The story shows truth survives long spans despite apparent triumph of coercive falsehood. — Tabatabai compares the century-old revival at 2:259 and says time neither destroys truth nor revives falsehood. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:19/q42 What inward reading is given to their duration and awakening?  [بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ · inward]
turns on: The notes read the temporal disorientation as a spiritual state.
- 18:19/q42/p1 Spiritual awakening follows absorption; apparent time vanishes in love or union. — Qashani reads the youths as faculties awakening to discuss latent truths; al-Qushayri says self-absorption removes detailed awareness and cites poetry on short-feeling union; Ibn Ata says longing makes any union seem short. Alusi describes sobriety after intoxication and cites a poem comparing a life of union to uncounted nights. [8 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:19/q42/p2 Return to embodied sobriety makes food an immediate concern. — Al-Qushayri says bodily appetite returns after absorption; Alusi says spiritual principles must then be enacted in ordinary life. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:19/q43 What do the silver, city and food signify inwardly?  [بِوَرِقِكُمْ ٱلْمَدِينَةِ أَزْكَىٰ طَعَامًۭا · inward]
turns on: The allegorical mappings differ: knowledge, intellect, or scrupulous livelihood.
- 18:19/q43/p1 Silver is innate first principles, the city a gathering place of knowledge, and food sound knowledge that nourishes the heart. — Qashani cites the saying that Muhammad is the city of knowledge and Ali its gate; he rejects empty dialectic and superficial appearances, with 88:7 as a contrast. One learner may then teach the rest, as 9:122 suggests. [5 notes: QASHANI(عليه السلام)]
- 18:19/q43/p2 The envoy is thought among spiritual faculties, seeking intellect instead of illusion, imagination or sensation. — Qashani gives this alternate symbolic mapping of youths and city. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 18:19/q43/p3 Pure food signifies scrupulous lawful sustenance that preserves insight. — Alusi quotes Dhu al-Nun on not extinguishing gnosis with scruple; he criticizes a shaykh accepting money known to come from an oppressor. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ذو النون)]

## 18:19/q44 What inward instruction is drawn from acting gently and concealing the youths?  [وَلْيَتَلَطَّفْ وَلَا يُشْعِرَنَّ بِكُمْ أَحَدًا · inward]
turns on: The inward application concerns spiritual training and the audience for hidden knowledge.
- 18:19/q44/p1 Choose an upright, verified guide and deal gently with spiritually mature people. — Qashani warns against pretenders; Sulami transmits Abu Uthman that gnostics receive gentleness and novices firmness; Yusuf ibn al-Husayn gives gnostics delicate food and ascetics ordinary food. Qushayri reports the same food distinction. Alusi criticizes a mandatory delicate-food rule because major exemplars lived simply. [6 notes: QASHANI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان المعزي,يوسف بن الحسين); QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل,يوسف بن الحسين); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:19/q44/p2 Conceal spiritual realities from people unable to receive them. — Qashani says ignorant people should not be told the youths' state or faith; Alusi says veiled people cannot perceive such light. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:19/q45 Why must discovery be avoided, and what threat follows?  [وَلَا يُشْعِرَنَّ بِكُمْ أَحَدًا · interpretation]
turns on: Sources parse the danger as discovery followed by bodily or verbal attack and coercion.
- 18:19/q45/p1 The youths feared being found by persecutors, stoned, or forced back to idolatry. — Yahya and the narrative sources say discovery would destroy their religious success; Tabatabai says stoning signals collective hostility. Abu Hayyan prefers literal stoning over verbal abuse and says 'return' can mean entering an old creed even without earlier membership. Tabatabai says forced participation, not merely verbal assent, was feared. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI(مكسلمينا); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 18:19/q45/p2 The next verse's 'appearance over you' can mean discovering their location or gaining ascendancy. — Abu Hayyan gives both; Tabatabai cites al-Raghib's semantic path from visibility to finding and prevailing, preferring location discovery because it leads to capture. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); TABATABAI+]
- 18:19/q45/p3 The threatened rajm can mean verbal abuse. — Hajjaj and Ibn Jubayr are reported with this gloss; Abu Hayyan favors stoning because the king meant to kill and earlier people used it against dissent. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 18:19/q45/p4 Someone trapped under coercion may outwardly comply while preserving faith, but a person free to leave must avoid voluntarily losing religious practice. — Tabatabai cites 16:106 and 3:28 for coerced dissimulation, 4:97 against self-imposed helplessness, and rejects proposed explanations that coercion itself causes apostasy or that dissimulation was once barred. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+; TABATABAI-]

## 18:19/q46 How do later variant readings shape the threatened discovery?  [وَلَا يُشْعِرَنَّ بِكُمْ أَحَدًا · readings]
turns on: The following verse's verb has an active/passive variant and disputed pronoun antecedent.
- 18:19/q46/p1 Zayd ibn Ali reads yuẓhirū passively in the following threat. — Abu Hayyan reports this reading as a continuation of the secrecy warning. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي)]
- 18:19/q46/p2 The ordinary active form means the city people find or overpower the youths. — Abu Hayyan says the pronoun may denote inferred unbelievers, generic aḥad, or elided townspeople; al-Zamakhshari favors the townspeople, invoking 23:8 for plural after a generic. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 18:19/q47 What does the scene show about the youths' fellowship?  [بَيْنَهُمْ بِوَرِقِكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw communal ethics and an inward pedagogy from the plural dialogue.
- 18:19/q47/p1 They share inquiry, resources, responsibility and concern for one another's faith and safety. — Al-Biqai sees affectionate agreement in deferring knowledge to God; Tabatabai highlights equal delegation, communal money and tact. Alusi reads 'your silver' as shared even if one youth had none, and the narratives recall their pledge never to abandon each other. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL; THALABI]
- 18:19/q47/p2 One fully developed member can learn on behalf of the group and awaken the others. — Qashani appeals to 9:122 for the sufficiency of one envoy in learning. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 18:19/q48 How does the verse fit the surah's structure?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize either the local narrative expansion or the larger surah purpose.
- 18:19/q48/p1 It expands the earlier compressed cave account and answers the surah's central challenge about resurrection. — Abu Hayyan links the sleep-and-awakening sequence to 18:11; Islahi says the present questioning expands the summary near 18:13; al-Biqai ties the story to Quraysh's consultation of Jewish questioners and says acceptance or denial of the sign exposes whether they seek guidance. ## 18:19/q49 Where is the Quran's textual midpoint counted?  [وَلْيَتَلَطَّفْ · sciences]
turns on: Different counting conventions yield different midpoint markers.
- 18:19/q49/p1 The tāʾ in walyatalaṭṭaf is counted as the midpoint letter. — Bursevi mentions an old count; Ibn Ashur records the saying. [3 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:19/q49/p2 The nūn in nakran at 18:74 is counted as midpoint. — Ibn Ashur cites Ibn Atiyya and gives this alternate reckoning. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:19/q49/p3 The midpoint of the thirty parts is at 18:75. — Ibn Ashur distinguishes the division into thirty from letter counts and calls the surah's placement divinely arranged. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:19/q50 How reliable and complete are the historical cave reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: The extant narratives supply different historical details, and the Quran does not settle them.
- 18:19/q50/p1 The reports disagree extensively, and the Quran selects only details needed for guidance. — Ibn Atiyya deliberately shortens conflicting accounts to sounder essentials. Tabatabai lists conflicting names, dates, lineage, rulers, occupations and cave sites; he attributes variation to the tale's spread among People of the Book and to Muslim uptake of converts' outside reports, including Wahb and Ka'b. He says the Quran omits biography and gives withdrawal, sleep, awakening, exposure and mosque in sequence. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:19/q50/p2 The broader story includes a later encounter with the cave or its remains. — Al-Tabari reports Ibn Abbas passing a cave whose bones had vanished; Study Quran reports another account of preserved bodies after a renewed sleep. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 18:19/q50/p3 Christian accounts and Muslim reports partly overlap but differ on numbers, dog and duration. — Tabatabai says most historians place events between Jesus and Muhammad, outside the two Testaments, while Christian preservation was extensive; he compares Christian accounts with Ibn Ishaq's report. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أهل الرواية والتاريخ)]

## 18:19/q51 Where was the cave located according to later identifications?  [* · reports]
turns on: Archaeological and historical fit is debated without decisive identification.
- 18:19/q51/p1 Rujayb near Amman is the strongest proposed fit, though identification remains unproved. — Tabatabai points to Jordanian excavation and some Muslim reports while stating no proposed cave has decisive evidence; he places Amman at ancient Philadelphia under Roman control until Muslim conquest. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 18:19/q51/p2 Other proposals place it near Damascus, Petra, Scandinavia or Nakhchivan. — Tabatabai lists Mount Qasiyun, Petra, seven preserved Roman-looking bodies in Scandinavia and a Caucasus cave; he objects that the northern claims conflict with the Roman setting and reach. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
