# 78:16: 11 questions

## 78:16/q01 What are the gardens mentioned here?  [وَجَنَّٰتٍ · meaning]
- 78:16/q01/p1 Tree-covered orchards or their covering trees. — Al-Ālūsī explains that a janna conceals the ground with trees; al-Farrāʾ distinguishes a palm grove from a vine-bearing firdaws, and janna can itself name the covering trees. He cites a line of Zuhayr for the latter usage. Al-Ṭabrisī calls them orchards dense with trees; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the word evokes shade, fruit, water, and beauty. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(زهير); TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:16/q02 How does the phrase name gardens when it describes their trees?  [وَجَنَّٰتٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the tree reference is explained by omitted words or by the word ‘gardens’ standing for its trees.
- 78:16/q02/p1 The words ‘trees of’ are understood but omitted. — Al-Ṭabrisī says the context supplies the omitted words ‘trees of’ before the description. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 78:16/q02/p2 ‘Gardens’ stands for the trees within them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains this use of the garden name by its trees and the shade, fruit, water, and beauty they evoke. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 78:16/q03 How is ‘and gardens’ joined to the preceding list?  [وَجَنَّٰتٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the noun directly continues the preceding coordination or has an understood verb repeated before it.
- 78:16/q03/p1 It is coordinated with the grain that rain brings forth. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly coordinates ‘interwoven gardens’ with grain and says their trees intertwine. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 78:16/q03/p2 An implied ‘we bring forth’ may be supplied before ‘gardens.’ — Al-Qurṭubī allows omission of the governing ‘we bring forth’ before this noun; he also notes that laffa describes a dense garden or plant and lafʾa a thick-limbed woman. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 78:16/q04 Where may the recitation pause before the end of this phrase?  [وَجَنَّٰتٍ أَلْفَافًا · grammar]
- 78:16/q04/p1 There is no stop in the sequence from ‘Did We not make’ through ‘thick gardens’; a necessary pause falls at ‘pegs’ or ‘livelihood.’ — Al-Nasafī gives the uninterrupted reading and the two earlier pause locations, referring to 78:6, 78:10, and 78:11. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 78:16/q05 What does ‘alfāf’ describe about the gardens?  [أَلْفَافًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the description points chiefly to interwoven trees or crops, a combination of ground crops and upper trees, or the gardens' many colors.
- 78:16/q05/p1 Their trees, branches, or plants grow densely together and intertwine. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Zayd and the exegetes describe clustering, adjacent growth, or parts interwoven above one another; al-Ṭabarī says early interpreters agree on this sense. Abū ʿUbayda specifies no open space between trees; al-Qurṭubī mentions strong close branches, Ibn ʿAṭiyya fresh well-watered leaves and branches, and al-Biqāʿī abundant water. A poetic line cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya uses alfāf for mingled groups and origins; al-Bayḍāwī also cites poetry. Ibn Kathīr compares diverse gardens sharing water with 13:4. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the density creates beauty even if farmers sometimes prefer spacing for yield. Sources vary between ‘intertwined,’ ‘clustered,’ and ‘wrapped’ in wording. [36 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشاعر,جمهور اللغويين); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 78:16/q05/p2 The phrase can describe crops growing densely side by side so that they form gardens. — Al-Māwardī transmits ʿIkrima's crop reading; al-Durr transmits ʿIkrima's explanation that crops become gardens as their parts grow close. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 78:16/q05/p3 Crops densely cover the ground while trees densely fill the space above. — Al-Māwardī reports this layered account as another explanation of the gardens' dense growth. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 78:16/q05/p4 The gardens contain many colors. — Al-Māwardī transmits this explanation from al-Kalbī. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي)]

## 78:16/q06 What singular or formation is proposed for ‘alfāf’?  [أَلْفَافًا · grammar]
turns on: Which singular is attested and which plural pattern can yield alfāf; authorities dispute the vowel, an intermediate plural, and whether deletion or a singularless plural is acceptable.
- 78:16/q06/p1 It is a plural of liff or laf, a word for a wrapped or dense garden. — Al-Akhfash, Abū ʿUbayda, al-Kisāʾī and most lexicographers are reported with this singular, usually with kasra; al-Ṭabarī prefers liff or lafīf since agreement on intertwining favors it over lafāʾ, which directly means thick. Al-Naḥḥās favors liff for the interpreters' gathered-garden sense. Abū Ḥayyān says the form is recorded by most lexicographers. Al-Samīn cites Abū ʿAlī al-Ṭūsī's ‘jannat liff’ line; al-Ālūsī cites al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī al-Ṭūsī's line with the same expression. Al-Ālūsī calls it the regular plural of a descriptive ‘wrapped’ word; Elmalılı calls it leff. Some sources leave the vowel unmarked. [16 notes: AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور اللغويين); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي الطوسي); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن بن علي الطوسي); ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)+; ALUSI-FULL(جمهور اللغويين)+; NAHHAS+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس)-]
- 78:16/q06/p2 It is a second plural: lafāʾ or a collective alf yields lif or laf, then alfāf. — Ibn Qutayba gives collective alf or lifāʾ as starting points; Abū al-ʿAbbās and al-Mubarrad give lifāʾ or lifa as the singular, then lif and alfāf. Al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī report versions of the two plural steps. Abū Ḥayyān describes liff as itself a plural of liff. Al-Zamakhsharī doubts the pattern because he knows no khudr/akhḍar or ḥumr/aḥmar parallel; al-Samīn replies that liff can behave like qufl and plural-of-plurals need not be regular. Al-Ālūsī finds the plural-of-plurals account unlikely and non-productive. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن قتيبة); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن قتيبة)-]
- 78:16/q06/p3 Its singular is lafīf, on the pattern sharīf/ashrāf. — Al-Qaffāl and al-Kisāʾī give the sharīf/ashrāf analogy; al-Samīn adds shahīd/ashhad and a poetic use of alifāf for interwoven groups. Al-Akhfash and Abū ʿUbayda are also cited for lafīf. Al-Ālūsī calls it a wrapped form on the faʿīl/afʿāl pattern. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش وأبو عبيدة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي); TAB-FULL+]
- 78:16/q06/p4 It has no singular of the same form. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares awzāʿ and akhyāf, words for scattered or varied groups. Abū Ḥayyān replies that liff is attested and accepted by most lexicographers. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 78:16/q06/p5 It is derived from multaffah by dropping added letters. — Al-Zamakhsharī proposes the clipped formation; al-Samīn objects that such deletion is chiefly discussed in diminutives and verbal nouns. Al-Kashf finds no comparable clipped plural, and al-Ālūsī calls it strained. Al-Bayḍāwī includes it among possible derivations. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)-]
- 78:16/q06/p6 Its singular is luff, with ḍamma. — Al-Kisāʾī allows the ḍamma form alongside liff; al-Mubarrad rejects that form and instead derives the word through lifa and lif. The Kasra form is separately disputed by Abū al-ʿAbbās as unattested. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش والكسائي); RAZI-FULL(الأخفش والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(المبرد)-]
- 78:16/q06/p7 Its singular is liffa, a densely grown garden. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya proposes liffa and describes its branches and leaves as intertwining in fresh, well-watered growth. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 78:16/q07 What kinds of growth does the rain produce in this sequence?  [* · interpretation]
- 78:16/q07/p1 Grain for food, grazing plants, and fruit-bearing or luxuriant gardens. — Al-Ṭabarī explains grain as harvested seed in husks, including barley and dates, and nabāt as grazing fodder such as grasses and crops. Al-Rāzī divides terrestrial growth exhaustively into husked grain, stemless or huskless grasses, and orchard trees, linking the first two to 20:54. Ibn Kathīr adds fresh edible plants and gardens with varied fruits, colors, flavors, and scents; Iṣlāḥī distinguishes food for people, grass for cattle, and orchards. The Study Quran says rain is the means and God the primary cause, citing living things made from water at 21:30, 24:25, and 25:54. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 78:16/q08 Why are grain, plants, and orchards mentioned in this order?  [* · rhetoric]
- 78:16/q08/p1 Their order runs from essential human food to animal fodder, then less essential fruit. — Al-Rāzī says grain comes first as basic food, plants next for animals, and orchards last because fruit is less necessary. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 78:16/q09 How does the passage picture the production of vegetation?  [* · rhetoric]
- 78:16/q09/p1 The sky and earth resemble a reproductive pair that produces vegetation through water. — Al-Biqāʿī likens sky to male, earth to female, water to semen and plants to offspring; springs and pools are implied in the scene. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 78:16/q10 What do these created signs establish?  [* · interpretation]
- 78:16/q10/p1 God's purposeful power to resurrect people and bring them to judgment. — Abū Isḥāq and Ibn al-Jawzī say created growth is evidence for resurrection. Muqātil calls each preceding sign more astonishing than resurrection. Al-Ālūsī argues from power, wisdom, purpose, waking after sleep, and vegetation emerging from earth, rebuking deniers. Asad uses 3:191 for a conscious Creator who made nothing purposeless and says moral standards known through instinct and revelation lead to judgment. Iṣlāḥī says divine providence, mercy, wisdom, and power show humanity cannot be unaccountable and its appointed reckoning will come, rewarding the God-fearing and punishing rebels. Kuranyolu cites the seven firm heavens, sun, clouds and life-sustaining growth as signs of power to create the afterlife, with 2:29 and 67:3. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BIQAI-FULL(مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 78:16/q11 How does this passage connect with its surah and al-Mursalāt?  [* · links]
- 78:16/q11/p1 It shares al-Mursalāt's argument from providence to the certainty of Judgment, using affirmative questions to confront deniers. — Iṣlāḥī pairs the surahs: their providential signs in heaven and earth lead to the necessary end of a purposeful world, with reward for the God-fearing and punishment for rebels. He says the affirmative-question form reproaches deniers verse by verse, while glad tidings for believers are understood against that warning. The signs show people are accountable at a fixed day. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
