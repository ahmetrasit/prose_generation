# 2:264: 44 questions

## 2:264/q01 Why address believers and repeat the warning against spoiled charity?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَا تُبْطِلُوا۟ صَدَقَٰتِكُم · rhetoric]
- 2:264/q01/p1 The direct address strengthens a renewed prohibition because reproach and harm are especially damaging in charity. — Abū Ḥayyān says earlier conditions did not suffice, the repetition stresses the wrong, and charity is singled out because reproach is especially ugly there. Al-Ālūsī notes a shift from third person to direct address and says either vice spoils the gift. Jishumī says the parable reinforces the immediately preceding warning after charity was discussed. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains iltifāt as a meaningful shift in address that renews attention, quoting Ibn Jinnī's description of it as Arabic bravery. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي عبد الجبار); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جني)]
- 2:264/q01/p2 The warning implies that ostentation, reproach, and harm are traits believers must avoid. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the vices are insinuated to be qualities of disbelievers; al-Biqāʿī calls the description a severe warning. Al-Ālūsī treats the closing denial of guidance as completing the warning. Jishumī explicitly glosses the address as those who believe God and His messenger. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 2:264/q01/p3 The warning precedes the positive incentive because preventing harm takes priority; its repeated image makes the point clearer. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the order of prohibition, incentive, and renewed warning. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:264/q02 What does it mean for reminders or harm to invalidate charity?  [لَا تُبْطِلُوا۟ صَدَقَٰتِكُم بِٱلْمَنِّ وَٱلْأَذَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether reward first accrues and can later be erased, or the gift lacked sincere intention and was never accepted; other accounts distinguish merit or future increase from prior reward.
- 2:264/q02/p1 The particular gift loses its reward through the donor's reproach or harm. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī say injury and reproach cost the reward; Ibn Zayd likens total loss to washed soil and cites 2:270, 2:272. Al-Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār stresses that a completed act cannot be undone, only future recompense. Muʿtazilites say a formerly meritorious act loses reward later. Ibn Kathīr says its reward cannot outweigh the sin. Al-Biqāʿī says the wrong can match or exceed reward. Al-Qurṭubī limits the loss to this gift, not unrelated deeds; Bursevī distinguishes lost reward from the completed act. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the analogy's show-off was invalid from the start, whereas the believers' gift is voided later, and the verse does not settle whether charity cancels other sins. Qatāda says God disliked this behavior for believers; al-Ṭabrisī says a deed outside its commanded form earns no reward. Al-Biqāʿī says the comparison presents the show-off at his worst, without belief. Al-Dāmaghānī calls ibṭāl making charity fruitless and compares 47:33. Jishumī argues the completed act cannot be undone, so its reward is cancelled; total loss, the parable, and the final clause support his doctrine of iḥbāṭ. [37 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القاضي,المعتزلة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي عبد الجبار); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; JISHUMI+; RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي,أصحابنا,المعتزلة)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:264/q02/p2 The gift never qualified as accepted charity because its eventual reproach reveals corrupt intention. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this account: reproach exposes a defective intention, but does not invalidate an unrelated sound gift; the majority say God does not accept a gift He knows will be reproached over, while another account has an angel marked not to record it. Al-Rāzī's school says the command forbids giving in a null form and argues that later events cannot retroactively erase earlier merit; he prefers outward dust that never adheres to stone. Abū Ḥayyān prefers that image because dust merely rests on stone. Bursevī grounds reward in divine gift and the transaction with God (73:20; 9:111). Al-Ālūsī says the reading departs from the apparent wording though the simile supports it. [17 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور العلماء,قيل); RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي,القاضي عبد الجبار وأصحابه); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:264/q02/p3 Reminders erase the gift's excellence or its effect in the recipient's heart, while a sincere giver's reward remains. — Al-Māwardī distinguishes the show-off's lost reward from the sincere donor's lost merit and also proposes loss of effect on the recipient. Al-Qurṭubī reports and prefers the excellence reading over another account of reward's timing. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:264/q02/p4 Only reward accruing after reproach stops; earlier reward remains and further multiplication ceases. — Al-Qurṭubī reports that otherwise charity continues to grow like a mountain. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 2:264/q02/p5 Theological cancellation of prior merit by a later sin is incoherent; good and blameworthy consequences can coexist. — Al-Rāzī's school argues that if merits conflict a later one has no priority, and that a past, present, or future merit cannot be erased by a later event without circular causation. He rejects Abū Hāshim's mutual balancing as contradictory and Abū ʿAlī al-Jubbāʾī's one-way erasure as contrary to 99:7 and justice. He argues selecting parts of reward for cancellation is arbitrary, and illustrates coexisting praise and blame with a servant who saves his master but exposes property to theft. He rejects a grave sin erasing decades of faith and compares faith after long unbelief. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي,أبو هاشم,أصحابنا,الجبائي,المعتزلة)]
- 2:264/q02/p6 A grave sin may outweigh and erase prior obedient deeds. — The Muʿtazilite claim in al-Rāzī says immense divine gifts make disobedience exceptionally grave; ʿAbd Allāh ibn Abī Zakariyyā reports hearing that one ostentatious deed can cancel earlier deeds. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن أبي زكريا); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)-]

## 2:264/q03 Who receives the reproach called mann, and what does it mean?  [بِٱلْمَنِّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the boast is addressed to the recipient or to God.
- 2:264/q03/p1 It is reminding the recipient of a favor, claiming credit, or boasting about a gift. — Al-Zajjāj defines the reminder and credit claim; al-Wāḥidī gives boasting. Al-Rāzī calls the recipient reading clearer, and Abū Ḥayyān prefers it as the apparent and majority meaning. Jishumī reports the petitioner as one possible person reminded. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(الباقون)+]
- 2:264/q03/p2 It is reminding God of one's charitable act, while the injury falls on the recipient. — Ibn ʿAbbās is repeatedly cited for this division; al-Rāzī says it is possible but the recipient reading is clearer. Al-Dāmaghānī says the donor reminds God of the gift while injuring the recipient; Jishumī also reports this direction of the reminder. [9 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(الباقون)-]
- 2:264/q03/p3 The giver may boast of the gift before other Muslims. — Jishumī reports Muslims as a third possible audience of mann, besides God and the beggar. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:264/q04 What is the injury called adhā?  [وَٱلْأَذَىٰ · meaning]
- 2:264/q04/p1 It is rebuking, reproaching, or hurting the recipient. — Muqātil says reproach injures the recipient; al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī specify rebuke. Ibn ʿAbbās directs the harm toward the petitioner even when directing mann toward God. Al-Dāmaghānī and Jishumī explicitly put the resulting harm on the gift's recipient. [13 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; THALABI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI]

## 2:264/q05 What practical and reported warnings concern gifts followed by reproach?  [لَا تُبْطِلُوا۟ صَدَقَٰتِكُم بِٱلْمَنِّ وَٱلْأَذَىٰ · law]
- 2:264/q05/p1 Withholding a gift is better than giving it and then injuring its recipient. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk gives this practical judgment. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 2:264/q05/p2 A reproached gift loses gratitude and reward; donors should avoid recounting favors. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Arabic labels: a reproached gift is a black hand, an unsolicited gift white, and a requested gift green; al-Naḥḥās also notes this idiom. A rhetorician distinguishes lost gratitude from self-admiration's lost reward; Ibn Sīrīn stops a man enumerating favors. A Prophetic report recites this verse after warning that reproach erases gratitude and reward, without grading. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن سيرين,بعض البلغاء,رسول الله); NAHHAS]
- 2:264/q05/p3 A charity that loses reward has different consequences for voluntary and obligatory giving. — Al-Qurṭubī says voluntary secret charity is as though never done, whereas spoiled obligatory alms bring a threat as if the duty were unperformed. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:264/q05/p4 Giving obligatory alms through others can help avoid praise, reminders, and repayment. — Mālik disliked giving obligatory alms to relatives for these risks, preferred strangers, and preferred another distributor under an unjust ruler. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)]
- 2:264/q05/p5 Reproach can also forfeit the credit of spending on a campaign. — Abū Hānī al-Khawlānī and ʿAmr ibn Ḥurayth relay a fighter who curses his commander and campaign after hardship and vows not to return; his spending yields no credit. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هانئ الخولاني); DURR-FULL(عمرو بن حريث)]
- 2:264/q05/p6 Prophetic warnings place the reproacher of gifts among grave offenders. — Reports name habitual wine drinkers and those who mistreat parents alongside reproachers as barred from Paradise or denied God's gaze; another adds belief in magic and soothsaying. Ibn ʿAbbās says he was troubled until finding this verse on reproach and the wine verse 5:90; one report says he took comfort in sinners' ability to repent. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن عباس,ابن عمر,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 2:264/q06 How does the comparison introduced by kā work grammatically?  [كَٱلَّذِى يُنفِقُ مَالَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the implied comparison is between kinds of invalidation or between the persons' states.
- 2:264/q06/p1 It modifies an omitted noun for the act or its invalidation: do not invalidate charity as the show-off does. — Al-Mahdawī supplies 'the invalidation of'; al-Rāzī says both deeds lack God's pleasure. Al-Nasafī makes the kāf an adjective of an omitted verbal noun. Al-Samīn adds Sībawayh's circumstantial analysis from the implicit verbal noun. Ibn ʿĀshūr alternatively supplies spending with an omitted possessive. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(المهدوي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN-]
- 2:264/q06/p2 It is circumstantial from the addressed donors: do not invalidate gifts while resembling the showy spender. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls the kāf accusative as a state of the subject in تبطلوا; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats it as an established circumstantial expression. Al-Ālūsī says this favors invalidity from the outset. Al-Suyūṭī argues the kāf is circumstantial from the plural subject without ellipsis and rejects al-Makkī's elided verbal-noun analysis. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN+]
- 2:264/q06/p3 The comparison likens harmful donors to the show-off without making every legal ruling identical. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative denotes a class rather than an individual; the comparison vilifies its example while leaving other evidence in force. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:264/q07 What does riyāʾ mean and why this form?  [رِئَآءَ ٱلنَّاسِ · meaning]
- 2:264/q07/p1 It is displaying one's good deed to people to win praise or reputation. — Al-Ṭabarī says the giver appears pious but wants to be called generous, good, and pious; Ibn ʿAbbās says he seeks neither reward nor protection from punishment. Al-Ḥarallī calls it heedlessness of God's sight. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī explain the reciprocal form by display and returned praise; al-Samīn derives the original form رئايا. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it intensive and frequent display; Study Quran links 107:6–7. Abū al-Baqāʾ says the initial ف connects this comparison with what precedes. Jishumī derives riyāʾ from sight and distinguishes ostentation in deeds from hypocrisy in professed faith; he explains the retained two hamzas by the lightness of the singular and an intervening alif constraint in the plural. He says the giver wants to be seen and called generous. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI]

## 2:264/q08 How is riyāʾ parsed?  [رِئَآءَ ٱلنَّاسِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative expresses purpose or state.
- 2:264/q08/p1 It expresses the purpose of spending. — Al-Ṭabrisī and others make it a purpose complement; Makkī also offers a qualifying omitted verbal noun. [5 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:264/q08/p2 It is a circumstantial verbal noun describing the spender or manner. — Al-Ṭabrisī treats it as a state of the spender, while Makkī glosses it 'ostentatiously'. [5 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:264/q09 How is riyāʾ read with a lightened hamza?  [رِئَآءَ · readings]
- 2:264/q09/p1 The first hamza is replaced by yāʾ after kasra, yielding رياء without a change of meaning. — Ṭalḥa ibn Muṣarrif and, by report, ʿĀṣim read it so; al-Ālūsī adds al-Khuzāʿī and al-Shamūnī. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn explain easing a heavy hamza after kasra. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); SAMIN-DURR(طلحة); ALUSI-FULL(الخزاعي، الشموني); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 2:264/q10 Who is the showy spender who lacks faith?  [كَٱلَّذِى يُنفِقُ مَالَهُۥ رِئَآءَ ٱلنَّاسِ وَلَا يُؤْمِنُ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ · referent]
turns on: Whether public display with professed faith requires hidden unbelief, or can describe open unbelief or even a believer.
- 2:264/q10/p1 He is a hypocrite, outwardly religious but inwardly seeking human praise without faith. — Al-Ṭabarī argues a declared unbeliever is known to act for Satan, while ostentation requires outward piety and hidden desire for praise; al-Thaʿlabī, Makkī as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī's majority follow this rationale. Jishumī reports the argument that an open unbeliever does not pretend to faith, making the spender a hypocrite. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 2:264/q10/p2 He may be an openly unbelieving giver who seeks public praise. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this as an option; Abū Ḥayyān reports unnamed exegetes; Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts Muslims seeking reward but later harming recipients with unbelievers who seek only praise. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مفسرون غير مسمين); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:264/q10/p3 The class may include a believer or an unbeliever acting ostentatiously. — Al-Ālūsī reports this minority view before stating most call the figure a hypocrite. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:264/q10/p4 Every ostentatious spender is classed as an unbeliever and hypocrite. — Jishumī reports this categorical alternative to identifying the particular figure as a hypocrite. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:264/q11 What does the show-off's lack of belief mean?  [وَلَا يُؤْمِنُ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the denial is general creed or unbelief expressed specifically in the act of spending.
- 2:264/q11/p1 He denies divine oneness or lordship, resurrection, and recompense. — Al-Ṭabarī says this marks a hypocrite; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr glosses unbelief in God as not affirming His oneness. Jishumī specifies denial of God's oneness and attributes, resurrection, and requital. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI]
- 2:264/q11/p2 He fails to believe in God's call to spend and the promised reward in this deed, without necessarily disbelieving altogether. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī grounds this in the coordinated present-tense clauses and says ostentation entails unbelief in the relevant promised recompense. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 2:264/q12 Whose likeness is introduced by 'his likeness'?  [فَمَثَلُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Which antecedent the singular pronoun selects.
- 2:264/q12/p1 The pronoun returns to the nearest antecedent, the ostentatious spender. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn prefer proximity; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the analogy depicts the spender even when its pronoun can also describe the analogous figure. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:264/q12/p2 It returns to the donor who reproaches and harms, making two successive comparisons. — This is reported as an alternative; al-Samīn notes that it requires a shift in person and number. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:264/q13 What does ṣafwān denote?  [صَفْوَانٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is explained by smoothness and size or a particular softness/clarity.
- 2:264/q13/p1 It is a smooth stone or large smooth rock. — Al-Ṭabarī transmits ṣafā/ṣafāh and cites a line with ṣafī; al-Thaʿlabī cites gravel planted on a smooth expanse. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī report al-Aṣmaʿī's equivalent forms ṣafwā, ṣafwān, and ṣafā. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Imruʾ al-Qays's slipping horse image and Abū Dhuʾayb's blows on stone at al-Mushaqqar. Ibn ʿAbbās cites Aws ibn Ḥajar's poetry. Al-Qummī's report calls it a large rock. Jishumī calls it smooth stone. Al-Suyūṭī gives Aws ibn Ḥajar's slippery, oiled stone and describes a bare, solid rock. Ibn ʿAbbās cites the same poem according to Bint al-Shāṭiʾ; she adds dictionary descriptions of a large solid stone on which nothing grows. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes that ṣafwān occurs only once in the Qurʾān and relates its root to choosing, purified honey, and al-Ṣafā. [32 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع,السدي,الشاعر,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو ذؤيب,امرؤ القيس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام); JISHUMI; ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس)]
- 2:264/q13/p2 It may denote a softer stone, or a smooth stone named for its clarity. — Al-Māwardī reports the alternative from Abān ibn Taghlib. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāghib's derivation from purity and absence of admixture; ṣafwāna is its unit, and a related use describes a clear, cold, sunny day. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبان بن تغلب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]

## 2:264/q14 Is ṣafwān singular, collective, or plural?  [صَفْوَانٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether its number is lexical singular, collective with ṣafwāna, or plural of ṣafā; the singular pronoun عليه and disputed plural patterns serve as evidence.
- 2:264/q14/p1 It is a singular noun, with reported plurals ṣafwān, ṣafī, or ṣafiyy. — Al-Kisāʾī supplies plural variants; al-Naḥḥās favors singular because عليه uses a singular pronoun and rejects al-Kisāʾī's ṣafī plural analysis. Al-Mubarrad says ṣafī is instead a plural of ṣafā; al-Samīn rejects al-Kisāʾī's singular claim. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports a lexicographical analysis taking ṣafwān as singular in relation to ṣafī. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش,الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي,النحاس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 2:264/q14/p2 It is a collective noun whose unit is ṣafwāna. — Al-Akhfash and al-Thaʿlabī compare ṣafwāna/ṣafwān with tamra/tamr and nakhla/nakhl; Abū al-Baqāʾ favors the collective to explain عليه. Jishumī reports ṣafwān as plural of ṣafwāna; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports the unit analysis through al-Rāghib and lexicographers' disagreement. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)+]
- 2:264/q14/p3 It is a plural of ṣafā; ṣafī is likewise a plural of ṣafā. — Al-Mubarrad rejects al-Kisāʾī's derivations; al-Qurṭubī cites the weak crow's-day analogy for karawān and compares ṣafī/ṣafā to ʿaṣā. Abū al-Baqāʾ calls the fiʿlān plural pattern rare; al-Samīn prefers ṣafwān as plural of ṣafā. Jishumī reports Abū al-ʿAbbās's rejection of al-Kisāʾī's ṣafī plural: ṣafī is plural of ṣafā; he also mentions a jarab/jarbān pattern. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:264/q14/p4 Either singular or collective/plural analysis can describe the rock here. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Ālūsī allow multiple analyses; al-Naḥḥās allows singular or plural while preferring singular. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:264/q15 What variant vocalization of ṣafwān is reported?  [صَفْوَانٍ · readings]

## 2:264/q16 What does turāb mean and what related forms are noted?  [تُرَابٌۭ · meaning]
- 2:264/q16/p1 It is soil or dust, with related forms and associations of poverty and wealth. — Al-Ṭabrisī connects ذا متربة (90:16) to need and notes ترب can mean a peer; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn give توراب, ترب 'become poor,' and أترب 'become rich,' explained by removal of dust or abundance like earth. - 2:264/q16/p2 The related word tarāʾib names chest bones. — Al-Ṭabrisī adds this related lexical form. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 2:264/q17 How is 'upon it was dust' constructed?  [عَلَيْهِ تُرَابٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the whole dust clause or only the prepositional phrase qualifies the stone.
- 2:264/q17/p1 The phrase is an adjectival clause describing the stone. — Al-Ṭabrisī says the dust clause qualifies the smooth stone; al-Samīn lists the full clause as an option. [2 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:264/q17/p2 عليه alone modifies the stone, with dust as the subject of an understood 'settled upon it'. — Al-Samīn prefers this parsing and compares 2:261; Abū Ḥayyān supplies the underlying verb and coordinates فأصابه with it. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 2:264/q18 What is wābil?  [وَابِلٌۭ · meaning]
- 2:264/q18/p1 It is heavy, abundant, forceful rain, often with large drops. — Al-Ṭabarī cites Imruʾ al-Qays for driving rain and gives conjugations; al-Māwardī and al-Kashshāf specify large drops. Al-Naḍr lists stages from رش/طش through drizzle and drops to وابل and جودة; al-Samīn gives بلت/أوبل and says the adjective can stand alone. ʿIkrima glosses rain while al-Rabīʿ and Qatāda specify heavy rain. Al-Qummī also distinguishes nighttime dew ṭall. Jishumī derives wabīl from intense falling rain. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع,السدي,الضحاك,امرؤ القيس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيد); QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النضر); SAMIN-DURR(النضر بن شميل); DURR-FULL(عكرمة,قتادة); TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام); JISHUMI]
- 2:264/q18/p2 Its root also conveys severity or a bad consequence. — Al-Qurṭubī adduces severe taking at 73:16 and blows or punishment; al-Samīn adds وبيل for harshness, وبيلة for a firewood bundle and thick objects. Jishumī also cites the usage of wābil for severe taking. [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; JISHUMI]

## 2:264/q19 What is the antecedent of 'it' in 'it struck it,' and how is the verb formed?  [فَأَصَابَهُۥ · grammar]
- 2:264/q19/p1 The rain strikes either the stone or its dust; 'then left it' refers to the stone. — Both grammarians coordinate فأصابه with the understood settling verb; both restrict the later pronoun to the stone. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:264/q19/p2 The verb aṣāba derives from ṣāba/yaṣūbu, its alif from wāw. — Al-Samīn gives the morphological derivation. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:264/q20 What does ṣald mean?  [صَلْدًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the relevant quality is exposed hard barrenness or the reported softness of the stone.
- 2:264/q20/p1 It means bare, clean, hard, smooth, barren, with nothing remaining or growing on the stone. — Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others say no soil remains; Ibn Abī Ḥātim adds dry, fruitless and disgraced. Al-Ṭabarī cites Ruʾba's bare forehead and Taʾabbaṭa Sharran's hard isolated rock; al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya repeat the bald-head use. Al-Naqqāsh calls it Hudhayl usage. Al-Ṭabrisī extends it to sterile ground, miser, unproductive flint and resistance to flow; al-Wāḥidī gives a fire-starter that fails to spark; al-Ṭabarī and ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā mention a slow-boiling pot. Ibn ʿAbbās cites Abū Ṭālib's poetry. Jishumī compares ṣald with ṣalb for hard infertile soil. Al-Suyūṭī cites Abū Ṭālib for smoothness and Hudhayl usage for purity. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ stresses hardness and barrenness beyond touch alone, notes its sole Qurʾānic occurrence, extends the sense to stinginess, flint, and withheld milk, cites al-Ṭabarī and a poem, and rejects reducing the parable to a smooth stone left smooth. [42 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,السدي,الضحاك,تأبط شرا,رؤبة,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش,رؤبة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة,ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيد); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي,النقاش); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(النقاش,علي بن عيسى); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; JISHUMI; ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الطبري)]
- 2:264/q20/p2 It can specifically describe soft stone. — Al-Samīn reports Abān ibn Taghlib's gloss. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبان بن تغلب)]

## 2:264/q21 What grammatical role does ṣaldan have?  [فَتَرَكَهُۥ صَلْدًۭا · grammar]
- 2:264/q21/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial state of the stone after the downpour. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this parsing. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 2:264/q22 What does the rock, soil, and rain parable portray?  [فَمَثَلُهُۥ كَمَثَلِ صَفْوَانٍ عَلَيْهِ تُرَابٌۭ فَأَصَابَهُۥ وَابِلٌۭ فَتَرَكَهُۥ صَلْدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the act was empty from the outset and exposed at judgment, or earned reward later erased by reproach; sources also differ over one composite likeness and separate mapped elements.
- 2:264/q22/p1 Apparent good works conceal an unreceptive giver; judgment exposes their lack of reward, as rain exposes bare rock. — Al-Rāzī maps the giver to stone, visible work to dust, and judgment to rain; al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, and others say judgment reveals no works for God. Abū Ḥayyān says dust visibly rests without taking root, so the act was never truly charity. Al-Ḥarallī says a thin soil layer deceives the inexperienced; al-Biqāʿī contrasts the stone's sterility with a fertile garden. Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses concealed rock beneath apparently fertile soil and the farmer's frustrated hope. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says normal life-giving rain and soil cannot bear fruit on an insincere heart. Al-Rāzī says the taunting donor is first compared with the show-off unbeliever, then maps stone to unbeliever, soil to apparent donation, and downpour to unbelief or taunting. Jishumī maps the hypocrite to stone, outward works to soil, and rain to unbelief removing reward. Al-Zarkashī says familiar sensory parables make abstract meanings graspable; he quotes al-Zamakhsharī on representation bringing them near to observation. Ḥāzim says such examples prepare the soul and warn against avoidable harm. [50 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع,السدي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الربيع,السدي,قتادة,مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي عبد الجبار وأصحابه); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI; BURHAN(الزمخشري,حازم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:264/q22/p2 The rain depicts later reproach and harm washing away reward already earned by charity. — The Muʿtazilites and Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār equate the rain with later reproach and soil with an initially rewarded gift; Abū Ḥayyān says the analogy can equally show initial invalidity. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي عبد الجبار,القاضي عبد الجبار وأصحابه); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); RAZI-FULL-]
- 2:264/q22/p3 It is a composite image of promising growth that fails; alternatively stone, dust, and rain can each be mapped separately. — Al-Ālūsī reports a view that the composite is correct but records stone as useless donor, dust as apparent hope, and rain as ostentation. Ibn ʿĀshūr favors rock concealed under soil, distinct from simply blown dust at 14:18. Al-Jurjānī argues that a performed or visible example affects the soul beyond merely confirming a report: Abraham sought visual reassurance at 2:260, Abū Tammām's verse describes sight renewing affection, and concrete demonstrations surpass bare assertion. He rejects an account limited to dispelling doubt. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Qurʾān calls attention to parables that prompt reflection; Arabs valued simile, metaphor, complete likeness, and objection-preventing detail. He cites other Qurʾānic images, including the lamp niche, as comparable rhetorical craft. [11 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; JURJANI-ASRAR(أبو التمام); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:264/q22/p4 The analogy can imply seed stored on sterile stone: unlike fertile ground, it yields no harvest when needed. — Al-Qaffāl contrasts sincere deeds growing as seed in a garden with seed left on bare stone; Abū Ḥayyān relates this to 2:265. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال)]
- 2:264/q22/p5 Only the explicitly stated failure matters; any cleansing benefit of rain does not defeat the analogy. — Al-Rāzī answers the objection that rain cleans rock; he rejects al-Qāḍī's recourse to benefits of dust and relies on the stated likeness. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 2:264/q23 To whom does the plural 'they cannot' refer?  [لَّا يَقْدِرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural resumes the generic show-off, the addressed believers, an implied planter, or all analogous donors.
- 2:264/q23/p1 It refers by meaning to the class of showy spenders despite the earlier singular relative. — Al-Kashshāf says الذي and من alternate; al-Bayḍāwī cites poetry shifting from singular الذي to plural هم; al-Samīn cites 2:17 and allows an independent or circumstantial clause. Ibn ʿAṭiyya objects to reversing Arabic's normal form-then-meaning order; al-Samīn disputes the rule. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:264/q23/p2 It returns to the believers addressed earlier, shifting from direct address to third person. — Al-Qaffāl cites a similar shift at 10:22; al-Samīn calls the return distant. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:264/q23/p3 It refers to any would-be planter unable to retrieve seed from the washed-off soil. — Al-Qaffāl uses the implied seed to show the donor likewise cannot benefit; al-Biqāʿī says elliptical correspondence joins the represented spender and the planter. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مفسرون غير مسمين); BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:264/q23/p4 It encompasses show-offs, disbelievers, and reproachful donors together. — Al-Qurṭubī says all fail to benefit when reward is needed, since the spending was for other than God. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:264/q24 How does the 'they cannot' clause attach syntactically?  [لَّا يَقْدِرُونَ عَلَىٰ شَىْءٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is syntactically independent or a circumstantial state despite the intervening likeness.
- 2:264/q24/p1 It may be independent, explanatory, or a summary of the parable. — Al-Ālūsī says it can explain the comparison or answer an implied question; Ibn ʿĀshūr lists explanatory resumption and summary among deliberately flexible attachments. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:264/q24/p2 It may be circumstantial from the spender or the figurative rock. — Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn permit a state clause; Abū al-Baqāʾ objects that فمثله intervenes, while al-Samīn says the intervening comparison reinforces the point. Al-Ālūsī calls al-Samīn's analysis weak; Ibn ʿĀshūr also offers a state with omitted relative pronoun. [4 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السمين)-]

## 2:264/q25 What do the spenders fail to obtain?  [لَّا يَقْدِرُونَ عَلَىٰ شَىْءٍۢ مِّمَّا كَسَبُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether incapacity means no recoverable reward or failure to use earnings rightly.
- 2:264/q25/p1 They cannot obtain reward or benefit from their spending at judgment. — Muqātil compares windblown ashes at 14:18; al-Kashshāf and Bursevī cite scattered dust at 25:23. Ibn Abī Ḥātim says the money yields neither reward nor help on Judgment Day; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that donors cannot retrieve the gift itself. Ibn Qutayba says the show-offs cannot draw on erased earnings when needed. Jishumī says ostentation annuls the reward and the total removal rules out a merely reduced return. Al-Jurjānī states as a general semantic principle that only a capable agent can truly cause an act; this is supplied as background to the incapacity wording. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(الربيع,مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مفسرون غير مسمين); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; JISHUMI; JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 2:264/q25/p2 They cannot direct their earnings to their proper end, having spent fruitlessly for the afterlife. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the subsequent denial of guidance to disbelievers suggests this possible sense. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:264/q26 Why is spending called what they 'earned'?  [مِّمَّا كَسَبُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 2:264/q26/p1 They spent hoping to acquire recompense, so their spending is called earnings. — Al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, and Abū Ḥayyān explain the intended return; al-Samīn simply notes charity is termed acquisition. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:264/q27 What does God not guiding disbelievers mean here?  [وَٱللَّهُ لَا يَهْدِى ٱلْقَوْمَ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · theology]
turns on: Whether guidance means right conduct now, divine recompense, Paradise and special success, or the fate of those who die unbelieving.
- 2:264/q27/p1 He does not guide persistent unbelievers to right spending or good deeds while they prefer falsehood. — Al-Ṭabarī says their preference for falsehood prevents right spending; al-Nasafī limits it to their persistence in disbelief. Abū Ḥayyān says it may mean no guidance in their deeds while unbelieving and that this favors a disbeliever in the parable. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:264/q27/p2 He does not make them guided because of their disbelief. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq attribute the lack of guidance to disbelief. Jishumī reports al-Aṣamm's gloss that God does not make them rightly guided. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI(الأصم)]
- 2:264/q27/p3 He does not make guidance the reward of disbelief. — The alternative appears alongside the causative account in al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 2:264/q27/p4 He does not guide unbelievers into Paradise by their works or give them believers' special care. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports Paradise and extra care/success readings. Al-Rāzī reports a Muʿtazilite account of being led away from reward and Paradise by one's own choices, with removal of faith as another gloss. Jishumī reports Abū ʿAlī that God grants them no reward admitting them to Paradise and Abū Muslim that He does not reward them. [4 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); JISHUMI(أبو علي,أبو مسلم)]
- 2:264/q27/p5 The saying generalizes why the show-off's act belongs to unbelief. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the closing rule with his reading of unbelief in the promised reward. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 2:264/q27/p6 It refers to those who die unbelieving. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya records this as one possible referent. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 2:264/q27/p7 He does not accept their works as He accepts the guided believers' works. — Jishumī reports this separate explanation of the closing denial of guidance. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:264/q28 How does this verse relate to nearby teaching and other Qur'anic images?  [* · links]
- 2:264/q28/p1 It reverses the fertile seed and garden images of sincere spending in the surrounding passage. — Ibn ʿĀshūr sets the fruitless rock against the seven-hundred-grain seed at 2:261; Study Quran contrasts ruined charity with giving for God's pleasure and secure faith. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 2:264/q28/p2 It echoes images of unbelieving deeds as ashes, a mirage, or scattered dust. — Sources cite 14:18, 24:39, and 25:23; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes a concealed rock exposed by rain from merely swept-away soil in 14:18. Al-Zarkashī notices the reversed order of 'anything' and 'what they earned' between this verse and 14:18. [7 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 2:264/q28/p3 The warning shifts from spending in God's path to needy persons, where personal contact makes injury likelier. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links this to the prior discussion at 2:262. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:264/q28/p4 It anticipates the orchard owner's loss and shattered hopes. — Al-Biqāʿī likens abusive donors' loss to the orchard owner's family loss; Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the barren rock to the person turning his hands over spending at 18:42. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:264/q28/p5 The parable centers acceptance on sincere intention. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says seeking God's face is necessary for acceptance and cites the widely transmitted report that deeds follow intentions. [2 notes: TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 2:264/q29 What is reported about the judgment of people who acted for public praise?  [رِئَآءَ ٱلنَّاسِ · reports]
- 2:264/q29/p1 They are sent to seek reward from the people whose praise they pursued. — ʿIkrima transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's caller saying God accepts no deed mixed with worldly aims. Maḥmūd ibn Labīd and Bursevī transmit the Prophet's identification of ostentation as lesser shirk and this judgment warning. [3 notes: THALABI(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمود بن لبيد); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 2:264/q29/p2 A praised donor, warrior, and scholar or reciter receive no divine reward for their reputations. — The Abū Hurayra narrative is introduced through ʿAbd Allāh al-Madanī's report of repeated tears: the donor was called generous, the warrior brave, and the scholar learned as intended. Bursevī's hadith names reciter, martyr, and donor among the first judged and condemned to Hell. [5 notes: THALABI(عبد الله المدني); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:264/q29/p3 Early givers hid their charity to avoid public notice. — Bursevī reports donors seeking blind recipients, fastening money to a sleeping poor person's clothing, or leaving it to be found. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 2:264/q30 What does the passage teach in an inward reading?  [* · inward]
- 2:264/q30/p1 Seeking any aim besides the Real corrupts devotion, and reminding a poor person reveals misplaced intention. — Al-Sarrī says adorning oneself with deeds turns good into sin, still worse when claiming their value. Bursevī applies 10:32 to ulterior aims; a giver seeking God feels indebted to the poor as a way to Him. He interprets the report that rich would perish without poor and reverses the upper-hand saying in the recipient's favor; inward harm is turning toward anything other than the Real. [6 notes: SULAMI(السري); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 2:264/q30/p2 The mature servant seeks God himself without demanding wages or public fame. — An unnamed scholar compares ostentatious worship to a full sack of valueless stones. Rūmī's verses seek pure guidance and say the lover's joy, grief, service, and wage belong to the beloved; Saʿdī urges hidden humility; Ḥāfiẓ urges trust in the master's generosity. Bursevī says divine love cuts rival attachment to wealth, children, and life and closes with prayer for hope in God alone. [8 notes: BURSEVI(السعدي,بعض العلماء,جلال الدين الرومي)]

## 2:264/q31 How is the broader spending passage organized?  [* · links]
- 2:264/q31/p1 It likely forms one revealed unit ordered around purpose, manner, quality, recipients, and reward. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces giving for God, freedom from reproach, good wealth, needy people constrained in God's path, and multiplied recompense. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Qurʾān's eloquent details are intended and calibrated to challengers; its wording can carry conventional, rhetorical, and contextually implicit meanings, including ellipses. [5 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:264/q31/p2 The explanation here draws on earlier exegetes whose wording was not always filed under invalidating charity. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the provenance and organization of the linguistic and interpretive material. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 2:264/q32 Whom does the opening address as believers include?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address follows outward profession or actual belief.
- 2:264/q32/p1 It addresses hypocrites by their outward claim to belief. — Jishumī reports this view of the addressees. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 2:264/q32/p2 It addresses actual believers. — Jishumī calls this the apparent reading. [2 notes: JISHUMI; JISHUMI+]

## 2:264/q33 Why is the Day of Judgment called the Last Day?  [وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ · meaning]
- 2:264/q33/p1 It follows life in this world. — Jishumī explains the name by its succession to worldly life. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:264/q34 What does ibṭāl mean in the prohibition, and what related senses are distinguished?  [لَا تُبْطِلُوا۟ · meaning]
- 2:264/q34/p1 It means nullifying a deed or making its reward fruitless. — Al-Dāmaghānī gives this sense and cites 47:33; Ibn al-Jawzī lists the Qurʾānic sense of bringing works to nothing and the underlying sense of perishing or collapse. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 2:264/q34/p2 The wider word bāṭil can also mean falsehood or injustice. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports falsehood and lies in 29:48, 40:78, 41:42, 45:27, and unjust property taking and bribery in 2:188 and 4:29. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 2:264/q35 How do jurists distinguish an invalid act from a corrupt one?  [لَا تُبْطِلُوا۟ · law]
- 2:264/q35/p1 A void act never takes effect; a corrupt act exists with a missing condition and may have legal effects. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports the distinction and Ḥanafī examples: corrupt sale can transfer ownership through possession while a child's or insane person's void sale cannot; intercourse after corrupt sale has no fixed penalty and the child is free. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أصحاب أبي حنيفة,بعض العلماء)]
- 2:264/q35/p2 Invalidation need not itself be forbidden, though deliberate ruin of worship is. — Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts loss of ritual purity with deliberately ruining prayer, fasting, or pilgrimage. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 2:264/q36 What does mathal mean when the rock image is introduced?  [فَمَثَلُهُۥ كَمَثَلِ · meaning]
- 2:264/q36/p1 It denotes an unusual or noteworthy state, quality, or story set forth descriptively. — Al-Zarkashī explains mathal this way and names this rock image as a descriptive example. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 2:264/q37 Why is 'anything' placed before 'what they earned' here, unlike 14:18?  [شَىْءٍۢ مِّمَّا كَسَبُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 2:264/q37/p1 The order suits a donor who gives rather than an earner, while 14:18 foregrounds workers' earnings. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī and both parallel accounts explain the contrast by the local contexts; he also lists other contextual Quranic order reversals. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 2:264/q38 What general Arabic grammar is recorded alongside analysis of this verse?  [* · grammar]
- 2:264/q38/p1 Land names vary in gender and declension, while light native and foreign names behave differently. — Sībawayh gives light feminine names, foreign names, and regional alternatives for Wāsiṭ, Dābiq, Minā, Hajar, Ḥajar al-Yamāma, ʿUmān, al-Zāb, Irāb, Falaj, Qubāʾ, Ḥirāʾ, and Aḍākh; al-Khalīl discusses derived names and a fixed feminine dialect. Poetry attests several uses. [13 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 2:264/q38/p2 Derived verb patterns can express finding a quality, seeking something, assuming a quality, or reinforcing another form. — Sībawayh explains istifʿāl for judgment and request, tafaʿʿul for self-assumed quality, and overlapping tafaʿʿala and istafʿala pairs. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 2:264/q38/p3 Nisba and inflection can restore or reshape an underlying short noun stem. — Sībawayh examines ذيت, ذية, بنت, أخت, ابن, كلتا, فم, and ذو مال; he uses plurals, duals, poetry from al-Farazdaq, and derived nisba forms to trace deleted or substituted radicals. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 2:264/q38/p4 The diminutive of a plural follows its small-number pattern or a diminutive singular plus a plural suffix. — Sībawayh lists four paucity patterns, al-Khalīl's treatment of dūr, and alternatives for forms such as fityān, fuqarāʾ, marābid, and mafātīḥ. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 2:264/q38/p5 Demonstratives and other vague forms change inflection when made proper names. — Sībawayh discusses ذا, ذي, تا, ألاء, الذي, التي, اللائي, اللاتي, ذين, ذو مال, and أمس as names; he attributes alternatives to Yūnus, al-Khalīl, and ʿĪsā. [9 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,يونس والخليل)]
- 2:264/q38/p6 Adjective and expanded noun patterns govern their broken plurals. — Sībawayh gives fiʿāl and afʿāl for faʿl adjectives, feminine -āt, collective singulars, basic four-letter mafāʿil, and related expanded triliteral patterns. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 2:264/q38/p7 Arabic substitution changes consonants, vowels, and weak letters according to their environment. — Sībawayh surveys alif for wāw or yāʾ, hāʾ for feminine tāʾ and rare alternatives, tāʾ for initial wāw or yāʾ, and wāw/yāʾ interchange; al-Khalīl relates short vowels to long vowels. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 2:264/q38/p8 Some expressions accept diminutives of nearness; pronouns, particles, and relative time names often do not. — Sībawayh includes mithayl for an insignificant analogue; al-Khalīl discusses an irregular exclamation, while other notes delimit diminutives of pronouns, interrogatives, time words, relative labels, and verbal nouns. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 2:264/q38/p9 Idhan governs a future reply only in appropriate initial or connected constructions. — Sībawayh distinguishes initial response, oath interruption, embedded and nominal clauses, current from future reference, and connected subjunctive from fresh indicative; he cites Yūnus, ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar, al-Khalīl, and poetry. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,عيسى بن عمر,يونس)]
- 2:264/q38/p10 Independent pronouns occur where verbal subject markers or clitics cannot carry the construction. — Sībawayh treats أنت, هو, أنا, and demonstrative combinations; al-Khalīl, Abū al-Khaṭṭāb, and Yūnus explain ها أنا ذا, أنا هذا, and هذا أنت. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو الخطاب,الخليل,يونس)]
- 2:264/q38/p11 Repeated vocatives retain their special initial form, with poetic and analogical evidence. — Sībawayh cites Jarīr's يا تيم تيم عدى and analyses from al-Khalīl and Yūnus; he treats name fusion, optional final h, tanwīn loss, and comparable adjective patterns. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,الخليل ويونس)]

## 2:264/q39 What wider rules of Qurʾānic recitation are cited with this verse's reading material?  [رِئَآءَ صَفْوَانٍ · readings]
- 2:264/q39/p1 Recitation follows transmitted practice from earlier readers. — Ibn al-Munkadir, al-Shaʿbī, ʿUrwa, and other teachers call recitation a transmitted sunnah; attributions also reach the two ʿUmars. Ibn Mujāhid corrects the route through al-Zuhrī in one report. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أشياخهم,عامر الشعبي,عروة بن الزبير,عمر بن الخطاب، عمر بن عبد العزيز,محمد بن المنكدر)]
- 2:264/q39/p2 Sound transmission, written form, analogy, and explicit documentation govern variant readings. — Ibn al-Jazarī rejects the unsound report of codex errors, affirms legitimate regional variants, surveys hamza spelling and stopping by script, records al-Hudhalī and al-Dānī, and gives explicit transmission priority over silence in manuals. [6 notes: NASHR(أبو القاسم الهذلي,محمد بن واصل)]
- 2:264/q39/p3 Underlying alifs and their inclination are identified by morphology and inherited pronunciation. — Ibn al-Jazarī tests noun alifs by the dual and verb alifs by conjugation, including extended wāwī verbs, and lists faʿlā forms read with imāla. Al-Suyūṭī reports imāla studies, al-Dānī's dialect distribution, Ibrāhīm's alif/yāʾ equivalence, and Kūfan appeal to script. [7 notes: NASHR; ITQAN(إبراهيم,الداني,الكوفيون)]

## 2:264/q40 What broader principles of wording and rhetorical arrangement are cited beside the verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 2:264/q40/p1 Question form, predication, circumstance, and negation depend on syntax and position. — Al-Jurjānī explains false concession in 6:143, questions implying an event, verb-first present questions, omitted subjects, second predicates, a satire's occasion, and how circumstantial addition or the placement of kull changes a negative claim; poetry and the Dhū al-Yadayn report illustrate. [12 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 2:264/q40/p2 Nazm coordinates grammatical meanings, and rhetorical figures gain force from their syntactic relations. — Al-Jurjānī distinguishes reciting words from composing them, treats meaning as prior to word order, locates inimitability in syntactic arrangement, and uses 19:4 to show metaphor depends on predication; he rejects bare sound as the source of eloquence. [6 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 2:264/q40/p3 Qurʾānic eloquence uses paronomasia and antithesis as well as context-specific images. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the paired sounds at 6:26 and opposed verbs at 22:4 as examples of these arts. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:264/q41 What transmission and scholarship reports accompany the source material on this verse?  [* · reports]
- 2:264/q41/p1 Early Qurʾān collectors, readers, and teachers are identified through competing reports. — Ibn Kathīr examines Abū Zayd's identity, Anas's Aws and Khazraj report, al-Zuhrī's death notice, Abū Bakr's leading recitation, ʿUthmān's one-rakʿa reading, ʿAlī's compilation, Ibn Masʿūd's knowledge, Sālim, Mujāhid's study with Ibn ʿAbbās, and ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr's nightly recitation. [15 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو عمر بن عبد البر,أنس,ابن نمير,الزهري,عبد الله بن عمرو,عبد الله بن مسعود,مجاهد)]
- 2:264/q41/p2 Ibn ʿAbbās's lexical question collection circulated in several forms and later scholarly accounts. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ recounts Nāfiʿ and Najda's demand for Arabic evidence, al-Suyūṭī's count of 190 questions with omissions, selections by Ibn al-Anbārī and al-Ṭabarānī, and her inability to locate a marked old copy. [4 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(السيوطي,نافع بن الأزرق)]

## 2:264/q42 What theology of divine speech occurs in the collected source material?  [* · theology]
- 2:264/q42/p1 Divine speech is eternal, with inner and manifested levels distinguished. — Al-Ālūsī cites an angelic-hearing report, locates inner speech beyond material modality, describes manifested speech as modal, presents al-Ashʿarī's act and thing-spoken distinction, and explains unsounded mental letters and their relation to audible words, citing ʿUmar and Imām al-Ḥaramayn. [9 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الأشعري)]

## 2:264/q43 What wider arguments about the Qurʾān's lasting eloquence occur in these sources?  [* · rhetoric]
- 2:264/q43/p1 The Qurʾān's linguistic challenge and enduring rational miracle establish its distinctive eloquence. — Al-Suyūṭī surveys inimitability treatises and explanations contrasting lasting Qurʾānic evidence with earlier sensory miracles; Ibn Ḥajar combines the explanations. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Khālid ibn Ṣafwān, the first Arabs' inability to answer the challenge, and al-Ṭabarī on its continuing miracle. [9 notes: ITQAN(ابن حجر); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الطبري,خالد بن صفوان)]

## 2:264/q44 How do modern writers frame the study and renewal of Qurʾānic interpretation?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:264/q44/p1 Reform arose in religious schools but often remained within inherited methods; some writers sought broader critical tools. — Rifāʿī describes al-Azhar, Zaytuna, and Najaf reformers, their differing and sometimes controversial questions, critique of inherited authority, contemporary contextual reading, limits of traditional methods, and Amīn al-Khūlī's life, teaching, academy, journal, and writings. [16 notes: RIFAI-KHULI]
- 2:264/q44/p2 Sustained fresh ijtihād should answer present needs while maintaining living religious sources. — Y. Khūlī criticizes past-ism and extremism for denying contemporary reality, argues language keeps the community connected to sacred texts, and calls for renewed Qurʾān and Sunna research. [5 notes: YKHULI-TAJDID]
