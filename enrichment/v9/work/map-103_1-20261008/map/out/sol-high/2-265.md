# 2:265: 36 questions

## 2:265/q01 Who are the spenders and what do they seek?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَٰلَهُمُ ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 2:265/q01/p1 They give sincerely in obedience to God to obtain His approval and reward, without display, reproach, or injury. — Muqatil and al-Tabari exclude reproach and harm; al-Tabari and al-Mawardi include aid to fighters and obedient Muslims. Al-Hasan excludes reputation. Al-Biqa'i excludes worldly motives and reads marḍāt as lasting satisfaction. Tabataba'i explains God's face as His satisfaction with obedience. Ibn Kathir compares the Ramadan report about faith and seeking reward. Jishumi says wealth is spent in works of goodness and worship should seek God's pleasure and nearness to Him. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(الحسن,مقاتل بن حيان); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; JISHUMI+]

## 2:265/q02 How are ابتغاء and تثبيتا construed grammatically?  [ٱبْتِغَآءَ وَتَثْبِيتًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether coordinated تثبيتا can name a purpose of giving, and whether its implied object is God's reward.
- 2:265/q02/p1 Both are circumstantial verbal nouns describing the spenders' state. — Ibn Atiyya argues coordination makes this the sounder parse and rejects making strengthening the purpose of spending; Abu Hayyan reports and disputes Makki's purpose analysis. Ibn Ashur adopts the circumstantial parse. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)+]
- 2:265/q02/p2 Both are accusatives of purpose, with تثبيتا coordinated to ابتغاء. — Al-Nahhas, al-Tabrisi and Makki offer the parse; Abu Hayyan allows it if the object of strengthening is God's reward, since securing reward can motivate spending. Ibn Atiyya objects that spending is not undertaken for the sake of being strengthened. [7 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي)-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 2:265/q02/p3 The wording admits either purpose or circumstance; ابتغاء alone may be purposeful even if تثبيتا is circumstantial. — Ibn Atiyya accepts a purpose sense for ابتغاء alone; al-Samin and Abu Hayyan allow both analyses. Al-Qurtubi records both proposals. Jishumi separately parses ibtigha as an accusative object of yunfiqun or as accusative after an elided li-, both expressing seeking as the purpose. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 2:265/q03 What does تثبيتا من أنفسهم mean?  [وَتَثْبِيتًۭا مِّنْ أَنفُسِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The causative force of tathbīt, its implied object and agent, and whether the wording can carry the sense of deliberation.
- 2:265/q03/p1 Inward faith, certainty, and belief in God's promised recompense strengthen their resolve to give. — Al-Tabari derives causative strengthening from Ibn Rawaha's verse and says the donors trust the promise; al-Zajjaj contrasts the hypocrite. Al-Sha'bi, Qatada, Abu Salih, Ibn Abbas and others transmit certainty. Al-Tabari, Ibn Atiyya, al-Nahhas and Tabataba'i dispute alternative constructions. Ibn Qutayba describes confirmation as realized from within the donors themselves; Jishumi also attributes the certainty-and-insight gloss to Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Zayd, al-Suddi and Abu Muslim. - 2:265/q03/p2 They deliberate over where and to whom to give, and test their motive before proceeding. — Mujahid and Ata emphasize proper allocation; Hasan gives only when the motive is wholly for God. Al-Wahidi and al-Tabrisi defend the sense as making oneself firm. Al-Tabari, Ibn Atiyya, al-Nahhas and Abu Hayyan object that deliberation requires tathabbatan or an explicit licensing verb; 73:8 is debated. Jishumi attributes careful selection of recipients to al-Hasan and Mujahid, paraphrasing it as firming oneself in the choice. - 2:265/q03/p3 They train or fortify themselves in obedience by giving up beloved wealth, making virtue habitual. — Al-Kashshaf says wealth is close to life and discipline restrains desire; giving wealth steadies part of the self and wealth plus life all of it, citing 61:11. Razi cites repeated action forming disposition, 14:27, 13:28, 76:9 and the Abu Bakr report. Bursevi cites Busiri and Rumi. Ibn Ashur stresses difficult practice. Jishumi reports al-Asamm and Abu Ali's gloss of preparing oneself to remain firm in obedience to the Lord. - 2:265/q03/p4 The self firmly preserves an already pure intention throughout the act of giving. — Tabataba'i makes the self both agent and implied object; the sequence excludes show from the outset and subsequent reproach or harm. Abu Hayyan likewise speaks of protecting the gift from corruption. Jishumi reports the Qadi's gloss as committing oneself to preserve the charity from anything that could corrupt it. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI(القاضي); TABATABAI+]
- 2:265/q03/p5 They seek and expect reward for themselves. — Qatada and Ibn Abbas give this gloss. Al-Tabari objects that the root does not mean seeking reward unless the soul's hope is what strengthens its owner; al-Nahhas likewise calls it unfamiliar. Jishumi attributes the reward-seeking gloss to Qatada. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي، وقتادة، والسدي، وأبو صالح، وابن زيد); JISHUMI(قتادة); TAB-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-]
- 2:265/q03/p6 Their giving makes their sincere faith apparent to other believers. — Al-Kashshaf and Razi invoke Mujahid's tabyīnan variant; Alusi repeats it. The reading describes proof of faith, rather than its internal strengthening. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 2:265/q03/p7 It lessens their sins. — Al-Tha'labi transmits Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Abu Malik. [1 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن جبير وأبو مالك)]
- 2:265/q03/p8 They give acknowledging that God watches them. — Al-Tha'labi reports al-Zajjaj. [1 notes: THALABI(الزجاج)]
- 2:265/q03/p9 They give willingly, from generous intention. — Abu Hayyan reports satisfaction in giving zakat; Ibn Atiyya calls Qatada's generosity gloss close to certainty, and al-Durr transmits intention. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي والضحاك والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 2:265/q04 How does من أنفسهم work?  [مِّنْ أَنفُسِهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the self is part of what is strengthened, the source of strengthening, or the intended object.
- 2:265/q04/p1 من is partitive: giving wealth commits or steadies part of the self. — Al-Kashshaf uses the idiom of stirring some energy; 61:11 distinguishes wealth from wealth and life. Al-Biqa'i, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur likewise treat wealth as figuratively part of the self; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin attach min to the verbal noun. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:265/q04/p2 من marks source: confirmation or resolve arises from within their own selves. — Al-Kashshaf contrasts this with partitivity; al-Nasafi, Bursevi and Alusi locate conviction in the heart. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin say the self can be the semantic agent; Tabataba'i makes it both agent and object. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 2:265/q04/p3 من takes the place of ل or marks an object or an adjective of تثبيتا. — Abu al-Baqa supplies deeds as object and permits min as li; al-Samin also offers an adjectival attachment. Al-Jubba'i takes min as li for training oneself in obedience. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي)]

## 2:265/q05 Who or what is compared with the garden?  [وَمَثَلُ ٱلَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ كَمَثَلِ جَنَّةٍۭ · grammar]
turns on: What implied term completes the comparison and whether its elements correspond one by one.
- 2:265/q05/p1 The sincere spending or its standing with God is like the fruitful garden. — Al-Wahidi supplies an omitted 'spending'; Ibn Atiyya permits that syntax. Al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi stress the expenditure's growth or standing. Al-Biqa'i says the named spender implies the owner and the named garden implies the expenditure. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:265/q05/p2 The giver is like the garden or its planter, while greater and lesser gifts are like heavy and light rain. — Ibn Atiyya supplies a planter; al-Kashshaf gives a differentiated matching; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi allow the giver or the expenditure to be the primary term. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:265/q05/p3 The likeness is a composite of pure, growing expenditure and the fertile, fruitful garden. — Al-Alusi describes the common feature as growth with purity; the imagery includes both the soil and either amount of rain. Al-Jurjani's separate example of Bashshar's dust-and-swords simile shows how a likeness can be one inseparable scene rather than pairwise correspondences; his note does not expressly apply this to 2:265. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال); JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 2:265/q06 What does جنة mean here?  [جَنَّةٍۭ · meaning]
- 2:265/q06/p1 An orchard or tree-covered garden. — Al-Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi explain the root by trees concealing the ground; al-Qurtubi compares jinn and embryo. Al-Farra, al-Mubarrad and others distinguish a palm garden, janna, from a vineyard, firdaws. Jishumi attributes the palm-orchard versus vineyard distinction to al-Farra and derives janna from concealment. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(المبرد والفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI(الفراء)]

## 2:265/q07 Is the image read as a garden or a grain?  [جَنَّةٍۭ · readings]
turns on: A reported alternative word in the comparison.
- 2:265/q07/p1 جنة, a garden. — The standard wording is retained by the sources recording the variant. Jishumi identifies the established readers' consonants as jim and nun. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI(السلمي)]
- 2:265/q07/p2 حبة, a grain or seed. — Mujahid, Asim al-Jahdari and al-Jahdari are named for the variant; al-Kashshaf and al-Alusi also report it. Al-Samin relates its comparison to 2:261. Jishumi attributes the ha-and-ba form to al-Sulami. [7 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI(السلمي)]

## 2:265/q08 What does ربوة mean?  [بِرَبْوَةٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether rabā denotes geographical elevation, soil's swelling growth, or a low place.
- 2:265/q08/p1 Raised ground, often level and fertile, above its surroundings or runoff. — Mujahid and Hasan stress level elevation; Ibn Abbas and Dahhak say rivers do not flow through this garden, though al-Qurtubi notes hills may have water. Al-Yazidi specifies above the watercourse. The root rabā means rise or increase; al-A'sha's poem depicts upland gardens. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi limit the no-rivers detail to this rain-fed setting. Al-Damghani allows level raised ground or a hill and also points to 23:50; Jishumi says the word has seven dialectal forms and attributes the elevation gloss to Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, al-Dahhak, Mujahid, al-Suddi and al-Rabi'. [58 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن,مجاهد); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الأعشى,الحسن,الربيع,السدي,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن); THALABI(أشهب العقيلي); MAWARDI-FULL(اليزيدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الخليل,الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الأخفش,الحسن,الخليل,الخليل بن أحمد); SAMIN-DURR(الخليل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الجمهور، ابن عباس، الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI+]
- 2:265/q08/p2 Fertile soil that swells and rises after rain, rather than an ordinary hill. — Razi cites 22:5 and the contrast with sterile stone, objecting that hills lack streams and face wind while hollows flood. Bursevi and Study Quran repeat the growth argument. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرازي); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Others,al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:265/q08/p3 A low place. — Al-Suddi's rabāwa is transmitted by Ibn Atiyya and others; Ibn Atiyya calls it awkward, and al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin reject it because the root denotes increase and height. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش)-; SAMIN-DURR(الخليل)-]

## 2:265/q10 Why is the garden placed on a rabwa, and what setting is envisaged?  [جَنَّةٍۭ بِرَبْوَةٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Elevation and water's agricultural effects, and whether a named region is intended.
- 2:265/q10/p1 Raised, fertile ground produces especially fine, abundant fruit. — Al-Tabari invokes richer soil and al-A'sha's upland verse. Al-Biqa'i cites sun and pollinating winds; Bursevi and Alusi cite mild, unstagnant air; al-Baydawi cites cool air. Ibn Atiyya rejects identifying Riyad al-Hazn as the precise setting, explaining Najd and Tihama differ; Abu Hayyan follows. Ibn Qutayba says orchards are generally best on heights; Jishumi describes a high, level site where streams run without flooding it and growth is greater. ## 2:265/q29 What do ابتغاء and مرضات mean?  [ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ · meaning]
- 2:265/q29/p1 ابتغاء means seeking, and مرضات names satisfaction or approval. — Al-Qurtubi derives marḍāt as the verbal noun of raḍiya. Jishumi derives ibtigha from ibtaghaytu and notes related forms for seeking something or helping another seek it. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 2:265/q30 What verbal sense underlies تثبيتا?  [وَتَثْبِيتًۭا · grammar]
- 2:265/q30/p1 Causative ثبّت means to make firm or establish, distinct from intransitive ثبت, to become firm. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin's al-Shaykh insist on this distinction, opposing replacement by the intransitive verbal noun unless a licensing verb precedes it. Jishumi illustrates the root's firmness by a steadfast person who does not waver in battle; illness can likewise keep a person from leaving. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); THALABI; JISHUMI]

## 2:265/q31 How are جنة and ربوة pronounced when pausing?  [جَنَّةٍۭ بِرَبْوَةٍ · readings]
- 2:265/q31/p1 Their feminine tāʾ is pronounced hāʾ at pause according to written form and practice. — Al-Nashr states the pause rule for singular feminine nouns without open ta, using janna bi-rabwa as an example; it also contrasts indefinite nonfeminine accusatives and rejects Ibn Jabbara's alleged hāʾ stops at other words as contrary to the script and performance practice. [3 notes: NASHR(ابن جبارة); NASHR+]

## 2:265/q32 How should ربوة be classified when distinguishing a word's forms and meanings?  [بِرَبْوَةٍ · meaning]
- 2:265/q32/p1 Its occurrence should not be inflated into a distinct sense of riba merely because the forms are related. — Ibn al-Jawzi rejects a grouping that makes the garden-on-a-height expression a separate riba sense. He defines wujuh as meanings and naza'ir as repeated forms, criticizes unexamined copying and maximal category counts, and surveys earlier compilers. His separate ifk entry illustrates how one root can have genuinely different contextual senses, from lie and diversion to overturning, sorcery, slander and idols; Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Faris and Abu Ubayda connect the root to turning or diversion. [18 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو عبيدة,ابن فارس,ابن قتيبة,بعض أهل التفسير,بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA+]

## 2:265/q33 What wider revelation setting of al-Baqara is noted alongside this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 2:265/q33/p1 Ibn Kathir situates al-Baqara within staged revelation and discusses the Prophet's Meccan and Medinan periods and their signs. — Ibn Kathir grades Ibn Abbas's report of the Qur'an's descent to the lowest heaven on Laylat al-Qadr and subsequent twenty-year revelation authentic. He discusses the Prophet's years in Mecca and Medina, possible reconciliations of their counts, an Ahmad report about Mika'il and Gabriel, Ramadan's honor and increased recitation, the before-or-after-Hijra definition, disputed surah criteria, disjoint letters, believer-address, and Medinan al-Baqara examples of people-address. [10 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد,ابن عباس,بعضهم)]

## 2:265/q34 What reports about prayer and recitation occur alongside the discussion of rain?  [وَابِلٌۭ طَلٌّۭ · reports]
- 2:265/q34/p1 Al-Nashr recounts supplication in drought and other prayer reports, while rejecting a report about prayer upon completing the Qur'an. — Al-Nashr relates Abu Sulayman's resolve to pray with both hands extended, a report through Abu Awana of the Prophet directing people to kneel and invoke God during drought before rain came, a prayer in Muslim asking forgiveness and good character, and its author's rejection of a report of the Prophet standing to supplicate at Qur'an completion. [4 notes: NASHR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 2:265/q35 How do modern discussions explain the guiding purpose of scriptural parables and stories?  [وَمَثَلُ · rhetoric]
- 2:265/q35/p1 Their intended lesson can be moral guidance and reflection, with later writers debating the relation of that lesson to historical detail. — Rifai reports Taha Husayn's caution about historical inference from shared names; Abduh's account of Qur'anic stories as admonition rather than detailed chronicle; Khalaf Allah's symbolic account likened to parables and the criticism it received; and Iqbal's symbolic reading of Adam. The author calls Khalaf Allah's book pioneering but largely unacceptable and says current studies remain bound by inherited methods instead of al-Khuli's proposed modern perspective. These notes discuss stories generally, not a specific reading of 2:265. [11 notes: RIFAI-KHULI(الشيخ محمد عبده,خلف الله,طه حسين,كتاب,محمد إقبال,محمد عبده)]

## 2:265/q36 What role does reflection on natural signs have in modern discussion of Qur'anic interpretation?  [جَنَّةٍۭ بِرَبْوَةٍ وَابِلٌۭ طَلٌّۭ · interpretation]
- 2:265/q36/p1 Abduh and al-Khuli urge rational reflection, moral learning, and critical examination of inherited interpretations. — Ykhuli places al-Khuli in Abduh's renewal tradition. Abduh says cosmic signs invite moral lesson, gratitude and thought rather than fixed natural laws; he emphasizes inquiry, reason, evolving beliefs, and practical responses to new cases. Both criticize uncritical imitation; al-Khuli says each generation belongs to its own time, while the author contrasts their judgments on imitators and reports Abduh's list of social and intellectual harms. These notes state general interpretive principles rather than an explicit exposition of 2:265's garden. [9 notes: YKHULI-TAJDID(أمين الخولي,محمد عبده)]
