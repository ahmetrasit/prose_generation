# 2:265: 30 questions

## 2:265/q01 Who are the spenders and what do they seek?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ أَمْوَٰلَهُمُ ٱبْتِغَآءَ مَرْضَاتِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 2:265/q01/p1 They give sincerely in obedience to God to obtain His approval and reward, without display, reproach, or injury. — Muqatil and al-Tabari exclude reproach and harm; al-Tabari and al-Mawardi include aid to fighters and obedient Muslims. Al-Hasan excludes reputation. Al-Biqa'i excludes worldly motives and reads marḍāt as lasting satisfaction. Tabataba'i explains God's face as His satisfaction with obedience. Ibn Kathir compares the Ramadan report about faith and seeking reward. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(الحسن,مقاتل بن حيان); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:265/q02 How are ابتغاء and تثبيتا construed grammatically?  [ٱبْتِغَآءَ وَتَثْبِيتًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether coordinated تثبيتا can name a purpose of giving, and whether its implied object is God's reward.
- 2:265/q02/p1 Both are circumstantial verbal nouns describing the spenders' state. — Ibn Atiyya argues coordination makes this the sounder parse and rejects making strengthening the purpose of spending; Abu Hayyan reports and disputes Makki's purpose analysis. Ibn Ashur adopts the circumstantial parse. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)+]
- 2:265/q02/p2 Both are accusatives of purpose, with تثبيتا coordinated to ابتغاء. — Al-Nahhas, al-Tabrisi and Makki offer the parse; Abu Hayyan allows it if the object of strengthening is God's reward, since securing reward can motivate spending. Ibn Atiyya objects that spending is not undertaken for the sake of being strengthened. [7 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي)-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 2:265/q02/p3 The wording admits either purpose or circumstance; ابتغاء alone may be purposeful even if تثبيتا is circumstantial. — Ibn Atiyya accepts a purpose sense for ابتغاء alone; al-Samin and Abu Hayyan allow both analyses. Al-Qurtubi records both proposals. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:265/q03 What does تثبيتا من أنفسهم mean?  [وَتَثْبِيتًۭا مِّنْ أَنفُسِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The causative force of tathbīt, its implied object and agent, and whether the wording can carry the sense of deliberation.
- 2:265/q03/p1 Inward faith, certainty, and belief in God's promised recompense strengthen their resolve to give. — Al-Tabari derives causative strengthening from Ibn Rawaha's verse and says the donors trust the promise; al-Zajjaj contrasts the hypocrite. Al-Sha'bi, Qatada, Abu Salih, Ibn Abbas and others transmit certainty. Al-Tabari, Ibn Atiyya, al-Nahhas and Tabataba'i dispute alternative constructions. - 2:265/q03/p2 They deliberate over where and to whom to give, and test their motive before proceeding. — Mujahid and Ata emphasize proper allocation; Hasan gives only when the motive is wholly for God. Al-Wahidi and al-Tabrisi defend the sense as making oneself firm. Al-Tabari, Ibn Atiyya, al-Nahhas and Abu Hayyan object that deliberation requires tathabbatan or an explicit licensing verb; 73:8 is debated. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,مجاهد); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,عطاء ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء ومجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد والحسن); TABRISI(الحسن ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن ومجاهد وأبو صالح); RAZI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد وعطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والحسن); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Q, R); TAB-FULL-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,النحاس)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; TABATABAI-]
- 2:265/q03/p3 They train or fortify themselves in obedience by giving up beloved wealth, making virtue habitual. — Al-Kashshaf says wealth is close to life and discipline restrains desire; giving wealth steadies part of the self and wealth plus life all of it, citing 61:11. Razi cites repeated action forming disposition, 14:27, 13:28, 76:9 and the Abu Bakr report. Bursevi cites Busiri and Rumi. Ibn Ashur stresses difficult practice. [29 notes: THALABI(ابن كيسان); MAWARDI-FULL(بعض المتكلمين); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي,صاحب الكشاف); TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كيسان,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(البوصيري,جلال الدين الرومي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 2:265/q03/p4 The self firmly preserves an already pure intention throughout the act of giving. — Tabataba'i makes the self both agent and implied object; the sequence excludes show from the outset and subsequent reproach or harm. Abu Hayyan likewise speaks of protecting the gift from corruption. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 2:265/q03/p5 They seek and expect reward for themselves. — Qatada and Ibn Abbas give this gloss. Al-Tabari objects that the root does not mean seeking reward unless the soul's hope is what strengthens its owner; al-Nahhas likewise calls it unfamiliar. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي، وقتادة، والسدي، وأبو صالح، وابن زيد); TAB-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-]
- 2:265/q03/p6 Their giving makes their sincere faith apparent to other believers. — Al-Kashshaf and Razi invoke Mujahid's tabyīnan variant; Alusi repeats it. The reading describes proof of faith, rather than its internal strengthening. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 2:265/q03/p7 It lessens their sins. — Al-Tha'labi transmits Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Abu Malik. [1 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن جبير وأبو مالك)]
- 2:265/q03/p8 They give acknowledging that God watches them. — Al-Tha'labi reports al-Zajjaj. [1 notes: THALABI(الزجاج)]
- 2:265/q03/p9 They give willingly, from generous intention. — Abu Hayyan reports satisfaction in giving zakat; Ibn Atiyya calls Qatada's generosity gloss close to certainty, and al-Durr transmits intention. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي والضحاك والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 2:265/q04 How does من أنفسهم work?  [مِّنْ أَنفُسِهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the self is part of what is strengthened, the source of strengthening, or the intended object.
- 2:265/q04/p1 من is partitive: giving wealth commits or steadies part of the self. — Al-Kashshaf uses the idiom of stirring some energy; 61:11 distinguishes wealth from wealth and life. Al-Biqa'i, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur likewise treat wealth as figuratively part of the self; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin attach min to the verbal noun. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:265/q04/p2 من marks source: confirmation or resolve arises from within their own selves. — Al-Kashshaf contrasts this with partitivity; al-Nasafi, Bursevi and Alusi locate conviction in the heart. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin say the self can be the semantic agent; Tabataba'i makes it both agent and object. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 2:265/q04/p3 من takes the place of ل or marks an object or an adjective of تثبيتا. — Abu al-Baqa supplies deeds as object and permits min as li; al-Samin also offers an adjectival attachment. Al-Jubba'i takes min as li for training oneself in obedience. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي)]

## 2:265/q05 Who or what is compared with the garden?  [وَمَثَلُ ٱلَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ كَمَثَلِ جَنَّةٍۭ · grammar]
turns on: What implied term completes the comparison and whether its elements correspond one by one.
- 2:265/q05/p1 The sincere spending or its standing with God is like the fruitful garden. — Al-Wahidi supplies an omitted 'spending'; Ibn Atiyya permits that syntax. Al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi stress the expenditure's growth or standing. Al-Biqa'i says the named spender implies the owner and the named garden implies the expenditure. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:265/q05/p2 The giver is like the garden or its planter, while greater and lesser gifts are like heavy and light rain. — Ibn Atiyya supplies a planter; al-Kashshaf gives a differentiated matching; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi allow the giver or the expenditure to be the primary term. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:265/q05/p3 The likeness is a composite of pure, growing expenditure and the fertile, fruitful garden. — Al-Alusi describes the common feature as growth with purity; the imagery includes both the soil and either amount of rain. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال)]

## 2:265/q06 What does جنة mean here?  [جَنَّةٍۭ · meaning]
- 2:265/q06/p1 An orchard or tree-covered garden. — Al-Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi explain the root by trees concealing the ground; al-Qurtubi compares jinn and embryo. Al-Farra, al-Mubarrad and others distinguish a palm garden, janna, from a vineyard, firdaws. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(المبرد والفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:265/q07 Is the image read as a garden or a grain?  [جَنَّةٍۭ · readings]
turns on: A reported alternative word in the comparison.
- 2:265/q07/p1 جنة, a garden. — The standard wording is retained by the sources recording the variant. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:265/q07/p2 حبة, a grain or seed. — Mujahid, Asim al-Jahdari and al-Jahdari are named for the variant; al-Kashshaf and al-Alusi also report it. Al-Samin relates its comparison to 2:261. [6 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:265/q08 What does ربوة mean?  [بِرَبْوَةٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether rabā denotes geographical elevation, soil's swelling growth, or a low place.
- 2:265/q08/p1 Raised ground, often level and fertile, above its surroundings or runoff. — Mujahid and Hasan stress level elevation; Ibn Abbas and Dahhak say rivers do not flow through this garden, though al-Qurtubi notes hills may have water. Al-Yazidi specifies above the watercourse. The root rabā means rise or increase; al-A'sha's poem depicts upland gardens. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi limit the no-rivers detail to this rain-fed setting. [55 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن,مجاهد); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الأعشى,الحسن,الربيع,السدي,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن); THALABI(أشهب العقيلي); MAWARDI-FULL(اليزيدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الخليل,الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الأخفش,الحسن,الخليل,الخليل بن أحمد); SAMIN-DURR(الخليل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الجمهور، ابن عباس، الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators)]
- 2:265/q08/p2 Fertile soil that swells and rises after rain, rather than an ordinary hill. — Razi cites 22:5 and the contrast with sterile stone, objecting that hills lack streams and face wind while hollows flood. Bursevi and Study Quran repeat the growth argument. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرازي); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Others,al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:265/q08/p3 A low place. — Al-Suddi's rabāwa is transmitted by Ibn Atiyya and others; Ibn Atiyya calls it awkward, and al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin reject it because the root denotes increase and height. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش)-; SAMIN-DURR(الخليل)-]

## 2:265/q10 Why is the garden placed on a rabwa, and what setting is envisaged?  [جَنَّةٍۭ بِرَبْوَةٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Elevation and water's agricultural effects, and whether a named region is intended.
- 2:265/q10/p1 Raised, fertile ground produces especially fine, abundant fruit. — Al-Tabari invokes richer soil and al-A'sha's upland verse. Al-Biqa'i cites sun and pollinating winds; Bursevi and Alusi cite mild, unstagnant air; al-Baydawi cites cool air. Ibn Atiyya rejects identifying Riyad al-Hazn as the precise setting, explaining Najd and Tihama differ; Abu Hayyan follows. [29 notes: TAB-FULL(الأعشى); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخليل بن أحمد); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون)-]
- 2:265/q10/p2 The image is the rain-fed, raised garden familiar in Arabia, with no stream in this instance. — Ibn Atiyya confines Ibn Abbas's no-rivers description to this garden, citing another raised place with a spring in 23:50; al-Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan agree the word need not exclude water universally. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الخليل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 2:265/q10/p3 The fertile raised garden may have flowing water. — Al-Tha'labi and al-Baghawi describe level raised land supplied by water; Ibn Kathir reports streams in Ibn Abbas's and Dahhak's descriptions, while al-Qurtubi says a rabwa can have water. [4 notes: THALABI(أشهب العقيلي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الجمهور، ابن عباس، الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:265/q10/p4 The parable's land is Egypt. — Zayd ibn Aslam says dew supports Egyptian soil and heavy rain doubles its yield. Al-Tha'labi's report instead says rain weakens it, a conflicting detail preserved here. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]

## 2:265/q11 What does وابل mean?  [وَابِلٌۭ · meaning]
- 2:265/q11/p1 A heavy, abundant downpour. — Al-Akhfash gives paired Arabic verb forms for raining and the adjective mobūla; al-Mawardi cites Adi ibn Zayd's verse; al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi describe intense rain. [14 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عطاء الخراساني,مقاتل بن حيان); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عطاء الخراساني,مقاتل); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام)]

## 2:265/q12 What is the syntax of أصابها وابل?  [أَصَابَهَا وَابِلٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the rain clause is descriptive or circumstantial, and whether its antecedent is garden or ground.
- 2:265/q12/p1 It is a second adjective describing the garden. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin put lasting location before temporary rainfall; al-Qurtubi says the pronoun points to the raised tract. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:265/q12/p2 It describes the rabwa, of which the garden is part. — Abu al-Baqa gives this alternative; al-Qurtubi takes the pronoun as the elevated ground. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:265/q12/p3 It is circumstantial from the garden or the pronoun within its location phrase. — Abu Hayyan allows a circumstantial clause; al-Samin specifies either the implicit pronoun or the now specified garden, with qad understood in the latter. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:265/q13 What does آتت أكلها mean and how is آتت construed?  [فَـَٔاتَتْ أُكُلَهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether آتت retains 'gave' with an omitted recipient or means 'produced' with one object.
- 2:265/q13/p1 The garden gives its edible produce, with an implicit owner or people as first object and أكلها as second. — Al-Samin prefers the ordinary two-object construction and calls the giving metaphorical; Abu Hayyan supplies owner or people. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi say the possessive is association, not garden ownership. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:265/q13/p2 آتت means produced or brought forth, taking أكلها as its sole object. — Abu al-Baqa offers this and Bursevi permits it; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin's al-Shaykh object that Arabic does not attest this meaning of آتت. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:265/q14 What does أكلها mean?  [أُكُلَهَا · meaning]
- 2:265/q14/p1 Its edible fruit, crop, food or produce. — Al-Akhfash, al-Tabari and Abu al-Hasan distinguish food from the act of eating; the poet's meal/hunger line and 14:25 support edible produce. Al-Mufaddal broadens akl to what strengthens something, like yarn in a garment; Abu Zayd uses it for a person's livelihood. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi explain the possessive as association. [29 notes: AKHFASH(الشاعر); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الشاعر); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(المفضل,نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن,أبو زيد,عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 2:265/q16 How much and how often does ضعفين signify?  [ضِعْفَيْنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the dual counts amount, harvests, or repeated multiplication, and the baseline to which a double is compared.
- 2:265/q16/p1 Twice the ordinary or comparable garden's crop. — Most interpreters count an amount plus its equal; Ata and Ibn Abbas compare one year here with two elsewhere. Al-Qurtubi prefers twice the fruit of other land over two yearly harvests. The cause is the high ground plus heavy rain. [34 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,زيد بن أسلم,مقاتل بن حيان); THALABI(عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL(الجمهور); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس في رواية عطاء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,مجاهد); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:265/q16/p2 Two harvests in one year. — Ikrima reports two fruitings; al-Tabrisi specifies six months apart and cites 14:25. Al-Qurtubi prefers a quantity comparison instead. [5 notes: THALABI(عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة وعطاء); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 2:265/q16/p3 Four times the ordinary crop. — Al-Biqa'i prefers this: each double is original plus its equal, so two doubles are four. Al-Tabrisi, al-Baydawi, Bursevi and others transmit the reckoning. [6 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 2:265/q16/p4 Three times the ordinary crop. — Abu Muslim is reported by Abu Hayyan; Taj al-Qurra rejects it as unsupported in Arabic. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم)]
- 2:265/q16/p5 Repeated or manyfold abundance, without an exact count. — Abu Hayyan favors repeated multiples to match multiplied good deeds; Ibn Ashur compares the dual to labbayk. Al-Alusi reports this reading. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 2:265/q17 What grammatical role does ضعفين have?  [ضِعْفَيْنِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether آتت has an omitted recipient and the doubled amount is a state, or the doubled amount is made its object.
- 2:265/q17/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative describing the crop as multiplied. — Al-Tabrisi and al-Baydawi parse it this way; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin reject treating it as a second object of آتت. [5 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:265/q17/p2 It is the second object of آتت. — Reported as a proposal only; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin call it erroneous because the garden's produce is already the object. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:265/q19 How does فإن لم يصبها وابل فطل work grammatically?  [فَإِن لَّمْ يُصِبْهَا وَابِلٌۭ فَطَلٌّۭ · grammar]
turns on: Which constituent is omitted after the conditional, and whether any inversion is necessary.
- 2:265/q19/p1 An omitted verb or predicate completes the condition: if no downpour strikes, drizzle does or suffices. — Al-Farra and al-Tabari supply kāna or a prior event and cite a poem; al-Wahidi compares a one-or-two-slave example. Al-Mubarrad supplies 'rain is enough'; al-Zajjaj says 'what reaches it is light rain.' Abu Hayyan and al-Samin set out subject/predicate and omitted-verb parses, with Imru al-Qays's line as parallel. - 2:265/q19/p2 The order is inverted, so yielding follows either downpour or drizzle. — Al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin report and reject this as unnecessary; ordinary order already conveys the sense. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:265/q25 What inward lesson is drawn from the garden and rain?  [* · inward]

- 2:265/q25/p1 Giving fixes generosity in the soul; the ridge signifies higher rank, and stronger or weaker rain signifies different shares of mercy. — Al-Qashani contrasts the enduring orchard with a grain to express an acquired lasting quality; he calls generosity a divine attribute.
