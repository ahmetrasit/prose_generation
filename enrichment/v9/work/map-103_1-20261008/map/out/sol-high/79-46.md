# 79:46: 17 questions

## 79:46/q01 Who are the people described as seeing it?  [كَأَنَّهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the verse's general plural is read as humanity at resurrection or as the deniers addressed by the closing warning.
- 79:46/q01/p1 People generally, when confronted with the Resurrection or Hereafter. — Muqatil, Qatada as transmitted by Abd al-Razzaq and al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and others describe people without restricting the group. Ibn Kathir places the perception after they rise from their graves. [11 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:46/q01/p2 The disbelievers, specifically the Meccan or Quraysh unbelievers in some accounts. — Al-Tabari calls them deniers; al-Wahidi al-Basit and Ibn al-Jawzi identify Quraysh, as does al-Baghawi; Bursevi particularly names Meccan unbelievers. Al-Biqai says disbelievers give differing estimates of their stay. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:46/q02 What do they see?  [يَوْمَ يَرَوْنَهَا · referent]
- 79:46/q02/p1 The Hour, Resurrection, or the reality of the Hereafter that had been promised. — Muqatil and al-Tabari say the Hour; al-Wahidi al-Basit says what they denied; al-Razi says what was promised. Ibn Kathir places the sight after rising from the graves; al-Tusi's reported Qatada wording speaks of the affair of the Hereafter. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI]

## 79:46/q03 Which stay will seem brief?  [لَمْ يَلْبَثُوٓا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The antecedent of the implied stay: earthly life, the grave period, or the time since the question and warning; al-Tabatabai tests this against what is witnessed at resurrection and what immediately precedes it.
- 79:46/q03/p1 Their earthly life. — Qatada, reported by Abd al-Razzaq, al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti, says the worldly life shrinks when the Hereafter is seen. Al-Mawardi cites 10:45; al-Wahidi al-Basit and al-Razi invoke 46:35. Al-Tabatabai cites 23:114 while rejecting this as the intended sojourn. Alusi allows this for some people. [32 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جرير,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(others); TABATABAI-]
- 79:46/q03/p2 Their time in the graves, or the interval from death to resurrection. — Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz attributes their underestimation to the terror of resurrection. Al-Qurtubi reports this from some exegetes. Study Quran calls it the view of most commentators and cites 10:45, 17:52, 20:103–104, 23:112–114, 30:55 and 46:35. Al-Tabatabai prefers it because all earthly life is counted for judgment; Alusi allows it for others. Some sources present world and grave as alternatives, while al-Biqai mentions both together. [12 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(most commentators); TABATABAI+]
- 79:46/q03/p3 The interval from asking about, or being warned of, the Hour until its arrival. — Al-Tabatabai reports and rejects the interval from asking: people witness the span only at resurrection, and death rather than their question precedes that sight. Alusi prefers understanding a short stay after the warning or threat; the context of the impatient challenge supports it. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]

## 79:46/q04 How long does their stay seem to have lasted?  [إِلَّا عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَىٰهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stated evening or morning remains a fraction of one day or is expressed in related reports as an hour or a day.
- 79:46/q04/p1 Only one end or part of a day, an evening or morning, less than a complete day. — Muqatil describes afternoon to sunset or morning until the sun rises higher; al-Tabari calls them the first and last parts of a day. Al-Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, al-Nasafi, al-Biqai and others stress less than a whole day. Al-Qummi, reported by al-Tabatabai, also says part of a day; al-Qurtubi relates the short stay to 10:45. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 79:46/q04/p2 As little as an hour or moment. — Al-Wahidi al-Basit and al-Razi compare 46:35. Al-Biqai recalls the judgment scene in 10:45 where people say an hour, alongside reports of a day or part of one; he says differing estimates express the same brevity beside the endless future. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 79:46/q04/p3 One whole day. — Al-Qurtubi transmits this from al-Dahhak on Ibn Abbas. Al-Biqai links an answer of a day or part of one to the question about years in 23:112–113. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL]

## 79:46/q05 What time does ʿashiyyah denote here?  [عَشِيَّةً · meaning]
- 79:46/q05/p1 The later part of the day, from noon or afternoon until sunset. — Muqatil gives afternoon to sunset; Ibn Abbas as transmitted by Ibn Kathir defines it from noon to sunset. Al-Tabari and al-Farra treat it as the day's final end; al-Qurtubi calls it afternoon. [8 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL]

## 79:46/q06 What does ḍuḥā mean in this phrase?  [ضُحَىٰهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether ḍuḥā names a bounded morning period, extends to the whole day, or symbolizes worldly life; Bint al-Shati appeals to other Quranic uses.
- 79:46/q06/p1 The day's bright morning or forenoon, from sunrise until noon in explicit definitions. — Muqatil says morning until the sun rises higher; Ibn Abbas as transmitted by Ibn Kathir and Bursevi define sunrise to noon. Bint al-Shati cites Quranic uses opposite evening, the bringing forth of sunlight at 79:29, and appointed event time at 7:98 and 20:59; these oppose taking it as the whole day. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; BINTSHATI]
- 79:46/q06/p2 The early or dawn side of the day. — Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz and al-Baghawi describe the early morning or dawn; Ibn al-Jawzi calls it the first part. Al-Farra's Arabic pairing makes it the beginning opposed to evening's end. [4 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 79:46/q06/p3 It can stand metaphorically for the whole day. — Al-Tibi, reported by Alusi, allows evening or forenoon to stand for the day; the added pronoun reinforces it, like saying one saw with one's own eyes. Alusi allows the pronoun to reinforce this reading too. Bint al-Shati weighs against it from Quranic usage. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); BINTSHATI-]
- 79:46/q06/p4 The morning represents worldly life when people see the Hereafter. — Study Quran reports this from some commentators and mentions 93:1. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 79:46/q07 How does 'or its forenoon' attach the morning to the evening?  [عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَىٰهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the suffix has a same-day antecedent, licenses an adjacent morning, is merely additive, or reinforces a whole-day metaphor; the Quranic phrasing and Arabic idiom are invoked.
- 79:46/q07/p1 The suffix refers to the evening by an expansive attachment: its forenoon is the morning of the same day. — Al-Zajjaj and Ibn al-Jawzi identify the final alif and ha as referring to ʿashiyyah; al-Tabrisi, al-Razi's grammarians, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi and others license the attachment because both periods belong to one day, though morning ordinarily precedes evening. Al-Samin calls it figurative and notes the cadence. Al-Farra's example from a Banu Uqayl poet uses ʿashiyyat al-hilal for the new moon's setting or hidden phase; al-Thaʿlabi, Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Qurtubi repeat it. Ibn Atiyya cites brevity; Abu Hayyan calls the idafa expansive. [23 notes: FARRA(بعض بني عقيل); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,الفراء,بعض بني عقيل); RAZI-FULL(الفراء والزجاج,النحويون); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء,بعض بني عقيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 79:46/q07/p2 The expression can pair an evening with its following morning, as Arabic pairs the ends of adjacent periods. — Al-Farra allows an evening with the next morning or a morning with its previous evening. Al-Zajjaj distinguishes 'morning and its evening' of the same day from 'evening and its morning' of the following day. Al-Wahidi and al-Razi report al-Farra and al-Zajjaj's day association while also citing the next-morning idiom; Ibn al-Jawzi transmits al-Farra's paired-end explanation. Al-Kashshaf as reported by Alusi says the possessive wording rules out a stretch from one day's evening to another day's forenoon. [5 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء والزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 79:46/q07/p3 The final alif and ha are grammatical additions, so the sense is simply 'an evening or a morning'. — Ata from Ibn Abbas, reported by al-Wahidi al-Basit and al-Razi, treats the ending as additive rather than a time relation. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس)]
- 79:46/q07/p4 The suffix reinforces a metaphor in which either end stands for the entire day. — Al-Tibi, reported by Alusi, compares the emphasis to 'I saw with my own eyes.' Alusi allows this reinforcement, though he prefers the less forced ordinary attachment. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]

## 79:46/q08 Why is their past stay perceived as so brief?  [كَأَنَّهُمْ لَمْ يَلْبَثُوٓا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously ground the perceived compression in encounter with the Hereafter, terror, ordinary perception of the past, or the nature of earthly time.
- 79:46/q08/p1 Seeing the Hereafter or Resurrection makes worldly duration shrink in retrospect. — Qatada's formulation appears through Abd al-Razzaq, al-Tabari, al-Tabrisi, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti. Al-Mawardi compares 10:45; al-Wahidi al-Basit 46:35. Al-Biqai contrasts the moment with endless future and cites the Prophet's image of a finger dipped in the sea. [13 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:46/q08/p2 The terror of what they see makes the grave or worldly stay seem short. — Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz expressly attributes underestimation to terror; al-Qurtubi's reported grave view does likewise. Al-Nasafi and Jishumi stress the Resurrection's overwhelming condition and terrors. [4 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); NASAFI-FULL]
- 79:46/q08/p3 Past time feels short because it is finished, while an uncertain future draws attention and anxiety. — Kur'an Yolu gives this psychological explanation. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:46/q08/p4 Earthly time is illusory and loses meaning in the ultimate reality of the Hereafter. — Asad cites 2:259, 17:52, 18:19, 20:103, 23:112 and 30:55 for this conception. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 79:46/q09 What lesson does the brief comparison convey?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The authors draw different lessons from the same perceived brevity: worldly transience, imminent judgment, or the gravity of Resurrection.
- 79:46/q09/p1 Worldly pleasures and life pass quickly, so the warning calls for preparation. — Al-Nahhas warns that worldly people soon leave pleasures. Elmalili says the cautious escape the Hour's terrors while the heedless meet lasting calamity; Kur'an Yolu contrasts short life with everlasting afterlife. [4 notes: NAHHAS; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:46/q09/p2 The Hour is near or should not be dismissed as distant. — Abu Hayyan says the comparison brings the Hour close. Islahi says judgment will make the worldly stay seem only an evening or morning, so delay is no ground for dismissing it; Alusi connects this to the challengers' impatient question. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 79:46/q09/p3 The Resurrection is tremendous and terrifying. — Jishumi says these verses demonstrate its great condition and many terrors; al-Wahidi al-Wajiz and al-Nasafi say its terror makes the stay seem short. [3 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI-FULL]

## 79:46/q10 How does this comparison relate to other Quranic statements about time at judgment?  [* · links]
- 79:46/q10/p1 It echoes statements that people will reckon their past as an hour, a day, or part of one. — Al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi cite 10:45; al-Wahidi al-Basit, al-Baghawi, al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi invoke 46:35. Al-Biqai quotes the judgment scene of an hour and connects the day-or-part-day reply to 23:112–113. Study Quran lists 10:45, 17:52, 20:103–104, 23:112–114, 30:55 and 46:35; Asad cites 2:259, 17:52, 18:19, 20:103, 23:112 and 30:55. Al-Tabatabai cites 23:114 while disputing an earthly-life referent. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(most commentators,others)]

## 79:46/q11 How does this closing comparison answer the earlier question about the Hour?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the preceding question is treated as a request for its unknown date, an impatient taunt, or a warning whose fulfillment will seem near; the reports differ on asking versus mentioning it.
- 79:46/q11/p1 It confirms the warning: the Hour will arrive swiftly and the Prophet's task is warning, while God knows its time. — Alusi prefers a short stay after the warning or threat and says the sentence may be circumstantial or independent. Islahi says timing demands should not trouble the Prophet. Alusi rejects construing 79:43 as a substitute for their question or a quotation of it, because 79:44 directly assigns knowledge of the endpoint to God; Ibn Jurayj, reported by al-Suyuti, also glosses that endpoint as God's knowledge. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جرير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 79:46/q11/p2 It rebuts disbelievers who mockingly or impatiently demand to know when the Hour will come. — Alusi ties the rebuke to 79:42 and prefers an interval after the warning. Study Quran says the final verses answer taunts over the Prophet's ignorance of the date. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 79:46/q11/p3 Earlier questions about the Hour ended with revelation assigning its timing to God. — Aisha reports that the Prophet was repeatedly asked until 79:43–44 came and then stopped asking; al-Hakim authenticated this report. Tariq ibn Shihab instead reports that he often mentioned the Hour, then stopped mentioning it after those verses. Aisha also reports the Prophet telling visiting Bedouins that if their youngest lived a century their own Hour would have come. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(طارق بن شهاب,عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 79:46/q12 What reading or construction is reported for mundhir in the surrounding warning?  [* · readings]
- 79:46/q12/p1 Several readers use mundhir with tanwin, governing its object. — Alusi reports Abu Jaʿfar, Shaybah, Khalid al-Hadhdhaʾ, Ibn Hurmuz, Isa, Talha, Ibn Muhaysin, Ibn Miqsam and Abu Amr through al-Mundhir; he says this is the original construction, while genitive attachment can lighten the expression. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة وخالد الحذاء وابن هرمز وعيسى وطلحة وابن محيصن وابن مقسم وأبو عمرو)]

## 79:46/q13 How does the verse serve the surah's overall design and transition?  [* · links]
- 79:46/q13/p1 It closes the surah's proof of resurrection and human destiny, then returns to the opening and leads into the next surah. — Al-Biqai says the surah establishes resurrection and gathering after preceding surahs' full exposition, with the oaths bringing the matter to a climax. Its name al-Naziʿat evokes angels extracting souls and the Pharaoh–Moses account; al-Sahirah and al-Tammah also fit the flow. The close returns to the opening through resurrection, then links fear and remembrance to 80:1. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 79:46/q14 What does the passage imply about the experience of the afterlife?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the verse's account of seeing and sensing the Hour entails sensory experience.
- 79:46/q14/p1 The afterlife involves felt experience; a merely spiritual account of comfort or pain is rejected. — Al-Qurtubi says the passage answers those who claim only spiritual comfort or pain without sensation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 79:46/q14/p2 Comfort and pain in the afterlife are only spiritual, without sensation. — Al-Qurtubi identifies and rejects this claim. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 79:46/q15 What inward meanings are given to the evening and morning?  [عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَىٰهَا · inward]
turns on: Which paired spiritual phases the two short spans symbolize.
- 79:46/q15/p1 They evoke the setting of truth's light in bodies or its rising from the west; unity makes worldly existence seem imagined. — Al-Qashani reports this possible inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 79:46/q15/p2 They signify the bodily and spiritual worlds, passed in two steps before arrival. — Al-Qashani reports that sensation and intellect conceal these two worlds. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:46/q16 What recitation virtue is reported for Surat al-Naziʿat?  [* · reports]
- 79:46/q16/p1 Its reciter will be held in the grave or at resurrection only as long as an obligatory prayer, then enter Paradise. — Al-Kashshaf reports both grave and resurrection; al-Baydawi reports resurrection and supplies no grading. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 79:46/q17 How are the verses' brief-time expressions used in a reported prayer?  [* · reports]
- 79:46/q17/p1 A prayer for success invokes the briefness-of-time verses alongside mercy, forgiveness, safety, good deeds, Paradise and rescue from Hell. — Al-Suyuti transmits the prayer from Anas. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
