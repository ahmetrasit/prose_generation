# 12:42: 27 questions

## 12:42/q01 Who expected one prisoner to survive, and how certain was that expectation?  [ظَنَّ أَنَّهُۥ نَاجٍۢ مِّنْهُمَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ظن names Joseph's certainty or inference, or the prisoner's response to Joseph's revealed interpretation.
- 12:42/q01/p1 Joseph knew the cupbearer would survive; “thought” expresses certain knowledge or conviction. — Al-Tabari says prophetic prediction cannot be falsely certain; Ibn Atiyya and al-Tabataba'i cite قضي الأمر; al-Tabrisi cites 69:20 and al-Alusi and al-Tabataba'i cite 2:46. Al-Biqa'i says human knowledge is modest beside God's; al-Alusi notes the imperfect prepares Joseph's request. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور المفسرين)+; TABRISI(أكثرون، الجبائي)+; THALABI+]
- 12:42/q01/p2 Joseph inferred the cupbearer's survival with ordinary, fallible conjecture. — Qatada says dream interpretation is conjectural; al-Wahidi adds that God may confirm or annul it; al-Mawardi proposes Joseph doubted whether the prisoners told true dreams. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan object that قضي الأمر sounds decisive unless it merely ends the interpretation. Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, and al-Samin tie this to interpretation by reasoning. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); TAB-FULL-; THALABI-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 12:42/q01/p3 The saved prisoner himself expected release. — Al-Kashshaf and followers propose this if Joseph interpreted through revelation: Joseph knew, while the hearer only expected, since the men had not accepted his prophethood (al-Razi). Ibn Atiyya says joy raised the prisoner's expectation. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 12:42/q02 How does “among the two” attach grammatically?  [مِّنْهُمَا نَاجٍۢ · grammar]
- 12:42/q02/p1 It may describe the saved one or be circumstantial from the relative pronoun, but it is not a complement meaning escape from both men. — Al-Samin records Abu al-Baqa's objection to taking the preposition as a complement of ناج; al-Alusi gives the same constraint. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:42/q03 Who is the “lord” in Joseph's request?  [رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the report's identified master is the reigning king or the Aziz.
- 12:42/q03/p1 The cupbearer's master is the king of Egypt. — Al-Mawardi and Ibn al-Jawzi name him al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan. Ibn Qutayba and al-Qurtubi cite al-A'sha's poetry for rabb meaning master; al-Qurtubi cites 12:23 and ordinary managerial usage. Al-Razi answers the objection that this would make Joseph affirm another god. [33 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(الأعشى); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأعشى); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,ابن سابط); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 12:42/q03/p2 The master meant in one transmitted report is the Aziz. — Al-Tabataba'i quotes a report in al-Ayyashi from Sama'a. [1 notes: TABATABAI(سماعة)]

## 12:42/q04 What did Joseph ask the cupbearer to tell his master, and why?  [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Joseph's desired audience with the king chiefly served release, testimony to his qualities, or religious guidance.
- 12:42/q04/p1 Tell the king of Joseph's wrongful imprisonment and innocence so he could be released. — Al-Tabari says he had committed no crime; al-Razi includes his brothers' sale and the accusation that led to prison. Ibn Kathir says Joseph spoke privately so the baker would not learn his fate. Al-Iṣlāḥī says other paths to justice had closed. [23 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:42/q04/p2 Tell the king Joseph's knowledge, dream interpretation, virtue, and standing. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan allow the qualities, the wrongful detention, or both; al-Qurtubi combines interpretation and injustice; al-Biqa'i stresses clean character against the charge; al-Iṣlāḥī stresses wisdom and dream interpretation. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:42/q04/p3 Use the request to guide the king to faith. — Abu Hayyan points to Joseph's earlier guidance of both prisoners. Al-Alusi calls it fitting to Joseph's rank but contrary to the apparent sense and reports. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 12:42/q05 What happened when Joseph made the request?  [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · reports]
- 12:42/q05/p1 At or near the prison gate, the released man asked what Joseph wanted, and Joseph asked to be mentioned to the king. — Ibrahim al-Taymi gives a prison-door or escort setting; al-Alusi specifies the third day. The accounts differ slightly in setting. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); RAZI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:42/q05/p2 Joseph asked the surviving prisoner privately to avoid revealing the baker's impending execution. — Ibn Kathir infers the private setting from Joseph's concern for the other prisoner. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 12:42/q06 Whom did Satan cause to forget, and what was forgotten?  [فَأَنسَىٰهُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ ذِكْرَ رَبِّهِۦ · referent]

- 12:42/q06/p1 The freed cupbearer forgot to mention Joseph to his master, the king. — Ibn Ishaq and others give this reading. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi say distraction and palace duties account for the lapse; 12:45 says the man later remembered (al-Qurtubi, al-Alusi, al-Tabataba'i, Kur'an Yolu). Ibn Kathir rejects the alternative reports as weak; al-Tabataba'i invokes Joseph's protection from Satan. - 12:42/q06/p2 Joseph forgot to invoke or rely on God and sought help from a human. — Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Tabrisi and al-Razi favor it; Ibn al-Anbari says grammar favors Joseph because the other reading needs an omitted 'mention him.' [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس,الفراء,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد ومقاتل والزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Kl and Q); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(some commentators); RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; TABATABAI-]
- 12:42/q06/p3 Both forgettings are intended: Joseph neglected appeal to God, causing the cupbearer to forget him. — Ibn Ashur says the indirect phrasing tactfully reproaches Joseph while also narrating the cupbearer's lapse. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 12:42/q07 How does “remembrance of his lord” work grammatically?  [ذِكْرَ رَبِّهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: Whether 'his lord' is Joseph's God or the cupbearer's king.
- 12:42/q07/p1 On the cupbearer reading, the phrase means mentioning Joseph to his master by association or ellipsis. — Al-Kashshaf licenses the possessive through association with the king or omission of 'reports'; al-Baydawi proposes mentioning the lord's affairs; Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi explain the loose genitive, while al-Iṣlāḥī compares another annexation. Ibn al-Anbari objects that omission is required. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)-]
- 12:42/q07/p2 On the Joseph reading, the possessive directly names God's remembrance. — Al-Razi and al-Tabari take the wording as a reason to favor Joseph, since the other reading needs an omitted object. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 12:42/q08 How can Satan cause forgetfulness?  [فَأَنسَىٰهُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · theology]
- 12:42/q08/p1 Satan distracts and whispers, while God alone directly effects loss of recall. — Al-Kashshaf compares the attribution of forgetting and mentions 2:106; al-Razi says Satan cannot erase knowledge from a heart, but can occupy it with other activity. Abu Hayyan says the cupbearer was distracted; al-Iṣlāḥī says Satan makes people forget virtuous deeds. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 12:42/q09 Why did Joseph remain in prison after the request?  [فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ · interpretation]

- 12:42/q09/p2 The cupbearer's forgetting prolonged Joseph's stay as part of God's wise plan; the trial could increase his reward. — Abu Hayyan holds that the lapse was Satan's stratagem but God used the delay to increase Joseph's reward; al-Iṣlāḥī says plans succeed only by God's wisdom; al-Biqa'i says means have no independent effect. ## 12:42/q10 What does the request teach about seeking help from people?  [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · law]
turns on: How lawful practical means relate to trust, prophetic rank, and the attributed cause of Joseph's delay.
- 12:42/q10/p1 Seeking lawful human help is permitted, sometimes required, while prophets or spiritual elect may choose fuller reliance on God. — Al-Kashshaf cites 5:2, 3:52 and reports of mutual help; al-Tabrisi cites the Prophet's help from the Emigrants and Helpers; Abu Hayyan cites Jesus' helpers and the Prophet's guard. Al-Razi distinguishes legal permission from spiritual rank; al-Alusi calls the higher resolve fitting to prophets. [12 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:42/q10/p2 Trust in God includes legitimate planning and effort; failure of a plan remains subject to divine wisdom. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says Joseph used a possible lawful route to justice, and distinguishes this from illicit means; al-Qurtubi says God arranged causes in a chain and cites 18:63; al-Tabataba'i denies that ordinary means imply spiritual reliance on creatures. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:42/q10/p3 Joseph's unqualified request lacked mention of God's will. — Al-Razi reports that he could properly ask the cupbearer but should have added a condition such as 'if God wills.' [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 12:42/q10/p4 Turning to people in hardship illustrates a recurrent human weakness. — Al-Hasan repeatedly weeps at the Prophetic report; al-Razi says personal experience finds reliance on people brings difficulty and reliance on God brings the best result; Ibn Ashur sees allusive reproach. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:42/q11 May a person call a human master rabb?  [رَبِّكَ · law]
turns on: How the human sense of rabb and hadith advice about address relate to Joseph's usage.
- 12:42/q11/p1 The term can denote a human master, but more careful speech uses sayyid, mawla, or similar wording. — Al-Qurtubi cites Quranic human uses and hadith discouraging 'my lord' and 'my slave'; he treats the prohibition as guidance in preferred wording. Ibn Sha'ban notes divine servitude and the humiliation of slave labels. One report calls Rabb an agreed divine name, whereas Sayyid is disputed. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شعبان,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 12:42/q11/p2 Such address may have been allowed by Joseph's law. — Ibn al-Arabi explains the verse as belonging to a prior legal dispensation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

- 12:42/q13/p1 It functions as a temporal adverb specifying the duration of Joseph's stay. — Al-Samin reports this i'rab and notes disagreements over the lexical range of بضع. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 12:42/q14 How long was Joseph imprisoned, and which part of the term followed the request?  [فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ بِضْعَ سِنِينَ · reports]
turns on: Whether biḍʿ counts the entire confinement or only the period after Joseph's request, and how competing numerical reports are assessed.
- 12:42/q14/p1 Seven years in all. — Qatada, Wahb and many others report seven; Wahb compares Job's affliction and Nebuchadnezzar's beastly punishment. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi read the verse as possibly giving the entire term; al-Alusi reports two years after the request within seven. [21 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة,وهب بن منبه); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); THALABI(أكثر المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج وقتادة,وهب); BAGHAWI-FULL(وهب); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، علي بن الحسين، أبو عبد الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والضحاك وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج وقتادة ووهب بن منبه,وهب بن منبه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); DURR-FULL(قتادة,وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK and N); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:42/q14/p2 Twelve years total: five before the request and seven afterward. — Al-Kalbi and many commentators give the sequence; a Prophetic report in al-Baydawi specifies it. Al-Qurtubi treats the penalty years as distinct from total imprisonment. Al-Alusi rejects the precise wording of the seven-additional-years report. [12 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,الأكثرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 12:42/q14/p3 Twelve years total: seven before the request and five afterward. — Muqatil reverses the periods and relates the extra years to 12:35. [2 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 12:42/q14/p4 Twelve years total, without a specified division. — Ibn Abbas is quoted for twelve, though other reports divide it into five and seven. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والضحاك وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:42/q14/p5 Fourteen years in all. — Tawus and al-Dahhak transmit fourteen; al-Mawardi cautions the period of punishment could be only part of the total, and al-Alusi calls applying fourteen to biḍʿ unusual. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(طاوس); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والضحاك وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(طاوس والضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(طاوس والضحاك); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:42/q14/p6 Five years in all, or five years plus an unspecified biḍʿ. — Kur'an Yolu lists five among reported totals; al-Qurtubi reports a formula of five years and a further biḍʿ. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل عن مجاهد عن ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:42/q14/p7 Two years were added after Joseph's request. — Ibn Atiyya records this as an alternative to seven additional years; Abu Hayyan also lists two, and al-Alusi reports a total of seven with two after the request. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:42/q14/p8 Seven or nine penalty years were added after five already served. — Al-Qurtubi leaves the exact penalty span open between seven and nine. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:42/q14/p9 The exact duration should be left unspecified. — Al-Alusi rejects firm numbering because reports diverge and precise additional-seven wording is not established; Abu Hayyan notes that the verse itself leaves the figure vague. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 12:42/q15 What reports describe a divine or angelic rebuke to Joseph?  [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ · reports]
turns on: The identity of the rebuking speaker and the details and reliability of the transmitted stories.
- 12:42/q15/p1 Gabriel enumerated God's past favors and asked why Joseph sought human aid. — Ka'b and other reports list creation, father's love, rescue from the well, protection from women, and dream interpretation; the lists vary. Joseph answers God at each step; in some versions he asks mercy or accepts confinement if God is pleased. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; THALABI(كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن,كعب); TABRISI(أبو عبد الله); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد العزيز بن عمير الكندي); DURR-FULL(أنس); ALUSI-FULL(أنس); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); TABATABAI-]
- 12:42/q15/p2 God directly reproached Joseph for making the cupbearer an intermediary and announced longer confinement. — Malik ibn Dinar and Hasan report Joseph wept, explaining that anguish distracted his heart and an utterance escaped; variants name a divine oath or promise not to repeat the lapse. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(مالك بن دينار); IBNABIHATIM(أنس,مالك بن دينار); THALABI(مالك بن دينار); BAGHAWI-FULL(مالك بن دينار); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مالك بن دينار); RAZI-FULL(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(أنس); TABATABAI-]
- 12:42/q15/p3 A report says Joseph prayed in prison for relief through God. — Abu al-Malih invokes Jacob's face and asks for relief, a way out, and unexpected provision; another report says Gabriel taught a prayer for relief, forgiveness, firm hope in God, and detachment from others. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو المليح,عبد الله مؤذن الطائف)]

## 12:42/q16 Which reports connect Joseph's request to other alleged lapses?  [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the source links the request to the woman episode or to Joseph's later statement about betrayal.
- 12:42/q16/p1 Ibn Abbas reports three lapses: the episode with the woman, the request to the cupbearer, and calling the brothers thieves. — The later taunt concerned Benjamin; Ibn Atiyya cites 12:24, 12:70 and 12:77. Al-Tabataba'i rejects this as inconsistent with divine election, purification and Joseph's truthfulness. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI-]
- 12:42/q16/p2 Another three-lapse report lists the request, the theft accusation, and Joseph's claim that he had not betrayed the Aziz. — Gabriel is said to remind Joseph of his desire, after which he says he does not absolve himself. Al-Tabataba'i rejects the implied lies; transmitted verse references vary. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI-]

## 12:42/q17 What does the Torah account add around the prison episode?  [* · reports]
- 12:42/q17/p1 It narrates Joseph's sale, temptation, imprisonment, the prisoners' dreams, their outcomes, and the cupbearer's failure to remember him. — Al-Biqa'i quotes Midianites and Potiphar; Joseph refuses the wife and is falsely accused, then prospers under the jailer. The vine's three branches and baker's three baskets mean three days; Pharaoh restores one, executes the other, and the cupbearer forgets. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL(التوراة)]

## 12:42/q18 How does this verse connect to Joseph's later release and the sura's wider lesson?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the later recollection, Joseph's changed conduct, or the broader sura explains this scene.
- 12:42/q18/p1 The saved man's recollection in 12:45 confirms that he had forgotten Joseph. — Al-Qurtubi, al-Alusi, al-Tabataba'i and Kur'an Yolu use the later verse to support the cupbearer reading; al-Qurtubi adds that prophets may forget ordinary matters, while revelation is protected. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(many commentators)]
- 12:42/q18/p2 Joseph's eventual refusal to answer the king immediately may repair his earlier appeal for royal help. — Al-Razi says Joseph put vindication of his name before favor from the king. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 12:42/q18/p3 The sura illustrates God's hidden direction of human affairs. — Asad contrasts Genesis' romantic account and cites 2:216: people may dislike what benefits them and like what harms them. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 12:42/q19 Can prophets forget ordinary matters?  [فَأَنسَىٰهُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ · theology]
turns on: Whether the reported forgetting is ordinary and permissible or entails blameworthy satanic influence.
- 12:42/q19/p1 Yes; protection concerns conveying revelation, not every ordinary lapse. — Al-Qurtubi cites 12:45, 17:65 and hadith that Adam and the Prophet forgot; he uses this to answer an objection to the cupbearer reading. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 12:42/q19/p2 Joseph's sincerity and divine purification rule out blameworthy satanic access in this episode. — Al-Tabataba'i rejects reports alleging grave lapses, while Ibn Kathir challenges their chains; Abu Hayyan says the stories should not be attributed to prophets. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI]

## 12:42/q20 What inward reading is given to Joseph's request and continued imprisonment?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which spiritual faculty or stage is represented by the forgotten messenger.
- 12:42/q20/p1 The request represents seeking stable spiritual existence through love, with a remnant of self that prolongs the journey. — Al-Qashani maps intoxicated love, spirit and heart toward unity, yet sees veiling rather than annihilation; ego or illusion can persist, and prison marks seclusion until subsistence through God begins. [4 notes: QASHANI]
- 12:42/q20/p2 The alternative forgetting is the lover's loss of the love-messenger in bewildered witnessing. — Al-Qashani says unity makes the lover forget creation and self until annihilation ends and divine life restores distinction. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 12:42/q21 What institutional lesson is drawn from Joseph's long imprisonment?  [* · law]
- 12:42/q21/p1 Prisons need regular judicial oversight so detainees do not disappear from official attention. — Ibn Ashur infers that the prison lacked a reliable register or periodic royal report, then says Islamic law requires a judge to review prisoners daily. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:42/q22 What did Joseph's patience after imprisonment show?  [* · reports]
- 12:42/q22/p1 He interpreted the king's dream before asking for release and sought vindication before leaving prison. — Abd al-Razzaq transmits a Prophetic expression of admiration for his patience and generosity, while connecting the earlier request to longer confinement. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 12:42/q23 How is the term of imprisonment connected to an earlier decree?  [فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ · links]
- 12:42/q23/p1 The added years fulfill the earlier decision to imprison Joseph in 12:35. — Muqatil says the initial seven years and five later punitive years made twelve in all. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 12:42/q24 What did Ali say about disciplining the soul in relation to this episode?  [* · inward]
- 12:42/q24/p1 The soul repeatedly scatters from discipline like a flock moving away from the shepherd. — Al-Tustari transmits Ali's comparison as an inward reflection on attention and self-discipline. [1 notes: TUSTARI(علي ابن أبي طالب)]

## 12:42/q25 Which prisoner was expected to survive?  [ٱلَّذِى ظَنَّ أَنَّهُۥ نَاجٍۢ · referent]
- 12:42/q25/p1 The surviving prisoner was the cupbearer. — Ibn al-Jawzi identifies him explicitly. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 12:42/q26 Does a report of Joseph's request necessarily imply blame?  [ٱذْكُرْنِى عِندَ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
- 12:42/q26/p1 Some scholars read Joseph's words without blame. — Al-Alusi reports this interpretive possibility, which accords with lawful use of means discussed elsewhere in the notes. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض ذوي الألباب)]

## 12:42/q27 How does al-Tabataba'i assess the al-Qummi account of the two prisoners?  [* · reports]
- 12:42/q27/p1 Its description of them as royal attendants assigned to guard Joseph is textually disordered. — Al-Tabataba'i says it conflicts with the Qur'an's description of them as Joseph's prison companions. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
