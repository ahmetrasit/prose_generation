# 68:27: 9 questions

## 68:27/q01 What earlier sense of being lost does 'but' correct?  [بَلْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the earlier claim of being lost concerned the road to an unrecognizable garden or their conduct and mistaken expectation about the harvest.
- 68:27/q01/p1 They initially thought they had lost the road, then recognized the ruined garden. — Al-Tabari says some brothers thought they had lost the way until another identified their garden. Al-Wahidi, citing Ibn Abbas, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi, and Islahi say the burned or devastated orchard was unrecognizable at first. Al-Nahhas reports that one who recognized it corrected the mistake. Abu Hayyan, reporting Qatada, says they thought they had taken the wrong road until they saw that punishment had destroyed its bounty. Ibn Kathir reports Ibn Abbas and others; al-Alusi calls this a retraction of their first guess. Elmalili adds that recognition brought despair about present and future loss. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 68:27/q01/p2 They had gone astray in planning to take the harvest while excluding the poor, or in assuming that plan would succeed. — Al-Razi says they had resolved to bar the poor and assumed they would benefit from the orchard. Abu Hayyan reports an interpretation that their intended treachery rebounded on them. Tabatabai says they first spoke of straying from right conduct in trying to harvest while excluding the poor, before correcting themselves. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 68:27/q02 What does 'but' mark in their admission?  [بَلْ · rhetoric]
- 68:27/q02/p1 It corrects their first account of being lost with the graver realization that they themselves have been deprived. — Muqatil and Ibn Jurayj, as reported by al-Durr, connect the correction to recognizing the garden's landmarks. Al-Tabari, al-Nahhas, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Alusi, Elmalili, Tabatabai, and Islahi describe the movement from an initial assessment to recognition of loss. Ibn Ashur calls the shift more serious: they intended to deny the poor surplus, but lost all the fruit themselves. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 68:27/q03 Why is 'we' stated explicitly before 'deprived'?  [نَحْنُ · rhetoric]
- 68:27/q03/p1 The fronted pronoun restricts the admission to them as the ones truly deprived. — Ibn Ashur says the passive construction could have left its subject implicit; stating and fronting نحن adds this restriction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 68:27/q04 What does 'deprived' mean here?  [مَحْرُومُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the deprivation denotes the lost harvest and its benefits or their resulting destitution.
- 68:27/q04/p1 They lost the garden's crop, fruit, benefit, blessing, or expected good. — Muqatil says they recognized punishment; al-Tabari's own account speaks of denied benefit, and his report from Qatada calls it repayment by deprivation of the garden. Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Nahhas, al-Wahidi's exegetes, al-Jishumi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqai, al-Alusi, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai, and Islahi variously name the garden, its fruit, crop, yield, bounty, benefit, blessing, or expected good; al-Tabari and others describe its destruction. Al-Durr's report from Ibn Jurayj also calls them afflicted by misfortune after they recognized its landmarks. The Study Quran takes the lost harvest as its main contextual sense. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:27/q04/p2 They were rendered destitute. — The Study Quran offers this as an additional possible meaning of the words. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 68:27/q05 Why were they deprived?  [مَحْرُومُونَ · interpretation]
- 68:27/q05/p1 Their wrongdoing, especially intending to deny the poor, brought deprivation as punishment. — Al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi's exegetes, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Ibn Ashur connect the lost fruit to withholding it from the poor. Al-Jishumi and al-Baghawi add their failure to make an exception. Al-Razi attributes the loss to their miserly resolve; Abu Hayyan says their intended treachery rebounded on them. Al-Biqai says God took the expected good in response to their intention to deprive the poor, and Elmalili says their punishment exceeded what they had planned for the poor. Al-Mawardi reports Qatada calling it punishment for wrongdoing; al-Tabari reports Qatada calling it repayment. Al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, and al-Alusi likewise assign the loss to their own deeds or wrongdoing; Muqatil says they recognized it as punishment. Abu Hayyan's report from Qatada says God's punishment destroyed the garden's bounty. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 68:27/q06 What earlier ownership is reported for the garden?  [* · reports]
- 68:27/q06/p1 It had belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town. — Bint al-Shati reports this as part of exegetes' elaboration of the garden story. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 68:27/q07 Where or among whom is the garden story placed?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give different settings or identify the people differently; the note supplies no grounds for choosing among them.
- 68:27/q07/p1 In Yemen near Sana'a. — Bint al-Shati lists this among the reported settings. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 68:27/q07/p2 In Abyssinia. — Bint al-Shati lists this among the reported settings. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 68:27/q07/p3 Among the People of Scripture. — Bint al-Shati lists this reported identification of the people alongside the reported places. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 68:27/q08 Did Qatada assign the garden's owners to Paradise or Hell?  [* · reports]
- 68:27/q08/p1 He gave no answer. — Al-Durr reports that when asked whether the garden owners were people of Paradise or Hell, Qatada said the question burdened him. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 68:27/q09 What wider lesson is drawn from the loss?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw different, compatible lessons from the same loss; they give no argument against any of these lessons.
- 68:27/q09/p1 God controls whether crops flourish, so any harvest depends on Him. — The Study Quran says this story extends to all harvests and recalls 56:67 together with 56:63-66. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 68:27/q09/p2 God's taking their good responds to their own changed intention. — Al-Biqai cites 13:11, that God does not change a people until they change themselves, in connection with their intention to deprive the poor. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 68:27/q09/p3 Calamity softens and leads to repentance for one God intends to guide, but increases another person's disbelief. — Al-Biqai draws this distinction about the effect of calamity. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
