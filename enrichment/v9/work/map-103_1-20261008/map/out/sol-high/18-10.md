# 18:10: 37 questions

## 18:10/q01 What governs the opening “when”?  [إِذْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the earlier clause supplies a suitable governing word and whether the two events must be simultaneous.
- 18:10/q01/p1 An omitted command, “remember,” governs it. — Wahidi and Razi exclude attachment to the preceding question because the Prophet's time and the youths' retreat were far apart. Ibn Ashur calls this an explanatory restart; Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi and Alusi also allow the ellipsis. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:10/q01/p2 It is governed by the preceding “wonder.” — Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi and Alusi give this as a syntactic possibility; Bursevi calls it an adverbial attachment. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:10/q01/p3 It attaches to “were” in verse 9. — Ibn Ashur treats it as a temporal adverb linking the scene to the preceding clause; Alusi also allows this attachment. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:10/q01/p4 It attaches to “did you think” in verse 9. — Alusi argues against this because the Prophet's perception was not simultaneous with their retreat; Wahidi and Razi likewise reject attaching it to the preceding question because of the long interval. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-; WAHIDI-BASIT-; RAZI-FULL-]

## 18:10/q02 What does “the youths” designate?  [ٱلْفِتْيَةُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the form is analyzed as a broken plural or collective, and how strictly youth limits its sense.
- 18:10/q02/p1 It denotes a small group of young men, the Companions of the Cave. — Abu Hayyan calls it a broken plural of paucity, fitting their few number; Ibn Ashur calls it a small-group plural of fully grown youths; Biqai says youth made them receptive to truth. Alusi identifies them as the Cave companions. Ibn Jawzi reports the usual sense as young men; Bursevi adds vigor and the metaphorical use for rulers even when old, and cites a prophetic preference for 'my young man' over 'my slave,' while Abu Yusuf allows bondage. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب وغيره); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:10/q02/p2 It is a collective plural of fatā on a nonproductive pattern. — Zajjaj, Tabrisi and Ibn Jawzi compare ghulām/ghilma and ṣabī/ṣibya; Ibn Jawzi says the pattern cannot freely form analogues such as 'ravens' or 'wealthy men.' Ibn al-Sarraj, cited by Abu Hayyan, calls it a collective noun; Alusi reports other authorities likewise. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب وغيره)]
- 18:10/q02/p3 Fatā can also mean a fully grown man. — Ibn Qutayba, reported by Ibn Jawzi, cites the verse about believing women; Ibn Ashur describes a fully grown youth. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:10/q03 Why does the verse repeat “the youths” as a noun?  [ٱلْفِتْيَةُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the explicit noun chiefly foregrounds youth or respects the order by which they became cave companions.
- 18:10/q03/p1 It draws attention to their youth and its significance. — Alusi says the explicit noun highlights their youth; Ibn Ashur says their close ages evoke mature courage, sound judgment, steadfastness and defense of truth, while Biqai sees youth as receptive to truth. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 18:10/q03/p2 It avoids calling them owners of the cave before they took refuge there. — Alusi says cave ownership results from their retreat, so a pronoun referring back to the earlier title would presuppose it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:10/q04 Who were the youths socially, and how many were there?  [ٱلْفِتْيَةُ · reports]
turns on: The divergent historical reports give different numbers and social roles; the Quran supplies neither their number nor their lineage.
- 18:10/q04/p1 They were young Roman nobles or sons of prominent families. — Ibn Ishaq through Tabari calls them freeborn nobles; Tabari's Ubayd report calls them richly dressed princes, with gold ornaments in Abu Hayyan and Alusi. Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi call them sons of nobles; Baydawi, Bursevi and Study Quran report the same setting. Mujahid says one was still downy-bearded. Ibn Ishaq's narrative gives eight. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير,مجاهد); THALABI(ابن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق بن يسار); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs, Q); TABATABAI]
- 18:10/q04/p2 They were close associates or ministers of the king. — Tabrisi's Ubayd account calls them close associates; Study Quran reports courtiers, and Tabatabai lists ministers advising the king among variant reports. [3 notes: TABRISI(عبيد بن عمير); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs, Q); TABATABAI]
- 18:10/q04/p3 They numbered eight. — Ibn Abbas in Tabari names the eldest spokesman Mكسلمينا and seven others; Tabari's king confronts eight, and Ibn Ishaq's account in Baghawi and Thalabi has eight elite youths. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق بن يسار)]
- 18:10/q04/p4 They numbered seven, including a shepherd in one account. — Ibn Abbas's account in Qurtubi has seven young secret worshippers and a shepherd with a dog; Tabatabai lists seven including a shepherd among reports. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 18:10/q04/p5 Reports also call them scholars or bathhouse workers. — Tabatabai lists these alternative social identities; Wahb's bathhouse account concerns a disciple who worked there and converted the youths. [4 notes: TABATABAI; TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 18:10/q05 When and within which faith did the youths live?  [ٱلْفِتْيَةُ · reports]
turns on: Reports place them before or after Jesus, while the Quran itself omits the date.
- 18:10/q05/p1 They followed Jesus and lived after him. — Amr in Tabari says they followed Jesus; Ibn Ishaq's account makes them Christian believers under a pagan Roman ruler, as do Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Study Quran. Tabrisi says they followed Jesus after the Gospel community had passed. Tabatabai says most historians date them between Jesus and Muhammad and that Christians preserved the story widely. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,عمرو); THALABI(ابن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق بن يسار); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Th); TABATABAI(أهل الرواية والتاريخ)]
- 18:10/q05/p2 They entered the cave before Jesus. — Ibn Qutayba says Jesus disclosed their story and they rose between Jesus and Muhammad; Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan preserve the alternative pre-Jesus chronology. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 18:10/q05/p3 The Quran leaves their religious affiliation and date unspecified. — Kuranyolu says reports link them to Jesus but the Quran selects only instructive details, with no date or place; Tabatabai says the Quran omits historical particulars that do not serve guidance. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]

## 18:10/q06 What persecution led to their flight?  [ٱلْفِتْيَةُ أَوَى · reports]
turns on: Reports differ on the ruler's religion and the immediate cause of pursuit.
- 18:10/q06/p1 An idolatrous ruler demanded worship of idols and threatened believers with death. — Yahya says they feared death for faith. Ibn Ishaq names Deqianus/Decius and describes forced sacrifice, mutilation and believers displayed on city walls; Tabari says informers found their prostration, the king summoned eight, stripped their rank and delayed punishment. Qurtubi and Baydawi report refusal and flight; Tabrisi calls the king Daqiyanus in Ephesus. Names vary Daqyus, Daqliyus and Daqinus; Study Quran notes Decius as a possible source of the name. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,عمرو); THALABI(ابن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق بن يسار); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد عن ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(Th,Th, Ṭs,Ṭs)]
- 18:10/q06/p2 A Magian or Zoroastrian king imposed his religion. — Tabrisi reports this alternative to an idol-worshiping king, also noted by Study Quran from Christian sources. [2 notes: TABRISI; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabrisī)]
- 18:10/q06/p3 One youth was accused of a crime and the group fled the ruler's pursuit. — Tabari gives this as a separate reported explanation. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 18:10/q07 How did the youths come together and leave for the cave?  [أَوَى ٱلْفِتْيَةُ · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted narratives give different occasions, escape devices and sequences.
- 18:10/q07/p1 The young believers met by chance while leaving an idolatrous festival, tested one another's faith, and fled together. — Ubayd ibn Umayr in Tabari and Ibn Atiyya describes independent faith, cautious pairing and disclosure; Mawardi's Mujahid and Tabrisi describe their chance meeting. Some accounts add a hunting dog and 309 years' sleep. Islahi says recognition of truth made them disregard danger. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); TABRISI(عبيد بن عمير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:10/q07/p2 They escaped under cover of play or a hunt. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi preserve a polo-ball ruse or flight during a public festival; Tabatabai's report says they feigned hunting. These accounts differ on whether the king knew of their resistance before escape. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 18:10/q07/p3 After the king delayed punishment, they gathered money and fled to a mountain cave. — Tabari says Deqianus removed their insignia, gave them time to reconsider, and each took money, gave some away and left with a dog; Qurtubi recounts the reprieve. Ibn Ishaq says they had already prayed, fasted and given alms under persecution. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:10/q07/p4 A disciple of Jesus converted them at a bathhouse, then fled with them after two deaths were blamed on the group. — Wahb's report says the king's son and a woman or prostitute died there; a farmer and dog joined, and the king later sealed the cave. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 18:10/q07/p5 Accounts differ on whether they planned together or met by chance, and whether the ruler knew before they fled. — Tabatabai explicitly records these axes of variation. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:10/q08 What happened after they took shelter in the cave?  [أَوَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports]
turns on: The accounts vary in discovery, sealing and later reopening, while agreeing on extraordinary sleep.
- 18:10/q08/p1 They worshipped there, obtained food secretly, feared the king's search, and were sealed inside. — Tabari says Yemliha entered town disguised as a poor man, returned with news and a meal, and the youths prostrated and prayed for safety. Amr says the people sealed them and a later Christian king reopened the cave. Study Quran says the king expected hunger and thirst to kill them. Tabari also recounts the king's search and his stated willingness to forgive repentance. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(عمرو); STUDYQURAN(Th)]
- 18:10/q08/p2 They slept for 309 years, later awoke and debated how long they had slept. — Ubayd's Tabari report gives 309 years; Amr says they guessed a day or part of one. Mawardi's Mujahid gives three hundred plus nine, and Ibn Qutayba places their raising between Jesus and Muhammad. Tabatabai summarizes their flight, sleep, awakening and eventual discovery. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير,عمرو); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة,مجاهد); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:10/q08/p3 The people failed to find the cave, and later generations found the youths under a Muslim king. — Ubayd's report in Tabari says the search failed, names and lineage were recorded, and a Muslim ruler succeeded the earlier king. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير)]

## 18:10/q09 What is reported about their dog?  [ٱلْفِتْيَةُ ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports]
turns on: Whether the dog already belonged to a youth or accompanied a shepherd.
- 18:10/q09/p1 It was a hunting dog belonging to one youth. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi give this account; Muqatil in Qurtubi specifically assigns it to Maksalmina, the eldest youth and owner of sheep. The dog's reported names vary Himran and Qitmir. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل)]
- 18:10/q09/p2 It was a shepherd's dog that followed its owner and the youths. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi report a shepherd joining them; Tabatabai's report says the shepherd accepted their faith, while other accounts say only the dog followed. Names vary Himran and Qitmir. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 18:10/q10 What are the youths' names, and how were they recorded?  [ٱلْفِتْيَةُ · reports]
turns on: Transmission of the foreign names is weak, and reports differ on who inscribed the record and where it was kept.
- 18:10/q10/p1 Reports give foreign names, but their transmission is weak. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi note weak transmission; Tabari's list includes Makselminya or Maksalmina as eldest spokesman, Majseliniya, Tamlikha or Yemliha as the one sent with money, Martus, Kashutunus, Birunus, Dinamus and Yatunus. Spellings vary. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:10/q10/p2 Their identities and story were inscribed on metal tablets. — Tabari says two secret believers Bedros and Ronas placed lead tablets in a sealed copper chest by the cave, hoping later believers would find them. Ibn Atiyya reports lead or copper tablets by two believing judges, hidden at their homes or in a building over the cave; Ubayd's report says people recorded their names and lineage. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير); IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 18:10/q11 Where was their cave?  [ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports]
turns on: Geographic identifications depend on divergent reports and archaeological signs; none is conclusive in Tabatabai's judgment.
- 18:10/q11/p1 It was Khayram in Mount Yanjalus or Banjulus near Ephesus. — Thalabi and Baghawi give variant forms of the mountain name; reports in Ibn Ishaq and Study Quran place the youths in Ephesus. [3 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Th, Ṭs)]
- 18:10/q11/p2 Rujayb near Amman best fits the site. — Tabatabai prefers Jordanian archaeological signs and some Muslim reports after centuries of ruin and forgetting; he notes Amman's predecessor Philadelphia was a major Roman city. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+; TABATABAI-]
- 18:10/q11/p3 Other proposed sites include Qasiyun, Petra, Scandinavia and Nakhchivan. — Tabatabai lists them, but argues no proposed cave is identified conclusively; Scandinavian and Caucasian claims conflict with Roman geography. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 18:10/q11/p4 The Quran does not locate the event. — Kuranyolu says the place and date are not supplied; Tabatabai says such details are omitted because they do not determine guidance. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]

## 18:10/q12 What does “took refuge” mean?  [أَوَى · meaning]
- 18:10/q12/p1 They went to the cave, settled there and made it their refuge or dwelling. — Zajjaj, Thalabi, Baghawi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Bursevi and Ibn Jawzi's report from Zajjaj use going and sheltering; Ibn Ashur says it means settling in a dwelling, comparing maʾwā in 10:8. Wahidi transmits Ibn Abbas's gloss of fleeing to it. Jishumi and Tabrisi explain the root as returning to a refuge. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:10/q13 Why did they seek the cave, and what does their flight teach?  [أَوَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · interpretation]
- 18:10/q13/p1 They fled persecution to preserve their faith, making religious flight a model. — Yahya, Tabari, Nahhas, Wahidi, Mawardi's Hasan, Tabrisi, Ibn Kathir and Study Quran say they protected religion from an unbelieving people. Qurtubi says believers may leave family, homeland, wealth and friends, citing 16:41 and 9:20 and the Prophet's Companions; Biqai compares Muhammad and Abu Bakr at Thawr. Study Quran's Aj says they despaired of worldly kindness and sought God alone. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj,IK, Ṭs)]

## 18:10/q14 What form of seclusion does their retreat commend?  [أَوَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · law]
turns on: Capacity and social conditions determine which kind of seclusion is apt; a prophetic report values patient mixing.
- 18:10/q14/p1 Retreat from corruption is commendable, with mountains and caves its strongest form. — Qurtubi calls retreat prophetic and saintly, especially in fitna. He cites a Bukhari report on sheep in mountain heights, reports advising staying home and guarding one's tongue, weeping over sin, flight from peak to peak, and a shepherd whose prayer earns forgiveness. He prefers mountain retreat for those able; a reported year-180 permission for celibacy and mountain retreat also appears. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,الحسن بن واقد,النبي ﷺ); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 18:10/q14/p2 Seclusion can be at home, by the coast or frontier, or within a mosque. — Qurtubi says mountains are named as common retreats, but any separation from people counts. He cites reports calling homes cloisters and Badr veterans who stayed home after Uthman's killing; scholars allow coastal and frontier posts, and a prophetic counsel advises staying home. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,العلماء,النبي ﷺ)]
- 18:10/q14/p3 One may remain among people while withdrawing from evil or keeping inward distance. — A group of scholars defines this as inward and practical separation; Ibn al-Mubarak joins remembrance and keeps silent otherwise. Wahb counsels a recluse that people need one another. Qurtubi says some can mix outwardly while distinct inwardly. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المبارك,طائفة من العلماء,وهب بن منبه)]
- 18:10/q14/p4 Patiently mixing with people can be better than avoiding them. — Qurtubi reports the Prophet's comparison of a believer who bears people's harm with one who does not mix or bear it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]

## 18:10/q15 What does the cave signify in inward readings?  [ٱلْكَهْفِ · inward]
turns on: Inward interpreters map the physical cave to different dimensions of spiritual refuge.
- 18:10/q15/p1 It is outward shelter and inward divine care. — Qushayri says God took charge of the youths and brought spiritual states upon them while they were absent from themselves; Study Quran's Aj says they sought God's protection after despairing of the world. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 18:10/q15/p2 It symbolizes the body, where souls develop toward intellectual and practical perfection. — Qashani reads the youths' descent into bodily existence, attachment and tools as a path toward God. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 18:10/q15/p3 It symbolizes the heart. — Study Quran states this mystical reading. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 18:10/q15/p4 It symbolizes solitary encounter with God and escape from the self. — Bursevi's cited Taʾwilat Najmiyya contrasts their flight from Daqyanus with seekers' flight from existence's darkness to divine beauty and majesty; Alusi reports spiritual chivalry as abandoning everything besides God and ascending through blissful stations. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 18:10/q16 What does the “so” before their prayer convey?  [فَقَالُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 18:10/q16/p1 They prayed immediately on reaching the cave. — Ibn Ashur reads the fāʾ as indicating haste. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:10/q17 What mercy did they request?  [رَحْمَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mercy names one concrete need or an inclusive gift, and whether it is outward safety, religious aid or inward remembrance.
- 18:10/q17/p1 They sought God's broad help, including forgiveness, provision, protection, guidance, patience or knowledge. — Zajjaj and Wahidi give forgiveness and provision; Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi and Study Quran add safety from enemies; Razi adds guidance, knowledge and patience. Alusi prefers an encompassing sense beyond these glosses. Ibn Ashur calls it abundant good in both worlds; Biqai calls it exceptional mercy and al-Harali's highest hidden order. Tabatabai gives divine support or God's exclusive gifts, especially guidance, citing 2:201, 3:194 and 3:8. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs, Z); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 18:10/q17/p2 They asked for provision. — Muqatil, al-Suddi, Ibn Jawzi, Ibn Atiyya and exegetes cited by Abu Hayyan give this concise gloss; Baghawi reports it as an alternative to guidance in religion. [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون)]
- 18:10/q17/p3 They asked for guidance in religion. — Baghawi gives this alternative; Tabatabai calls special guidance a gift reserved to God. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:10/q17/p4 They asked to be preserved through remembrance and gratitude. — Tustari says preservation in God's remembrance; Sahl in Sulami adds gratitude in every state; Study Quran transmits the remembrance gloss. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); STUDYQURAN(ST)]
- 18:10/q17/p5 They asked for rescue from persecution. — Nahhas says rescue from unbelievers; Thalabi says lifting persecution, Tabrisi rescue, Ibn Kathir concealment from their people, and Ibn Ashur protection from exile's hardship and humiliation. [6 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:10/q18 What does “from Your presence” add?  [مِن لَّدُنكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the phrase is a simple source marker or suggests special, unowed divine favor.
- 18:10/q18/p1 It asks for mercy directly from God's own stores and special bounty. — Yahya's Suddi glosses it as God's presence; Ibn Abbas in Wahidi says this makes them independent of others. Razi says it magnifies the gift fitting divine generosity; Bursevi imagines hidden treasuries beyond hostile sight; Ibn Ashur distinguishes special abundant mercy from universal mercy; Study Quran transmits the storehouse gloss. Abu Hayyan transmits Zamakhshari's gloss of this mercy as forgiveness, provision and safety from enemies. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Z); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 18:10/q18/p2 It emphasizes that mercy is a gratuitous favor, not something owed. — Alusi draws this theological implication from the phrase; Tabatabai sees desperate recourse after every other means failed. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:10/q18/p3 It simply means “from You.” — Ibn Jawzi gives the direct gloss; al-Suddi likewise says 'from God's presence.' [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي)]

## 18:10/q19 Does their prayer seek worldly good, afterlife good, or both?  [رَحْمَةًۭ رَشَدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of mercy and the relation between the prayer's two clauses.
- 18:10/q19/p1 It chiefly concerns their worldly predicament. — Ibn Atiyya says they trusted their afterlife outcome, though mercy might include it; Abu Hayyan reports his view and Alusi reports it while rejecting the restriction. Tabatabai identifies the peril after flight from coercion and death. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 18:10/q19/p2 It asks for good in both worlds. — Ibn Atiyya allows afterlife mercy; Alusi rejects restricting it to worldly affairs; Ibn Ashur calls it comprehensive good in both worlds, and Biqai asks deliverance and guidance in both. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)+]
- 18:10/q19/p3 Mercy and guidance may name one gift or two distinct requests. — Tabatabai says if mercy means divine support, the guidance clause explains it; if mercy means a specific divine gift, the clause adds another request. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:10/q20 What does “prepare” mean?  [وَهَيِّئْ · meaning]
- 18:10/q20/p1 Set right, arrange or make easy the means to the desired result. — Wahidi glosses it as set right or prepare; Baghawi says make easy, Ibn Jawzi set right; Baydawi and Tabatabai define the root as bringing a thing into a formed state; Ibn Ashur says furnishing the means to an outcome. Muqatil uses facilitation. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(البيضاوي)]

## 18:10/q21 How is “prepare” read and explained phonologically?  [وَهَيِّئْ · readings]
turns on: Whether hamza is retained, replaced with yāʾ, or removed by a further weak-letter process.
- 18:10/q21/p1 The common reading retains hamza after the doubled yāʾ. — Samin states this is the majority form; the alternative replaces or deletes the hamza. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:10/q21/p2 Abu Jaʿfar, Shayba and al-Zuhri read two yāʾs without hamza. — Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi say a quiescent hamza is replaced with yāʾ. Qiraat-ER argues the replacement could change the root from h-y-ʾ to h-w-y and shade the sense toward making desirable or inclining. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة والزهري); QIRAAT-ER]
- 18:10/q21/p3 A reading transmitted from ʿAsim lacks hamza by replacement or deletion. — Abu Hayyan cites Ibn Khalawayh's al-Aʿsha from Abu Bakr from ʿAsim: replacement is regular, while deletion is disputed but allowed in imperatives and jussives. Samin says the replacement may be treated as an original weak letter and then deleted, citing a poem; Alusi also gives both routes. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعشى); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة والزهري)]

## 18:10/q22 How is the hamza of “prepare” written?  [وَهَيِّئْ · grammar]
- 18:10/q22/p1 Here it is seated on alif, though common spelling normally follows the preceding vowel. — Farra says Arabs sometimes use alif in every position and reports the ʿAbd Allah codex spelling شيأ and يستهزأون with alif. The more common book pattern uses vowel-conditioned seats, though the alif pattern reflects the hamza's origin. [3 notes: FARRA]

## 18:10/q23 Why are the prepositional phrases placed before the requested object?  [لَنَا مِنْ أَمْرِنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fronting chiefly highlights the beneficiaries and affair or recasts the whole affair as rightness.
- 18:10/q23/p1 Their fronting emphasizes the desired favor and brings it into view early. — Muqatil notes fronting; Alusi says it focuses the petition's object from the outset. Bursevi says both phrases attach to 'prepare.' [3 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:10/q23/p2 The construction rhetorically extracts guidance from their affair, meaning make the whole affair right. — Alusi gives this as another rhetorical analysis; Zamakhshari's comparison to calling someone a lion is reported by Abu Hayyan. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 18:10/q24 What is “our affair” in the prayer?  [أَمْرِنَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the noun points narrowly to their migration or broadly to every aspect of their situation.
- 18:10/q24/p1 It is their flight from unbelievers and their effort to remain faithful. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi and Alusi identify separation from unbelievers; Bursevi adds perseverance in obedience. Ibn Ashur includes faith and refuge, for which God prepared concealment, bodily preservation, long sleep and their later public sign. Tabatabai describes their peril after persecutors killed or coerced believers. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:10/q24/p2 It is their entire situation or all their affairs. — Kashshaf, Baydawi and Nasafi allow the whole affair to be ordered; Razi and Abu Hayyan report making the affair itself guided, with a simile to calling someone a lion. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 18:10/q25 What is the right course they ask God to prepare?  [رَشَدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether right guidance refers chiefly to religious conduct, safe escape, sound judgment, facilitation, or the character of the whole affair.
- 18:10/q25/p1 Guidance to conduct pleasing to God, nearness to Him and steadfast faith. — Tabari links ease in what pleases God, escape from idolatry and sound action, citing 4:6; Zajjaj, Wahidi's Abu Ishaq, Ibn Jawzi, Baghawi, Thalabi, Tabrisi and Nasafi speak of nearness and pleasure. Wahidi's Ibn Abbas asks deeds directed to God's love. Abu Hayyan says steadiness in monotheism; Islahi calls this wise in peril. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:10/q25/p2 A safe escape and deliverance from their immediate peril. — Ibn Abbas, reported by Thalabi, Baghawi, Tabrisi, Qurtubi and Study Quran, says a safe exit from the cave. Nahhas says guidance by which they are saved; Ibn Atiyya calls it a good way out; Tabatabai a path to safety, while Ibn Kathir asks a sound outcome and Biqai deliverance. [12 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 18:10/q25/p3 Correctness, sound judgment and the opposite of error. — Thalabi and Qurtubi preserve 'what is correct'; Wahidi says the root opposes error; Raghib in Tabatabai calls it guidance to the desired end; Asad says consciousness of right; Islahi says guided steadfastness; Ibn Ashur adds benefit and soundness, while Study Quran compares Moses' request for sound judgment. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:10/q25/p4 That God make their whole affair itself rightly guided. — Razi, Nasafi and Zamakhshari in Abu Hayyan offer this strong predication; Alusi calls it rhetorical extraction. Wahidi's 'people of meanings' posit an omitted 'thing of guidance,' then a further ellipsis: prepare what lets us attain guidance. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:10/q25/p5 Facilitation or divine enabling. — Muqatil glosses rushd as facilitation; Sahl in Sulami asks for the easiest route of divine enabling, and Baghawi says make easy. [3 notes: MUQATIL; SULAMI(سهل); BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 18:10/q26 How is “right guidance” read here?  [رَشَدًۭا · readings]
turns on: Both forms convey guidance, but the common form matches the surrounding cadence and has a different usage profile.
- 18:10/q26/p1 The common reading is rashadan, with fatḥa on both consonants. — Zajjaj, Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Samin and Alusi say it matches the verse endings; Abu Hayyan reports Ibn Atiyya's preference. Ibn Ashur says it is the only famous reading here and at 18:24, contrasting 2:256 and 4:6; at 18:66 the cadence favors ḍamma, though Abu Amr and Yaqub have fatḥa. He says fatḥa conveys good, benefit and soundness. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو رجاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو رجاء); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; NAHHAS+; SAMIN-DURR+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 18:10/q26/p2 Abu Raja reads rushdan, with ḍamma on rāʾ and still shīn. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi report it; Alusi says its meaning is the same. Ibn Ashur says this form commonly concerns sound management of property, and contrasts its occurrence at 2:256, 4:6 and 18:66. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو رجاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو رجاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو رجاء); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر)]

## 18:10/q27 What does their prayer teach about dependence on God?  [رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا وَهَيِّئْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw complementary lessons from the appeal rather than dispute its wording.
- 18:10/q27/p1 It models helpless, sincere recourse to God and a request for a good outcome. — Qushayri says they disavow their own power and receive divine care; Tabatabai sees exhausted means; Ibn Kathir cites a transmitted prayer for every decree to end well, and a report from Busr says the Prophet sought good ends and protection from worldly disgrace and afterlife punishment. Study Quran compares the same hadith; Qurtubi says the Prophet prayed when troubled. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan recommend this verse as sufficient for believers' worldly needs; Islahi calls their request wise. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 18:10/q27/p2 Their prayer expresses God's lordship and refusal to invoke another deity. — Thalabi connects it with their declaration that God alone is Lord of heaven and earth and that another invocation would be an outrageous falsehood. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 18:10/q28 How do inward readers understand the youths' state and request?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different inward readings map the story to divine witnessing, embodied development or remembrance.
- 18:10/q28/p1 They receive divine care and pass through loss of self-awareness into sober witnessing. — Qushayri says God shelters them inwardly, effaces their self-awareness in unity and self-sufficiency, then returns them to discrimination; their prayer relinquishes reliance on their own power. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 18:10/q28/p2 Their request seeks the beautiful names, spiritual development and perfection through bodily life. — Qashani reads mercy as gifts fitting their readiness, and guidance as intellectual and practical perfection along a steady course toward God. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 18:10/q28/p3 Their continued life can symbolize remembrance beyond ordinary time. — Study Quran relates this to the inward sense of divine remembrance during their prolonged sleep. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 18:10/q29 What explains the awe and flight associated with seeing the youths later in the story?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether emphasis falls on the divine awe manifested in them or the observer's standpoint.
- 18:10/q29/p1 Divine light, majesty and overpowering spiritual states in them cause the onlooker to flee. — Ibn Ata in Sulami says lights of the Real surround them; Husayn points to their states despite their nearness; Jafar calls the awe God's power, care and guardianship. Another voice distinguishes angelic honoring lights from human outward and inward guidance, comparing Satan fleeing Umar. [4 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الحسين,بعضهم,جعفر)]
- 18:10/q29/p2 Flight follows seeing them from the human self; seeing from God yields unity and firmness. — Jafar contrasts human and divine perspectives and compares the Prophet's flight from unbelievers; Ibn Ata says the Prophet beholds God and is beheld from Him, so merely created sight turns away. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر)]

## 18:10/q30 How does this verse connect to the surrounding account?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the entry prayer to the story's structure, later speech, and the Moses narrative.
- 18:10/q30/p1 It opens a short summary of their flight, prayer, sleep and awakening before the fuller story. — Ibn Ashur says the outline shows their protection as support for faith and that the episode is not God's most astonishing sign. Tabatabai identifies three summary elements, then later discovery and mosque; Islahi says this Quranic narrative pattern protects the main lesson from incidental details. Asad says the prayer previews verse 13 onward. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:10/q30/p2 Their later confidence that God will grant mercy and ease recalls this entry prayer. — Tabatabai links their advice after leaving the people and gods with the gifts first requested on entering the cave. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 18:10/q30/p3 The certainty of the promise of mercy and provision in verse 16 marks it as God's speech. — Islahi rejects exegetes who attribute that promise to the youths: a humble speaker could express it only as prayer or yearning, as in verse 10. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 18:10/q30/p4 Khiḍr's mercy and knowledge and Moses' sound judgment echo the youths' two requests. — Study Quran compares the gift to Khidr at 18:60 and Moses' request for sound judgment with the cave youths' prayer. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 18:10/q31 Why was the Cave story related to the Prophet?  [* · reports]
- 18:10/q31/p1 Quraysh tested him with questions obtained from Jewish scholars. — Abu Hayyan names al-Nadr and Uqba and questions about the youths, a world traveler and the spirit; another report says answering two while withholding one would confirm prophethood. Kuranyolu says Meccans heard the story from Jewish scholars in Medina. Al-Sadiq in Tabatabai says three envoys went to Najran and asked also about Moses' companion, Dhul-Qarnayn and the Hour. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 18:10/q32 What larger themes does the account serve?  [* · links]
turns on: The story bears on resurrection, faith under persecution and the sura's presentation of revelation.
- 18:10/q32/p1 It demonstrates resurrection and the coming Hour. — Kuranyolu says long sleep and awakening make these ideas easier to grasp; the townspeople's recognition is part of the instructive narrative. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:10/q32/p2 It supports faith and places this sign within the sura's larger praise of revelation. — Ibn Ashur says protection supports their faith; Abu Hayyan links this sura's praise of the upright warning Quran, its warning against claims of a divine son and its promise to believers, with the preceding sura's truth of revelation and humility at 17:105. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 18:10/q33 How should the many historical reports about the cave be treated?  [* · reports]
- 18:10/q33/p1 Their particulars diverge, while the Quran selects the details needed for guidance. — Tabatabai lists disagreements on rulers, date, number, dog, names and titles; he attributes them to widespread Christian and Jewish storytelling and Muslim collection of outside reports via converts such as Wahb and Ka'b, later elevated to prophetic reports. The Quran omits ancestry, occupations, locations and names because guidance does not depend on them. Kuranyolu calls number guesses baseless; Christian reports differ on number, dog and sleep duration, and parallels occur in Indian, Talmudic and Christian stories. [11 notes: TABATABAI(أهل الرواية والتاريخ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 18:10/q34 What is the adjacent verse's “raqim”?  [* · links]
turns on: The word may derive from writing, but a transmitted uncertainty leaves a building possible.
- 18:10/q34/p1 It is something written or inscribed, perhaps a book or a stone tablet of the youths' story. — Ibn Kathir prefers Ibn Jarir's derivation from inscription, comparing qatil and jarih. Ibn Abbas and Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd say book, the latter citing 83:9; Sa'id ibn Jubayr describes a stone tablet at the cave entrance. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 18:10/q34/p2 It may be a building rather than a book; its meaning was uncertain to Ibn Abbas. — Ibn Abbas says he knew the Quran except hannan, awwah and raqim, and through Ikrima says he cannot decide between book and building. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 18:10/q35 What does “cave” mean and who are its companions?  [ٱلْكَهْفِ · meaning]
- 18:10/q35/p1 It is a large, broad mountain cave, associated with the long-sleeping youths. — Kuranyolu supplies the lexical size and says the title refers to people said to sleep there for years and awaken; Ibn Ashur says their refuge became their dwelling. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:10/q36 What effect does dropping the hamza after “when” have?  [إِذْ · readings]
- 18:10/q36/p1 It smooths liaison without changing the meaning. — Qiraat-ER describes elision after the dhāl of idh. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 18:10/q37 What is the status of the report about reciting Surat al-Kahf?  [* · reports]
- 18:10/q37/p1 A report promises light for reciting its beginning and end, and greater light for the whole sura. — Thalabi transmits it from Muadh as a saying of the Prophet: head-to-foot light for the ends and earth-to-heaven light for the whole. [1 notes: THALABI(معاذ)]
