# 18:16: 31 questions

## 18:16/q01 What does withdrawing (iʿtizāl) mean here?  [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ · meaning]
- 18:16/q01/p1 It is separation from the people and their worship, inwardly in belief and outwardly by leaving them. — Jishumi defines withdrawal from an affair and cites a poem on avoiding a house for fear of enemies; Tabrisi cites taʿazzul in poetry and Alusi a line about ʿAtika's house for bodily or inward avoidance. Abu Hayyan and Bursevi distinguish belief from physical departure; Tabatabai glosses withdrawal. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 18:16/q02 What does their separation from the people's worship show about that community?  [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ · interpretation]
- 18:16/q02/p1 Their community practiced polytheism. — Biqai infers this from the separate mention of the people and their worship. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:16/q03 Who speaks the advice to withdraw and enter the cave?  [وَإِذِ ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ فَأْوُۥٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the certainty of the promised mercy is fitting for the youths' speech, and how verse 16 resumes the speech of verse 14.
- 18:16/q03/p1 The youths advise one another after deciding to flee. — Muqatil, Yahya, Tabari, Thalabi, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, and others present it as mutual counsel. Ibn Atiyya prefers this; if 18:14 is an audience with the king, an omitted 'some said to others' is needed. Nahhas says one youth addresses the others. Qurtubi and Alusi cite the possibility. The advice was fulfilled, says Alusi. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 18:16/q03/p2 Their leader Tamlīkha or Yamlīkha speaks to them. — Ibn Abbas names him through Tabrisi; Ibn Jawzi calls him Yamlīkha, Wahidi calls Temlikha their leader, and Kashifi places his speech on the road after Decianus gave respite. Study Quran notes variant names Tamlīkha, Yamlīkha, Iamblichus. Qurtubi attributes this to Ibn Atiyya. [8 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 18:16/q03/p3 Their leader Maksalmina urges the retreat. — Al-Ghaznawi's name is reported by Qurtubi. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الغزنوي)]
- 18:16/q03/p4 God instructs them to seek the cave. — Qurtubi records this option. Islahi sees the certain promise as divine speech and the cave guidance as inspiration comparable to God's instruction to Moses' mother; he objects to treating the promise as the youths' own confident words. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 18:16/q04 What prompted the youths' withdrawal and how did they leave?  [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over prior concealment, public confession, and whether the youths coordinated before leaving or recognized each other only in the wilderness.
- 18:16/q04/p1 They fled persecution and forced idolatry, acting together or resolving to leave together. — Thalabi says they feared Daqyanus and took provisions; Ibn Abbas says seven fled by night after an idol-worship summons. Biqai says they lacked force against many opponents. Ibn Kathir, Tabatabai, Islahi, and Kuran Yolu cite threats to faith, stoning, or forced conversion. Tabatabai prefers a public confession at an enforcing assembly, citing a report that six were royal advisers, yet allows earlier concealed belief. Islahi sees imminent stoning and a chosen cave. [13 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]
- 18:16/q04/p2 They left separately, unaware of one another's faith, and pledged common belief when they met outside the city. — Reports of al-Sadiq and Ibn Abbas, cited by Tabatabai, describe separate departures and cautious exchange in the wilderness; Ibn Abbas says God brought them together unexpectedly. Tabatabai notes traditions disagree over planning versus chance meeting. [4 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 18:16/q04/p3 They concealed belief and outwardly conformed before departure. — Al-Sadiq says God rewarded them twice for concealed belief and outward disbelief; other reports mention zunnār belts and festivals. Tabatabai argues their later public declaration or private resolve does not exclude earlier taqiyya. [4 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 18:16/q05 Who accompanied them to the cave?  [إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports]
turns on: Whether reports add a shepherd or field worker to the dog that accompanied the youths.
- 18:16/q05/p1 A dog followed or entered with the youths. — Thalabi says their dog followed their remaining provisions; Kaʿb depicts it repeatedly following, professing love for God's beloved, and offering to guard them; Ubayd ibn Umayr calls it a hunting dog. Kuran Yolu also mentions the dog. [4 notes: THALABI(عبيد بن عمير,كعب الأحبار); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:16/q05/p2 A believing shepherd or field worker joined, bringing his dog. — Ibn Abbas reports the shepherd and his dog joining the seven; Wahb reports a fellow believer working in a field and his dog joining the pursued youths at night. Tabatabai notes this is one disputed report variant. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس,وهب بن منبه); TABATABAI]
- 18:16/q05/p3 Only a dog, rather than a shepherd, joined them. — Tabatabai records this alternative among the divergent accounts. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:16/q06 What happened as they settled in the cave and after they disappeared?  [إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports]
turns on: Reports disagree whether the fugitives remained hidden, were sealed in, or met the king; Ibn Kathir tests the sealing story against the sunlight of 18:17.
- 18:16/q06/p1 They lived there briefly with provisions, then God put them to sleep. — Thalabi says they took remaining supplies; Ibn Abbas says they prayed and praised God while Tamlikha secretly bought food, disguised in poor clothes, and checked news. Ubayd ibn Umayr says they slept 309 years; Wahb says God put them to sleep. Kuran Yolu says God accepted their plea through a long sleep; Elmalili links sleep to 18:10–11 and protection from distress. [7 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس,عبيد بن عمير,وهب بن منبه); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 18:16/q06/p2 The ruler searched but could not find them. — Ibn Kathir compares their concealment to the Prophet and Abu Bakr in Thawr and cites 9:40. Tabatabai records this as one version. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:16/q06/p3 Their people found the sleepers and sealed the cave entrance. — Ibn Kathir rejects this because 18:17 describes sunlight entering morning and evening; other accounts add a shepherd later breaking the wall. Elmalili mentions a walled entrance as one possibility. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 18:16/q06/p4 They met the king and died, or died before he arrived and were then hidden. — Tabatabai lists both variants and says a mosque was later built. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 18:16/q06/p5 The youths' parents said they had spent their money and withdrawn to Mount Yanjalus; the ruler released the parents. — Thalabi gives this account. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 18:16/q07 Where was the cave?  [ٱلْكَهْفِ · reports]
turns on: Archaeological signs, Muslim reports, the story's Roman setting, and the lack of decisive identification.
- 18:16/q07/p1 The cave was at Rujayb near modern Amman. — Tabatabai prefers this candidate after Jordanian excavation, using archaeological signs and some Muslim reports; he notes Amman occupies ancient Philadelphia, a Roman city. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+; TABATABAI-]
- 18:16/q07/p2 The cave was called Hayyum. — Abu Jaʿfar also says the youths were polishers; Qurtubi reports him. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي بن الحسين)]
- 18:16/q07/p3 The cave was near Mount Yanjalus. — Thalabi reports the parents' statement to the ruler. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 18:16/q07/p4 It lay between two mountains. — Al-Suddi's report through Durr is not involved; Durr here cites Mujahid. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 18:16/q07/p5 A cave near Damascus, Petra, Scandinavia, or Nakhchivan is proposed. — Tabatabai lists Mount Qāsiyūn, Petra, a Scandinavian cave with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and a cave near Nakhchivan; he says no site is securely identified and the Scandinavian and Caucasus claims conflict with a Roman setting and Roman power's reach. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]

## 18:16/q08 How does 'what they worship' relate grammatically to 'you withdrew from them'?  [وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā names objects, makes worship a verbal noun, or negates a parenthetical clause.
- 18:16/q08/p1 Mā is a relative pronoun in the accusative, coordinated with the object pronoun: they left the people and their worshipped objects. — Tabari, Zajjaj, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Atiyya as cited by Qurtubi, and Samin explain coordination; Samin notes an omitted object in the relative clause and that the exception can attach to mā or its object pronoun. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 18:16/q08/p2 Mā is infinitival: they left the people's worship, with an implied 'worship' supplying the exception. — Baydawi, Samin, Bursevi, and Alusi give this alternative; Alusi requires the omitted act of worship for a connected exception. Samin says the two possibilities for the exception remain. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:16/q08/p3 Mā is negative, making a parenthetical declaration that the youths worship none but God. — Zamakhshari, Samin, Abu Hayyan, Baydawi, Nasafi, Kashshaf, and Alusi give this divine parenthesis; illā is an unrestricted exception. Alusi says al-Kashf relates it to the following clause. Samin criticizes Abu al-Baqa for wrongly tying connected and disconnected exceptions solely to the negative analysis. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:16/q09 What does 'except God' imply about the people's worship?  [إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the idolaters also worshipped God; Tabatabai and Alusi cite the surrounding passage against that inference.
- 18:16/q09/p1 The exception is connected: the people acknowledged or worshipped God alongside idols, and the youths abandoned only the idols. — Muqatil says they retain knowledge of their Lord and Creator. Baghawi explicitly says the common reading implies worship of God and idols. Ata al-Khurasani and Farra report such shared worship; Kashshaf compares Meccans. Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi, and Alusi make this conditional on the people's practice. [21 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء الخراساني والفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,عطاء الخراساني); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); DURR-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); TABATABAI-]
- 18:16/q09/p2 The exception is disconnected: the people worshipped only idols, so God was never among the objects abandoned. — Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Samin, Bursevi, and Alusi allow this conditional reading. Alusi says it better fits 18:15. Tabatabai prefers it because the passage gives no sign the idolaters worshipped God too. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 18:16/q09/p3 The phrase means 'other than God': they gave up every worshipped object apart from Him, without deciding whether the townspeople worshipped Him. — Qatada's reading is treated this way by Ibn Jawzi and Ibn Atiyya as quoted by Qurtubi; Ibn Masud's explanatory wording makes it explicit. [8 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:16/q09/p4 Their people's coerced submission to God's will and decrees can itself be called worship. — Biqai offers this as a possible account of the wording. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:16/q10 What alternate wording is transmitted for 'what they worship except God'?  [وَمَا يَعْبُدُونَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهَ · readings]
turns on: The wording of codex reports; Abu Hayyan judges both against the universally transmitted consonantal text.
- 18:16/q10/p1 Ibn Masud's codex says 'what they worship besides God.' — Qatada or Ibn Masud is cited across Yahya, Tabari, Thalabi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Durr, and Alusi. Abu Hayyan and Alusi's citation of al-Bahr say it is an explanatory gloss rather than Qurʾān because it conflicts with the transmitted consonantal text. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(عبد الله بن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(في البحر,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 18:16/q10/p2 Some copies instead say 'what they worship besides us.' — Harun reports this Ibn Masud codex variant; Abu Hayyan quotes it, and Alusi also records it. It too is described as an explanatory gloss rather than a canonical reading. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(هارون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(في البحر,قتادة)]

## 18:16/q11 What is the grammatical role of 'when you withdrew' relative to 'take refuge'?  [وَإِذِ ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ فَأْوُۥٓا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether idh can carry a conditional force, or is temporal or causal with an explicit or omitted governor.
- 18:16/q11/p1 The cave command is the response to 'when,' treated like a conditional: when you withdraw, take refuge. — Farra is cited by Wahidi, Razi, Bursevi, and Alusi. Nahhas says the command in turn has 'your Lord will spread' as its answer. Alusi records the objection that idhā without mā is not properly conditional. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL(الفراء); BURSEVI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:16/q11/p2 The cave command reveals an omitted consequence: inward separation calls for bodily withdrawal. — Bursevi and Alusi give this analysis; Alusi reports a grammatical objection that the conditional force is weak without mā. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:16/q11/p3 'When' is temporal, governed by omitted words of speech or an implied command. — Nahhas supplies 'remember'; Samin supplies 'someone said'; Alusi allows temporal attachment to an unstated or stated command but calls Abu al-Baqa's omitted 'they said to one another' astonishing. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:16/q11/p4 'When' is causal: because they withdrew, they are told to take refuge. — Samin reports then rejects this analysis; Alusi allows a causal value linked to the command. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم)-]

## 18:16/q12 What does 'take refuge in the cave' mean?  [فَأْوُۥٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · interpretation]
- 18:16/q12/p1 Go to the cave and make it your place of shelter or dwelling. — Muqatil glosses awā as reaching a place, citing 18:63; Yahya, Wahidi's citation of Farra, Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, and others describe making the cave a home and remaining there. Thalabi says they planned it as a place to worship God; Study Quran says they hid from disbelievers. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 18:16/q13 What does 'spread' mean in the promise?  [يَنشُرْ · meaning]
- 18:16/q13/p1 To extend or expand mercy over them. — Jishumi says nashr opposes folding, as with unfolding a book, reviving the dead, or a pleasant wind spreading blessing; Yahya glosses it as widening, while Wahidi and Alusi say spreading over them. Tabatabai also gives the lexical sense. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 18:16/q14 What mercy is promised to them?  [يَنشُرْ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُم مِّن رَّحْمَتِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the benefits stressed are worldly sustenance and safety, broader divine care, or spiritual provision.
- 18:16/q14/p1 God will expand provision and protective care, including their sleep and relief from danger. — Muqatil calls their sleep the provision. Zajjaj, Ibn Jawzi, Nasafi and Baydawi gloss provision; Ibn Abbas in Wahidi and Tabrisi specifies relief from the king's oppression. Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi include this world and the next; Alusi calls rabb their guardian who guided them to faith. Biqai says God fulfilled the hope by sleep and later awakening. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 18:16/q14/p2 The promise supplies spiritual provision arising from world renunciation and solitude. — Asad takes marfiq as concrete or abstract benefit but here reads it as spiritual provision. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 18:16/q15 Why could they speak so confidently of God's help?  [يَنشُرْ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُم وَيُهَيِّئْ لَكُم · theology]
turns on: Whether certainty is explained by faith, prior prophetic knowledge, or God's direct promise; Alusi rejects prophetic speculation and Islahi rejects a human assurance.
- 18:16/q15/p1 Their trust, hope, and certainty in God gave them confidence. — Kashshaf, Nasafi, Baydawi, Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi, and Tabatabai connect the assurance with trust. Abu Hayyan adds God would not abandon those brought from disbelief to faith. Bursevi cites a hadith on praying with certainty. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 18:16/q15/p2 A contemporary prophet told them, or one youth was a prophet receiving revelation. — Kashshaf offers both alternatives; Nasafi mentions a prophet's prior announcement. Alusi rejects both as conjectures without evidence. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:16/q15/p3 The assured promise is God's own speech or inspiration. — Islahi says certainty marks a divine promise, while a human could only ask or yearn as in 18:10; he compares the guidance to Moses' mother's inspiration. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 18:16/q16 What does 'He will prepare' imply?  [وَيُهَيِّئْ · meaning]
- 18:16/q16/p1 God will arrange and make easy what benefits them. — Ibn Abbas in Abu Hayyan says the king's threats become easy to bear with kindness and comfort; Alusi glosses yuhayyiʾ as making easy and takes its omitted object as good or like benefit. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس)]

## 18:16/q17 How does 'from your affair' attach, and what does 'from' mean?  [مِّنْ أَمْرِكُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether min expresses source, part, or replacement, and whether the phrase attaches to the verb or to marfiqan.
- 18:16/q17/p1 It attaches to 'prepare'; min marks source or a portion of their affair. — Samin gives both source and partitive possibilities, repeated by Alusi. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:16/q17/p2 Min means 'instead of': God prepares relief in place of their difficult situation. — Ibn al-Anbari cites a poem wishing for Zamzam in place of another water; Abu Hayyan notes the same line, and Alusi also invokes 9:38. Ibn Jawzi reports a poetic example of substitute. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري)]
- 18:16/q17/p3 The phrase modifies marfiqan circumstantially, with an omitted governor. — Samin and Alusi record this alternative attachment. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:16/q18 What does mirfaq mean in this promise?  [مِّرْفَقًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the general useful comfort is specified as food, escape, or replacement of hardship.
- 18:16/q18/p1 A useful means of comfort, ease, support, or beneficial accommodation. — Abu Ubayda, Akhfash, Tabari, Thalabi, and many later authors define practical support; Tabari ties it to distress and fear for life and faith. Mawrdi also offers spacious relief, and Tabatabai gentle treatment. Islahi calls it anything useful, Asad concrete or abstract benefit. Lexicographers also use it for the elbow, the bodily support joint. [20 notes: MAJAZ; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الأخفش); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي والحسن وطلحة والأعمش وابن أبي إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QIRAAT-ER]
- 18:16/q18/p2 Specifically food or provisions, even breakfast. — Kalbi in Wahidi glosses breakfast, Suddi in Durr says food; Wahidi Wajiz says an easy source of food and Thalabi describes a comfortable provision. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DURR-FULL(السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:16/q18/p3 A way out, rescue, or deliverance. — Mawardi offers rescue; Wahidi reports others' gloss as an exit. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 18:16/q18/p4 A replacement of their hardship with a better situation. — Ibn al-Anbari is cited by Ibn Jawzi and Abu Hayyan, with a poetic use of substitute. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري)]

## 18:16/q19 How is mirfaq read?  [مِّرْفَقًۭا · readings]
turns on: Regional and transmitted vocalization; sources disagree on some individual reader attributions, but Tabari accepts both main forms.
- 18:16/q19/p1 Kasra on mīm and fatḥa on fāʾ: mirfaq. — Five canonical readers Ibn Kathir, Abu Amr, Asim, Hamza and Kisai are repeatedly listed; Ibn Atiyya adds Hasan, Talha, Aʿmash, and Ibn Abi Ishaq. Tabari prefers this Iraqi form as common and eloquent, while accepting both. Farra says Aʿmash and Hasan use it; Abu Hayyan and Razi give transmission variants for Abu Amr or Asim. [19 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي والحسن وطلحة والأعمش وابن أبي إسحاق); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر والأعرج وشيبة وحميد وابن سعدان ونافع وابن عامر وأبو بكر وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر); TAB-FULL+]
- 18:16/q19/p2 Fatḥa on mīm and kasra on fāʾ: marfiq. — Nafi and Ibn Amir, with Medina and Syria, recur in the lists; Farra also names Medina's readers and Asim, Baghawi adds Abu Jaʿfar, and Abu Hayyan and Alusi list Abu Jaʿfar, Aʿraj, Shayba, Humayd, Ibn Saʿdan and one Abu Amr report. Ibn Mujahid and Farisi report an Abu Bakr or Kisai transmission; Baydawi calls the form an anomalous verbal noun. [23 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي,نافع وابن عامر); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); NASAFI-FULL(المدني والشامي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر والأعرج وشيبة وحميد وابن سعدان ونافع وابن عامر وأبو بكر وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر)]
- 18:16/q19/p3 Both mīm and fāʾ may be open. — Muʿadh permits this as a verbal noun in Abu Hayyan and Samin; Alusi also records it. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(معاذ); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:16/q20 Do the mirfaq forms differ in sense or formation?  [مِّرْفَقًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the vocalizations are equivalent dialects or mark different nouns; reports conflict even about Kisai's and Farra's usage.
- 18:16/q20/p1 Both are dialect forms usable for a helpful thing and, in many accounts, an elbow. — Farra cites a rajaz pairing the forms; Zajjaj, Qutrub, Abu al-Hasan, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, and Alusi allow both. Akhfash and Abu al-Hasan explain a noun like masjid, a dialect form, or rifq; Razi says derived from irtifāq. Farra says most Arabs prefer kasra for both, though some use fatḥa. Samin disputes a report that Farra knew only kasra by citing the transmitted Nafi and Syrian readings. [26 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(الشاعر,الفراء,نحويي أهل البصرة); ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الأصمعي وقطرب,الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي والحسن وطلحة والأعمش وابن أبي إسحاق); TABRISI(أبو الحسن,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر والأعرج وشيبة وحميد وابن سعدان ونافع وابن عامر وأبو بكر وأبو عمرو,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفَرّاء,ثعلب,مكي); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 18:16/q20/p2 The vowels distinguish the aid or facility from the body's elbow, though sources assign them differently. — Ibn Khalawayh, Abu Ubayda, Yunus, Farra on Hijaz speech, and Ibn Jawzi associate kasra with the elbow or comfort in differing ways; Kisai is quoted both as accepting only kasra for elbow (Tabari/Razi) and only open mīm (Abu Hayyan/Samin/Alusi). Abu Hatim makes the open form a place noun, Abu Zayd a verbal noun. Mawrdi distinguishes received benefit from benefit extended; Qiraat-ER notes instrumental, locative, or nominal patterns. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(الفراء,الكسائي); ZAJJAJ(الأصمعي); NAHHAS(الأخفش سعيد,الكسائي والفراء); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,أبو علي,يونس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو زيد,الفراء,الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو حاتم وأبو زيد,الكسائي,ثعلب); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); QIRAAT-ER]
- 18:16/q20/p3 Only the kasra-on-mīm form is known or fluent across senses. — Asmaʿi says he knows only kasra; Nahhas reports him across matter, hand, and all senses. Farra is reported similarly by Abu Hayyan and Alusi, though other Farra reports allow both. Nahhas calls kasra the fluent form; Samin objects that Nafi and Syrian transmission proves open mīm exists. [5 notes: NAHHAS(الأصمعي,الكسائي والفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي وقطرب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-]

## 18:16/q21 Why is 'for you' placed before the promised provision?  [لَكُم وَيُهَيِّئْ لَكُم · rhetoric]
- 18:16/q21/p1 It first signals that the coming provision benefits them and builds anticipation. — Alusi explains the fronting and links it to the youths' trust, hope, and certainty. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:16/q22 What does their flight teach about migration or seclusion under persecution?  [وَإِذِ ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ فَأْوُۥٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · law]
turns on: Whether the specific flight from coercion supports a general rule of solitary life.
- 18:16/q22/p1 Fleeing to protect faith is warranted when remaining demands disbelief or endangers belief. — Tabrisi and Alusi condemn staying where one must profess disbelief. Ibn Kathir cites the sheep-in-mountains hadith for escape from trials but says ordinary seclusion forfeits communal prayers. Tabatabai sees their escape from forced conversion and stoning, allowing earlier taqiyya. Biqai notes they were outnumbered. [7 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:16/q22/p2 The verse commends general withdrawal or isolation from the world. — Bursevi's quoted continuation praises the pleasant corner of seclusion; inward authorities take retreat as spiritual discipline. Islahi objects that the youths faced mortal danger and cites the Prophet's refuge in Thawr, not monastic withdrawal. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض العارفين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]

## 18:16/q23 What inward meaning is given to leaving the people and entering the cave?  [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ · inward]
turns on: Whether the cave signifies solitary devotion, bodily discipline, or the path of divine union.
- 18:16/q23/p1 Leave everything besides God, including bad company and desires, and retreat into solitude with Him. — Qushayri says detachment yields union and divine shelter; Bursevi cites al-Hujandi's line that union follows severance. Alusi compares the Prophet's solitude in Hira. Bursevi describes sincere reliance and taking guidance from a perfected shaykh; his Mathnawi couplet warns solitary travel breeds conceit and error, while the cave youths' direct divine training was rare. A hadith about God's direct education of the Prophet illustrates the exception. [12 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(الحجندي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(بعض العارفين)]
- 18:16/q23/p2 Leave attachment to self and faculties; the body becomes a disciplined cave for knowledge, action, and transformation. — Qashani calls it voluntary death to lower impulses; 6:122 supplies the image of life and light after death, and a saying about Abu Bakr as a dead man walking means death to self. His alternative describes abandoning idols and desires, limiting bodily excess, receiving celestial aid, and guiding others; he also associates the cave with the Mahdi before emergence and Jesus's descent. [5 notes: QASHANI(عليه السلام)]
- 18:16/q23/p3 Special mercy is God's drawing seekers into His attributes, and the prepared benefit is a path to union. — Bursevi transmits the Najmiyya distinction between special favor and general mercy shared by believers, unbelievers, jinn, humans, and animals. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 18:16/q24 Does separation in belief require social separation or affect marriage?  [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ · law]
- 18:16/q24/p1 Inward or doctrinal separation entails physical separation; marriage between Sunnis and Muʿtazilites is impermissible. — Bursevi invokes Rasteghfani's ruling and cites his explicit answer on marriage. [2 notes: BURSEVI(رستغفني)]

## 18:16/q25 How are this promise and the cave episode linked to other verses?  [يَنشُرْ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُم وَيُهَيِّئْ لَكُم مِّنْ أَمْرِكُم مِّرْفَقًۭا · links]
turns on: Which cited passage best illuminates the promise: the earlier prayer, God's provision rule, or another cave refuge.
- 18:16/q25/p1 The promised mercy and ease answer the youths' earlier request for mercy and right guidance in 18:10. — Tabatabai explicitly equates the gifts; Study Quran says the ease answers the request for sound judgment. Kuran Yolu says God accepted the prayer by causing long sleep; Elmalili links sleep to 18:10–11. [4 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 18:16/q25/p2 God's aid here accords with al-Talaq's promise to provide for those who fear Him and move toward Him. — Islahi cites 65:2–3 for God's supplying needed means. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:16/q25/p3 The refuge recalls the Prophet and Abu Bakr in Thawr, though Ibn Kathir ranks that story greater. — Ibn Kathir quotes the Prophet's reassurance and 9:40; Study Quran cites the Hijrah parallel and Islahi uses Thawr as an example of refuge from danger. Study Quran adds that the cave in both episodes symbolizes the heart's interior and miraculous sanctuary. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:16/q26 What wider lesson does their reliance on God convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 18:16/q26/p1 Those who abandon idolatry for God may expect protection, needed means, and eventual mercy. — Qushayri links sincere reliance to divine shelter; Biqai says God protects whoever takes refuge in Him, proved by their long sleep and awakening. Islahi connects initiative and trust with 65:2–3; Elmalili says faith's light leads to mercy and success. Tabatabai says the Quran selects lessons for guidance rather than a full historical record. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 18:16/q27 How does the Qurʾanic account relate to later historical reports?  [* · reports]
- 18:16/q27/p1 The reports vary widely, while the Qurʾān selects details needed for guidance. — Tabatabai lists differences over disclosure to the ruler, joint plans, shepherd and dog, discovery and death, dates, rulers, lineage, names, sleepers' number and duration. He says Christian and People-of-the-Book circulation and Muslim transmission through converts such as Wahb and Kaʿb reshaped accounts. He outlines the Quranic sequence: withdrawal and long sleep, awakening and exposure, discovery and a mosque. Names, occupations, dates and dog identity are omitted as irrelevant to guidance. Most historians place the story between Jesus and Muhammad; Christian versions differ from Muslim ones. [11 notes: TABATABAI(أهل الرواية والتاريخ)]

## 18:16/q28 What was the Raqim connected with their disappearance?  [* · reports]
- 18:16/q28/p1 A lead tablet on which the king recorded their names and predicted their great destiny. — Ibn Abbas and another report in Durr say the tablet was kept in the ruler's treasury; both connect it to 18:9. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 18:16/q29 What other report concerns the youths' age or status?  [* · reports]
- 18:16/q29/p1 The youths illustrate that prophets were sent and scholars received learning while young. — Ibn Abbas is cited by Alusi, with Abraham, Moses, and the cave youths as examples from 21:60, 18:60, and 18:13. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 18:16/q30 Why is the name Muʿtazila connected with withdrawal?  [ٱعْتَزَلْتُمُوهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Which historical act of separation gave the group its name.
- 18:16/q30/p1 The name refers to leaving al-Hasan's circle, associated with ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd. — Jishumi says it followed al-Hasan's death; Tabrisi relates it to ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd's circle leaving al-Hasan's gathering. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]
- 18:16/q30/p2 The name means rejecting innovations and following the early community. — Jishumi records this alternative derivation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 18:16/q31 What recitation change affects the hamza of 'take refuge'?  [فَأْوُۥٓا۟ · readings]
- 18:16/q31/p1 Substitution of yāʾ for hamza softens the glottal stop without changing meaning. — Qiraat-ER describes the sound change. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
