# 68:24: 17 questions

## 68:24/q01 How is أن لا يدخلنها connected to their whispering?  [أَن لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether أن explains an implied utterance or introduces a verbal noun, and whether the reading without it requires an implied قول or lets يتخافتون act as speech.
- 68:24/q01/p1 أن introduces an explanation of the speech implicit in their whispering. — Naḥḥās says the answer lies in the earlier أن and that يتخافتون already implies secret speech. Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr take the clause as explanatory; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses that تخافت implies speech without quoting it. [8 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:24/q01/p2 أن introduces a verbal-noun clause conveying the content or purpose of their whispering. — Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn allow this alongside the explanatory parse. Ālūsī says an omitted ب is understood before the verbal noun. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:24/q01/p3 Without أن, an unspoken verb of saying or قول introduces the quoted prohibition. — Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī supply a speech verb for the reading without أن. Abū Ḥayyān also gives this analysis; Samīn calls it the Basran analysis, and Ālūsī reports the supplied قول. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله وابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله وابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:24/q01/p4 Without أن, يتخافتون itself governs the quoted prohibition because it conveys speech. — Naḥḥās says the particle may be left out because يتخافتون means speaking secretly. Abū Ḥayyān offers this alternative; Samīn identifies it as Kufan analysis, also reported by Ālūsī. [4 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله وابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله وابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL]

## 68:24/q02 How is the opening of the prohibition read?  [أَن لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا · readings]
turns on: The reported forms differ in the presence of أن; Samīn's quoted form also lacks the emphatic ن found in Ibn ʿAṭiyya's and Abū Ḥayyān's quoted form.
- 68:24/q02/p1 The cited verse reads أن لا يدخلنها, with أن and the emphatic ن. — The wording quoted by the verse and discussed by Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī has أن; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that the emphatic ن reinforces the owners' oath. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:24/q02/p2 Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh and Ibn Abī ʿAbla are reported to read لا يدخلنها without أن. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī name both readers; Rāzī names Ibn Masʿūd. Ālūsī says the omission supports the explanatory parse of the wording with أن. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن أبي عبلة); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله وابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:24/q02/p3 Samīn reports Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh and Ibn Abī ʿAbla reading لا يدخلها, omitting both أن and the emphatic ن. — Samīn gives the wording لا يدخلها and offers Basran ellipsis of قول or Kufan treatment of يتخافتون as speech. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله وابن أبي عبلة)]

## 68:24/q03 What did the owners resolve by saying no poor person should enter?  [لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا ٱلْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكُم مِّسْكِينٌۭ · interpretation]
- 68:24/q03/p1 They meant to keep poor people out of the garden that day and deny them any of its harvest. — Mujāhid, Muqātil, Ibn Qutayba, Yaḥyā ibn Sallām in Māwardī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Ṭabrisī give the exclusion as their private resolve. Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, Elmalılı and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say secrecy kept the poor from hearing or noticing and claiming fruit. Elmalılı says they intended to finish the harvest and leave nothing. Biqāʿī says they planned to search through the day so no poor person could reach fruit or an opening. Bursevī says they barred the poor. [20 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 68:24/q04 Why is the ban phrased as a poor person's not entering?  [لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا عَلَيْكُم · rhetoric]
- 68:24/q04/p1 The phrasing emphatically orders the owners themselves to admit no poor person, rather than addressing the absent poor. — Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain the intended prohibition as not letting anyone in. Bayḍāwī calls this hyperbolic; Ālūsī compares لا أرينك ههنا. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the emphatic ن reinforces their oath and each brother is to admit none. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:24/q04/p2 The emphatic wording also anticipates the outcome: no poor person entered, although the owners' scheme failed in the way this came about. — Al-Zarkashī says the emphatic prediction was fulfilled despite the failure of the owners' plan and expectation. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 68:24/q05 What does miskīn denote here?  [مِّسْكِينٌۭ · meaning]
- 68:24/q05/p1 A person with nothing, more destitute than a faqīr. — Bursevī supplies this lexical distinction while explaining whom the owners excluded. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 68:24/q06 What claim did poor people have on this harvest?  [مِّسْكِينٌۭ · law]
turns on: Jishumī transmits two specifications of the poor person's due, a tenth or fruit falling during harvest; other notes establish a share without fixing its amount.
- 68:24/q06/p1 They had a rightful share of the harvest, without a quantity fixed in these notes. — Jishumī calls it an enforceable due. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, says the father gave every rightful person a share and his sons withheld an abundant harvest from Muslim poor. Asad cites the long-standing understanding of a harvest share and 6:141. Study Quran cites both ancient Near Eastern gleaning custom and the Quranic command to give the harvest due on harvest day (6:141). Elmalılı says God and needy servants had rights in the orchard. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]
- 68:24/q06/p2 The poor person's due was a tenth of the harvest. — Jishumī reports this as one identification of their enforceable due. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 68:24/q06/p3 The poor person's due was fruit that fell during picking. — Jishumī reports this as another identification of their enforceable due; Study Quran separately notes a custom of gleaning. [2 notes: JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN]

## 68:24/q07 Why did the owners whisper their plan?  [* · interpretation]
- 68:24/q07/p1 They wanted to keep the poor or other listeners from hearing the plan and claiming fruit. — Ibn Qutayba and Baghawī describe private whispering. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn Kathīr explicitly say the poor or anyone else were not to hear; Elmalılı likewise says no one was to hear. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they consulted quietly lest the poor notice and claim a share. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ explains تخافت as whispering to one another so outsiders would not hear. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; MUJAHID(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## 68:24/q08 What do reports say about the garden's owners and their decision?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in the owners' provenance, the number and conduct of the sons, and whether the place is specified.
- 68:24/q08/p1 They were Ethiopian sons who called their charitable father foolish and vowed to exclude the poor after his death. — ʿIkrima, reported by Ṭabarī, says their father fed the poor from his garden. The Ethiopian identification varies from reports that specify another provenance or none. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 68:24/q08/p2 They were sons of a charitable old man who opposed his charity and excluded the poor after he died. — Qatāda, reported by Ṭabarī, gives this account without naming the place or number of sons. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة)]
- 68:24/q08/p3 Five sons inherited from a father who gave everyone their due; four planned to deny Muslim poor an unusually abundant crop, and the moderate brother objected. — Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, gives the number, the unusual abundance, the intended recipients and the dissent. [1 notes: TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 68:24/q08/p4 Exegetical elaboration makes the garden the estate of a righteous man left to his people and town. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this elaboration and notes variant provenance reports: near Ṣanʿāʾ in Yemen, Abyssinia, or among People of Scripture. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 68:24/q09 Where do reports place the garden or its people?  [* · reports]
turns on: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports alternative provenance traditions; ʿIkrima specifically calls the owners Ethiopians.
- 68:24/q09/p1 The owners were Ethiopians or the garden was in Abyssinia. — ʿIkrima in Ṭabarī calls the owners Ethiopians; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports Abyssinia as one proposed setting. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); BINTSHATI]
- 68:24/q09/p2 The garden was in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this as one proposed setting. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 68:24/q09/p3 The garden's people belonged to the People of Scripture. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this as another provenance account. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 68:24/q10 How does the next verse's ḥard characterize their plan?  [* · links]
turns on: Qushayrī reports ability and anger as alternatives; Bursevī brings both into his account of ḥard.
- 68:24/q10/p1 They thought themselves capable of carrying out the exclusion. — Qushayrī offers ability as one interpretation of ḥard. Bursevī says they thought they could deny the poor their share, yet achieved only their own deprivation. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]
- 68:24/q10/p2 They approached the poor with angry, forceful prevention. — Qushayrī offers anger at the poor as another interpretation. Bursevī defines ḥard as forceful prevention accompanied by anger. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]

## 68:24/q11 What should the owners have remembered instead of excluding the poor?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: These notes emphasize different neglected duties: invoking God, honoring needy people's rights, and preserving mutual trust.
- 68:24/q11/p1 They should have made an exception by saying إن شاء الله and remembered God. — Farrāʾ explains the justest brother's later call to tasbīḥ this way, invoking the instruction at 18:24 to remember God after forgetting. [1 notes: FARRA]
- 68:24/q11/p2 They should have remembered God's gift and protection of the orchard and the rights of God and needy people in it. — Elmalılı says God gave and guarded the orchard while they slept; gratitude called for generosity, sharing the crop and cultivating beneficiaries and well-wishers rather than enemies through greed and despotism. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 68:24/q11/p3 They should have remembered their own reliance on oaths and mutual trust the previous day. — Elmalılı says they had needed oaths to reassure each other and establish their company; the yield of the orchard would likewise depend on trust. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 68:24/q12 What does their exclusion teach about its consequences and social wrong?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw complementary lessons about the owners' ensuing deprivation and the social wrong of withholding the poor's due.
- 68:24/q12/p1 Their attempt to deprive the poor turned into their own loss and deprivation. — Bursevī says they thought they could refuse the poor their share but only deprived themselves. Al-Kashshāf, reported by Bursevī, says they intended to distress the poor although able to benefit them, then lost their wealth and suffered deprivation. Al-Zarkashī observes that their ban was fulfilled literally, since no poor person entered, but the manner of its fulfillment defeated their plan and expectation. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الكشاف); BURHAN]
- 68:24/q12/p2 Denying the poor their due is a social sin that exposes the owners' stinginess. — Asad calls denial of the poor's right the parable's second, specifically social, sin and connects it to 68:10–13. Study Quran says exclusion violated an ancient Near Eastern gleaning custom and the harvest-day command of 6:141. Iṣlāḥī says this whispered ban repeats the stinginess signaled in 68:18. Elmalılı calls their plan extreme greed and says gratitude required sharing rather than making enemies through greed and despotism. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]

## 68:24/q13 Which other verses illuminate the ban on the poor?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources point to different textual connections: the earlier characterization, the later response, the social critique and the harvest command.
- 68:24/q13/p1 Verse 18 already signals the owners' stinginess, which the whispered ban makes explicit. — Iṣlāḥī points to 68:18 as the earlier signal. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 68:24/q13/p2 Verses 10–13 share the parable's concern with social wrongdoing. — Asad connects the resolve to deny the poor with 68:10 and 68:13 as the parable's second, social sin. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 68:24/q13/p3 Quran 6:141 states the poor's harvest due. — Asad invokes 6:141 for the anciently recognized harvest share; Study Quran cites its command to give the due on harvest day. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:24/q13/p4 Quran 18:24 illuminates the later call to tasbīḥ as remembrance of God and saying إن شاء الله. — Farrāʾ cites the instruction to remember God after forgetting. [1 notes: FARRA]

## 68:24/q14 How is أن لا written in this verse's muṣḥaf text?  [أَن لَّا · readings]
- 68:24/q14/p1 Its two elements are written separately, with the nūn of أن retained; this is one of ten agreed instances of separated أن لا. — Al-Zarkashī says أن لا is ordinarily joined but separated at ten places where certainty and necessity are apparent, including this verse. Ibn al-Jazarī lists أن لا among split forms and counts this verse among ten agreed separate spellings. Ibn al-Anbārī's authorities count ten such forms written with nūn and note codex variation at 21:87. Al-Suyūṭī also lists this verse among ten exceptions to the usual joined spelling. [5 notes: BURHAN; NASHR(أبو بكر بن الأنباري); ITQAN]

## 68:24/q15 What does separation of أن لا imply for pausing between its parts?  [أَن لَّا · readings]
- 68:24/q15/p1 Under the general rule for written-separated compounds, a pause can be made at either component. — Ibn al-Jazarī states the general rule and lists أن لا among the split forms requiring attention. He identifies this verse as one of ten agreed locations where أن لا is separated. [3 notes: NASHR]

## 68:24/q16 How is the definite article treated in writing and pause?  [ٱلْيَوْمَ · readings]
- 68:24/q16/p1 The article's lām is normally written as a mark of definiteness, and the article is an exception to pausing at components of a compound. — Al-Zarkashī says written definiteness should clarify and expose a word; he relates concealment in semantically obscure forms to three kinds of darkness. Ibn al-Jazarī excludes the frequent definite article from the general license to pause at either component of separately written compounds. [2 notes: BURHAN; NASHR]

## 68:24/q17 Is ghunna pronounced when the nūn of أن assimilates into the lām of لا?  [أَن لَّا · readings]
turns on: Whether assimilation into lām suppresses ghunna even for an originally mobile nūn, and whether reports of ghunna refer to a non-assimilated mode.
- 68:24/q17/p1 Ghunna is pronounced despite the written form; showing it before lām is also supported by analogy with silent nūn and tanwīn. — Ibn al-Jazarī quotes a reader who clearly pronounced ghunna whether or not nūn was written. He argues that readers who show ghunna on silent nūn and tanwīn before lām or rāʾ should analogously show it on a mobile nūn that becomes silent through assimilation. [1 notes: NASHR; NASHR-]
- 68:24/q17/p2 Ghunna is omitted in the assimilated form, including when the nūn was originally mobile. — Ibn al-Jazarī says omission of written nūn in some forms reflects a dialect without pronounced ghunna. He learned the ghunna reading from some teachers but usually does not adopt it; for Abū ʿAmr he reads both silent and mobile nūn without ghunna and suggests that ghunna reports may concern only a non-assimilated mode. [3 notes: NASHR; NASHR+; NASHR-]
