# 35:37: 37 questions

## 35:37/q01 Who are the people crying in the Fire?  [وَهُمْ · referent]
- 35:37/q01/p1 The disbelievers condemned to Hell, continuing the people of 35:36. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the pronoun to the disbelievers of 35:36 and coordinates this clause with their having the Fire, rather than making it circumstantial; Study Quran likewise describes disbelievers facing the judgment they denied. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 35:37/q02 What does يَصْطَرِخُونَ mean?  [يَصْطَرِخُونَ · meaning]
- 35:37/q02/p1 They cry loudly and intensely for help or rescue in Hell. — Qatāda and Ibn Jurayj gloss it as seeking help. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bayḍāwī connect the plea to ṣarākh, a loud cry; al-Thaʿlabī says ṣārikh can name either supplicant or helper, while al-Qurṭubī distinguishes ṣārikh from muṣrikh and cites a poem about a frightened caller and hoofbeats. Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the verb's intensity and the sufferers' helplessness. Al-Bursawī also says it is used for seeking intercession. Their following plea explains the cry, according to al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [20 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 35:37/q03 How is the form يَصْطَرِخُونَ derived?  [يَصْطَرِخُونَ · grammar]
- 35:37/q03/p1 It is an intensive iftiʿāl form from ṣarākh or ṣurākh, with the form's tāʾ changed to ṭāʾ near emphatic ṣād. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya explain the nearby articulation and emphatic matching; al-Wāḥidī cites iṣṭarakha and taṣārakhū. Al-Bursawī describes the inserted emphatic letter as strengthening the cry. Al-Samīn's note describes the consonant change differently, saying tāʾ changes to ṣād before ṭāʾ. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the intensive form with yaṣrukhūn. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:37/q04 To whom are their cries for help directed?  [يَصْطَرِخُونَ فِيهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the cry is addressed to God, as the ensuing rabbana indicates, or exchanged among the damned.
- 35:37/q04/p1 They call on God to rescue them. — Al-Ālūsī reports Qatāda's account and rejects the suggestion that they call to one another in bewilderment; the address rabbana supports their appeal to God. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)+]
- 35:37/q04/p2 They call to one another in bewilderment. — Al-Ālūsī records this suggestion only to reject it. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)-]

## 35:37/q05 What do the condemned ask to do if brought out?  [رَبَّنَآ أَخْرِجْنَا نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا · interpretation]
- 35:37/q05/p1 They ask to leave Hell, return to worldly life, and replace their former conduct with faith and righteous obedience. — Muqātil specifies abandoning association of partners; Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the promised deed as professing lā ilāha illā Allāh; al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī specify belief for disbelief and obedience for sin. Al-Bursawī says deeds require faith; al-Qurṭubī adds following messengers. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the promise belated repentance, with an implicit condition of release. Al-Rāzī says a captive progresses from hope, to request, to a promise of reform. Study Quran relates the plea to 32:12, 19:38, 67:11, 40:11, 6:27, 7:53 and 26:102. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 35:37/q06 How is the plea beginning رَبَّنَآ attached syntactically?  [رَبَّنَآ أَخْرِجْنَا · grammar]
- 35:37/q06/p1 An implied verb of saying introduces the quoted plea; it can explain the shouting, stand independently, or describe their state. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies omitted 'they say'; al-Samīn allows it as specification of what is shouted or a circumstantial phrase. Al-Ālūsī also reports coordination with prior speech or an independent explanatory sentence. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:37/q07 How does نَعْمَلْ depend on أَخْرِجْنَا?  [أَخْرِجْنَا نَعْمَلْ · grammar]
- 35:37/q07/p1 The jussive 'we shall do' answers the request, implying 'if You bring us out, we shall do good.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the jussive as the answer to the plea and the understood condition of release. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:37/q08 How can صَٰلِحًا غَيْرَ ٱلَّذِى كُنَّا نَعْمَلُ be parsed?  [صَٰلِحًا غَيْرَ ٱلَّذِى كُنَّا نَعْمَلُ · grammar]
turns on: Which omitted noun the two accusatives describe, or whether ghayra is the direct object.
- 35:37/q08/p1 Both ṣāliḥan and ghayra qualify an omitted verbal noun or omitted object. — Al-Samīn gives the two ellipsis options, and al-Ālūsī transmits Abū al-Baqāʾ's parallel parses. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 35:37/q08/p2 Ṣāliḥan qualifies an omitted verbal noun, while ghayra is the object of 'we do.' — Al-Samīn and Abū al-Baqāʾ, as reported by al-Ālūsī, offer this alternative. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 35:37/q09 What does 'other than what we used to do' add to their plea?  [غَيْرَ ٱلَّذِى كُنَّا نَعْمَلُ · rhetoric]
- 35:37/q09/p1 It heightens their regret and admits that their former deeds were corrupt, even though they once mistook those deeds for good. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn citing al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī explain that the apparent suggestion of other righteous deeds disappears once their mistaken self-assessment is recognized; al-Samīn cites 32:12 and 18:104. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they now know their past deeds were false and harmful. Al-Rāzī says the qualification exposes their former corruption, while al-Biqāʿī allows admission of error or regretful recognition. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 35:37/q10 What is the force of the question أَوَلَمْ نُعَمِّرْكُم?  [أَوَلَمْ نُعَمِّرْكُم · rhetoric]
- 35:37/q10/p1 It answers and rebukes their plea by confirming that God gave them ample time to reflect, repent, and heed warning. — Abū ʿUbayda calls it rhetorical confirmation, not information-seeking; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it censure. Al-Ṭabarī addresses the rebuke to Quraysh; al-Biqāʿī adds delayed punishment and reason, with the past tense closing the register of reflection at Resurrection. Al-Nasafī says longer lives intensify the rebuke. Abū Ḥayyān reports confirmation and reminder, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says taste and no-helper follow from this answer. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr stress that the time allowed any willing person to benefit. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:37/q11 What does نُعَمِّرْكُم contribute?  [نُعَمِّرْكُم · meaning]
- 35:37/q11/p1 God prolonged their worldly life enough for reflection and amendment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr defines taʿmīr as prolonging life, citing 2:96 and 35:11; al-Bursawī says reflection is opportunity to consider one's condition and amend it; al-Jishumī contrasts this worldly opportunity with its absence after death. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]

## 35:37/q12 How should مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ be construed?  [مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether fīhi requires mā to be a noun, can refer to an implied age, or permits a temporal verbal noun; the negative parse is rejected on wording and sense.
- 35:37/q12/p1 Mā is an indefinite noun meaning a span or age, qualified by the clause 'in which one remembers.' — Al-Samīn prefers this because fīhi can refer back to a noun. Al-Ālūsī takes it as the years in which anyone willing can reflect; al-Bursawī allows a period or time generally, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause describes adequate time for instruction. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 35:37/q12/p2 Mā is a temporal verbal noun or temporal adverbial, expressing the duration for reflection. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, the Shaykh cited by al-Samīn, and Abū Ḥayyān as reported by al-Ālūsī give this construal; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the related phrase a masdar-based temporal adverb. Al-Samīn objects that fīhi lacks a nominal antecedent, except on al-Akhfash's and Ibn al-Sarrāj's allowance; al-Ālūsī says it may instead refer to implied 'age.' [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 35:37/q12/p3 Mā is negative. — Ibn al-Ḥājib, cited by al-Ālūsī, calls this untenable in both wording and meaning. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب)-]

## 35:37/q13 What age or span counts as enough life to remember?  [نُعَمِّرْكُم مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ مَن تَذَكَّرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase identifies a fixed age, the start of mature responsibility, a final age before decline, or a sufficient opportunity; reports differ in numbers, and al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr assess the sixty-year transmissions differently.
- 35:37/q13/p1 Sixty years is sufficient and removes a person's excuse. — Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī and many others transmit sixty; the Prophet is reported to say God has left no excuse after sixty. Al-Wāḥidī prefers it on Abū Hurayra's report; Ibn Kathīr prefers its sounder Ibn ʿAbbās route and Bukhārī's authentication against al-Ṭabarī's reservation. Reports describe a Resurrection caller asking for sixty-year-olds, though Ibn Kathīr questions one route through Ibrāhīm ibn al-Faḍl. Al-Khaṭṭābī explains sixty as near death and suited to repentance. Sources also give sixty or seventy in a Prophetic wording; al-Bukhārī titles a chapter for sixty. Al-Biqāʿī cites al-Bukhārī's opening of al-Riqāq and Aḥmad ibn Manīʿ; al-Ālūsī cites al-Ḥākim and Sahl ibn Saʿd. [61 notes: MUJAHID(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن ابن عباس,علي); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي,علي بن أبي طالب); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); TABRISI(أمير المؤمنين,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,البخاري,الخطابي,علي وابن عباس وأبو هريرة); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سهل بن سعد,علي,مجاهد); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(علي وابن عباس); ELMALILI(Peygamber); TABATABAI(ابن عباس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,علي عليه السلام); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 35:37/q13/p2 Forty years is the significant threshold for mature reflection. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers forty because a sixty-year transmission has a transmitter requiring caution and understanding matures at forty, diminishing before and after. Al-Qurṭubī calls it sound and cites 46:15. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a good view and cites reports about reaching forty without repentance and a poem on forty without modesty. Masrūq urges caution then; Medina worshippers and Mālik's townspeople are reported to shift toward worship at forty. Al-Ḥasan and al-Kalbī also specify this age. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مسروق); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومسروق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,منصور عن هلال); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، مسروق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشاعر); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن البصري ومسروق)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 35:37/q13/p3 Eighteen years suffices for the rebuke. — Qatāda says an eighteen-year-old was included when the verse was revealed; ʿAṭāʾ, Wahb, Abū al-ʿĀliya, al-Kalbī and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās or al-Bāqir are also cited. Al-Bursawī says duty intensifies around eighteen or twenty. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة والكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء ووهب بن منبه وأبو العالية وقتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الباقر عليه السلام)]
- 35:37/q13/p4 The threshold begins at puberty or mature discernment. — Al-Ḥasan calls puberty the first period of mature recollection; al-Māwardī and Abū Ḥayyān similarly call it the start of reflection. Elmalılı applies the term to anyone dying after puberty, and Iṣlāḥī says those dying before maturity receive more lenient treatment. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:37/q13/p5 Twenty years is the threshold. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a group and Abū Ḥayyān cites ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz; al-Bursawī says duty becomes more urgent around eighteen or twenty. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمر بن عبد العزيز); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 35:37/q13/p6 Seventy years is the threshold. — Ibn ʿUmar is cited for a rebuke of those reaching seventy; al-Māwardī says it is the final age for reflection before senility. A Prophetic wording says sixty or seventy, and al-Bursawī treats seventy as the last period after the old age of sixty. [6 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عمر); BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 35:37/q13/p7 Seventeen years is the threshold. — Al-Zajjāj transmits this number; later lists preserve it. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:37/q13/p8 Fifty years is the threshold. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites two poems portraying fifty as old age and close to death; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī preserve the number. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:37/q13/p9 Forty-six years is the threshold. — Al-Durr and al-Ālūsī report the number from Ibn ʿAbbās; Elmalılı includes it among the reported estimates. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 35:37/q13/p10 The relevant span extends from twenty to sixty years. — Al-Ālūsī reports this from Mujāhid; Elmalılı lists it among the views. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 35:37/q13/p11 Any sufficient span for a receptive person to reflect counts, with longer life increasing accountability. — Al-Nasafī stresses capacity rather than one fixed age; Ibn Kathīr and al-Bursawī likewise speak of anyone willing to remember. Al-Qushayrī calls it a completed respite for considering evidence. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 35:37/q14 What related reports describe ordinary human lifespans?  [نُعَمِّرْكُم · reports]
turns on: The transmitted numbers and, for the sixty-to-seventy report, differing chains and assessments.
- 35:37/q14/p1 Most of the Prophet's community live or die between sixty and seventy, with few exceeding it. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī and al-Bursawī transmit this. Ibn Kathīr records a route he calls weak, another graded ḥasan gharīb by al-Tirmidhī, and al-Bazzār's warning that a route through ʿUthmān ibn Maṭar is weak. [10 notes: THALABI(أبو هريرة); JISHUMI(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,البزار,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(علي); BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 35:37/q14/p2 The community's span is fifty to sixty, with a prayer of mercy for those reaching seventy or eighty. — Ibn Kathīr transmits Ḥudhayfa's question and the Prophet's reply. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(حذيفة)]
- 35:37/q14/p3 Some physicians give a natural lifespan of 120 years, with growth until sixty and decline thereafter. — Ibn Kathīr adds a poet's illustration of loss of youthful pleasure at sixty. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأطباء)]
- 35:37/q14/p4 The Prophet most famously lived sixty-three years; other reports give sixty or sixty-five. — Ibn Kathīr records and ranks these reported ages. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 35:37/q15 What other reported admonitions mark stages of life?  [نُعَمِّرْكُم · reports]
- 35:37/q15/p1 A caller warns at forty that harvest nears, at sixty to account for one's deeds, and at seventy to come to reckoning. — Al-Bursawī transmits this daily and nightly call. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 35:37/q15/p2 Worshippers intensified their devotion at forty or at the appearance of gray hair. — Al-Bursawī describes Companions and successors increasing night prayer, reducing social contact and leaving beds, while noting that death's unknown time makes no age safe. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 35:37/q16 How is مَن تَذَكَّرَ read?  [مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ مَن تَذَكَّرَ · readings]
turns on: Al-Aʿmash's assimilation and the pronounced linking hamza distinguish the alternate reading.
- 35:37/q16/p1 The majority reads mā yatadhakkaru fīhi man tadhakkar. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān name the majority reading. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 35:37/q16/p2 Al-Aʿmash reads an assimilated form, reported as mā yudhakkaru or mā yadhkur with man adhkar or udhkira. — The reports vary in transliteration and vocalization; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī say it comes from idhkar and note the unusual pronunciation of hamzat al-waṣl in connected recitation; al-Samīn also records the assimilation and the Shaykh's observation. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش)]

## 35:37/q17 Who or what is ٱلنَّذِيرُ?  [ٱلنَّذِيرُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the warning is delivered by a messenger or scripture, or embodied in life signs and reason; proponents of the Messenger cite other verses and binding scriptural proof, while gray-hair proponents cite usage, poetry and signs of death.
- 35:37/q17/p1 Muhammad, God's messenger, came as the warner. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Zayd cite 53:56; al-Wāḥidī cites 33:45 and 48:8; Ibn Jarīr as reported by Ibn Kathīr cites 43:77–78, 17:15 and 67:8–9. Al-Qurṭubī says prophetic warning ends excuses, citing 4:165 and 17:15. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the title highlights the relevant aspect of Muhammad's mission. Al-Zajjāj, al-Jishumī, al-Wāḥidī and Ibn Jarīr prefer this reading; al-Jishumī calls it literal. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Bursawī say most commentators hold it. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن زيد,السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة وابن زيد وابن السائب ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن علي وابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي وعبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(السدي وابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وابن جرير)+; JISHUMI(ابن زيد، أبو علي)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 35:37/q17/p2 Gray hair warns of old age and approaching death. — ʿIkrima, Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna, Wakīʿ and others transmit this. Al-Qurṭubī says gray hair shows youth's play is past. Poets cited by al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī call it a warning; a transmitted saying makes each white hair warn its neighbor. Al-Kāshifī likens its whiteness to a life-flame waning. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it good but says binding proof comes through scriptural warning. The notices differ on whether al-Ṭabarī reports or holds it. [28 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); THALABI(شاعر,عكرمة وسفيان بن عيينة ووكيع والحسين بن الفضل); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء والطبري); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة وسفيان بن عيينة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); JISHUMI(عكرمة، سفيان بن عيينة، وكيع); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة وسفيان بن عيينة ووكيع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI(ابن زيد,زيد بن علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عمر وعكرمة وسفيان بن عيينة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة وسفيان ووكيع والحسين بن الفضل والفراء والطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، وعكرمة، وسفيان، ووكيع، والحسن بن الفضل، والفراء، والطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة وأبو جعفر الباقر وقتادة وسفيان بن عيينة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI(الجمهور,الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة وسفيان بن عيينة ووكيع والحسين بن الفضل والفراء والطبري)]
- 35:37/q17/p3 The Qur'an itself is the warner. — Al-Thaʿlabī attributes this to Zayd ibn ʿAlī, as do al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī; other sources report it among the alternatives. [9 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI(زيد بن علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن زيد,زيد بن علي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(السدي وابن زيد)]
- 35:37/q17/p4 Prophets generally are the warners, each to his own people and Muhammad to the later world. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān take the word generically; al-Ālūsī says the plural reading supports this. Al-Biqāʿī adds that messengers and revealed books reinforce what reason alone could not establish. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السدي وابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 35:37/q17/p5 Deaths of relatives, friends, or neighbors warn the living of their own death. — Al-Qurṭubī cites two poems on repeated departures of companions; al-Bursawī and several others list deaths among the signs. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، وعكرمة، وسفيان، ووكيع، والحسن بن الفضل، والفراء، والطبري); BURSEVI(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:37/q17/p6 Mature reason or intellectual discernment itself warns a person. — Al-Qurṭubī says it distinguishes reality and moral differences and directs work toward the afterlife. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī include reason, while al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī give completed maturity. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:37/q17/p7 Fever warns that death is approaching. — Al-Qurṭubī cites a Prophetic description of fever as death's forerunner and al-Azharī's explanation that it is death's messenger. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:37/q17/p8 Any sign of departure from life can count as a warner. — Al-Kawāshī, cited by al-Bursawī, extends the expression to every such sign and calls for heedfulness. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الكواشي)]
- 35:37/q17/p9 The warning is the subsidiary branches of religious rulings. — Elmalılı gives this explanation. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 35:37/q17/p10 The word means warning itself, as a verbal noun. — Al-Qurṭubī includes 'warning' among the lexical possibilities, and Abū ʿAlī says a verbal noun is preferable if the referent is gray hair. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)]

## 35:37/q18 How does ٱلنَّذِيرُ work grammatically for different referents?  [ٱلنَّذِيرُ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical form tracks whether the referent is a person or an impersonal sign.
- 35:37/q18/p1 It is an active participle when it names Muhammad. — Abū ʿAlī, cited by al-Wāḥidī, distinguishes the personal referent. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)]
- 35:37/q18/p2 It is better taken as a verbal noun when it names gray hair. — Abū ʿAlī makes this grammatical distinction because gray hair is a warning rather than a personal warner. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)]

## 35:37/q19 How is وَجَآءَكُمُ ٱلنَّذِيرُ connected to the question before it?  [وَجَآءَكُمُ ٱلنَّذِيرُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether coordination follows the rhetorical question's asserted meaning or the verb within the question.
- 35:37/q19/p1 It coordinates with the affirmative sense of the preceding rhetorical question: 'We gave you life, and a warner came.' — Abū Ḥayyān cites 28:18 and 94:1–2; al-Samīn cites 26:18 and 94:1–2. Al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise say the interrogative form carries an assertion, allowing coordination. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:37/q19/p2 It coordinates directly with 'We gave you life.' — Al-Ālūsī reports this as a second syntactic parse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:37/q20 Is there a variant reading of ٱلنَّذِيرُ?  [ٱلنَّذِيرُ · readings]
turns on: Singular versus plural wording and, in al-Qurṭubī's form, feminine plural agreement.
- 35:37/q20/p1 The verse is read with singular al-nadhīr. — Al-Ālūsī says the singular suits the immediate point. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:37/q20/p2 An alternate reading has plural al-nudhur, 'warners.' — Al-Qurṭubī transmits the feminine plural verbal agreement wajāʾatkum al-nudhur; al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān also report the plural, which supports a generic prophetic referent. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قراءة); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 35:37/q21 What does فَذُوقُوا۟ command, and why does it follow?  [فَذُوقُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 35:37/q21/p1 They must taste the punishment of Hell, with remorse, as the consequence of ignored lifetime and warning. — Al-Rāzī calls the command humiliating and signifying lasting punishment; al-Qurṭubī links it to failure to reflect. Ibn Kathīr links it to disobedience to prophets. Ibn ʿĀshūr says fāʾ marks consequence, 'punishment' is the omitted object, and the imperative conveys continuity and inability to escape. Al-Biqāʿī cites 17:56 and 77:36 for their helplessness. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:37/q22 Who are ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ here?  [ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'wrongdoers' is limited in context to the disbelievers in Hell or expresses a rule about all injustice.
- 35:37/q22/p1 The wrongdoers are the unbelievers or polytheists, who wronged themselves through disbelief and idolatry. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī make disbelief explicit; Yaḥyā calls them polytheists. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls shirk the gravest injustice, violating God's oneness and harming the self, citing 31:13. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:37/q22/p2 The wording reaches every wrongdoer whenever wrongdoing is established. — Al-Biqāʿī says wrongdoing weakens as truth strengthens; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the general statement includes these disbelievers and all wrongdoers. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:37/q22/p3 A wrongdoer can be one with compounded ignorance who mistakes falsehood for truth. — Al-Rāzī offers this as a possible explanation of why such a person lacks beneficial knowledge in the afterlife. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 35:37/q23 What does having no helper mean?  [فَمَا لِلظَّٰلِمِينَ مِن نَّصِيرٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether helper covers every possible rescuer, specifically intercession, or even beneficial knowledge.
- 35:37/q23/p1 No one can protect or rescue them from God's punishment. — Al-Rāzī's broad preferred sense excludes help from God and every other helper; he says 'sultan' names proof because authority or power aids. Ibn Kathīr mentions torment, restraints and chains. Study Quran says they turned away from God and the help He sent in this life, citing 2:270, 3:192, 5:72 and 22:71. Al-Ālūsī treats the denial as the cause for tasting punishment; al-Biqāʿī applies it to eternal torment. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 35:37/q23/p2 There is no intercession for major sinners. — Al-Jishumī draws this theological conclusion from the denial of a helper. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 35:37/q23/p3 No beneficial knowledge remains to help someone who mistook falsehood for truth. — Al-Rāzī reports this as a possible narrower sense of helper. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 35:37/q24 Why is 'helper' singular here?  [نَّصِيرٍ · rhetoric]
- 35:37/q24/p1 At this stage in Hell they have despaired of human allies and idols and hope only for God; elsewhere expected idol-help is still in view. — Al-Rāzī compares 3:192 and distinguishes their actual state in Hell from warnings in worldly life or early gathering. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 35:37/q25 How is مِن نَّصِيرٍ parsed?  [مِن نَّصِيرٍ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic status of the preposition and the nominal subject.
- 35:37/q25/p1 The noun can be a subject governed by the preposition. — Al-Samīn gives this parse. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 35:37/q25/p2 The noun can be the topic, with the preceding prepositional phrase as predicate. — Al-Samīn gives this alternative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 35:37/q26 Who hears the final no-helper statement?  [فَمَا لِلظَّٰلِمِينَ مِن نَّصِيرٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the sentence continues God's address to the damned or turns outward into a general conclusion.
- 35:37/q26/p1 It continues the rebuke to the condemned, ending their hope of rescue and challenging their imagined allies. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this reading; al-Ālūsī says naming them wrongdoers itself rebukes them. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:37/q26/p2 It is a separate conclusion addressed to listeners, applying to every wrongdoer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this alternative, including the disbelievers just described in 35:36. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:37/q27 What does the verse teach about accountability and reform?  [* · theology]
- 35:37/q27/p1 Worldly life gives people choice and opportunity to amend themselves; after death the chance ends and prior warning removes excuses. — Al-Jishumī argues from the interrogation and lifetime as evidence for human choice and action. Qatāda calls long life proof against people. Al-Rāzī says the cry cannot reduce punishment, and that this is punishment rather than correction, for a corrected pupil would be released. Al-Biqāʿī adds that messengers and books supplied what reason alone could not. Iṣlāḥī distinguishes more lenient treatment for those who die before maturity; Study Quran cites 4:18 against repentance at death. Al-Bursawī's inward reading says heedless people awaken fully only after death. [11 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]

## 35:37/q28 Why is their request to return refused?  [رَبَّنَآ أَخْرِجْنَا · interpretation]
- 35:37/q28/p1 The requested return is impossible, and if returned they would repeat their sins. — Ibn Kathīr cites 6:28 and 40:11–12; Study Quran cites 23:100 and 6:28. Al-Rāzī says they fail to recognize how remote and impossible the return is, invokes 17:72 for continuing loss, and contrasts punitive confinement with correction. Kuran Yolu compares their ineffective cries with 23:107–108. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 35:37/q29 When do Hell's people utter these words?  [وَهُمْ يَصْطَرِخُونَ فِيهَا · links]
turns on: The notes give two distinct timing details rather than competing chronologies.
- 35:37/q29/p1 They speak before the command in 23:108 that they be despised and speak no more. — Qatāda gives this ordering for all speech attributed to the people of Hell. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة)]
- 35:37/q29/p2 They receive God's answer after an interval equal to worldly life. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this timing. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 35:37/q30 What does their vow reveal about reliance on God?  [نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا · theology]
- 35:37/q30/p1 Their unconditional promise reveals self-reliance and failure to honor God's majesty. — Al-Rāzī says they omit reliance on God and cannot produce works proportionate to His majesty. He contrasts believers, who confess that their deeds are small beside divine grace, ask for forgiveness more than reward, and praise God's guidance and blessings beyond expectation. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 35:37/q31 What obstacles to faith does the answer rule out?  [أَوَلَمْ نُعَمِّرْكُم مَّا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ مَن تَذَكَّرَ وَجَآءَكُمُ ٱلنَّذِيرُ · interpretation]
- 35:37/q31/p1 Neither inadequate opportunity to reflect nor failure of guidance explains their disbelief; they had time and a messenger. — Al-Rāzī identifies the two possible impediments as the people's failure to reflect on revelation and a guide's failure to recite it. God's answer removes both excuses; Qatāda names both lifetime and messengers as proofs. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL]

## 35:37/q32 How does the passage connect with other afterlife discourse?  [* · links]
- 35:37/q32/p1 This exchange belongs to a wider dialogue between God and Hell's people about their futile plea and judgment. — Study Quran compares 40:11–12; its other cited pleas are 32:12, 19:38, 67:11, 6:27, 7:53 and 26:102. Kuran Yolu compares 23:107–108. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:37/q32/p2 The sūra contrasts believers' enduring reward with the ruin and helplessness of disbelievers, then moves to God's power and idols. — Iṣlāḥī says wealth and children without faith and good deeds increase ruin and remain exposed to danger, while the faithful's chambers give enduring safety and reward; ḍiʿf may mean equal or unbounded multiples, not only double. Study Quran describes verses 29–39 as the long comparison that leads to God's power, idols and the outcomes of unbelief. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 35:37/q33 What inward lesson is drawn from the condemned person's late awakening?  [فَذُوقُوا۟ · inward]
- 35:37/q33/p1 Those asleep in worldly heedlessness awaken fully after death and resurrection, when punishment becomes directly felt. — Al-Bursawī gives this ishārī reading. He also transmits ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlānī's addresses to young and elderly penitents: God preserves and draws the young back; after pardon and delay through youth and adulthood, the estranged elder can still return. His Persian couplet contrasts youthful spiritual haste with old age's coldness. [4 notes: BURSEVI(عبد القادر الكيلاني)]

## 35:37/q34 What practical duty does a clear warning create for delayed Hajj?  [وَجَآءَكُمُ ٱلنَّذِيرُ · law]
- 35:37/q34/p1 Hajj may be delayed while completion remains likely; clear warning that delay may prevent it makes postponement impermissible. — Al-Bursawī attributes the sounder view to Muḥammad al-Shaybānī. Sudden death after delay entails no sin under this account, but clear warning changes the duty. [1 notes: BURSEVI(محمد بن الحسن)]

## 35:37/q35 What reports describe the origin or status of gray hair?  [ٱلنَّذِيرُ · reports]
- 35:37/q35/p1 Abraham was the first descendant of Adam to turn gray, and God called it worldly dignity and otherworldly light. — Al-Bursawī reports that Abraham then asked for more of it. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 35:37/q35/p2 God dislikes an old man whose hair has not turned gray. — Al-Bursawī reports the hadith with an explanation in al-Maqāṣid al-Ḥasana. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 35:37/q36 What other lexical or reading notes accompany this passage?  [* · links]
- 35:37/q36/p1 Nearby bayyinah may be singular for scripture or what a prophet brought, or plural for each prophet's proof and scripture's kinds of evidence. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports both readings and cites 11:28, 7:73 and 7:85. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 35:37/q36/p2 Nearby maqt means hatred; a hated person is mamqūt or maqīt. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives the lexical forms for the adjacent passage. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 35:37/q37 What ethical rule follows from the general denial of help to wrongdoers?  [فَمَا لِلظَّٰلِمِينَ مِن نَّصِيرٍ · law]
- 35:37/q37/p1 Helping an oppressor means restraining the injustice, since aiding it violates justice. — Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects the pre-Islamic maxim of assisting one's brother whether oppressor or victim and cites the Prophet's correction that the oppressor is helped by being restrained. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
