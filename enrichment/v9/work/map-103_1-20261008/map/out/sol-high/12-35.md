# 12:35: 22 questions

## 12:35/q01 What does 'then' convey here?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 12:35/q01/p1 It marks the surprising later reversal after Joseph's innocence had become clear. — Ibn Ashur says the turn is surprising precisely because the signs should have prevented imprisonment; al-Biqa'i calls the choice contrary to ordinary expectation and part of God's overriding plan. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 12:35/q02 What does 'it appeared to them' mean?  [بَدَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the wording chiefly signals a changed opinion or the emergence of a plan through consultation.
- 12:35/q02/p1 A new view appeared: they changed their earlier opinion and resolved on prison. — Al-Zajjaj says Arabic badā lī badāʾan expresses a changed opinion and commonly leaves its noun understood. Al-Jishumi distinguishes appearing from a change of opinion. Al-Razi, al-Biqa'i, Bursavi, al-Tabataba'i, and Asad describe the newly occurring thought; al-Tabari and al-Baghawi say it reversed a previous plan to release or merely ignore Joseph. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)]
- 12:35/q02/p2 It means that imprisonment emerged as their plan through deliberation. — Ibn al-Anbari relates it to discussion and consultation; al-Tabari describes a reversal of their apparent decision to release Joseph. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); TAB-FULL]

## 12:35/q03 Who are 'them'?  [لَهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural denotes the governing household or consultative entourage, one ruler spoken of collectively, or the wife's mixed group.
- 12:35/q03/p1 The Aziz or ruler and his household, advisers, or associates. — Al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi name the ruler or minister and household; Abu Hayyan names the Aziz and household. Al-Tha'labi, al-Qurtubi, and al-Wahidi include officials or companions; al-Tabataba'i includes his wife, advisers and attendants. Study Quran reports both family and entourage readings. Al-Tabari explains how a singular actor can be referred to by a plural group, citing 3:173. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(وهب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 12:35/q03/p2 The king or Aziz alone, described by a plural because his decision involved consultation. — Al-Tabari compares a single speaker described as 'the people' in 3:173; Ibn Atiyya explicitly says the king alone with consultation. Al-Tabrisi favors the king despite the preceding women. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI]
- 12:35/q03/p3 Zulaykha and her helpers or the wife's group of advisers and commanders. — Al-Tabrisi and Abu Ali allow a mixed group with masculine agreement; Ibn Ashur identifies the chief wife's advisers and commanders. [3 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:35/q04 What is the grammatical subject of 'badā'?  [بَدَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether an entire oath sentence can be the subject of a verb, or an implicit noun/object and quoted speech must be supplied; the sources also transmit conflicting positions from Sibawayh.
- 12:35/q04/p1 An omitted noun for the newly appearing decision, opinion, or imprisonment is its subject; the following clause explains it. — Al-Zajjaj notes Arabic normally omits the understood badāʾ; Muhammad ibn Yazid and Abu Uthman supply an implicit noun or badw. Al-Mubarrad invokes a poem with omitted ḥaqq. Ibn Atiyya cites badw or raʾy; al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi specify the decision; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin prefer imprisonment inferred from the threat or sijn, and al-Samin cites readings of al-sijn. Al-Mazini selects the first subject analysis in al-Alusi's account. Sibawayh is reported by Ibn Atiyya as rejecting a sentence as subject, although other transmitters attribute the opposite view to him. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عثمان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,المازني)+; SAMIN-DURR+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 12:35/q04/p2 The clause 'they will surely imprison him' itself serves as the subject. — Al-Farra presents the full clause as filling the subject position; al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi report this from Sibawayh, whereas Ibn Atiyya reports Sibawayh explicitly rejecting it. Al-Samin ascribes it to Kufans. Al-Razi challenges the premise that a verb cannot be discussed as a subject, using ḍaraba fiʿl: treating the form as a noun would make the predicate 'verb' false. Al-Alusi cites 36:31 and 14:45 for a full proposition as subject but says most grammarians reject it. Ibn Ashur reports Hisham and Tha'lab permit clauses generally, while al-Farra and others permit them with cognitive verbs and suspension. [8 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه)-; QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 12:35/q04/p3 The oath clause is quoted speech after an omitted 'they said,' while an omitted decision or object belongs to 'badā'. — Abu Uthman compares an omitted 'they said' in 39:3. Al-Farra's parse supplies raʾy and qālū; Ibn Ashur treats the oath sentence as detached from badā and its omitted object as understood, an instance of suspension beyond verbs of thought according to Yunus ibn Habib. Al-Alusi reports an object of implicit saying or explanatory clause; Ibn Ashur says suspension fits better than a clause as subject. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عثمان); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(يونس بن حبيب); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 12:35/q05 What signs had they seen?  [ٱلْءَايَٰتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which reported events count as evidence: the shirt and testimony directly exonerate Joseph, while several commentators dispute whether scratches or cut hands do.
- 12:35/q05/p1 The signs were proofs of Joseph's innocence, chiefly his shirt torn from behind, sometimes with the witness's judgment. — Mujahid names the rear tear; Ibn Jawzi transmits the shirt and cousin's judgment, and Ibn Atiyya says the shirt test is probative whether an infant's miraculous speech or an adult's inference. Abu Hayyan makes the same distinction. Al-Alusi reports Mujahid restricting the signs to the tear because the women's cuts are not proof; he also reports Qatada limiting the signs to the tear while treating the dual as plural. The witness's ruling and the wife's admission appear in al-Wahidi; Ibn Ashur says the proofs established Joseph's truth and her falsehood. [20 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن اسحاق); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(JJ and Kl)]
- 12:35/q05/p2 The signs included the rear shirt tear and the women's cut hands, often with further evidence. — Qatada, al-Suddi and many later sources give both. The additions vary: face scratches in al-Tabari and al-Tha'labi; the witness, awe or child's testimony in Ibn Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi, al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi; the child's testimony and race to the door in al-Tabataba'i. Al-Alusi reports an argument that hand cuts show Joseph's beauty made the women likely initiators, while his resistance to them supplied a further sign. Al-Wahidi doubts the cuts prove innocence, as do Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan. [21 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وغيره); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل عن مجاهد عن ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي)-]
- 12:35/q05/p3 The signs included the shirt tear and scratch, bodily or knife marks. — Ibn Abbas reports scratches on the woman's face in al-Tabari and marks on Joseph's body plus knife marks in al-Durr and al-Alusi. Ikrima reports the scratched face in Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Durr. Al-Razi adds the Aziz's verdict in 12:28 and says other decisive signs were hidden to cover the scandal. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan object that scratches themselves do not prove innocence. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عكرمة); THALABI; RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة)-]
- 12:35/q05/p4 The signs included the child's or nursing witness's speech and the shirt test. — Ibn Zayd calls the nursing woman's courtyard statement a sign; al-Durr calls the speaker an infant. Al-Baghawi and al-Baydawi include the child's testimony; Bursavi and al-Tabataba'i add it to the rear tear, and al-Tabataba'i also mentions the race to the door. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say infancy would make the testimony a miracle, whereas an adult's statement would be an inference. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 12:35/q05/p5 The signs were Joseph's evident beauty and chastity, which made the women call him an angel. — Al-Mawardi gives this as an alternative to shirt and hand cuts; Ibn Jawzi repeats it. Al-Qurtubi judges a 'blessings' interpretation less sound than signs of innocence. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 12:35/q05/p6 The signs showed that the women had despaired of obtaining Joseph. — Al-Tabrisi reports this alternative interpretation. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 12:35/q05/p7 The plural refers broadly to clear circumstances and perhaps unmentioned proofs of Joseph's innocence. — Ibn Atiyya says no single sign need be identified, and the proofs varied in degree; Abu Hayyan prefers several manifest proofs suggested by the plural, perhaps the witness's statement and unmentioned evidence. Al-Alusi likewise allows omitted prophetic signs. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 12:35/q06 What decision did they make after the signs?  [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · interpretation]
- 12:35/q06/p1 They knowingly resolved to imprison innocent Joseph for a temporary, open-ended period, reversing an earlier plan to release or merely avoid him. — Al-Tabari says they reversed an apparent release; al-Zajjaj, al-Baghawi and Ibn Jawzi say the master had first ordered only that Joseph be ignored. Ibn Kathir says they judged confinement expedient despite proofs of chastity. Al-Tabari says they would decide their next course later. Al-Biqa'i emphasizes the injustice and folly; Islahi notes the Aziz's unrestricted power over an enslaved person and his office and rank in the royal guard made imprisonment easy. Al-Tabataba'i says firm resolve is expressed by the oath lām. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 12:35/q07 How did the decision to imprison Joseph arise?  [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · reports]
turns on: Whether the initiative came from the wife's complaint, women's earlier plotting, or an adviser's counsel; these reports may describe stages of the same decision.
- 12:35/q07/p1 The wife accused Joseph of publicly disgracing her and demanded either freedom to defend herself or his imprisonment; the Aziz and advisers complied. — Al-Suddi, Wahb and later transmitters vary whether she asked to leave, defend herself, apologize, or be jailed as she had been confined. Ibn Atiyya says she framed the account after Joseph refused sin; al-Razi calls the accusation false. Al-Wahidi and Ibn Abi Hatim say she warned that people would believe Joseph if he stayed free. Al-Kashshaf, al-Alusi and Asad stress the husband's submission; Asad quotes Zamakhshari's image of the wife holding his camel's reins. Al-Wahidi and al-Qurtubi state he knew Joseph was innocent. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي); THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,وهب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي,وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:35/q07/p2 The women urged the wife to imprison Joseph, hoping isolation would let them entice him; when he resisted, she appealed to the master. — Wahb says their proposal also sought to stop public gossip that she hated Joseph; the master later treated him harshly. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه)]
- 12:35/q07/p3 An adviser urged imprisonment so the public would excuse the Aziz and blame the household if Joseph remained free to tell his story. — Al-Durr reports this from Ikrima. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 12:35/q08 Why did they want Joseph imprisoned?  [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources attribute overlapping aims to different actors: rulers' public reputation, public inference of guilt, the wife's desire to control Joseph, or a provisional wait.
- 12:35/q08/p1 To quiet gossip, conceal the scandal, protect the household's reputation, and separate Joseph from the wife for a time. — Al-Qurtubi prefers this account; al-Razi says the Aziz sought to quiet scandal; Ibn Kathir calls it a conjecture and al-Suddi says her behavior would otherwise be exposed while Joseph's name was cleared. The wife feared discussion after the women dispersed, according to Ibn Ashur; Islahi says confinement could reduce her infatuation. Al-Tabataba'i also suggests avoiding disorder or wider infatuation with Joseph's beauty. Al-Kuranyolu says the household sought to conceal the affair. [24 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي,عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(عكرمة، الكلبي، الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 12:35/q08/p2 To make the public believe Joseph was guilty because criminals are imprisoned. — Al-Tabrisi and al-Baydawi give the appearance of guilt as a motive; Bursavi assigns it especially to the wife. Ibn Ashur says the wife meant to conceal Joseph's innocence, and Islahi says people would infer he was punished. A report from Ikrima says an adviser expected blame to shift to the household if Joseph spoke freely. [7 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:35/q08/p3 The wife hoped prison would humble, punish, or coerce Joseph into yielding to her. — Al-Kashshaf gives both her possible loss of hope and her hope for submission; al-Qurtubi reports concealment and renewed desire or a plot to wear him down. Al-Nasafi, Bursavi, al-Alusi, al-Tabataba'i, Asad and Kuranyolu state variants of her coercive purpose. Al-Tabrisi reports the prison was near her so she could see him from her window. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]
- 12:35/q08/p4 They meant to observe Joseph's behavior and decide their next course later. — Al-Tabari says the decision was provisional; al-Baydawi says they would see what happened; al-Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan connect the term to what the wife would see Joseph do. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 12:35/q09 How does 'they will surely imprison him' fit grammatically after 'badā'?  [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the oath force is supplied by implicit speech or an interrogative setting, and whether emphatic nūn can occur after a question.
- 12:35/q09/p1 It is the response to an unspoken oath, introduced through an implicit statement of their resolve. — Al-Farra says the lām works after expressions of speech, opinion, or knowledge and cites 12:26, 12:36, 12:80. Al-Tabari's rejecting grammarians say emphatic nūn does not follow a plain question. The Kufan explanation lets badā carry 'they said/decided'; al-Samin says the majority posit implied speech. Al-Alusi allows an implied saying or explanatory clause. Al-Tabataba'i calls the lām an oath of firm resolve, and Ibn Ashur treats the oath clause as suspended from badā. [16 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية,نحوي من الكوفة); THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عثمان,الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 12:35/q09/p2 An interrogative environment licenses the emphatic nūn and lām. — Al-Akhfash says the clause can host a question like 'which of them will take someone'; a Basran explanation makes an interrogative with ay implicit. The Kufan grammarian allows speech after badā to introduce either an oath or question. Other grammarians object that emphatic nūn would not follow a question such as 'will you stand?'. [3 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(أهل العربية من البصرة,نحوي من الكوفة); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية)-]
- 12:35/q09/p3 The heavy emphatic nūn belongs to this form, with different pause and connected forms. — Al-Jishumi contrasts the heavy nūn in la-yasjunanna with the light or heavy emphatic nūn in la-yakūnan, and compares the pause form with la-nasfaʿan. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 12:35/q10 How is the verb 'they will imprison him' alternatively read?  [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the resolve is narrated in the third person or voiced as a direct address to the ruler or his group.
- 12:35/q10/p1 With third-person yāʾ: 'they will surely imprison him.' — Al-Wahidi says the third person follows the unseen subject; the written verse has this form. Al-Hasan's alternative addresses the Aziz or his circle. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 12:35/q10/p2 Al-Hasan reads with address tāʾ: 'you will surely imprison him.' — Al-Kashshaf says the address may target the Aziz and those around him, or the Aziz alone deferentially. Al-Baydawi allows people addressing the ruler honorifically or the ruler addressing attendants; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin also give singular or respectful plural possibilities. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 12:35/q11 How is 'ḥattā' alternatively read?  [حَتَّىٰ · readings]
turns on: Dialectal substitution of ʿayn for ḥāʾ and Umar's preference for Quraysh recitation.
- 12:35/q11/p1 The ordinary form is ḥattā with ḥāʾ, identified as Quraysh usage. — The report of Umar's letter instructs Ibn Masud to teach Quraysh Arabic after a Hudhayli recitation. Ibn Jinni says the common form is preferable although the other is permissible, since ḥāʾ and ʿayn have close articulation points. Al-Samin says ḥattā marks the endpoint of what precedes. [9 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(عمر); KASHSHAF-FULL(عمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(عمر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB+]
- 12:35/q11/p2 Ibn Masud's Hudhayl form reads ʿattā with ʿayn. — Umar heard this form, asked who had taught it, and wrote Ibn Masud to teach Quraysh usage; al-Durr adds that the Qur'an was revealed in clear Quraysh Arabic. Ibn Jinni explains the consonant exchange by proximity of articulation and allows the variant; al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi identify it as Hudhayl speech. [10 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(عمر); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود,عمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(عمر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 12:35/q12 What does 'ḥīn' mean as a word?  [حِينٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ḥīn is taken in its unrestricted lexical range or assigned a length from a particular usage or report.
- 12:35/q12/p1 An indefinite span of time, short or long; the word itself fixes no length. — The linguists in al-Wahidi and al-Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Bursavi and al-Alusi distinguish lexical indefiniteness from estimates of Joseph's actual confinement. Ibn Atiyya says Qur'anic usage supports short and long spans; Abu Hayyan says any specific duration comes from the prison report, not the lexeme. Bursavi says a juristic unqualified oath of 'a while' defaults to six months unless a particular period was meant. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أهل اللغة); QURTUBI-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:35/q12/p2 It can denote a morning or evening. — Ibn Abbas is reported with this lexical possibility. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 12:35/q12/p3 It can denote six months. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr gives this meaning, with Tariq reported to agree; Bursavi says unqualified juristic oaths use six months unless a specific duration was intended. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI]
- 12:35/q12/p4 It can denote seven years. — Ikrima is reported with this lexical interpretation. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة)]

## 12:35/q13 What period or endpoint was intended by 'until a time'?  [حَتَّىٰ حِينٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The verse gives no number; sources differ between the rulers' intended endpoint and reports estimating Joseph's actual stay. Transmissions even vary for Ikrima, al-Kalbi and Muqatil.
- 12:35/q13/p1 No fixed length was stated; they meant an open-ended period, and the exact stay is unknown or only known to have been long. — Many commentators leave it unspecified. Ibn Jawzi prefers this because the jailers set no fixed term, while later commentators estimate Joseph's stay. Ibn Ashur says the authorities' words may be open-ended or the narrator omits an irrelevant duration. Al-Razi cites 12:45 for a long imprisonment while declining exactness; al-Alusi similarly avoids choosing a number. Ibn al-Shaykh in Bursavi says the verse only indicates a long stay. Ibn al-Anbari says withholding the period directs people to scholars; Ibn Abi Hatim's report describes the Qur'anic term as unbounded. Al-Aṣamm calls it a long period. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة,ابن الأنباري); JISHUMI(عكرمة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي)+]
- 12:35/q13/p2 Until city gossip and scandal faded or Joseph's news was forgotten. — Ibn Abbas is reported by al-Wahidi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi and al-Alusi; Ata appears in al-Baghawi and Ibn Jawzi. Al-Jubba'i adds that Joseph's news faded. The wife's request and the ruler's apparent goal explain the endpoint. ## 12:35/q14 What does Joseph's imprisonment teach about coercion to commit adultery?  [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · law]
turns on: The degree of coercion: prison threat versus severe beating, and the treatment of sin and penalty.
- 12:35/q14/p1 Threat of imprisonment does not permit adultery, by consensus. — Al-Qurtubi cites Joseph's refusal despite the threat and his prayer for protection from the women's plot. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:35/q14/p2 Under severe beating, the sound view removes the sin of coerced adultery; the claim that its penalty remains is weak. — Al-Qurtubi distinguishes severe beating from imprisonment and argues from 22:78 that God does not combine two torments. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 12:35/q14/p3 The legal penalty remains even under severe beating. — Al-Qurtubi reports this claim but calls it weak because God does not combine two torments, citing 22:78. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 12:35/q15 How was Joseph publicly taken to prison?  [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · reports]
- 12:35/q15/p1 He was paraded on a donkey through Egypt's markets while a drum and callers announced a false charge against him. — Ibn Abbas says the announcement accused the Hebrew of desiring his master and deserving prison; Abu Salih says Ibn Abbas wept whenever recounting the humiliation. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس)]

## 12:35/q16 What did Joseph do during and after imprisonment?  [لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ · reports]
- 12:35/q16/p1 In prison he comforted, cared for, and interpreted dreams for others, proclaimed his prophetic lineage, and called people to monotheism. — Muqatil gives the acts of care and descent through Jacob, Isaac and Abraham; al-Tabataba'i says prison is where he first identifies his Abrahamic family and teaches monotheism. [2 notes: MUQATIL; TABATABAI]
- 12:35/q16/p2 He refused the king's later summons until his innocence was established and he could leave with his reputation clear. — Ibn Kathir links this to 12:50. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 12:35/q16/p3 The baker and cupbearer were also jailed over suspicion of a poisoning plot against the king. — Al-Durr says the baker tried to poison the king and the cupbearer was suspected of collusion. [1 notes: DURR-FULL]

## 12:35/q17 Why did God allow Joseph's imprisonment, and what did it reveal?  [* · theology]
turns on: The sources give differing theological accounts of divine purpose and human agency, while also recounting human injustice.
- 12:35/q17/p1 God made prison a punishment or expiation for Joseph's earlier inclination toward the woman. — Al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi and al-Qurtubi state purification from desire. Ibn Abbas counts three lapses: inclination led to prison, asking the cupbearer prolonged it, and accusing the brothers of theft brought their accusation concerning Benjamin; Ibn Atiyya cites 12:24, 12:42, 12:70 and 12:77. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 12:35/q17/p2 God used an unjust prison to protect Joseph's faith, defeat the women's plot, and raise him toward authority. — Al-Biqa'i says divine power overturns expected causes and establishes Joseph's rank; al-Jishumi's report from al-Asamm says the women gained only his apparent confinement, not religious corruption. Al-Tabataba'i says imprisonment brings him near the king and Egyptian authority. Al-Islahi says Joseph valued faith and character above hardship; Wahb reports Gabriel's message that his resistance through grief, slavery and prison made him among the purified and righteous. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(وهب بن منبه); JISHUMI(الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 12:35/q17/p3 The plotting and imprisonment were the people's own acts, not acts created by God. — Al-Jishumi states this agency claim even while reporting that the women failed to corrupt Joseph. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 12:35/q18 Was Joseph's earlier prayer a request to be imprisoned?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the preceding response answers Joseph's request for protection or a supposed request for prison; the later decision wording is the cited test.
- 12:35/q18/p1 No; the response to his prayer was protection from sin, and only afterward did they decide to imprison him. — Al-Tabataba'i uses the sequence of the preceding verse and this verse to reject the claim that God granted a prayer for imprisonment. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 12:35/q18/p2 Yes; the response granted a prayer for imprisonment. — Al-Tabataba'i reports this interpretation only to reject it on the wording of the sequence. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 12:35/q19 How does this turn in Joseph's story connect to the wider narrative?  [* · links]
- 12:35/q19/p1 The imprisonment begins another trial and becomes the route to Joseph's eventual vindication and authority. — Al-Tabataba'i links the years in prison with monotheist teaching, disclosure of his family, and his later proximity to the king; Ibn Kathir points to the later summons in 12:50 and Joseph's insistence on exoneration. Al-Islahi calls it another trial that Joseph desired over surrendering his faith. Asad compares the Qur'anic account with Genesis and invokes 2:216: people may dislike what benefits them, while God's direction of affairs is hidden. [6 notes: TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 12:35/q20 What inward meaning is given to Joseph's imprisonment?  [* · inward]
turns on: The two inward readings map prison to different inner subjects: spirit in seclusion, or heart held by law and protection.
- 12:35/q20/p1 It is the spirit's seclusion, agreed by spirit, soul, faculties, intellect and thought for vision, defeat of illusion, guidance and the authority of thought. — Al-Qashani connects spiritual retreat with failed attraction and the inner faculties' agreement. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 12:35/q20/p2 The heart is confined by religious law and divine protection when it resists worldly and lower-self prompting. — Al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya, as cited by Bursavi, says even a prophet's heart would incline without divine protection. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 12:35/q21 What general moral or historical point does the imprisonment illustrate?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:35/q21/p1 It shows wrongful social power and women's plotting: a compliant husband jailed an innocent man to protect appearances. — Abu Hayyan stresses a husband yielding authority despite his wife's treachery; al-Biqa'i calls the choice unjust. Islahi observes that masters had unrestricted power over enslaved people and that the Aziz's royal guard office removed obstacles. Kuranyolu says the household displaced blame onto the enslaved Joseph. Asad cites Zamakhshari's camel-reins simile for the wife's control. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari)]

## 12:35/q22 Which terms in this narrative does Abu Hayyan explain lexically?  [* · meaning]
- 12:35/q22/p1 His opening list treats imprisonment alongside trade, gentle entreaty, seeking marriage, locking, tearing cloth, and master or chief. — Abu Hayyan lists these lexical explanations for the surrounding narrative without a further argument in this note. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
