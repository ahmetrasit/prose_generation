# 68:20: 11 questions

## 68:20/q01 What does أصبح convey here?  [فَأَصْبَحَتْ · meaning]
- 68:20/q01/p1 The garden entered the morning in its ruined state. — Al-Jishumī explicitly glosses aṣbaḥa as entering morning. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 68:20/q02 What does كالصريم liken the ruined garden to?  [كَٱلصَّرِيمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which attested sense or referent of ṣarīm supplies the simile: darkness, a cut or barren state, dawn/day, ashes, sand, the named place, or the unusual gold gloss; the notes also differ over whether the trees themselves or only their fruit were removed.
- 68:20/q02/p1 A black, dark night: the burned garden was blackened. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj and others tie the color to burning. Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ specifies the moonless last night of a month. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī cite a poem about a long dark night that dawn does not clear; al-Ṭabarī also cites poetry. Shammar and others say night and day are both ṣarīm because each separates from the other. Another derivation makes night ṣarīm because darkness stops movement or sight; al-Wāḥidī notes that this fails to explain the day usage, and al-Qushayrī objects to it. Qatāda's report says God's command came at night; Qaṭr ibn Maymūn agrees. Some authors present this alongside other possible comparisons. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites al-Nābigha's use of the related aṣrām in a dark-night comparison and reports Abū ʿAmr al-Shaybānī's citation of Zuhayr as another witness for the night sense. She reports al-Rāghib's alternative image of trees blackened as though burned. [57 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الشاعر,الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء والزجاج,شمر,غيره,مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء ومنذر وجماعة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء، ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس والفراء,شمر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء والقاضي منذر بن سعيد وجماعة,شمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الثوري,منذر والفراء وجماعة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BURHAN(قيل); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو عمرو الشيباني,ابن عباس,ابن قتيبة,الأصمعي وأبو حاتم السجستاني,الراغب,الفراء,النابغة); FARRA+; MUQATIL+; QUSHAYRI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 68:20/q02/p2 A harvested orchard or crop with its fruit cut away and nothing left. — Ibn Qutayba says the fruit looked cut and harvested. Al-Qushayrī and al-Bursawī say the fire left a harvested field, and al-Bursawī specifies nothing remained. Al-Thawrī and al-Suddī describe dry harvested stubble. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī explain ṣarīm as a passive-form adjective, 'cut'; al-Biqāʿī specifies trees stripped of fruit. ʿAṭāʾ argues that fruit removed while trees remain better fits a fire than trees physically cut down. The Study Quran renders it 'like a garden plucked.' Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāghib's explanation of trees stripped of fruit and favors cutting or harvesting on contextual grounds: the owners had sworn to harvest the garden, not make it disappear. [21 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(الثوري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب,سفيان); ALUSI-FULL+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; BINTSHATI+; BURSEVI+]
- 68:20/q02/p3 A white, bare dawn or day, like a dried and harvested field. — Al-Akhfash derives ṣarīm for dawn from its separation from night; al-Mubarrad says daylight with nothing left in the orchard. Al-Thawrī says it whitened like harvested grain; the opposing account says it turned black. Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Qurṭubī connects the day sense to vanished trees and crops and bare soil. Shammar and others explain both temporal senses from night and day cutting one another off. [23 notes: THALABI(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش,شمر); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سفيان الثوري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الأخفش,المبرد,شمر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش,الثوري,المبرد,شمر); ALUSI-FULL(الثوري); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN(قيل)]
- 68:20/q02/p4 A detached, barren patch of sand or dune, including a reported Yemeni sandy tract. — Abū ʿUbayda, al-Muʾarrij and others use ṣarīm or ṣarīmah for sand severed from a larger dune. Al-Aṣmaʿī calls a large separated section ṣarāʾim and relates its barrenness to the charred fruitless orchard. Ibn ʿAbbās and later authors report a known barren sandy tract in Yemen; al-Muʾarrij emphasizes that nothing useful grows there. Some reports thus treat the tract as a known referent, others as a generic sand image. [20 notes: MAJAZ; THALABI(المروج); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي,المؤرج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); TABRISI(مؤرج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المؤرج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,مورج); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,مؤرج); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 68:20/q02/p5 Black ashes, in a reported tribal dialect. — Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās; the tribal name varies in the notes as Khudhaym, Khuzaima, Khuzaymī, Judhayma or Khuzaʿa. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Judhayma or Khuzayma. Al-Elmālī additionally reports the orchard uprooted and burned black like ash. [15 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(جذيمة أو خزيمة); BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس)]
- 68:20/q02/p6 A garden from which all good was cut off, leaving nothing. — Al-Ḥasan and authors reporting him apply the root sense 'cut off' to its good rather than specifying its color or a physical harvest. Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī connect this to a passive sense of ṣarīm. [7 notes: THALABI(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)]
- 68:20/q02/p7 Something wholly severed or cut off, without specifying a single physical comparison. — Al-Naḥḥās calls ṣarīm equivalent to maṣrūm, as qatīl can mean maqtūl; al-Bayḍāwī likewise allows passive faʿīl. Al-Thaʿlabī says this is the basic meaning and explains night and dawn by their mutual separation. Al-Elmālī extends it to cut fruit, harvested land, a portion of night and barren sand. Al-Biqāʿī also mentions something severed from its destination. Al-Wāḥidī reports the alternative active-form derivation from darkness cutting off movement, but says it cannot account for day. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports Abū Ḥātim's general 'cut off' sense, al-Rāghib's derivation from ṣarm as severance, and Ibn al-Athīr's cutting gloss. [13 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(غيره); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الأصمعي وأبو حاتم السجستاني,الراغب); ELMALILI+; NAHHAS+; THALABI+]
- 68:20/q02/p8 Cut-down trees or an orchard severed at the roots and burned. — Qatāda takes it as an orchard physically cut down. Al-Rāzī says burned trees resembled fruitless, cut trees. Al-Elmālī transmits a stronger reading in which the whole orchard, not merely its fruit, was uprooted and burned black. ʿAṭāʾ objects that fire burns trees rather than making them resemble a harvested orchard and favors fruit removed while trees remain. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس)-]
- 68:20/q02/p9 Al-Ṣarīm, a particular Yemeni place called Ḍarwān near Ṣanʿāʾ. — Al-Ṭabarī reports the place identification generally and through Saʿīd ibn Jubayr; the latter locates Ḍarwān six miles from Ṣanʿāʾ. Other authors describe a known barren Yemeni sand without this place name. Al-Itqān reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr calling al-Ṣarīm a land in Yemen bearing that name, without specifying Ḍarwān or its distance from Ṣanʿāʾ. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل,سعيد بن جبير); ITQAN(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 68:20/q02/p10 A black jar, to which the burned garden was compared. — Ibn Kaysān supplies this comparison. [1 notes: THALABI(ابن كيسان)]
- 68:20/q02/p11 Gold, as a reported sense of al-ṣarīm. — In a dialogue with Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq, Ibn ʿAbbās gives this sense and cites an anonymous poem using the word. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 68:20/q02/p12 The garden was like something gone or vanished. — Al-Itqān gives 'that which has gone' as a gloss and begins to introduce a poetic witness whose line is absent from the excerpt. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports Ibn ʿAbbās giving the same sense with a poetic usage; she rejects it in favor of cutting or harvesting because the owners intended to harvest, not make the garden disappear. [2 notes: ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]

## 68:20/q03 Why can night be called ṣarīm?  [كَٱلصَّرِيمِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name comes from the mutual separation of night and day or from an effect peculiar to darkness; day also being called ṣarīm is the stated objection to the latter.
- 68:20/q03/p1 Night and day are severed from one another, so each can bear the name. — Al-Thaʿlabī, Shammar, al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others explain the two temporal senses by mutual separation. Al-Qushayrī points to the day usage when objecting to a derivation based only on darkness stopping activity. Ibn Qutayba explicitly uses the mutual separation of night and day to explain both senses, as Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports; she also transmits Abū Ḥātim's same explanation. [16 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(شمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(الأخفش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء، ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(شمر); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(شمر); ALUSI-FULL(الثوري); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن قتيبة,الأصمعي وأبو حاتم السجستاني); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; JISHUMI+; THALABI+]
- 68:20/q03/p2 Darkness cuts off activity or sight, hence night bears the name. — A linguist reported by al-Wāḥidī derives it as an active form from darkness restricting movement, while al-Rāzī also mentions the cutting off of sight. Al-Wāḥidī says it fails to explain the day sense; al-Qushayrī rejects it on that same ground. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(غيره); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(شمر); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)-]

## 68:20/q04 What happened to the garden overnight?  [* · reports]
- 68:20/q04/p1 A divine visitation or calamity struck while the owners slept and burned or devastated the orchard. — Al-Ṭabarī notes that a ṭāʾif ordinarily arrives at night unless daytime is specified. Al-Farrāʾ describes a fire and says the sons first thought they had lost their way, then understood their refusal of the poor. Al-Qushayrī says a calamity from heaven burned its fruits. Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also describe a nocturnal divine visitation; al-Durr transmits Qatāda and Qaṭr ibn Maymūn. Al-Elmālī calls the result a scorched husk. Iṣlāḥī says attribution to God marks a wholly unexpected, instantaneous, miraculous devastation. [10 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; QUSHAYRI+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]

## 68:20/q05 What do the owners' loss and the ruined garden teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw complementary lessons from the same loss: deprivation for wrongdoing, repentance after a trial, and the reversal of a sworn plan.
- 68:20/q05/p1 Their refusal and sin caused the loss; sin may deprive a person of expected provision. — Al-Farrāʾ says the owners recognized their refusal to help the poor as the cause. Al-Biqāʿī says heirs who sought everything and withheld charity lost the orchard entirely. A report from Ibn Masʿūd and reports attributed to the Prophet apply the warning that sin withholds prepared provision to the owners' lost crop; the latter also mentions lost knowledge and missed night prayer. [5 notes: FARRA; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); BIQAI-FULL+]
- 68:20/q05/p2 The loss could be punishment or a trial that became a grace through repentance. — Al-Jishumī gives both possibilities because the owners repented. Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise says repentance remained open to the survivors, as it did for the brothers. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 68:20/q05/p3 Their sworn plan was fulfilled against them, unexpectedly and by another hand. — Al-Elmālī says the oath came true in a harmful way they neither intended nor executed. Iṣlāḥī says punishment can ruin a plan secured by pledges just as people wake to enact it. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI+]

## 68:20/q06 How does this episode address Quraysh and reassure the Prophet?  [* · links]
- 68:20/q06/p1 Quraysh can likewise be punished in this life despite apparent security, while repentance remains possible; God's swift ruin reassures the Prophet. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares Quraysh's possible worldly punishment and the brothers' opportunity to repent. Iṣlāḥī says 'from your Lord' reassures the Prophet amid Quraysh's ease, and the parable answers those who call him possessed because they feel secure. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 68:20/q07 What rhetorical effects connect this wording to the surrounding account?  [فَأَصْبَحَتْ كَٱلصَّرِيمِ · rhetoric]
- 68:20/q07/p1 The morning verb contrasts with the possible night sense of ṣarīm; ṣarīm echoes the earlier verb for harvesting. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies antithesis between aṣbaḥat and night, paronomasia between yaṣrimunnahā and ṣarīm, and favors the word's multiple fitting senses. Al-Elmālī observes that the owners' oath was fulfilled in a harmful, unplanned way. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 68:20/q08 What does a transmitted account say about the garden's origin and location?  [* · reports]
- 68:20/q08/p1 It belonged to a righteous man and passed to his people and town; reports place it near Ṣanʿāʾ, in Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports these variants in the exegetical elaboration of the story, without choosing a location. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 68:20/q09 How is the owners' morning call connected to their oath?  [* · links]
- 68:20/q09/p1 The following fāʾ makes their call to one another follow from the oath as they rise to carry out the plan. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the fāʾ in fa-tanādaw, following the garden's morning ruin in the narrative. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 68:20/q10 How should the competing senses of الصريم be assessed?  [كَٱلصَّرِيمِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the surrounding oath supplies a contextual clue favoring cutting or harvesting over disappearance.
- 68:20/q10/p1 Use a clue to choose the apparent sense; seek clarification for conflicting senses, or suspend judgment if equally possible senses cannot be combined. — Al-Burhān reports Ibn al-Qushayrī's general rule for a word with multiple senses. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن القشيري)]
- 68:20/q10/p2 The context favors cutting or harvesting over the gloss 'gone.' — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues that the owners swore to harvest the garden, not make it vanish. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]

## 68:20/q11 Which supplied notes give no direct explanation of this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 68:20/q11/p1 These excerpts discuss other Arabic usage, grammar, poetry, rhetoric, Qur'anic style, historical counsel, and borrowed vocabulary without explaining 68:20. — Sībawayh discusses descriptive epithets, insult, compassion, and their parses. Ibn Qutayba discusses contrary names and the preposition bāʾ; Ibn al-Jawzī also discusses bāʾ. Al-Bāqillānī reports Abū Bakr's succession counsel, evaluates poetry against Qur'anic composition, and discusses the jinn challenge. Al-Jurjānī analyzes other similes and poetic syntax. Al-Sāmarrāʾī discusses Arabized vocabulary. None of these notes states an interpretation of the garden, its ruin, or ṣarīm.
