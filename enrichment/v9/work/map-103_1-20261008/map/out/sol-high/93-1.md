# 93:1: 26 questions

## 93:1/q01 What does al-ḍuḥā denote in this oath?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term denotes a whole day, a particular time or light, or an event or symbolic referent.
- 93:1/q01/p1 The whole daylight or daytime. — Tabari, Tha'labi, Baghawi, Tabrisi, Razi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Kuranyolu and Shawkani appeal to its contrast with night; Baghawi, Kashshaf, Qurtubi and Alusi cite 7:98, Qurtubi also 7:97. Mujahid links it with exposure to sun. Alusi says the objection assumes an intensely dark night without proof. Bint al-Shati reports Tabari's choice of visible daylight, but cites 79:46, 20:59 and 91:1 against the whole-day sense. Al-Ṭabarī cites 20:119 for exposure to sunlight; al-Māwardī attributes this derivation to ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā. Al-Ālūsī reports an objection based on an allegedly intense night, then denies that the qualifying phrase requires intensified darkness. [35 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الطبري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğu); STUDYQURAN(al-Shawkānī,most); MAWARDI(مجاهد); KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(قتادة); RAZI(أهل التفسير); BAYDAWI; NASAFI; DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI(قيل); QURTUBI+; TAB+; BINTSHATI-; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-; ALUSI(معترض)-]
- 93:1/q01/p2 A defined forenoon or midmorning after sunrise. — Qatada and Muqatil describe temperate heat or cold. Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Alusi, Elmalili, Islahi and Samarrai describe the sun rising high and strong light. Alusi prefers a time over sunlight or sunrise because of the next oath. Bint al-Shati cites 79:46, 20:59 and 91:1 for a particular hour; Abduh calls it sunlight in the day's prime. Study Quran gives daybreak-to-noon as a wider range. Bint al-Shati stresses that the oath is limited to forenoon rather than daylight generally, and that its light can be weaker than at noon. Al-Ālūsī specifically distinguishes the time when the sun has appeared above the horizon from sunlight itself or the sun's height, finding the time more fitting to the following oath. [26 notes: NAHHAS(أبو روق); THALABI(قتادة ومقاتل); BAGHAWI(قتادة ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الشيخ محمد عبده); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(most); RAZI(أهل التفسير); BAYDAWI; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; BINTSHATI+; KASHSHAF+; NASAFI+]
- 93:1/q01/p3 The hour Moses' opponents prostrated. — Tha'labi, Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursawi, Alusi and Study Quran relate it to the gathering at 20:59. Razi sees a promise that God can honor Muhammad and turn his enemies' hearts. Bint al-Shati calls the reading anachronistic because these passages came later. [14 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(others); RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF; QURTUBI(قيل); BAYDAWI; ALUSI(قيل); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 93:1/q01/p4 The beginning or first hour of day. — Qatada calls it an hour or the start; Suddi and Muqatil specify the sun moving onward. Jishumi derives it from appearing in sunlight. Bursawi allows metonymy for the time or an omitted noun. [15 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي,قتادة); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي ومقاتل,قتادة); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(قتادة); TAB(قتادة); MAWARDI(السدي,قتادة); DURR(قتادة)]
- 93:1/q01/p5 Sunlight, brightness, or visible exposure. — Ibn Atiyya calls it the rise and strength of light; Tabari's daylight is visible sunlight; Ibn Kathir swears by forenoon and its light. Study Quran says the title most commonly means rising sunlight and can extend to all creation it reveals. Bint al-Shati says the root centers on visibility; Wahidi and Ibn Ashur refer to the parallel at 91:1. Yaqub derives aḍḥā from sheep sacrificed at this hour. Ibn ʿĀshūr directs readers to his explanation of the term at 91:1. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الطبري,يعقوب); STUDYQURAN(most); IBNASHUR; BIQAI+; IBNATIYYA+; IBNKATHIR+]
- 93:1/q01/p6 The morning when God spoke to Moses. — Tha'labi, Qushayri, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Bursawi and Study Quran report this; Ja'far pairs it with the Night Journey, citing 7:143 and 17:1. Bint al-Shati objects that the cited passages were revealed later. [7 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة ومقاتل وجعفر الصادق); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(جعفر الصادق); STUDYQURAN(others); QURTUBI(قتادة ومقاتل وجعفر الصادق); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 93:1/q01/p7 The forenoon prayer. — Qushayri lists it; Study Quran notes the term also names a late-morning supererogatory prayer. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN(most)]
- 93:1/q01/p8 The Prophet's radiant face. — Bursawi cites a Persian couplet pairing it with his dark hair; Elmalili reports the association. Bint al-Shati excludes such esoteric identifications. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q01/p9 Sunrise itself. — Qutrub identifies it with sunrise. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قطرب); MAWARDI(قطرب)]
- 93:1/q01/p10 The dawn of the Muhammadan mission and truth. — Elmalili prefers a specific dawn of truth radiating mercy and light over a generic definite article. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]

## 93:1/q02 How is the final vowel of al-ḍuḥā written and explained?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether spelling follows spoken form, root origin or muṣḥaf and neighboring endings.
- 93:1/q02/p1 Its final alif is written in yāʾ shape in the muṣḥaf despite a wāw root. — Ibn Ashur says this follows neighboring endings, mostly yāʾ-derived, supports imāla in relevant dialects, and cites Ibn Malik. Nahhas notes inflected forms shift toward yāʾ. Ibn Mālik, cited by Ibn ʿĀshūr, describes dialectal imāla induced by a neighboring inclined alif. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNASHUR(ابن مالك); IBNASHUR+]
- 93:1/q02/p2 It should be written with final alif according to pronunciation. — Muhammad ibn Yazid derives it from ḍaḥā yaḍḥū and cites rāmā and ghazā. Nahhas rejects root-based Kufan spelling as inconsistent with speech and muṣḥaf. Abu Ishaq cites mā zakkā and rabayān against Qur'anic yarbū at 30:39. [5 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,محمد بن يزيد); NAHHAS+]
- 93:1/q02/p3 The yāʾ-root spelling convention was politically promoted. — Ali ibn Sulayman transmits an allegation that Akhfash and Kisa'i devised it for profit and influence and that Kisa'i's political power spread it. [1 notes: NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان)]

## 93:1/q03 How is the ending of al-ḍuḥā recited?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Different reader transmissions and whether the final alif behaves as yāʾ-derived.
- 93:1/q03/p1 Open pronunciation without imāla. — Ibn Kathir, Asim and Ibn Amir open endings here and in al-Layl; Ibn Khalawayh says the difference here is openness versus imāla. Some Nafi, Warsh and Qalun transmissions also open all, while one opens talāhā alone. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر,نافع)]
- 93:1/q03/p2 Full imāla. — Kisa'i inclines these endings throughout; Hamza inclines yāʾ-origin forms but darkens wāw-origin forms. Abbas and a report from Abu Amr incline selected endings. Nahhas licenses inclination when a wāw root shifts to yāʾ. Farisi cites yāʾ-like behavior, derived verbs and nouns, and says rare inclined wāw forms do not overturn the general rule. Al-Burhān explains imāla as sound correspondence, including influence from adjacent endings such as talāhā, jalāhā and yaghshāhā in Sūrat al-Shams. [8 notes: NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,أبو عمرو ونافع,الكسائي); BURHAN+]
- 93:1/q03/p3 Intermediate inclination. — Nahhas gives Medinan intermediate treatment; Farisi reports it for Nafi and Abu Amr, among variant transmissions. Abu Amr may follow the sūra's ending pattern. [4 notes: NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,أبو عمرو ونافع,نافع)]

## 93:1/q04 What is the grammatical object of the oath?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the expressed time is itself the oath object or an omitted Lord is supplied.
- 93:1/q04/p1 An omitted 'Lord' is understood: by the Lord of morning and night. — Semantic scholars in Tha'labi and Qurtubi and a view in Tabrisi supply the ellipsis. [4 notes: THALABI(أهل المعاني); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; QURTUBI(أهل المعاني)]
- 93:1/q04/p2 Al-ḍuḥā itself is sworn by. — Ibn Khalawayh calls it an oath; Tabari identifies ḍuḥā as the oath object. Al-Itqān includes the forenoon among created things by which the Qur'an swears. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; TAB-FULL; ITQAN+]

## 93:1/q05 Why is the forenoon singled out for an oath?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether its sensory quality, a past event, or a divine sign supplies the point.
- 93:1/q05/p1 It recalls divine speech to Moses and the magicians' prostration. — Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi, Bursawi, Alusi and Elmalili cite 20:59 and God's ability to honor Muhammad and change his enemies. Bint al-Shati rejects this appeal as anachronistic because the Moses passages were revealed later. Al-Qurṭubī transmits Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq's pairing of Moses' morning with the night of the Ascension; al-Ālūsī relates the event directly to reassurance that God has not left the Prophet. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KASHSHAF; QURTUBI(قتادة ومقاتل وجعفر الصادق,قيل); NASAFI; ALUSI(قيل); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 93:1/q05/p2 It is the day's vigorous, bright and honored phase. — Razi says people gather and find ease after night, offering comfort after the revelation pause. Biqa'i calls it the brightest portion, suited to the most honored creature. Alusi calls it the sun's youthful daily honor; Islahi connects it with renewed work. Al-Biqāʿī calls the span gentle and expansive and relates its nobility to the highest-ranked creation, with Abū Bakr first among the pious. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI+; BIQAI+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 93:1/q05/p3 Its light is a great divine sign and blessing. — Abduh emphasizes the light's grandeur. Ibn Kathir says the contrast shows Creator power. Ibn al-Qayyim appeals to lordship, wisdom and mercy, which Bint al-Shati says misses the occasion's abandonment claim. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(ابن قيم الجوزية,الشيخ محمد عبده); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BINTSHATI-; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]

## 93:1/q06 What does the oath form do rhetorically?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the oath primarily magnifies its object or prepares the reader for an unseen point.
- 93:1/q06/p1 It glorifies what is sworn by as a great sign. — Bint al-Shati reports Ibn al-Qayyim's account as the prevailing earlier view; Abduh stresses the light's greatness. [2 notes: BINTSHATI(ابن قيم الجوزية,الشيخ محمد عبده)]
- 93:1/q06/p2 It directs attention to familiar senses to prepare for unseen meaning. — Bint al-Shati prefers this role for the wāw, and says any impression of glorification strengthens attention. The chosen sensory quality must suit context. Al-Burhān points to the cadence of the sūra's opening oaths as a formal feature. [3 notes: BINTSHATI; BURHAN; BINTSHATI+]

## 93:1/q07 What inward or symbolic reality is signified by ḍuḥā?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · inward]
turns on: The different spiritual correspondences assigned to daylight.
- 93:1/q07/p1 Spirit or God's unveiling of an inner secret. — Tustari identifies the spirit; Ibn Ata says God unveils the secret as the servant is occupied with calling people through attention to creation. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 93:1/q07/p2 The station of witnessing. — Junayd pairs it with night's inner-eye station or ghayn, clouding over the heart in a Prophetic report; Sulami and Bursawi transmit related forms. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); BURSEVI(الجنيد)]
- 93:1/q07/p3 The Prophet's radiant face. — Bursawi's Persian couplet pairs face with hair. Bint al-Shati excludes this and other esoteric pairs including relatives, knowledge/forgiveness, and Islam's rise/estrangement. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q07/p4 Divine disclosure, gentleness and beauty. — Kashf al-Asrar pairs it with night's veiling, severity and majesty. [1 notes: BURSEVI(صاحب كشف الأسرار)]
- 93:1/q07/p5 The Garden's light or the light of knowers' hearts. — Study Quran reports counterparts in the Fire's darkness and unbelievers' darkened hearts. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(others); QURTUBI]
- 93:1/q07/p6 Worshippers who serve God at forenoon, paired with worshippers at nightfall. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this interpretation of the two oaths. [1 notes: QURTUBI]

## 93:1/q08 What is the status and form of the ḍuḥā prayer?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · law]
turns on: The jurists differ over the number of units.
- 93:1/q08/p1 It is a sunnah from sun-high time until shortly before noon. — Bursawi calls this unanimous and cites a sound report that the Prophet prayed eight units in Umm Hani's house on the conquest of Mecca, having prayed it earlier too. [2 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله)]
- 93:1/q08/p2 Two or four units with one salutation. — Bursawi reports Abu Hanifa's prescription. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو حنيفة)]
- 93:1/q08/p3 No fixed upper limit. — Bursawi reports Malik's view. [1 notes: BURSEVI(مالك)]
- 93:1/q08/p4 At least two, at most twelve units. — Bursawi reports Shafi'i's count. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الشافعي)]
- 93:1/q08/p5 At least two, at most eight units. — Bursawi reports Ahmad's count, followed by most Shafi'i authorities and endorsed by Nawawi in al-Tahqiq. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الشافعي)]
- 93:1/q08/p6 The two midmorning units may use al-Shams and al-Ḍuḥā. — Durr transmits Uqba ibn Amir's Prophetic instruction. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عقبة بن عامر)]

## 93:1/q09 What does the daylight-and-night pairing suggest about revelation and the Prophet?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How the sensory alternation relates to revelation's pause, guidance, divine care and the oath's answer.
- 93:1/q09/p1 Alternation of light and night images revelation's pause and renewal without abandonment. — Razi and Nisaburi compare waxing and waning by divine wisdom with sending and withholding revelation. Bint al-Shati prefers the sensory analogy. Elmalili sees revealed knowledge and rest before renewal. Ibn al-Qayyim likens light after darkness to revelation after its pause, but Bint al-Shati says his lordship rationale misses the stated occasion. Al-Biqāʿī describes revelation's pause as a trial for the Prophet, who brings good in religion, this world and the afterlife. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(ابن قيم الجوزية,الشيخ محمد عبده,فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري); BINTSHATI+; BIQAI+; BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q09/p2 Day is revelation and guidance; night suggests recitation, reflection and calm. — Ibn Ashur says nearby idolaters heard the Prophet's night recitation and cites 73:2-3. Qutb sees a tender setting where the spirit senses creation worshipping, cosmic companionship and graceful musical cadence. Kuranyolu reports Ibn Ashur's sunlight analogy. Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the night qualification to the Prophet's night prayer in 73:2–3, whose recitation nearby polytheists heard. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); IBNASHUR+]
- 93:1/q09/p3 The contrast answers the disbelievers' unsupported taunt. — Razi and Nisaburi say the taunters could not prove abandonment. Ibn Ashur says the oath targets their inference from missed night recitation, while the Prophet himself had no doubt. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the oath's emphasis indirectly rebukes the polytheists because the Prophet did not doubt God's report. [4 notes: BINTSHATI(فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري); IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR+]
- 93:1/q09/p4 Light and dark illustrate changing spiritual and epistemic states. — Biqa'i applies mixed and pure light to clear and ambiguous teachings, bright hearts and darkening appetite; obscurity can yield certainty through remembering signs and seeking refuge. Bint al-Shati relates the senses to guidance or falsehood; Razi/Nisaburi say all creatures undergo change. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI(فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري)]
- 93:1/q09/p5 Night's rest and day's work together are divine mercy. — Islahi cites 10:67 and 28:73. Abduh says quiet night restores strength after first revelation; Bint al-Shati says that account nearly loses the sensory analogy. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده); BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q09/p6 The contrast visibly demonstrates the Creator's power. — Ibn Kathir relates this pairing to 92:1-2 and 6:96. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 93:1/q09/p7 Day and night model developmental trials leading to relief and duties. — Islahi traces movement from the models to reassurance, better times, past help and duties arising from favor. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:1/q09/p8 Earlier explanations fail to connect the oath clearly to its answer. — Bint al-Shati faults Tabari, Zamakhshari and Abu Hayyan for lacking a clear connection and for not comparing explicit Qur'anic denials of oaths. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/q09/p9 The day-and-night oath testifies to God's favor and honor of the Prophet, affirming prophethood, resurrection, and recompense. — Itqan says the oath by forenoon and night bears witness to God's favor and honor of the Prophet and thereby confirms his prophethood and the resurrection and recompense. [1 notes: ITQAN+]

## 93:1/q10 Why does this sūra place day before night?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order emphasizes daylight's honor, the duration of joy and sorrow, or moral stages.
- 93:1/q10/p1 Brief joy is set against much longer sorrow. — Bursawi equates one forenoon hour to the whole night as Muhammad equals all prophets. He cites a cloud raining grief for 300 years versus a cloud raining joy for an hour. Bint al-Shati reports the Razi/Nisaburi prophet comparison. [3 notes: BURSEVI; BINTSHATI(النيسابوري والرازي)]
- 93:1/q10/p2 Day leads here for its inherent and contextual honor. — Bursawi says night precedes in al-Layl because darkness is the original state; sunlight makes day, then air returns to darkness at sunset. He cites 6:1. Al-Biqāʿī calls forenoon the noblest, gentlest and most luminous period of the day. [2 notes: BURSEVI; BIQAI]
- 93:1/q10/p3 Life is preferred before warning of despair and divine plotting. — Bint al-Shati reports an interpretation giving this staged moral order. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين)]

## 93:1/q11 What interrupted revelation before this sūra?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports name different circumstances before revelation resumed.
- 93:1/q11/p1 Illness prevented night prayer for one or two, or two or three, nights. — Jundub's versions vary. Ibn Ashur understands the taunt as a reaction to absent nighttime Quran recitation. Al-Itqān cites the report in both Ṣaḥīḥ collections; Ibn Ḥajar prefers it to the puppy account. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI; BAGHAWI(جندب بن سفيان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(جندب البجلي); SUYUTI-LUBAB(جندب); STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR(جندب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASHR(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); DURR(جندب البجلي); ITQAN(جندب); IBNASHUR(جندب بن سفيان البجلي); ITQAN(ابن حجر)+]
- 93:1/q11/p2 Gabriel or revelation paused, with no precipitating cause specified. — Tabari, Ibn Khalawayh, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Suyuti, Qutb and Study Quran relate pause and ensuing taunt. Qutb says Gabriel's visits sustained the Prophet through rejection and persecution, so absence distressed him. Ibn al-Jazarī treats the pause reported by Jundub as firmly established; al-Tirmidhī grades the report cited by Ibn ʿĀshūr ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. Al-Durr identifies reports from Qatāda and al-Ḍaḥḥāk as mursal. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; BAGHAWI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مشركون); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,جندب,زيد بن أرقم,عروة,قتادة والضحاك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(جندب,زيد بن أرقم,عبد الله بن شداد,عروة); QUTB-ZILAL(روايات كثيرة); STUDYQURAN(others,several commentators); IBNKATHIR(جندب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASHR(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); DURR(ابن عباس,جندب,قتادة والضحاك); IBNASHUR(جندب البجلي); NASHR+]
- 93:1/q11/p3 A puppy or dead puppy was in the Prophet's house. — Zayd ibn Aslam says Gabriel explained angels avoid a house with a dog or image. Khawla and Umm Hafs locate a dead puppy under the bed and give four days. Ibn Hajar says a puppy-related delay is known, but rejects this as the cause of the sūra because it conflicts with sound reports. Al-Itqān's Khawla report says a dead puppy beneath a bed kept Gabriel away; Ibn Ḥajar calls the causal attribution strange because its chain has an unknown narrator. [7 notes: THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI(زيد بن أسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(أم حفص); SUYUTI-LUBAB(خولة); ITQAN(خولة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن حجر)-; ITQAN(ابن حجر)-]
- 93:1/q11/p4 The Prophet promised answers to Jewish questions without saying 'if God wills'. — Tha'labi, Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi name Dhu al-Qarnayn, the Cave Companions and the Spirit. [3 notes: THALABI(المفسرون); BAGHAWI(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 93:1/q11/p5 A stone injured the Prophet's finger, after which he missed night prayer. — A second Jundub version gives two or three nights. Ibn Kathir calls the injury authentic but the causal link unusual; Durr cites Tirmidhi's authentication. Ibn Kathīr accepts the finger verse as authentic in the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections but calls its role as the sūra's cause unusual; Ibn al-Jazarī likewise questions that causal link. Al-Tirmidhī graded the finger report cited by al-Durr authentic. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(جندب); IBNKATHIR(جندب); NASHR(ابن أبي حاتم); DURR(جندب); IBNASHUR(جندب البجلي,جندب بن سفيان البجلي); IBNKATHIR-; NASHR-]
- 93:1/q11/p6 The Prophet or companions neglected grooming. — Tha'labi reports the Prophet referring to unclean spaces between fingers and untrimmed nails; Ibn al-Jawzi lists a companion's lack of cleanliness. [2 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 93:1/q12 How long was the reported interruption?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted numerical counts differ.

## 93:1/q13 Who voiced the claim that the Prophet had been abandoned?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the speaker was hostile, compassionate, or identified as a cousin's daughter.
- 93:1/q13/p1 Polytheists or Quraysh collectively. — Their taunt follows Gabriel's delay; Razi/Nisaburi say they lacked proof, and Ibn Ashur ties their insinuation to missed nighttime recitation. [18 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI; BAGHAWI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مشركون); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة والضحاك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(جندب); QUTB-ZILAL(روايات كثيرة); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري); STUDYQURAN(others); IBNKATHIR(جندب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASHR(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); DURR(ابن عباس,جندب,قتادة والضحاك); IBNASHUR(جندب البجلي)]
- 93:1/q13/p2 A woman, identified in some accounts as Umm Jamil, Abu Lahab's wife. — Jundub calls her a woman. Tha'labi, Baghawi, Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Kathir, Ibn al-Jawzi and Zayd ibn Arqam name Umm Jamil. She calls his companion a devil or Satan. Durr places her confrontation after Sūrat Abī Lahab. Ibn Hajar says her remark was spiteful. Al-Durr's Zayd ibn Arqam account places Abū Lahab's wife's taunt after Sūrat al-Masad. Ibn ʿĀshūr doubts Jundub could have heard her taunt, given his age and possible post-Hijra conversion, and suspects two reports were joined. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI; BAGHAWI(جندب بن سفيان,قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جندب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(جندب,جندب البجلي,زيد بن أرقم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(جندب,زيد بن أرقم,عروة); STUDYQURAN(several commentators); IBNKATHIR(جندب,جندب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); NASHR(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي,قيل); DURR(جندب,جندب البجلي,زيد بن أرقم); ITQAN(جندب); IBNASHUR(جندب بن سفيان البجلي); IBNASHUR-]
- 93:1/q13/p3 Khadija spoke with concern over apparent rejection. — Abdullah ibn Shaddad and Urwa transmit her remark after a pause or distress. Ibn Hajar distinguishes compassion from Umm Jamil's spite. Tha'labi relates this to the first pause after al-Alaq. The later reports are mursal. Ibn Kathīr notes that the two Khadīja reports are mursal and that attribution to her may be unpreserved or express sorrow. [9 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن شداد); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن شداد,عروة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عبد الله بن شداد,عروة); IBNKATHIR(خديجة); DURR(خديجة,عروة)]
- 93:1/q13/p4 A cousin's daughter said his companion had left him. — Durr's Jundub report gives this identification. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(جندب); NASHR(قيل); DURR(جندب)]

## 93:1/q14 What followed Gabriel's return and reassurance?  [* · reports]
turns on: The accounts describe different responses to Gabriel's return.
- 93:1/q14/p1 The Prophet and Gabriel expressed mutual longing; Gabriel said he only descends by God's command. — Tha'labi and Baghawi transmit the dialogue, and Baghawi relates it to 19:64. [3 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI]
- 93:1/q14/p2 The Prophet rejoiced and pronounced takbīr. — Ibn Khalawayh links the greeting, joy and takbīr to the forty-morning pause and the reassuring revelation. The qirāʾāt imams explain the takbīr as gratitude for exposing the polytheists' claim as false; others say it affirmed the Prophet's standing or expressed joy. Ibn Kathīr says al-Farrāʾ gave no isnād for the causal story, so it cannot be graded. [8 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNKATHIR(الفراء); BURHAN(سليم الرازي); NASHR(أبو الحسن بن غلبون، أبو عمرو الداني، أبو الحسن السخاوي,البزي,بعضهم,قيل); ITQAN(أبو العلاء الهمداني); NASHR(أبو الفدا بن كثير)-]
- 93:1/q14/p3 The Prophet praised God's graciousness and expectation of favor after the sūra was revealed. — Al-Durr transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's report of the Prophet's response to this revelation. [1 notes: DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 93:1/q15 How should the following phrase about night be understood beside this oath?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether sajā means arrival, departure or stillness.
- 93:1/q15/p1 Night settles, darkens, or grows still among its people. — Tabrisi cites poetry about a calm sea and night; Ibn Kathir adds darkness. Durr reports Qatada's quiet people. Bint al-Shati reports Tabari, Zamakhshari and Abduh on stillness. Al-Qurṭubī cites al-Aʿshā's calm sea, a rajaz line on a calm night, and Jarīr's still eyes as lexical evidence; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Aṣmaʿī describe night covering the day like cloth, while al-Ḥasan and Ibn ʿAbbās speak of darkness covering everything. Ibn ʿĀshūr says night stretches and darkness lasts; he gives sajā a wāw root. [17 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الشيخ محمد عبده,الطبري); QURTUBI(ابن عباس,الأعشى,الحسن وابن عباس,الراجز,الضحاك والأصمعي,جرير,مجاهد); IBNASHUR+; QURTUBI+]
- 93:1/q15/p2 Night arrives. — Durr gives Ibn Abbas's gloss; Bint al-Shati includes this among Tabari's reported alternatives. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI(الطبري); QURTUBI(سعيد بن جبير وقتادة)]
- 93:1/q15/p3 Night departs. — Bint al-Shati lists this among Tabari's reported alternatives. [2 notes: BINTSHATI(الطبري); QURTUBI(ابن عباس)]

## 93:1/q16 Where and when in the revelation sequence was this sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The notes preserve different revelation-order lists and first-complete-sūra claims.
- 93:1/q16/p1 It is Meccan. — Baghawi, Abu Hayyan, Durr, Ibn Abbas, Alusi and Bint al-Shati affirm Meccan origin; Study Quran calls it early Meccan. Bint al-Shati says there is no disagreement. Al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr state that the Meccan classification is agreed upon. [17 notes: BAGHAWI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN; MAWARDI(الجميع); KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; IBNKATHIR; DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI+; IBNASHUR+; IBNATIYYA+; QURTUBI+]
- 93:1/q16/p2 It followed al-ʿAlaq after Gabriel's first pause. — Abdullah ibn Shaddad gives this order and Khadija's concern. [1 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن شداد)]
- 93:1/q16/p3 It followed 'Read', al-Muddaththir and al-Qalam through 68:4. — Tha'labi gives this sequence. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 93:1/q16/p4 It is commonly eleventh, after al-Fajr. — Bint al-Shati gives this revelation rank. Ibn ʿĀshūr places it immediately before al-Inshirāḥ. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR+]
- 93:1/q16/p5 It is fourth in one list or seventh in another. — Study Quran reports both rankings, which vary in the note. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 93:1/q16/p6 It may be the first sūra revealed whole. — Study Quran reports this and the competing assignment to al-Fatiha. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 93:1/q16/p7 It was revealed when Gabriel appeared in his created form and descended at al-Abṭaḥ. — Ibn Kathīr attributes this account to Ibn Isḥāq and other early authorities and links it to 53:10. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR(ابن إسحاق وبعض السلف)]

## 93:1/q18 How is takbīr recited from this sūra onward?  [* · readings]
- 93:1/q18/p1 Say Allāhu akbar at each sūra ending from al-Ḍuḥā through the Qur'an's end. — Ibn Kathir's practice includes the basmala; Durr reports a Prophetic instruction. Ibn Khalawayh links the practice with the Prophet's joy when Gabriel returned. Ibn Kathīr says al-Bazzī alone transmitted the raised report; Abū Ḥātim and al-ʿUqaylī weakened him, and Ibn Khuzayma suspected a missing transmitter. Ibn al-Jazarī answers that many reliable narrators took it from al-Bazzī, although routes differ between Prophetic and Companion attribution. Al-Shāfiʿī praised its use in prayer as Sunnah. Al-Ḥalīmī compares it to magnifying God at Ramadan's completion; others see it marking the felt end of revelation. Sulaym al-Rāzī requires pauses around the formula. Ibn al-Jazarī records wider Meccan transmission, and al-Dānī describes continuing after al-Nās with al-Fātiḥa and 2:1–5 before the completion prayer. Al-Dānī rejects an inserted Sufyān in one chain. Ibn Jurayj recommends it inside and outside congregational prayer, and reports describe its use in Ramadan. [42 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(ابن كثير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR(آخرون,أبو الحسن البزي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعضهم); BURHAN(الحليمي,سليم الرازي); NASHR(أبو الفتح فارس بن أحمد,أبو عمرو الداني,ابن كثير,البزي,الشافعي,الشريف أبو عبد الله الكارزيني,سفيان بن عيينة,علي,عمر بن سهل وابن جريج,مجاهد,مجاهد وابن جريج,مجاهد وابن عباس,محمد بن عمر بن عيسى وأبوه,مكي); DURR(عبد الله بن كثير); ITQAN(أبو العلاء الهمداني,الحاكم,الحليمي,السخاوي وأبو شامة,سليم الرازي,محمد بن إدريس الشافعي); IBNKATHIR(الشافعي)+; ITQAN+; NASHR(أبو الطيب عبد المنعم بن غلبون)+; IBNKATHIR(أبو حاتم الرازي وأبو جعفر العقيلي)-; BURHAN(أبو حاتم الرازي)-; NASHR(أبو حاتم والعقيلي,ابن خزيمة)-]
- 93:1/q18/p2 Begin takbīr at the end of Sūrat al-Layl. — Ibn Kathīr reports this start among some reciters. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR(بعضهم)]
- 93:1/q18/p3 Begin takbīr at the opening of al-Ḍuḥā. — Al-Burhān describes this as the Meccan reading; al-Itqān reports the disputed starting point, whereas Ibn al-Jazarī says he found no authority for this start. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; NASHR(ابن كثير)-]
- 93:1/q18/p4 Use the longer formula including lā ilāha illā Allāh before Allāhu akbar. — Ibn Kathīr and al-Itqān report this alongside the shorter Allāhu akbar formula; al-Sakhāwī and Abū Shāma allow it inside and outside prayer. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR(ومنهم); ITQAN(السخاوي وأبو شامة)]
- 93:1/q18/p5 Do not use takbīr as a regular recitation formula. — Al-Burhān reports concern that listeners may mistake it for Qur'anic text; Ibn al-Jazarī reports other transmitters from al-Sūsī without takbīr and Abū al-Fatḥ saying omission is blameless. [3 notes: BURHAN; NASHR(أبو الفتح فارس بن أحمد,سائر الرواة عن السوسي)]
- 93:1/q18/p6 Make takbīr at the beginning of every sūra for all readers. — Al-Hudhalī reports al-Dīnawarī's practice; Ibn al-Jazarī also mentions authorities who adopted takbīr for all readers or every sūra opening. [2 notes: NASHR(الهذلي,سائر الرواة عن السوسي)]
- 93:1/q18/p7 Use takbīr at completion of the Qur'an. — Al-Ahwāzī calls it a transmitted Meccan practice in recitation and prayer; Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna reports Ṣadaqa ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Kathīr doing so for more than seventy years. [2 notes: NASHR(الأهوازي,سفيان بن عيينة)]

## 93:1/q20 How does this sūra connect to its neighbors?  [* · links]
turns on: The relation to the preceding and following sūras is explained in different ways.
- 93:1/q20/p1 It follows al-Layl's most God-fearing figure with the Prophet and God's favors to him. — Jishumi links earlier reward to the Prophet's resurrection honor; Abu Hayyan and Alusi cite 92:17. Biqa'i makes enduring favor through perfected qualities the theme and daylight its sign. Al-Biqāʿī says the Prophet, as the most pious, receives the earlier sūra's promise in the form of consolation during revelation's pause. [7 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI+; BIQAI+]
- 93:1/q20/p2 Sūras 93 and 94 are a pair moving from opposition toward success. — Islahi distinguishes intense Meccan opposition from emerging success; Study Quran distinguishes external needs here from spiritual relief in 94, both recalling favor and duties. Abū Shāma counts the following Sūrat al-Inshirāḥ among six sūras with interrogative openings. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ITQAN(أبو شامة)]
- 93:1/q20/p3 Al-Layl concerns Abu Bakr; this sūra concerns the Messenger, with no intervening sūra. — Alusi reports the Imam's inference of closeness, while denying precedence implies superiority; he rejects the Imam's flower metaphor. The Imam denies that al-Layl's precedence implies Abū Bakr's superior rank, appealing to oath order and other examples; al-Ālūsī finds the Imam's accompanying spring-flower analogy weak. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); ALUSI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 93:1/q20/p4 Sūras 93 and 94 may once have been a single sūra. — Study Quran reports Companions reciting them in one prayer cycle without an intervening basmala. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 93:1/q21 What is the sūra's central assurance?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it is framed as specific consolation or a broad account of perfected piety.
- 93:1/q21/p1 God has not abandoned the Prophet and will keep favoring and consoling him. — Qutb describes hope, closeness and calm amid persecution, future reward, and past care as proof. Study Quran adds care for the vulnerable and proclamation of God's gifts. Ibn Ashur says past care proves continuing care; Islahi traces better times and duties; Jishumi includes honor at resurrection. Itqan says the day-and-night oath attests God's favor and honor of the Prophet, and thereby affirms his prophethood, resurrection, and recompense. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the assurance to a better Hereafter, divine care through childhood and maturity, and gratitude expressed in service and praise. Al-Durr reports the Prophet's response that his Lord is gracious and worthy of bestowing favor. [11 notes: JISHUMI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR+; ITQAN+]
- 93:1/q21/p2 The most God-fearing remains in divine favor through perfected qualities. — Biqa'i identifies this purpose and reads the daylight title as spiritual light of perfected character. Al-Biqāʿī identifies the Prophet as the most pious and mentions Abū Bakr as first among the pious after him. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BIQAI+]

turns on: Whether the verb is doubled or lightly pronounced; the light verb's rarity is relevant.
- 93:1/q22/p1 The standard recitation doubles the consonant in mā waddaʿaka. — Tabrisi sets it against a noncanonical light-consonant reading. [1 notes: TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، عروة بن الزبير)]

## 93:1/q23 How short is this verse among Qur'anic verses?  [* · sciences]
- 93:1/q23/p1 It is a one-word verse and among the shortest verses. — Burhan defines a word as one utterance of two to ten letters and counts al-Duha, like al-Fajr and al-Asr, as a one-word verse. He ranks al-Duha and then al-Fajr as shortest, rejecting shorter claims for mudhammatan and thumma nazar on their letter or word counts; he contrasts the 128-word, 540-letter debt verse as longest. [2 notes: BURHAN+]

## 93:1/q24 What titles are transmitted for this sūra?  [* · sciences]
