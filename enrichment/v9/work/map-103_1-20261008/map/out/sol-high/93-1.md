# 93:1: 22 questions

## 93:1/q01 What does al-ḍuḥā denote in this oath?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term denotes a whole day, a particular time or light, or an event or symbolic referent.
- 93:1/q01/p1 The whole daylight or daytime. — Tabari, Tha'labi, Baghawi, Tabrisi, Razi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Kuranyolu and Shawkani appeal to its contrast with night; Baghawi, Kashshaf, Qurtubi and Alusi cite 7:98, Qurtubi also 7:97. Mujahid links it with exposure to sun. Alusi says the objection assumes an intensely dark night without proof. Bint al-Shati reports Tabari's choice of visible daylight, but cites 79:46, 20:59 and 91:1 against the whole-day sense. [24 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الطبري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğu); STUDYQURAN(al-Shawkānī,most); BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q01/p2 A defined forenoon or midmorning after sunrise. — Qatada and Muqatil describe temperate heat or cold. Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Alusi, Elmalili, Islahi and Samarrai describe the sun rising high and strong light. Alusi prefers a time over sunlight or sunrise because of the next oath. Bint al-Shati cites 79:46, 20:59 and 91:1 for a particular hour; Abduh calls it sunlight in the day's prime. Study Quran gives daybreak-to-noon as a wider range. [19 notes: NAHHAS(أبو روق); THALABI(قتادة ومقاتل); BAGHAWI(قتادة ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الشيخ محمد عبده); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(most); ALUSI-FULL+; BINTSHATI+]
- 93:1/q01/p3 The hour Moses' opponents prostrated. — Tha'labi, Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursawi, Alusi and Study Quran relate it to the gathering at 20:59. Razi sees a promise that God can honor Muhammad and turn his enemies' hearts. Bint al-Shati calls the reading anachronistic because these passages came later. [10 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(others); RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 93:1/q01/p4 The beginning or first hour of day. — Qatada calls it an hour or the start; Suddi and Muqatil specify the sun moving onward. Jishumi derives it from appearing in sunlight. Bursawi allows metonymy for the time or an omitted noun. [11 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي,قتادة); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي ومقاتل,قتادة); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 93:1/q01/p5 Sunlight, brightness, or visible exposure. — Ibn Atiyya calls it the rise and strength of light; Tabari's daylight is visible sunlight; Ibn Kathir swears by forenoon and its light. Study Quran says the title most commonly means rising sunlight and can extend to all creation it reveals. Bint al-Shati says the root centers on visibility; Wahidi and Ibn Ashur refer to the parallel at 91:1. Yaqub derives aḍḥā from sheep sacrificed at this hour. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الطبري,يعقوب); STUDYQURAN(most)]
- 93:1/q01/p6 The morning when God spoke to Moses. — Tha'labi, Qushayri, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Bursawi and Study Quran report this; Ja'far pairs it with the Night Journey, citing 7:143 and 17:1. Bint al-Shati objects that the cited passages were revealed later. [6 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة ومقاتل وجعفر الصادق); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(جعفر الصادق); STUDYQURAN(others); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 93:1/q01/p7 The forenoon prayer. — Qushayri lists it; Study Quran notes the term also names a late-morning supererogatory prayer. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN(most)]
- 93:1/q01/p8 The Prophet's radiant face. — Bursawi cites a Persian couplet pairing it with his dark hair; Elmalili reports the association. Bint al-Shati excludes such esoteric identifications. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q01/p9 Sunrise itself. — Qutrub identifies it with sunrise. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قطرب)]
- 93:1/q01/p10 The dawn of the Muhammadan mission and truth. — Elmalili prefers a specific dawn of truth radiating mercy and light over a generic definite article. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]

## 93:1/q02 How is the final vowel of al-ḍuḥā written and explained?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether spelling follows spoken form, root origin or muṣḥaf and neighboring endings.
- 93:1/q02/p1 Its final alif is written in yāʾ shape in the muṣḥaf despite a wāw root. — Ibn Ashur says this follows neighboring endings, mostly yāʾ-derived, supports imāla in relevant dialects, and cites Ibn Malik. Nahhas notes inflected forms shift toward yāʾ. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS]
- 93:1/q02/p2 It should be written with final alif according to pronunciation. — Muhammad ibn Yazid derives it from ḍaḥā yaḍḥū and cites rāmā and ghazā. Nahhas rejects root-based Kufan spelling as inconsistent with speech and muṣḥaf. Abu Ishaq cites mā zakkā and rabayān against Qur'anic yarbū at 30:39. [5 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,محمد بن يزيد); NAHHAS+]
- 93:1/q02/p3 The yāʾ-root spelling convention was politically promoted. — Ali ibn Sulayman transmits an allegation that Akhfash and Kisa'i devised it for profit and influence and that Kisa'i's political power spread it. [1 notes: NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان)]

## 93:1/q03 How is the ending of al-ḍuḥā recited?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Different reader transmissions and whether the final alif behaves as yāʾ-derived.
- 93:1/q03/p1 Open pronunciation without imāla. — Ibn Kathir, Asim and Ibn Amir open endings here and in al-Layl; Ibn Khalawayh says the difference here is openness versus imāla. Some Nafi, Warsh and Qalun transmissions also open all, while one opens talāhā alone. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر,نافع)]
- 93:1/q03/p2 Full imāla. — Kisa'i inclines these endings throughout; Hamza inclines yāʾ-origin forms but darkens wāw-origin forms. Abbas and a report from Abu Amr incline selected endings. Nahhas licenses inclination when a wāw root shifts to yāʾ. Farisi cites yāʾ-like behavior, derived verbs and nouns, and says rare inclined wāw forms do not overturn the general rule. [7 notes: NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,أبو عمرو ونافع,الكسائي)]
- 93:1/q03/p3 Intermediate inclination. — Nahhas gives Medinan intermediate treatment; Farisi reports it for Nafi and Abu Amr, among variant transmissions. Abu Amr may follow the sūra's ending pattern. [4 notes: NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,أبو عمرو ونافع,نافع)]

## 93:1/q04 What is the grammatical object of the oath?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the expressed time is itself the oath object or an omitted Lord is supplied.
- 93:1/q04/p1 An omitted 'Lord' is understood: by the Lord of morning and night. — Semantic scholars in Tha'labi and Qurtubi and a view in Tabrisi supply the ellipsis. [3 notes: THALABI(أهل المعاني); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المعاني); TABRISI]
- 93:1/q04/p2 Al-ḍuḥā itself is sworn by. — Ibn Khalawayh calls it an oath; Tabari identifies ḍuḥā as the oath object. [2 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; TAB-FULL]

## 93:1/q05 Why is the forenoon singled out for an oath?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether its sensory quality, a past event, or a divine sign supplies the point.
- 93:1/q05/p1 It recalls divine speech to Moses and the magicians' prostration. — Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi, Bursawi, Alusi and Elmalili cite 20:59 and God's ability to honor Muhammad and change his enemies. Bint al-Shati rejects this appeal as anachronistic because the Moses passages were revealed later. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 93:1/q05/p2 It is the day's vigorous, bright and honored phase. — Razi says people gather and find ease after night, offering comfort after the revelation pause. Biqa'i calls it the brightest portion, suited to the most honored creature. Alusi calls it the sun's youthful daily honor; Islahi connects it with renewed work. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:1/q05/p3 Its light is a great divine sign and blessing. — Abduh emphasizes the light's grandeur. Ibn Kathir says the contrast shows Creator power. Ibn al-Qayyim appeals to lordship, wisdom and mercy, which Bint al-Shati says misses the occasion's abandonment claim. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(ابن قيم الجوزية,الشيخ محمد عبده); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BINTSHATI-]

## 93:1/q06 What does the oath form do rhetorically?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the oath primarily magnifies its object or prepares the reader for an unseen point.
- 93:1/q06/p1 It glorifies what is sworn by as a great sign. — Bint al-Shati reports Ibn al-Qayyim's account as the prevailing earlier view; Abduh stresses the light's greatness. [2 notes: BINTSHATI(ابن قيم الجوزية,الشيخ محمد عبده)]
- 93:1/q06/p2 It directs attention to familiar senses to prepare for unseen meaning. — Bint al-Shati prefers this role for the wāw, and says any impression of glorification strengthens attention. The chosen sensory quality must suit context. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]

## 93:1/q07 What inward or symbolic reality is signified by ḍuḥā?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · inward]
turns on: The different spiritual correspondences assigned to daylight.
- 93:1/q07/p1 Spirit or God's unveiling of an inner secret. — Tustari identifies the spirit; Ibn Ata says God unveils the secret as the servant is occupied with calling people through attention to creation. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 93:1/q07/p2 The station of witnessing. — Junayd pairs it with night's inner-eye station or ghayn, clouding over the heart in a Prophetic report; Sulami and Bursawi transmit related forms. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); BURSEVI(الجنيد)]
- 93:1/q07/p3 The Prophet's radiant face. — Bursawi's Persian couplet pairs face with hair. Bint al-Shati excludes this and other esoteric pairs including relatives, knowledge/forgiveness, and Islam's rise/estrangement. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q07/p4 Divine disclosure, gentleness and beauty. — Kashf al-Asrar pairs it with night's veiling, severity and majesty. [1 notes: BURSEVI(صاحب كشف الأسرار)]
- 93:1/q07/p5 The Garden's light or the light of knowers' hearts. — Study Quran reports counterparts in the Fire's darkness and unbelievers' darkened hearts. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(others)]

## 93:1/q08 What is the status and form of the ḍuḥā prayer?  [وَٱلضُّحَىٰ · law]
turns on: The jurists differ over the number of units.
- 93:1/q08/p1 It is a sunnah from sun-high time until shortly before noon. — Bursawi calls this unanimous and cites a sound report that the Prophet prayed eight units in Umm Hani's house on the conquest of Mecca, having prayed it earlier too. [2 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله)]
- 93:1/q08/p2 Two or four units with one salutation. — Bursawi reports Abu Hanifa's prescription. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو حنيفة)]
- 93:1/q08/p3 No fixed upper limit. — Bursawi reports Malik's view. [1 notes: BURSEVI(مالك)]
- 93:1/q08/p4 At least two, at most twelve units. — Bursawi reports Shafi'i's count. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الشافعي)]
- 93:1/q08/p5 At least two, at most eight units. — Bursawi reports Ahmad's count, followed by most Shafi'i authorities and endorsed by Nawawi in al-Tahqiq. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الشافعي)]
- 93:1/q08/p6 The two midmorning units may use al-Shams and al-Ḍuḥā. — Durr transmits Uqba ibn Amir's Prophetic instruction. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عقبة بن عامر)]

## 93:1/q09 What does the daylight-and-night pairing suggest about revelation and the Prophet?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How the sensory alternation relates to revelation's pause, guidance, divine care and the oath's answer.
- 93:1/q09/p1 Alternation of light and night images revelation's pause and renewal without abandonment. — Razi and Nisaburi compare waxing and waning by divine wisdom with sending and withholding revelation. Bint al-Shati prefers the sensory analogy. Elmalili sees revealed knowledge and rest before renewal. Ibn al-Qayyim likens light after darkness to revelation after its pause, but Bint al-Shati says his lordship rationale misses the stated occasion. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(ابن قيم الجوزية,الشيخ محمد عبده,فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري); BINTSHATI+; BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q09/p2 Day is revelation and guidance; night suggests recitation, reflection and calm. — Ibn Ashur says nearby idolaters heard the Prophet's night recitation and cites 73:2-3. Qutb sees a tender setting where the spirit senses creation worshipping, cosmic companionship and graceful musical cadence. Kuranyolu reports Ibn Ashur's sunlight analogy. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]
- 93:1/q09/p3 The contrast answers the disbelievers' unsupported taunt. — Razi and Nisaburi say the taunters could not prove abandonment. Ibn Ashur says the oath targets their inference from missed night recitation, while the Prophet himself had no doubt. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري); IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 93:1/q09/p4 Light and dark illustrate changing spiritual and epistemic states. — Biqa'i applies mixed and pure light to clear and ambiguous teachings, bright hearts and darkening appetite; obscurity can yield certainty through remembering signs and seeking refuge. Bint al-Shati relates the senses to guidance or falsehood; Razi/Nisaburi say all creatures undergo change. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI(فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري)]
- 93:1/q09/p5 Night's rest and day's work together are divine mercy. — Islahi cites 10:67 and 28:73. Abduh says quiet night restores strength after first revelation; Bint al-Shati says that account nearly loses the sensory analogy. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده); BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/q09/p6 The contrast visibly demonstrates the Creator's power. — Ibn Kathir relates this pairing to 92:1-2 and 6:96. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 93:1/q09/p7 Day and night model developmental trials leading to relief and duties. — Islahi traces movement from the models to reassurance, better times, past help and duties arising from favor. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:1/q09/p8 Earlier explanations fail to connect the oath clearly to its answer. — Bint al-Shati faults Tabari, Zamakhshari and Abu Hayyan for lacking a clear connection and for not comparing explicit Qur'anic denials of oaths. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 93:1/q10 Why does this sūra place day before night?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order emphasizes daylight's honor, the duration of joy and sorrow, or moral stages.
- 93:1/q10/p1 Brief joy is set against much longer sorrow. — Bursawi equates one forenoon hour to the whole night as Muhammad equals all prophets. He cites a cloud raining grief for 300 years versus a cloud raining joy for an hour. Bint al-Shati reports the Razi/Nisaburi prophet comparison. [3 notes: BURSEVI; BINTSHATI(النيسابوري والرازي)]
- 93:1/q10/p2 Day leads here for its inherent and contextual honor. — Bursawi says night precedes in al-Layl because darkness is the original state; sunlight makes day, then air returns to darkness at sunset. He cites 6:1. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 93:1/q10/p3 Life is preferred before warning of despair and divine plotting. — Bint al-Shati reports an interpretation giving this staged moral order. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين)]

## 93:1/q11 What interrupted revelation before this sūra?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports name different circumstances before revelation resumed.
- 93:1/q11/p1 Illness prevented night prayer for one or two, or two or three, nights. — Jundub's versions vary. Ibn Ashur understands the taunt as a reaction to absent nighttime Quran recitation. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI; BAGHAWI(جندب بن سفيان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(جندب البجلي); SUYUTI-LUBAB(جندب); STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:1/q11/p2 Gabriel or revelation paused, with no precipitating cause specified. — Tabari, Ibn Khalawayh, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Suyuti, Qutb and Study Quran relate pause and ensuing taunt. Qutb says Gabriel's visits sustained the Prophet through rejection and persecution, so absence distressed him. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; BAGHAWI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مشركون); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,جندب,زيد بن أرقم,عروة,قتادة والضحاك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(جندب,زيد بن أرقم,عبد الله بن شداد,عروة); QUTB-ZILAL(روايات كثيرة); STUDYQURAN(others,several commentators)]
- 93:1/q11/p3 A puppy or dead puppy was in the Prophet's house. — Zayd ibn Aslam says Gabriel explained angels avoid a house with a dog or image. Khawla and Umm Hafs locate a dead puppy under the bed and give four days. Ibn Hajar says a puppy-related delay is known, but rejects this as the cause of the sūra because it conflicts with sound reports. [6 notes: THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI(زيد بن أسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(أم حفص); SUYUTI-LUBAB(خولة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن حجر)-]
- 93:1/q11/p4 The Prophet promised answers to Jewish questions without saying 'if God wills'. — Tha'labi, Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi name Dhu al-Qarnayn, the Cave Companions and the Spirit. [3 notes: THALABI(المفسرون); BAGHAWI(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 93:1/q11/p5 A stone injured the Prophet's finger, after which he missed night prayer. — A second Jundub version gives two or three nights. Ibn Kathir calls the injury authentic but the causal link unusual; Durr cites Tirmidhi's authentication. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(جندب)]
- 93:1/q11/p6 The Prophet or companions neglected grooming. — Tha'labi reports the Prophet referring to unclean spaces between fingers and untrimmed nails; Ibn al-Jawzi lists a companion's lack of cleanliness. [2 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 93:1/q12 How long was the reported interruption?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted numerical counts differ.
- 93:1/q12/p1 Forty days or mornings. — Muqatil gives forty days; Ibn Khalawayh cites forty mornings; Baghawi and Study Quran include forty. [4 notes: THALABI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI(ابن جريج وابن عباس ومقاتل); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; STUDYQURAN(several commentators)]
- 93:1/q12/p2 Twelve days. — Ibn Jurayj gives twelve; Baghawi repeats it and Study Quran includes it. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن حريج); BAGHAWI(ابن جريج وابن عباس ومقاتل); STUDYQURAN(several commentators)]
- 93:1/q12/p3 Four days. — Umm Hafs and Khawla give four days in the dead-puppy account. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أم حفص); SUYUTI-LUBAB(خولة)]
- 93:1/q12/p4 Fifteen days. — Ibn Abbas gives fifteen; Baghawi repeats it. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI(ابن جريج وابن عباس ومقاتل)]
- 93:1/q12/p5 Twenty-five days. — Tha'labi transmits this number without a named transmitter. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 93:1/q12/p6 Fifty days. — Study Quran includes fifty among the reports. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(several commentators)]

## 93:1/q13 Who voiced the claim that the Prophet had been abandoned?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the speaker was hostile, compassionate, or identified as a cousin's daughter.
- 93:1/q13/p1 Polytheists or Quraysh collectively. — Their taunt follows Gabriel's delay; Razi/Nisaburi say they lacked proof, and Ibn Ashur ties their insinuation to missed nighttime recitation. [11 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI; BAGHAWI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مشركون); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة والضحاك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(جندب); QUTB-ZILAL(روايات كثيرة); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري); STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 93:1/q13/p2 A woman, identified in some accounts as Umm Jamil, Abu Lahab's wife. — Jundub calls her a woman. Tha'labi, Baghawi, Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Kathir, Ibn al-Jawzi and Zayd ibn Arqam name Umm Jamil. She calls his companion a devil or Satan. Durr places her confrontation after Sūrat Abī Lahab. Ibn Hajar says her remark was spiteful. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI; BAGHAWI(جندب بن سفيان,قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جندب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(جندب,جندب البجلي,زيد بن أرقم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(جندب,زيد بن أرقم,عروة); STUDYQURAN(several commentators)]
- 93:1/q13/p3 Khadija spoke with concern over apparent rejection. — Abdullah ibn Shaddad and Urwa transmit her remark after a pause or distress. Ibn Hajar distinguishes compassion from Umm Jamil's spite. Tha'labi relates this to the first pause after al-Alaq. The later reports are mursal. [5 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن شداد); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن شداد,عروة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عبد الله بن شداد,عروة)]
- 93:1/q13/p4 A cousin's daughter said his companion had left him. — Durr's Jundub report gives this identification. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(جندب)]

## 93:1/q14 What followed Gabriel's return and reassurance?  [* · reports]
turns on: The accounts describe different responses to Gabriel's return.
- 93:1/q14/p1 The Prophet and Gabriel expressed mutual longing; Gabriel said he only descends by God's command. — Tha'labi and Baghawi transmit the dialogue, and Baghawi relates it to 19:64. [3 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI]
- 93:1/q14/p2 The Prophet rejoiced and pronounced takbīr. — Ibn Khalawayh links the greeting, joy and takbīr to the forty-morning pause and the reassuring revelation. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 93:1/q15 How should the following phrase about night be understood beside this oath?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether sajā means arrival, departure or stillness.
- 93:1/q15/p1 Night settles, darkens, or grows still among its people. — Tabrisi cites poetry about a calm sea and night; Ibn Kathir adds darkness. Durr reports Qatada's quiet people. Bint al-Shati reports Tabari, Zamakhshari and Abduh on stillness. [7 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الشيخ محمد عبده,الطبري)]

## 93:1/q16 Where and when in the revelation sequence was this sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The notes preserve different revelation-order lists and first-complete-sūra claims.
- 93:1/q16/p1 It is Meccan. — Baghawi, Abu Hayyan, Durr, Ibn Abbas, Alusi and Bint al-Shati affirm Meccan origin; Study Quran calls it early Meccan. Bint al-Shati says there is no disagreement. [7 notes: BAGHAWI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]
- 93:1/q16/p2 It followed al-ʿAlaq after Gabriel's first pause. — Abdullah ibn Shaddad gives this order and Khadija's concern. [1 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن شداد)]
- 93:1/q16/p3 It followed 'Read', al-Muddaththir and al-Qalam through 68:4. — Tha'labi gives this sequence. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 93:1/q16/p4 It is commonly eleventh, after al-Fajr. — Bint al-Shati gives this revelation rank. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/q16/p5 It is fourth in one list or seventh in another. — Study Quran reports both rankings, which vary in the note. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 93:1/q16/p6 It may be the first sūra revealed whole. — Study Quran reports this and the competing assignment to al-Fatiha. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 93:1/q18 How is takbīr recited from this sūra onward?  [* · readings]
- 93:1/q18/p1 Say Allāhu akbar at each sūra ending from al-Ḍuḥā through the Qur'an's end. — Ibn Kathir's practice includes the basmala; Durr reports a Prophetic instruction. Ibn Khalawayh links the practice with the Prophet's joy when Gabriel returned. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(ابن كثير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 93:1/q19 What reward is reported for reciting the sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 93:1/q19/p1 Intercession and reward for every orphan and asker. — Jishumi transmits a Prophetic report through Ubayy scaling the reward to every such person. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي)]

## 93:1/q20 How does this sūra connect to its neighbors?  [* · links]
turns on: The relation to the preceding and following sūras is explained in different ways.
- 93:1/q20/p1 It follows al-Layl's most God-fearing figure with the Prophet and God's favors to him. — Jishumi links earlier reward to the Prophet's resurrection honor; Abu Hayyan and Alusi cite 92:17. Biqa'i makes enduring favor through perfected qualities the theme and daylight its sign. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 93:1/q20/p2 Sūras 93 and 94 are a pair moving from opposition toward success. — Islahi distinguishes intense Meccan opposition from emerging success; Study Quran distinguishes external needs here from spiritual relief in 94, both recalling favor and duties. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 93:1/q20/p3 Al-Layl concerns Abu Bakr; this sūra concerns the Messenger, with no intervening sūra. — Alusi reports the Imam's inference of closeness, while denying precedence implies superiority; he rejects the Imam's flower metaphor. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 93:1/q20/p4 Sūras 93 and 94 may once have been a single sūra. — Study Quran reports Companions reciting them in one prayer cycle without an intervening basmala. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 93:1/q21 What is the sūra's central assurance?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it is framed as specific consolation or a broad account of perfected piety.
- 93:1/q21/p1 God has not abandoned the Prophet and will keep favoring and consoling him. — Qutb describes hope, closeness and calm amid persecution, future reward, and past care as proof. Study Quran adds care for the vulnerable and proclamation of God's gifts. Ibn Ashur says past care proves continuing care; Islahi traces better times and duties; Jishumi includes honor at resurrection. [7 notes: JISHUMI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 93:1/q21/p2 The most God-fearing remains in divine favor through perfected qualities. — Biqa'i identifies this purpose and reads the daylight title as spiritual light of perfected character. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 93:1/q22 What reading and wording are reported in the following reassurance?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is doubled or lightly pronounced; the light verb's rarity is relevant.
- 93:1/q22/p1 The standard recitation doubles the consonant in mā waddaʿaka. — Tabrisi sets it against a noncanonical light-consonant reading. [1 notes: TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، عروة بن الزبير)]
- 93:1/q22/p2 A noncanonical reading lightens the consonant in mā wadaʿaka. — Tabrisi attributes it to the Prophet and Urwa. Ibn Jinni calls the light verb rare; Sibawayh says Arabic ordinarily uses taraka and cites a line by Abu al-Aswad. [2 notes: TABRISI(ابن جني، سيبويه,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، عروة بن الزبير)]
