# 11:114: 29 questions

## 11:114/q01 What does it mean to establish prayer?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
- 11:114/q01/p1 Perform the prescribed prayers fully, with their required acts and at their appointed times. — Al-Tabrisi and Ibn al-Jawzi specify complete bowing and prostration. Alusi includes regular observance or prayer at the beginning of its time. Jishumi says the verse fixes times generally while the Prophet explained details; Asad and Qurtubi likewise refer forms and exact times to prophetic teaching. [9 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; ASAD-NOTES; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور أهل التأويل); STUDYQURAN]
- 11:114/q01/p2 Maintain prayer continuously, evenly, or at its best times. — These are alternative glosses reported by al-Tabrisi and Abu Hayyan; al-Alusi also reports regular maintenance and beginning-of-time performance. [3 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:114/q01/p3 Fill one's time with obligatory and voluntary worship or remembrance. — Sufi authorities take the command to cover all time. Asad extends its stress on prayer to remembrance throughout waking life. Bursevi allows work, human needs, and restorative sleep. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(شيوخ الصوفية); ASAD-NOTES; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-]
- 11:114/q01/p4 The five prayers close the five sensory doors and restore presence with God. — Qashani says the senses veil the heart; praying at day edges preserves gathered light through intervening times (23:9), while night wakefulness refines powers drawn toward sleep. [3 notes: QASHANI]
- 11:114/q01/p5 Prayer is the foremost worship after faith and a refuge in hardship. — Razi relates its placement after steadfastness to its rank; Biqai calls it the broadest aid to uprightness; Qurtubi reports the Prophet turned to prayer when troubled. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور أهل التأويل)]

## 11:114/q02 Which prayers are at the two ends of the day?  [طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ · referent]
turns on: Whether dawn and sunset count within daytime, and whether the latter half of the day can contain both Zuhr and Asr as an end.
- 11:114/q02/p1 Fajr at the first end and Zuhr and Asr at the second. — Wahidi prefers Zajjaj: Maghrib belongs to night. Zamakhshari calls Zuhr and Asr evening prayers; Elmalili calls them the after-noon division. Tabatabai says the five-prayer report points to before and after noon. Qurtubi reports Ibn Atiyya's choice. Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Dahhak accepts all three second-end views. Al-Damghani also identifies dawn, noon, and afternoon and notes 20:130. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد,محمد بن كعب القرظي); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الزجاج,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج، مجاهد، الضحاك، محمد بن كعب القرظي، الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك,مجاهد والقرظي); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,مجاهد ومحمد بن كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); DAMGHANI; WAHIDI-BASIT+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 11:114/q02/p2 Fajr and Asr. — Razi says sunrise and sunset mark opposite edges, leaving Maghrib to night; he parallels 20:130. Abu Hayyan says each boundary belongs to what it bounds and cites fasting from dawn. Biqai notes morning and afternoon prayer before the Night Journey. Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Dahhak accepts all three second-end views. Razi says Abu Hanifa inferred later performance of Fajr and Asr near the boundaries. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(الحسن,الضحاك,محمد بن كعب القرظي); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك,قتادة والحسن); RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 11:114/q02/p3 Fajr and Maghrib. — Tabari argues that all agree on Fajr and that Maghrib alone supplies the opposite end; Wahidi says the apparent wording supports it. Tabrisi says Zuhr and Asr are obvious daytime prayers or specified by 17:78. Qurtubi defends dawn as daytime from fasting law but retains the sunset objection. Kuranyolu defines day as dawn to sunset. Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Dahhak accepts all three second-end views. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس، ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وابن زيد,الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن,الطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن,الطبري); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري)+; TAB-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,ابن عطية)-]
- 11:114/q02/p4 Zuhr and Asr are the two boundaries; Fajr is grouped with night prayers. — Some connect this grouping with quiet versus loud recitation. Elmalili argues dawn is the day's root and noon and afternoon its two prominent parts; the night set of Maghrib, Isha and Fajr gives five prayers, with 17:78 and 20:130. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI+; TAB-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 11:114/q02/p5 Fajr and Isha. — Ibn Abi Hatim reports this from Mujahid. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد)]
- 11:114/q02/p6 Fajr and Zuhr at one end, Asr and Maghrib at the other. — Wahidi and Baghawi attribute this division to Muqatil. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 11:114/q02/p7 The first end is Fajr, while the evening end remains disputed. — Tabari says all agree on Fajr but differ about evening; Ibn al-Jawzi reports the majority agrees, but notes Ibn Jarir gives Zuhr instead. Qurtubi transmits Mawardi's claimed agreement and qualifies it by the Zuhr/Asr view. Asad gives the literal wording, 'the two ends of the day.' [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)]

## 11:114/q03 Why is the yāʾ of طرفي pronounced with a vowel?  [طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ · grammar]
- 11:114/q03/p1 It meets a following still consonant and takes the usual kasra. — Akhfash compares صاحبي السجن at 12:39. [1 notes: AKHFASH]

## 11:114/q04 Does avoiding major sins already require performing the five prayers?  [ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · law]
- 11:114/q04/p1 Yes, because abandoning prayer is a major sin. — Ibn Hajar argues this; al-Alusi says it merits examination. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر)]

## 11:114/q05 How does al-Tirmidhi grade the principal confession reports?  [* · reports]
- 11:114/q05/p1 The Ibn Masud account is hasan sahih; the Muadh and Abu al-Yusr routes are weak. — Ibn Ashur reports al-Tirmidhi's distinctions. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الترمذي)]

## 11:114/q06 Can night prayer erase sins committed during the day?  [زُلَفًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · interpretation]
- 11:114/q06/p1 Yes, a believer's night prayer removes daytime sins. — Tabatabai cites Abu Abdillah and notes parallel transmissions in several collections. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 11:114/q07 What does زلفا mean?  [زُلَفًۭا · meaning]
- 11:114/q07/p1 Near or successive hours, stages, or portions of the night; singular zulfa. — Akhfash lists zulfat and zulf as other plurals. Layth and Thalab specify the first night hours; Abu Ubayda, Akhfash and Ibn Qutayba allow any hour. Al-Ajjaj's poem uses successive zulf. Ibn Qutayba and Abu Hayyan derive nearness; Tabari and Mawardi give Muzdalifa etymologies, while Raghib includes rank, favor and euphemistic punishment. Zajjaj rejects an unattested night meaning for zalif. Al-Samarra'i stresses gradual approach and concludes that zulfa conveys drawing near generally. [29 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة والعجاج,ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(الليث,الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة، والأخفش، وابن قتيبة,الليث,ثعلب); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش وابن قتيبة,الراغب,العجاج,ثعلب); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+; ZAJJAJ+]

## 11:114/q08 How is زلفا read?  [زُلَفًۭا · readings]
turns on: The vowel on lam and whether the form is a plural, collective, or singular.
- 11:114/q08/p1 Zulafan, with damma on zay and fatha on lam, a plural of zulfa. — Tabari prefers this because Isha follows portions of the night; Sam in calls it the common reading, and Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan the majority reading. Ibn al-Jazari says the fatha form, like the damma form, is an attested dialectal plural of zulfa, comparing zulam/zulma and yusr/yusra. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASHR; TAB-FULL+]
- 11:114/q08/p2 Zulufan, with damma on both letters. — Attributed to Medinan readers, Abu Jafar, Ibn Abi Ishaq, Talha and others. Analyses include a plural with vowel harmony, plural of zalif, or dialectal singular; Qiraat-ER says the plural pattern changes without changing meaning. Ibn al-Jazari names Abu Ja'far, Talha, Shayba, Isa ibn Amr and transmitters from Abu Amr for the damma on lam; he treats both vowel forms as dialectal plurals of zulfa. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو جعفر وشيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن قعقاع وابن أبي إسحاق وغيرهما); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة وعيسى البصرة وابن أبي إسحاق وأبو جعفر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر وابن أبي إسحاق); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(أبو جعفر)]
- 11:114/q08/p3 Zulfan, with silent lam. — Attributed to some Meccan readers and to Ibn Muhaysin and Mujahid. It may shorten the vowelled form or function as a collective singular. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن محيصن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن محيصن ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد وابن محيصن); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:114/q08/p4 Zulfa or zulfa, a singular near interval or rank. — Mujahid and Ibn Muhaysin transmit the feminine fuʿla form; Kashshaf compares qurba; Sam in calls it a singular adjective for a near rank or hour. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وابن محيصن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن محيصن ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد وابن محيصن); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 11:114/q09 How do طرفي النهار and زلفا function grammatically?  [طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ زُلَفًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether زلفا names a time coordinate with the day ends or a prayer coordinate with الصلاة.
- 11:114/q09/p1 Both are accusative time expressions governed by establish; زلفا is coordinated with طرفي. — Kashshaf, Nasafi and Sam in say taraf becomes temporal through annexation to a time, as do 'beginning of day' and 'half of night'; Sam in prefers governance by أقم. [10 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 11:114/q09/p2 The night expressions are prayers that draw one near to God and coordinate with الصلاة. — Kashshaf supplies the alternative parsing as devotional night prayers; Sam in and Abu Hayyan report it. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 11:114/q09/p3 من الليل modifies زلفا as an adjective. — Sam in explicitly parses the prepositional phrase as a qualifier. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:114/q10 Which prayers are meant by the night intervals?  [زُلَفًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ · referent]
turns on: Which hours count as night approaches and whether the plural requires at least three prayers.
- 11:114/q10/p1 Maghrib and Isha. — Hasan transmits this as the Prophet's description of two night intervals; Tabari lists early authorities. Zajjaj and Wahidi put sunset within night. Al-Samarra'i reports that many commentators identify Maghrib and Isha because their night portions are nearest daytime. [33 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(الحسن,بعضهم,مجاهد,محمد بن كعب القرظي); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن,الزجاج، مجاهد، الضحاك، محمد بن كعب القرظي، الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والحسن ومجاهد وقتادة ومقاتل والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك,الزمخشري,مجاهد ومحمد بن كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(كثير من المفسرين)]
- 11:114/q10/p2 Isha alone, in a later portion of night. — Mujahid calls it an hour at Isha. Ibn Abbas cited the phrase in favor of delaying Isha; Tabari says Isha follows portions of night. [17 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس، ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:114/q10/p3 Voluntary night vigil or tahajjud. — Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn Abbas's voluntary-night gloss. Ibn Kathir describes the earlier obligation of night vigil and its later abrogation; Bursevi cites 17:79 for the Prophet. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:114/q10/p4 Maghrib, Isha, and Fajr. — The dawn-as-night classification may follow loud recitation; Elmalili argues this yields five prayers. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]
- 11:114/q10/p5 Maghrib, Isha, and witr or night vigil; or Isha, witr, and tahajjud. — Razi and Elmalili infer at least three from the broken plural; Razi argues witr obligation using 34:20, while Elmalili describes a possible sixth prayer and 17:79. Bursevi reports Abu Hanifa's witr view. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 11:114/q10/p6 Night prayer without specifying which one. — Qurtubi attributes this to Akhfash. Al-Samarra'i reports an unrestricted reading of night prayer as drawing near to God. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(بعضهم)]

## 11:114/q11 What do the prayer times establish as a legal duty?  [وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ وَزُلَفًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ · law]
turns on: Whether the plural of night intervals denotes specific obligatory prayers and how much detail the verse itself supplies.
- 11:114/q11/p1 They indicate the five obligatory prayers, while prophetic practice supplies their exact times and form. — Qurtubi cites 16:44 and other prayer verses, the Prophet's 'pray as you have seen me pray,' and completion of religion at 5:3. Elmalili infers five from two day periods and three night periods. [8 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(some interpreters); STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]
- 11:114/q11/p2 Only two daily prayers are obligatory. — The Kharijites construe the night phrase as an adjective to the day ends and argue the two prayers erase even omission of other prayers. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الخوارج); RAZI-FULL-]
- 11:114/q11/p3 Witr is obligatory as a third night prayer. — Razi invokes the plural minimum of three and 34:20; Bursevi cites Abu Hanifa; Elmalili says a sixth prayer is probable, so witr is practical wājib rather than a creed obligation. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 11:114/q11/p4 Five established prayers are performed in congregation. — Elmalili says the call is sunnah and congregational prayer wājib. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 11:114/q12 What are the good deeds that remove bad deeds?  [ٱلْحَسَنَٰتِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite good deeds refer to the preceding prayer, particular devotions, or the general class of obedience.
- 11:114/q12/p1 The five prescribed prayers, especially with ablution. — Tabari prefers this from context and recurrent sound prophetic reports; Ibn al-Jawzi prefers the majority and a connected report. Ka'b, Salman and the Prophet compare prayer to washing dirt or shaking leaves; Uthman says each prayer expiates the interval since the prior prayer. [49 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(النبي,سلمان,عبد الله بن مسعود,كعب، محمد بن كعب القرظي، ابن عباس، مجاهد، الحسن، الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(عثمان بن عفان); NAHHAS; THALABI(أكثر المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); TABRISI(ابن عباس وأكثر المفسرين,سلمان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وابن عباس وابن المسيب ومسروق ومجاهد والقرظي والضحاك ومقاتل,عثمان عن رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور المتأولين من الصحابة والتابعين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جمهور المتأولين من الصحابة والتابعين); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو أيوب الأنصاري,أبو مالك الأشعري,أبو هريرة,عثمان بن عفان); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عثمان,عطاء بن أبي رباح); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; TAB-FULL+]
- 11:114/q12/p2 All acts of obedience or accepted good deeds, with prayer as a leading example. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan treat specific deeds as examples of general wording. Alusi adduces reports on voluntary affirmation, fasting Arafat, and gratitude. Qushayri lists nearness, remorse, tears, kindness, generosity, good opinion, sincerity and truthfulness as remedies for particular faults. Bursevi ranks knowledge of God, monotheism and resisting the lower self highly. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 11:114/q12/p3 Remembrance formulas: glorification, praise, testimony of divine unity, and magnification. — Most reports attribute these to Mujahid, with slight variation: Ibn Abi Hatim and Abu Hayyan include reliance on God; Ata calls them the enduring good works. Abu Dharr's report calls the testimony the best good deed; Bursevi transmits the same answer from the Prophet. Al-Itqan cites Abu Dharr's report naming la ilaha illa Allah as the best good deed. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(عطاء بن دينار); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(منصور عن مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو ذر); DURR-FULL(عثمان); BURSEVI(النبي); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 11:114/q12/p4 Repentance and return to obedience. — Jishumi prefers Abu Ali's reading: unbelievers can do good without erasure, so repentance is essential; Tabrisi says repentance certainly cancels punishment. [6 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(أبو علي)+]
- 11:114/q12/p5 The Prophet's and believers' good actions that overcome polytheists' evil actions. — This interpretation makes the verse a promise of religious victory; Abu Muslim links it to truth crushing falsehood in 21:18. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 11:114/q13 How do good deeds remove bad deeds?  [إِنَّ ٱلْحَسَنَٰتِ يُذْهِبْنَ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether removal means retrospective expiation, prevention of future wrongdoing, or spiritual transformation.
- 11:114/q13/p1 They expiate the guilt, punishment, or effects of earlier sins. — Abu Hayyan and Bursevi distinguish expiation from making a past act never occur. Alusi says liability is removed; another explanation has records erased. Tabatabai speaks of traces in believers' souls. Ibn Kathir cites ablution and two-unit prayer reports. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عثمان بن عفان,علي بن أبي طالب); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:114/q13/p2 Regular good deeds help a person stop committing sins. — Al-Asamm, Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi cite prayer restraining indecency at 29:45; Elmalili adds that regular prayer cultivates remorse for major sins. ## 11:114/q14 Which sins are removed, and on what conditions?  [ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · interpretation]
turns on: How to read the condition about major sins and the scope of divine expiation.
- 11:114/q14/p1 Minor sins are expiated if major sins are avoided. — The prayer report conditions expiation on avoiding majors. Ibn Ashur cites 53:32 and 4:31. Sunni majority make avoidance a condition of the whole promise, while another group treats majors as excluded from what is erased; Alusi prefers that distinction and argues prayers erase minors even when majors exist. Al-Damghani gives minor sins as a sense of sayyi'at that good deeds remove and cites 46:16 for overlooking them. [25 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور أهل السنة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(المحب الطبري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); QASHANI(عليه السلام); DAMGHANI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 11:114/q14/p2 Minor sins require repentance and nonpersistence for erasure. — Ibn Atiyya invokes sins falling with ablution water and expert legal theorists; Abu Hayyan concurs. Alusi records disagreement and stresses that expiation does not end the duty to repent. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأشعري,الزركشي); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 11:114/q14/p3 Repentance can expiate major sins. — Jishumi favors Abu Ali's reading; Tabrisi says punishment is cancelled by repentance without dispute. Al-Subki reports debate over whether forgiveness after repentance for sins is certain or presumed. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السبكي); JISHUMI+]
- 11:114/q14/p4 Some good deeds can expiate major as well as minor sins. — Al-Sadiq says a later good deed can remove a forgotten grave sin. Al-Dhakha'ir cites promises of forgiveness of past and future sins; Ibn al-Mundhir inclines to this. Alusi answers critics who invoke Murji'ism by retaining the duty of repentance. [4 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الذخائر)]
- 11:114/q14/p5 Faith erases unbelief and at least removes eternal punishment, so believers' sins cannot ultimately harm them. — Razi reports this argument by its proponents. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 11:114/q14/p6 A major sin may be lightened when an expiatory act finds no minor sins. — Al-Nawawi presents this as a hope. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(النووي)]
- 11:114/q14/p7 Avoidance of major sins can itself expiate minor sins at each time, when restraint is intentional. — Al-Balqini reads 4:31 over a lifetime; Alusi rejects that reading with al-Qadi and Ghazali. Ghazali requires ability and desire to commit the major sin. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلقيني,الغزالي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 11:114/q14/p8 Punishment for minor sins remains possible despite avoidance of majors. — Alusi reports Sunni authorities citing 4:48 and 18:49; Mu'tazila deny this from 4:31, which may concern unbelief. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أهل السنة)]

## 11:114/q27 How is the ta in الصلاة طرفي pronounced in large assimilation?  [ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ · readings]
