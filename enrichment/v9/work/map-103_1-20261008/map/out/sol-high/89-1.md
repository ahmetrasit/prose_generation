# 89:1: 16 questions

## 89:1/q01 What does al-fajr mean as an Arabic word?  [ٱلْفَجْرِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether splitting is the root idea or an instance of a broader emergence.
- 89:1/q01/p1 Morning light breaking from night, or the time when it appears. — Tustari gives the outward sense as morning. Zajjaj, Mawardi, Tabarisi, Ibn Jawzi and Bint al-Shati describe darkness cleft by light. Ibn Jawzi relates the root to water bursting out and fājir breaking from obedience. Bint al-Shati cites six Quranic dawn uses, 2:187's white thread and 17:78's sequence after night; she also explains ghasaq as darkness mingled with first morning light or lingering evening light. Islahi distinguishes fajr's transition from subh, which extends beyond sunrise and is qualified in oaths (74:34; 81:18). Kuran Yolu gives brightness or daybreak time. [14 notes: TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، علي بن عبيد الله); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:1/q01/p2 The root broadly denotes emergence or outburst, physically or figuratively. — Bint al-Shati prefers this to al-Raghib's narrower splitting derivation. She cites springs, gushing and volcanoes, metaphorical emergence in goodness, generosity or evil, and the four pre-Islamic Fijar battle days. She counts 24 Quranic root uses, ten verbs mostly about water; 75:5 is the sole non-water verb. Nouns name rivers in 17:91, a spring in 76:6, and wickedness opposite piety in 91:8 and other passages. [6 notes: BINTSHATI(الراغب); BINTSHATI+]

## 89:1/q02 What color is the light called fajr?  [ٱلْفَجْرِ · meaning]
turns on: The Kufan and Medinan grammarians give different colors; the latter invoke garment usage.
- 89:1/q02/p1 White. — The Kufan grammarians call it whiteness; Elmalili describes first whiteness and Bint al-Shati cites 2:187's white thread. [3 notes: NAHHAS(أهل الكوفة); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]
- 89:1/q02/p2 Red. — The Medinan grammarians cite Arab usage for a red-dyed garment. [1 notes: NAHHAS(أهل المدينة)]

## 89:1/q03 What does al-fajr designate in the oath?  [ٱلْفَجْرِ · referent]

## 89:1/q04 Which dawn governs prayer and fasting?  [ٱلْفَجْرِ · law]
- 89:1/q04/p1 The horizontal true dawn; the earlier vertical false dawn is legally night. — Mawardi says the first narrow streak misleads and cites poetic al-musaddaq for truthful dawn. Jishumi, Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalili assign prayer and fasting rules only to spreading true dawn; Bursevi compares false dawn to a wolf's tail. Tabarisi distinguishes the two. Alusi says the legal role makes true dawn especially apt for the oath. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الشاعر); JISHUMI; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(علي وابن عباس وابن الزبير); ELMALILI]

## 89:1/q05 How is the oath phrase grammatically construed?  [وَٱلْفَجْرِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the sworn object is named directly or an omitted head noun is supplied.
- 89:1/q05/p1 The oath wāw governs al-fajr in the genitive, directly swearing by dawn. — Nahhas gives the case analysis. Ibn al-Zubayr reports that God swears by dawn; Qurtubi counts five oaths. [5 notes: NAHHAS; TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن الزبير); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن الزبير); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن الزبير)]
- 89:1/q05/p2 An omitted noun makes it an oath by the Lord of dawn. — Samin reports this; Bint al-Shati attributes supplied genitives to Tabari, Naysaburi and Zamakhshari. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BINTSHATI(الطبري)]
- 89:1/q05/p3 An omitted noun makes it an oath by dawn prayer or Quran of dawn. — Samin gives prayer and Bint al-Shati reports Quran of dawn as another completion. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BINTSHATI(الطبري)]

## 89:1/q06 Who may swear by a created dawn?  [وَٱلْفَجْرِ · law]
- 89:1/q06/p1 God may swear by any created thing; humans may swear only by God. — Maymun ibn Mihran draws this rule from the divine oath. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ميمون بن مهران)]

## 89:1/q07 Is al-fajr recited with verse-final tanwin?  [ٱلْفَجْرِ · readings]
turns on: Whether verse endings are pronounced like poetic pauses; grammarians also dispute the form's name.
- 89:1/q07/p1 Most readers leave al-fajr without tanwin. — Samin says they likewise leave al-watr and yasr without it. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو الدينار الأعرابي)]
- 89:1/q07/p2 Abu al-Dinar al-Aʿrabi adds poetic pausal tanwin to al-fajr, al-watr and yasr. — Kashshaf says it substitutes for a lengthening letter and also reports Ibn Abbas's separate annexed layālin ʿashr. Abu Hayyan adduces a poem and grammatical discussion of open rhyme and ordinary pause. Ibn Khalawayh calls it tanwin al-tarannum, even on verbs and definite nouns. Samin notes some prefer tanwin tark al-tarannum and distinguish disputed tanwin al-ghali on closed rhymes; he cites 33:66-67, 33:10 and 13:9. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الدينار الأعرابي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو الدينار الأعرابي,ابن خالويه)]

## 89:1/q08 What is the answer to the opening oath?  [وَٱلْفَجْرِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether verse 14 states the response, ensuing stories imply an omitted one, or the preceding sura supplies one.
- 89:1/q08/p1 The expressed answer is that the Lord is surely watchful (89:14). — Zajjaj, Tabarisi, Ibn al-Anbari and Razi identify it; Bint al-Shati reports Razi treating intervening material as parenthetical. Ibn Masud says the oaths affirm watchfulness. Kuran Yolu adds knowledge of deeds and requital. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأنباري); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 89:1/q08/p2 An unstated answer implies resurrection, judgment, punishment or requital. — Tabarisi suggests seizing oppressors or vindicating victims. Samin infers each person's recompense from earlier peoples and reports Zamakhshari's 'He will surely punish' from 89:6 and 89:13. Kuran Yolu gives resurrection and judgment. Bint al-Shati reports Ibn al-Qayyim's implicit answer in what befell Abraham and Muhammad. [5 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI(ابن القيم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 89:1/q08/p3 The omitted answer is the end of al-Ghashiya: return to God for reckoning. — Samin reports the Shaykh's appeal to 88:25-26. Bint al-Shati reports Abu Hayyan and Muhammad Abduh arguing from printed adjacency; she rejects this because al-Ghashiya was revealed 58 suras after al-Fajr. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); BINTSHATI(أوب حيان); BINTSHATI-]

## 89:1/q09 Why is dawn chosen as an oath sign?  [وَٱلْفَجْرِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources stress divine order, resurrection, particular rites, moral symbolism and the oaths' evidentiary role.
- 89:1/q09/p1 Its ordered light and benefit show the Creator's sole power. — Jishumi calls these signs proof of the Creator; Razi notes that dawn sends people and animals seeking provision. Islahi says God alone brings forth and removes, grants respite and harmonizes opposing forces (21:22); 28:71-72 illustrates His power over perpetual night or day. Elmalili sees changing time as lordship. Qutb portrays creation joyfully waking and praying. Corpus Coranicum cites a likely authentic Umayya poem greeting the Lord each dawn, suggesting a cultic connotation. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/q09/p2 Its daily return after dark images resurrection and judgment. — Razi and Bursevi compare dawn's spreading life with rising again. Biqai likens waking from sleep's lesser death to return, work and eventual recompense. Islahi invokes a waking supplication, 74:32-34, moral incompleteness without judgment and 68:35-36; ease and hardship prepare for 89:28. Kuran Yolu cites waking living things and 81:18. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:1/q09/p3 The particular pilgrimage dawn carries collective joy and major rites. — Elmalili prefers its collective joy; the Dhu al-Hijja nights, Arafah and sacrifice sequence fits the sura and urges worship during limited life. Biqai mentions entry to the sanctuary and release from consecration; Bursevi cites Arafah supplication and Adha rites; Qurtubi links the dawn to pilgrimage completion. Corpus Coranicum finds a cultic ten-night interpretation persuasive despite objections to Neuwirth's liturgical reading. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج عن عطاء عن ابن عباس ومجاهد); BURSEVI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ELMALILI+]
- 89:1/q09/p4 Darkness yielding to light prompts moral reflection and repentance. — Bint al-Shati reads dawn, crescent and night as truth emerging after tyranny, with wealth and poverty tests and the tyrants' and tranquil soul's outcomes to follow. Bursevi urges washing sins with tears. Qutb pictures a joyful waking creation. Bint al-Shati reports Zamakhshari's admonitory reading of 89:6 and 89:14. [5 notes: BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 89:1/q09/p5 The oath objects are evidence for the sura's claim. — Islahi says identify the claim and compare parallel Quranic passages. Bint al-Shati rejects making every object a symbol of honor; she reports Tabari's caution against unsupported interpretation and Razi's and Zamakhshari's criticism of speculation. Elmalili notes disagreement on definiteness, article scope and oath objects. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI(الطبري); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 89:1/q10 How does dawn relate to the following and parallel oaths?  [وَٱلْفَجْرِ · links]
turns on: The next oath supports different calendars; other sources read the series through cosmic contrasts.
- 89:1/q10/p1 Dawn and departing night are the chief contrast; intervening oaths develop it. — Ibn Ashur says the blessed ten are a subset of night. Corpus Coranicum compares 74:34, 81:18, 91:1-3, 92:2 and 93:1 and notes that the ten uniquely quantify night. Islahi calls first and last primary and the middle secondary. Tabatabai glosses dawn, even, odd and withdrawing night; al-Aswad pairs familiar dawn with Dhu al-Hijja's ten. [5 notes: FARRA(الأسود بن يزيد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/q10/p2 Cosmic oppositions anticipate moral oppositions and final recompense. — Corpus Coranicum compares 91:9-10 and 92:4-10, plus similar oath openings in 91-93, 95, 100 and 103. Bint al-Shati sees truth after tyranny; Islahi says worldly pairs call for an afterlife settling good and evil. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/q10/p3 The formula resembles Quranic oaths and may echo pre-Islamic soothsayer language. — Corpus Coranicum calls the soothsayer link probable but under-studied, compares 100:1-5, points to discussion of 93:1-2 and lists similar sura openings. Islahi compares receding night and appearing morning in 74:32-34. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/q10/p4 Dawn and ten nights point to Dhu al-Hijja and sacrifice rites. — Al-Dahhak and Ibn al-Qayyim argue from the next oath. A Jabir report identifies the dawn and ten with the ten of sacrifice; Elmalili reports Tabari endorsing it and prefers the ten nights, Arafah and Sacrifice Day sequence. [10 notes: THALABI(ضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(مجاهد والضحاك); RAZI-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الزبير عن جابر عن النبي,الضحاك ومجاهد والسدي والكلبي وابن عباس); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(ابن قيم الجوزية); STUDYQURAN(others); ELMALILI+]
- 89:1/q10/p5 Dawn and ten nights point to Muharram's first days. — Study Quran explicitly pairs Muharram's beginning and ten nights; Qatada calls it the year's first day. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(some); THALABI(عثمان بن محصن); RAZI-FULL(قوم)]
- 89:1/q10/p6 Dawn and ten nights point to Ramadan's final ten and first revelation. — Study Quran cites 97:1. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(others)]

## 89:1/q11 How does this opening connect to the sura and al-Ghashiya?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources stress judgment, moral testing, divine power and the basmala.
- 89:1/q11/p1 It supports return, reckoning and divine watchfulness announced at al-Ghashiya's end. — Jishumi links the prior chapter's return and judgment to this chapter's warning and encouragement. Biqai repeatedly cites 88:26, waking from death-like sleep and work before recompense; pilgrimage clothing, talbiya and sacred travel evoke resurrection. Islahi says the prior signs of divine power, wisdom and providence require accountability. Alusi reports Suyuti's view that the oath affirms al-Ghashiya's promise and threat and parallels its opening questions. [6 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/q11/p2 It prepares tyrants' stories, human tests and the tranquil soul at judgment. — Alusi connects al-Ghashiya's two classes of faces (88:2, 88:8) with deniers and the blessed soul (89:27). Study Quran traces oaths, tyrants, fortune, stinginess and judgment. Tabatabai says worldly attachment causes tyranny and ingratitude; wealth and poverty test preparation for the next life, while the tranquil soul stays submitted through gain and loss. Bint al-Shati anticipates these oppositions in the natural contrasts. Qutb describes Meccan scenes, rhythms, rhyme, warning, history and moral correction. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 89:1/q11/p3 The signs establish sole divine rule and the need for a just afterlife. — Islahi says the Prophet warns through heavenly and earthly signs even when some cannot see; harmonized opposites require one sovereign (21:22), and unrequited good and evil would make creation purposeless (68:35-36). Alternating day and night show even the powerful cannot escape God (28:71-72). [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/q11/p4 The basmala evokes mercy, lovers' self-offering and gathering at resurrection. — Qushayri says reciting satisfies worshippers while lovers offer life; the basmala unsettles the poor, disorients the enamored and brings mercy to lovers. Biqai portrays God reassembling dispersed people, giving guidance and granting His allies contentment and gardens. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 89:1/q12 What inward meaning is given to dawn?  [ٱلْفَجْرِ · inward]
turns on: These readings transpose dawn's light to prophetic, devotional and eschatological experience.
- 89:1/q12/p1 Muhammad, through whom faith and obedience shine and unbelief disappears. — Tustari adds the lights of both worlds; Ibn Ata as reported by Sulami stresses faith dispelling unbelief. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 89:1/q12/p2 At the great resurrection, divine light appearing in the soul's night. — Qashani relates ten to dormant senses during disclosure, pair to witness and witnessed, odd to oneness after duality and night to disappearing selfhood. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 89:1/q12/p3 At death's lesser resurrection, the sun rising from the west. — Qashani relates ten to dimmed senses, pair to spirit and body, odd to separated spirit and night to bodily darkness dispersing. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 89:1/q12/p4 The saints' opening worship or lovers' communion with God. — Qushayri reports the former and Kashifi through Bursevi the latter. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 89:1/q13 Where and when was the sura revealed, and how many verses has it?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Most call it Meccan, with a Medinan report; verse counts vary.
- 89:1/q13/p1 Meccan, often placed early after al-Layl. — Kashshaf gives thirty verses with twenty-nine as an alternative. Durr cites Ibn Abbas and Ibn al-Zubayr for Meccan revelation and thirty verses. Alusi says this is the majority, while regional verse counts differ. Bint al-Shati places it tenth, after al-Layl and before al-Duha; Study Quran says probably tenth. Tabatabai infers Meccan origin from context; Qutb calls it Meccan. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN(most commentators); TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 89:1/q13/p2 Medinan. — Alusi attributes this to Ali ibn Abi Talha; Study Quran notes some commentators hold it. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]

## 89:1/q14 What reports concern reciting Surat al-Fajr?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe distinct merits and uses.
- 89:1/q14/p1 Recitation brings forgiveness; daily recitation brings light on Resurrection Day. — Jishumi transmits the attributed Prophetic promise through Ubayy ibn Kab. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي بن كعب)]
- 89:1/q14/p2 The Prophet recommended it among short suras for communal prayer. — Ibn Kathir and Durr recount a worshipper leaving Muadh's long prayer and the Prophet rebuking Muadh for driving people away; the suggested chapters also include 87:1, 91:1 and 92:1. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 89:1/q15 What Muharram and Ashura reports accompany this dawn interpretation?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are distinct accompanying reports; the eye-treatment report is explicitly weakened.
- 89:1/q15/p1 Muharram and Ashura are especially recommended for fasting. — Durr reports the Prophet calling Muharram God's month and best for fasting after Ramadan, with night prayer best after obligatory prayer. Ali recommends Muharram because of repentance and forgiveness. Reports commend an adjacent day to Ashura or ninth and tenth to differ from Jews. Ibn Shihab fasted while traveling because Ashura would pass; Abu Musa and Abu Hurayra connect its fast to Jewish festivity and prophets' practice. [7 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو جبلة,أبو موسى,أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب)]
- 89:1/q15/p2 Generosity to one's family on Ashura brings provision through the year. — Durr gives reports through Jabir, Ibn Masud, Abu Said and Abu Hurayra and a traditional saying; Bayhaqi says individually weak chains support one another. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو هريرة,إبراهيم بن محمد بن المنتشر,ابن مسعود,جابر)]
- 89:1/q15/p3 Antimony on the eyes on Ashura prevents inflammation forever. — Durr reports it through Urwa from Ibn Abbas and notes Bayhaqi weakened it. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عروة عن ابن عباس)]

## 89:1/q16 What do attached reports say the ten nights, even and odd denote?  [* · reports]
turns on: They differ over even and odd: pilgrimage days or creation and God.
- 89:1/q16/p1 The ten are Dhu al-Hijja's first ten, odd is Arafah and even is Sacrifice Day. — Thalabi and Durr transmit Jabir's report from the Prophet. [2 notes: THALABI(جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي); DURR-FULL(جابر)]
- 89:1/q16/p2 The ten are the ten of sacrifice, even is created pairs and odd is God. — A report through Atiyya and Abu Said links pairs to 78:8 and God's oneness to 112:1; Durr gives a related report citing 78:8. [2 notes: THALABI(عطية العوفي عن أبي سعيد الخدري عن النبي); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري)]
