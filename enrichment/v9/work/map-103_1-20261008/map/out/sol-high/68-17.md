# 68:17: 25 questions

## 68:17/q01 Who is meant by 'We tested them'?  [بَلَوْنَٰهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural is confined to the historical deniers or includes their type.
- 68:17/q01/p1 The Meccan or Quraysh deniers addressed in the preceding passage. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī and many later authors identify the addressees as Meccans; Ibn ʿĀshūr links the pronoun to the preceding deniers (68:8, 14–15). Iṣlāḥī takes the preceding portrait to describe Quraysh leadership collectively. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:17/q01/p2 The deniers and, more broadly, people of the same contemptible oath-breaking character. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ explicitly extends the plural beyond its immediate deniers; her objection to a narrowly retrospective Badr reading leaves a general warning to tyrants. [2 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 68:17/q02 How were the people addressed tested?  [بَلَوْنَٰهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the comparison names an experienced famine, a test by gifts and guidance, or the Badr reversal.
- 68:17/q02/p1 Through famine, drought and hunger, often linked to the Prophet's prayer for years like Joseph's. — Al-Zajjāj names disease, death and lost provisions; al-Qushayrī, Biqāʿī and others say they ate carrion; Bursawī adds hides, bones and blood. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Elmalılı give seven years. Abū Ḥayyān cites the Prophet's supplication. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the famine setting but observes that the owners' mutual blame does not parallel Mecca easily; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports the seven-year famine comparison. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,قيل); BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)]
- 68:17/q02/p2 Through prosperity, secure trade, wealth, children and Muhammad's mission, which called for gratitude and right conduct. — Al-Rāzī sees blessings as a test of gratitude and obedience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares Muhammad's guidance with the brothers' inherited duties. Biqāʿī says ease made deniers disdain poor believers; Ibn ʿĀshūr details security and distant trade; Asad says affluence exceeded moral deserts. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:17/q02/p3 Through the defeat at Badr, when Quraysh's apparent power over opponents failed. — Ibn Jurayj relates Abū Jahl's order to bind captives in mountains rather than kill them; the owners' supposed control of the orchard supplies the likeness. Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees Badr as worldly proof that apparent causes fail if the passage preceded it. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports the comparison with a pre-Badr vow, then rejects it as reading an event about five years later into the passage. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI-]

## 68:17/q03 What does balāʾ mean here, and how can God test?  [بَلَوْنَٰهُمْ بَلَوْنَآ · theology]
- 68:17/q03/p1 A trial or affliction that exposes conduct; divine testing assigns duties without giving God new knowledge. — Jishumī says trial, testing and examination are synonymous and that acts become manifest although God knew them beforehand, so the expression is figurative of His treatment. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ distinguishes balāʾ as affliction or trial from ibtilāʾ as examination; Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows both test and affliction. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BINTSHATI; TABATABAI]

## 68:17/q04 How is 'as We tested' constructed?  [كَمَا بَلَوْنَآ · grammar]
- 68:17/q04/p1 The kāf qualifies an omitted cognate accusative; mā can be a verbal noun or a relative pronoun. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give both analyses of mā; al-Samīn explicitly calls the kāf an accusative adjective of the omitted verbal noun. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 68:17/q05 What is the point of comparing their trial with the orchard owners' trial?  [كَمَا بَلَوْنَآ أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which feature of the story the comparison selects: withdrawal of blessings, famine, or Badr.
- 68:17/q05/p1 Blessings can be withdrawn suddenly when their recipients respond with pride, ingratitude and denial of others' rights. — Muqātil calls the story a parable inviting Meccan unbelievers to return to God; Ibn ʿAṭiyya leaves repentance open to survivors. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Cave and Qarun (18:32; 28:76); Elmalılı says the law spans generations. Iṣlāḥī stresses that Quraysh's peace and leaders' authority can vanish, making later grief futile; Kur'an Yolu cites 4:147, 14:7 and 31:12 for gratitude and its opposite. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:17/q05/p2 Mecca's famine parallels the garden's destruction as a worldly punishment. — Al-Zajjāj associates the trial with the Prophet's prayer against Muḍar; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports seven years; al-Zamakhsharī says punishment of the afterlife is still greater. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the final mutual blame does not fit this comparison easily. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 68:17/q05/p3 The owners' overconfidence parallels Quraysh's presumed power before Badr. — Ibn Jurayj supplies Abū Jahl's proposal; Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates Badr to the failure of apparent causes. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects on chronology: Badr occurred about five years after this sūra. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI-]

## 68:17/q06 What does janna denote here?  [ٱلْجَنَّةِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is read only as the physical orchard or also as a social symbol.
- 68:17/q06/p1 An earthly orchard or garden. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces janna to concealment, comparing embryo, jinn, insanity, night and shield; she says Qur'anic context distinguishes earthly gardens from Paradise and counts singular, dual and plural uses. Elmalılı calls it an earthly paradise and also suggests a deeper image of homeland. [3 notes: ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]
- 68:17/q06/p2 At a deeper level, a homeland held and managed collectively. — Elmalılı notes the plural owners, unlike the single owner in al-Kahf 18:35, to support a social or national parable. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 68:17/q07 Is 'the garden' a particular known orchard or a generic parable?  [ٱلْجَنَّةِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite article points to a known place or serves the parable generically.
- 68:17/q07/p1 A particular orchard known from accounts about Yemen and its owners. — Al-Qurṭubī says the story was locally known; Ibn ʿĀshūr associates it with Darwan; Elmalılı says Arabs knew it. Qatāda says God has recounted and made clear these people's story. [8 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 68:17/q07/p2 The definite article is generic, presenting a complete parable rather than identifying one historical orchard. — Iṣlāḥī explicitly rejects using the article to identify an orchard or owners in Yemen or Ṣanʿāʾ. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 68:17/q08 Where was the garden said to be, and what was it called?  [أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ · reports]
turns on: Reported place names, distances and whether a name denotes the garden or its locality.
- 68:17/q08/p1 In Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, without a fixed name or distance. — Accounts vary between an orchard, fertile estate and farmland; some place it outside the city. Ibn Kathīr transmits Yemen or Abyssinia as alternate localities. [17 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 68:17/q08/p2 At Darwān, Ḍarwān or Darawan near Ṣanʿāʾ. — This is variously the village, orchard or locality; reported distances differ: al-Māwardī and al-Wāḥidī give twelve miles, al-Qurṭubī one farsakh, al-Suhaylī several farsakhs, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says some call Darwan the garden's name. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(السهيلي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:17/q08/p3 At Ṣarwān, Ṣurawān or Sawran near Ṣanʿāʾ. — Al-Ālūsī's report from Ibn Jarīr says six miles; al-Nasafī relates Ṣarwān to nearby Darwān. Spellings vary across the sources. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جرير); ELMALILI]
- 68:17/q08/p4 Two farsakhs from Ṣanʿāʾ; one account calls it al-Sarim. — Muqātil gives al-Sarim, palms, crops and grapes; al-Kalbī's distance is repeated by al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī, and al-Kalbī says it burned. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 68:17/q08/p5 At Dar, six miles from Ṣanʿāʾ. — Al-Durr transmits this from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 68:17/q08/p6 At al-Ridwan in Yemen, nine miles from Ṣanʿāʾ. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits this from Abū Jaʿfar. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر)]
- 68:17/q08/p7 In Abyssinia. — Ibn Kathīr reports this as one alternative to Yemen; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ mentions it among exegetical elaborations. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BINTSHATI]

## 68:17/q09 Who were the garden owners by people, religion or kinship?  [أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ · referent]
turns on: Conflicting reports identify nationality, religion and historical period differently.
- 68:17/q09/p1 Heirs of a righteous father, commonly described as sons or brothers. — Muqātil calls their forebears righteous Muslims after Jesus; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the heirs brothers. Some accounts name three sons, while Ibn ʿAbbās in Ṭabāṭabāʾī names five; these number reports are set out separately below. [21 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:17/q09/p2 Abyssinians or Ethiopians. — ʿIkrima and Ibn ʿAbbās relate a charitable father whose sons called him foolish and vowed to stop feeding the poor; al-Māwardī separately reports this identification. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 68:17/q09/p3 People of the Book, without a fixed Jewish or Christian identification. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the father was righteous and believing, without specifying Jewish or Christian; Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās and al-Durr. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI]
- 68:17/q09/p4 Israelites, including an elder who gave away his annual surplus. — Qatāda's account gives the elder and sons; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī report the identification as an alternative. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 68:17/q09/p5 A Muslim man or family of Thaqīf in Yemen. — Al-Wāḥidī attributes Thaqīf to the exegetes; al-Rāzī reports a Muslim Thaqīf owner near Ṣanʿāʾ. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL]
- 68:17/q09/p6 A Jewish owner. — Some exegetes cited by Ibn ʿĀshūr reason from Jewish settlement in Yemen in or after Bilqīs's era and Torah tithes on fruit. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]
- 68:17/q09/p7 Unbelievers. — Al-Nasafī reports this from al-Ḥasan, while saying the majority held otherwise. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 68:17/q09/p8 People living shortly after Jesus was raised. — Some exegetes date them before Christianity spread in Yemen; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the proposed chronology near the Abyssinian conquest and Year of the Elephant. [5 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن); IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]

## 68:17/q10 How many heirs were there?  [أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ · reports]
turns on: Different numerical reports.
- 68:17/q10/p1 Three brothers or sons. — Al-Baghawī and Bursawī give three heirs; ʿIkrima's report via Ibn ʿĀshūr calls them three wealthy sons. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 68:17/q10/p2 Five sons, four supporting the plan and a moderate brother objecting. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī relays Ibn ʿAbbās's account of the unusually abundant harvest and the dissenting fifth brother. [1 notes: TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]

## 68:17/q11 What had the father given to the poor?  [أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ · reports]
turns on: Accounts differ over surplus, gleanings and an owed share, which may coexist.
- 68:17/q11/p1 He kept his family's annual food and gave the surplus to the poor. — Al-Zajjāj also records this alongside gleanings; Qatāda describes the old man's sons objecting during his lifetime. Ibn Kathīr says the eventual loss covered crop, profit and charity. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 68:17/q11/p2 He let the poor collect gleanings, missed fruit, fallen dates, grapes and what harvesting tools or wind left. — Muqātil, al-Farrāʾ, al-Baghawī, al-Bayḍāwī and others specify what pickers, sickles or wind missed. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says one account had the father bring the poor along at harvest; al-Wāḥidī says he announced it to them. [17 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 68:17/q11/p3 He paid God's due or gave a prescribed share to its rightful recipients. — Al-Naḥḥās describes the poor's obligatory due; al-Qurṭubī says the Yemenite father paid God's due; Ibn ʿAbbās's report in Ṭabāṭabāʾī says each rightful person received a share. [7 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]

## 68:17/q12 Why did the heirs plan to exclude the poor?  [أَقْسَمُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Different reports emphasize household need or rejection of the father's charitable practice.
- 68:17/q12/p1 They feared their many dependents would be left short by continued giving. — Muqātil says they thought food plentiful yet had many dependents; al-Farrāʾ says wealth had shrunk. Al-Wāḥidī says this excuse ended in ruin; later reports describe fear of poverty. [13 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:17/q12/p2 They judged their father's charity foolish and resolved to keep its benefit for themselves. — ʿIkrima and Ibn ʿAbbās report their contempt for their father; Qatāda says they opposed him while he lived. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they judged his generosity mistaken. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة)]

## 68:17/q13 How does 'when they swore' attach, and why mention an oath?  [إِذْ أَقْسَمُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The notes give both syntactic attachment and differing rhetorical functions of the oath.
- 68:17/q13/p1 Idh is governed by balawnā. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give the syntactic attachment. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:17/q13/p2 The oath shows collective commitment to the plan, perhaps overcoming one brother's dissent. — Ibn ʿĀshūr infers a hesitant brother from 68:28; a report says he warned them to fear God and recall punishment, but the others secured participation by oath. Elmalılı likens the opening oath to an agreement that governs a shared enterprise's profit and loss. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 68:17/q13/p3 The oath warns against overconfident vows made without regard to knowledge or power. — Elmalılı links it to the earlier 'frequent swearer'; Iṣlāḥī says the owners trusted the garden's past survival of weather. Jishumī faults swearing to deny the poor. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 68:17/q14 What did the oath resolve to do?  [أَقْسَمُوا۟ لَيَصْرِمُنَّهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the oath is read only as intended harvest or also as an ominous literal cutting of the garden.
- 68:17/q14/p1 Harvest all the fruit secretly before the poor arrived, leaving them no share. — Muqātil says they would avoid announcing harvest as their father did; al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī and others describe dawn gathering. Al-Ālūsī says cutting was planned when ripe; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ stresses that everything would be taken. Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests the oath bound a hesitant brother. [24 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI+]
- 68:17/q14/p2 Their confident oath came true as a cutting of the orchard itself, to their harm and without their hand. — Elmalılı distinguishes their intended gathering from the literal words 'cut the garden,' then says the sworn event occurred unexpectedly in the ruin. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 68:17/q15 How is layasrimannahā related to the oath?  [لَيَصْرِمُنَّهَا · grammar]
- 68:17/q15/p1 It is the oath's response in reported third person; direct quotation would be first-person plural. — Al-Samīn supplies hypothetical lanasrimannahā; al-Ālūsī says the wording reports rather than quotes; Abū Ḥayyān calls it the oath response. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 68:17/q16 What does ṣarm or layasrimannahā mean?  [لَيَصْرِمُنَّهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is explained by its ordinary harvest sense or its force of complete severance.
- 68:17/q16/p1 Cutting or harvesting fruit, especially date clusters, or gathering the crop. — Al-Ṭabarī cites Imruʾ al-Qays using ṣaram for severing a relationship; Jishumī adds ṣarām and jadhādh for harvest and ṣarm for separation between companions. Al-Naḥḥās distinguishes jadhādh and says the verb means cut and gather; al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī explain aṣrama as palms ready for cutting. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes later figurative extension. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ELMALILI]
- 68:17/q16/p2 An emphatic complete severing of the garden that excludes the poor. — Biqāʿī hears finality in the expression; Elmalılı notes that their literal oath named cutting the garden itself. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 68:17/q17 When was the harvest planned, and how does muṣbiḥīn work?  [مُصْبِحِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether 'entering morning' emphasizes dawn itself or the dark period immediately before it.
- 68:17/q17/p1 At the beginning of morning or dawn, before the poor learned of it. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Samīn call muṣbiḥīn circumstantial; al-Samīn links it to the verb's subject and compares 37:137 and a proverb about a journeyer arrived by morning. Ibn ʿAṭiyya simply glosses entering morning. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:17/q17/p2 In late-night or predawn darkness while keeping the action secret. — Al-Wāḥidī calls the dimness sadfa; al-Farrāʾ says late-night darkness; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Qurṭubī also put it before dawn. [6 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 68:17/q18 What did they fail to except from their plan?  [أَقْسَمُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the omitted exception concerns God's will in the oath or the poor's share of the harvest.
- 68:17/q18/p1 They did not say 'if God wills' when vowing about the future. — Al-Zamakhsharī explains that the conditional expression functions as an exception because their meanings coincide; al-Rāzī calls this the majority view and connects it to overconfidence. Jishumī says one cannot be sure a future statement will occur. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects this as the verse's apparent gloss. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; JISHUMI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-]
- 68:17/q18/p2 They intended to make no exception for the poor and leave none of the crop. — Al-Rāzī reports this alternative; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers the apparent story of taking everything at dawn over the divine-will formula. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI+]

## 68:17/q19 What legal and moral lessons are drawn from the planned deprivation?  [أَقْسَمُوا۟ لَيَصْرِمُنَّهَا · law]
turns on: The notes draw distinct consequences for harvest rights, intention and future oaths.
- 68:17/q19/p1 The poor had a claim on the harvest; harvesters should give those present a share. — Al-Naḥḥās calls payment an obligatory due. Al-Qurṭubī prefers a distinct harvest right based on 6:141, separate from zakāt, and favors forbidding night harvest when used to deprive the poor. [4 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 68:17/q19/p2 Resolve to do evil is already blameworthy and can draw punishment before the act. — Jishumī says taking property and denying rights in fact would be worse; the owners were punished for resolve. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 68:17/q19/p3 Future-directed speech should include an exception because fulfillment is uncertain. — Jishumī treats the owners' omitted exception as support for this requirement. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 68:17/q20 What happened to the orchard after the oath?  [أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ on the garden's physical fate and the origin of Ṭāʾif's name.
- 68:17/q20/p1 It burned or was blighted overnight and appeared black, ruined or reduced to ashes. — Muqātil says it perished; al-Māwardī gives ashes; al-Baghawī says the heirs found it black; al-Nasafī calls it a blight. Al-Zamakhsharī reports ṭayf beside ṭāʾif for the calamity and refers to 18:42; he lists ṣarīm as cut, blackened, dry, empty or sandy. The owners initially thought they had lost their way, then admitted deprivation through their wrongdoing. [17 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:17/q20/p2 Gabriel swept the garden around the Kaaba and set it at what became Ṭāʾif. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as an explanation for the town's name and fertility. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 68:17/q20/p3 Ṭāʾif's name came instead from a man of Ṣadaf who built a wall around the town. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits al-Bakrī's competing etymology. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(البكري)]

## 68:17/q21 How do later phrases in the story describe the owners' conduct and response?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Different later expressions locate the action, the warning, the confession and the hoped-for restoration.
- 68:17/q21/p1 Their secret departure aimed to deny the poor but left them deprived themselves. — Al-Zamakhsharī explains ḥard through withholding good, withholding milk, determination or speed; he cites a line of poetry and swift birds called ḥarād. Another reading makes ḥard their garden's name or confidence in success; an interpretation of ʿalā ḥard allows only anger and mutual blame. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 68:17/q21/p2 They went toward the crop, whispered, and barred poor people from entering. — Al-Zamakhsharī explains ʿalā ḥarthikum by harvesting or by 'turned toward'; yatahāfatūn means whispering, supported by related roots and the bat name. 'An lā yadkhulannahā' is explanatory; Ibn Masʿūd reads it without an, with 'they say' supplied. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 68:17/q21/p3 The middle brother was the most just and best, and urged remembrance, repentance or restraint. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares 2:143 and prefers that 'Why do you not glorify?' mean remember God and repent before punishment. He reports alternatives: making an 'if God wills' exception or prayer; al-Ḥasan says neglected prayer would have restrained the wrong. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the hesitant brother's warning and the others' override. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 68:17/q21/p4 After recognizing the ruin they confessed their wrong and blamed each other. — Al-Zamakhsharī says 'Glory be to our Lord' denies injustice to God and admits withholding charity and omitting the exception. Some proposed the plan, others accepted, opposed or silently approved; hence mutual blame. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 68:17/q21/p5 Their words express hope for God's pardon and a replacement garden. — Al-Zamakhsharī notes readings of yubdilanā with and without doubling and glosses desire toward the Lord as hope for His good and pardon. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 68:17/q22 What became of the owners afterward?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports state different degrees of restoration or leave the final outcome unanswered.
- 68:17/q22/p1 They repented and were given a better garden. — Mujāhid reports this; al-Qushayrī says another account has God replace the garden, followed by a renewed secret harvest plan. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 68:17/q22/p2 They became sincere and received al-Ḥayawān, with grape clusters requiring a donkey to carry. — Al-Zamakhsharī transmits this from Ibn Masʿūd. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 68:17/q22/p3 Their ultimate destination is left unsettled. — When asked whether they were of Paradise or Hell, Qatāda said the question burdened him. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة)]

## 68:17/q23 How does this parable connect to its sūra and nearby verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources relate the parable to nearby deniers, shared responsibility and ultimate punishment.
- 68:17/q23/p1 It continues the warning against proud, wealthy deniers while consoling the Prophet and urging patience. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the new parable to wealth and children in 68:14; Elmalılı links the oath to the earlier frequent swearer. Ṭabāṭabāʾī summarizes the sūra's reply to accusations of madness, praise of the Prophet's character, rejection of compromise and command to await God's judgment. The Cave and Qarun stories supply parallels. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 68:17/q23/p2 The plot shows the shared wealth and joint responsibility implied by plural owners. — Elmalılı contrasts the single owner in al-Kahf 18:35 and treats the oath as the founding agreement of a communal enterprise. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 68:17/q23/p3 The worldly punishment foreshadows a greater punishment in the afterlife. — Al-Zamakhsharī makes the explicit comparison. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 68:17/q24 When were the garden verses revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether verses 17–33 share the sūra's early Meccan setting and how that bears on Badr.
- 68:17/q24/p1 The sūra is early Meccan, but the garden passage may have been revealed separately in Medina. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds the sūra's overall Meccan setting clear but judges a Medinan origin for the seventeen garden verses plausible. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ excepts 17–33 and 48–50 from the early Meccan sūra and notes the traditional placement after al-ʿAlaq (96:1), despite ordering disputes. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 68:17/q24/p2 The garden warning can be read with the preceding passage and Badr as a later worldly demonstration. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes this conditional on joint revelation; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects reading a Badr event into the passage because Badr came about five years afterward. [1 notes: TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-]

## 68:17/q25 How does the preceding threat of a mark relate to the parable's warning?  [* · links]
- 68:17/q25/p1 A report sees the mark on the nose as humiliation on Judgment Day for one who showed arrogance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this reading and mentions 3:106; the report belongs to the preceding threat, which the orchard warning follows. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
