# 18:25: 19 questions

## 18:25/q01 Who states the three hundred and nine year duration?  [وَلَبِثُوا۟ ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍۢ سِنِينَ وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause continues earlier quoted claims or resumes God's narrative; the variant with 'they said' and the force of 18:26 are disputed.
- 18:25/q01/p1 God states the sleepers' true duration. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr take the count literally. Al-Ṭabarī says unmarked quotation needs proof; al-Zajjāj calls the number a stronger sign. Al-Baghawī says God's subsequent claim to know best confirms disclosed knowledge. Al-Rāzī cites the intervening ban on dispute; al-Ṭabrisī says an unmarked quotation would leave the proof of divine power incomplete. Ibn Kathīr says the People of the Book gave 300 solar years without the nine and the Ibn Masʿūd report is anomalous with disconnected transmission. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī appeals to passage structure and says their reported figure was 200 or less. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a possible continuation of the narrative; al-Kuranyolu presents it as interpretation B. [33 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد، وعبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير، والضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مجاهد والضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبيد بن عمير ومجاهد والضحاك وابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,مجاهد); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(others,still others); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+; ZAJJAJ+; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 18:25/q01/p2 The number quotes the People of the Book or other disputants, whose claim God refers back to His own knowledge. — Qatāda connects it to the earlier guesses of 18:22; Maṭar al-Warrāq names Jews, while other accounts name Jews and Christians of Najrān. Ibn Masʿūd's reading inserts 'and they said'; Ibn ʿAbbās and others argue 18:26 would be strange after an exact divine statement. Al-Rāzī and Asad cite the following reply; al-Qāshānī says the duration is not established. Al-Kuranyolu prefers this by verse order and links the sleepers' admission in 18:19. Al-Ālūsī challenges use of 'God knows best' as decisive because it also follows a count Ibn ʿAbbās accepted at 18:22. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī demand evidence for an unmarked quotation. [47 notes: MUQATIL(النصارى); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة,مطر الوراق); THALABI(ابن مسعود,قتادة,مطر الوراق); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود,يهود ونصارى نجران); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس رواية الضحاك,قتادة,مطر الوراق); JISHUMI(قتادة,مطر الوراق); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة ومطر الوراق); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة ومطر الوراق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); ELMALILI(İbn Mes‘ud); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن مسعود); ASAD-NOTES(Abd Allah ibn Masud, Qatadah, Matar al-Warraq); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler,İbn Mes‘ûd); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TAB-FULL-; JISHUMI-; TABRISI-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 18:25/q01/p3 Some People of the Book said 300 years and others added nine. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Kuranyolu describe variant traditional counts. Al-Khafājī takes the pronoun of 'they added' as the People of the Book; al-Ālūsī finds this weak. Al-Kalbī says Najrān Christians knew 300 but not the extra nine, occasioning revelation of the divine answer. [4 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)-]
- 18:25/q01/p4 The victorious Cave party stayed hidden for 309 years. — Elmalılı reads the clause as a fresh resumption about the party whose cause prevailed; he links its emergence to Christians overcoming idolatrous Romans in the early fourth century and sees a foreshadowing of Islam's faster victory after the Hijra. [3 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]

## 18:25/q02 What interval does their stay in the cave measure?  [وَلَبِثُوا۟ فِى كَهْفِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the duration belongs to the first sleep, a later interval, the party's concealment, or an allegorical reckoning.
- 18:25/q02/p1 Their life and sleep from entering the cave until God woke and disclosed them. — Al-Ṭabarī ties this to awakening and discovery in 18:19, 18:21; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī connect the detailed count to the earlier sealed hearing in 18:11. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the first interval before awakening and notes Christian historians claimed 240 years. Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Ṭabarī distinguishing this from the unknown period after discovery; Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise distinguishes the two uses of 'remained'. Mujāhid, as transmitted by al-Suyūṭī, calls 300 plus nine the count of their stay. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد، وعبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير، والضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبيد بن عمير ومجاهد والضحاك وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(others); TABRISI; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 18:25/q02/p2 The count runs from entry until their final deaths. — Al-Māwardī reports this as a form of direct divine account, distinct from the awakening endpoint. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:25/q02/p3 The known sleep is followed by another, unknown period after discovery or death. — Al-Thaʿlabī says 18:26 concerns the span from their souls being taken to the present. Al-Baghawī contrasts the People of the Book's count up to their own era with what only God knows after death. Mujāhid says the later period reached the Qurʾān's revelation; al-Ḍaḥḥāk says until death. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports a proposed reading of 309 years from final death until revelation. [8 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,الضحاك,الطبري,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 18:25/q02/p4 The count denotes a hidden party's period before its victory. — Elmalılı connects 309 years with Christian victory over idolatrous Roman rule. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]
- 18:25/q02/p5 The count can mean 25 years plus nine months. — Al-Qāshānī takes 300 lunar cycles as 25 years and the nine as gestation; he appeals to possible ambiguity in the unit, while al-Ālūsī rejects reducing Quranic years to months as baseless. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل)-]

## 18:25/q03 What cave or place is associated with their stay?  [كَهْفِهِمْ · referent]
turns on: The location reports and archaeological signs do not establish identity conclusively.
- 18:25/q03/p1 Their cave lay between two mountains. — Mujāhid supplies the physical description. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 18:25/q03/p2 Rujayb near Amman best fits the cave, though identification is unproved. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Jordanian excavation, archaeological signs and some Muslim reports, and identifies nearby Amman with Roman Philadelphia; he also admits no proposed cave is demonstrably the Qurʾānic cave. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 18:25/q03/p3 Other proposed caves lie at Qāsiyūn, Petra, Scandinavia, or Nakhchivan. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the Scandinavian and Caucasus proposals clash with the Roman setting and reach of Roman power. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 18:25/q03/p4 A Roman cave was shown as the sleepers' site, but its identity is uncertain. — Al-Khwārizmī reports al-Wāthiq sent him to inspect it; he saw hair on displayed bodies, suspected contrivance and possible preservation with aloe or other drying medicines. Al-Qaffāl says Roman identification proves nothing, and al-Rāzī says era and location require unavailable revelation. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال,محمد بن موسى الخوارزمي)]
- 18:25/q03/p5 The cave's exact location cannot be established. — Al-Rāzī says reason cannot determine it without a revealed text; al-Qaffāl rejects proof from Roman identification. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says none of the proposed sites has decisive evidence. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); TABATABAI]

## 18:25/q04 When did the sleepers live?  [وَلَبِثُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: The reports disagree on dates and the available revelation does not specify them.
- 18:25/q04/p1 They entered after Jesus, with some disciples still alive. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī infer an early post-Jesus date; al-Qaffāl transmits Ibn Isḥāq's post-Jesus view. Most historians place the episode between Jesus and Muḥammad, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares Christian dates under Decius ending in 425, 437, or 439 CE with shorter Christian estimates. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال عن محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI(أهل الرواية والتاريخ)]
- 18:25/q04/p2 They entered before Jesus and awoke between Jesus and Muhammad. — Al-Rāzī reports this chronology, in which Jesus related their story. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 18:25/q04/p3 They lived before Moses. — Al-Rāzī reports a claim that Moses mentioned them in the Torah, explaining Jewish interest. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 18:25/q04/p4 Their precise era cannot be established. — Al-Rāzī says reason cannot settle the date, and no revealed text is available; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī observes divergent dates and rulers in transmitted accounts. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 18:25/q05 What was the sleepers' physical state during and after the interval?  [وَلَبِثُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Accounts differ about what happened after the awakening.
- 18:25/q05/p1 They remained alive and asleep throughout the stated cave interval. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī explicitly say alive asleep; al-Nasafī ties it to sealed hearing; Muqātil says colors, hair and clothes were unchanged. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes divine care and awakening after 309 years. [6 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:25/q05/p2 They never died and will live until the Resurrection. — Al-Rāzī reports this claim from some people. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(قوم)]
- 18:25/q05/p3 Reports differ on whether they died or slept again after discovery. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records the divergence; al-Thaʿlabī and the Study Quran explain God's knowledge as concerning the time after death. [3 notes: TABATABAI; THALABI; STUDYQURAN(others)]

## 18:25/q06 How is the phrase three hundred years read?  [ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍۢ سِنِينَ · readings]
turns on: Whether miʾa is nunated, annexed to sinīn, or replaced by a singular or nominative variant; grammarians dispute the plural after a hundred.
- 18:25/q06/p1 The majority read miʾa with tanwīn, then sinīn separately. — Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim and Ibn ʿĀmir; al-Ṭabarī names Medinans, Basrans and some Kufans and prefers it because hundreds usually take singular specifications. Al-Jishumī prefers its Arabic. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Naḥḥās criticize the annexed alternative; ʿUthmān is reported to recite sinīn with nunation. Al-Nashr says all remaining readers pronounce tanwīn, after naming Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Khalaf as the readers without it. - 18:25/q06/p2 Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read without tanwīn, annexing miʾa to genitive sinīn. — Other reported readers include Yaḥyā, Ṭalḥa, al-Aʿmash, al-Ḥasan and Khalaf; al-Nasafī also names ʿĀṣim. ## 18:25/q07 How does sinīn function grammatically?  [سِنِينَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical status of plural sinīn after a hundred and whether it clarifies the count, gives its time unit, modifies it, or belongs to an annexed construction.
- 18:25/q07/p1 It is an apposition or clarifying substitute for the numbered duration. — Al-Akhfash allows substitution for three or hundred with case varying accordingly; Abū Isḥāq calls it appositional emphasis, Abū ʿAlī a substitute for 300, and al-Zamakhsharī explanatory apposition. Al-Wāḥidī lists five parses and prefers an adjectival one; al-Bursawī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject specification because plural would falsely multiply the count. Al-Ālūsī notes substitution could imply the number was not intended; Abū Ḥayyān objects that Basran grammar disallows some appositional analyses. [23 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي الفارسي); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 18:25/q07/p2 It is an accusative duration governed by labithū, with the number placed before it. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Naḥḥās also express the underlying order as 'they remained years, three hundred'; Ibn Khalawayh makes sinīn governed by the verb. Al-Wāḥidī prefers this analysis and no pause after 300. [8 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الكسائي والفراء وأبو عبيدة); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء وأبو عبيدة والكسائي والزجاج); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء والكسائي وأبو عبيدة); WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 18:25/q07/p3 It is an accusative specification or explanation of the number. — Al-Farrāʾ cites the verse about forty-two black milch camels; al-Ṭabrisī compares 'ten measures of oil.' Al-Ālūsī reports the objection that plural specification would suggest 900 years, but answers from collective usage. Al-Samīn rejects specification except in poetry, and al-Bursawī calculates 600 or 900 under alternative plural minima. [12 notes: FARRA; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي,الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER; SAMIN-DURR-; BURSEVI-]
- 18:25/q07/p4 It modifies miʾa as a genitive adjective describing the whole counted group. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq use ʿAntara's poetic plural adjective for forty-two milch camels; al-Wāḥidī prefers this to al-Farrāʾ's explanation. Abū ʿAlī treats the milch-camel term as collective, enabling the adjective to describe the full group. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي,الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 18:25/q07/p5 In the annexed reading it is genitive as the complement of miʾa, with plural for singular. — Al-Naḥḥās says miʾa has a plural sense; al-Farrāʾ says some Arabs use sinīn for one year. Abū ʿAlī cites poetry with five hundred bows; al-Rāzī cites 18:103, al-Nasafī 18:104. Al-Ālūsī notes its rarity and argues the reading itself establishes usage; al-Wāḥidī reports al-Naẓm's objection. ## 18:25/q08 Why is the unit 'years' stated after three hundred?  [سِنِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the explicit plural primarily identifies the unit or adds emphasis.
- 18:25/q08/p1 It removes uncertainty over whether the three hundred were years, months, days, weeks or hours. — Ibn Qutayba contrasts bare 300 with measured dirhams. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil say the first revelation gave 300, listeners asked its unit, then Gabriel or revelation supplied 'years and nine more'; al-Wāḥidī notes Ibn Mujāhid permitted a pause after 300 because of the staged descent, but prefers no pause grammatically. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(الضحاك); TAB-FULL(الضحاك بن مزاحم); THALABI(الضحاك ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس رواية عطاء,ابن مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك بن مزاحم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:25/q08/p2 The plural can magnify the long duration. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests the plural wording emphasizes the length while remaining appositional. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:25/q09 What does 'nine' count?  [تِسْعًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the established unit carries into the following number, and whose act of adding is described.
- 18:25/q09/p1 Nine further years. — Ibn ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr explicitly supplies years; al-Zajjāj and al-Qushayrī rule out nights or hours because the prior classifier carries forward, as in 100 dirhams and five. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn and others identify an omitted noun; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī reject ambiguity after the specified count. Abū al-ʿAbbās uses 2:234 and Arab counting of day-night periods by nights; he says the feminine form excludes months. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير); ZAJJAJ(أبو العباس محمد بن يزيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي,القشيري); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI]
- 18:25/q09/p2 Its unit is left uncertain or might be months, days or hours. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk is reported to say people did not know the nine's unit, and al-Qāshānī takes it as gestation months; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī argue that the previous years supply the unit. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 18:25/q09/p3 On the quoted-speech reading, the nine is an additional claim by some disputants. — Al-Khafājī assigns 'they added' to the People of the Book, though al-Ālūsī rejects it as weak. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)-]

## 18:25/q10 Why are nine added to the three hundred?  [وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer a calendar conversion, an actual extension of sleep, a return to sleep, emphatic wording, or an allegorical monthly unit.
- 18:25/q10/p1 The extra nine convert 300 solar years into approximately 309 lunar years. — Al-Naqqāsh gives about one lunar year per 33⅓ solar years; Ibn Kathīr and al-Bursawī use roughly three per century. Al-Biqāʿī says 300 follows Jewish reckoning and nine the Arab reckoning. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the two-calendar precision a scientific aspect of inimitability; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī answers arithmetic objections by noting an error of under three months, while al-Bursawī cites al-Kāshifī's 309 years, two months, nine days. Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī raise astronomical objections; al-Khafājī says a fraction under half a year is rounded. [20 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النقاش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي,بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(السهيلي); TABATABAI(التفسير الكبير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bir tefsir); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 18:25/q10/p2 Their sleep genuinely continued nine more years after 300. — Al-Rāzī tentatively suggests circumstances prolonged it; al-Ṭībī says they nearly woke at 300 but something kept them asleep. Al-Ālūsī objects that this would require an unexpressed increase in their sleep. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:25/q10/p3 They woke briefly and then slept again, adding nine years. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this to Qatāda and considers it closer than the solar-lunar account. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 18:25/q10/p4 The separate nine emphatically confirms and heightens the long duration. — Al-Ālūsī compares the confirming exception in Noah's thousand years at 29:14. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the earlier passage minimizes the period while this one stresses 'not merely 300 but nine more'. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI]
- 18:25/q10/p5 Nine signifies gestation months after 300 monthly cycles. — Al-Qāshānī says the 300 lunar cycles yield 25 years before waking; al-Ālūsī rejects this allegorical reduction as baseless and contrary to the possible outward duration. [1 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل)-]

## 18:25/q11 How is tisʿan pronounced?  [تِسْعًۭا · readings]
turns on: The recorded vowel of the first letter differs.
- 18:25/q11/p1 The common form has kasra on the tāʾ. — Al-Qurṭubī contrasts it with Abū ʿAmr's reported fatḥa; the latter is described as dialectal. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو)]
- 18:25/q11/p2 Al-Ḥasan and, in one report, Abū ʿAmr open the tāʾ. — The sources compare the pronunciation to ʿashr; al-Ālūsī names the al-Luʾluʾī transmission and calls it dialectal. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وأبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وأبو عمرو)]

## 18:25/q12 How does azdādū govern 'nine'?  [وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic object is agreed in the main parse; the alternative concerns the pronoun's referent.
- 18:25/q12/p1 'Nine' is its direct object; the underlying noun for additional stay or years is omitted. — Abū ʿAlī denies an adverbial time parse. Al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say the derived verb takes one object though its base takes two; al-Samīn explains iftiʿāl with tāʾ assimilated to dāl after zāy and cites 18:13, while al-Ālūsī cites al-Rāghib and notes Abū al-Baqāʾ's contrary two-object claim. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:25/q12/p2 The pronoun can refer to People of the Book who added nine to a 300-year claim. — Al-Khafājī proposes this under quoted speech; al-Ālūsī rejects it. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)-]

## 18:25/q13 How does the stated duration connect to the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the clause attaches to quoted guesses, resumes the divine story, or attaches to the victorious party determines its relation to 18:26.
- 18:25/q13/p1 It specifies the previously summarized years of sleep. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect the exact count to God sealing their hearing for years at 18:11; Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the sleepers' discussion at 18:19. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:25/q13/p2 It continues the earlier catalogue of human guesses. — Qatāda attaches it to 18:22, and al-Wāḥidī reports the preceding 'they will say' as a frame. Asad sees idle guesses refuted by 18:22 and 18:26; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes Ibn Masʿūd's explicit 'they said'. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس رواية الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة ومطر الوراق); QASHANI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 18:25/q13/p3 It resumes God's narrative after the digression about their number. — Al-Rāzī points to the intervening command against disputation; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the narrative continuation possible. Iṣlāḥī says the parenthetical discussion in 18:22–24 ends here, returning to the earlier estimates of their number. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:25/q13/p4 It is linked to the group who proposed building a mosque over the sleepers. — Elmalılı reports this connection to the preceding victorious party, then prefers a fresh statement about that party's long hidden period. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]
- 18:25/q13/p5 The next verse reserves the later or disputed interval to God's knowledge. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Thaʿlabī distinguish known sleep from unknown time after discovery or death; the rival view takes 18:26 as refuting the quoted count. Al-Baghawī says God can state the true count and still proclaim that He knows best. [11 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,الضحاك,الطبري,مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(others)]

## 18:25/q14 What does the long stay demonstrate?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes offer theological and philosophical explanations of the possibility and significance of extraordinary sleep.
- 18:25/q14/p1 God can preserve sleepers and raise the dead; the duration is a sign of divine power. — Al-Ṭabrisī says making the count only a human claim weakens the passage's proof. Al-Rāzī argues from God's power over possibilities and knowledge of particulars; if a day's sleep is possible, so is 300 years. He compares the bodily night journey and Jesus' fatherless birth in adjacent sūras and cites Avicenna's report of an Aristotelian parallel; al-Ālūsī likewise cites the parallel. [8 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(أبو علي بن سينا,أبو علي بن سينا عن أرسطاطاليس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن سينا)]
- 18:25/q14/p2 Rare celestial conditions could also permit extraordinary preservation. — Al-Rāzī reports philosophers allowing unusual heavenly configurations to affect sublunary matter. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفلاسفة)]
- 18:25/q14/p3 The sleep represents an intermediate state between life and death. — The Study Quran analogizes the centuries-long sleep to the interval between individual death and universal resurrection. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 18:25/q15 What report describes attempts to inspect the sleepers?  [كَهْفِهِمْ · reports]
turns on: The two reports concern different visits and different kinds of evidence.
- 18:25/q15/p1 Muʿāwiya sought to uncover them; a divinely sent wind burned the men sent in. — Al-Rāzī transmits that Muʿāwiya passed the cave on a Roman campaign; Ibn ʿAbbās warned that God had forbidden inspection to someone better than Muʿāwiya, but he sent men anyway. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,معاوية)]
- 18:25/q15/p2 Al-Wāthiq sent al-Khwārizmī to a purported Roman cave; the bodies appeared preserved by human means. — Al-Khwārizmī saw chest hair, suspected a staged display and suggested drying medicines such as aloe; al-Qaffāl says neither the site nor Roman identification is certain. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال,محمد بن موسى الخوارزمي)]

## 18:25/q16 How reliable are detailed reports about the cave story?  [* · reports]
turns on: Accounts vary widely and no decisive text or physical identification resolves several historical particulars.
- 18:25/q16/p1 Many transmitted details conflict; the shared core is monotheists fleeing a coercive polytheist king and sleeping in a cave. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists reports from al-Qummī, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Ibn Isḥāq, Wahb and al-Nuʿmān; they differ on death, dates, rulers, lineage, looks, names and al-Raqīm. He traces divergence to widespread Christian stories and images, Quraysh's exposure, and Muslim collection of outside traditions through converts. Christian accounts diverge from Muslim reports on number, dog and duration; one Christian chronology names Decius and a fifth-century awakening. [8 notes: TABATABAI(أهل الرواية والتاريخ)]
- 18:25/q16/p2 Reason and purported site identifications cannot settle the era or location. — Al-Rāzī requires an unavailable revealed text; al-Qaffāl says Roman site identification is no proof, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds no decisive site evidence. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); TABATABAI]

## 18:25/q17 Why does the Quran omit many historical details of the sleepers?  [* · interpretation]
- 18:25/q17/p1 It selects what serves guidance rather than giving a complete history. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says names, family, occupations, status, dates, rulers, cities, persecutors and even the dog's identity do not bear on guidance; later historians filled the omissions with divergent reports. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:25/q18 Is an inward reading of the duration offered?  [* · inward]
turns on: The allegories assign nonliteral measures to the years, which al-Ālūsī contests.
- 18:25/q18/p1 The numbers can allegorize a 25-year cycle and nine months of gestation. — Al-Qāshānī reads 300 lunar cycles as 25 years and nine as pregnancy, arguing that the verse leaves the count indefinite; al-Ālūsī condemns this reduction of years to months. [3 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل)-]
- 18:25/q18/p2 Spiritual training may take years directly but only days with a guide. — Al-Ālūsī reports the allegory and replies that God can guide without intermediaries in hours. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 18:25/q19 What does sinīn mean?  [سِنِينَ · meaning]
- 18:25/q19/p1 It means ordinary years. — Al-Dāmghānī glosses the phrase as three hundred years and mentions 30:4. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
