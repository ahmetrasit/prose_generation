# 12:47: 27 questions

## 12:47/q01 What do the seven years of sowing signify in the king's dream?  [تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the dream's images primarily forecast agricultural conditions or prescribe the work and storage needed for famine.
- 12:47/q01/p1 Seven fertile years, followed by seven years of drought or famine. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran identify fat cows and green ears with fertile years, lean cows and dry ears with famine. Ibn Kathīr adds rain and explains that cows signify years because they plow fields. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates fatness to fertility, leanness to drought, and ears to provisions; he takes each green ear as one year's fresh food. Al-Qummī's report adds rain after the famine. Al-Biqāʿī connects this interpretation to the two dreams in 12:36 and 12:43. [16 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); TABATABAI-]
- 12:47/q01/p2 The dream depicts the practical measures needed to survive famine: repeated planting, storing grain in ears, and rationing. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues that fat cows picture plentiful harvests from sustained planting, lean cows shortages that consume saved food, green ears stores, and dry ears unthreshed preservation; the food exception permits necessary consumption rather than interpreting a dream symbol. He expressly rejects reducing the dream to a forecast of fertile and barren years. [5 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 12:47/q02 Is تزرعون a prediction or an instruction to sow?  [تَزْرَعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the declarative predicts ordinary cultivation or casts cultivation as an emphatic command; the force of the following فذروه is disputed.
- 12:47/q02/p1 It is a command expressed as a declarative statement. — Al-Wāḥidī's Ṣāḥib al-Naẓm, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn's report of al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ālūsī's reported view, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī cite the following explicit command فذروه; al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī say report form makes compliance emphatic or certain. Al-Rāzī adduces 2:228 and 2:233; al-Kashshāf and Ṭabāṭabāʾī adduce 61:11. Al-Wāḥidī compares prayer in present form at 23:118. Al-Ālūsī reports a grammatical objection to coordinating an imperative harvest clause with a declarative forecast and replies that both clauses may be read as guidance. Al-Burhān also compares the command in declarative form to the nursing and widow-waiting clauses. [20 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURHAN+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 12:47/q02/p2 It predicts their customary seven years of farming; the storage instruction then gives new advice. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers the apparent prediction, mentioning irrigation, and denies that the later imperative proves an earlier command. Al-Samīn says sowing is already habitual but storing in ears needs instruction; he reports al-Shaykh's same objection. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the familiar farming forecast prepares for the counsel. Al-Kashf, preferred by al-Ālūsī, reads the storage clause as urgent advice interrupting a forecast; al-Ālūsī also reports answers to the coordination objection: independent advice, response to an implied condition, or an imperative understood as report. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 12:47/q03 How do زرع and حرث differ?  [تَزْرَعُونَ · meaning]
- 12:47/q03/p1 حرث is sowing and preparing soil; زرع covers tending and growing a crop and can also include sowing. — Bursevī says زرع is broader, while God causes growth, citing 56:64. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 12:47/q04 Why can Joseph announce the seven years without saying 'if God wills'?  [تَزْرَعُونَ سَبْعَ سِنِينَ · theology]
turns on: Whether his speech is divinely certain, implicitly qualified, or an instruction.
- 12:47/q04/p1 He knew it by revelation. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives this as one answer; al-Wāḥidī reports direct revelation as a possible source for Joseph's interpretation. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 12:47/q04/p2 The interpretation God taught him was certainly true, leaving no doubt. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives certainty from God-taught interpretation as a second answer; al-Wāḥidī records the knowledge-versus-revelation question. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 12:47/q04/p3 He intended an unspoken exception. — Ibn al-Anbārī, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, compares Joseph's brothers' promise to bring their brother. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)]
- 12:47/q04/p4 His wording is a command rather than an uncertain forecast. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the imperative force of 'cultivate for seven years' as a fourth answer. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 12:47/q05 What does دأبا mean here?  [دَأَبًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word emphasizes habitual continuity, labor, or succession of the years; many notes combine these senses.
- 12:47/q05/p1 Continuous cultivation according to habitual practice. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr combine continuity with customary action. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya cite Imruʾ al-Qays's verse about habitual conduct; al-Qurṭubī cites ka-daʾbika. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects usage to 3:11; al-Rāghib, reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, starts from continued travel and cites 14:33 and 3:11. Al-Samīn points to his treatment of 3:11. Ibn ʿAbbās and others stress successive years. [27 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني واللغة,ابن عباس,الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(أهل اللغة); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); SAMIN-DURR]
- 12:47/q05/p2 Persistent, industrious effort at sowing. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī give diligent work alongside customary practice. Ibn Khalawayh cites al-Kumayt's line about persistent travel; al-Zajjāj links the word with perseverance. Al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report effort as an alternative nuance; Bursevī joins diligence to continuous farming. [20 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 12:47/q05/p3 Seven consecutive years of planting. — Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly interprets it as consecutive years; al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī transmit this application, with al-Ṭabrisī adding the people's usual agricultural practice. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني واللغة,ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 12:47/q06 How is دأبا read?  [دَأَبًۭا · readings]
turns on: Which transmitted vocalization or recitation context is followed, and whether fatḥa is explained by dialect or guttural easing.
- 12:47/q06/p1 With a quiescent hamza. — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī and Ibn al-Jawzī list Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim; al-Fārisī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Rāzī call it commoner. Ibn Khalawayh prefers it by analogy with the reading about Pharaoh's people at 8:54. He distinguishes a verbal noun with sukūn from a noun with fatḥa; other sources say the two are dialectal or both verbal nouns. Qiraat-ER says the vowel change leaves meaning intact. Ibn al-Jazarī reports that readers other than Ḥafṣ make the hamza quiescent. [26 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي,حفص عن عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,الزجاج,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم,الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حفص); SAMIN-DURR(حفص); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(حفص); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+]
- 12:47/q06/p2 With fatḥa on the hamza. — Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim is repeatedly named; Ibn Mujāhid also reports Mūsā al-Zābī transmitting fatḥa, and al-Fārisī a fatḥa transmission from ʿĀṣim. Al-Qurṭubī reports Abū Ḥātim's attribution to Yaʿqūb and derivation from daʾiba, alongside al-Naḥḥās's objection that linguists know only daʾb. Al-Farrāʾ and reported Kufans permit vowel opening with a guttural, while al-Zajjāj reports Basrans limiting this to attested forms. Al-Fārisī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Rāzī call it a dialect form like shamʿ and nahr variants; Ibn Khalawayh suggests easing a guttural environment. Qiraat-ER records full vocalization and tanwīn. Ibn al-Jazarī reports Ḥafṣ's fatḥa on the hamza. [24 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,الزجاج,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,حفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم,الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حفص); SAMIN-DURR(حفص); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(حفص)]
- 12:47/q06/p3 Abū ʿAmr drops or eases the hamza in connected recitation. — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī give this connected-flow detail; Qiraat-ER calls the hamza-less variant phonological simplification. [5 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); QIRAAT-ER]
- 12:47/q06/p4 At a pause it is pronounced dābā, resuming as daʾban. — Qiraat-ER specifies the pausal and resumed forms. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 12:47/q07 How is دأبا governed grammatically?  [دَأَبًۭا · grammar]
turns on: What accusative construction is supplied or understood, and whether it describes the work, cultivators, or years.
- 12:47/q07/p1 It is an accusative verbal noun governed by an omitted verb meaning 'work persistently'. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, Sībawayh and Abū Ḥayyān supply تدأبون, prompted by تزرعون; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī allow an understood verb. Al-Qurṭubī calls the usage figurative because sowing entails habitual perseverance. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:47/q07/p2 It is a verbal noun attached to تزرعون through the sense of diligent work. — Al-Mubarrad, reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān, makes the farming verb govern it by meaning. Al-Samīn objects that this is not a specific manner like sitting القرفصاء; al-Ālūsī likewise rejects al-Mubarrad's analogy. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(المبرد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو العباس)-; ALUSI-FULL(المبرد)-]
- 12:47/q07/p3 It is a circumstantial or adverbial expression describing the cultivators' persistent or customary work. — Other grammarians in al-Wāḥidī, al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give this parse. Al-Kashshāf allows verbal noun used adverbially or as ḥāl. Al-Samīn lists an intensive absolute noun, an adjective slot, omitted دائبين, or metaphorical identity as analyses of the ḥāl. Ibn Abī al-Rabī, reported by al-Burhān, says Arabs omit a circumstantial expression when the verb's verbal noun signals it; دأبا is ḥāl with implied تدأبون. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN(ابن أبي الربيع)]
- 12:47/q07/p4 It qualifies the seven years as continuous. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this alternative adjective analysis of the seven years. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 12:47/q08 What can ما in فما حصدتم be?  [فَمَا حَصَدتُّمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ما sets a condition or refers to the harvested grain.
- 12:47/q08/p1 A conditional particle: whatever you harvest, leave it. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this parse. Al-Suyūṭī counts this clause among thirteen exceptions to the generally negative force of ما before إلا, consistent with a nonnegative conditional ما. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN]
- 12:47/q08/p2 A relative pronoun: what you have harvested, leave it. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this alternative. Al-Burhān lists the harvest clause among relative ما constructions, alongside what was prepared, what fathers married, and what people worship besides God. Al-Suyūṭī counts this nonnegative ما before إلا among thirteen exceptions to the generally negative pattern. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; BURHAN+]

## 12:47/q09 Why leave the harvest في سنبله?  [فَذَرُوهُ فِى سُنۢبُلِهِۦٓ · interpretation]
- 12:47/q09/p1 Storing unthreshed grain in its ear preserves it longer, especially from insects and spoilage. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās name weevils or pests; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and many later commentators give preservation as the purpose. Ibn Zayd says grain still in its ear is not eaten. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say Egyptian wheat otherwise will not last two years and advise using older stores first. Al-Ṭabrisī reports that insects avoid grain in its ear. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī suggest the dream's green ears prompted the method; Iṣlāḥī specifies termites. The report of al-Ṣādiq prescribes seven years in the ears. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,عبد الرحمن بن زيد,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة وابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 12:47/q10 What is the force and relation of فذروه to the sowing clause?  [فَذَرُوهُ · grammar]
turns on: How an imperative harvest clause is joined to an apparently declarative sowing forecast.
- 12:47/q10/p1 It is an explicit instruction to store the grain; the preceding report may also function as an instruction. — Al-Māwardī contrasts the imperative storage clause with the apparent indicative sowing clause, then reports that the first may also command. Al-Qurṭubī agrees. Al-Ālūsī reports that on the command reading both clauses prescribe action; some regard storage as part of dream interpretation and some as advice outside it. Al-Burhān treats the following command to leave the harvest in its ears as confirmation that the sowing statement itself commands. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BURHAN+]
- 12:47/q10/p2 It is new, independent advice after a prediction of ordinary sowing. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Shaykh say the command does not turn the preceding verb into an imperative. Al-Kashf calls the interruption urgent. Al-Ālūsī answers the coordination objection by treating storage as independent advice or as the answer to an implied condition. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)]

## 12:47/q11 What is the lexical peculiarity of فذروه?  [فَذَرُوهُ · meaning]
- 12:47/q11/p1 ذر, like دع, has no past-tense form in use because ترك supplies it. — Al-Ṭabrisī makes this lexical observation. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 12:47/q12 How much of the harvest may be used, and is seed included?  [إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا مِّمَّا تَأْكُلُونَ · interpretation]
- 12:47/q12/p1 Use only the small amount needed for food and save the rest; seed for further planting is understood. — Ibn ʿAbbās says thresh only what is eaten. Al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others stress restraint or gradual use through the prosperous years. Al-Ālūsī says seed need not be separately excepted because seven years of cultivation presuppose retaining it; Kuranyolu explicitly mentions food and seed set aside. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the exception permission for needed food. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 12:47/q13 Is the eater addressed directly in مما تأكلون?  [مِّمَّا تَأْكُلُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is read with tāʾ or yāʾ and whether the third person names people or shifts address.
- 12:47/q13/p1 The standard reading addresses 'you' who eat. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī contrast the second person wording with a reported third person reading. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبد الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL(السلمي)]
- 12:47/q13/p2 Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān or al-Sulamī reads يأكلون, 'they eat'. — Al-Samīn explains 'they' as people or a shift of address; al-Ālūsī says it refers to what people eat. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبد الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL(السلمي)]

## 12:47/q14 How does Joseph's reply combine interpretation and advice?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the agricultural directions extend Joseph's interpretation or are themselves what the dream depicts.
- 12:47/q14/p1 It interprets the dream and adds practical storage guidance; the later abundant year is an unseen report. — Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes interpretation by meaning, counsel, and a future report. Ibn Zayd says Joseph did more than interpret; al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda stress reserves. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the guidance valuable to Egypt's king and views the dream as divine kindness, perhaps preparing the king for faith and reform. Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran likewise describe a drought strategy. Al-Ālūsī reports that even on an imperative reading some separate the storage advice from interpretation. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 12:47/q14/p2 The dream itself reveals the practical planting, storage, and rationing policy. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the cows and ears as depicting required agricultural measures, while allowing the small eating exception as permission rather than dream interpretation. Al-Ālūsī reports that some, on the imperative reading, count the storage instruction within the interpretation. [6 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI+]

## 12:47/q15 How was the further abundant year known?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the surplus forecast derives from direct revelation or already taught interpretation.
- 12:47/q15/p1 God disclosed it by revelation or gave Joseph knowledge of that year. — Al-Wāḥidī cites the unasked-for year in 12:49 as evidence of revelation. Al-Kashshāf calls it a blessed eighth year known through revelation; Qatāda says God gave Joseph knowledge of it, as transmitted by al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī. Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes the unseen report from the interpretation. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 12:47/q15/p2 It followed from the dream interpretation God had taught Joseph. — Al-Wāḥidī records the alternative that his knowledge was taught by God, rather than direct revelation; Ibn al-Jawzī says that interpretation was certainly true. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 12:47/q16 What related variant wording is reported in the provisioning account?  [* · readings]
- 12:47/q16/p1 A reading has ما قربتم, explained as 'what you bring near for them'. — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes ما قربتم to Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports al-Ṣādiq's wording and a gloss about bringing the harvest near in revelation's ordering. The notes do not place the wording in this verse. [2 notes: TABRISI(جعفر بن محمد); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 12:47/q17 What report gives Joseph's age and years away from home?  [* · reports]
- 12:47/q17/p1 He left at seventeen, spent seven years in the well and seven in prison, and stored food for seven years. — Al-Durr transmits Qatāda's report; its transmitters think Joseph met his father at that time. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 12:47/q18 What report illustrates Joseph's patience when summoned?  [* · reports]

## 12:47/q19 What report marks the start of the hard years?  [* · reports]
- 12:47/q19/p1 A man who usually left half of a two-person serving uneaten ate it all, and Joseph recognized the first hard year. — Al-Durr transmits this from Zayd ibn Aslam. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]

## 12:47/q20 How does the reported famine economy unfold?  [* · reports]
- 12:47/q20/p1 Joseph stockpiles harvests, then sells food for successive classes of assets and finally restores Egyptians' freedom and possessions. — The report from al-Riḍā lists money, valuables, livestock, servants, property, land and people's freedom before Joseph frees them and restores possessions and kingship. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he omits many similar reports because most do not directly interpret these verses. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام)]

## 12:47/q21 What does the al-Qummī report recount about the dream?  [* · reports]
- 12:47/q21/p1 The cupbearer recalled the king's cows and ears dream after a time; Joseph interpreted plenty, famine, then rain. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits al-Qummī's report of seven fat cows eaten by seven lean cows and seven green ears with dry ones. [1 notes: TABATABAI(تفسير القمي)]

## 12:47/q22 What legal principle does the storage policy illustrate?  [* · law]
- 12:47/q22/p1 Public benefit preserves religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property; preventing their loss is also benefit. — Al-Qurṭubī derives these five interests from the verse, treating their preservation as benefit and their loss as harm. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 12:47/q23 Why do revealed laws address worldly interests?  [* · theology]
- 12:47/q23/p1 Worldly guidance enables knowledge and worship of God and happiness in the afterlife; it is divine grace. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes to Sunni authorities the qualification that God grants this care by grace, not by an obligation owed by God. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 12:47/q24 What larger lesson does the Joseph story illustrate?  [* · links]
- 12:47/q24/p1 God directs human affairs in ways people cannot foresee; disliked events may prove good and liked events bad. — Asad contrasts the Qur'anic lesson with Genesis' account of Joseph's fortunes and invokes 2:216. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 12:47/q25 To whom did the messenger hope to return?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether 'the people' includes the king's community or is an honorific for the king alone.
- 12:47/q25/p1 The king and his people. — Al-Māwardī reports this as the sense of 'the people' in the messenger's earlier speech. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 12:47/q25/p2 The king alone, honorifically called 'people'. — Al-Māwardī reports this respectful alternative. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 12:47/q26 Why did the messenger say 'perhaps they will know'?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:47/q26/p1 He hoped they would understand and accept the interpretation, while fearing rejection or a false dream. — Al-Māwardī says the concern is about their response and confirmation by events, not doubt in Joseph. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 12:47/q27 What collateral material appears in the later notes supplied for this verse?  [* · reports]
- 12:47/q27/p1 The supplied notes also contain lexical senses, Quran-recitation reports, and accounts of other prophets that do not interpret the wording of 12:47. — Al-Dāmghānī lists senses of ʿadhāb: killing, punishment in the grave or afterlife, seven years of hunger, and clipping feathers and wings. Ibn al-Jazarī discusses Nahshal's weak transmission and reports sayings on Quran bearers, night vigil, and learning or teaching the Quran, together with al-Sulamī's decades of teaching and Ibn Masʿūd's preference for recitation over more fasting. Al-Suyūṭī transmits differing accounts of Job's time, age, and trial; Dhu al-Kifl's identity and vows; Jonah's parentage, era, and duration in the fish; Ilyas's lineage, name, and longevity; al-Yasaʿ's identity and name; Zakariyya's lineage, age, and name; Yaḥyā's name and life; and Jesus's birth, appearance, descent, and name. These accounts concern other subjects and offer no interpretation of this verse.
