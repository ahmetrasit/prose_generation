# 96:12/q01 Who is envisaged as commanding piety in this alternative? [أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the implied subject of 'was guided' and 'commanded' is the praying servant or the forbider; if the forbider, whether his supposed piety is his mistaken claim or a better course he failed to take.
- 96:12/q01/p1 The praying servant, Muhammad, is guided and commands piety; the description can also embrace any worshipper who calls others to right conduct.
  reasons: Muqātil identifies Muhammad and understands his command as sincerity; al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda identify his call as warning Abū Jahl of God and punishment. Al-Māwardī adds obedience to his Lord. Al-Farrāʾ contrasts the servant's guidance and command with the prohibiter's denial; Ibn ʿĀshūr says context restores the servant as the hidden subject despite nearby pronouns referring to the prohibiter, citing a verse of ʿAbbās ibn Mirdās for contextual shifts of pronouns. Jishumī allows either the Prophet specifically or every worshipper who calls to right. Ibn Kathīr distinguishes the servant's guided conduct from his spoken exhortation. Qatāda's report identifies Muhammad as guided and Abū Jahl as the denier. The question makes obstructing such a servant especially outrageous, according to Quṭb, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī.

- 96:12/q01/p2 The prohibiter is imagined as calling to what he himself considers piety, namely idol worship, although he actually denies the truth.
  reasons: Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī and the majority as reported by al-Ālūsī make this an ironic concession to the prohibiter's claimed righteousness. Al-Qāshānī calls it the arrogant denier's own misguided claim and invitation to associationism. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's contrast between this claim and the Muslim judgment that he turns from true religion. Al-Ālūsī says the next condition exposes his actual denial.

- 96:12/q01/p3 The prohibiter is imagined instead choosing guidance and commanding piety; this better course exposes the good he forfeits by opposing worship.

# 96:12/q02 To whom is the repeated challenge addressed? [* · referent]
turns on: Who receives the second question, and which person the hidden subjects of 'was' and 'commanded' denote.
- 96:12/q02/p1 It addresses the prohibiter, asking whether he would still oppose a servant whose prayer guides and whose call commands piety.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī compare God to a judge or master who turns between opposing parties. Al-Qurṭubī says Abū Jahl is directly confronted with the ruin entailed by opposing Muhammad. Abū Ḥayyān reports this as another view; al-Ālūsī's split-address version then returns to the Prophet for the question about God's sight. Elmalılı says this address follows if the hidden subjects are the servant.
  holders (12 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ALUSI(بعضهم); ELMALILI
  notes: RAZI:96:12/r3 RAZI-FULL:v32p21#3/r3 QURTUBI:96:11/r1 QURTUBI:96:12/r1 QURTUBI-FULL:96:11-12/r1 BAYDAWI-FULL:96:9-14/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r6 ALUSI:96:12/r35 ELMALILI:96:9-19#2/r11
- 96:12/q02/p2 It continues to address the Prophet, asking him to consider the prohibiter's claimed piety or the better course he could have chosen.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī says keeping the first and third challenges addressed to the Prophet preserves rhetorical order. Elmalılı says this follows if the hidden subjects are the prohibiter. Al-Ālūsī reports the Imām's reading of the whole address as directed to the Prophet.

- 96:12/q02/p3 The address may be to the Prophet alone or generally to him and his community.
  reasons: Al-Māwardī presents both scopes of address while keeping the inquiry about the Prophet's guidance.

# 96:12/q03 What does taqwā mean here? [بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is glossed as general God-fearing conduct, specifically sincere monotheism, a claimed idol-worshipping piety, or its inward and outward effects.
- 96:12/q03/p1 Reverence or consciousness of God that entails fearing Him, avoiding disobedience and acting responsibly.
  reasons: ʿAṭāʾ explicitly joins fear of God to abstention from disobedience. Al-Ṭabarī specifies fear of God and His punishment; al-Wāḥidī calls it reverence. Asad renders it God-consciousness throughout, calls this its earliest occurrence in revelation, and refers to 2:2. Kur'an Yolu adds responsible conduct.
  holders (6 notes): TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR
  notes: TAB:96:12/r1 TAB-FULL:v24p535/r2 WAHIDI-BASIT:v24p175#2/r1 WAHIDI-WAJIZ:96:9-17/r4 ASAD-NOTES:96:9#n6/r1 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:9/r3
- 96:12/q03/p2 Sincerity and God's oneness, expressed by avoiding association of partners with Him.
  reasons: Muqātil supplies sincerity; al-Wāḥidī reports his fuller gloss as sincerity and monotheism, and al-Baghawī likewise pairs sincerity with God's oneness.
  holders (4 notes): MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL
  notes: MUQATIL:96:9-19/r2 WAHIDI-BASIT:v24p175#2/r2 BAGHAWI:96:12/r1 BAGHAWI-FULL:v8p480#2/r2
- 96:12/q03/p3 In the ironic reading, it is the forbider's supposed piety: worship of idols.
  reasons: Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī qualify the description as what the forbider himself believes; al-Ālūsī's majority reading contrasts that supposition with his actual denial.
  holders (5 notes): KASHSHAF; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI(الجمهور)

- 96:12/q03/p4 It is inward illumination through guidance whose outward effects protect the worshipper from divine anger and destruction.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī calls taqwā the religion's pillar and connects inner light, outward devotion and safety from the sovereign Lord's displeasure.
