# 96:12/q07 What rhetorical force does this renewed question have? [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the repetition chiefly expresses astonishment, counterfactual reproach, or a prompt to reflect and judge one's course.
- 96:12/q07/p1 It expresses wonder and rebukes the perversity of forbidding a guided servant who commands piety.
  reasons: Al-Wāḥidī calls the repeated 'have you seen?' an expression of amazement; al-Baghawī contrasts the guided servant and denier. Al-Bayḍāwī says it repeats the first question. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a second, detached hypothetical that renews wonder about the same person's possible states; al-Qushayrī asks whether the worshipper's guidance or piety would not be better.

# 96:12/q08 What happened in the confrontation over prayer connected with this verse? [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in details of Abū Jahl's threat and whether his retreat before the angels is included; al-Baghawī notes uncertainty in the report's attribution of the revelation statement.
- 96:12/q08/p1 Abū Jahl found or threatened Muhammad at prayer, forbade him, and the passage was revealed about his opposition.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās reports the prohibition and Abū Jahl's boast about his council; he says angels would seize Abū Jahl if he summoned it. Al-Suyūṭī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's account extending to the lying, sinful forelock. Qatāda says Abū Jahl threatened to trample Muhammad, identifies his assembly as his clan and glosses zabāniya as police in Arabic. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ accepts that a specific occasion can coexist with general wording.

# 96:12/q09 What general warning or lesson does the verse convey? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasized lesson is the injustice of obstruction, accountability before God, or the duty to assess one's own acts.
- 96:12/q09/p1 Obstructing a rightly guided worshipper who urges piety is unjust and invites divine anger; the warning extends to tyrants beyond Abū Jahl.
  reasons: Jishumī says the mistaken tyrant destroys himself. Iṣlāḥī calls this obstruction satanic; Kur'an Yolu prefers the universal reading, applying it to tyrants in every age who obstruct religion's work through goodness, truth and justice. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says a specific occasion does not restrict the general wording.

# 96:12/q10 How does this question fit the surrounding passage and other verses? [* · links]
turns on: Whether the relation under discussion is the local argumentative sequence, the surah's revelatory arrangement, or a cross-verse link involving taqwā.
- 96:12/q10/p1 The passage proceeds from tyranny born of felt self-sufficiency to lost guidance and piety, then denial and divine warning.
  reasons: ʿIṣām al-Dīn maps 96:9–10 to tyranny, 96:11–12 to lost guidance, and 96:13–14 to deserved punishment and distance from God; al-Ālūsī explicitly distances himself from that elaboration. Al-Rāzī's general reading, reported by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, similarly links human creation, blessings, transgression and the failure to enjoin piety.
