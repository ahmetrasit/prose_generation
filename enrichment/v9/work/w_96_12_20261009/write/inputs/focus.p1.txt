# 96:12/q04 Why is commanding piety mentioned beside guidance and prayer? [أَمَرَ بِٱلتَّقْوَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the command is paired with prayer to show self-reform and reform of others, prayer's exemplary force, the two provocations to Abū Jahl, or the full scope of the prohibition and rebuke.
- 96:12/q04/p1 Guidance or prayer cultivates the person himself, while commanding piety reforms and guides others.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī says the Prophet alternated between self-reform in prayer and reform of others by command. Al-Farrāʾ, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, makes the same distinction; al-Biqāʿī describes an advance from inner illumination to outward help. Al-Bayḍāwī describes worship and invitation as the two ways human states are perfected.

  notes: RAZI:96:12/r5 RAZI-FULL:v32p21#3/r5 BAYDAWI:96:13/r6 BAYDAWI:96:14/r6 ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r11 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r11 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r11 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r11 BIQAI:96:12/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p484/r3
- 96:12/q04/p2 Prayer itself is an active call to guidance and piety, stronger than verbal exhortation.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī says witnessing the Prophet pray softened hearts and moved people toward faith. Al-Bayḍāwī says mentioning prayer suffices for both practice and invitation. Al-Ālūsī says an exemplar's action effectively says 'Do this,' and Ibn ʿĀshūr says obstructing prayer thus obstructs guidance.

- 96:12/q04/p3 Prayer and calling people to God are named because both aspects of Muhammad's conduct troubled Abū Jahl.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī identifies these as the two activities that provoked Abū Jahl; Abū Ḥayyān attributes the same pairing to al-Farrāʾ.

- 96:12/q04/p4 The added command heightens the rebuke: the opponent blocked prayer and perhaps its call to piety, while prayer alone was enough to name in the earlier prohibition.
  reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī invoke brevity and the comprehensive force of prayer; al-Ālūsī says the command may cover the prohibiter's verbal call in the ironic reading. # 96:12/q05 How is the repeated 'Have you seen?' construction parsed? [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether raʾayta here requires two objects, what supplies them, and whether an interrogative may answer a condition without fāʾ.
- 96:12/q05/p1 It is cognitive seeing or 'tell me': the first object is the prohibiter or preceding relative clause, while later objects or pronouns are supplied from context.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān argues the first object is the relative clause and the second an interrogative clause, citing 53:33–35, 19:77–78 and 56:58–59; later instances have omitted objects, whose pronouns can denote prohibiter or servant. He denies syntactic competition among the three verbs because clauses cannot be pronominalized. Al-Bursawī makes the first object the forbidding man. Al-Ālūsī supplies a pronoun or demonstrative in later clauses. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls raʾayta cognitive and links its understood object to the earlier 'forbids' or later reminder of God's sight.
  holders (17 notes): ABUHAYYAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN+
  notes: ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r16 ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r17 ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r18 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r16 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r17 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r18 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r16 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r17 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r18 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r16 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r17 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r18 BURSEVI:v10p365#2/r8 ALUSI-FULL:v30p183#2/r1 IBNASHUR:96:12/r3 IBNASHUR:96:12/r6 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p447/r6
- 96:12/q05/p2 The repeated conditional is the second object of raʾayta; its omitted answer is supplied by the later condition or by 'Did he not know that God sees?'.
  reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī treats the repetitions as grammatically parallel, with the condition as second object. Al-Bursawī says an interrogative or oath clause must complete that object. Al-Zamakhsharī, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, treats the later question about God's sight as the answer and cites two interrogative analogies to allow it without fāʾ. Abū Ḥayyān rejects that omission of fāʾ outside poetic necessity.
  holders (6 notes): BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-
  notes: BAYDAWI-FULL:96:9-14/r3 ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r14 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r14 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r14 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r14 BURSEVI:v10p365#2/r9 | against: ABUHAYYAN:96:11/r19 ABUHAYYAN:96:12/r19 ABUHAYYAN:96:13/r19 ABUHAYYAN:96:14/r19
- 96:12/q05/p3 The interrogative arayta serves as a challenge or stylistic prompt and need not have a second object supplied.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes it from knowledge-based vision, which takes two objects. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Meccan examples at 107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23 and 53:33 to argue that Qurʾānic usage often leaves the second object absent to direct attention to a lesson; she cautions against forcing such patterns into school grammar by conjecture.

- 96:12/q05/p4 The first object is the relative clause, but the second must be an interrogative clause rather than al-Zamakhsharī's proposed object analysis.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān specifically invokes Qurʾānic parallels at 53:33–35, 19:77–78 and 56:58–59 for his preferred grammatical analysis.

# 96:12/q06 What consequence is left implicit after the condition? [* · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier or later clause supplies the unspoken answer, whether it is an exhortation, warning or expression of wonder, and whether the next statement is independent.
- 96:12/q06/p1 Would the forbider still forbid the guided servant and his call to piety?
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr understands the answer from the earlier prohibition or the later reminder in 96:14; the particle in leaves the condition uncertain in keeping with the forbider's standpoint. Al-Ālūsī reports that, on the split-address reading, 'would you forbid him?' can be either the question's object or its conditional answer.
  holders (5 notes): ALUSI(قيل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: ALUSI:96:10/r51 IBNASHUR:96:12/r2 IBNASHUR:96:12/r6 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p447/r2 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p447/r6
- 96:12/q06/p2 Would guidance and commanding piety not be better for the forbider than his present course?
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī explicitly supplies 'would this not be better for him?'; al-Rāzī and the Imām reported by al-Ālūsī expand on the rational, wealthy man's forfeited higher station.
  holders (5 notes): BIQAI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الإمام)
  notes: BIQAI:96:12/r3 RAZI:96:12/r2 RAZI-FULL:v32p21#3/r2 ALUSI:96:12/r38 ALUSI:96:13/r41
- 96:12/q06/p3 The opponent is ruined or liable to God's judgment for opposing a rightly guided worshipper.
  reasons: Al-Qurṭubī says the question to Abū Jahl implies his doom. Jishumī says the mistaken tyrant destroys himself; Iṣlāḥī says ignoring the worshipper's possible guidance invites wrath.

- 96:12/q06/p4 How astonishing that a guided servant is opposed by a man who denies and turns away.
  reasons: Al-Farrāʾ's reading, reported by al-Ālūsī, supplies astonishment as the implied conclusion; al-Baghawī also stresses the contrast.

  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī says the repeated conditions work by substitution and culminate in God's sight. Al-Zamakhsharī makes this the answer supplied by the second condition, but Abū Ḥayyān objects to its lack of fāʾ. Ibn Kathīr adds that God hears and will repay the opponent fully.
