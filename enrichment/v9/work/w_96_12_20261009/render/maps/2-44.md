# 2:44: 31 questions

## 2:44/q01 What does the opening question do?  [أَتَأْمُرُونَ · rhetoric]
- 2:44/q01/p1 It affirms their conduct while rebuking it, often with astonishment and threat. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī call the hamza interrogative in form and a statement of their actual course; al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Itqān add astonishment. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān stress censure. Al-Samīn also gives denial or wonder. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:44/q02 What does the verb 'command' mean and how is it constructed?  [تَأْمُرُونَ · grammar]
- 2:44/q02/p1 It directs someone to act and governs a person and a commanded act. — Jishumī defines a command as a superior's utterance to a subordinate, literal of speech and figurative of action. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn describe two complements, al-nās direct and bi-l-birr prepositional; poetry licenses occasional omission of the preposition. Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes amr 'affair' and notes imperative hamza loss or retention after conjunction. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:44/q03 Who are the 'people' being commanded?  [ٱلنَّاسَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address concerns scholars or Israelites broadly and whether the audience is their followers, Arabs, or anyone besides them.
- 2:44/q03/p1 Muslim kin and others privately advised. — Muqātil, Tustarī, Ibn ʿAbbās accounts and later commentators mention companions, in-laws, milk kin, relatives and allies; this overlap is recorded in the occasion question. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]
- 2:44/q03/p2 Ordinary Jewish followers or common people. — Bursevī identifies lower-status common folk; Ibn ʿĀshūr says this follows if the address is specifically to scholars. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:44/q03/p3 Arab questioners or polytheists. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions Arab questioners; Ibn ʿĀshūr invokes Jewish descriptions of their religion and 2:89 if the addressees are all Israelites. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:44/q03/p4 Anyone other than the person giving the command. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Arabic idiom and 3:173 for this general sense. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:44/q04 What does birr mean as a word?  [ٱلْبِرِّ · meaning]
- 2:44/q04/p1 Expansive goodness, kindness, obedience, piety, or any virtuous deed. — Al-Ṭabarī cites reports of obedience; al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı relate breadth to open land and say it embraces all good. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and Study Quran include truthfulness, obedience, kinship, or piety. Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī discuss kindness and the hirr/birr proverb; al-Akhfash takes it as hissing versus kindness, al-Māzinī as cat versus mouse. Ibn al-Jawzī gives benefit to the recipient and Qur'anic senses of kinship (2:224, 60:8), obedience (5:2, 19:14, 19:32, 58:9, 83:18), and piety (2:177). Al-Ṭabrisī says birr requires intention unlike accidental khayr. Al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn give rare senses heart, fox cub, sheep-driving, and lexical poetry. Al-Rāzī cites 2:189; al-Bayḍāwī and Elmalılı classify devotion to God, kin, and others. Study Quran cites 2:177, 2:189, 3:92 and rewards of abrār at 3:193, 76:5, 82:13. [41 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي، قتادة); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الأخفش,المازني); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن فارس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 2:44/q05 Which good act is meant by birr in this verse?  [ٱلْبِرِّ · interpretation]
turns on: The reports specify different acts under the word's broad sense; some commentators say the cases converge or more than one occasion is possible.
- 2:44/q05/p1 Belief in and following Muhammad, including keeping the covenant recorded in the Torah. — Muqātil says Jews recognized his truth; Ibn ʿAbbās says they told others not to disbelieve the Torah's prophecy yet broke its covenant, or urged entry into Muhammad's religion. Some foretold him to Arabs before his advent, then rejected him. Al-Rāzī and Elmalılı mention fear of losing gifts, payments, taxes or rank. Bursevī distinguishes advice to poor people from delay urged on wealthy people. Al-Biqāʿī calls this the Torah's foundational righteousness. Sources identify Muslim kin, companions or followers as recipients. [43 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(الأحبار); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 2:44/q05/p2 General obedience, piety, good works and avoidance of disobedience. — Qatāda and al-Suddī give this broad account; al-Ṭabarī says the reports agree on what pleases God while the speakers do otherwise. Ibn Jurayj mentions People of Scripture and hypocrites; al-Qurṭubī considers the explanations close. [19 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي، قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج); TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,قتادة); TAB-FULL+]
- 2:44/q05/p3 Charity or almsgiving, which they withheld or even diverted. — Al-Zajjāj relates stinginess to hard hearts, usury and illicit gain. Al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Nasafī mention embezzled donations; al-Qurṭubī calls this close to other explanations. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 2:44/q05/p4 Adherence to the Torah, which they themselves violated by denying its description of Muhammad. — Al-Zajjāj calls this primary; Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū Isḥāq, al-Baghawī and others say that denying the Prophet, changing the Torah or concealing his description violates the very scripture they commend. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ZAJJAJ+]
- 2:44/q05/p5 Specific duties such as prayer, fasting and zakat, which the speakers neglected. — Ibn Jurayj and Ibn ʿAbbās name fasting, prayer and other commands; Abū Ḥayyān also reports prayer and zakat, while Study Quran includes prayer and alms under piety. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جريج,الضحاك); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:44/q05/p6 Inward states or spiritual advance demanded of others while teachers lack corresponding practice. — Al-Sulamī and al-Qushayrī contrast demands for inner realities, haste toward God and scrupulous attention to others with outward neglect and one's own heaps of faults; an anonymous poem contrasts a mote and a log. Al-Qushayrī's camels image asks why they water others but do not drink. [8 notes: SULAMI(قيل); QUSHAYRI(أنشدوا); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض أهل الإشارات); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السلمي,القشيري)]
- 2:44/q05/p7 Just dealing in questions without a bribe or personal stake. — Ibn Zayd says the Jews advised a questioner fairly when no right, bribe or benefit was at issue; Ibn Kathīr transmits the same point. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)]
- 2:44/q05/p8 Truthfulness, which they urged without practicing. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this variant along with other readings to Qatāda, Ibn Jurayj and al-Suddī. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة)]

## 2:44/q06 Who is directly rebuked?  [تَأْمُرُونَ تَنسَوْنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address is restricted to scholars, covers all Israelites, or shifts to believers.
- 2:44/q06/p1 Jewish scholars or rabbis, especially those in Medina. — Jishumī says scholars agree on the Jewish setting. Many reports identify scholars who instructed Muslim kin or ordinary followers; al-Qurṭubī says commentators direct the interrogative to them. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a collective address whose actual rebuke falls on this subgroup. [22 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(الأحبار); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:44/q06/p2 The Children of Israel or People of the Book collectively. — Qatāda and al-Ṭabarī say Israelites; Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr say People of Scripture. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the direct grammar covers all Israelites, either each as a preacher or by collective attribution. [8 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:44/q06/p3 Believers who recite the Qur'an, by a shift in address. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this alongside the Qur'an identification; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī find the shift unlikely, citing the sustained address to the People of the Book; they compare 12:29. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:44/q07 What does 'you forget' mean?  [تَنسَوْنَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it is a literal lapse or a deliberate neglect, given the rebuke.
- 2:44/q07/p1 You leave, neglect, or abandon yourselves and your duty. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Samīn and others invoke 9:67; al-Qurṭubī also cites 6:44 and 2:237. Al-Rāzī argues involuntary lapse would not be blameworthy. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī take the wording as 'as though forgotten.' Abū Ḥayyān says this choice intensifies heedlessness; Bursevī distinguishes lapse after knowledge from general inattention. Al-Qurṭubī notes the verbal noun nisyān, rejecting nisayān; and a report says Adam forgot and descendants therefore forget. [20 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:44/q07/p2 Literal forgetting is loss of what was once present, a God-created lapse. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī give the cognitive definition; al-Ṭabrisī says sahw may concern something never known. Al-Ḥarālī calls it a lapse after gained knowledge, though the verse's blame is usually read as neglect. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:44/q08 How is 'you forget' formed or used grammatically?  [تَنسَوْنَ · grammar]
- 2:44/q08/p1 It is a present-tense verb coordinated with 'you command'; its weak stem loses yāʾ. — Al-Naḥḥās coordinates it with the preceding verb. Al-Samīn derives it from tansiyūna after quiescence and deletion, comparing 2:16. Abū Ḥayyān says nasiyāna normally takes one object but can suspend government like ʿalima, supported by poetry. [5 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:44/q09 Who or what are 'yourselves'?  [أَنفُسَكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether anfus refers to the speakers themselves or their people.
- 2:44/q09/p1 The speakers' own persons, whose salvation and obedience they neglect. — Al-Ṭabarī asks why they do not command themselves as they command others. Al-Kashshāf and others describe leaving their own share of goodness; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains nafs as whole person, body and spirit, citing 5:45 and 2:85, or sometimes inward consciousness (5:116) and perceiving spirit (12:53, 16:111). Al-Qurṭubī cites 39:42, Bilāl and Zayd ibn Aslam for 'soul,' poetry for blood or body, and Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī's legal example about animals without flowing blood. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,الشاعر,بلال,زيد بن أسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:44/q09/p2 Their own people or community. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this alternative sense of al-anfus. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:44/q10 What is the form of anfus?  [أَنفُسَكُمْ · grammar]
- 2:44/q10/p1 It is a broken plural of nafs and a plural of paucity. — Al-Qurṭubī classifies it as such; al-Burhān treats four broken patterns and two sound plurals as inherently few, with other plurals context-dependent. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 2:44/q11 How are 'you command' and 'you forget' related?  [تَأْمُرُونَ وَتَنسَوْنَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the second clause is coordinated or circumstantial and what bears the blame.
- 2:44/q11/p1 The second present verb is coordinated with the first; the neglect itself is central to the rebuke. — Al-Naḥḥās parses coordination. Ibn ʿĀshūr says coordination can make neglect the main censured action. Al-Itqān raises the difficulty that command alone is good and the exact scope of the interrogative is subtle. [3 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN]
- 2:44/q11/p2 The second clause is circumstantial: they command while neglecting themselves. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this analysis, making the blame attach to the combination. ʿArūs al-Afrāḥ says sinning while warning others is graver because words and deeds contradict and knowledge compounds guilt; al-Itqān finds nuance still unresolved. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN(عروس الأفراح)]

## 2:44/q12 What does the present tense suggest?  [تَأْمُرُونَ تَنسَوْنَ · rhetoric]
- 2:44/q12/p1 Their contradiction was habitual or recurrent. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burhān and al-Itqān say the present forms evoke continuous or repeated conduct, even for acts already done. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 2:44/q13 What does 'you recite' mean?  [تَتْلُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether tilāwa is reading, study, or following the Book in conduct.
- 2:44/q13/p1 Reading, reciting, or studying scripture. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss it as study and recitation; al-Wāḥidī, Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn derive recitation from successive following of letters or words, with al-Samīn citing 91:2 for the moon following the sun. Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes gathering letters in reading from following them in recitation. Al-Qurṭubī lists other root senses, and Abū Ḥayyān offers a calf following its mother. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 2:44/q13/p2 Following the Book by emulating it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya offers this alternative, using the root's ordinary meaning of following. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 2:44/q14 What is the form of 'you recite'?  [تَتْلُونَ · grammar]
- 2:44/q14/p1 It derives from tatluwūna; a heavy ḍamma and then a quiescent wāw are dropped. — Al-Samīn gives the derivation and resulting tafʿūna pattern. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:44/q15 What is the function of 'while you recite the Book'?  [وَأَنتُمْ تَتْلُونَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · grammar]
- 2:44/q15/p1 It is a circumstantial nominal clause, in the accusative, dependent on 'you forget'. — Al-Samīn parses subject and predicate with tanṣūna governing the ḥāl. Al-Burhān notes circumstantial wāw can coexist with resumptive coordination. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN]

## 2:44/q16 Which Book do they recite?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the book is the Torah, Torah and Gospel, innate book, or Qur'an; the Qur'an view entails a shift of audience.
- 2:44/q16/p1 The Torah. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās identify it; al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī and later commentators say it contains Muhammad's description or commands good. Ibn al-Jawzī calls this the majority view; al-Qurṭubī says its commands intensify blame. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:44/q16/p2 The Qur'an, implying the addressees are believers. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī reject it as unlikely against the passage's address to the People of the Book, despite a comparison to 12:29. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:44/q16/p3 The Torah and Gospel. — Abū Ḥayyān reports a majority view that both books forbid the shameful trait. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 2:44/q16/p4 The innate book of human nature. — Al-Qāshānī reads the book inwardly as human innate nature commanding Muhammad's religion and divine unity. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 2:44/q17 Why mention their recitation of scripture?  [وَأَنتُمْ تَتْلُونَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · rhetoric]
- 2:44/q17/p1 It heightens their blame because they know the instruction they violate. — Al-Kashshāf compares 'while you know' (2:22); al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and others say Torah describes Muhammad or condemns betrayal, neglected good and contradiction. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it forbids their conduct. Al-Biqāʿī calls the prophecy explicit and the scholars' defiance graver. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that preaching and reading are two reminders to reform. Al-Burhān notes that leaving an object unexpressed can assert reading without restriction; al-Ṭībī argues the neighbouring 'you know' has a particular understood object lest knowledge be affirmed while reason is denied. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الطيبي)]

## 2:44/q18 How is the final interrogative joined and parsed?  [أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the interrogative hamza moves ahead of the conjunction or stays with an omitted clause, and whether reason has an object.
- 2:44/q18/p1 The final interrogative is independent and its object may be omitted or the verb intransitive. — Al-Samīn gives it no syntactic position and permits an unspoken object, either general reason or the ugliness of conduct; al-Ālūsī likewise offers transitive and intransitive readings. Ibn ʿĀshūr favors intransitivity to stress extreme heedlessness. [5 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:44/q18/p2 The conjunction is underlyingly before the hamza, which advances to sentence-initial position. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this to Sībawayh and grammarians; al-Samīn says the hamza is delayed in sense after fāʾ and can precede wāw and thumma as in 2:77 and 10:51; al-Ālūsī calls it the majority analysis. Abū Ḥayyān says al-Zamakhsharī later returned to it. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:44/q18/p3 The hamza stays where written and an omitted clause precedes the conjunction. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report al-Zamakhsharī's alternative; al-Samīn qualifies its rule with 34:9. Al-Ālūsī reports it too. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:44/q19 What does ʿaql mean as a word?  [تَعْقِلُونَ · meaning]
- 2:44/q19/p1 Understanding, perception or knowledge that restrains error and wrong. — Al-Wāḥidī cites the camel hobble, blood money, gazelle, ibex, fortress and stomach medicine; al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī explain restraint. Al-Ṭabrisī calls it discernment opposite ignorance and cites Ibn ʿAbbās's discerning heart, Kitāb al-ʿAyn's usages, and tethering a camel. Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān add textile, pattern, levy and firm sand usages; ʿAlqama's poem and Ibn Fāris distinguish linear ʿaql from round raqm. Al-Qurṭubī says al-Zajjāj links reason to doing obligations. [26 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,صاحب كتاب العين); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن فارس,الزجاج,علقمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:44/q20 What is the nature of human reason?  [تَعْقِلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether reason is knowledge, an innate capacity, a light, a substance, or a created accident.
- 2:44/q20/p1 Reason is knowledge or necessary cognitions that enable moral responsibility and judgment. — Jishumī gives necessary cognitions or knowledge of obligations and good and bad; al-Ṭabrisī and ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā define knowledge restraining evil, adding that stronger restraint means stronger intellect. Al-Ashʿarī and al-Isfarāyīnī infer reason and knowing accompany each other. Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr defines necessary knowledge of obligatory, possible and impossible; Abū al-Maʿālī first chose this in al-Irshād, then a capacity account in al-Burhān. Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes completeness of intellect and knowledge and denies predicating intellect of God because He acquires no knowledge and needs no restraint. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الحسن الأشعري وأبو إسحاق الإسفرايني,القاضي أبو بكر)]
- 2:44/q20/p2 Reason is a capacity, disposition, discriminating faculty, or means of acquiring insight. — Jishumī reports a capacity to infer unseen from witnessed; al-Ṭabrisī reports discernment or human distinctiveness as close definitions. Al-Muḥāsibī gives innate disposition; al-Shāfiʿī and Ibn Mujāhid are reported to call it an instrument, al-Qalānisī a faculty. Al-Ustādh Abū Bakr doubts the 'instrument' attribution, calls it metaphorical for an accident, and describes a means of gaining light and insight rather than a light or form. [6 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الأستاذ أبو بكر,الشافعي وابن مجاهد,القاضي أبو بكر,المحاسبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الأستاذ أبو بكر)-]
- 2:44/q20/p3 Reason is a spiritual light by which the soul apprehends knowledge. — Bursevī and al-Ālūsī describe necessary and theoretical apprehension and restraint; al-Muḥāsibī calls it lights and insights. Al-Ustādh Abū Bakr rejects identifying reason itself as a light. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي وابن مجاهد); BURSEVI(عند البعض); ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأستاذ أبو بكر)-]
- 2:44/q20/p4 Reason is an eternal subtle or simple substance. — Al-Qurṭubī reports philosophers' view of an eternal substance, sometimes in the body emitting a discriminating ray. He counters that substances are alike, so one substance's being reason would make all substances reason; eternity would rival God. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الفلاسفة); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الحق)-]
- 2:44/q20/p5 Reason is a created accident, not a substance that itself perceives. — Al-Qurṭubī says it must exist to distinguish and must be created to avoid rivaling divine eternity; he rejects substance theories and even 'perceiving realities,' since perception belongs to living things, while reason as an accident cannot itself perceive or desire. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الحق)+]

## 2:44/q21 Where is reason located?  [تَعْقِلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether sensation points to the brain or life and the senses point to the heart.
- 2:44/q21/p1 In the brain. — A philosophical group and Bursevī give the seat of sensation as their reason. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(طائفة من الفلاسفة); BURSEVI]
- 2:44/q21/p2 In the heart. — A philosophical group and Bursevī's reported alternative point to the heart as source of life and the senses. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(طائفة من الفلاسفة); BURSEVI(عند البعض)]

## 2:44/q22 Does reason vary in people, and do its judgments vary?  [تَعْقِلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether reason itself varies, while rational judgments remain stable.
- 2:44/q22/p1 Reason itself does not vary among people. — Jishumī reports this view; al-Ṭabrisī says disputes over its essence do not affect judgments such as a hundred exceeding one or a whole exceeding its part. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI]
- 2:44/q22/p2 Reason itself varies, but its judgments do not. — Jishumī reports this counterpart; al-Ṭabrisī allows greater restraint to mark greater intelligence through ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā, while maintaining stable rational judgments. [3 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(علي بن عيسى)]

## 2:44/q23 What does 'will you not reason?' ask them to recognize or do?  [أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it calls for self-restraint, recognizing the conduct's ugliness, accepting Muhammad, or recognizing punishment; the glosses can overlap.
- 2:44/q23/p1 Recognize the ugliness and irrationality of the contradiction, then stop it. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās call it understanding and rebuke of ugly conduct; al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe restraint from ruin. Abū Muslim says a rational person would not behave so; al-Burhān says the ending fits what contravenes reason. Al-Qāshānī reads it as severe rebuke and incitement. [31 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI]
- 2:44/q23/p2 Recognize Muhammad's truth and follow him. — Muqātil says the Torah's clear description should prompt following; al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī put this as the question's point. Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān report this gloss alongside the ugliness reading. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:44/q23/p3 Recognize that God will punish them for this conduct. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān report this alternative specification of the unexpressed object. [2 notes: TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:44/q24 Why is preaching without practicing irrational or harmful?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:44/q24/p1 It contradicts one's concern for salvation and makes counsel less credible and effective. — Al-Rāzī gives three grounds: one guides others but neglects oneself; a sinful scholar emboldens listeners to doubt warnings; inconsistent conduct frustrates counsel. Abū Ḥayyān adds salvation should begin at home; Elmalılı says the teacher refutes his learning, deceives hearers, and alienates them. Al-Ṭabrisī cites a poem condemning the same vice one forbids. Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Kathīr and Bursevī urge the preacher to act first. Abū al-Dardāʾ says true understanding turns greater disapproval on oneself; ʿAlī contrasts the corrupt scholar and ignorant devotee. Numerous poems liken the preacher to a sick doctor or one who sees another's mote but misses his own log. [43 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو الدرداء,ابن جريج); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الشاعر,علي رضي الله عنه,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الأسود الدؤلي,أبو العتاهية,أبو عثمان الحيري,أبو عمرو بن مطر,سلم بن عمرو,منصور الفقيه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الأسود الدؤلي,أبو الدرداء,أبو العتاهية,أبو عثمان الحيري,إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عباس,مسلم بن عمرو,ميمونة السوداء); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:44/q25 May someone who commits a sin still command good or forbid wrong?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether failure to practice removes the separate duty to exhort others.
- 2:44/q25/p1 Yes; both self-practice and exhortation remain duties, and this verse rebukes neglecting oneself. — Al-Rāzī says failing either duty does not cancel the other. Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes forbidding vice from committing it. Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr cite al-Ḥasan, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Mālik: no one does all he says or is faultless, and silence would please Satan. Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr agree. Bursevī quotes a report to command right even while falling short. [22 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,سعيد بن جبير); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير، مالك); BURSEVI(عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الفخر); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 2:44/q25/p2 No; a sinner should not exhort others until reformed. — Al-Rāzī reports this argument from 2:44 and 61:2–3, illustrated by an adulterer correcting the partner's uncovered face; Ibn Kathīr calls it weak. Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī disliked admonitory storytelling, citing this verse, 61:2 and 11:88; Ibn ʿAbbās tells an aspiring preacher to begin with himself. Poems advise reform before counsel, but these need not assert a legal ban. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم)-; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL(الفخر)-]

## 2:44/q26 What punishment or warning is reported for learned preachers who contradict their teaching?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe distinct scenes and vary in transmission strength.
- 2:44/q26/p1 On the Night Journey, preachers' lips were cut with fiery shears. — Anas reports Gabriel identifying preachers who urged good but neglected themselves; al-Baghawī specifies members of Muhammad's community, Ibn Kathīr collects ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd, Ibn Mardawayh, Yazīd ibn Hārūn, Ibn Ḥibbān and Ibn Abī Ḥātim routes, with one version adding tongues. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أنس بن مالك); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]
- 2:44/q26/p2 People of Paradise confront teachers in Hell whose advice they themselves followed. — Muḥammad ibn Wāsiʿ and al-Shaʿbī transmit versions; Ibn Kathīr cites an attribution through al-Walīd ibn ʿUqba. Abū Ḥayyān gives both the confrontation and the reply that the teachers acted differently. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(محمد بن واسع); RAZI-FULL(الشعبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن واسع); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الوليد بن عقبة); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن واسع)]
- 2:44/q26/p3 A preacher's entrails spill out in Hell as he circles like a donkey at a mill. — Usāma's version is in Muslim and, per Ibn Kathīr, Bukhārī; al-Qurṭubī notes qaṣab and aqṭāb for intestines, variant verbs tandaliq/tanfaliq, and a weak Abū Umāma counterpart through al-Ḥuṣayb ibn Jaḥdar, though Abū Ghālib was judged sound. Al-Qurṭubī says the sound report establishes increased punishment for ignored knowledge; Elmalılı repeats it. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي,شعبة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو أمامة,أسامة بن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(البخاري ومسلم,النبي ﷺ); ELMALILI(Buhârî ve Müslim)]
- 2:44/q26/p4 A learned person whose knowledge gives no benefit is more severely punished. — Al-Rāzī reports a man whose stench torments Hell and a teacher like a lamp burning itself; al-Qurṭubī attributes a severe-punishment saying to Ibn Mājah. Ibn Kathīr calls Jundub's lamp route strange, gives an Anas report that the unlettered are pardoned more readily, and an unattributed seventy-to-one comparison. Abū al-Dardāʾ and Ibn Masʿūd say sevenfold woe falls on a knowledgeable non-practitioner; 39:9 distinguishes knowing from not knowing. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي ﷺ,بعض الآثار); DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 2:44/q26/p5 Preaching and its intention will be examined before God. — Al-Shaʿbī says every preacher's sermon is presented and its intent examined; al-Rāzī's Yazīd ibn Hārūn dream says a lifetime of calling to God led to forgiveness, while a deathbed anecdote has al-Shiblī decline to repeat the profession of faith with an image of God's inhabited house needing no lamps. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(الشعبي); RAZI-FULL(الشبلي,يزيد بن هارون)]
- 2:44/q26/p6 One calling to a deed without doing it remains under divine anger until reform. — Ibn ʿUmar reports this prophetic warning; Ibn Kathīr judges its chain weak. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي ﷺ)]

## 2:44/q27 What inward warning is drawn from this rebuke?  [* · inward]
- 2:44/q27/p1 One must live the spiritual reality one asks of others. — Al-Qushayrī condemns scrutinizing others' love while ignoring one's own faults and gives images of visitors dispatched while the teacher stays behind; al-Qāshānī calls righteousness heart-purifying action that advances from witnessing acts to attributes. Al-Qushayrī says hearts have had clear inner warnings yet resist them. Bursevī likens postponing repentance to a sinner overtaken by death and quotes Hafez's partridge unaware of the falcon; Maimūna's sheep-and-wolves account links reconciliation with God to outward peace and warns against repentance pursued for worldly gifts. [17 notes: SULAMI(قيل); QUSHAYRI(أنشدوا); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض أهل الإشارات); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السلمي,القشيري); QASHANI; BURSEVI(حافظ); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الواحد بن زيد,ميمونة السوداء)]

## 2:44/q28 How does the verse relate to nearby commands and other verses?  [* · links]
- 2:44/q28/p1 It exposes disobedience amid surrounding calls to covenant, worship, and self-reform. — Al-Ṭabarī links hidden Jewish warnings about disclosing prophecy to 2:77. Al-Biqāʿī sets the rebuke after favors and covenant breaches, with resistance to Arab leadership leading into patience; al-Ālūsī connects gratitude. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls 2:44 a parenthesis between 2:43 and 2:45: congregational prayer cannot suffice without practicing taught righteousness; its lack of waw exposes their corrupt state. Elmalılı links prayer, zakat and congregation to moral refinement, especially for scholars, rulers and judges. Al-Sāmarrāʾī connects its prayer sequence to differing word order at 7:161. The warning is paralleled by 61:2–3 and Shuʿayb's 11:88; Study Quran and Kur'an Yolu point to other birr passages. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي)]

## 2:44/q29 Does this rebuke show that human actions are not created by God?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether divine creation or determination makes blame incoherent.
- 2:44/q29/p1 Yes; rebuke presupposes genuinely human acts rather than compulsion. — Al-Rāzī reports the Muʿtazilite analogy of blaming a black person for not becoming white; Abū Ḥayyān reports their appeal to the verse. Al-Ālūsī separately notes a claim of intrinsic rational wrongness, while observing that scripture recitation may suggest the opposite. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:44/q29/p2 No; the argument fails, and God is not questioned about His acts. — Al-Rāzī argues undetermined choice would be chance; a human determiner repeats the problem and divine determination makes the opposite impossible, so the objection rebounds. He cites 21:23 as ultimate answer. Al-Ālūsī rejects deriving Muʿtazilite intrinsic wrongness from the verse and notes the Torah clause. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]

## 2:44/q30 What does this verse contribute to the Qur'anic challenge?  [* · rhetoric]
- 2:44/q30/p1 A valid challenge requires a recognizable quality, which cannot be mere vocabulary, sound, or meter. — Al-Jurjānī cites 17:88, 11:13 and 2:23 and a ring-craft analogy: an opponent must know the craft and attempt the known thing before inability is proved. Words and lexical meanings preceded revelation; merely matching sound or meter would reduce the challenge to Musaylima-like imitation. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 2:44/q31 What recitation articulation is noted in relation to these words?  [أَنفُسَكُمْ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · readings]
- 2:44/q31/p1 The nūn and hāʾ require careful articulation in their contexts. — Ibn al-Jazarī says nūn is more nasal than mīm and should not be over-darkened, especially when vowelled after alif, and should be clear at pause; he says faint, remote hāʾ must be preserved near similar or doubled letters. [2 notes: NASHR]
