# 39:24: 25 questions

## 39:24/q01 What does يَتَّقِى mean?  [يَتَّقِى · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb emphasizes protective action or meeting what comes.
- 39:24/q01/p1 To shield or protect oneself against harm, using the face as the means here. — ʿAntara's battle line illustrates shielding from spears (Tabrisi); Bursevi glosses اتقى بكذا as taking something for a shield; Ibn ʿAshur distinguishes waqā, with two objects and bi for the instrument, from iftiʿāl with one object, and cites al-Nābigha's hand as instrument. [5 notes: TABRISI(عنترة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة)]
- 39:24/q01/p2 To receive or meet something. — Abu Hayyan cites a poem in which a woman raises her hand to shield herself from sight, showing this usage. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 39:24/q02 What is the form and government of يَتَّقِى?  [يَتَّقِى · grammar]
- 39:24/q02/p1 Frequent use eases two adjacent tāʾs by deleting one; the derived verb governs the punishment as its single object. — Sibawayh compares يتقي and يتسع to consonant deletion in doubled roots. Ibn ʿAshur says سوء العذاب is the object of the iftiʿāl verb and was the second object of base waqā. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:24/q03 What comparison or question does أَفَمَن leave implicit?  [أَفَمَن · grammar]
turns on: Whether the omitted material is a contrasting predicate or an identification of the person in 39:23.
- 39:24/q03/p1 The face-shielding condemned person cannot be like one safe, blessed, guided, righteous, or in Paradise. — Al-Akhfash, al-Zajjaj, and later commentators supply the omitted predicate or counterpart. Qushayri contrasts the believer's radiance, joy and honor; Tabarsi and Razi say the face is honored yet affords no defense; Ibn Kathir cites 67:22, 54:48 and 41:40 and a poet's unstated opposite. Study Quran cites 41:40 and 67:22. Kuranyolu says the formulations are substantially the same. Bursevi calls hamza interrogative denial and fa a link to an omitted premise; Islahi notes the suppressed khabar. Ibn ʿAshur allows reportive or rejecting force and cites 47:14. [34 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; AKHFASH; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الأخفش سعيد); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ)]
- 39:24/q03/p2 The question identifies the person God has led astray in 39:23: is he the one shielding his face? — Ibn ʿAshur prefers an omitted subject that makes the interrogative pick out the earlier verse's referent. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 39:24/q04 What happens to the person guarding with his face?  [يَتَّقِى بِوَجْهِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the wording evokes binding, dragging, first contact, or the last stage of resistance.
- 39:24/q04/p1 His hands are bound, so he must face the Fire without a free limb to shield himself. — Muqatil adds hands chained to neck and a mountain-sized burning sulfur stone hanging there; Farra, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya and others describe the bound castaway. Biqai combines upside-down casting, dragging, and the sulfur stone; Razi, Nasafi, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi stress the hand's usual protective role. Qashani reads bodily dispositions as binding the limbs. Elmalili also pictures the face used as a shield. [19 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 39:24/q04/p2 He is dragged through Hell on his face. — Mujahid relates the image to 41:40; Mawardi, Ibn Atiyya and later compilers transmit it. [10 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:24/q04/p3 He is thrown into Hell face-first, so the Fire first touches his face. — ʿAta is repeatedly named; Qurtubi adds Ibn Zayd, and Durr transmits Ibn Abbas. Mawardi offers this as another image. [9 notes: THALABI(عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); TABRISI(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء وابن زيد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); BIQAI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]
- 39:24/q04/p4 He first protects himself with other limbs, then must expose even his face as torment intensifies. — Ibn Atiyya prefers this as more eloquent and cites the poet's warrior meeting swords with face and neck. Abu Hayyan reports the preference and the poem. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشاعر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 39:24/q05 What does shielding oneself with the face convey?  [بِوَجْهِهِۦ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the face is a physical shield, a figure for no protection, or stands for the whole person.
- 39:24/q05/p1 It conveys utter helplessness: the precious face normally protected by other limbs becomes the only or impossible shield. — Razi lists beauty, senses, identity, emotions, and leaders or true aspects as reasons the face is honored, citing 80:38–42; his al-Nābigha comparison illustrates an apparent exception conveying negation. Tabarsi, Nasafi, Islahi and Kuranyolu stress the reversal; Ibn ʿAshur calls it sarcastic allusion and compares 18:29; Asad calls it an idiom. Alusi finds the purely figurative no-shield account strained. [11 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:24/q05/p2 It is a literal picture of complete and earnest self-protection. — Alusi accepts the literal face even without a chaining report and cites a sword poem in which the head serves as helmet. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:24/q05/p3 The face stands for the whole person, intensifying the image. — Alusi reports the synecdochic reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:24/q05/p4 The person turns his face toward the punishment to fend it off. — Al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya contrasts him with one who does not protect himself and so wrongs himself. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 39:24/q05/p5 The phrase can refer to warding off future punishment through piety. — Tabatabai reports this alternative but finds it forced because the face is not a shield. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 39:24/q06 What is سُوٓءَ ٱلْعَذَابِ?  [سُوٓءَ ٱلْعَذَابِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the phrase is glossed as the torment's severity or its fiery manifestation.
- 39:24/q06/p1 The severity or intensity of punishment. — This gloss is repeated by Thalabi, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Razi and Nasafi. [5 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 39:24/q06/p2 The flame of the Fire. — Bursevi quotes al-Kashifi's Persian commentary. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 39:24/q07 What does يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ modify?  [يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the time phrase attaches to the verb or to the punishment.
- 39:24/q07/p1 It modifies the act of shielding oneself. — Alusi calls this the more immediate attachment; Tabatabai adopts it. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:24/q07/p2 It belongs with the evil punishment: the punishment of that Day. — Alusi calls this grammatically sound, though less immediate. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:24/q08 How does وَقِيلَ relate grammatically and temporally to what precedes?  [وَقِيلَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the waw coordinates clauses or introduces a circumstance, and whether the past depicts certainty.
- 39:24/q08/p1 It is coordinated with the preceding relative clause; past tense states a future event as certain. — Alusi reports coordination with يتقي; Ibn ʿAshur says من can be collectively understood as they. Bursevi and Biqai stress the certainty conveyed by the past form. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:24/q08/p2 It is a circumstantial clause, possibly with implicit قد. — Baydawi specifies circumstantial waw with implicit qad; Alusi and Ibn ʿAshur allow the clause with or without implied qad, and Ibn ʿAshur says this leaves past tense literal. Tabatabai also allows either. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:24/q09 Why use the passive وَقِيلَ?  [وَقِيلَ · rhetoric]
- 39:24/q09/p1 The passive suits a general reproach of the condemned. — Biqai says it lets the speech apply generally. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:24/q10 Who are ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ?  [ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · referent]
- 39:24/q10/p1 They are the unbelievers and polytheists whose disbelief, denial, shirk, and disobedience brought this sentence. — Ibn al-Jawzi and Qurtubi explicitly say disbelievers. Bursevi details substitutions of disbelief for faith, denial for affirmation and disobedience for obedience. Biqai includes abandoning guidance, desire, and leading others astray; Islahi identifies arrogant rejecters of the Quran; Kuranyolu includes other wrongs. Ibn ʿAshur allows polytheists of all peoples, connecting 39:25. [8 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:24/q11 Why say ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ instead of using a pronoun?  [ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · rhetoric]
- 39:24/q11/p1 The overt description marks their guilt as the reason for punishment and makes the judgment general. — Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi and Tabatabai see the causal condemnation. Biqai adds that the plural shows their numbers cannot help them; Ibn ʿAshur specifies shirk as the cause. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:24/q12 Who says ذُوقُوا۟ to the wrongdoers?  [وَقِيلَ لِلظَّٰلِمِينَ · referent]
- 39:24/q12/p1 The speakers are the keepers or angels of Hell. — Muqatil, Thalabi, Jishumi, Baghawi, Tabarsi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Tabatabai identify them; Qurtubi says they rebuke and reproach the condemned. [11 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 39:24/q13 What does ذُوقُوا۟ mean here?  [ذُوقُوا۟ · meaning]
- 39:24/q13/p1 Experience or undergo the punishment directly; tasting is a metaphor for intense bodily sensation. — Ibn Atiyya glosses the verb as direct experience. Ibn ʿAshur says the tongue's sense exceeds skin sensation in intensity. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:24/q14 What is meant by مَا كُنتُمْ تَكْسِبُونَ?  [مَا كُنتُمْ تَكْسِبُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether punishment is viewed as future recompense or as a present state discovered at death.
- 39:24/q14/p1 The consequence or just recompense of their worldly deeds, especially disbelief, denial, shirk, and sins. — Many explain an implicit recompense after ذوقوا. Biqai says they had regarded deeds as beneficial fruit; Bursevi stresses continual earning; Ibn ʿAshur says equating deed and punishment underscores fitting justice; Study Quran cites the echo of 7:39, Qurtubi the parallel of 9:35. Abu Hayyan says evil deeds; Muqatil mentions disbelief and denial. Ibn ʿAshur says the unshielded person is told to taste torment. [20 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:24/q14/p2 They had already dwelt within punishment through vile acts and traits, without sensing it until death woke them from heedlessness. — Bursevi reports the inward reading of al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 39:24/q15 Why say تَكْسِبُونَ rather than تَعْمَلُونَ?  [تَكْسِبُونَ · rhetoric]
- 39:24/q15/p1 It attributes the punishment to what they acquired and blocks protest against the sentence. — Ibn ʿAshur compares the unchosen تعملون. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:24/q16 About whom was this verse revealed?  [* · reports]
- 39:24/q16/p1 About Abu Jahl. — Al-Musayyib/Saʿid ibn al-Musayyib is cited by Thalabi and Jishumi; Alusi states the report. [3 notes: THALABI(المسيب); JISHUMI(سعيد بن المسيب); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:24/q17 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 39:24/q17/p1 It unfolds the contrast between guidance and misguidance from the preceding verses: the astray meet punishment, the guided safety or bliss. — Tabarsi says those outside God's guidance remain unbelieving. Razi cites 13:33 for hard-hearted worldly misguidance and severe afterlife punishment. Biqai sees the interrogative as an interwoven account of two outcomes and infers a matching welcome to the righteous. Alusi says the argument resumes. Ibn ʿAshur places the interruption of Quran praise between 39:23 and 39:27; Tabatabai sees continuity with 39:23. [7 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:24/q18 How is وَقِيلَ read?  [وَقِيلَ · readings]
- 39:24/q18/p1 Qīla retains the passive q-w-l form with a rounded transitional vowel after wāw through vowel harmony. — Qiraat-ER gives this phonetic account. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 39:24/q19 What further judgment about the wrongdoers is drawn?  [* · theology]
- 39:24/q19/p1 God's threatened punishment will occur and the wrongdoers will receive no mercy in their helplessness. — Jishumi rejects a Murjiʾite view that divine promise or threat can fail; Islahi says God will show no mercy when they face punishment. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:24/q20 What further sound changes does Sibawayh use to illustrate easing near the form يَتَّقِى?  [يَتَّقِى · grammar]
- 39:24/q20/p1 Frequent or difficult consonant sequences undergo substitution, assimilation, or deletion in several Arabic forms. — Sibawayh derives ست from سدس by substitution and assimilation, rejects ṣād there; ييجل/أدل ease wāw beside yāʾ; Tamim ود assimilates وتد but risks ambiguity, and عتدان/عدان avoid tāʾ-dāl. أحست/مست/ظلت lose a doubled consonant. يسطيع either deletes t from يستطيع or adds sīn to أطاع; يستيع either deletes ṭāʾ or replaces it with t. استخذ either substitutes sīn for t or deletes t from استتخذ. Some say الطجع for اضطجع. Frequent tribal names delete n before article l, and علماء بنو فلان loses l of على الماء. [14 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض العرب)]

## 39:24/q21 What other examples of omitted words accompany the discussion of this verse?  [أَفَمَن · grammar]
- 39:24/q21/p1 Visible context can license an unspoken verb while an accusative noun supplies its sense. — Sibawayh gives عائذا بالله, a nominative variant عائذ بالله, Ibn al-Harith al-Sahmi's وعائذا بك interpreted as وعياذا بك, and زحارا أنانا instead of زحيرا وأنينا; these concern omitted verbs rather than the verse's missing predicate. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(منهم)]

## 39:24/q22 What general claims about metaphor and eloquence occur with the verse material?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Which conventional likeness or semantic gain makes expression effective.
- 39:24/q22/p1 A successful metaphor or simile conveys a recognizable likeness rather than literal identity or falsehood. — Jurjani says a raincloud identity for 2:19 cannot recover the intended comparison, while ṣayyib may convey generosity elsewhere. Conventional traits make sun, lion and sea metaphors work; roundness is not conventionally the sun's trait, though sphere conveys it. Hyperbolic identification can call people stars or lions. He praises Abu Firas's rational arrow simile, treats 19:4 as gray spreading like fire, reports the Prophet's mirror comparison of a believer as revealing qualities, and explains green growth on dung as beauty with corrupt origin. [11 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(النبي)]
- 39:24/q22/p2 Truthful and meaningful verbal invention can be eloquent, whereas bare sound repetition cannot explain Quranic inimitability. — Jurjani rejects a demand for factual contradiction. Al-Mutanabbi's invented motive for killing enemies is to feed expectant wolves, portraying generosity; Jurjani adds that decisive victory spares future bloodshed and contrasts rage-driven killing. Abu Talib al-Maʾmuni's minister dreams of aiding a late visitor, but overexplanation weakens praise. In Dalail, a modern poet's repeated word adds meaning where Abu Tammam's repeated madhhab does not; paronomasia like najā/najā may adorn meaning, yet phonetic ease alone cannot ground Quranic inimitability. [8 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(المتنبي); JURJANI-DALAIL; JURJANI-DALAIL+]

## 39:24/q23 What adjacent linguistic and exegetical examples are recorded in these sources?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:24/q23/p1 Ilā principally marks an endpoint or direction, whose limit may be included or excluded; other reported senses are with, for, in, and by. — Ibn al-Jawzi contrasts min and ilā; al-Tabrizi allows entry into or stopping before the endpoint. Ablution includes elbows (5:6), fasting excludes night (2:187). Examples of direction cite 20:24, 7:65, 7:73 and 7:85; maʿa cites 61:14, 4:2, 5:6; lām or fī explains 6:12; biʾ is proposed for 2:14 and 2:187, though al-Naḍr treats these as maʿa. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو زكريا يحيى بن علي التبريزي,أهل التفسير,النضر بن شميل,بعضهم)]
- 39:24/q23/p2 Conjunction may follow wording, syntactic position, or an imaginable particle. — Al-Itqan requires an original valid position and live governor for positional conjunction; its counterexamples include governed adjectives and coordination after إن. Imagined construction must be licit, strongest when common, illustrated by a genitive phrase and Zuhayr's line. [4 notes: ITQAN(زهير)]
- 39:24/q23/p3 Some Quranic words and apparent antonyms call for caution in interpretation. — Bint al-Shati notes scholarly wariness about ambiguities and rare words, and reports Abu Hatim al-Sijistani's criticism of interpreters exceeding their Arabic knowledge. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو حاتم السجستاني)]

## 39:24/q24 How should statements that a verse was revealed about an event be understood?  [* · sciences]
- 39:24/q24/p1 Such statements may identify an instance of a general ruling or a contextually fitting event without proving an exclusive occasion. — Ibn ʿAshur distinguishes recurring conduct and events fitting surrounding passages, warns that storytellers can overrestrict general wording, and cites Ibn Masud's general false-oath report versus al-Ashʿath's well dispute at 3:77, 2:105, Ibn Abbas's 4:94 flock story, and al-Zubayr's water dispute at 4:65. He mentions Ibn Abbas's name for the opening of Surat al-Tawba. Al-Zarkashi says revealed-about often means inclusion. Ibn Taymiyya reports that Bukhari counts a Companion's general claim as musnad while most compilers do not, but all count an account of an immediately preceding event as musnad. [16 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن تيمية,ابن عباس,الأشعث بن قيس,الزبير,الزركشي,عبد الله بن مسعود)]

## 39:24/q25 What forms of emulation are commended in Ibn Kathir's accompanying discussion?  [* · reports]
- 39:24/q25/p1 Ghibta is wishing for a similar blessing without wishing its removal; this is the praiseworthy sense of hasad. — Ibn Kathir distinguishes condemned envy, which wishes loss regardless of gain and was Iblis's first sin against Adam. He says the Quran companion's blessing merits congratulation and emulation. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 39:24/q25/p2 The Prophet commends emulation of devotion to the Quran and generous spending. — Ibn Kathir explains the two cases as private blessing through Quran and benefit to others through charity, citing 35:29. Yazid ibn al-Akhnas reports a saying limiting competition to the Quran devotee and charitable giver. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 39:24/q25/p3 The four states differ by knowledge, wealth, action, and intent. — Abu Kabsha transmits the Prophet's fourfold account: knowledgeable charitable wealth is best; a knowledgeable poor person's good intent earns equal reward; ignorant wealth is squandered; an ignorant poor person's corrupt wish incurs equal burden. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو كبشة)]
