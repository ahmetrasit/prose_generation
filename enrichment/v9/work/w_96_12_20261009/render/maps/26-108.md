# 26:108: 10 questions

## 26:108/q01 What does fearing God require here?  [فَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
- 26:108/q01/p1 Obey and worship God, especially by affirming His oneness. — Muqātil says worship God. Wāḥidī and Baghawī specify obedience and worship; Ṭabrisī says both. Biqāʿī specifies monotheism as the path to salvation. Bursevī and Ālūsī likewise connect it with monotheism and obedience. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:108/q01/p2 Shield yourselves from God's punishment through obedience and by leaving rebellion. — Qurṭubī explains piety as sheltering oneself from punishment by obedience. Iṣlāḥī says they must fear God's grasp and abandon rebellion to escape punishment; Biqāʿī connects the command with salvation and avoiding punishment. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:108/q01/p3 Fear God by obeying Noah, His messenger. — Kashshāf says Noah directs them to fear God through obeying him. Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains that Noah conveys only God's command, so obedience to the messenger is obedience to God. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 26:108/q02 What does obeying Noah require of his people?  [وَأَطِيعُونِ · interpretation]
- 26:108/q02/p1 Heed Noah's counsel and commands, particularly his call to faith, God's oneness, and obedience to God. — Muqātil and Kashshāf describe following his counsel; Kashshāf adds the truth to which he calls. Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī, and Study Quran specify belief, faith, or monotheism. Nasafī mentions the truth he commands; Abū Ḥayyān mentions his advice and exclusive worship. Biqāʿī says all his commands avert punishment. Bursevī says Noah will neither betray nor harm them. Iṣlāḥī adds that they must stop following rebellious leaders who lead them to destruction. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Noah conveys God's commands. Elmalılı restates the appeal to obey him. [16 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 26:108/q03 How is the first-person yāʾ of أطيعون treated?  [وَأَطِيعُونِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the first-person yāʾ is pronounced or omitted.
- 26:108/q03/p1 The first-person yāʾ is affirmed, including in connected recitation and at a pause. — Nashr reports Yaʿqūb affirming sixteen listed extra yāʾs in both forms: eight quoted forms and أطيعون at eight places. Qiraat-ER says explicit retention lengthens the final syllable without changing the meaning. [2 notes: NASHR(يعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]
- 26:108/q03/p2 The first-person yāʾ is omitted from أطيعون. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this occurs in both appearances of أطيعون here, as also in 26:14. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:108/q04 Why is fearing God placed before obedience to Noah?  [فَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُونِ · rhetoric]
- 26:108/q04/p1 Piety or reverence for God is the cause of obedience to Noah, so it comes first. — Rāzī and Ālūsī explicitly say the cause precedes its consequence. Study Quran reports the explanation that obedience depends on reverence. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 26:108/q05 How does the command follow Noah's claim to be trustworthy?  [فَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُونِ · rhetoric]
- 26:108/q05/p1 His trustworthiness gives grounds for the people to obey his command. — Biqāʿī says Noah moves from gentle invitation and proof of trustworthiness to a decisive command. Bursevī explains that the opening fa follows from that trustworthiness and says Noah will neither betray nor harm them. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Noah conveys only what God commanded, making obedience to him obedience to God. Abū Ḥayyān also notes the movement from gentle invitation to direct command. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 26:108/q06 Why is the call to fear God and obey Noah repeated?  [فَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُونِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give different rhetorical purposes for the renewed command: distinct supporting grounds, emphasis, or warning and concern.
- 26:108/q06/p1 The repeated command has separate grounds: Noah's trustworthiness and his asking no wage. — Kashshāf says each repetition has its own rationale: trustworthiness and no expectation from them. Ṭabrisī says the absence of a wage removes fear for their wealth. Rāzī distinguishes disobeying God's messenger from disobeying one who takes no wage, comparing this with repeating a rebuke for separate reasons. Bayḍāwī says each ground independently establishes obedience and both strengthen it together. Bursevī says each independently requires obedience and repetition emphasizes this. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 26:108/q06/p2 Repetition reinforces the command and gives it emphasis. — Kashshāf says Noah repeats it to fix it in their minds. Qurṭubī calls it emphasis. Bursevī says the renewed command emphasizes the grounds for obedience. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the command reiterates ألا تتقون, interrupting two coordinated clauses; Noah restates it after explaining it, after mentioning no wage, and at the speech's conclusion. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:108/q06/p3 The renewed call warns the people and expresses Noah's earnest concern for them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the warning and earnest concern as the force of the renewed appeal. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 26:108/q07 How does Noah's refusal of payment support this command?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:108/q07/p1 Noah seeks no payment from the people, so his appeal is free of financial interest; his reward is with God. — Yaḥyā says Noah's reward is recompense from the Lord of the worlds. Ibn Kathīr says he seeks no payment for advising them and keeps his reward with that Lord. Ṭabrisī says they need not fear losing their wealth. Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, and Bursevī treat refusal of payment as a distinct ground for obedience in the repeated appeal. Ibn ʿĀshūr places a repetition of the command after the statement that he seeks no wage. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:108/q08 What does obedience to God's messenger imply here?  [* · theology]
- 26:108/q08/p1 Obedience to the messenger is obedience to God because he conveys only God's commands. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes this equivalence explicit for Noah as a trustworthy messenger. Kashshāf expresses the linked duty by explaining that they should fear God through obedience to Noah. [2 notes: TABATABAI; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 26:108/q09 Where does Noah's account stand in the sūra's sequence?  [* · links]
- 26:108/q09/p1 It follows Moses and Abraham and turns to Noah, the first of the foremost prophets. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that most of Noah's people disbelieved; God drowned them and saved Noah with the believers. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 26:108/q10 How does Noah's call compare with Moses' and Abraham's accounts?  [* · links]
- 26:108/q10/p1 The three accounts share the same essential principles and depict similar beliefs and responses to true religion. — Kuranyolu says the three narrative groups deliver the same message and cites 11:25 and 11:49. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
