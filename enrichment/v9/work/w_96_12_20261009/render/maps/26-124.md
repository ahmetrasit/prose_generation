# 26:124: 12 questions

## 26:124/q01 How does 'when he said to them' relate to the account of denial?  [إِذْ قَالَ لَهُمْ · grammar]
- 26:124/q01/p1 It gives the time of ʿĀd's denial: Hūd addressed them as they rejected the messengers. — Bursevī explicitly attaches the temporal clause to the account of denial. Al-Ṭabarī describes ʿĀd as denying God's messengers when their brother Hūd urged them to fear punishment for disbelief. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 26:124/q02 Who are the people Hūd addresses, and where did they live?  [لَهُمْ · referent]
- 26:124/q02/p1 They are ʿĀd; a report places them in Yemen at al-Aḥqāf, the sand dunes. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī identify Hūd's people as ʿĀd. Al-Suddī, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, locates them in Yemen at al-Aḥqāf and explains the name as sand dunes. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); TABATABAI]

## 26:124/q03 Why is Hūd called their brother?  [أَخُوهُمْ · referent]
- 26:124/q03/p1 He belonged to their people by lineage and was personally known to them, though he did not share their religion. — Muqātil and al-Baghawī explicitly distinguish lineage from religion; al-Ṭabrisī and Bursevī also state lineage. Al-Biqāʿī says they already knew his sincerity, counsel and fidelity, so they should have reflected before rejecting him. Iṣlāḥī says he was no stranger and his impeccable life made their treatment of him as a liar or fabricator especially unfortunate. Samarrāʾī's comparison explains that Shuʿayb is called Midian's brother because he belonged to Midian, but not the Aykah people's brother; he cites 7:85, 11:84 and 23:36. [7 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:124/q04 What is reported about Hūd's name and life?  [هُودٌ · reports]
- 26:124/q04/p1 Some say his original name was ʿĀbir, he was called Hūd for dignity and calmness, lived 150 years, and was sent to ʿĀd at forty. — Bursevī transmits this as the view of 'some'; the reported reason for the name is his dignity and calmness. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 26:124/q05 What does Hūd's question call his people to do?  [أَلَا تَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
- 26:124/q05/p1 Fear God and His punishment by ending disobedience, especially associating partners with Him, and worship Him alone. — Muqātil glosses it as fearing God; al-Ṭabarī specifies fear of punishment for disbelief; Elmalılı likewise reads it as asking whether they fear God. Al-Ṭabrisī and Kur'an Yolu explain piety as avoiding disobedience. Bursevī adds stopping their conduct, especially shirk, lest they incur punishment. Al-Biqāʿī says they must fear their Creator, worship Him alone and leave powerless associates that can neither harm nor benefit. Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr refer the explanation to the preceding or parallel opening of Noah's story. Iṣlāḥī describes the warning as a remaining chance to abandon wrongdoing and follow the messenger instead of corrupt leaders. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:124/q06 What tone does the opening 'alā' give Hūd's appeal?  [أَلَا · rhetoric]
- 26:124/q06/p1 It is a gentle, earnest and affectionate warning that leaves the people a chance to change course. — Al-Biqāʿī says alā makes the exhortation courteous and soft enough to reach them. Iṣlāḥī calls the repeated prophetic appeal an earnest, affectionate final warning: they can still forsake wrongdoing and follow their messenger rather than corrupt leaders. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:124/q07 How did ʿĀd respond to Hūd's admonition?  [* · reports]
- 26:124/q07/p1 They rejected him and the messengers; a report says they demanded the punishment he warned of. — Al-Ṭabarī states that ʿĀd denied God's messengers when Hūd urged fear of punishment. Al-Suddī, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, says Hūd admonished them and recalled the Qur'anic account, but they denied him and demanded punishment. Al-Biqāʿī stresses that rejection came without pausing to consider their known brother's sincerity. Iṣlāḥī says they treated a man whose impeccable life they knew as a liar or fabricator. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:124/q08 How does Hūd's address follow the pattern of the other prophets' addresses?  [* · links]
- 26:124/q08/p1 He addresses his own people with the recurring prophetic call to piety, within the shared pattern of trustworthiness, obedience and seeking no wage. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Hūd addresses them as other messengers address their peoples. Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain his call by the preceding or parallel opening of Noah's story. The Study Quran says Hūd's first five verses match Noah's opening and those of later prophets, citing 26:105–109, 141–145, 160–164 and 176–180. Samarrāʾī notes that the other prophets in this sūra address their own peoples, contrasting Shuʿayb's relation to Midian and Aykah; he cites 7:85, 11:84 and 23:36. Some exegetes reported by Ṭabāṭabāʾī say the recurring assertions of prophetic trustworthiness, refusal of wages, and calls to piety and obedience show a shared mission of true knowledge and conduct leading to reward and away from punishment, despite differing detailed laws across eras. Iṣlāḥī describes the recurring appeal as an earnest, affectionate final warning to follow the messenger rather than corrupt leaders. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:124/q09 Why does the account speak of denying the messengers when ʿĀd rejected Hūd?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:124/q09/p1 The plural description accompanies their outward rejection of one messenger, as it does in Noah's story. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī observes that the people are called deniers of the messengers even though they outwardly rejected only Hūd; the same pattern occurs in Noah's story. Al-Ṭabarī also describes ʿĀd as denying God's messengers when Hūd warned them, but gives no further explanation here. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 26:124/q10 What role does Hūd's story have in this sūra's sequence?  [* · links]
- 26:124/q10/p1 It helps detail the intervening nations' dealings with their messengers and fates in historical order, each account ending with a refrain. — Iṣlāḥī compares the previous sūra, which detailed Moses and Noah but briefly mentioned the nations between them, with this sūra's fuller ordered accounts and recurring refrain. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:124/q11 Where else is the account of Hūd and ʿĀd discussed?  [* · links]
- 26:124/q11/p1 At 7:65, to which Asad directs readers along with its note 48. — Asad gives 7:65 and his note 48 as the cross-reference for the account of Hūd and ʿĀd. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 26:124/q12 How do terms for distance differ in related passages?  [* · links]
- 26:124/q12/p1 Buʿd is the opposite of nearness; naʾy specifically conveys turning away and refusal, the opposite of turning toward. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts their usage across 11:95, 11:44, 11:60, 11:68, 23:41, 23:44, 2:176, 22:52, 41:52, 14:3, 14:18, 4:6, 4:116, 4:136, 4:167, 22:12, 42:18, 34:8 and 50:27. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
