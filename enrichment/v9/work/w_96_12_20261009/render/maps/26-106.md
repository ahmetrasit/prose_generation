# 26:106: 11 questions

## 26:106/q01 How does إذ relate Noah's speech to his people's denial?  [إِذْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether إذ locates the denial in time or explains its cause.
- 26:106/q01/p1 It marks the time of their denial, spanning Noah's mission or the episode in which they rejected his warning. — Bursevî and Ālūsī extend the time over the long course of denial, from the mission's beginning to its end. Ibn ʿĀshūr places their denial when Noah spoke and they objected to his low-status followers (26:111). Ṭabarī says they denied God's messengers when Noah called them to fear the punishment of disbelief and denial. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:106/q01/p2 It has a causal force: they denied because Noah addressed them with this warning. — Ālūsī reports this as the reading of some interpreters. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 26:106/q02 To whom does لهم refer?  [لَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun's antecedent and whether brotherhood is kinship or shared species.
- 26:106/q02/p1 Noah's people. — Ālūsī takes this as the apparent referent, consistent with Noah being their kinsman. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:106/q02/p2 The messengers. — Ālūsī reports this alternative, which would make the brotherhood one of shared species, but calls it contrary to the apparent sense. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 26:106/q03 What does calling Noah their brother convey?  [أَخُوهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether brotherhood denotes kinship and belonging to the people or shared human kind; Ālūsī connects the latter to a different antecedent for لهم.
- 26:106/q03/p1 Noah was one of their own people, their kinsman or fellow tribesman, though he did not share their religion. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī reporting Ibn ʿAbbās and the commentators, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī, and Nasafī specify lineage rather than religion; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds common origin and Qurṭubī common fatherhood. Zajjāj, Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Study Quran describe membership among the people. Kashshāf and Qurṭubī citing Zamakhsharī adduce a Ḥamāsa line using 'their brother' for a tribesman summoned in adversity; Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Ālūsī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give the idiom 'brother of Tamīm' or similar tribal expressions, and Abū Ḥayyān notes poetic usage. Zajjāj, Wāḥidī, and Qurṭubī connect this to messengers speaking their people's language so the proof is clearer (14:4); Wāḥidī cites 14:4 again in the continuation. Bursevî says shared lineage, known honesty and faithfulness, and intelligible language eased acceptance. Biqāʿī says the kinship recalls gentle manners, helps win the hearers, and consoles Muḥammad. Ālūsī favors this over a shared-species reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions 7:65. [30 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وجميع المفسرين,الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:106/q03/p2 He was their brother in kind, sharing their human nature. — Qurṭubī reports brotherhood of kind or shared humanity. Abū Ḥayyān allows shared human kind alongside kinship. Ālūsī says that taking لهم as referring to the messengers would yield shared-species brotherhood, but rejects that antecedent as contrary to the apparent sense. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 26:106/q04 What is the grammatical role of نوح?  [نُوحٌ · grammar]
- 26:106/q04/p1 It is an explanatory apposition specifying who 'their brother' is. — Bursevî identifies Noah as the clarification of أخوهم. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:106/q05 What force does ألا have in Noah's question?  [أَلَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ألا is an interrogative hamza plus negative lā or one exhortative particle.
- 26:106/q05/p1 It is an interrogative hamza with negative lā, rebuking their lack of piety and warning them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question denies that they are pious and implies refusal to answer Noah's call. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the question reproachful; Iṣlāḥī hears a warning in it. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:106/q05/p2 It is a single particle of exhortation, implying that they are slow to believe Noah. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this as an alternative analysis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:106/q06 What does Noah ask them to fear or avoid in ألا تتقون?  [تَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of taqwā and, in Abū Ḥayyān's and Samīn's grammatical comments, which object of fearing is left unspoken: God, His punishment, or disobedience through idol worship.
- 26:106/q06/p1 To fear God. — Muqātil, al-Suddī as reported by Yaḥyā, and Elmalılı give the direct sense. Dāmghānī says اتقوا can mean 'fear' and cites 4:1 and 22:1. Nasafī calls God the Creator of all people. Ālūsī says Noah asks if they fear God while worshiping another besides Him. [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); DAMGHANI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 26:106/q06/p2 To fear and avert God's punishment for disbelief, denying Noah or the messengers, and associating partners with Him. — Ṭabarī specifies disbelief and denial of God's messengers; Ṭabrisī adds opposing Noah. Wāḥidī recalls the earlier explanation as averting punishment through obedience (mentioning 26:11). Ibn al-Jawzī connects fearing punishment with affirming God's oneness and obeying Him. Abū Ḥayyān gives punishment for shirk as one possible omitted object; Samīn identifies the unexpressed object as God's punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies punishment for associating partners with God. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:106/q06/p3 To abandon worship of others or idols, affirm God's oneness, and obey Him rather than disobey Him. — Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Ibn Kathīr, Bursevî, Ālūsī, and Nasafī apply the warning to idol worship or worship of other than God; Bayḍāwī and Nasafī explicitly urge abandoning it. Ibn al-Jawzī adds affirmation of God's oneness and obedience. Abū Ḥayyān offers disobedience through idol worship as the other possible omitted object. Study Quran describes taqwā as mindfulness or care in avoiding disobedience to God, citing 2:2. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 26:106/q07 Why does Noah begin by warning them to fear God?  [أَلَا تَتَّقُونَ · rhetoric]
- 26:106/q07/p1 Fear should prompt people who inherited religion by imitation to examine reasons and evidence. — Rāzī says blind followers need fear to start reflection and argument, explaining why Noah warns first. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 26:106/q08 Why is the call to fear God repeated in the following verses?  [أَلَا تَتَّقُونَ · rhetoric]
- 26:106/q08/p1 Each repetition has a distinct ground: obedience to God's messenger, then obedience to a messenger who asks no wage. — Rāzī compares the repetition to repeating a rebuke for separate reasons. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 26:106/q09 What do the sources say about Noah's appeals before this warning?  [* · reports]
- 26:106/q09/p1 He had likely called them repeatedly; this admonition came after they persisted in disbelief. — Ibn ʿĀshūr infers the sequence from their answer objecting to his low-status followers (26:111). [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:106/q10 How did Noah's people respond to his call?  [* · reports]
- 26:106/q10/p1 They denied him and objected to his low-status followers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites their objection at 26:111 to locate their denial when Noah made this appeal; Ṭabarī says they denied God's messengers when Noah warned them. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL]
- 26:106/q10/p2 They did not pause to ask for evidence or seek a gracious approach before responding. — Biqāʿī stresses the immediacy of their response. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:106/q10/p3 They strangled Noah until he fainted; he prayed for their forgiveness, then after generations of mounting defiance complained to God. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبيد بن عمير الليثي)]

## 26:106/q11 How does this account relate to the other prophetic stories and to Muhammad?  [* · links]
- 26:106/q11/p1 Its parallel with Muhammad's situation consoles him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sūra selects this episode for that parallel; Biqāʿī says the description of Noah as their brother also consoles Muhammad. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:106/q11/p2 Noah's call has the same principles as Moses's and Abraham's, and their peoples' beliefs and responses follow the same pattern. — Kur’an Yolu says the three narrative groups deliver one message and mentions 11:25 and 11:49. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:106/q11/p3 After Moses and Abraham, the account turns to Noah, the first of the foremost prophets; God drowned most of his unbelieving people and saved him and the believers. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī summarizes the narrative transition and outcome. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
