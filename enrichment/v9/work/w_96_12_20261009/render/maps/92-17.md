# 92:17: 13 questions

## 92:17/q01 What does being kept from it entail?  [وَسَيُجَنَّبُهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promise describes complete distance from Hell or a contrast with the severe torment others can suffer.
- 92:17/q01/p1 God sets the God-fearing person far from the Fire, so he does not enter it. — Al-Wahidi, al-Jishumi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, and al-Alusi explain the verb as distancing or putting to one side; al-Jishumi pictures him on Paradise's side. Al-Baydawi and al-Biqa'i specify that full avoidance of idolatry and sins prevents entry; Elmalılı adds an easy passage to Paradise. Al-Tabari calls it protection from the blazing Fire. [24 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:17/q01/p2 Lesser believers may enter Hell yet be spared its most severe burning or leave after punishment. — Al-Baydawi distinguishes entry from burning for one avoiding shirk but committing sins. Al-Biqa'i says such a sinner may enter and later leave. Ibn al-Munir, quoted by al-Alusi, describes ṣaly as roasting between layers of embers; this allows lesser punishments. Al-Alusi says the most pious do not enter. [6 notes: BAYDAWI; BIQAI; ALUSI(ابن المنير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)]

## 92:17/q02 How is yujannabuhā constructed?  [وَسَيُجَنَّبُهَا ٱلْأَتْقَى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the object is explicit and how the passive promotes the person protected.
- 92:17/q02/p1 Jannaba takes two objects; in the passive al-atqā becomes the subject and the Fire pronoun remains its other object. — Al-Alusi derives the verb from putting a person's side away and notes its common distancing sense; his two summaries give differing object numbering while agreeing on the passive construction. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:17/q02/p2 When an object is unexpressed, jannaba can mean keeping someone from good. — Al-Raghib, quoted by al-Alusi, connects this usage with putting a person's side away; the explicit Fire pronoun here specifies the object. [1 notes: ALUSI(الراغب)]

## 92:17/q03 What does the future passive wording suggest?  [وَسَيُجَنَّبُهَا · rhetoric]
- 92:17/q03/p1 The future particle promises certainty after a respite, and the passive portrays separation from Hell as effortless. — Al-Biqa'i draws both effects from the future particle and passive form. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 92:17/q04 What degree of piety does al-atqā express?  [ٱلْأَتْقَى · meaning]
turns on: Whether the afʿal form is an ordinary descriptor, an absolute rank, or a comparison within a stated group.
- 92:17/q04/p1 The form can mean simply a pious person, naming the pious class. — Al-Tabari and al-Tabrisi cite Tarafa's awḥad for wāḥid; al-Qurtubi adds akbar for kabīr and ahwan for hayyin, mentioning 30:27. Al-Alusi repeats the poetic evidence but says generic elatives alone do not settle the restriction difficulty. Al-Baghawi and Abu Ubayda, quoted by al-Nasafi, also use the ordinary class sense. [18 notes: TAB(طرفة); TAB-FULL(طرفة بن العبد); THALABI(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(طرفة); QURTUBI(بعض أهل المعاني); NASAFI(أبو عبيدة); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI(طرفة); ALUSI-FULL(طرفة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 92:17/q04/p2 It is an intensive or superlative: the most God-fearing in degree. — Al-Jishumi derives it and al-ashqā as elatives from taqī and shaqī; Ibn Ashur calls both common intensifying forms. Bint al-Shati notes that this al-atqā is unrestricted, unlike atqākum in 49:13. Al-Qurtubi describes the person as pious and fearful. [5 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; QURTUBI]
- 92:17/q04/p3 It is the most God-fearing among those who might fear worldly loss and who nevertheless spend for God. — Tabataba'i says the comparative identifies the giver among people mindful of poverty or loss; he rejects an absolute superlative confined to one person or one per age. [1 notes: TABATABAI+; TABATABAI-]
- 92:17/q04/p4 It may be the most pious among Paradise's inhabitants rather than among all humanity. — Al-Jishumi notes that Paradise includes children, the insane, compensated people, and houris, who are outside the warned moral audience. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 92:17/q05 Whom does al-atqā describe?  [ٱلْأَتْقَى · referent]
turns on: Whether reports of an occasion fix the referent and whether the singular definite elative also covers others.
- 92:17/q05/p1 Abu Bakr is its named instance. — Al-Farra, Ibn Abbas, Urwa and many commentators name him; al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, and al-Itqan report consensus in their respective circles. Ibn Kathir treats him as foremost under the wording. His freeing of persecuted slaves and selfless spending are cited as fitting traits; al-Razi rejects Ali because prophetic care would count as a favor to repay. Al-Alusi reports sound chains from Ibn Masud and Ibn Abbas. [48 notes: FARRA; TUSTARI; IBNABIHATIM(عروة); THALABI(عبد الله بن الزبير,عروة عن أبيه); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن الزبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(جميع المفسرين); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI(ابن الزبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن الزبير); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA(أهل التأويل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أهل التأويل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جميع المفسرين); RAZI; RAZI-FULL(أجمع المفسرون منا,ابن الزبير); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر,الزبير بن بكار); DURR(ابن عباس,عروة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عروة); ITQAN; SUYUTI-LUBAB(عروة); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI(الرازي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(others); RAZI+; TABATABAI-]
- 92:17/q05/p2 Abu al-Dahdah is its named instance. — Al-Tha'labi ties the designation to giving away the purchased palm solely for God's face; al-Qurtubi gives the same contextual alternative. Tabataba'i says al-Qummi's report has Shia routes from the Imams and regards it as reliable Shia evidence; al-Alusi reports al-Suyuti's judgment that the Ibn Abbas palm report is weak. [10 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(عطاء,عكرمة عن ابن عباس); TABATABAI+]
- 92:17/q05/p3 Ali is its named instance. — Al-Razi and Tabataba'i report that some Shia cite 5:55, the alms while bowing verse, for Ali. Al-Razi counters that the Prophet's upbringing of Ali creates a prior worldly favor relevant to the following verses; Tabataba'i rejects the claim that all Shia sources hold this view. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أجمع المفسرون منا); TABATABAI(الرازي); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 92:17/q05/p4 The description applies generally to every pious believer or giver with these qualities. — Ibn Atiyya includes everyone sharing the traits despite Abu Bakr as occasion; Ibn Kathir and Kuran Yolu likewise preserve the general wording. Tabataba'i says a descriptor is general even with a specific occasion. Al-Itqan objects that the singular definite elative has an antecedent, cannot be a relative al, and lacks a generalizing form. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أهل التأويل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+; ITQAN-]
- 92:17/q05/p5 The wording is particular to one person rather than a general class. — Al-Itqan invokes consensus on Abu Bakr, the singular form, its antecedent, and a comparative that separates one referent from others; it denies that al is relative here. [2 notes: ITQAN+; IBNATIYYA-; TABATABAI-]

## 92:17/q06 What event is reported behind the pious giver?  [ٱلْأَتْقَى · reports]
turns on: Which donation story is transmitted and, within the Bilal story, how he was purchased.
- 92:17/q06/p1 Abu Bakr bought and freed vulnerable people persecuted for faith. — Urwa counts seven, including Bilal, Amir ibn Fuhayra, al-Nahdiyya and daughter, Zunayra, Umm Isa, and a Banu al-Muammal bondwoman. Ibn al-Zubayr reports his father's advice to buy strong defenders and Abu Bakr's reply about protecting his own back; versions phrase the reply as seeking protection, while al-Wahidi says worldly protection was not his aim. Al-Tha'labi says he spent forty thousand. Biqa'i adds Zahra's recovered sight after rejecting idols; the Study Quran tells of Bilal's defiance and a charge that Abu Bakr repaid a favor. [20 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عروة); THALABI(عبد الله بن الزبير,عروة عن أبيه); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن الزبير); BAGHAWI(ابن الزبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن الزبير); RAZI-FULL(ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر,الزبير بن بكار,زهرة); DURR(عروة); DURR-FULL(عروة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عروة); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 92:17/q06/p2 Abu Bakr purchased Bilal for gold after his torture and testimony to God's oneness. — Al-Tha'labi's Ata report has the Prophet reassure Bilal and Abu Bakr pay gold; the Study Quran gives the idol desecration, testimony and the Prophet's assurance, then the gold purchase. The idolaters alleged a prior favor, answered by revelation. [4 notes: THALABI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:17/q06/p3 Abu Bakr obtained Bilal by exchanging Nustas, a wealthy slave who refused Islam. — Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib reports this form of the transaction and says revelation affirmed that Abu Bakr sought God alone and would be repaid in the hereafter. [1 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن المسيب)]
- 92:17/q06/p4 Abu al-Dahdah bought a palm and donated it to secure its owner a promised palm in Paradise. — Al-Tha'labi says the original owner refused the Prophet's offer, then Abu al-Dahdah bought and donated the palm; the Prophet later spoke of his abundant clusters in Paradise. Al-Qurtubi names the miser as the Ansari owner. Tabataba'i transmits a version with forty palms as the purchase price, while another names Abu al-Dahdah; al-Suyuti calls the Ibn Abbas report weak. [6 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI(عطاء,عكرمة عن ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]

## 92:17/q07 Does this verse establish Abu Bakr's rank after the Prophet?  [ٱلْأَتْقَى · theology]
turns on: Whether a verse about piety identifies an absolute personal rank and whether the reported referent is exclusive.
- 92:17/q07/p1 Abu Bakr is the community's best after the Prophet. — Al-Razi joins the most God-fearing here to the most honored in 49:13: visible piety reveals otherwise hidden rank. He says consensus narrows the candidate to Abu Bakr or Ali, then excludes Ali because of the Prophet's prior care, invoking 25:57. Al-Itqan reports this reasoning. Al-Biqa'i adds the promise of satisfaction and a report that God and believers accept Abu Bakr. [11 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ITQAN(فخر الدين الرازي); BIQAI-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); RAZI+; TABATABAI-]
- 92:17/q07/p2 The description does not prove that one person is absolutely best of all people. — Tabataba'i rejects the absolute rank inference and the assertion that all Shia sources name Ali; al-Alusi describes the Imam's rank argument as disputable. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); TABATABAI+]

## 92:17/q08 How do the two apparent restrictions about Hell fit together?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'only' yields a logical converse, whether ṣaly means any entry or severe torment, and whether the superlatives mark classes or exemplary figures.
- 92:17/q08/p1 The paired superlatives are rhetorical portraits of exemplary opponents, not strict exclusions of everyone else. — Al-Zamakhshari likens the Fire and Garden to places made for the leading disbeliever and believer; examples are Abu Jahl or Umayya and Abu Bakr. Al-Kashf approves. Al-Alusi prefers this after weighing alternatives, though he notes the Mu'tazilite premise about grave sinners. Islahi rejects a literal inference that only the extremes are damned or saved. [13 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI(الزمخشري); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,صاحب الكشف); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI+]
- 92:17/q08/p2 The earlier ṣaly is exceptionally severe burning, so other sinners may enter or receive lesser punishment. — Ibn al-Munir's lexical report likens ṣaly to placing an animal between layers of coals. Al-Alusi accepts this resolution; al-Baydawi and al-Biqa'i distinguish sinful believers' temporary entry from the wretched unbeliever's abiding fate. [7 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI(ابن المنير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)]
- 92:17/q08/p3 Descriptive restriction has no valid converse: less pious people are not thereby condemned. — Al-Razi says al-Qadi himself rejects such implication and that it would wrongly send children and the insane to Hell. Al-Alusi gives this possible reply but finds the earlier 'only the most wretched' harder. Al-Jishumi says children and the insane are outside the warned audience. Islahi says the inference would equally and wrongly restrict salvation to only the most pious. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:17/q08/p4 The two elatives name broad classes of wretched unbelievers and pious believers. — Abu Ubayda, quoted by al-Nasafi, and the generic readings use afʿal as an ordinary descriptor. Al-Alusi objects that this alone leaves sinful believers, poor pious people, children and the mentally ill difficult to place. [3 notes: NASAFI(أبو عبيدة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 92:17/q08/p5 The indefinite 'a Fire' can denote one level of Hell rather than every level. — Al-Qadi proposes this; al-Zamakhshari objects that the following restriction still seems to exclude all but the most pious from that level. Al-Alusi also reports the broader apparent tension. [3 notes: ALUSI(القاضي); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي)]
- 92:17/q08/p6 If sinners are not the most pious, the second restriction implies that they enter Hell. — Al-Qadi argues this from the converse. Al-Razi rejects the inference, citing children and the insane and al-Qadi's own view of implication. [2 notes: RAZI(القاضي); RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:17/q08/p7 Only the most wretched enter Hell, so less-wretched people are spared. — Islahi reports this inference; Abu Hurayra's hadith quoted by al-Alusi says only the wretched enter and describes one who neither obeys God nor refrains from sin for God. Al-Alusi says Sunnis read such reports alongside evidence that grave sinners may be punished. Al-Nasafi rejects the Murji'ite restriction of entry to unbelievers. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some people); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); NASAFI-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 92:17/q08/p8 At Islam's beginning the contrast matched two actual groups: deniers and fully committed converts. — Ibn Ashur says the first converts did not commit major sins, so the promise supplies the converse to the warning. Islahi reads the contrast as Messenger rejecters and responsive believers rather than degrees within either group; a conclusive warning leaves rejecters no excuse. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 92:17/q09 What qualities make this person God-fearing?  [ٱلْأَتْقَى · interpretation]
turns on: Which traits the adjacent verses and reported exemplary acts attach to the title.
- 92:17/q09/p1 He avoids idolatry and sins, and purifies himself through sincere giving for God. — Al-Biqa'i speaks of a firm knowing faculty and peak piety; al-Wahidi reports Muqatil's sincere affirmation of God's oneness. Ibn Kathir calls him pure. Islahi connects heedfulness, judgment and cleansing wealth; Kuran Yolu traces taqwā to protection from danger and explains that giving seeks God's approval without repayment. The Study Quran notes charity purifies giver and wealth, with tazakkā related to zakāh. Corpus Coranicum calls the Lord's face a biblical metaphor. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM; RAZI-FULL]
- 92:17/q09/p2 He gives without settling a debt or repaying a favor, seeking God and His Messenger alone. — Tustari recounts Abu Bakr's answer about what he retained; Ibn Ata says the highest pious abandon the transient world. Al-Razi says a repayment earns no reward, whereas giving in response to God's command does. The Bilal reports say revelation answered a claim of prior favor. [7 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SULAMI(ابن عطاء); RAZI-FULL; THALABI(سعيد بن المسيب); BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:17/q09/p3 He is the fortunate counterpart to the most wretched. — Qutb places the two figures against one another, drawing on verses 15–16. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 92:17/q10 What inward state does the most God-fearing reach?  [ٱلْأَتْقَى · inward]
turns on: Whether the piety is expressed as renunciation, union, or posthumous light.
- 92:17/q10/p1 The most pious abandons the transient world for God and His Messenger. — Tustari and Ibn Ata cite Abu Bakr's giving and his answer that he kept God and the Messenger; Ibn Ata calls ordinary pious people ascetics and the highest those who abandon the world entirely. [3 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 92:17/q10/p2 The absolutely God-fearing rejects all otherness and is absorbed in unity, escaping every level of fire. — Qashani contrasts this with stopping at attributes: such a person may be forgiven but remains veiled from the Essence's joy. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 92:17/q10/p3 Good deeds, including night prayer, bring light after death. — Al-Biqa'i reports this as an interpretation of spiritual light becoming sensory light. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 92:17/q11 How does the wording connect this verse with the surrounding passage and sura?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding verses or earlier Qur'anic wording the interpreter takes as the link.
- 92:17/q11/p1 It completes the sura's contrast of conduct and recompense for the saved and damned. — Bint al-Shati links the opening oaths over night, day and creation to divergent efforts and final fates. Corpus Coranicum describes two antitheses, a reversal of the earlier saved/damned order, the tie between ittaqā in verse 5 and atqā here, and the final stanza's inverted order of conduct and result. Islahi says verses 15–21 spell out both traits and rewards; Kuran Yolu notes the transition from the Fire warning to sincere giving and protection. Al-Alusi says the sura details the preceding sura's success and failure. [7 notes: BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]
- 92:17/q11/p2 The phrase parallels 87:11 and recalls taqwā or ittaqā in verse 5. — Corpus Coranicum gives both the external parallel and the internal word link. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:17/q11/p3 The promise that Abu Bakr will be pleased echoes the promise that God's gift satisfies the Prophet. — Al-Biqa'i uses this connection to describe Abu Bakr as closest in the community to the Prophet's station. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 92:17/q12 What rhetorical contrast does al-atqā make with al-ashqā?  [ٱلْأَتْقَى · rhetoric]
- 92:17/q12/p1 The two labels directly oppose the damned and saved, with a near rhyme or near paronomasia. — Ibn Ashur names jinās muḍāriʿ and says the later relative clauses point to deeds causing each verdict. Corpus Coranicum says the moral opposition is named explicitly. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:17/q13 What other grammatical observations occur in the verse's collected notes?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Which of two separate general grammatical observations is being made.
- 92:17/q13/p1 A nominal circumstantial clause may omit wāw when its fronted predicate makes it closely resemble a single-object construction. — Al-Jurjani analyzes wajadtuhu ḥāḍirāhu al-jūd wa-l-karam: the verb takes one object, and fronting ḥāḍirāhu makes omission of wāw sound better than the reverse order. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 92:17/q13/p2 Adjective agreement normally follows the preceding word's form; agreement by meaning is rarer. — Al-Zarkashi's Burhan states the form-versus-meaning rule for adjectives and similar constructions. [1 notes: BURHAN]
