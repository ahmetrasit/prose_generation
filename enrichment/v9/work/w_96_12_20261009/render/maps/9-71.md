# 9:71: 23 questions

## 9:71/q01 Who are the believing men and women?  [وَٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَٱلْمُؤْمِنَٰتُ · referent]
turns on: Whether faith is identified by assent, security from punishment, or persistent supplication.
- 9:71/q01/p1 Those who affirm God, His Messenger, and the revelation. — Muqātil includes monotheism and the Companions, naming ʿAlī; al-Ṭabarī includes the signs of God's Book. [2 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL]
- 9:71/q01/p2 Those secure from God's punishment. — Al-Naḍr b. Shumayl supplies this gloss in Ibn Abī Ḥātim. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النضر بن شميل)]
- 9:71/q01/p3 Those who continue beseeching God until He answers. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Qatāda's description of their night and day prayer. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]

## 9:71/q02 Why are both believing men and women named?  [وَٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَٱلْمُؤْمِنَٰتُ · rhetoric]
- 9:71/q02/p1 Both sexes share the described traits and spiritual and social responsibilities. — Kur'an Yolu compares 9:67; Study Quran cites 33:35 and includes moral authority and protection. Burhān observes that the plural form here can denote abundance although conventionally associated with a small number. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN]

## 9:71/q03 What does mutual awliyāʾ mean?  [بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَآءُ بَعْضٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether awliyāʾ highlights mutual support, affection, lexical nearness, or God-directed guardianship; these can overlap.
- 9:71/q03/p1 Believers are mutually loyal allies who help, protect, and strengthen one another. — Muqātil and al-Dāmghānī specify religion; al-Ṭabarī says support. Abū ʿAlī says they share one word rather than disavow one another. Al-Jishumī derives muwālāh from nearness through support. Al-Baghawī adds unity of purpose; al-Ṭabrisī says even a wife helps and protects her traveling husband. Al-Qushayrī includes counsel, worship, companionship, and renouncing self-interest for God. Abū ʿUthmān cites building and body hadiths; Ibn Kathīr calls both sound and describes interlocking fingers and shared fever and sleeplessness. Al-Biqāʿī uses the body image and says loyalty maintains victory over corrupters. Bursevī includes worldly aid and spiritual training; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says different sexes and numbers form one responsible community. A Prophetic report names the Emigrants and Helpers, Quraysh's freedmen, and Thaqīf as allies in both worlds. Elmalılı says protectors; Asad and Study Quran give friends/protectors or allies. Study Quran cites 5:51, 8:72, 45:19; Kur'an Yolu points to 2:257, 4:2, 4:138–40, 4:144, 6:14, 8:72–73. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 9:71/q03/p2 They are united in love, compassion, and brotherhood for God's sake. — Abū Bakr al-Warrāq calls believers' friendship instinctive. Ibn ʿAbbās says mercy, love, and brotherhood for God. Al-Qurṭubī stresses united hearts; al-Nasafī combines compassion with support. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 9:71/q03/p3 The primary sense is nearness or closeness. — Asad prefers this rendering from the contrast with hypocrites in 9:67; al-Rāzī gives nearness as the root of wilāya and contrasts enmity, derived from crossing away; Abū ʿAlī explains that opposed root. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); ASAD-NOTES+]
- 9:71/q03/p4 The guardianship is specifically for God and includes mutual prayer and intercession. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts hypocrites' lack of guardianship, intercession, and prayer for each other; Abū Ḥayyān reports him. Al-Qushayrī likewise describes companionship and love for God. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); QUSHAYRI]
- 9:71/q03/p5 Loyalty includes refraining from harm. — Al-Tustarī gives this practical meaning. [1 notes: TUSTARI]

## 9:71/q04 What ruling is drawn from mutual guardianship?  [بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَآءُ بَعْضٍۢ · law]
- 9:71/q04/p1 In the reported circumstance, a believer may accompany a Muslim woman who has no maḥram. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh permits Ṣafwān to transport a woman he knows and calls a believer a maḥram for a believing woman, reciting this verse. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes al-ʿAyyāshī's parallel transmission. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 9:71/q05 What does enjoining al-maʿrūf call people to?  [يَأْمُرُونَ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the good is primarily faith, the full range of approved acts, or following the Prophet.
- 9:71/q05/p1 It calls to faith, monotheism, Islam, and worship. — Muqātil names faith in Muḥammad; al-Ṭabarī names faith in God, Messenger, and revelation. Abū al-ʿĀliya says it calls from polytheism to Islam throughout the Quran, transmitted by al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Abū Ḥayyān. Ibn ʿAbbās names the confession of God's oneness. Al-Jishumī reports Abū al-ʿĀliya and al-Aṣamm; al-Dāmghānī cites 9:112 and 31:17, and Ibn al-Jawzī groups 9:112. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī add what follows from monotheism. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(أبو العالية والأصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية)]
- 9:71/q05/p2 It calls to all recognized good, including faith, obedience, and legally or rationally approved acts. — Muqātil b. Ḥayyān names obedience. Al-Jishumī includes faith and acts of obedience because reason and revelation recognize them; al-Ṭabrisī includes what God obliges or recommends. Al-Biqāʿī says all permitted by revealed law. Bursevī adds seeking God, the real good, citing 'I loved to be known.' Ibn al-Jawzī treats enjoining and forbidding as reform and cites Hūd's reformers in 11:117. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk also includes spending in God's cause. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; DURR-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 9:71/q05/p3 It means following the Prophet. — Al-Dāmghānī gives this second gloss, paired with rejecting his denial, citing 3:113. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 9:71/q06 What does forbidding al-munkar prohibit?  [وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the forbidden wrong is identified chiefly as shirk, all prohibited conduct, or denial of the Prophet.
- 9:71/q06/p1 It forbids association with God and worship of idols or devils. — Ibn ʿAbbās names shirk; Abū al-ʿĀliya names idols and devils across several transmissions. Al-Dāmghānī cites 9:112 and 31:17; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī extend the explanation to what follows from idol worship. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(أبو العالية والأصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية)]
- 9:71/q06/p2 It forbids all disobedience, sins, and acts rejected by reason or divine law. — Muqātil b. Ḥayyān names disobedience; al-Baghawī adds what Islamic law does not recognize; al-Ṭabrisī includes prohibited and discouraged acts. Ibn al-Jawzī derives munkar from nakira, lack of recognition, and notes tanakkara as change from a pleasant state. Al-Biqāʿī adds impartiality and Bursevī says sins sever one from God. [11 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 9:71/q06/p3 It can mean denial of the Prophet. — Al-Dāmghānī cites 3:113 and Ibn al-Jawzī cites 3:114 for this sense. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 9:71/q07 What duty of enjoining and forbidding follows from the verse?  [يَأْمُرُونَ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ · law]
- 9:71/q07/p1 Believers must enjoin right and forbid wrong. — Al-Ṭabrisī infers an individual duty because all believers are described without restriction. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk calls it imposed by God. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran cross-reference 3:104. Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends it throughout the community by mutual guardianship. Ibn al-Jawzī links the pair to reform and 11:117. [6 notes: TABRISI; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 9:71/q08 How is yaʾmurūna related syntactically to the alliance clause?  [يَأْمُرُونَ · grammar]
- 9:71/q08/p1 It has no syntactic position and explains the preceding relation. — Al-Samīn compares the parallel clause in 9:67. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 9:71/q09 What reports illustrate good and evil here?  [يَأْمُرُونَ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different images of good and evil in the next life.
- 9:71/q09/p1 People of good or evil here remain of that kind in the next life. — Al-Durr transmits this from the Prophet via Salmān and in a mursal version via Abū ʿUthmān. Saʿīd b. al-Musayyib repeats it with a report praising considerate treatment after faith and consultation. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن المسيب)]
- 9:71/q09/p2 Good and evil rise as two beings at Judgment. — Al-Durr transmits Abū Mūsā's Prophetic report: good gives glad tidings and evil draws its people to itself. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 9:71/q10 What prayer is established?  [وَيُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase names the prescribed five, voluntary devotion, or an inward state.
- 9:71/q10/p1 The prescribed five prayers are duly performed. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the five in four transmissions; Muqātil says complete them. Al-Biqāʿī includes pillars, conditions, limits, and attentive reliance in hardship; al-Ṭabrisī stresses persistence. Al-Jishumī contrasts hypocritical show; Kur'an Yolu contrasts reluctant hypocrites with sincere, eager believers. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:71/q10/p2 Voluntary prayer would be more emphatic praise, implying the obligations are fulfilled. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits Ibn ʿAṭiyya's preferred reasoning. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)+]
- 9:71/q10/p3 Its inward realization is constant remembrance with a vigilant heart. — Bursevī says trade cannot distract the people of unveiling and hearts, unlike those who forget God. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 9:71/q11 Why is prayer separately named among the good acts?  [وَيُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the separate mention emphasizes prayer's rank or its pairing with zakat.
- 9:71/q11/p1 It specially honors the greatest form of enjoining good. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls prayer the greatest such form; al-Ālūsī adds the contrast with hypocrites' forgetting God. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:71/q11/p2 Prayer with zakat expresses worship toward God and care for people. — Ibn Kathīr gives this pair; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls them the law's two pillars, prayer linking God and creation and zakat linking people socially. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 9:71/q12 What does giving zakat entail?  [وَيُؤْتُونَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether only the obligatory due is named or broader generosity is praised.
- 9:71/q12/p1 Believers pay the obligatory alms to their rightful recipients. — Al-Ṭabarī names the recipients; Ibn ʿAbbās pairs obligatory zakat with the five prescribed prayers. Al-Ṭabrisī says wealth is distributed as God directs. Al-Jishumī and al-Biqāʿī contrast hypocrites' stinginess; the latter names support for the poor. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL]
- 9:71/q12/p2 The praise includes gifts beyond the obligatory due. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says voluntary alms more strongly imply payment of the obligation. Bursevī urges giving surplus beyond sufficiency to purify worldly attachment; Kur'an Yolu says believers regret being unable to give more. Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes zakat the social pillar alongside prayer. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]

## 9:71/q13 What is obedience to God and His Messenger?  [وَيُطِيعُونَ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the two objects express comprehensive obedience or distinguish divine law and the Messenger's governing authority.
- 9:71/q13/p1 It is carrying out commands and avoiding prohibitions in all matters. — ʿAṭāʾ specifies Book and Sunna; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes recommended acts. Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes God's obligations and the Messenger's prescriptions. Al-Biqāʿī adds gracious conduct; Kur'an Yolu includes readiness to fight with life and wealth against hypocrites' excuses and mockery. Al-Ālūsī compares their complete rebellion, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says obedience entails attention rather than forgetting God. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:71/q13/p2 God's obedience encompasses revealed law; the Messenger's includes authoritative governance and reform. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists campaigns, judgments, and penalties; in another respect obeying revealed law is included in following the Messenger who proclaims fundamentals and branches. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 9:71/q14 Whom does ulāʾika indicate, and why use the distant form?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · rhetoric]
- 9:71/q14/p1 It indicates the described believers and grounds mercy in their listed qualities. — Al-Ālūsī says the distant form is emphatic; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it signals their worthiness for what follows. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 9:71/q15 What mercy is promised?  [سَيَرْحَمُهُمُ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mercy is explained by final salvation or aid at specified trials.
- 9:71/q15/p1 God saves them from punishment and grants Paradise and lasting reward. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Jishumī contrast hypocrites' Hell; al-Ṭabrisī specifies the afterlife; Kur'an Yolu adds divine pleasure. Bursevī includes worldly support and victory, rescue from distance, and nearness to God. Al-Biqāʿī describes gracious treatment after forgiveness and says none reaches the end of the difficult road without it. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:71/q15/p2 Mercy helps believers through five decisive trials. — Bursevī reports inward interpreters naming death, grave, record, balance, and standing before God. An ungraded hadith there links the five prayers to ease at death, grave, questioning by Munkar and Nakīr, balance, and bridge; the lists differ. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,بعض أهل الإشارة)]

## 9:71/q16 What does the future sīn add to the mercy promise?  [سَيَرْحَمُهُمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether certainty is in the particle or the context, and what delay adds.
- 9:71/q16/p1 It strengthens the certainty and force of fulfillment even if delayed. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares delayed threats and cites 19:96, 93:5, and 4:152. Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, al-Itqān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Ālūsī give this force. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares qad with a past verb. Burhān records replies based on delay in glad tidings and effective double reporting; it compares emphatic nūn to repeating a verb twice or thrice. Al-Taqrīb contrasts affirmative sa- with negative lan. Ibn Qāsim objects to Ibn Ḥajar's dismissal of the transmitted interpretation. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(الزمخشري); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,صاحب التقريب); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن قاسم)+; BURHAN-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر)-]
- 9:71/q16/p2 Its future form lets souls anticipate mercy while fulfillment is guaranteed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī mention hope and bounty. Al-Samīn says special mercy is reserved for the Hereafter. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 9:71/q16/p3 Certainty belongs to context, not the particle itself. — Burhān reports the objection that the verb indicates occurrence and sīn does not signal inevitability; one reply allows that context may do the work. Ibn Ḥajar ties the opposed reading to al-Zamakhsharī's doctrine of necessary reward; Ibn Qāsim contests his argument. [3 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر)+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن قاسم)-]

## 9:71/q17 What do ʿAzīz and Ḥakīm mean?  [عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
- 9:71/q17/p1 God is irresistible in rewarding, punishing, aiding, and showing mercy, and wise in assigning each thing its place. — Muqātil names dominion and command; al-Ṭabarī says none can ward off punishment. Al-Jishumī, al-Kashshāf, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, and al-Ālūsī stress rightful distribution. Ibn Kathīr says God honors obedient believers and cites 63:8; Bursevī says He honors allies and subdues enemies. Al-Biqāʿī says none can undo the decree; Ibn ʿĀshūr says wisdom awards the deserving; Ṭabāṭabāʾī denies obstruction, weakness, defect, or arbitrariness. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 9:71/q18 Why end the mercy promise with 'Mighty, Wise'?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 9:71/q18/p1 God's unrestricted might and fitting wisdom ground the mercy he promises. — Al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī make the link. Burhān gives the close as a parallel to an earlier explanation of attributes. Al-Itqān says mercy and pleas for pardon can fittingly end so. Ṭabāṭabāʾī elsewhere says all honor is God's and reverence for Messenger, believers, and signs is legitimate when it leads to Him. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 9:71/q19 How does the portrayal answer the neighboring portrayal of hypocrites?  [* · links]
- 9:71/q19/p1 Believers' alliance, five practices, and mercy reverse hypocrites' divided hearts, contrary acts, and threatened fate. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī say hypocrites share only label or judgment. Al-Rāzī contrasts their imitation, appetite, and habit with believers' reasoning and divine guidance; Abū Ḥayyān reports this and al-Biqāʿī adds individual proof. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the turn invites faith; al-Biqāʿī says hope invites repentance. Al-Rāzī lists right, wrong, prayer, zakat, and obedience as five contrary marks; Abū Ḥayyān adds fighting. Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr match prayer to forgetting God, zakat to withheld hands, obedience to rebellion or forgetting, and mercy to divine forgetting; Ṭabāṭabāʾī mirrors the last. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause identifies recipients of pardon. Burhān compares 'some from some' with 3:34 and 2:166; al-Itqān compares allies in 8:73 and divided hearts in 59:14. Kur'an Yolu relates 9:67–68 to 9:71–72. [43 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي,ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN]

## 9:71/q20 What inward life accompanies these virtues?  [* · inward]
- 9:71/q20/p1 Genuine faith serves others, follows revelation, repents, and purifies the heart. — Al-Tustarī likens believers to earth that serves God's creatures and gives seven foundations: Book, Messenger, lawful food, non-harm, avoiding sin, repentance, and fulfilling rights. Najmiyya says hypocrites may pray and give outwardly while following ego and worldly desire. Bursevī's Persian couplet contrasts worldly kingship with a poor person's secure kingdom and urges reflection through death and burial. [4 notes: TUSTARI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 9:71/q21 What earlier form of alliance is reported abrogated?  [بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَآءُ بَعْضٍۢ · sciences]
- 9:71/q21/p1 Inheritance-based allegiance between Emigrants and Helpers was replaced by kinship inheritance. — Al-Ṭabarī reports Mujāhid's account of al-Anfāl's last three verses and its final kinship verse, 8:75. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 9:71/q22 What lexical notes accompany the adjacent promise in 9:72?  [* · meaning]
- 9:71/q22/p1 ʿAdn means lasting residence; riḍwān is a verbal noun; jihād derives from effort in difficulty. — Al-Ṭabrisī cites al-Aʿshā's line for ʿadana, remaining or settling; he derives riḍwān from raḍiya and jihād from jahd. [2 notes: TABRISI]

## 9:71/q23 What verbal usage is noted in an accompanying comparison?  [* · grammar]
- 9:71/q23/p1 The Quran uses atā for the relevant present meaning and no present form of jāʾa. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī therefore excludes present forms from that comparison. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
