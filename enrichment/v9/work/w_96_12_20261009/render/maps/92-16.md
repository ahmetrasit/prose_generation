# 92:16: 13 questions

## 92:16/q01 What can kadhdhaba mean here?  [كَذَّبَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether takdhib is confined to explicit rejection or also names failure in conduct.
- 92:16/q01/p1 It is religious denial or rejection of the truth. — Bint al-Shati defines ordinary kadhb as a statement at odds with reality or one's inward state, or a disappointed expectation; she says Qur'anic takdhib denotes denial of God, His signs, blessings, revelation, messengers, meeting, or the Last Day. Corpus Coranicum traces the term through early Meccan punishment legends, warnings, vice lists and judgment formulas, with possible earlier psalmic or Syriac kaddeb usage for lying, deception, treachery and religious denial; a Qur'anic coinage is therefore unnecessary. [4 notes: BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:16/q01/p2 It can mean failing to obey, without spoken denial. — Al-Farra and grammarians reported by al-Tabari liken it to a fighter who retreats after facing the enemy. Abu Tharwan's praise of Banu Numayr means that they fight truly and do not retreat. Al-Farra, al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi compare 56:2: the Event has no 'falsehood' because it certainly occurs. Al-Nahhas cites another Arab idiom meaning someone persisted without falling short. [15 notes: FARRA(أبو ثروان); TAB(بعض أهل العربية); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); NAHHAS; QURTUBI(أبو ثروان,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ثروان,الفراء)]

## 92:16/q02 What did the person deny?  [كَذَّبَ · referent]
turns on: The notes variously supply the Messenger and revelation, God's signs or Book, and truth or the divine call as the implicit object.
- 92:16/q02/p1 He denied the Messenger, his message, or the Qur'an he brought. — Abu Umama names what Muhammad brought; Muqatil names the Qur'an. Al-Mawardi gives denial of the Messenger as an alternative to Qatada's Book. Al-Wahidi combines the Messenger and Qur'an. Ibn Abbas's Umayya report names Muhammad and earlier prophets; al-Nasafi says the messengers. Ibn Ashur says the early audience recognized denial of the Messenger and turning from the Qur'an. [19 notes: MUJAHID(أبو أمامة); MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو أمامة); MAWARDI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR(أبو أمامة)]
- 92:16/q02/p2 He denied God's Book or signs. — Qatada says God's Book. Al-Tabari says God's signs, which the person failed to affirm; al-Razi also names God's signs. [8 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI(قتادة); QURTUBI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); RAZI]
- 92:16/q02/p3 He denied truth, guidance, God, or the true call. — Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi say truth; Qashani says God, with stubbornness and shirk; Elmalili says God's guidance; Qutb says the call and promised guidance; Tabatabai says the true call. Bint al-Shati describes the denier without a narrower object. [10 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]

## 92:16/q03 What does tawallā mean here, and from what does he turn?  [وَتَوَلَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The implicit object is variously faith or revelation, obedience, and truth or its proofs.
- 92:16/q03/p1 He turns away from faith, the Messenger, or the Qur'an. — Abu Umama says he turns away from Muhammad; Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi say faith. Ibn Ashur says the Qur'an. Bint al-Shati gives the literal sense of turning away or retreating and cites parallel pairings with disbelief or denial at 88:22-23, 75:31-32, 20:48 and 70:15-16. [15 notes: MUJAHID(أبو أمامة); MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو أمامة); MAWARDI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 92:16/q03/p2 He turns away from obedience or practical action. — Qatada specifies obedience to God, al-Mawardi alternatively obedience to the Messenger. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi likewise say obedience. Al-Razi says obedience and Ibn Kathir explains refusal to act with one's limbs. Al-Biqai adds refusal to give wealth for self-purification. [13 notes: MAWARDI(قتادة); QURTUBI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:16/q03/p3 He turns away from truth, guidance, or examining the proofs. — Al-Biqai calls this arrogant and stubborn rejection of truth; Elmalili and Qutb emphasize guidance; Tabatabai calls it turning from the true call. Al-Razi argues that every unbeliever turns away from examining the prophet's proofs, so all fit the wording. [9 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]

## 92:16/q04 Who is the person described by 'the one who denied and turned away'?  [ٱلَّذِى كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the relative description marks a general class, disobedient refusers, or particular Meccan opponents.
- 92:16/q04/p1 The description identifies an unbeliever or polytheist as the wretched person. — Al-Hasan identifies the most wretched as a polytheist, while Mu'adh adds the phrase 'who denied and turned away.' Ibn Atiyya says the denial marks the preceding ashqa as an unbeliever. Al-Razi, al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi, al-Biqai, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur and Tabatabai read the category as unbelievers. Tabatabai says it is any unbeliever answering the true call this way, rather than one individual; Elmalili distinguishes a sinner who turns away without denial. [15 notes: TAB(الحسن); WAHIDI-QT; IBNATIYYA; RAZI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; TABATABAI+]
- 92:16/q04/p2 It includes whoever refuses God or the Messenger through disobedience. — Abu Hurayra's and Abu Umama's reports explain the person as a refuser of Paradise, one who flees God. Prophetic reports define refusal as disobedience and the wretch as one who neither obeys God nor abstains from sin for Him. Al-Farra's figurative sense allows practical failure to count as denial. [18 notes: TAB(أبو هريرة); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNABIHATIM(أبو أمامة); IBNKATHIR(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(أبو أمامة,أبو هريرة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو أمامة الباهلي عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,أبو هريرة,أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 92:16/q04/p3 The wretched one was Umayya ibn Khalaf and others like him. — Ibn Abbas says the passage was revealed about Umayya and others who denied Muhammad and earlier prophets; the report through al-Dahhak likewise names Umayya and those like him. Al-Zamakhshari offers Abu Jahl or Umayya as the eminent unbeliever balanced against Abu Bakr. [4 notes: RAZI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن الضحاك); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 92:16/q04/p4 It identifies whoever was wretched in God's prior knowledge. — Al-Farra is reported by al-Qurtubi as locating the person's wretchedness in God's knowledge. [1 notes: QURTUBI(الفراء)]

## 92:16/q05 How do denial and turning away work together?  [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · interpretation]
- 92:16/q05/p1 Denial is inward rejection, and turning away is failure in outward action. — Ibn Kathir assigns denial to the heart and refusal of action to the limbs. Al-Biqai assigns denial to the knowing faculty and arrogant turning away to the acting faculty, including refusal to give purifying wealth. Corpus Coranicum notes the established pairing in 96:13, 79:21-22 and 75:32; 88:23 pairs turning away with kafara instead. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:16/q06 How does the relative clause explain the warning?  [ٱلَّذِى كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Ibn Ashur gives alternative syntactic attachments for the preceding exclusion clause.
- 92:16/q06/p1 It specifies the wretched people and shows that their acts cause their outcome. — Ibn Ashur says the clause identifies people who knew they had denied the Messenger and turned from the Qur'an, and that the relative clauses for the wretched and pious point to the actions causing their respective fates. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:16/q06/p2 The preceding exclusion clause can be another descriptor or a circumstantial clause. — Ibn Ashur says this clause concerns the particular Fire prepared for disbelievers, comparing 2:24, and that 92:17 confirms it. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:16/q07 What force does 'the most wretched' have in identifying this person?  [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether af'al is noncomparative, emphatically superlative, or a class description grounded in eternal misery.
- 92:16/q07/p1 Ashqa means 'wretched' without comparative force. — Abu Ubayda and Ibn Atiyya cite Tarafa's awhad for Arabic af'al used as fa'il. Ibn al-Jawzi gives the same proof; al-Razi compares 'not the one-and-only.' Al-Samin says this reading allows the Fire and its avoidance to include less extreme cases. Al-Burhan notes that adjectives normally agree in form with their antecedents, while agreement by meaning is rarer. [7 notes: MAJAZ; IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN]
- 92:16/q07/p2 It retains a superlative or comparative force for an eminent unbeliever. — Al-Zamakhshari balances an eminent unbeliever, Abu Jahl or Umayya, against Abu Bakr and calls the exclusive wording hyperbole as though Fire and Paradise were created for the two. Bint al-Shati calls the definite unqualified ashqa absolutely most wretched. She distinguishes the Thamud construct ashqaha, limited to their own most wretched member, and compares definite ashqa in al-A'la and al-Layl; she traces shaqa' as hardship, then Qur'anic misguidance and disobedience through 20:123, 23:106, 11:105-106, 20:117 and Maryam. Al-Raghib says misery entails toil but not all toil is misery. Islahi says rejected messengers after conclusive warning have no excuse, so are the most wretched. [8 notes: BINTSHATI(الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 92:16/q07/p3 It names any unbeliever, since eternal misery outweighs temporary worldly suffering. — Tabatabai says ashqa does not single out the most wretched human being; the relative description marks any unbeliever who denies the true call and turns away. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 92:16/q08 Whom does the Fire's restriction include or exclude?  [ٱلَّذِى كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether the exclusion is absolute, limited to a particular Fire or duration, figurative, or supplied with omitted entrants; whether the traits cover all unbelievers.
- 92:16/q08/p1 Only unbelievers enter Hell or are threatened with its punishment. — The Murji'ites infer exclusivity from the warning's restriction to the denier. Al-Qadi points out that a literal restriction would even exclude an unbeliever who had not denied and turned away, and says it appears to assure sinful believers that their sin cannot harm them. Jishumi and al-Zajjaj reject the exclusionary inference. [3 notes: RAZI(المرجئة); QURTUBI(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); JISHUMI-; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)-; QURTUBI(أبو إسحاق الزجاج)-; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج)-]
- 92:16/q08/p2 The verse concerns one particular Fire or level, so other sinners may face punishment. — Abu Ishaq al-Zajjaj says Hell has ranks; al-Qurtubi mentions the hypocrites' lowest level and other threatened punishments. Al-Wahidi likewise distinguishes levels. Ibn Ashur compares the specific Fire prepared for unbelievers at 2:24 and points to 92:17. Al-Zajjaj and al-Qurtubi cite 4:48: discretionary forgiveness of sins short of shirk would be idle if all such sinners were exempt. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); RAZI(المرجئة)-]
- 92:16/q08/p3 The exclusion is about permanent burning; sinful believers may enter but do not remain. — Al-Wahidi says sinful non-polytheists are omitted in abbreviated wording and yusliha denotes enduring heat, not necessarily eternity; abiding applies to the denying polytheist. Al-Nasafi and al-Biqai explicitly limit eternal exposure to the unbeliever. Elmalili says a sinner who turns away without denial may enter but does not remain. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 92:16/q08/p4 The stated traits identify those who approach the Fire, without excluding other sinners. — Abu Muslim's first explanation of 'none enters it but the most wretched' is that the stated traits mark its intended subject. Jishumi prefers this first explanation and objects that absolute exclusion contradicts consensus, invites sin and conflicts with threats to the miser and sinner. Al-Razi counters that blame, divine anger and loss of honor deter sin, and God can punish otherwise. [6 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI+]
- 92:16/q08/p5 The Fire is named for the majority or its intended occupants. — Abu Muslim offers this as a way to read the restriction without excluding every other entrant. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]
- 92:16/q08/p6 The wording names those most deserving of the Fire. — Abu Muslim offers this alternative; al-Razi rejects reducing ashqa to 'more deserving' because it abandons the apparent meaning without evidence. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:16/q08/p7 An ellipsis includes the most wretched and sinners like him. — Jishumi reports the supplied phrase 'and whoever is like him among the sinners.' Al-Wahidi also reports an abbreviated mention of sinful non-polytheists, adding that entry need not mean eternal abode. [2 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 92:16/q08/p8 Not every unbeliever must have performed each listed act. — Jishumi reports a view that some unbelievers enter without each specified denial, miserliness and turning away; he prefers Abu Muslim's first explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI; JISHUMI-]
- 92:16/q08/p9 All unbelievers do fit the paired description. — Al-Razi says every unbeliever denies the prophet's claim and turns from examining its proofs; this answers al-Qadi's hypothetical unbeliever without those traits. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI(القاضي)-]
- 92:16/q08/p10 The apparent restriction could encourage or virtually permit sin. — Al-Qadi says a sinful believer might infer that sin cannot harm one who neither denied nor turned away. Jishumi raises the same concern. Al-Razi rejects it: present disgrace, divine anger, withheld honor and reward, and other forms of punishment still deter. [3 notes: RAZI(القاضي); JISHUMI; RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-]

## 92:16/q09 What do the transmitted sayings say about the denier's fate?  [ٱلَّذِى كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · reports]
turns on: The reports give complementary formulations of refusal of Paradise and qualification for the Fire.
- 92:16/q09/p1 Everyone enters Paradise except one who refuses; the denier who turns away is the refuser. — Abu Hurayra's report identifies the refuser by reciting this description. Versions through Makhul and Abu Umama say the community enters Paradise except one who flees God like a bad camel from its owner; one calls it the gentlest saying heard. Prophetic versions explain that obedience to the Prophet leads to Paradise and disobedience is refusal. [17 notes: TAB(أبو هريرة); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNABIHATIM(أبو أمامة); QURTUBI(أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL(مكحول، أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(أبو أمامة,أبو هريرة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو أمامة الباهلي عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,أبو هريرة,أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 92:16/q09/p2 Only a wretch enters Hell: one who neither obeys God nor leaves a sin for His sake. — The Prophet's definition is transmitted through Abu Hurayra and Abu Umama in the cited collections; the report shifts attention from a named person to conduct. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة)]

## 92:16/q10 What occasion or incident is connected to this warning?  [ٱلَّذِى كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports specify the original occasion or serve as applications of a general early Meccan warning.
- 92:16/q10/p1 The passage concerns Umayya ibn Khalaf and others who denied Muhammad. — Ibn Abbas names Umayya and others who denied Muhammad and earlier prophets; the al-Dahhak route adds those like him. Al-Zamakhshari also offers Abu Jahl or Umayya as the outstanding unbeliever in a balanced contrast. [4 notes: RAZI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن الضحاك); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 92:16/q10/p2 The refusal can be mapped to a Medinan palm owner who mistreated poor children. — Study Quran reports that a palm owner snatched fallen dates from the children and rejected the Prophet's offer of a Paradise palm; another man bought the tree for forty palms and donated it. Al-Qurtubi Full calls the donor Abu al-Dahdah and the owner an Ansari, mapping ease to Paradise, hardship to Hell and death to loss of wealth. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(several commentators); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:16/q10/p3 The palm story is unlikely to be the occasion if the sura is early Meccan; the warning is general. — Study Quran reads the verses as generally condemning those who rejected Muhammad as prophet. Kur'an Yolu likewise says that even if verses 5-19 were revealed about Abu Bakr, the message and rulings remain general. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 92:16/q11 Why pair this description with the opposite figure in the sūra?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes explain the contrast both through omitted opposing acts and through the sūra's repeated, reversed arrangement.
- 92:16/q11/p1 The opposed conduct of denier and giver makes their opposite outcomes intelligible. — Al-Biqai says reciprocal omission lets denial imply its opposite in the pious person and later giving imply its omitted opposite for the wretched. Ibn Ashur says the relative clauses make each action the cause of its outcome. Al-Samarrai says the evil sequence moves from particular acts to a general description, while the good sequence rises from giving to utmost piety and purification; he also says the opposite figure gives his own wealth with maximal sincerity. Islahi calls it a contrast of messenger rejecters and believers rather than grades of virtue. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:16/q11/p2 The closing stanza reverses and mirrors the earlier antitheses. — Corpus Coranicum says the sūra first contrasts conduct and recompense in verses 5-13, then again in 14-21; the final stanza reverses the earlier saved/damned order, links ittaqa at 92:5 to atqa at 92:17, and presents warning or promise before the conduct rather than after it. It says 92:14 reveals the blazing Fire implicit in 92:10, comparable to 104:4-7 and 102:3-8. Islahi says verses 15-21 identify those cast in and shielded, with reward. [6 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 92:16/q12 How is the warning directed and received?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:16/q12/p1 It warns the rejecters of guidance, especially Quraysh, of a blazing Fire. — Al-Baghawi calls it a warning to Mecca. Islahi addresses persistent Quraysh deniers, setting them against those who spend to purify themselves; Kur'an Yolu says God guides through revelation and prophets and warns against Hell as the consequence of the wrong path. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:16/q12/p2 The warning moved Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz to tears in prayer. — Malik recounts that during Maghrib Umar cried so hard at the blazing Fire warning that he could not continue this sūra and recited another. [1 notes: QURTUBI(مالك)]

## 92:16/q13 What does the surrounding language say about burning and being spared?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:16/q13/p1 Salaa means roasting or punishment in blazing fire. — Bint al-Shati distinguishes it from warmth sought for comfort. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 92:16/q13/p2 The most pious is spared the Fire by God before reaching its harm. — Al-Samarrai says the passive verb attributes rescue to God, creator of people and Fire, rather than human power, even for the most pious giver; he cites 3:185 and 19:71-72. He distinguishes deliverance after entry into harm from sparing before it, citing 2:49, 19:72 and 21:88; the Qur'an uses deliverance for the pious in general and sparing for the singular superlative. He says the wording also leaves God's mercy open to other believers. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
