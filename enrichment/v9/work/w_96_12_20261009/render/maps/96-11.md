# 96:11: 20 questions

## 96:11/q01 How is أَرَءَيْتَ read?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · readings]
turns on: The pronunciation of the hamza or vowel length, without semantic difference.
- 96:11/q01/p1 Its hamza is eased by tashīl. — QIRAAT-ER says the softened hamza becomes a glide and leaves the meaning unchanged. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 96:11/q01/p2 Its hamza is omitted. — QIRAAT-ER identifies this as a canonical pronunciation, contrasting it with Ḥafṣ's retained hamza; the difference is phonological. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 96:11/q01/p3 The vowel before yāʾ is lengthened by madd. — QIRAAT-ER says the lengthening adds rhetorical weight but has the same meaning as the Ḥafṣ form. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 96:11/q02 What does the question أَرَءَيْتَ do?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the verb is an idiom of inquiry or is distributed between visual and cognitive senses.
- 96:11/q02/p1 It prompts the hearer to consider or tell what he thinks, rebuking and astonishing him. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an interrogative challenge rather than knowledge-based vision with two objects. Bursawī calls it inward seeing meaning 'tell me.' Al-Ālūsī derives this usage from seeing leading to knowledge and allows visual or mental seeing. Elmalılı says it seeks attention, admonition, rebuke or wonder rather than information. Al-Rāghib, cited by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, compares it to akhbirnī; she cites Meccan parallels in 107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23, 53:33 and the araytum series in 56:58, 63, 68, 71, plus 10:59, 26:75, 35:40, 39:38, 53:19, 46:4. [15 notes: IBNATIYYA; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الراغب); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:11/q02/p2 It can represent literal sight for the first question and inward cognition for the later questions. — Al-Ālūsī reports a parse in which the first occurrence needs no second object while the later two are cognitive; another view makes all three cognitive. [4 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]

## 96:11/q03 Who is addressed by أَرَءَيْتَ?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun sequence, contextual exclusion of the two men, or an intentional shift like a judge addressing opposing parties.
- 96:11/q03/p1 The Prophet is addressed throughout. — Al-Rāzī says matching the preceding and following addresses preserves order. Abū Ḥayyān says the pronoun sequence supports it; al-Baḥr prefers it. Al-Māwardī allows an address to the Prophet alone. [13 notes: MAWARDI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(البحر); ELMALILI]
- 96:11/q03/p2 The unbelieving forbider, Abu Jahl, is addressed in this question. — Al-Rāzī likens the shift to a judge or master speaking to two parties. Al-Qurṭubī asks whether Abu Jahl grasps the ruin of obstructing a guided Prophet. Abū Ḥayyān reports this reading as a direct question about whether he would still forbid such prayer; Ibn Kathīr addresses the challenge to the forbider. [17 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); IBNKATHIR; ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); ELMALILI]
- 96:11/q03/p3 The address is general, to the Prophet and community or to anyone able to understand. — Al-Māwardī includes the Prophet and community. Al-Ālūsī reports humanity or any understanding hearer, comparing 96:8; this contextual reading excludes both men as direct addressees. Elmalılı says the first address plainly includes the Prophet and extends to all. [8 notes: MAWARDI; ALUSI(البحر); ELMALILI]
- 96:11/q03/p4 The questions alternate between the disputants. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī compare a judge addressing litigants; one reading asks the unbeliever about guidance and then addresses the Prophet about denial. Elmalılı finds this turn-taking more meaningful. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); ELMALILI]

## 96:11/q04 How do the repeated أَرَءَيْتَ questions and conditional answers fit grammatically?  [أَرَءَيْتَ إِن كَانَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether raʾayta needs two syntactic objects, whether the conditional can serve as its second object, and whether an interrogative answer can lack fāʾ.
- 96:11/q04/p1 The first question takes the forbidding man as its first object; later objects and conditional answers are omitted, and the final question about God's sight supplies their sense. — Al-Ṭabarī calls the repetitions elliptical substitutions. Al-Bayḍāwī says the conditional serves as second object. Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, places the relative and conditional clauses as two objects and lets 'did he not know?' supply the answer without fāʾ. Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and al-ʿĀshūr recover omitted objects or answers from 96:14 or the prior prohibition; al-Ālūsī says this is semantic ellipsis, not tanāzuʿ because clauses cannot be pronominalized. Elmalılı favors omitted consequences and an independent continuation. [31 notes: AKHFASH; TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Keşşâf); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-; ALUSI(أبو حيان,الدماميني,الزمخشري)-]
- 96:11/q04/p2 The second object must be an interrogative or oath-like clause, so the proposed conditional object or interrogative answer without fāʾ is invalid. — Abū Ḥayyān cites 53:33–35, 19:77–78 and 56:58–59 for the interrogative object. He says an interrogative conditional answer requires fāʾ except in poetic necessity; al-Damāmīnī cites the difficulty with 6:47. Al-Ālūsī reports the objection but rejects the exclusive-object restriction as contrary to Sībawayh's preferred analysis; al-Khafājī answers that the condition and question jointly occupy the object and answer positions, literally or by implication, citing al-Raḍī and al-Damāmīnī. [17 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(أبو حيان,الدماميني,الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN+; ALUSI(الخفاجي,الرضي)-]
- 96:11/q04/p3 The conditional and interrogative together can occupy the required object and answer places. — Al-Khafājī allows literal occupancy or semantic implication; al-Raḍī and al-Damāmīnī relate it to Sībawayh's preferred analysis. Al-Ālūsī rejects restricting the second object to an interrogative alone. [6 notes: ALUSI(الخفاجي,الرضي); ALUSI+; ALUSI(أبو حيان)-]
- 96:11/q04/p4 The first seeing is visual and the later two intellectual. — On this reported parse, the visual first question needs no second object. The later conditionals fill the second-object place; the unjoined last question stresses a fact and ensuing threat. Al-Ālūsī objects to the fāʾ-less interrogative answer. [5 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]
- 96:11/q04/p5 All three seeings are intellectual; the third question stands independently and the first conditional's answer is inferred from the second. — Al-Ālūsī reports this parse; the last unjoined clause intensifies rebuke, asserts occurrence, and makes the threat explicit. He notes the fāʾ objection. [5 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]
- 96:11/q04/p6 The second and third questions repeat the first, with both conditions filling its second-object slot. — The second conditional is answered by 96:14; the first answer is omitted as understood from the later one, according to al-Ālūsī's reported analysis. [3 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]
- 96:11/q04/p7 The questions need no reconstructed second object: their rhetorical pattern itself directs attention to the warning. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ adduces Meccan arayta and araytum parallels (107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23, 53:33; 56:58, 63, 68, 71; 10:59, 26:75, 35:40, 39:38, 53:19, 46:4) and urges reading Qurʾānic style on its own terms rather than forcing technical reconstruction. [4 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 96:11/q05 Why is أَرَءَيْتَ repeated, and how do the questions build?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the repeated challenges form separate escalating censures, a single ironic concession, or a sequence of lost benefits.
- 96:11/q05/p1 Repetition reinforces the challenge and sharpens wonder at the contrast between guided worshipper and denier. — Al-Baghawī calls it reinforcement; al-Bayḍāwī an echo of the earlier question. Al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya see omitted completions gathered by the final threat; the latter says one concise conclusion suffices. Al-Biqāʿī says the listener asks about the forbider's state, not his identity. Al-ʿĀshūr says each detached hypothetical renews wonder about the same person. [21 notes: BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 96:11/q05/p2 The three stages rebuke prohibition first in general, then even under the forbider's claimed guidance, then under his actual denial. — Al-Ālūsī says the separated clauses each independently warrant condemnation and the sequence escalates in severity, with divine sight and punishment underscoring each stage. [6 notes: ALUSI]
- 96:11/q05/p3 The two conditionals make one taunting point: claimed guidance is impossible in one who forbids worship, and he actually denies. — The majority, reported by al-Ālūsī, see ironic concession and its reversal, not separate stages. His note says forbidding God's worship while directing people to idols cannot be guidance. [6 notes: ALUSI(الجمهور)]
- 96:11/q05/p4 The sequence moves from tyranny born of self-sufficiency, to lost guidance and piety, to earned punishment and a final threat. — ʿIṣām al-Dīn gives this progression; al-Ālūsī calls the overall elaboration far-fetched and leaves its ultimate intent to God. [5 notes: ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-]

## 96:11/q06 What does the particle إِن contribute?  [إِن · grammar]
- 96:11/q06/p1 It frames the servant's guidance as a hypothetical from the forbider's viewpoint. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says in is used when the condition is uncertain, matching the stance of the man trying to stop the servant; it heightens the wonder of forbidding even in that case. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:11/q07 Who is the unspoken subject of كَانَ?  [كَانَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun returns to the nearby forbider or shifts back to the praying servant.
- 96:11/q07/p1 The praying servant, Muhammad, is the one on guidance. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr take kāna and the following 'commanded' with the servant, then shift later pronouns to the forbider. Ibn ʿĀshūr says context resolves the shifts and cites a verse by ʿAbbās ibn Mirdās as analogy. Elmalılı allows either antecedent. [37 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عطية,الفراء); ALUSI(الفراء); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 96:11/q07/p2 The man forbidding prayer, Abu Jahl, is the unspoken subject. — Al-Ālūsī calls this the apparent antecedent in his principal parse. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī ask whether the forbider himself is on a sound course; al-Biqāʿī similarly tests the claimed guidance. Elmalılı allows this antecedent and the alternative servant antecedent. [26 notes: MAWARDI; KASHSHAF; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري,قيل); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(الجمهور); ELMALILI]

## 96:11/q08 What is the force of عَلَى in عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ?  [عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · meaning]
- 96:11/q08/p1 It pictures firm establishment in full guidance. — Al-Biqāʿī stresses settled, complete guidance. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats 'upon' as figurative elevation and cites the description of believers at 31:5. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:11/q09 What guidance is contemplated in عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ?  [ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance is predicated of the praying servant or ironically tested as the forbider's own claim.
- 96:11/q09/p1 The worshipper's guidance is upright, sound prayer and a straight course. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies sound prayer to God; Ibn Kathīr contrasts guided conduct with enjoining piety in speech. Al-Wāḥidī and others identify the worshipper as Muhammad. Corpus Coranicum points to its discussion of hudā at 93:7 without stating a further interpretation here. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 96:11/q09/p2 The prohibiter's claimed guidance is his supposed sound course in forbidding worship. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī test the propriety of forbidding God's worship. Al-Qāshānī and al-Zamakhsharī say the arrogant man can construe idol worship as piety, while from the Muslim view he denies truth. Al-Biqāʿī says he acknowledges the Lord yet falsely assigns Him a partner and permits prostration to idols. Al-Ālūsī's majority reading treats the claim ironically. [17 notes: KASHSHAF; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(الجمهور)]

## 96:11/q10 What consequence does the verse's conditional imply?  [إِن كَانَ عَلَى ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Which antecedent is chosen for kāna and whether the omitted result is a rebuke to the forbider, a missed alternative for him, God's sight, or astonishment.
- 96:11/q10/p1 Would the opponent still forbid someone on guidance, and is his opposition not ruinous? — Al-Qurṭubī stresses the opponent's doom. Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr frame it as the shock of stopping even a guided person; the latter reconstructs 'would he forbid him too?' Al-Qushayrī asks whether guidance or enjoining piety would not be better for the worshipper. Al-Jishumī says the mistaken tyrant destroys himself. [16 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; IBNKATHIR; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI(قيل)]
- 96:11/q10/p2 Would it not have been better if the prohibiter himself followed guidance and commanded piety? — Al-Māwardī reports this elliptical alternative. Al-Rāzī says a rational, wealthy man forfeited a high rank for a low one by choosing disbelief and obstruction; Elmalılı likewise addresses the Prophet about the prohibiter's missed better course. ʿIṣām al-Dīn reads a rebuke for lost benefit. [12 notes: MAWARDI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الإمام,عصام الدين); ELMALILI]
- 96:11/q10/p3 The ending asks whether the forbider knows that God sees him and will judge him. — Al-Ṭabarī supplies the final sight question to the prior conditions; Ibn Kathīr adds that God hears him and repays every act. Al-Ālūsī treats divine sight as the threat at every stage. [10 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; AKHFASH; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ALUSI]
- 96:11/q10/p4 How astonishing that the servant guides and calls to piety while the opponent denies and turns away. — Al-Farrāʾ's contrast, reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī, supplies 'How astonishing!' as an omitted answer; some take aw as waw and allow sight to be mental or literal. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī also stress the contrast. [12 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ALUSI(الفراء,قيل)]

## 96:11/q11 Why pair guidance here with commanding piety in the following clause?  [ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the clauses describe the worshipper's prayer and preaching or the forbider's supposed command to piety.
- 96:11/q11/p1 Prayer reforms the Prophet himself, while enjoining piety reforms or calls others; both angered Abu Jahl. — Al-Rāzī says Abu Jahl especially resented prayer and calling people to God. The Prophet's prayer also itself preached by example, softening hearts toward faith more powerfully than words. Al-Farrāʾ, cited by Abū Ḥayyān, makes the self/other distinction; al-Ālūsī says forbidding can target prayer and its enacted call, and action precedes verbal enjoining as the stronger call. [19 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ALUSI(قيل); IBNKATHIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:11/q11/p2 On the forbider reading, banning prayer entails urging its opposite, idol worship, so the next clause exposes his claimed piety. — Al-Ālūsī says the mention is delayed for brevity and that the forbidder's command to idols is the actual opposite of prayer; the majority reading makes this claim an ironic concession. [6 notes: ALUSI(الجمهور)]

## 96:11/q12 How does أو between guidance and commanding piety function?  [ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the next clause offers an alternative quality or joins it to guidance.
- 96:11/q12/p1 It marks alternatives or division. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as one account of the conjunction. [2 notes: ALUSI(قيل)]
- 96:11/q12/p2 It has the force of wāw, joining both qualities. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative in the al-Farrāʾ reading, so the worshipper is guided and commands piety. [3 notes: ALUSI(قيل)]

## 96:11/q13 What happened when Abu Jahl tried to stop the Prophet's prayer?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in whether they narrate the initial threat and revelation or the subsequent retreat and angelic barrier.
- 96:11/q13/p1 Abu Jahl threatened or forbade Muhammad's prayer, occasioning or illustrating the passage. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports a threat, Abu Jahl's boast of his council, revelation of 96:10–13, and angels seizing him if summoned. Another Ibn ʿAbbās report has Abu Jahl encounter and forbid the Prophet, prompting the passage through the lying forelock. Qatāda names Muhammad the guided one, Abu Jahl the denier, his council his clan, and explains zabāniya as police in Arabic. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says a specific occasion still permits general wording. [11 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(ابن عباس); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; BINTSHATI]
- 96:11/q13/p2 Abu Jahl retreated from the Prophet, seeing fire, terror or wings; the Prophet said angels would seize him. — Abū Hurayra's reports describe a trench or barrier of fire and wings, with the Prophet saying angels would seize him limb by limb. Musnad Aḥmad identifies Abu Jahl as denier, his people as council, angels as zabāniya. The narrations leave uncertain whether attributing the passage's revelation was Abū Hurayra's own statement or a later transmission. [4 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); MUSLIM(أبو هريرة)]

## 96:11/q14 Does this warning extend beyond Abu Jahl and his encounter?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the historical setting restricts the warning's reach.
- 96:11/q14/p1 It applies to anyone who obstructs prayer or other worship, including distraction by action. — Al-Ālūsī says the specific revelation about Abu Jahl does not narrow the warning; diversion from worship is practical prohibition. He distinguishes objections to prayer at usurped places or disliked times, which concern an accompanying attribute. Kuran Yolu favors a universal reading about tyrants obstructing religion's goodness, truth and justice and calls it unjust to stop guided worship or responsible religious duty. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ likewise allows general application despite a particular occasion. [6 notes: ALUSI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI; JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 96:11/q14/p2 The clause can describe any worshipper who calls others to right, not just Muhammad. — Al-Jishumī expressly allows both the Prophet and every such worshipper as the one commanding piety; Kuran Yolu's universal application likewise concerns guided people who urge reverence and responsibility. [2 notes: JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 96:11/q15 How does this passage relate to the sūra's opening and its revelation?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is chronological placement after a later occasion or the sūra's continuous portrayal of human conduct.
- 96:11/q15/p1 The later passage was placed after the opening although revealed afterward in the Abu Jahl setting. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāzī's first solution: the remaining sūra came later about Abu Jahl and the Prophet was directed to place it at the beginning. She thinks the passage followed public preaching and prayer. [2 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 96:11/q15/p2 Generic 'man' links the opening blessings to human transgression and the need for guidance. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāzī's second solution: humanity, despite creation and blessings, forgets its return, transgresses and forbids worship when it ought to follow guidance and enjoin piety. Al-Ālūsī says the passage from 96:9 recounts the consequences of human tyranny. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); ALUSI]

## 96:11/q16 What warning is attached to commanding right while acting against it?  [* · reports]
- 96:11/q16/p1 A hypocritical preacher is punished in Hell despite enjoining right. — Usāma says he counsels an official privately to avoid public controversy and refuses to praise a ruler after hearing the Prophet warn of a man whose entrails spill as he circles like a donkey at a mill: he commanded right and forbade wrong but did the reverse. [2 notes: BUKHARI(أسامة,رسول الله)]

## 96:11/q17 What reward is reported for reciting the sūra?  [* · reports]
- 96:11/q17/p1 The reciter receives a reward like reciting all the shorter sūras. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this as a prophetic report. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 96:11/q18 Why is the servant introduced indefinitely in the surrounding challenge?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether tanwīn merely leaves the noun indefinite or heightens the person and event.
- 96:11/q18/p1 It can simply mark an indefinite servant. — Al-Ālūsī reports the basic reading of the tanwīn as indefiniteness. [1 notes: ALUSI]
- 96:11/q18/p2 It can magnify the Prophet and the enormity of forbidding him. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the tanwīn heightens astonishment at such a servant being prevented from prayer. [1 notes: ALUSI]

## 96:11/q19 Why does the challenge use future-oriented wording?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
- 96:11/q19/p1 It makes the past encounter vivid and draws hearers into its unusual confrontation. — Al-Ālūsī says the future-oriented form presents the earlier scene as if unfolding before the audience. [1 notes: ALUSI]

## 96:11/q20 What is omitted in the later أَرَءَيْتَ clauses?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the gaps are separately omitted objects or a paired omission recovered by iḥtibāk.
- 96:11/q20/p1 Their objects are omitted separately, and the pronouns can point to different participants. — Abū Ḥayyān says the later questions require omitted objects, whose antecedents may be the forbider or, in the second instance, the servant. He rules out tanāzuʿ because whole clauses cannot be pronominalized. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN]
- 96:11/q20/p2 The first question has an omitted counterpart inferred from the later clause by iḥtibāk. — Al-Biqāʿī prefers this reading: the challenge first tests the forbider's supposed guidance or piety, and its omitted answer is recovered from the subsequent clause. [1 notes: BIQAI]
