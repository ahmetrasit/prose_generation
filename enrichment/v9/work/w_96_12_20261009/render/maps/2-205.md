# 2:205: 22 questions

## 2:205/q01 How does the conditional opening work?  [وَإِذَا تَوَلَّىٰ · grammar]
- 2:205/q01/p1 Idhā is a temporal adverb with conditional force; the clause can continue the preceding description or begin a fresh report. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls idhā temporal and conditional. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow either coordination with the earlier relative or adjective of man, or a fresh sentence after a pause at aladdu al-khiṣām. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:205/q02 What does tawallā mean here?  [تَوَلَّىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether tawallā describes bodily departure, a change of allegiance or mood, or acquisition of wilāya; the preceding sweet speech and following corrupt acts are read differently.
- 2:205/q02/p1 He turns away or physically leaves the Prophet or his interlocutor. — Muqātil and Ibn Qutayba identify departure from the Prophet; Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās likewise. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies an omitted 'from you' and cites 2:142. Al-Ḥasan and others say he withdraws after agreeable speech; Rāzī favors this sequence in context. [19 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك ومجاهد); JISHUMI(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(الضحاك ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد والضحاك); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI(الضحاك); RAZI-FULL-]
- 2:205/q02/p3 He becomes angry and proud. — Ibn Jurayj, Ibn ʿAbbās, and al-Naqqāsh report anger; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī describe an inward turn followed by scheming against Islam. - 2:205/q02/p4 He turns away from his promise, previous speech, or good conduct. — Al-Ḥasan says he breaks or turns from his promise; Ibn Jurayj gives the same view in Jishumī and Ṭabrisī. Abū Ḥayyān calls it a figurative extension of physical turning. [9 notes: THALABI(الحسن); JISHUMI(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); TABRISI(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:205/q03 What does saʿā describe?  [سَعَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: The verb can denote physical motion, work or effort, or purposeful action through planning and speech.
- 2:205/q03/p1 He works, acts, or exerts deliberate effort. — Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī and Abū Ḥayyān cite al-Aʿshā's Kinda line. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites 53:39 and 17:19 and Banu Mālik poetry. Ibn Kathīr compares Pharaoh's striving in 79:22–26 and Friday prayer in 62:9, where the Sunnah excludes physical rushing. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 17:19, Ibn Kulthūm, and Labīd for work and mediation. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الأعشى); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعشى,شاعر); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(عمرو بن كلثوم)]
- 2:205/q03/p2 He walks, travels, or hurries across the land. — Ibn Qutayba reads haste; al-Thaʿlabī and Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī read travel on foot. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the running man in 28:20 and allows literal movement alongside deliberate raiding. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); TABRISI(الأعشى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو سليمان الدمشقي وابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:205/q03/p3 He schemes to stir discord, fighting, or harm to Islam. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn Jurayj read inward scheming; Rāzī takes brisk walking metaphorically for provoking fighting and cites 9:47. Al-Qurṭubī sees swift movement as an image of incitement; Abū Ḥayyān includes tale bearing with an anonymous poem. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج وغيره); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وابن جريج وابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:205/q03/p4 He heads or intends toward something. — Ibn Kathīr compares Pharaoh in 79:22–26 and the Friday prayer command in 62:9. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:205/q03/p5 He goes away from Muhammad. — Al-Aṣamm makes this a gloss of striving in the land. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:205/q04 What is the force of 'in the earth'?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فِيهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the locative is read generally, as emphatic specification, or by the particular setting of the report.
- 2:205/q04/p1 It generalizes the scene: wherever he settles or travels, he causes extensive corruption. — Abū Ḥayyān compares 2:11; al-Samīn says the encompassing setting implies general corruption; Bursawī says it signals extensive movement. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 2:205/q04/p2 It specifies activity among the land's inhabitants, and 'therein' restates and reinforces it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr attaches fī al-arḍ to saʿā and treats fīhā as explicative repetition. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:205/q04/p3 In the al-Akhnas account, the land can refer to Medina. — Abū Ḥayyān derives the reference from the earlier context. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:205/q05 How does li-yufsida relate to his striving?  [لِيُفْسِدَ فِيهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether saʿā is motion with an intended end, a verb of devising with an object, or conduct whose corruption is its result.
- 2:205/q05/p1 The lām states his purpose: he strives in order to cause corruption. — Al-Zajjāj, Ṭabrisī, and al-Samīn posit an implicit an; al-Naḥḥās calls the verb subjunctive. Abū Ḥayyān and Bursawī make the clause the motive. Ṭabrisī contrasts the lām of 3:179. Most exegetes infer conscious intent, as Asad notes. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 2:205/q05/p2 The lām can mark the consequence of his conduct even without conscious intent. — Asad says his rendering leaves purpose and consequence open. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 2:205/q05/p3 If saʿā means intending or devising, the lām introduces its object. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 2:185 and 9:32, cites Juʾz ibn Kulayb as read by al-Marzūqī, and says the object reading is recovered through implicit an. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:205/q06 What corruption does he pursue?  [لِيُفْسِدَ فِيهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Sources differ over whether fasād is all sin, a specified violent act, religious subversion, or a wider social result; Rāzī's preference depends on whether crop destruction adds a distinct evil.
- 2:205/q06/p1 Corruption includes every sin, injustice, and harmful act, without restriction to one offense. — Ṭabarī explicitly resists limiting the term, though road terror fits the context best. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Biqāʿī call it all sins. Abū Ḥayyān includes injustice, killing, plunder, captivity, and unbelief and cites 11:61; Ṭabrisī defines unjustified, useless harm. Study Quran includes moral decay and contemporary social and environmental harm. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some Muslims); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:205/q06/p2 He commits ruin and devastation, especially killing and destroying property. — Ibn Jawzī lists ruin as this verse's sense, alongside 27:34; Dāmghānī connects ruin with oppression and 27:34. Qatāda says he devastates God's servants; Mālik compares corruption to killing. Al-ʿAbbās ibn al-Faḍl and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss it as ruin. [11 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة,مالك); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL(العباس بن الفضل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(من فسر الفساد بالتخريب); RAZI-FULL-]
- 2:205/q06/p3 He spreads unbelief, religious doubts, heresy, and disobedience. — Māwardī gives unbelief or blocking guidance; al-Aṣamm mentions unbelief and sins. Rāzī prefers spread of doubts because crop destruction then names a distinct later evil, citing 7:127, 40:26 and 2:11. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes altered teachings and broken unity; Qāshānī identifies permissiveness and heresy. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(الأصم وغيره); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وابن جريج وابن عباس); QASHANI; TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:205/q06/p4 He severs kinship and spills Muslim blood. — Ibn Jurayj says the actor claims thereby to draw near to God; Rāzī links religious corruption, kinship division, and bloodshed to 47:22. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI(ابن جريح); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن جريج); JISHUMI(ابن جريج); TABRISI(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL]
- 2:205/q06/p5 He cuts roads and terrorizes travelers. — Ṭabarī says this best fits the adjacent destruction of crops and offspring, while acknowledging other sins; Ibn ʿAbbās's physical reading includes waylaying. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو سليمان الدمشقي وابن عباس); TAB-FULL+]
- 2:205/q06/p6 He causes social harm through selfish worldly behavior. — Asad takes the worldly only attitude as yielding moral decay and social disintegration. Abduh treats crops and offspring as a metaphor for severe hardship imposed to satisfy selfish desires; Kuran Yolu describes ruined income and descendants under deceptive politicians. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Muhammed Abduh); STUDYQURAN(some Muslims)]
- 2:205/q06/p7 He obstructs people from the right path. — Māwardī offers this as one form of corruption. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 2:205/q07 What does fasād mean as an Arabic word?  [ٱلْفَسَادَ · meaning]
- 2:205/q07/p1 It is loss of soundness, order, or useful condition. — Ibn Jawzī says an object may remain, be undone, or vanish; his examples distinguish invalid worship, contracts, evidence, speech and deeds, and list disobedience, destruction, drought, killing, ruin, disbelief and sorcery across verses. Al-Rāghib says removal from a praiseworthy state without valid purpose; Ṭabrisī defines harm without right or benefit. [17 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:205/q07/p2 It may denote ruin caused by injustice or oppression. — Dāmghānī applies this sense to 2:205 and compares 27:34; al-ʿAbbās ibn al-Faḍl calls it ruin. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; QURTUBI-FULL(العباس بن الفضل)]
- 2:205/q07/p3 The verbal noun may be fasād or fasūd. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Wāḥidī compare dhahāb/dhuhūb and kasād/kusūd. [2 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 2:205/q08 How is yuhlik read and connected syntactically?  [وَيُهْلِكَ ٱلْحَرْثَ وَٱلنَّسْلَ · readings]
turns on: Voice, the vowels of the imperfect, case of crops and offspring, and whether the verb shares li-yufsida's purpose or forms a new assertion.
- 2:205/q08/p1 The common causative subjunctive yuhlika makes the man destroy crops and offspring and coordinates with li-yufsida. — Al-Farrāʾ prefers this; al-Ṭabarī rejects the nominative as against readers' consensus despite grammatical possibility. Ubayy's codex adds li before yuhlika, making purpose explicit. Rāzī says this syntax expresses an aim rather than established destruction. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبي); THALABI(العامة); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر والحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب,أكثر القراء); QURTUBI-FULL(أبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي,الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبي,الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; FARRA+; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:205/q08/p2 A raised causative yuhliku describes his action, linked to the earlier predicate, saʿā, or an implied huwa. — Al-Farrāʾ links it to yaʿjibuka; al-Ḥasan and Qatāda report the nominative. Abū Ḥātim links it to saʿā, Abū Isḥāq makes a new clause; al-Zajjāj allows implied huwa. Rāzī notes a separate clause asserts actual destruction. [16 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعض القراء); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,أبو حاتم,الحسن وقتادة); THALABI(الحسن وابن أبي إسحاق); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر والحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو إسحاق,أبو حاتم,الحسن وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المهدوي,قوم); SAMIN-DURR(قوم); RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL-]
- 2:205/q08/p3 An intransitive yahluku makes crops and offspring the nominative subjects: they perish. — Reported for al-Ḥasan, Ibn Abī Isḥāq, Abū Ḥaywa and Ibn Muḥayṣin, with variant attribution to Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr. Some parse it with saʿā. [8 notes: NAHHAS(ابن كثير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وابن أبي إسحاق وأبو حيوة وابن محيصن); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وابن أبي إسحاق وأبو حيوة وابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(أبو حيوة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:205/q08/p4 A dialectal intransitive yahlak or yahlaku has open imperfect vowels and crops as subject. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it anomalous; al-Samīn compares rakana/yarkan and notes that neither root consonant is guttural; al-Zamakhsharī also calls it irregular. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم والزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 2:205/q08/p5 A passive form makes crops the deputy subject. — Transmitted from al-Ḥasan by al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 2:205/q08/p6 Ḥammād's report from Ibn Kathīr has a causative verb and accusative crops. — Al-Mahdawī gives this variant against other reports attributing the intransitive reading to Ibn Kathīr; Abū Ḥayyān says the causative verb is raised. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(المهدوي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المهدوي)]

## 2:205/q09 What does ihlāk mean here?  [وَيُهْلِكَ · meaning]
- 2:205/q09/p1 It is wasting, ruining, or removing the possibility of benefit from crops and offspring. — Dāmghānī and Ibn Jawzī classify this verse's halāk as corruption, comparing wasted wealth in 90:6; Ibn Jawzī distinguishes corruption while an object remains from destruction that may also remove it. Ṭabrisī says ihlāk ends its benefit; Bursawī says waste or destruction. [8 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); TABRISI; BURSEVI]

## 2:205/q10 What is al-ḥarth here?  [ٱلْحَرْثَ · referent]
turns on: The literal agricultural sense, the wife metaphor in 2:223, symbolic religion, and broader human livelihood.
- 2:205/q10/p1 Crops, cultivated plants, fields, and by extension trees or orchards. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya traces the word from plowing soil to produce, trees and orchards, citing the vineyard of 21:78. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes cultivated crops as what sustains people; al-Ḥarallī distinguishes human cultivation from planting attributed to God. Qurṭubī lists plowing, earning, crops, study, an exhausted camel and stirring a fire; Ibn Fāris derives gathering, while ʿAlī ibn ʿUbayd Allāh lists arable land, a horse's first track and furrow length. Dāmghānī compares 2:71 and 21:78. [41 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع,الضحاك,عطاء,قتادة,مجاهد,مكحول); ZAJJAJ(قالوا); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,أبو العالية ومجاهد وعطاء وعكرمة والربيع بن أنس وقتادة ومكحول والسدى,ابن عباس,السدي,عطاء); THALABI(المفسرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن فارس,علي بن عبيد الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وعكرمة وآخرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(الضحاك)]
- 2:205/q10/p2 Women or wives, as a field where children are conceived. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Azharī and others cite 2:223; al-Wāḥidī accepts its possibility, Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the use metaphorical, and Abū Ḥayyān calls it euphemistic bayān. Asad says this reading makes the phrase about disrupted family and society. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ(قالوا); WAHIDI-BASIT(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الأزهري); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج عن قوم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج وابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(some commentators)]
- 2:205/q10/p3 Religion, with nasl as people. — Al-Ṣādiq is reported with this paired symbolic interpretation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī mentions narrations of religion and humanity. [3 notes: TABRISI(الصادق); ALUSI-FULL(الصادق); TABATABAI]
- 2:205/q10/p4 The gain of labor or broader worldly and social endeavors. — Asad says ḥarth can be acquisition, goods, crops, or cultivated land, with tilth metaphorically covering human and social work; Ibn Jawzī notes the harvest of the Hereafter as reward in 42:20. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:205/q10/p5 Men, who cultivate the field of reproduction. — Al-Aṣamm gives this view; Rāzī reports men as the growers of children. [3 notes: JISHUMI(الأصم); RAZI-FULL(قوم من المفسرين)]
- 2:205/q10/p6 The origin or established root, glossed as al-ḥannān. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk gives this less common gloss of ḥarth in Ibn Abī Ḥātim. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك)]

## 2:205/q11 What is al-nasl here?  [ٱلنَّسْلَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the paired words are literal agriculture and animal life, include human descendants, or stand for women and children or religion and people.
- 2:205/q11/p1 The offspring of every kind of living creature, including animals and humans. — Ibn ʿAbbās transmissions expressly add people; Mujāhid says every walking creature, and ʿAṭāʾ names people and livestock. - 2:205/q11/p2 Human children or descendants. — The women-as-ḥarth interpretation pairs this with children; Rāzī also applies children to the attack report. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ(قالوا); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(قيل); JISHUMI(الأصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الأزهري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج عن قوم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج وابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(some commentators)]
- 2:205/q11/p3 Animal or livestock offspring, in the incident specifically the donkeys. — Ibn Qutayba names animals, and the al-Akhnas report makes donkeys the immediate referent; Ibn ʿĀshūr says animal young. [10 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK, R, Ṭ)]
- 2:205/q11/p4 People or humanity, paired with religion as ḥarth. — Al-Ṣādiq's reported interpretation is echoed by Ṭabāṭabāʾī's notice of narrations. [3 notes: TABRISI(الصادق); ALUSI-FULL(الصادق); TABATABAI]

## 2:205/q12 Why is offspring called nasl?  [ٱلنَّسْلَ · meaning]
- 2:205/q12/p1 It derives from emerging or falling out, including successive emergence. — Al-Wāḥidī, Rāzī, and Abū Ḥayyān connect it to emergence from father and mother; bird feathers and animal hair falling illustrate it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Jishumī stress succession, citing Imruʾ al-Qays's tansilu and 21:96; al-Wāḥidī and Rāzī cite 36:51, and al-Qurṭubī cites both verses. Al-Samīn says either quick or successive emergence fits 21:96. Abū Ḥayyān calls offspring a verbal noun transferred to its object. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(امرؤ القيس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:205/q13 How are crops and offspring destroyed?  [وَيُهْلِكَ ٱلْحَرْثَ وَٱلنَّسْلَ · interpretation]
turns on: The report of direct violence, the drought consequence of unjust power, indirect loss through human killing or misguidance, and a proverb for sweeping harm.
- 2:205/q13/p1 By direct burning, killing, cutting, captivity, or other devastation. — Al-Suddī links burning fields with killing or hamstringing donkeys; Ṭabarī prefers direct destruction as nearest the wording. Ibn Jawzī reports the majority's literal reading; Māwardī includes captivity and killing. Ṭabarī extends the blame to any unjust killing of an animal. Al-Qurṭubī reports blame and lasting disgrace for killing a donkey or burning a grain stack. [42 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,السدي,عطاء); THALABI(السدي,مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(السدي والكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); RAZI-FULL(من فسر الفساد بالتخريب); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري,بعض العلماء); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض المفسرين,قيل); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); TAB-FULL+]
- 2:205/q13/p2 By God withholding rain because of oppression and injustice. — Mujāhid makes drought the consequence of unjust rule, often linked to 30:41; al-Ṭabarī allows it but prefers direct burning and killing. His explanation of 30:41's sea includes settlements near flowing water. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TAB-FULL-]
- 2:205/q13/p3 As a vivid image of the maximum harm to human livelihood and continuity. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says worldly affairs chiefly rest on cultivation and reproduction; Rāzī says plants and animals or life at roots and branches. - 2:205/q13/p4 As the particular result of corrupt rulers' killing and dispossession. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī describe murderous rulers; Nasafī allows the verse to include them as well as al-Akhnas. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:205/q13/p5 By killing those who cultivate and reproduce, ending cultivation and later generations. — Ṭabarī allows crop loss when caretakers die and offspring loss when parents die; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports killing people as the mechanism. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 2:205/q13/p6 By misguidance that eventually produces captivity, killing, and social ruin. — Māwardī and Ibn Jawzī distinguish the indirect outcome from immediate killing. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says religious distortion damages morals and unity before worldly destruction. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI]

## 2:205/q14 Why mention crop and offspring destruction after corruption?  [لِيُفْسِدَ فِيهَا وَيُهْلِكَ ٱلْحَرْثَ وَٱلنَّسْلَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second clause specifies the first, adds a separate kind of evil, or functions as a compact emphatic image.
- 2:205/q14/p1 It gives the destructive detail after the general statement of corruption. — Rāzī reports the physical reading as general then specific; Abū Ḥayyān says crops and offspring are singled out as the greatest harm to settlement, comparing 2:98 through al-Samīn. Al-Ālūsī says the closing general term then covers both harms. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(من فسر الفساد بالتخريب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:205/q14/p2 The concise pair depicts the greatest imaginable damage. — Rāzī compares brief descriptions of Paradise and the earth in 43:71 and 79:31. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it emphatic, and Ibn ʿĀshūr a proverb for livelihood. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:205/q14/p3 Religious corruption is followed by a distinct additional evil of physical destruction. — Rāzī prefers this because otherwise crop destruction repeats the first clause; for him doubts lead to deeds. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:205/q14/p4 The wording uses paired ellipses: corruption implies destruction, and crops imply offspring. — Al-Biqāʿī calls both pairings ihtibāk: each expressed term implies its counterpart; crops are a cause of animal life and offspring an effect. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:205/q15 Why say fasād here rather than another form of destruction?  [ٱلْفَسَادَ · rhetoric]
- 2:205/q15/p1 Fasād emphasizes wrongful harm; destruction can sometimes be beneficial or deserved. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts outward destruction in requital; Ibn ʿĀshūr says corruption defeats God's useful purposes, while war may remove greater harm and necessity is limited. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:205/q15/p2 The noun fasād points to intentional corruption more clearly than ifsād. — Al-Biqāʿī says ifsād can include corruption without intent. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:205/q16 What does 'God does not love corruption' mean?  [وَٱللَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلْفَسَادَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the negated love is for the act or its doers, and whether the statement is unrestricted, religiously restricted, or phrased as command or approval.
- 2:205/q16/p1 God does not approve, commend, or accept corrupt acts and warns of punishment. — Ibn ʿAbbās says neither love nor approval; al-Wāḥidī extends this to sins. Al-Bayḍāwī warns of wrath; al-Ālūsī calls the sentence parenthetical rebuke; Ibn ʿĀshūr says omnipotence makes punishment possible. Al-Biqāʿī says God forbids, changes and punishes corruption, even after delay. [31 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس في رواية الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الرسول عليه السلام)]
- 2:205/q16/p2 The phrase concerns corruption among the righteous or corruption taken as religion or law. — Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī report these restrictions; the religious reading is one response to theological argument. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أصحابنا)]
- 2:205/q16/p3 He does not love the people who cause corruption. — Māwardī supplies corrupt people as the referent, and Ibn Kathīr includes the person characterized by corruption. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:205/q16/p4 He does not command corruption. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this gloss, Abū Ḥayyān calls love a figure for command, and al-Biqāʿī says God forbids corruption. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:205/q16/p5 He dislikes every kind of corruption in land, property, and religion. — Al-Qurṭubī prefers the general scope over restricted glosses; Abū Ḥayyān calls it the apparent meaning. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes destruction of beneficial things even owned by polytheists. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:205/q16/p6 It rejects his claim of love, since a lover obeys the beloved. — Qāshānī cites an unnamed poet's couplet on disobedience contradicting love. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 2:205/q17 Does God's not loving corruption mean He does not will or create it?  [وَٱللَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلْفَسَادَ · theology]
turns on: Whether love equals will, whether God's will has a religious and an existential sense, and whether the act is purposeless human corruption or purposeful divine change.
- 2:205/q17/p1 Yes: divine love is will, so God neither wills nor creates corruption. — Jishumī says willing an act is loving it and uses the verse against the Mujbira; Ṭabrisī makes the same argument. Rāzī reports Muʿtazilite evidence from 24:19 and 40:31; Abū Ḥayyān reports that argument. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المعتزلة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أصحابنا)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جمهور العلماء)-]
- 2:205/q17/p2 God may will an occurrence without loving or approving it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says love adds preference beyond will. Ibn Jawzī prefers an analogy with bitter medicine or cautery; Abū Ḥayyān's majority uses a painful wound and 39:7. Rāzī argues a determining cause of evil preference must come from God and that willing a known future evil not to occur would negate divine knowledge. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(المتكلمون); RAZI-FULL(الأصحاب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جمهور العلماء)+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المعتزلة)-; RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المعتزلة)-]
- 2:205/q17/p3 The denial concerns religious approval or legal will, not God's will that an event exist. — Ibn Jawzī reports this response; theologians cited by Ibn ʿAṭiyya say God may will events while not approving them religiously. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects approval and will in his school and the Muʿtazilites. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(المتكلمون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أصحابنا); RAZI-FULL(الأصحاب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المعتزلة)-]
- 2:205/q17/p4 Divine acts and decreed natural decay have valid purposes, so they are not the rejected corruption. — Al-Rāghib, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī define corruption as purposeless loss of a praiseworthy state; al-Ālūsī cites slaughter for food and death leading to eternal life. Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes natural change, death and life from unlawful human corruption. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراغب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:205/q17/p5 Love means praise or exaltation, rather than will. — Rāzī reports this Ashʿarite response; Māwardī gives noncommendation as a gloss. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الأصحاب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المعتزلة)-; RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)-]
- 2:205/q17/p6 The statement is restricted to corruption for believers, or its definite noun need not be universal. — Ibn Jawzī reports the believer restriction; Rāzī's Ashʿarite colleagues dispute universal force for the definite noun. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أصحابنا); RAZI-FULL(الأصحاب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المعتزلة)-]

## 2:205/q18 What practical rulings follow from the verse?  [ٱلْحَرْثَ وَٱلنَّسْلَ وَٱللَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلْفَسَادَ · law]
turns on: The boundary between needless loss of beneficial things and a proportionate measure to remove greater harm.
- 2:205/q18/p1 Needless destruction of property, crops, animals, or clothing is blameworthy. — Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz count clipping coins as corruption; ʿAṭāʾ uses the verse to oppose tearing an iḥrām tunic, and ʿIkrima opposes slitting pilgrims' leather socks. Al-Qurṭubī censures burning grain and killing donkeys; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes polytheists' crops and prohibits needless burning in war. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن المسيب,عمر بن عبد العزيز); THALABI(سعيد بن المسيب,قتادة وعطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض العلماء,سعيد بن المسيب,عطاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:205/q18/p2 A corrupt governor faces severe judgment. — Bursawī quotes a report of a governor at Hell's bridge: the obedient cross and the disobedient fall during a fifty year journey; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the perpetrator is liable to afterlife punishment. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:205/q18/p3 Cultivation and animal breeding are legitimate means for sustaining human life. — Al-Qurṭubī says the verse supports farming fields and trees and seeking animal offspring, against rejecting practical means. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:205/q18/p4 Destruction may be justified to remove a greater harm, within necessity's limits. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says burning homes or cutting trees in war is allowed only if leaving them strengthens the enemy, prolongs fighting, and risks defeat; milk and firewood have substantial benefit, whereas wine and pests do not. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:205/q18/p5 A community must restrain an evident wrongdoer or may share punishment. — Al-Qurṭubī reports a hadith warning of collective punishment if observers fail to restrain wrongdoing. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:205/q20 To whom does the warning apply beyond an occasion of revelation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the described traits identify a hypocrite necessarily or provide a moral warning that includes other sinners.
- 2:205/q20/p1 To anyone whose pleasing words conceal unbelief, hypocrisy, lies, or harm, including later corrupt rulers. — Qatāda and Mujāhid generalize it; Rāzī says the five traits do not prove formal hypocrisy since a showoff or sinner may share them. Ṭabarī rejects restriction to a single offense, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya says sinners share the warning. Elmalılı, Kuran Yolu and Study Quran stress that the quality, rather than the initial person, governs application. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع,سعيد المقبري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الترمذي,قتادة ومجاهد وجماعة من العلماء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(غيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI(müfessirînin ekser-i muhakkikīni); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN(IK, R, Ṭ)]
- 2:205/q20/p2 Specifically hypocrites whose words contradict their hearts and deeds. — The majority reading reported by Ṭabarī makes his God-witness claim a lie. Ibn Kathīr calls his belief and actions corrupt, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī frames the passage as hypocrisy against sincere faith. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد المقبري,عامة القراء); JISHUMI(الأصم وغيره); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:205/q20/p3 The action itself does not prove hypocrisy, since a Muslim could commit it. — Rāzī distinguishes a bad act from proof of inward disbelief. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:205/q20/p4 Even the righteous require warning, while sinners may be encouraged by divine forgiveness. — Qāshānī quotes a saying that sinners should hear of God's forgiveness and the righteous should be warned by that same forgiveness. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 2:205/q21 What conduct should readers learn from this warning?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:205/q21/p1 Test pleasing speech by conduct and attend to hidden motives. — Rāzī urges upright hearts and limbs and recalls that God knows inward affairs; Ibn ʿĀshūr says appearances should be tested. Elmalılı applies this especially to appointments, while Kuran Yolu warns about deceptive politicians. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:205/q21/p2 Corruption of social life and religion is condemned because revelation seeks reform and balance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 47:22–23 as evidence of the Lawgiver's aim to reform the world; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says revelation reforms deeds and character for happiness in both lives. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 2:205/q22 How does this verse connect with its neighboring verses?  [* · links]
- 2:205/q22/p1 It turns the pleasing speaker of 2:204 into a doer of evil and prepares the rebuke in 2:206. — Al-Biqāʿī says the next verse displays contentiousness in action; Rāzī contrasts charming outward speech with inward hostility; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 2:206 as support for taking office. [6 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:205/q22/p2 The passage contrasts hypocritical worldly seeking with sincere self-giving for God, after earlier Hajj verses. — Ibn ʿAbbās's al-Rajīʿ account contrasts mockers with martyrs; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the earlier verses sort worldly and otherworldly aims, while these sort hypocrisy and sincerity, with a link to 2:200. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
