# 28:20: 29 questions

## 28:20/q01 How did Moses' killing become known and lead to the warning?  [وَجَآءَ رَجُلٌۭ إِنَّ ٱلْمَلَأَ يَأْتَمِرُونَ بِكَ لِيَقْتُلُوكَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the Egyptian or an unnamed enemy exposed the killing, and whether Pharaoh had learned of the plan before the warning.
- 28:20/q01/p1 The Egyptian heard the Israelite disclose the earlier killing, told the Copts or Pharaoh's council, and Pharaoh or his chiefs sought Moses' death. — Yahya's Qatada identifies the last Egyptian; Tabari's Ibn Abbas, Abu Bakr's companions and Ibn Abi Najih, Wahidi, Baydawi, Alusi, and Study Quran link the exposure to the Israelite's words in 28:19. Wahidi says he informed the Copts; Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Qurtubi, Ibn Ashur and Kur'an Yolu report Pharaoh's death or arrest order. Ibn Ashur cites Exodus for Pharaoh hearing and seeking Moses' death. Islahi says the secret's spread naturally prompted the plan. Muqatil and Biqai say the slain person was a Copt; Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas and Muqatil that it was yesterday's Egyptian. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عبد الله,ابن أبي نجيح,ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 28:20/q01/p2 An enemy overheard Moses' exchange and spread the accusation. — Tabari reports Ibn Jurayj's wording that an enemy heard and broadcast what he heard; it leaves the hearer's identity less specific than the Egyptian reports. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 28:20/q01/p3 The man knew of the chiefs' plan and warned Moses before Pharaoh was informed. — Qushayri's Israelite acquaintance reports the leaders' plot and urges departure before they tell Pharaoh, unlike reports that Pharaoh had already ordered his death. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 28:20/q02 Who was the man who warned Moses?  [رَجُلٌۭ · referent]

Reports differ in spelling and patronymic. Zajjaj adds carpenter; Kalbi calls him Pharaoh's paternal cousin; Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, and Qurtubi also call him cousin. Ibn Jurayj in Durr says Hizqil was not a notable. Thalabi, Baghawi, Razi, and Qurtubi ascribe this to most exegetes; Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas and majority. Alusi prefers the well-known family identification; Islahi considers it probable and connects him to Sura al-Mu'min. Ibn Jawzi points to a discussion in Ghafir. Some reports only identify the family believer without a name. ## 28:20/q03 What moved the man to warn Moses?  [رَجُلٌۭ يَسْعَىٰ إِنِّى لَكَ مِنَ ٱلنَّٰصِحِينَ · interpretation]
- 28:20/q03/p1 He cared for Moses and sincerely wanted to save him. — Biqai calls him one who loves Moses; Razi says concern made him hurry; Ibn Ashur cites admiration for Moses' uprightness; Islahi sees sympathy in a courtier running to Moses' settlement. Alusi links his haste to distance and eagerness to advise. Tabari calls him Moses' partisan. Biqai says the emphatic assurance counters any assumption that the king's favor made danger unlikely. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:20/q04 What does the city's farthest part denote here?  [مِّنْ أَقْصَا ٱلْمَدِينَةِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the geographical phrase merely describes the outskirts or permits inference about the palace and rulers' quarter.
- 28:20/q04/p1 The remote edge or outskirts of Pharaoh's city. — Tabari specifies Pharaoh's city; Wahidi and Ibn Jawzi gloss farthest and most distant; Bursevi notes the root's distance sense. Ibn Ashur distinguishes Abu Tammam's line about a settled city center becoming an edge, and cites 33:13 for another usage. Samarrai says this order can describe the man's residence or his point of departure. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 28:20/q04/p2 The remote part was probably Pharaoh's palace or ruling quarter, away from the Israelite settlement. — Ibn Ashur reasons that rulers lived at city edges for security against revolt or raids and easier escape; the messenger's origin suggests proximity to the court and acquaintance with Moses. Tabatabai infers the palace stood beyond the city and the conspiracy was ordered by Pharaoh. Islahi locates the rulers' center far from the Israelites' quarter. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:20/q05 How does 'from the farthest part of the city' attach grammatically?  [مِّنْ أَقْصَا ٱلْمَدِينَةِ · grammar]
turns on: The phrase's attachment determines whether the wording fixes the man's departure point or characterizes where he belonged; Alusi rejects an attachment to yas'a.
- 28:20/q05/p1 It modifies 'came,' giving the place from which he arrived. — Alusi calls this the most natural attachment. Tabatabai says it grammatically qualifies 'came.' Abu Hayyan allows it, and Samarrai says it makes the origin definite in the comparable word order. Ibn Ashur's palace inference rests on the man coming from that direction. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 28:20/q05/p2 It describes the man, indicating his origin or residence. — Kashshaf says the phrase has specified the indefinite man; Baydawi, Nasafi, Samin, Alusi, and Samarrai allow the adjectival phrase, with yas'a a further modifier or circumstantial clause. Samarrai says this can denote a resident of the far end, not necessarily a departure from there. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 28:20/q05/p3 It attaches to 'hurrying.' — Alusi mentions this proposed construction but dismisses it as implausible. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 28:20/q06 What does 'yas'a' mean here?  [يَسْعَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether sa'y specifies rapid movement, simple walking, work, or a figurative spiritual aim; reports differ on whether the speed was running.
- 28:20/q06/p1 He hurried on foot, moving rapidly toward Moses. — Yahya's Suddi, Ibn Qutayba, Tabari's Ibn Jurayj, Kalbi, Wahidi's Ibn Abbas, Ibn Jawzi, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur gloss haste. Ibn Qutayba says sa'y commonly denotes rapid walking and may include running; Ibn Atiyya prefers hurrying and cites Malik's treatment of going to Friday prayer, while Zajjaj and others there say short of running. Ibn Ashur compares 20:20 and 17:19; Alusi takes the literal haste as apparent because of distance and eagerness. Baghawi describes hurrying on foot along a near route. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج وغيره); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 28:20/q06/p2 He ran quickly. — Zajjaj explicitly says running; Ibn Qutayba allows running within sa'y's range. Biqai emphasizes speed that outstripped others, and Muqatil says he ran on foot. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ; BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:20/q06/p3 He was walking on his feet. — Thalabi and Wahidi transmit Muqatil's basic on-foot gloss; the reports do not specify speed. [2 notes: THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 28:20/q06/p4 He was working or exerting himself. — Ibn Atiyya transmits this from Ibn Jurayj as an alternative to haste, despite Tabari's report from Ibn Jurayj giving haste. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 28:20/q06/p5 He sought God's good pleasure. — Alusi reports a figurative interpretation and compares the wording of 17:19. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 28:20/q07 What is the syntactic role of 'yas'a'?  [يَسْعَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an indefinite rajul can license a circumstantial clause when the place phrase modifies the verb rather than the noun.
- 28:20/q07/p1 It can be an adjective of 'man' or a circumstantial clause after the man has been specified. — Kashshaf, Razi citing Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Samin, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, and Ibn Ashur allow the two parses when the prepositional phrase specifies the man; Ibn Ashur takes it as circumstantial. Samarrai treats it as an adjective because rajul is indefinite. Abu Hayyan also permits the place phrase to attach to came. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 28:20/q07/p2 If the place phrase attaches to 'came,' 'yas'a' can only be an adjective under the majority rule. — Kashshaf and Samin attribute this limit to the usual ban on a circumstantial clause from an unqualified indefinite noun; Alusi reports the majority's position. Baydawi likewise rules out treating it as a complement of came because qualification makes the man definite-like. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه والجمهور); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 28:20/q07/p3 The circumstantial parse is possible even without that restriction. — Abu Hayyan rejects Zamakhshari's limit, and Samin prefers Sibawayh's allowance of a circumstantial clause from a bare indefinite; Alusi says Sibawayh allows it because the prepositional phrase gives the noun specificity. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR+]

## 28:20/q08 How did the warning man reach Moses before the pursuers?  [وَجَآءَ رَجُلٌۭ مِّنْ أَقْصَا ٱلْمَدِينَةِ يَسْعَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports specify a shortcut and pursuit routes or only the man's urgent arrival.
- 28:20/q08/p1 He took a short or side route and outran Pharaoh's messengers, who used the main road or searched other byways. — Ibn Abbas in Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim and Thalabi says Pharaoh's executioners took the main road while Moses' partisan took a shortcut. Baghawi says the man arrived first and warned Moses to use another route; Tabarsi, Ibn Kathir and Biqai likewise emphasize the short route and urgency. Suddi in Tabari and Ibn Abi Hatim says Pharaoh had Moses sought along side paths, thinking the young man did not know them; Moses nevertheless took them. Ibn Kathir says this explains the man's description as active. Tabari's Qatada says the warning reached Moses at his far-off location. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي); THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:20/q08/p2 He hurried to warn Moses, without a specified route. — Tabari's Qatada simply places the man at Moses' distant location; Qurtubi says he hurried after Pharaoh ordered execution. Qushayri says the acquaintance hurried before Pharaoh was notified. Kur'an Yolu says a well-wisher ran to advise escape. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:20/q09 Why is the order of 'a man' and 'from the farthest part' different from 36:20?  [رَجُلٌۭ مِّنْ أَقْصَا ٱلْمَدِينَةِ · rhetoric]
- 28:20/q09/p1 Al-Qasas uses the ordinary or less definite order for a private warning; Ya Sin foregrounds the remote origin for a public mission and surprise. — Burhan says Ya Sin preposes the place to make readers wonder whether everyone rejected the messengers, whereas Qasas retains normal order. Samarrai contrasts Ya Sin's public call against a hostile town (36:18, 36:20) with Moses' private warning and says public proclamation deserves fronting; the two orders also differ in how firmly they state origin or residence. Samin notes these are different men and stories. Biqai links Qasas's subject-first order to the gravity of this moment. [8 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:20/q10 What does the indefinite 'rajul' convey?  [رَجُلٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 28:20/q10/p1 It introduces one particular man. — Burhan and Itqan explicitly take the indefinite as a single individual. Itqan also surveys other possible functions of indefiniteness elsewhere: a kind or type (21:50, 2:7, 2:96), magnification (2:279, 3:21, 19:15, 37:109, 9:72), abundance or abundance with greatness (26:41, 6:34), and small quantity (9:72, with a poet's line that a little from the beloved suffices). These are general comparisons, not claims that rajul has each of those senses here. [6 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 28:20/q11 What is the function of 'he said' and the address 'O Moses'?  [قَالَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
- 28:20/q11/p1 Calling Moses by name signals affection and makes the addressee certain. — Biqai connects the direct address with the man's concern for Moses. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:20/q11/p2 The speech clause is a substitution of inclusion for the man's arrival. — Ibn Ashur parses qal as badal ishtimal because arriving includes speaking. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:20/q12 Who are 'al-mala' here?  [ٱلْمَلَأَ · referent]
- 28:20/q12/p1 Pharaoh's influential Coptic notables or governing council. — Ibn Qutayba, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Ibn Jawzi, Bursevi, Alusi, and Study Quran gloss leaders, nobles or a council; Zajjaj notes others look to them. Biqai says they possess authority to decide and command. Ibn Ashur explains their consequence and cites 7:60; Study Quran points to other hostile notables in stories of Sheba, Quraysh, Hud, Shuayb and Joseph's king (27:29, 38:6, 7:65, 7:88, 12:43). [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:20/q13 What does 'ya'tamiruna bika' mean?  [يَأْتَمِرُونَ بِكَ · meaning]
turns on: Lexical force of reciprocal ifta'ala, evidence from poetry and 65:6, whether consultation can be malign, and whether the verb alone denotes mere intent.
- 28:20/q13/p1 They consult or deliberate with one another about Moses' death. — Abu Ubayda, Tabari, Kalbi, Zamakhshari, Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, and others use mutual counsel. Tabari cites an anonymous ta'tamir line and al-Namir's yu'tamar; Majaz cites al-Namir and Rabia ibn Jashm, whose line warns that apparently good judgment may ruin one. Mawrdi, Thalabi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Samin, and Jishumi cite poetic parallels, including a poet's line on deliberating over every event; Mawrdi also cites Imru al-Qays. Wahidi's Shammar says consultation may be wise, desire-driven or fallible self-consultation, illustrated by a proverb about confusion. Ibn Atiyya argues ifta'ala can be reciprocal like tafa'ala, citing izdawaja and 65:6. Ibn Ashur derives consultation from accepting another's order, supports it with 65:6, and says an implied intention remains; Baydawi, Biqai, Bursevi and Alusi say each participant commands and complies. Qurtubi's Azhari allows both counsel and reciprocal command. Wahidi endorses Abu Ubayda and Zajjaj as compatible; Ibn Qutayba rejects this sense as contextually ill-suited, though Wahidi disputes him. [40 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(النمر بن تولب); THALABI(النمر بن تغلب); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي,النمر بن تولب,امرؤ القيس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,شمر); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الشاعر); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ربيعة بن جشم); TABRISI(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأزهري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT+; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة)-; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة)-]
- 28:20/q13/p2 They intend or resolve evil against Moses. — Ibn Qutayba argues consultation is ordinarily good and does not fit a murder plot; Ibn Tulub's verse about someone following his own opinion illustrates intent and its potential error. He treats 65:6 as resolving on a good course, so neither spouse harms the other; he says mutual consultation would require yata'amaruna fika. Wahidi reports this reading but denies Arabic uses i'tamara bi-shay' for mere intending. Majaz and Thalabi combine intention with counsel; Ibn Ashur says consultation implies intent. [10 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري)-]
- 28:20/q13/p3 They command or urge one another to kill Moses. — Zajjaj, Azhari, and reports in Thalabi, Mawrdi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Tabarsi, Ibn Jawzi, Nasafi and Samin explain reciprocal exhortation. Azhari prefers it to Ibn Qutayba's mere intent; he, Thalabi and Mawrdi cite 65:6 for commanding one another to good, while Abu Hayyan cites 2:233. Wahidi treats command and counsel as compatible. Ibn Atiyya reports Ibn Qutayba as supporting command but rejects his restriction on reciprocal ifta'ala, invoking 65:6 and al-Namir; the reports of Ibn Qutayba's exact gloss diverge. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن قتيبة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الأزهري); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن قتيبة); BIQAI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 28:20/q13/p4 They are watching or lying in wait for Moses. — Jishumi records this as a reported interpretation without argument. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 28:20/q14 How does 'bika' work in the warning?  [بِكَ · grammar]
- 28:20/q14/p1 The pronoun is genitive after the preposition ba'. — Jishumi gives the case explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 28:20/q14/p2 The ba' marks what the council intends to do to Moses. — Ibn Ashur connects the preposition with the implied object of their consultation and murderous intention. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:20/q15 Why do the chiefs mean to kill Moses?  [لِيَقْتُلُوكَ · reports]
- 28:20/q15/p1 They had heard he killed an Egyptian Copt the previous day and decided to kill him for it. — Muqatil, Yahya's Qatada, Wahidi's Ibn Abbas and Muqatil, Qurtubi, Biqai, and Islahi connect the decision with the revealed killing; Tabari and Ibn Abi Hatim report Pharaoh's order and searches. Ibn Ashur says the consultation implies the case reached Pharaoh, matching Exodus. Kur'an Yolu says Pharaoh ordered his capture once the killer was known. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:20/q16 What is Moses told to leave?  [فَٱخْرُجْ · interpretation]
turns on: Literal flight from a threatened city versus Qashani's inward symbolic city.
- 28:20/q16/p1 He must leave the city or Egypt immediately to escape the chiefs. — Wahidi, Baghawi, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Biqai, Bursevi and Islahi specify the city; Tabarsi specifies Egypt. Ibn Ashur says the destination is omitted and supplied by context. Kur'an Yolu continues that Moses left for Midian; Abu Hayyan says he escaped his pursuers on an unfamiliar route, trusting God and asking rescue from the wrongdoers. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:20/q16/p2 In an inward reading, Moses must leave the psychic city's bounds for the spirit's station. — Qashani reads the city as psychic rule and the departure as an inward movement toward spirit. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 28:20/q17 What does the claim of sincere advice convey?  [إِنِّى لَكَ مِنَ ٱلنَّٰصِحِينَ · interpretation]
- 28:20/q17/p1 The man assures Moses that the urgent warning and counsel to flee are genuine and for his benefit. — Muqatil, Wahidi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, and Islahi connect the assurance to departure. Tabatabai says sincere counsel is the opposite of betrayal. Biqai says the assurance removes Moses' possible confidence in royal favor; Ibn Ashur says it explains the command to leave. Zajjaj notes advice is given for another's benefit. Qushayri's messenger stresses he knows the plot. [11 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:20/q18 What governs the advanced phrase 'laka'?  [لَكَ ٱلنَّٰصِحِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether relative al in al-nasihin bars its complement from appearing before it, or whether broader adverbial or definite-article analyses allow it.
- 28:20/q18/p1 It is an explanatory lām attached to an understood expression of advice, rather than governed by al-nasihin. — Zajjaj, Kashshaf, Tabarsi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi and Alusi say a complement cannot precede the relative al or its clause; Nasafi compares 'welcome to you,' Alusi 'sugya laka.' Abu Hayyan and Samin report an omitted 'advisor for you' or an explicative phrase. Tabarsi says it attaches to an understood word. Alusi calls this the majority grammatical view. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض النحاة)]
- 28:20/q18/p2 It can attach to al-nasihin through a permitted advanced complement or broad adverb usage. — Abu Hayyan reports this among three grammatical positions; Samin allows broad adverb usage; Alusi reports grammarians who permit advancement before this al or treat al as a definite article. ## 28:20/q19 How is the verb for advising constructed in Arabic here?  [لَكَ ٱلنَّٰصِحِينَ · meaning]
- 28:20/q19/p1 Nasaha can take li for the person benefited, a common construction, and can also take a direct object. — Zajjaj says nasahtu laka is more common than nasahtuka; Tabarsi permits both. Ibn Ashur calls the li construction common and cites 9:91, while acknowledging direct-object usage. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:20/q20 Why is 'laka' brought forward?  [لَكَ · rhetoric]
- 28:20/q20/p1 The advance preserves the verse ending's cadence. — Ibn Ashur gives the rhythmic reason for putting the prepositional phrase before al-nasihin. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:20/q21 What does the verse imply about warning someone of danger?  [إِنِّى لَكَ مِنَ ٱلنَّٰصِحِينَ · law]
- 28:20/q21/p1 Disclosing a plot against someone is permissible when it serves a religious interest. — Alusi attributes this inference to Qurtubi and others; the warning man's action supplies the example. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي وغيره)]

## 28:20/q22 How does the story continue after the warning?  [* · reports]
- 28:20/q22/p1 Moses fled on an unfamiliar route and sought God's rescue. — Abu Hayyan says Moses trusted the man, escaped pursuit alone, and relied on God; Kur'an Yolu says he left for Midian. Durr's Suddi says a Copt exposed him and searchers pursued him through byways; after Moses prostrated in fear, an angel on horseback guided him toward Midian. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; DURR-FULL(السدي)]

## 28:20/q23 What inward reading is given to the warning?  [* · inward]
- 28:20/q23/p1 The visitor is love or will arising after the self's desire is killed; it warns against the faculties' plot and drives Moses toward spirit. — Qashani reads the far city as hidden preparedness, the slain one as desire, and the fast messenger as love. Imaginal power rises through dispute and misguidance, while the faculties threaten dominance; departure is from psychic rule into the station of spirit. [3 notes: QASHANI]

## 28:20/q24 What does this rescue episode teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:20/q24/p1 God can place His chosen servant in acute danger and then prepare unexpected means of rescue. — Biqai stresses sudden relief after danger. Ibn Ashur says God chooses whom He wills, prepares the means of His purpose, and rescues His messenger; uprightness and love of truth let a perceptive observer see God's care, with a connection to 28:14. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:20/q25 How does Moses' warning bear on Muhammad and the Meccans?  [* · links]
- 28:20/q25/p1 Moses' rescue invites Meccans to recognize Muhammad's character and foreshadows his departure and rescue from oppression. — Ibn Ashur compares the appeal to reason about Muhammad's lifetime among his people in 10:16 and sees the warning as a hint of his later exit from Mecca. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:20/q26 Why does this Moses narrative interrupt and recur with distinct wording?  [* · links]
- 28:20/q26/p1 The man's arrival interrupts an omitted exchange because it is more important; repeated Moses episodes each convey their own nuance. — Ibn Ashur says the man arrives during Moses' exchange with the Egyptian and the dialogue is omitted at the interruption. Burhan says repeated accounts of Moses and Pharaoh differ in wording, order and detail because each supplies a nuance; it contrasts Joseph's collected story in one sura with earlier scripture's separate tellings, citing the surrounding sequence 28:3-43 and 12:1. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 28:20/q27 Where was Surat al-Qasas revealed according to the cited report?  [* · sciences]
- 28:20/q27/p1 The entire sura was revealed in Mecca. — Ibn Jawzi reports this from Hasan, Ata and Ikrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:20/q28 How does context distinguish the senses of a flexible Arabic interrogative?  [* · meaning]
- 28:20/q28/p1 The context selects whether anna asks how, from where, or when. — Ibn Jawzi's Nuzha reports Ibn Qutayba's paired 'how' and 'from where' senses and possible interchange, al-Kumayt's line combining them, and Ali ibn Ubayd Allah's rule that state, place and time determine the reading. These notes discuss a wider lexical principle rather than a word quoted in 28:20. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة,الكميت,شيخنا علي بن عبيد الله)]

## 28:20/q29 How does sincere advice here contrast with Satan's claim to advise?  [إِنِّى لَكَ مِنَ ٱلنَّٰصِحِينَ · theology]
- 28:20/q29/p1 Satan's claimed counsel deceived Adam, whereas true safety calls for seeking refuge in Allah. — Razi says Satan once worshipped God but was expelled for refusing to honor Adam; he then opposed humankind despite people's obedience to him. Satan swore he advised Adam yet caused expulsion, and later swore to mislead humans (38:82), making his claim less credible. Razi says seeking refuge in the name Allah invokes the one worthy of worship whose power, knowledge and wisdom jointly deter wrongdoing: power without knowledge may be evaded by a thief, and both without wisdom may tolerate evil. These are a broader thematic comparison to the verse's sincere adviser. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL]
