# 96:8/q10 What warning and moral point does the verse teach? [* · interpretation]
- 96:8/q10/p1 The inescapable return and reckoning check tyranny, pride, and the illusion of self-sufficiency.
  reasons: Muqātil, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, ʿAlūsī, and Quṭb call it a threat. Biqāʿī calls remembered recompense tyranny's remedy and temporary wealth humbling; Tabrisī says God can destroy and punish the wealthy tyrant. Ibn Kathīr applies judgment to how wealth was acquired and spent. Elmalılı says none may claim sufficiency. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds consolation to the Prophet and that arrogant people do not know their fate; Asad says responsibility before supreme power grounds moral distinctions. Kur'an Yolu calls for preparation for judgment; its preferred translation stresses that God owns possessions people mistake as their own. Bursawī's Persian couplet says deeds last after wealth passes.

# 96:8/q11 How does the verse connect to its surrounding passage and other verses? [* · links]
turns on: The notes offer complementary links: nearby transgression, the sūra's knowledge and wealth contrast, and the wider creation-to-return arc.
- 96:8/q11/p1 It carries the warning from human enrichment and transgression into the specific prayer-forbidding tyrant episode.
  reasons: Muqātil links the warning about Abū Jahl to the forelock in 96:15–16. Rāzī prefers humanity generally in 96:6–8 despite the Abū Jahl reports. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 96:6–8 prepare for the episode beginning at 96:9; he calls 96:8 a parenthesis before the main subject.
  holders (6 notes): MUQATIL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+
  notes: MUQATIL:96:1-8/r9 RAZI:96:6/r7 RAZI-FULL:v32p18#2/r7 IBNASHUR:96:8/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p442/r4 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r2
- 96:8/q11/p2 The ending balances the opening's praise of knowledge with a warning against worldly wealth and urges learning and religion.
  reasons: Bursawī gives this sequence; ʿAlūsī reports it and his full version accepts the woven warning. Bursawī also reports a prayer for refuge from wealth breeding tyranny and poverty bringing forgetfulness.
  holders (4 notes): BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p365/r5 BURSEVI:v10p365/r6 ALUSI:96:8/r6 ALUSI-FULL:v30p182#3/r3
- 96:8/q11/p3 It completes an arc from creation and divine teaching to final return, with God as both beginning and end.
  reasons: Quṭb connects creation, honor, teaching, and return without a partner. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ links creation from a clot to 92:13 and cites 37:68 and 89:28 for later return language. Study Quran calls return a central Quranic theme and cites 2:156.
