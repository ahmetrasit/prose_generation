# 96:8/q07 What reading and codex-spelling observations are supplied? [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · readings]
turns on: Whether atypical codex forms have sound orthographic reasons or are writing mistakes; none of these notes ties an alternative spelling to this verse.
- 96:8/q07/p1 One reported choice concerns a hāʾ's vowel: both forms are sound, but ḍamma is preferred as more regular.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī reports al-Taysīr accepting both and al-Kāfī preferring ḍamma; he also prefers ḍamma because Ḥamza uses it where preceding yāʾ derives from alif. The note does not assert that this hāʾ occurs in 96:8.
  holders (2 notes): NASHR(صاحب التيسير وصاحب الكافي); NASHR+
  notes: NASHR:v1p432/r1 NASHR:v1p432/r2
- 96:8/q07/p2 Several observations explain unusual codex spellings of bāyidin, bāyīkum, bāyiyah, and bāyātinā, without identifying a variant of 96:8.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī says the apparent extra yāʾ is a hamza spelling after an extra alif; al-Sakhāwī reports two yāʾs in Iraqi and Syrian codices and explains imālah, but Ibn al-Jazarī queries his wording or a copying omission. He regards most orthographic departures as purposeful or analogical. A report attributes to ʿUthmān the claim that Arabs would correct errors orally; al-Dānī rejects it because ʿUthmān would not leave baseless errors to repair.
  holders (5 notes): NASHR(السخاوي، بعض الناس); NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني)-
  notes: NASHR:v1p458/r1 NASHR:v1p458/r2 NASHR:v1p458/r3 NASHR:v1p458/r4 NASHR:v1p458/r5 | against: NASHR:v1p458/r6
- 96:8/q07/p3 The reported attribution to ʿUthmān that Arabs would correct codex errors orally is rejected.
  reasons: Al-Dānī says leaving such errors would make writing pointless and burdensome; Ibn al-Jazarī instead expects a sound rationale in early spelling.
  holders (1 notes): NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني); NASHR(بعض الناس)-
  notes: NASHR:v1p458/r6 | against: NASHR:v1p458/r5
sources: NASHR = Ibn al-Jazarī, d. 833

# 96:8/q08 When and in what sense does the return to God occur? [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether return is restricted to the afterlife, includes reversals and dependence in worldly life, also evokes origin, or is read inwardly as self-annihilation.
- 96:8/q08/p1 It is the return after death for resurrection, gathering, judgment, and recompense.
  reasons: Ṭabarī says Muhammad's opponent will taste intolerable punishment; Wāḥidī, Qurṭubī, and Nasafī stress requital for tyranny. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says resurrection and gathering diminish worldly wealth; Abū Ḥayyān adds reckoning. Rāzī cites 14:42–43 for deferred punishment and says the warning benefits the steadfast more than pleasure-seekers. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 78:21–22 for Hell and 84:6 for universal return. Islahi says neglected lordship and divine power will become plain. Kurdish? No: Kur'an Yolu calls it the first Hereafter allusion if this is the first sūra, citing 2:177 and 4:136 for later explicit accountability.

- 96:8/q08/p2 God also returns people in this life from maturity, wealth, and strength to weakness, poverty, decline, and death.
  reasons: Māwardī mentions maturity to decline and life to death; Rāzī makes the reversal symmetrical with God's earlier raising from inertness to life, poverty to wealth, and lowliness to honor.
  holders (4 notes): MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL

- 96:8/q08/p3 Return embraces present dependence on God's decree as well as the final judgment after death.
  reasons: Biqāʿī says no one acts in worldly life except by God's decree and all deeds receive fitting reward or punishment later, citing 95:7–8 in the full version. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that wealth never removes dependence on God or knowledge of one's fate; return can metaphorically suggest need of the one returned to.

- 96:8/q08/p4 All existence returns to God as its source as well as returning for judgment.
  reasons: Asad gives both senses; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connects human origin in a clot with God as beginning and end and cites 92:13.
