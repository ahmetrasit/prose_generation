# 96:8/q13 What other transmitted sayings illustrate knowledge, wealth, and return? [* · reports]
turns on: The notes transmit two different sayings for the same moral contrast.
- 96:8/q13/p1 Two seekers are never satisfied: the seeker of knowledge and the seeker of worldly gain.
  reasons: Ibn Kathīr says it was transmitted as a Prophetic report; Bursawī attributes it to Ibn Masʿūd and adds that knowledge increases divine favor whereas world-seeking increases rebellion.
  holders (2 notes): IBNKATHIR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود)
  notes: IBNKATHIR:96:8/r3 BURSEVI:v10p365/r1
- 96:8/q13/p2 The Prophet sought refuge from wealth that causes tyranny and poverty that causes forgetfulness.
  reasons: Bursawī reports this prayer as part of the knowledge and wealth contrast.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p365/r6
sources: BURSEVI = İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127; IBNKATHIR = Ibn Kathīr, d. 774

# 96:8/q14 What inward error makes people imagine themselves independent of the Lord? [* · inward]
- 96:8/q14/p1 People mistake entrusted divine names and attributes manifested within them for their own possessions and become proud.
  reasons: Bursawī says these manifestations should instead be recognized as signs of their Creator and of continued dependence on Him.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p365/r7
sources: BURSEVI = İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127

# 96:8/q15 What sūra facts and chronology are reported? [* · sciences]
turns on: The chronology turns on whether the prayer passage belongs immediately after the first five revelations, given the reported later start of obligatory prayer.
- 96:8/q15/p1 The sūra is Meccan and has seventeen verses.
  reasons: Al-Jishumī states both particulars.
  holders (1 notes): JISHUMI
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7475/r1
- 96:8/q15/p2 Whether these verses followed the first five immediately is disputed.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports that mention of prayer seems difficult if obligatory prayer began years later at the Night Journey; some place intervening revelation and later attachment, while others deny the first five were the earliest revelation.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p442/r5
- 96:8/q15/p3 If al-ʿAlaq was the first sūra, this is the Quran's first allusion to the Hereafter.
  reasons: Kur'an Yolu attributes this conditional conclusion to those accepting the first-sūra premise and contrasts later explicit statements of accountability, citing 2:177 and 4:136.
  holders (1 notes): KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bu görüşü benimseyenler)
  notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:8/r1
- 96:8/q15/p4 Reciting this sūra earns a reward like reciting the whole set of shorter sūras.
  reasons: Bayḍāwī transmits this as a Prophetic report.
  holders (1 notes): BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)
  notes: BAYDAWI-FULL:96:18-19/r4
sources: BAYDAWI-FULL = al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; JISHUMI = al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR = Hayreddin Karaman, Mustafa Çağrıcı, İbrahim Kâfi Dönmez, Sadrettin Gümüş

# 96:8/q16 How is the earlier rebuke kalla related to this warning? [* · links]
- 96:8/q16/p1 Kalla anticipates and rebukes the later description of one who forbids a servant's prayer.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says kalla normally follows the speech it rejects; its initial placement here points forward to the target in 96:9 and following, and he compares an initial negative particle in Imru al-Qays.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p441/r7 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p442/r1
sources: IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393
