# 96:8/q01 Why is the return asserted with inna? [إِنَّ · rhetoric]
- 96:8/q01/p1 It reinforces a warning that tyrants behave as though they deny their return.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says their thorough forgetfulness warrants treating them as deniers and strengthening the indirect warning.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR
  notes: IBNASHUR:96:8/r3
sources: IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393

# 96:8/q02 Who is addressed by 'your Lord,' and what does the address do? [رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the rhetorical shift addresses the human directly or the sequence of preceding addresses continues to address the Prophet.
- 96:8/q02/p1 The human being, especially the transgressor, is addressed directly as a warning.
  reasons: Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, and Nasafī call the turn to direct address a threat about transgression; ʿAlūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report this as an emphatic shift, although they prefer the Prophet as addressee.

# 96:8/q03 What does placing 'to your Lord' first convey? [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the advance is explained as exclusivity or emphasis; the notes offer no argument that these effects exclude each other.
- 96:8/q03/p1 It restricts the return to God alone, with no independent or shared recourse elsewhere.
  reasons: ʿAlūsī applies the restriction to all creation through death and resurrection; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ joins it to God's sole possession of the first and last, citing 92:13. Bursawī says the tyrant then sees his transgression's outcome.

- 96:8/q03/p2 It gives the phrase emphasis.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly names emphasis as the effect of advancing ilā rabbika.

# 96:8/q04 What does al-rujʿā mean? [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · meaning]

- 96:8/q04/p1 The act of returning.
  reasons: Abū ʿUbayda and al-Akhfash are reported by Wāḥidī; Wāḥidī equates it with rujūʿan and marjiʿan. Ibn Qutayba, Ṭabrisī, Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurtubī, Rāzī, Bursawī, ʿAlūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Abū Ḥayyān give the same basic gloss. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this majority gloss.

# 96:8/q05 What is the form and syntactic case of al-rujʿā? [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: The notes treat form and case as compatible analyses, while differing in their reported pattern labels.
- 96:8/q05/p1 It is a verbal noun on a fuʿlā or faʿlā type pattern, with a feminine final alif.
  reasons: Wāḥidī calls it faʿlā; Baghawī and Qurṭubī give a faʿlā/faʿla description. Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give fuʿlā; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares ʿuqbā and Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, ʿAlūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr compare bushrā. Abū Ḥayyān and Bursawī identify the feminine alif. The sources' pattern labels vary while sharing a verbal-noun analysis.
