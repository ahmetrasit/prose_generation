# 7:168: 26 questions

## 7:168/q01 What does God dividing them across the earth describe?  [وَقَطَّعْنَٰهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ أُمَمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the narrative continues the earlier favors of 7:160 or describes later punitive dispersal.
- 7:168/q01/p1 He scattered the Israelites or Jews into separate communities throughout the lands. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās say Jews lived in virtually every region; Ibn ʿAbbās says no territory lacked a group. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī and others say their common affairs and unity broke apart. Ibn Kathīr cites 17:104. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the Assyrian, Babylonian and Roman exiles, including the dates 721, 578 and 530 BCE as stated in his account; al-Biqāʿī relates dispersion to captivity. Elmalılı contrasts the earlier unified twelve tribes in 7:160 with this decline. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the expression can describe either blameworthy dispersion or a favorable division according to context. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported in al-Durr, says that every part of the earth contains a group of the dispersed Jews. [39 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(commentators); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:168/q01/p2 God multiplied them and settled them throughout the promised Holy Land as a favor. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's own alternative continues the account of divine favors in 7:160; he says 'earth' would then mean the land of Jerusalem, now inhabited throughout, and that God alternated mercy with requital of their masses' deeds. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:168/q02 Who are the people divided into communities?  [وَقَطَّعْنَٰهُمْ أُمَمًۭا · referent]
- 7:168/q02/p1 The Children of Israel, identified also as the Jews. — Mujāhid names the Jews; Muqātil names the Children of Israel. Al-Rāzī says this shows the preceding promise in 7:167 concerned the whole Jewish people. The Study Quran calls it the Jewish Diaspora. Al-Durr reports Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly identifying the dispersed people as Jews. [14 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]

## 7:168/q03 When did the described division and mixture of righteous and others occur?  [وَقَطَّعْنَٰهُمْ مِّنْهُمُ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ · reports]
turns on: The historical point at which Israel was dispersed, the force of 7:169, and whether righteousness remained after Jesus.
- 7:168/q03/p1 Before Jesus and the later apostasy, after Israel's earlier unity ended. — Al-Ṭabarī says the distinction predates Jesus. Ibn ʿAṭiyya places it after Solomon's kingdom, before Jesus, since he says none remained righteous after rejecting Jesus, but warns that historical accounts are confused. Ibn ʿĀshūr also reports a Babylonian setting with Daniel among the righteous. The Study Quran notes commentators who put righteous Jews before Muhammad or even Jesus, citing 7:159, 3:113 and 3:199. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); ALUSI-FULL(الطبري); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 7:168/q03/p2 The division concerns Jews living before Islam, after the dispersion that began later than Moses. — Kuranyolu says 7:169's opening points to pre-Islamic Jewish groups and that dispersal began after Moses' time. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 7:168/q03/p3 It speaks of Jews in Moses' own time. — Reported by some exegetes; Kuranyolu argues that the dispersion began later and 7:169 indicates pre-Islamic Jewish groups. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı müfessirler); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 7:168/q04 Why were they divided and scattered?  [وَقَطَّعْنَٰهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether division is punitive exile, spiritually useful separation, or favorable settlement.
- 7:168/q04/p1 As punishment that broke their solidarity and power. — Al-Māwardī lists further punishment and broken mutual support. Al-Ṭabrisī says their differing inclinations weakened them and brought humiliation like jizya. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say it prevented renewed collective strength. Al-Biqāʿī links exile to covenant breaking and captivity, leaving only a few per town, unable to resist oppression. Elmalılı says wicked leaders and a wicked majority brought political collapse despite righteous individuals. Kuranyolu prefers this explanation; Ibn ʿĀshūr says righteous exiles were given honor. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı)+]
- 7:168/q04/p2 To distinguish the righteous from the wicked and serve their religious welfare. — Al-Māwardī mentions distinguishing the two groups, anticipating the next clause; al-Ṭabrisī reports division for religious welfare, with one group righteous and another disobedient. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 7:168/q04/p3 As a favor, to multiply and settle them throughout the Holy Land. — Ibn ʿĀshūr relates this reading to the favors of 7:160 and says God treated them with mercy and requited their deeds in turn. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:168/q05 What does 'in the earth' designate?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: The choice between global dispersal and Ibn ʿĀshūr's favored-land interpretation.
- 7:168/q05/p1 The regions of the earth generally. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly calls the expression generic; many accounts say scarcely any inhabited region lacks a Jewish group. Al-Durr reports Ibn ʿAbbās saying that every part of the earth contains a group of them. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:168/q05/p2 The promised land of Jerusalem. — On Ibn ʿĀshūr's favorable interpretation, the wording unlike 7:160 indicates settlement throughout that land. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:168/q06 How does 'cutting' express their dispersal?  [وَقَطَّعْنَٰهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the lexical emphasis is separation after union or intensity.
- 7:168/q06/p1 It figuratively applies physical separation of joined things to a formerly united people. — Al-Jurjānī says tearing properly applies to cloth, so this is a close metaphor for scattering, with 34:19 as a parallel; cutting communities likewise removes prior union. He distinguishes expressions for interrupting speech or spending time, and says apparent oddity in 'bankrupt in manliness' or 'deprived of a liver' can arise from convention rather than metaphor. Ibn al-Jawzī says the root means separating joined things, with contextual uses including estrangement, a portion of night, a flock and short garments. [7 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 7:168/q06/p2 The usage here conveys severity or intensity. — Ibn al-Jawzī's lexical source labels this occurrence in that way. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 7:168/q07 What does 'communities' mean?  [أُمَمًۭا · meaning]
- 7:168/q07/p1 Distinct groups or factions. — Ibn ʿAbbās cites Bishr b. Abī Ḥāzim's verse about the groups of Qays Ghaylān for this sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains umam as the plural of umma, community or group. [4 notes: MAJAZ; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 7:168/q08 What is the grammatical role of 'communities'?  [أُمَمًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verb has a transformative two-object sense or 'communities' describes the already divided object.
- 7:168/q08/p1 A circumstantial accusative describing the object 'them' as groups. — Abū Ḥayyān gives this parsing; al-Samīn says it is a ḥāl from the object of qaṭṭaʿnāhum; al-Bursawī also gives it. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:168/q08/p2 A second object of 'We divided them.' — Al-Ḥawfī gives this in Abū Ḥayyān; al-Samīn and al-Bursawī explain that qaṭṭaʿnāhum implies 'made them' (ṣayyar or jaʿal). [6 notes: JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:168/q09 How is the hamza at the junction 'in the earth, communities' read?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ أُمَمًۭا · readings]
turns on: The permitted hamza easing processes and rejection of naql.
- 7:168/q09/p1 In connected reading it may be fully realized, eased between the vowels, or replaced by pure yāʾ. — Ibn al-Jazarī's Nashr treats the separated hamzas here in reverse order to yashāʾu ilā; it says open medial hamza after kasra becomes yāʾ when eased, with other vowel pairings eased between-between, and enumerates nine separated-word combinations. Its discussion of fī l-kitāb ulāʾika adds six options involving duration. [3 notes: NASHR]
- 7:168/q09/p2 Additional naql forms across the two mīm-vowel states are inadmissible. — Nashr says allowing naql would yield twenty-four additional forms, but neither mīm-vowel state is valid. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 7:168/q10 Who are 'the righteous' among them?  [مِّنْهُمُ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ · referent]
turns on: The required content and time of righteousness: faith in Muhammad, earlier covenantal fidelity, geographical community, or continuing Torah observance.
- 7:168/q10/p1 Israelites who believed in Muhammad, often those who met him or accepted Islam in Medina. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid are cited for those who met the Prophet and believed; Mujāhid says Muslims from the People of the Book and Saʿīd calls them members of Muhammad's community. Some include belief in Jesus. Al-Rāzī and others mention Medina. Ibn ʿĀshūr names ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and Mukhayriq as later examples. Kuranyolu and Study Quran report this reading but object to limiting the verse to converts; Study Quran invokes present tense and an indefinite future. [17 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(عطاء ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(eski müfessirlerin çoğunluğu); STUDYQURAN(commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-; STUDYQURAN-]
- 7:168/q10/p2 Israelites faithful to God, His prophets and commands, including observant followers of Moses' law. — Al-Ṭabarī says belief in God and His messengers before Jesus. Al-Ṭabrisī adds obedience; al-Bursawī identifies observant Mosaic adherents. Al-Biqāʿī says they followed the later abrogating dispensation when it came, as their scripture called them to do. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes Moses' law, later prophets and Jesus, then belief in Muhammad when he came. Study Quran says Torah law and Sabbath observance remain possible for Jews, citing 7:163; Kuranyolu prefers al-Ṭabarī's broader sense over a convert-only reading. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطبري); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)+]
- 7:168/q10/p3 A righteous Israelite community beyond China. — Al-Kalbī links it to the truth-guiding community of 7:159, beyond China; al-Baghawī locates it beyond the river Udaf. Al-Ālūsī also locates it there. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:168/q10/p4 Those of Moses' time, when some guided by truth, or those who died before Mosaic law was abrogated. — Al-Rāzī reports the Moses-era view; al-Qurṭubī reports those who kept religion unaltered and died before abrogation. Study Quran cites 7:159, 3:113 and 3:199 for commentators' earlier-Jewish reading. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(commentators); STUDYQURAN-]
- 7:168/q10/p5 Righteous Jews can continue as Jews by observing Torah law. — Study Quran argues from the present tense and open future against limiting righteousness to the past or converts; it mentions Sabbath observance in 7:163. Kuranyolu argues that the Quran does not impose the narrower convert-versus-Jew classification. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN+]

## 7:168/q11 How is 'the righteous' parsed grammatically?  [ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause starts a subject-predicate statement or 'the righteous' relates appositionally to 'communities'.
- 7:168/q11/p1 A nominative subject, with its predicate supplied or brought before it. — Al-Qurṭubī calls it the subject; al-Ālūsī permits a fronted, omitted predicate or the agent of the adverbial phrase. Al-Ālūsī adds that the clause may describe the communities or be circumstantial. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:168/q11/p2 An appositional substitute (badal) for 'communities'. — Abū al-Baqāʾ allows badal as well as an adjective analysis in al-Samīn. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 7:168/q12 Who are those 'below that'?  [وَمِنْهُمْ دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether ṣalāḥ means faith itself or a higher degree of obedience, and whether the paired communities are geographically identified.
- 7:168/q12/p1 Unbelievers, especially Israelites who rejected Muhammad or remained Jewish. — Muqātil contrasts believers with unbelievers. Saʿīd identifies nonbelievers in Muhammad; al-Kalbī says disbelievers. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's condition is that ṣalāḥ means faith. Al-Rāzī cites 7:169's call to return to argue they had persisted in Judaism; Abū Ḥayyān prefers unbelievers for the same reason. Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān reject making dūn lexically synonymous with ghayr, while accepting that the lower group is different. Kuranyolu reports the early convert-versus-Jew classification and rejects making it exclusive. [18 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(عطاء ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(eski müfessirlerin çoğunluğu); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 7:168/q12/p2 Those lower in righteousness, including sinners or disobedient believers as well as possibly unbelievers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says if ṣalāḥ means worship and goodness beyond faith, lesser believers fit. Al-Ṭabrisī describes those obeying some commands and violating others before apostasy. Al-Rāzī reports a lesser-righteous objection as closer to the surface wording but rejects it on 7:169. Al-Ālūsī says they believe but sin; Ibn ʿĀshūr sees varying degrees. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Zamakhsharī include unbelievers and sinners; Study Quran identifies Jews who do not follow their law. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(قائل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 7:168/q12/p3 Jews on this side of the river, as opposed to the righteous community beyond China. — Al-Kalbī's geographical report in al-Baghawī sets the two communities on opposite sides of the river Udaf. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي)]

## 7:168/q13 What does 'below' mean as a word?  [دُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'other than' is the word's meaning or merely follows from its sense of lower rank.
- 7:168/q13/p1 Originally short of a spatial limit, extended to lower rank. — Abū Ḥayyān derives rank from locative use. Sibawayh describes falling short of a limit, and al-Ṣaffār says specifically short of the next limit, with lower knowledge a metaphor. Al-Zarkashī notes spatial directions, noun and adjective uses, including an adjective meaning bad. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(الصفار,سيبويه)]
- 7:168/q13/p2 It means 'other than' in this context when righteousness means faith. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's gloss yields unbelievers. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn reject exact lexical synonymy with ghayr but accept that whoever is below a group is necessarily other than it. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 7:168/q14 How is 'among them are those below that' parsed and inflected?  [وَمِنْهُمْ دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted noun supplies the subject, whether adverbial form is retained, and whether dūna can function as the predicate here.
- 7:168/q14/p1 A fronted predicate 'among them' and an omitted subject such as 'people' or 'group', described by 'below that'. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 37:164 and 'some of us departed, some stayed' for omitted described nouns. Al-Taftāzānī says the first of two adverbials is predicate and the second subject, with reversal less apt. Al-Ālūsī says omission after min or fī is normal. Ibn ʿĀshūr says dūna can describe an omitted group; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Majmaʿ give the same group analysis. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(التفتازاني); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع)]
- 7:168/q14/p2 Dūna is underlyingly nominative as subject, but retains an adverbial accusative form. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Akhfash know no reader who raises it. Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū al-Ḥasan compare 6:94 and 60:3; al-Majmaʿ similarly compares 'between' in 6:94 and another judgement expression, cited as 3:94 and 6:159 in its note. Al-Zarkashī records two dialects of omitted-noun inflection and nominative and accusative readings. [8 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS; TABRISI(أبو الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي وأبو الحسن); BURHAN; TABATABAI(المجمع)]
- 7:168/q14/p3 Dūna is nominative as the adjective standing in for an omitted noun. — Al-Zamakhsharī points to 37:164. Al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān give an omitted people/group analysis. Al-Zarkashī notes that some dialects decline the adjective when its noun is suppressed. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN]
- 7:168/q14/p4 Dūna is an adverb or predicate in the construction. — Abū al-Baqāʾ compares 6:94. Al-Samīn rejects making it a predicate here. Ibn ʿĀshūr alternatively makes min a noun meaning 'some', minhum the subject and dūna the predicate. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 7:168/q15 What does 'that' in 'below that' point to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative points to the quality of righteousness or the group bearing it.
- 7:168/q15/p1 Righteousness itself, with 'people of' understood where needed. — Al-Samīn says this balances the two groups; al-Ālūsī reports the same alternative. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:168/q15/p2 The righteous group, with singular 'that' used for a plural referent. — Al-Bursawī notes a singular demonstrative can point to dual or plural; al-Samīn says this requires no omitted 'people of'. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:168/q16 What does 'We tested them' mean and how does it join the earlier clause?  [وَبَلَوْنَٰهُم · grammar]
- 7:168/q16/p1 It means God tested them and is coordinated with 'We divided them'. — Al-Jishumī explicitly joins the verbs: God divided them into groups and tested them, while some were righteous and others less so. Al-Wāḥidī says God dealt with them as a tester would. [5 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 7:168/q17 What are the good and bad things used in the test?  [بِٱلْحَسَنَٰتِ وَٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pairs designate experienced worldly conditions, divine recompense, or inward effects of deeds.
- 7:168/q17/p1 Worldly blessings and afflictions, especially prosperity versus hardship, abundance versus drought, and health versus illness. — Ibn Qutayba adds good and bad fortune; al-Ṭabarī details comfort, provision, austere living, calamities and lost wealth. Ibn ʿAbbās gives al-ḥaṣb for good and tribulation or punishment for bad; Mujāhid gives ease. Al-Dāmghānī specifies plentiful versus scarce rain and cites 7:95 and 30:36; Ibn al-Jawzī cites 7:131 and 7:95. Al-Ṭabrisī mentions harms to life and property. Al-Biqāʿī recalls historical blessings and calamities; Elmalılı adds wealth and encounters with Magi and Romans; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the terms cover what pleases or troubles, citing 7:131, and characterize God's distribution of fortune. Study Quran points to general worldly provision and adversity. [34 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:168/q17/p2 Reward and punishment. — Al-Māwardī includes this as one explanation alongside blessings and afflictions and fertile and barren years. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 7:168/q17/p3 Inwardly, good works can bring self-admiration while misdeeds can bring remorse and repentance. — The Najmiyya interpretation contrasts Iblis' self-admiration with Adam's repentant return in 7:23. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 7:168/q17/p4 Inwardly, good deeds involve witnessing the divine agent and forgetting oneself; sins involve wavering and conspicuous awareness of oneself. — Al-Qushayrī says good deeds are acts in which the divine agent is witnessed without distraction from the Originator, while sins involve wavering between action and delay, permission and shortcoming. A saying he reports adds that a good deed makes one forget oneself, while a sin makes the self conspicuous. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 7:168/q17/p5 Inwardly, good deeds are wakeful moments and days free of affliction; sins are divine abandonment and continuing deprivation. — Al-Qushayrī reports this as another saying about good deeds and sins. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 7:168/q18 What responses do good and bad trials test or prompt?  [وَبَلَوْنَٰهُم بِٱلْحَسَنَٰتِ وَٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتِ · interpretation]
turns on: The general purpose of testing and the reported response of this people.
- 7:168/q18/p1 Blessings test gratitude and draw hope for continued or greater good; hardship tests patience and moves people to seek relief and obey. — Ibn Qutayba says both are tests, as is fitna. Al-Wāḥidī's people of meaning, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Bursawī explain hope of more or fear of loss and desire for relief. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts patience and gratitude with distress and disbelief. Kuranyolu adds fulfilling duties and steadfast worship, with worldly unity and afterlife salvation as benefits; Study Quran cites 21:35 for the universal scope. Al-Bursawī quotes a Persian touchstone poem on tests exposing truth. Al-Qushayrī says that testing by blessing and hardship calls for gratitude and patience respectively, and makes people's sincerity or hypocrisy manifest to creation. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أهل المعاني); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; QUSHAYRI]
- 7:168/q18/p2 These Israelites failed both tests, showing neither gratitude in ease nor patience in hardship. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb says they did not endure prosperity; al-Sulamī says they failed both. Kashifi says they were arrogant in blessing and in hardship insulted God as poor and His hand as bound, citing 3:181 and 5:64. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب القرظي); SULAMI; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 7:168/q19 What return is sought from them?  [لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'return' presupposes prior obedience, or can mean a first turn toward truth or a return to innate disposition.
- 7:168/q19/p1 Repentance, leaving wrongdoing or unbelief and returning to God, truth and obedience. — Al-Ḥasan and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss it as repentance. Al-Ṭabarī specifies obedience and repentance from sins; al-Zamakhsharī says cease wrongdoing. Al-Wāḥidī's people of meaning picture a turn from falsehood toward truth. Al-Ṭabrisī adds compliance with God's command, al-Qurṭubī return from unbelief, and Ibn ʿĀshūr return from covenant breaking; he says blessings and hardships or the contrasting groups' fortunes may bring reflection. Elmalılı says some repented and others had not by Muhammad's era; Asad says return to righteousness. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 7:168/q19/p2 Turning toward the right path or returning to an original innate disposition, even if they had not followed the command before. — Al-Ṭabrisī compares urging someone in danger to return to the straight path and offers innate disposition as another sense of return. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 7:168/q20 How is the hoped-for return worded?  [لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 7:168/q20/p1 The hope is expressed from a human observer's perspective, and the phrase explains the preceding tests. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the wording is from the human observer's standpoint. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an explanatory resumption after the tests. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:168/q21 How does this passage connect to its surrounding account?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether this is a continuation of punishment, a fresh address, or a return to favors.
- 7:168/q21/p1 It continues Israel's story of covenant breaking, punishment and later circumstances. — Abū Muslim connects it to earlier notice to Israel that Muhammad would come and prevail over the Jews until Resurrection. Al-Biqāʿī relates Torah warnings to the earlier warning at 5:60. Al-Ālūsī calls the dispersion, like 7:167, a Quranic report of the unseen. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a resumption of their circumstances and says the good-and-bad tests detail God's treatment of both groups. Study Quran says the discussion returns to later disobedience and threatened punishments. [6 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:168/q21/p2 It is a new address to the Prophet telling people about these matters. — Abū ʿAlī proposes a fresh address in contrast to Abū Muslim's continuity with the Israelite story. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)]
- 7:168/q21/p3 It continues the account of divine favors, echoing Israel's division in 7:160. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's own alternative reads the division as multiplication and settlement through the promised land; he says mention of 'earth' distinguishes this from 7:160 and acknowledges righteous members fairly after accounts of aggression. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:168/q22 What events in the cited Torah account illuminate the trials and scattering?  [* · reports]
- 7:168/q22/p1 The covenant narrative recounts divine sustenance and warnings that disobedience would lead to affliction, dwindling, terror and exile. — Al-Biqāʿī quotes the fifth book of the Torah on commandments and reverence, threatened afflictions for descendants, dwindling numbers, dispersal, terror and movement toward Egypt for enslavement. Moses recalls plagues against Pharaoh and Israel's lack of understanding; forty wilderness years without worn clothes or sandals and without bread taught them God was their Lord. Moses commands obedience and warns of individuals or tribes turning to other gods. The quoted warning explains curse, destruction and exile as covenant breaking. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL(السفر الخامس,موسى)]

## 7:168/q23 What broader judgment about Israel and divine testing is drawn?  [* · theology]
- 7:168/q23/p1 Righteous and corrupt people coexist, so condemning an entire people is unjust; trials test everyone. — Elmalılı says a wicked majority controlled Israel's polity despite righteous individuals. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'some of them' implies both groups were numerous and that naming the righteous fairly acknowledges them. Kuranyolu argues collective condemnation contradicts Quranic justice and the conditional hope of mercy in 17:4–8; it says 7:168–170 affirm justice for Jews and all people. Study Quran cites 21:35 to universalize good and bad trials. [8 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı); STUDYQURAN]
- 7:168/q23/p2 God lets people proceed according to His knowledge of whether they will become righteous or corrupt. — Al-Qushayrī says God gives people scope to proceed in keeping with what He knows they will become: sound and righteous or corrupt and disobedient. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 7:168/q24 What incidental neighboring expressions or examples are explained in the verse's discussion?  [* · meaning]
- 7:168/q24/p1 Nearby lexical and literary examples clarify successors, worldly temptation, studying, and the danger of human company. — Abū ʿUbayda's Majāz says khalaf forms with silent or vocalized second consonant can coincide, though some reserve one for corrupt and one for good successors; ʿaraḍ hādhā al-adnā is nearby worldly desire; darasū is studying books, with Arabic examples for memorizing and reading a teacher's text. Al-Bursawī quotes al-Yāfiʿī advising withdrawal from people and God's companionship because examined hearts resemble scorpions. [4 notes: MAJAZ; BURSEVI(اليافعي)]

## 7:168/q25 What is the point of wording the verse as two 'among them' groups?  [مِّنْهُمُ ٱلصَّٰلِحُونَ وَمِنْهُمْ دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
- 7:168/q25/p1 It divides the Israelites into two groups. — Al-Ālūsī identifies the paired wording as a two-part division. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:168/q26 What follows when divine warning and respite fail to bring people back?  [* · inward]
- 7:168/q26/p1 God warns people to establish the case, then punishes them if admonition fails; once respite ends and a servant is rejected, there is no recovery. — Al-Qushayrī says God gives warning and advance instruction before destroying those unmoved by admonition. He says the end of respite leaves only annihilation and a servant fallen from God's regard never recovers. He likens rejection to dismissal by a king and cites an anonymous poem about lasting rejection. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
