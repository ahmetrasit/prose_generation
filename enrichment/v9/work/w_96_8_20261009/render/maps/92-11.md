# 92:11: 18 questions

## 92:11/q01 What force does mā have in 'mā yughni'?  [وَمَا يُغْنِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is a negative particle, an interrogative pronoun, or carries both effects; the interrogative can intensify rebuke, while the negative makes the prediction explicit.
- 92:11/q01/p1 It is a negation: his wealth will not benefit him. — Qurṭubī says the denial is absolute at death. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a prediction at the fall into Hell. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers it for its emphatic assertion; Corpus Coranicum prefers it by comparison with 111:2 and two biblical intertexts. Asad gives this as a possible translation. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM(Bergsträßer); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI+; CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 92:11/q01/p2 It is a rhetorical question denying or rebuking any claim that wealth could help. — Kashshāf explains that the question denies any benefit. Qurṭubī calls it rebuke; Biqāʿī and Iṣlāḥī say it adds force. Naḥḥās makes mā the direct object of yughni. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the initial wāw can then start a new sentence. [23 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM(Bergsträßer); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 92:11/q01/p3 The wording intentionally bears both a reproachful question and a negation. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says it asks what wealth can avail while asserting that it will avail nothing. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:11/q02 What kind of wealth is meant, and what value can it have at the fall?  [مَالُهُۥٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the explanation specifies the hoarded wealth and its missed uses, or states the broader inability of wealth to avert the end.
- 92:11/q02/p1 The wealth he withheld or hoarded cannot rescue him then; benefit belongs to wealth spent rightly and good deeds sent ahead. — Muqātil names Abū Sufyān's hoard. Wāḥidī and Baghawī identify what was withheld from good. Rāzī says wealth stays with heirs, citing 6:94 and 19:80; only charity and proper dues accompany a person. Biqāʿī contrasts ordinary crises, when money helps, with this hour. Elmālılı says the hoard increases loss and punishment. Quṭb says wealth can be hoarded or treated as a substitute for God and guidance. Iṣlāḥī says spending for God preserves it for eternal life. Kur'an Yolu says it cannot ransom him, citing 5:36 and 70:11–16. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts withholding here with giving for purification in 92:18. [18 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:11/q02/p2 Wealth in general cannot benefit its owner when death, ruin, or punishment overtakes him. — Mujāhid and Qushayrī place the loss of benefit at death; Ṭabarī places it at Resurrection and ruin. Qāshānī specifies Hell's abyss. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says this certain end warns misers and tyrants that escape cannot be bought. [9 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:11/q03 Why is 'idhā' used for the fall?  [إِذَا · rhetoric]
- 92:11/q03/p1 It marks the fall as certain or recurrent, making the warning unavoidable. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares certain events introduced by idhā—death, the end of sacred months, sunrise, and completed prayer, citing 5:106, 9:5, 18:17, and 62:10. When idhā and in occur together, idhā marks the more common event; in can mark rare, doubtful, hypothetical or impossible events, as in 13:5, 5:6, 4:25, and 28:71. Death, ruin, grave, or Hell is certain here, so in would not serve. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:11/q04 What does 'taraddā' mean here?  [تَرَدَّىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the fall points to death, the grave, Hell, a burial shroud, moral error, or a general image of ruin; authorities also dispute whether radā or physical falling is the controlling Arabic sense.
- 92:11/q04/p1 He falls into the Fire or Hell's depths. — Abū Ṣāliḥ, Qatāda, Zayd b. Aslam and Ibn ʿAbbās are among those cited. Thaʿlabī has a pit in Hell; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān picture falling from its edges. Ṭabarī prefers this as taraddā's established use, while death usually uses radā. Rāzī cites 5:3 for a fall; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the implied destination is Hell from 92:10 and the wealth cannot avert its punishment. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers this with Ṭabarī; in her later discussion Ibn ʿAbbās grounds the Islamic contextual sense in the following warning (92:14–16), 37:55–56, and 41:22. ʿAdī b. Zayd's death poem does not, she says, prove the specifically Hellish sense. - 92:11/q04/p2 He dies or perishes. — Mujāhid is repeatedly cited; Wāḥidī also reports Muqātil and Abū Ṣāliḥ. Abū ʿUbayda glosses perishing and al-Mubarrad derives the verb from destruction. Kashshāf, Nasafī, Ālūsī and others connect it to al-radā. Qurṭubī cites a line for destruction. ʿAdī b. Zayd's verse uses the verb of one overtaken by death while hoping for long life; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says that context proves death, though not specifically Hell. Ṭabarī argues against death as the usual sense of taraddā, distinguishing radā. - 92:11/q04/p3 He falls into the grave or its pit. — Māwardī attributes death and the grave to Mujāhid and Qatāda. Rāzī derives this from falling from a height, invoking 5:3. Ibn ʿAbbās is cited for the grave by Ālūsī; Biqāʿī joins grave and Hell as downward ruin. Samīn calls it a euphemism. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects that burial shrouds cover believers too, so this does not fit a warning to the miser. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN(آخر,قوم,مالك بن الذئب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(قوم); ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI-]
- 92:11/q04/p5 He sinks into error and disobedience. — Māwardī offers this further interpretation of the fall, distinct from death and Hell. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 92:11/q04/p6 The verb conveys a literal fall from height or, by extension, destruction or ruin, without fixing its destination. — Jishumī derives the destructive sense from al-radā. Qurṭubī gives a well, a mountain fall, and the idiom for where a person went. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ describes a rock or well fall extending to general ruin, calls taraddā the intransitive counterpart of arda, and surveys 20:15–16, 41:23, 37:56, 6:137, and 5:3 as the root's Quranic occurrences. Elmālılı alternatively derives it from taraddud, originally taraddada, rolling or falling. Wāḥidī points to al-mutardiya at 5:3. Ibn al-Athīr glosses a hadith's causative form as bringing into ruin. ## 92:11/q05 How does the wording of verse 11 connect to verse 10?  [وَمَا يُغْنِى عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥٓ إِذَا تَرَدَّىٰٓ · links]
turns on: The notes range from the immediate link to the hard path in verse 10 to the larger contrast between the surah's two paths.
- 92:11/q05/p1 It continues the promise of facilitation to the hard path and shows its end: Hell, where the miser's wealth cannot save him. — Tustarī says God enables the deeds of Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the clause as coordinated with verse 10 and supplies Hell as the unstated destination. Quṭb says sin can feel easy in this world although Hell is the utmost hardship, and the person's stumbles end there. Ibn ʿAṭiyya connects the critical moment of ruin with the earlier giving and withholding of wealth. [6 notes: TUSTARI; IBNATIYYA; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:11/q05/p2 The surah contrasts two chosen paths, their corresponding ease or hardship, and the fate of wealth. — Quṭb sees two enduring paths and says God facilitates the one each person chooses. Corpus Coranicum traces opposed behavior and recompense through verses 5–13 and 14–21. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ relates the hoarder in verse 11 to the giver seeking purification in verse 18. Iṣlāḥī takes 11–14 as an address to Quraysh: revelation guides without compulsion, and acceptance benefits them while God owns both worlds. [4 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 92:11/q06 Does verse 11 close the account of the wicked or begin the next theological statement?  [وَمَا يُغْنِى عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥٓ إِذَا تَرَدَّىٰٓ · links]
turns on: The pronoun and ġ-n-y echo point backward, while Neuwirth's classification groups the verse with the following theological lines.
- 92:11/q06/p1 It belongs to the wicked person's section. — Corpus Coranicum prefers this because 'him' refers back to the wicked person, the root ġ-n-y echoes verse 8, and inna starts a fresh statement at verse 12; the result is a structural asymmetry. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 92:11/q06/p2 It belongs with the theological predication of verses 12–13. — Corpus Coranicum reports Neuwirth's classification. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); CORPUSCORANICUM-]

## 92:11/q07 How does verse 11 fit the surah's wider arrangement?  [* · links]
turns on: Different notes analyze the mirror pattern, the relation to the previous surah, and the move to the final outcomes.
- 92:11/q07/p1 The surah mirrors two groups, reversing the order of salvation and damnation in its final stanza. — Corpus Coranicum says the outer blessed sections are joined by ittaqā in verse 5 and atqā in verse 17; the earlier lists of conduct precede reward or warning, while the last stanza gives warning and promise before conduct. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:11/q07/p2 The oath and its response situate differing human efforts and the failure of wealth within the surah's relation to the preceding surah. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts the previous surah's oath by the shaped soul, intellect, and knowledge with this surah's oath by the body and its sexes; the response says human striving differs and emphasizes wealth's failure at the fall. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:11/q07/p3 The section after this verse states the just and inevitable ends of both groups. — Quṭb says the next section first establishes that both outcomes are just, true, and inevitable. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 92:11/q08 Which people or event are linked with the revelation of this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different attributions name Abū Sufyān, Abū Jahl, the palm owner and Abū al-Daḥdāḥ, or Abū Bakr; the palm report's status as an actual occasion is disputed.
- 92:11/q08/p1 It concerns Abū Sufyān ibn Ḥarb and his withheld wealth. — Muqātil identifies his hoarded money as useless at death and the Fire; Thaʿlabī transmits al-Kalbī's attribution. [2 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(الكلبي)]
- 92:11/q08/p2 It concerns Abū Jahl. — Al-Durr transmits Ibn ʿAbbās telling Nāfiʿ b. al-Azraq that the verse concerns Abū Jahl while explaining the fall into Fire. [2 notes: DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 92:11/q08/p3 The contrasting figures are Abū al-Daḥdāḥ and the owner of the date palm. — Thaʿlabī reports that a palm dropped dates into a neighbor's yard, its owner refused the Prophet's offer of a palm in Paradise, and Abū al-Daḥdāḥ bought and gave it away. Qurṭubī identifies the owner as an Anṣārī and maps ease to Paradise, hardship to Hell, and death to loss of wealth. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports that a hypocrite took fallen dates from poor Muslim children, Abū al-Daḥdāḥ paid an orchard of forty palms, the Prophet praised his future date clusters, and Bashshār b. Burd alluded to the small palm in verse. Ibn ʿAbbās attributes revelation through al-ʿusrā to the story, but Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the report gharīb and cautions that 'revealed concerning' can mean a verse covers the event rather than that it occasioned revelation. [7 notes: THALABI(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بشار بن برد)]
- 92:11/q08/p4 Verses 5–19 were said to concern Abū Bakr, while their message remains general. — Kur'an Yolu reports other interpreters' attribution and stresses that any occasion does not limit the passage's message and rulings. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 92:11/q09 What does the funeral report teach about written fate and action?  [* · reports]
- 92:11/q09/p1 Each person's place is written, yet the Prophet commands action because everyone is facilitated toward their outcome. — Thaʿlabī reports ʿAlī's account: at a funeral a man asked whether they should rely on the written places in Paradise and Hell. The Prophet answered 'Act' and recited the passage on giving and withholding. [2 notes: THALABI(النبي,علي بن أبي طالب عن النبي)]

## 92:11/q10 What is reported about the surah's place of revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 92:11/q10/p1 Some called the surah Medinan on the basis of the Abū al-Daḥdāḥ story. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Ibn ʿAbbās's report attributes revelation through al-ʿusrā to the episode and is gharīb; he cautions that 'revealed concerning' may mean a verse applies to a story rather than that the story caused revelation. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(ابن عباس)]

## 92:11/q11 What lexical distinction is drawn around 'yughni'?  [يُغْنِى · meaning]
- 92:11/q11/p1 Ghinā may mean self-sufficiency without wealth, whereas tharā normally denotes possessing wealth. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares the terms with reference to 93:8 and allows figurative use of tharā. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 92:11/q12 What does the nearby verb 'fasanuyassiruhu' mean?  [* · meaning]
- 92:11/q12/p1 It means 'We will prepare him,' as Arabic yassarat can describe sheep ready to lamb. — Al-Farrāʾ supplies the sheep idiom and a poet's line about two masters whose sheep are ready for lambing. [2 notes: FARRA]

## 92:11/q13 What does the separate root rādiʾa signify?  [تَرَدَّىٰٓ · meaning]
- 92:11/q13/p1 It is a distinct root meaning base or defective. — Naḥḥās gives rādiʾ and murdīʾ as examples, separating this root from the explanation of taraddā. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 92:11/q14 What literary weaknesses does Bāqillānī find in the cited poems?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes assess different lines and images rather than record a disagreement over one line.
- 92:11/q14/p1 The first line has little information and several weak or incoherent images. — Bāqillānī says polished wording lacks meaning, scent appearing only while the women stand is an unnecessary limitation, the clove breeze weakens the initial musk comparison, and the east-wind phrase hangs syntactically. [4 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 92:11/q14/p2 The tears passage uses filler, repetition, and an implausible, weak image. — 'From me' and 'on the neck' add nothing; the saddle's wetness is repeated to pad meter. Bāqillānī says tears would more likely fall on ground or clothing and could have been described as soaking the beloveds' dwellings. [3 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 92:11/q14/p3 The second line lacks beauty and striking meaning despite its unusual place name. — Bāqillānī warns against mistaking an unfamiliar location for strong diction. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 92:11/q14/p4 Al-Khabzarzī has a stronger and more remarkable poem than the criticized line. — Bāqillānī offers this as a comparative literary judgment. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 92:11/q15 Can applying too much grammar spoil an utterance?  [* · grammar]
- 92:11/q15/p1 For each clause a grammatical rule either applies correctly or fails; correctness cannot become excessive. — Jurjānī rejects the comparison with too much salt and Abū Bakr al-Khwārizmī's dislike of 'much inflection.' Each sentence must be correct separately; one correct sentence cannot repair another. Grammar is likened to a balance pointer indicating equality. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 92:11/q16 What meaning is given for saʿy in the surah's oath response?  [* · meaning]
- 92:11/q16/p1 It literally means brisk, forceful walking and figuratively work or striving. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the metaphor while analyzing the oath's response. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:11/q17 What other examples illustrate broad wording that bears multiple readings?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:11/q17/p1 Indefinite expressions can denote either the object or degree of an act; more specific wording can settle the scope. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares 'do not associate anything' in 4:36 with a verse using 'anyone' in 18:110, and says kathīran also permits several readings, with 4:160 among his examples. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:11/q18 Why is ownership emphasized elsewhere in the surah?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:11/q18/p1 The emphatic lām suits the surah's repeated concern with wealth and possession. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 53:25, whose context lacks wealth and omits this lām. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
