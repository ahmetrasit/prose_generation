# 75:11: 6 questions

## 75:11/q01 What does kallā do here?  [كَلَّا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether kallā answers and rebukes the preceding flight question or opens the declaration with affirmation.
- 75:11/q01/p1 It rebukes the preceding wish or question about fleeing. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identify the hope of escape as its target. Ibn al-Ḥājib, reported by al-Burhān, says kallā requires preceding speech to rebut according to the speaker's intent, whether quoted, narrated, or denied; he cites the fleeing person and Moses episodes. Al-Samīn refers the discussion of kallā to his earlier treatment without giving a new gloss. [8 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(ابن الحاجب); ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 75:11/q01/p2 It is an opening particle meaning ‘indeed’ or an emphatic ‘truly.’ — Al-Ālūsī reports these as alternative functions of kallā. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:11/q02 What is the syntax of lā wazar?  [لَا وَزَرَ · grammar]
- 75:11/q02/p1 The negation has an omitted predicate: ‘there is no wazar for him.’ — Al-Samīn supplies له as the implicit predicate. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+]

## 75:11/q03 What does wazar mean, and what does its denial rule out?  [وَزَرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names refuge generally, a particular shelter such as a mountain or fortress, a means of escape, or a reported dialectal sense; several sources present the particular shelters as examples of the broad sense.
- 75:11/q03/p1 A refuge or shelter of any kind is unavailable; the word extends to anything that could protect the fugitive. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and others gloss the denial as no refuge. Ibn Qutayba and al-Zajjāj trace the broad sense to a mountain used for protection; al-Zajjāj extends it to every shelter or escape. Al-Mubarrad and al-Zajjāj, as reported by al-Rāzī, specify an inaccessible mountain; al-Mubarrad adduces Kaʿb ibn Mālik's poem calling swords and spearpoints the people's sole wazar. Al-Zamakhsharī includes every mountain or other place of flight; al-Suddi, reported by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī, says even the sheltering mountain fails. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, gives fortress or refuge; al-Māwardī specifies refuge from the Fire. Al-Rāzī says nothing shelters one from God's command; al-Samīn cites poetry in which no shelter saves a youth from death and old age. Al-Bayḍāwī calls refuge a metaphor from a mountain stronghold and relates its derivation to burden. Al-Naḥḥās glosses maʾwā as refuge and connects wazīr either to entrusting affairs to a protector or to carried burdens, including precious metals; al-Jishumī connects wazīr to a helper one turns to and compares a wall strengthened by its foundation. Al-Ṭabrisī connects the word with a minister one seeks for help. Al-Ḥasan, reported by al-Suyūṭī, describes a herdsman surprised by cavalry and told to find mountain shelter; Abū Qilāba adds caves. ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's verse is cited by al-Suyūṭī for Arab usage denying refuge. Al-Ālūsī includes fort, weapon, and person among extensions of the original impregnable mountain and cites the poem about no refuge from death or age. Elmalılı describes an originally heavy, steep, fortified mountain and extends it to fortress, weapon, person, or shield, citing the same poem. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, cites Ṭābin al-Dathniya's poetry for refuge; Ibn al-Sikkīt lists refuge, escape, fortress, and stronghold with an Anṣārī poet's line; al-Rāghib specifies refuge sought in a mountain. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ relates the root to a heavy physical load, sin, and anxiety, cites burden, sin, and minister forms at 94:2, 20:87, 47:4, 20:29, 25:35, 6:31, 6:164, 35:18, 39:7, 16:25, and 20:100, and explains refuge as shelter for one burdened by hardship. Her context is terror of resurrection and the return to the Lord at 75:12. Ibn Zayd says there is nowhere to hide from the matter. Qatāda and other reports vary among mountain, fortress, refuge, and escape rather than offering one fixed example. [44 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(المبرد,المبرد والزجاج); DURR-FULL(أبو قلابة,الحسن,عمرو بن كلثوم,قتادة); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن السكيت,ابن عباس,الراغب); ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; FARRA+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB+; ITQAN+; JISHUMI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NAHHAS+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+; SAMIN-DURR+; STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; THALABI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 75:11/q03/p2 It denotes a mountain, especially one used as a refuge; the verse denies even that place of flight. — Muqātil says a mountain protecting a fugitive and calls it Himyarite speech. Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, and Muṭarrif are reported with mountain glosses; al-Ḍaḥḥāk also names the Himyarite language. Al-Ḥasan says Arabs said wazr or wazīr for a mountain refuge when endangered and illustrates it with the herdsman and cavalry. Al-Suddi specifies the mountain sought for safety, and Qatāda also lists fortress, refuge, and escape. Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, al-Mubarrad, al-Rāghib, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı treat mountain as the original or exemplary sense; Elmalılı adds heaviness, steepness, and fortification. Al-Kashshāf extends the word to every place of flight. [21 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(المبرد والزجاج); DURR-FULL(الحسن,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد,مطرف); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; ELMALILI+; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; MUQATIL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 75:11/q03/p3 It denotes a fortress or stronghold; there is no such protection. — Qatāda, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk as reported by al-Ṭabrisī are given fortress glosses. Al-Suyūṭī also reports the same gloss from ʿAṭiyya and Abū Qilāba. Ibn Qutayba says the basic word names a protective mountain or fortress. Al-Jishumī gives fortress as a type of refuge; Ibn al-Sikkīt includes fortress and stronghold. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Zajjāj, al-Baghawī, and al-Biqāʿī deny fortification along with other refuges. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); TABRISI(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن السكيت); BAGHAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB+; JISHUMI+; THALABI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 75:11/q03/p4 It denotes an escape, stratagem, or hiding place; none remains. — Ibn Zayd denies a hiding place or escape from the matter; Ibn Jubayr gives no way to escape. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, reported in al-Itqān, says no stratagem or escape in Yemeni usage. Ibn al-Sikkīt lists escape among the glosses. Qatāda lists escape with mountain, fortress, and refuge. Al-Zajjāj and al-Biqāʿī include a place of escape in their wider denial. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN(الضحاك); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن السكيت); BIQAI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 75:11/q03/p5 In the Hudhayl dialect, wazar means a grandchild. — Al-Suyūṭī reports this gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās without an argument applying it to the denial in this verse. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس)]
- 75:11/q03/p6 In Nabatean, wizr means rope as well as refuge. — Al-Suyūṭī reports Abū al-Qāsim's Nabatean gloss, which gives both rope and refuge. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو القاسم)]

## 75:11/q04 Who says ‘kallā lā wazar’?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the declaration is God's answer or the person's self-correction; Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the alternatives to pausing after the flight question or reading the clauses continuously.
- 75:11/q04/p1 God answers the person asking where to flee. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report this option. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it requires a pause after ‘where to flee.’ Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī holds that the words are God's speech rather than part of the person's plea. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 75:11/q04/p2 The fleeing person corrects himself and voices his own despair of refuge. — Al-Samīn says the quoted clause is accusative; al-Ālūsī reports self-correction after the question. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this reading lets the clauses run together; on it, a hadith describes the person looking around and seeing only Fire. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 75:11/q05 What effect does the person's recognition of no refuge have?  [* · rhetoric]
- 75:11/q05/p1 His own recognition after asking about escape makes the scene's terror more vivid. — Al-Biqāʿī observes the shift from the initial question to recognition that escape is impossible. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]

## 75:11/q06 How does the denial of refuge fit the surrounding judgment scene?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw complementary links: the flight question and return to God, the implied arrival of resurrection, and the reckoning that follows.
- 75:11/q06/p1 It answers the person's question about flight and points to the Lord as the only destination. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the questioner as the disbeliever mentioned at 75:3 and says he wishes for a place of escape. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reads 75:10–12 together: the terror of resurrection leaves no escape and the final resting place is with the Lord. Iṣlāḥī says that in 75:7–15 the upheaval answers demands to hasten Judgment: all paths close except refuge with God. Kur'an Yolu says all gather before God. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the fugitive searches for shelter but can turn only to God, where alone there is a place of settlement. The Study Quran adduces 18:53, 18:58, 34:51, 38:3, 41:48, 42:35, and 42:47 for the disbelievers' lack of escape at the Hour. [8 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+; STUDYQURAN+]
- 75:11/q06/p2 It signals that resurrection's terrors have arrived without explicitly narrating their arrival. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the flight question and the following final return already imply that arrival. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 75:11/q06/p3 It leads into unavoidable accountability and the disclosure of every deed. — Iṣlāḥī says each person answers for every deed in 75:7–15 despite attempts to conceal it. Kur'an Yolu says everyone gathers before God and is told the good and evil they did or failed to do. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
