# 23:99: 23 questions

## 23:99/q01 How does 'until' connect this scene to what precedes?  [حَتَّىٰٓ · links]
turns on: Whether حتى retains endpoint force, and which earlier clause or implied clause supplies its endpoint.
- 23:99/q01/p1 Their bad descriptions or speech persist until death; the intervening prayer is parenthetical. — Al-Kashshaf, reported by Razi and Abu Hayyan, links it to the earlier bad description; the intervening plea asks divine help against Satan (23:96). Baydawi similarly treats the appeal as intervening; Nasafi also allows persistent ill-repute. Samīn reports attachment to بما يصفون. Alusi specifies the second 'they describe' and explains the interruption as urging protection and gracious restraint; he rejects attachment to the first. Study Quran instead traces the persistent rejection to 23:83. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:99/q01/p2 It is an introductory particle that also marks the endpoint of an implied prior statement. — Ibn Atiyya prefers the introductory reading because the ensuing death scene is the subject, while allowing an omitted endpoint. Abu Hayyan and Samīn's Shaykh object that even introductory حتى has endpoint force and Ibn Atiyya left the omission unexplained. Abu al-Baqa gives it endpoint force like coordination; Abu Hayyan reconstructs that the Prophet will not be like those attended by devils until death. Samīn's Shaykh supplies an omitted broad clause, citing a poem. Bursevi says an opening sentence ends the account of unbelievers' false claims at death. Alusi illustrates an omitted general statement with the 'even Kalīb insults me' example. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء,ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,ابن عطية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 23:99/q01/p3 It ends their continuing association of partners with God. — Nasafi reports this as an alternative to persistent ill-repute, with the intervening account urging restraint from revenge. Alusi judges attachment to 'they associate partners' untenable. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:99/q01/p4 It attaches to 'they are liars,' with intervening denial of a divine son. — Samīn reports this possible attachment. Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Alusi, treats the denial of a son as an interruption that establishes their falsehood and punishment; Alusi rejects the connection. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 23:99/q01/p5 It is an independent transition linked to God's ability to show the threatened punishment. — Ibn Ashur links the transition to 23:95 and rejects making حتى an endpoint of 'they describe.' The scene moves from worldly punishment to punishment in the afterlife, or details a prior afterlife threat. His reading of the devils in the preceding passage suits this endpoint. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:99/q01/p6 It opens a sentence without any endpoint force. — Alusi reports Ibn Atiyya's allowance of this reading; Abu Hayyan and Samīn's Shaykh object that even inceptive حتى retains endpoint meaning. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 23:99/q02 What does death's coming mean here?  [جَآءَ ٱلْمَوْتُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'death' names its angel or the onset and signs of dying.
- 23:99/q02/p1 The Angel of Death arrives. — Al-Kalbi and Muqatil give this gloss in Wahidi. Muqatil and al-Suddi describe the Angel taking or appearing to the unbeliever; Qurtubi invokes angels seizing disbelievers in 8:50. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 23:99/q02/p2 The causes, signs, or direct apprehension of death and the next life appear. — Wahidi reports others' reading from 14:17. Alusi says signs of death and conditions of the next life appear. Ibn Zayd places it as worldly life ends before death is tasted; Thalabi agrees. Razi reports most interpreters' moment of direct apprehension, which compels recognition of God and disobedience. Abu Hayyan says the person sees death on its arrival. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(غيرهما); ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد)]

## 23:99/q03 Why use 'came' for death?  [جَآءَ ٱلْمَوْتُ · rhetoric]
- 23:99/q03/p1 Coming fits discourse about death itself or the dying person's condition. — Samarrai distinguishes coming as movement between places from presence or witnessing (حضور); he cites Qur'anic examples of each for death and arriving events (18:98, 11:40, 4:18, 5:106, 2:133, 6:61). He describes God as everywhere present and takes this as evidence of His existence everywhere. He says جاء الموت is chosen here because the dying person's condition is under discussion. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 23:99/q04 Why is death emphasized here compared with al-Zumar?  [ٱلْمَوْتُ · rhetoric]
- 23:99/q04/p1 Death and mortality recur throughout al-Mu'minun, so death receives doubled emphasis here. — Samarrai contrasts al-Zumar, where death occurs only twice (39:30, 39:42), with the repeated death scenes in al-Mu'minun (23:27, 35, 37, 41, 44, 48, 80, 82, 99). [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:99/q05 Who is 'one of them' who asks to return?  [أَحَدَهُمُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the universal wording and 63:10 extend the plea to negligent believers, despite the context about resurrection deniers and believers' promised Paradise.
- 23:99/q05/p1 An unbeliever or polytheist, especially one denying resurrection. — Muqatil, Tabari, al-Dahhak, Thalabi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Kuranyolu and others identify the unbeliever or people of shirk. Ibn Ashur traces the pronoun to the polytheists disputing resurrection after dust and bones in 23:82, rather than to devils. Abu Hayyan reports al-Qushayri that their denial persists despite proofs until angels and certainty arrive. Razi prefers this: believers shown Paradise would not ask for more, and otherwise even the least rewarded would lament higher ranks; he says 63:10 concerns worldly life. Kuranyolu says death severs worldly desires and makes their error clear. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الضحاك); YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القشيري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 23:99/q05/p2 The asker can include a believer who neglected obligatory charity or pilgrimage. — Ibn Abbas identifies one who withheld alms and failed pilgrimage, citing the warning to spend before death in 63:10 when challenged that only unbelievers ask. Al-Awza'i specifies withheld alms. Qurtubi says believers also ask, citing the end of al-Munafiqun. Ibn Kathir includes those who neglected God's commands. Abu Hayyan reports the objection that this verse concerns unbelievers; Razi argues 63:10 depicts worldly life rather than the state of reward. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 23:99/q05/p3 Anyone lacking God's friendship asks, even a person who never spoke. — Al-Hasan says the Fire becomes clear at death; even someone mute asks inwardly or unheard by those nearby. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن)]

## 23:99/q06 When is the plea made?  [إِذَا جَآءَ ٱلْمَوْتُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse's 'when death comes' fixes this plea before death or permits identifying it with the later pleas at the Fire.
- 23:99/q06/p1 At the onset or direct apprehension of death, before it has been tasted. — Ibn Zayd says worldly life ends and the next life appears before death is tasted; Thalabi repeats this. Razi reports the majority's direct apprehension and prefers the verse's explicit death timing, while allowing another plea later. Abu Hayyan says the person sees death. Study Quran reports the before-death reading, connected with the address to soul-taking angels. Kuranyolu reports Razi's death reading over the weak Hell view. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); STUDYQURAN(Some commentators); RAZI-FULL+]
- 23:99/q06/p2 After death, on seeing the Fire or in the afterlife. — Razi reports appeals by condemned people who see Hell in other verses; Study Quran reports this timing. Kuranyolu calls it a weak view attributed to Razi. Razi prefers the apparent death timing but allows that they ask again later. Ibn Kathir describes recurring refusals at resurrection, judgment, and sight of the Fire. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Others (R)); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 23:99/q07 What does the dying person see or undergo that prompts the plea?  [جَآءَ أَحَدَهُمُ ٱلْمَوْتُ قَالَ · reports]
turns on: The notes describe different experiences at death: exposure of neglected acts, sight of the Fire, or confusion under assault.
- 23:99/q07/p1 He recognizes neglected deeds, withheld rights, disobedience, or the truth of the afterlife. — Muqatil says the Angel of Death reveals sins and shame; al-Suddi says few good deeds and many bad deeds. A prophetic report through a man of Banu Haritha says impediments to obligations appear before or between his eyes; Razi's prophetic report says withheld rights appear; Jabir's report says obstacles to truth gather before his eyes. Tabari and Kashshaf stress remorse over neglected obedience, faith and good works. Baydawi, Qushayri, Bursevi and Kuranyolu likewise describe regret once truth or the afterlife is visible. Qurtubi cites 8:50 for soul-taking angels. Al-Qushayri, through Abu Hayyan, says denial persisted despite proofs until certainty and angels arrived. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,رسول الله); TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القشيري); DURR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:99/q07/p2 He sees his allotted place in the Fire and asks to repent. — Abu Hurayra's report, transmitted by Ibn Kathir and al-Durr, says a dead unbeliever sees his seat in the Fire, is told his lifespan is over, and then suffers constriction and creatures in the grave. Al-Hasan says the Fire becomes clear to one without God's friendship. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن)]
- 23:99/q07/p3 Devils and punishment guards bewilder him into a habitual appeal to created beings. — Al-Suhayli, reported by Burhan and Itqan, says the person speaks incoherently under their assault; Burhan calls the appeal a worldly habit. [2 notes: BURHAN(السهيلي); ITQAN(السهيلي)]

## 23:99/q08 Is the dying request spoken aloud?  [قَالَ · interpretation]
- 23:99/q08/p1 It may be inward or inaudible to people nearby. — Al-Hasan says even a mute person asks and bystanders do not hear; al-Suddi says descendants do not hear. Nahhas points to speech within oneself in 58:8; Qurtubi applies the same verse to the request. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,السدي); NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 23:99/q09 Where does the speaker ask to return?  [ٱرْجِعُونِ · interpretation]
- 23:99/q09/p1 To worldly life. — Tabari, Ibn Abbas as reported by Wahidi, Wahidi's Wajiz, Thalabi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and others explicitly name the world. Damghani gives rajaʿa the sense 'send back or return,' grouping this plea with 20:40 and 67:4. Ibn Kathir says return would allow repair of ruined deeds. Biqai calls the world the abode of knowledge. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 23:99/q10 What does the speaker hope to do if returned?  [ٱرْجِعُونِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sought good is left general, specified as faith or its testimony, or exemplified by neglected financial and pilgrimage duties.
- 23:99/q10/p1 Do the righteous work neglected, especially faith and obedience, rather than resume worldly ties. — Muqatil and Kashshaf specify neglected faith; Tabari, Thalabi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir and the prophetic reports specify good works or withheld duties. Qatada says it is obedience, not family or tribe. Ikrima and Ibn Abbas identify the wished-for good as saying 'There is no god but God.' Samarrai says this verse asks for righteousness generally rather than charity specifically, since salvation here is framed outside a wealth passage. Study Quran compares pleas for charity or righteousness in 63:10, 32:12, 2:167, 26:102, 35:37 and 39:58. The general and specific formulations are compatible descriptions of the intended good. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله); TAB-FULL; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,جابر بن عبد الله,عكرمة); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:99/q10/p2 Complete withheld alms and pilgrimage. — Ibn Abbas says one who neglected charity and pilgrimage seeks return, citing 63:10; al-Awza'i narrows it to withheld alms. This is a specific instance of repairing neglected obligations, but its proponents use it to broaden the asker beyond unbelievers. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 23:99/q11 Why is 'return me' phrased as a plural command?  [رَبِّ ٱرْجِعُونِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the plural is conventional reverence, a change to angelic addressees, repetition, self-magnification, combined address, or confused speech; Ibn Malik disputes the permissibility of plural prayer to God.
- 23:99/q11/p1 The singular God is addressed with an honorific plural. — Ibn Qutayba compares royal 'we' and scriptural usage (12:3, 54:49); the Kufan grammarian cites 19:9; Nahhas, Wahidi's grammarians (50:43), Zajjaj, Thalabi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi and others invoke Arabic reverence or divine plural forms. Samīn prefers this and cites poetry against Ibn Malik's prayer restriction; he notes scriptural plurals. Bursevi rejects the claim this usage is only late Arabic. Ibn Ashur says masculine plural can honor even a woman, citing al-Arji's siwakum and Ja'far's ba'dakum. Asad says most commentators favor it. Baydawi, Qurtubi, Biqai and Ibn Atiyya also offer it. Alusi says poetry confirms it and explains one honored person as treated like a group. [29 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة); NAHHAS(قول للنحويين); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج وجميع أصحاب العربية); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الشاعر); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جريج); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(بعضهم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(العرجي,جعفر بن علبة الحارثي); ASAD-NOTES(most commentators); SAMIN-DURR+; SAMIN-DURR(ابن مالك)-]
- 23:99/q11/p2 He first invokes God, then addresses the soul-taking or punishment angels in the plural. — Tabari and Ibn Jurayj describe a shift of addressee. Al-Mubarrad prefers it and cites al-Ahwas and al-Nabigha shifting from a house to its owner. Razi says 'Lord' can be an oath by God's right; Samīn and Alusi posit an omitted 'angels of my Lord,' with Samīn citing pronoun shift in 7:4. Burhan treats رب as a cry for help. The same reading appears in Thalabi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Itqan and Study Quran. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن جرير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جريج); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BURHAN(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد)+]
- 23:99/q11/p3 The plural expresses repeated pleading: 'return me, return me, return me.' — Al-Mazini, Nahhas and Qurtubi compare 50:24, 'throw him,' understood as repeated throwing; Abu al-Baqa also invokes the dual command and qifa nabki. Baydawi offers repetition; Asad reports Baydawi arguing from pre-Islamic poetry and reflects it by repeating the plea. Alusi compares qifa nabki but al-Khafaji objects that singular imperative has no plural pronoun; al-Khafaji instead proposes swapping word forms regardless of meaning. Alusi rejects this as excessive, suggesting honorific group address can make the action resemble repeated group action; he leaves open whether dual address can similarly honor one. [9 notes: NAHHAS(المازني); QURTUBI-FULL(المزني); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURHAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي,المازني); ASAD-NOTES(Baydawi); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)-]
- 23:99/q11/p4 The speaker addresses himself in the plural to magnify himself. — Burhan reports this minority explanation: ارجعون means singular ارجعني, with the speaker's self-aggrandizing 'we.' [1 notes: BURHAN(بعضهم)]
- 23:99/q11/p5 The plural addresses God and the angels together. — Biqai offers this among alternatives while describing the angels of punishment at death. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 23:99/q11/p6 A plural prayer form to God is impermissible because it suggests plurality in Him. — Ibn Malik says he knows no one allowing a supplicant to say يا رحيمون. Samīn rejects the restriction with scriptural plural forms used for reverence. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن مالك); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 23:99/q11/p7 Confusion under devils and punishment guards produces an incoherent habitual appeal. — Al-Suhayli, reported in Burhan and Itqan, regards the utterance as a confused appeal of the kind the person addressed to created beings in life. [2 notes: BURHAN(السهيلي); ITQAN(السهيلي)]

## 23:99/q12 How are the adjacent hamzas in 'came one' recited?  [جَآءَ أَحَدَهُمُ · readings]
turns on: Treatment of two adjacent open hamzas, including which hamza Abu Amr elides and the resulting madd relation.
- 23:99/q12/p1 Both hamzas are fully pronounced. — Ibn Amir, Asim, Hamza, al-Kisai, Khalaf and Rawh fully pronounce them in all five relevant classes; Ibn Mihran alone reports easing for Rawh. Al-Tadhkira, al-Taysir, al-Kafi, al-Shatibiyya, Talkhis al-Ibarat and al-Tajrid transmit both full pronunciation and easing. Ibn al-Jazari classifies جاء أحدهم among 16 two-fatha forms in 29 places. [3 notes: NASHR(أصحاب التذكرة والتيسير والكافي والشاطبية والتجريد,ابن عامر، وعاصم، وحمزة، والكسائي، وخلف، وروح)]
- 23:99/q12/p2 The adjacent hamzas are eased rather than both fully pronounced. — Makki, al-Mahdawi, Ibn Sufyan and al-Unwan choose easing; al-Rawda, al-Mubhij, both al-Ghayas and Talkhis also transmit it. Al-Tajrid's author says he read it from al-Farisi and Abd al-Baqi; multiple manuals also give both options. Ibn Shurayh's particular easing like waw is rejected by Ibn al-Jazari because it would require impermissible conversion or rounding of the hamza's kasra. [3 notes: NASHR(أصحاب التذكرة والتيسير والكافي والشاطبية والتجريد,ابن شريح); NASHR(مكي، والمهدوي، وابن سفيان، وصاحب العنوان)+]
- 23:99/q12/p3 Abu Amr drops the first hamza. — Most performance authorities say so and Ibn al-Jazari chooses it by analogy with like letters; which hamza drops determines whether the preceding madd is connected or separate. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو الطيب بن غلبون، وأبو الحسن الحمامي)+]
- 23:99/q12/p4 Abu Amr drops the second hamza. — Ibn Ghalbun and al-Hammami report this, following al-Khalil and grammarians; the position changes whether the preceding madd is connected or separate. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو الطيب بن غلبون، وأبو الحسن الحمامي)]

## 23:99/q13 What other hamza and madd rules are cited alongside this reading?  [جَآءَ · readings]
- 23:99/q13/p1 A second like hamza changed to a madd letter lengthens before a consonant, but otherwise has ordinary madd length. — Ibn al-Jazari says the longer duration resolves two adjacent sukuns. For جاء آل لوط and جاء آل فرعون, al-Dani reports disagreement between replacement and easing because an alif follows; some reject replacement to avoid consecutive alifs. These are related examples, not variant wording of this verse. [2 notes: NASHR(أصحاب الداني)]

## 23:99/q14 How is the ra in 'return me' pronounced?  [ٱرْجِعُونِ · readings]
- 23:99/q14/p1 Its sakin ra remains emphatic because the preceding kasra is incidental. — Ibn al-Jazari gives ارجعون and ارجعي as examples and applies the same rule to ارجعوا. He states that sakin ra is emphatic after fatha or damma and, despite a preceding kasra, where that kasra is incidental or the ra's underlying quality is emphatic. He contrasts rules for final ra and ra with genuine kasra, and lists قرية, مريم and المرء as exceptions among medial forms. [4 notes: NASHR]

## 23:99/q15 Is the final first-person ya in 'return me' retained in recitation and pause?  [ٱرْجِعُونِ · readings]
- 23:99/q15/p1 The ya is retained. — Qiraat-ER says ten-reader recitations retain the full object-pronoun ya. Ibn al-Jazari says Yaqub affirms this among six extra yas in both connection and pause, alongside بما كذبون twice, فاتقون, أن يحضرون and ولا تكلمون. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(يعقوب)]

## 23:99/q16 What reports contrast a believer's response to death with this plea?  [قَالَ رَبِّ ٱرْجِعُونِ · reports]
- 23:99/q16/p1 A believer offered return declines the world's sorrow and chooses to go forward to God; the unbeliever asks to return. — Thalabi reports the Prophet speaking to Aisha; Ibn Atiyya and al-Durr transmit comparable reports. Jishumi reports a prophetic account of angels asking the believer and supplies no grading. Abu Hayyan also reports the hadith; Alusi attributes a version through Ibn Jurayj to Ibn Jarir. Versions vary between seeing death or angels and call the world a house of grief, worries, or sorrows. [6 notes: THALABI(عائشة); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جرير عن ابن جريج)]

## 23:99/q17 What happens in Abu Hurayra's report about the dead unbeliever?  [جَآءَ أَحَدَهُمُ ٱلْمَوْتُ ٱرْجِعُونِ · reports]
- 23:99/q17/p1 He sees his seat in the Fire, requests return and repentance, is refused, and suffers in the grave. — Ibn Kathir and al-Durr transmit Abu Hurayra: his allotted lifespan is said to be over; the grave constricts and its creatures torment him. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 23:99/q18 What response and practical warning accompany the plea?  [قَالَ رَبِّ ٱرْجِعُونِ · interpretation]
- 23:99/q18/p1 The request is refused; people should act and turn to God while return is still possible. — Thalabi says the following كلا rebukes and refuses; Muhammad ibn Ka'b depicts God answering that it is only a word. Ibn Kathir says return is refused at death and repeatedly at resurrection, judgment and sight of the Fire, citing 63:10–11, 14:44, 7:53, 32:12, 6:27–28, 42:44, 40:11–12 and 35:37. Al-Qushayri says the distressed submit when escape ends and are told to have done so earlier; he reports a poem urging the soul to return before the road is blocked. Abu Uthman's inward counsel urges obedience, avoiding spiritual claims and correcting practice. Qatada transmits al-Ala ibn Ziyad's exercise of imagining a granted reprieve and says the living should perform the obedience the unbeliever wished to perform. Biqai warns that devils are most dangerous at death, when the veil lifts, and urges striving and prayer for protection. Study Quran describes the conclusion as warning disbelievers that their wish comes too late. [11 notes: THALABI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة,محمد بن كعب القرظي); BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:99/q19 Can the dead return to worldly life after this plea?  [ٱرْجِعُونِ · theology]
- 23:99/q19/p1 No return to worldly life is granted after death, though resurrection will occur. — Jishumi explicitly opposes the Imamis and affirms resurrection. Kuranyolu argues the refusal rules out reincarnation, which it traces to ancient Indian religions and Plato: migration until purification would allow sinners to repair their lives. Ibn Kathir says the plea is refused at death and later stages; Abu Hurayra's report says the allotted lifespan has ended. [5 notes: JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 23:99/q20 What does the requested return imply about human action?  [ٱرْجِعُونِ · theology]
- 23:99/q20/p1 People own their deeds and ability, so the plea to return for good work rebuts compulsion. — Jishumi argues the promise to do good on return would be meaningless if human deeds and capacity were not their own. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 23:99/q21 What does the request show about knowledge of one's fate before death?  [جَآءَ أَحَدَهُمُ ٱلْمَوْتُ ٱرْجِعُونِ · theology]
- 23:99/q21/p1 Before death people know with certainty whether they belong to God's allies or enemies. — Qurtubi infers this from the request itself: without such certainty, they would not seek return. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 23:99/q22 Why ask for return if it is known to be impossible?  [رَبِّ ٱرْجِعُونِ · theology]
- 23:99/q22/p1 A person may permissibly supplicate for the impossible, or merely express hope and grief. — Razi says knowledge that return cannot happen does not prevent such supplication; Kuranyolu reports Razi that the dying person knows return is impossible yet grieves at dying unbelieving. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî)]

## 23:99/q23 How does this scene relate to other passages and the sura as a whole?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby and recurring passages the source uses to frame the scene.
- 23:99/q23/p1 It is one of several Qur'anic scenes where the doomed seek return to do good. — Ibn Kathir cites pleas at death, resurrection, judgment and the Fire in 63:10–11, 14:44, 7:53, 32:12, 6:27–28, 42:44, 40:11–12 and 35:37. Study Quran compares 63:10, 32:12, 2:167, 26:102, 35:37 and 39:58. Samarrai distinguishes this general request for righteousness from charity in a wealth passage. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:99/q23/p2 The passage resumes and concludes the sura's account of rejection by disbelievers and opponents of prophets. — Asad connects it to 23:74 and 23:90. Study Quran links the 'until' to rejection in 23:83 and notes that affluent, powerful opposition to prophets recurs across the sura and other Qur'anic passages (7:59–62, 7:136). Ibn Ashur takes the pronoun back to resurrection deniers in 23:82 and sees a threat of punishment and regret; his account moves from worldly to afterlife punishment. Biqai connects the death scene to the danger of devils at death. [6 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
