# 17:13: 32 questions

## 17:13/q01 What does “every” cover here?  [وَكُلَّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the phrase ranges over all humans or only accountable persons.
- 17:13/q01/p1 Every human individual is included in the expression. — Burhān and Itqān explain that kull with an indefinite complement ranges over its individuals; agreement can follow the complement's meaning. Alūsī cites Mujāhid's leaf on every newborn for the unrestricted wording. [11 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 17:13/q01/p2 The reckoning applies to every morally accountable person. — Bursevī lists believer and unbeliever, male and female, learned and ignorant, ruler and subject, free and enslaved. Alūsī links this restriction to the verse's ending. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 17:13/q02 How is “every human” construed grammatically?  [وَكُلَّ إِنسَٰنٍ · grammar]
turns on: Accusative with an implicit verb versus nominative as a subject.
- 17:13/q02/p1 Kullu insānin may be nominative as a subject. — Zajjāj says this is grammatically possible but knows no reader using it; Ibn Abī ʿAbla reads it so. Abū al-Fatḥ prefers placing kull before its governor; al-Suhaylī discusses detached kull's subject status. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي عبلة); BURHAN(أبو الفتح,السهيلي)]
- 17:13/q02/p2 Kulla insānin is accusative, governed by an implicit verb. — Naḥḥās compares it to the preceding construction. Zajjāj allows accusative; the received text has it. [2 notes: NAHHAS; ZAJJAJ]

## 17:13/q03 What ancillary points are made about kull and textual argument?  [وَكُلَّ · grammar]
- 17:13/q03/p1 Kull's syntax and agreement depend on its complement and position. — Burhān says omitted complements remain understood, with dispute over their tanwīn; Itqān says negation governing kull negates comprehensiveness. [10 notes: BURHAN(السهيلي); ITQAN]
- 17:13/q03/p2 Clear verses can counter an opponent's appeal to ambiguous ones. — Burhān describes redirecting argument toward transmitted evidence. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 17:13/q04 What is the derivation and grammatical gender of insān?  [إِنسَٰنٍ · meaning]
turns on: Basran sociability derivation versus Kufan forgetfulness derivation.
- 17:13/q04/p1 Basrans derive it from sociability or humanity, on faʿlān. — Ṭabrisī reports that they call the yāʾ in the diminutive unaysiyān extra and irregular; insān is common gender, as is faras. [3 notes: TABRISI]
- 17:13/q04/p2 Kufans derive it from forgetfulness, originally insiyān. — Ṭabrisī reports that they cite the diminutive unaysiyān and explain the omitted yāʾ as simplification. [2 notes: TABRISI]

## 17:13/q05 What other source comments on the human subject?  [إِنسَٰنٍ · meaning]
- 17:13/q05/p1 A human may be defined as an animal in human form. — Jishumī prefers this first of two definitions; the competing definition is not preserved in the note. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 17:13/q06 What does 'We bound' express?  [أَلْزَمْنَٰهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Inseparable attachment of deed or decree versus a claim about moral agency.
- 17:13/q06/p1 God makes the person's deed or allotment inseparable from its owner. — Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses ilzām from ordinary lazima. Muqātil says it lasts to reckoning. Ibn Qutayba likens it to debt held in one's neck. This entails full consequence for one's deeds. [19 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:13/q06/p2 The attribution to the person supports freely acquired action and responsibility. — Jishumī challenges determinism by asking why God would charge people for deeds He created. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects a fate independent of action, citing 53:39–41, 15:43,45; Asad stresses moral choice. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:13/q06/p3 The binding is evidence that prior divine knowledge and decree cannot fail. — Rāzī argues from the necessity of what is bound, God's agency in 'We bound,' 48:26, and the saying that the Pen has dried. Biqāʿī compares Israel's imposed scripture; Study Quran says foreknowledge of moral choices remains an alternative. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI-]

## 17:13/q07 What is a person's attached ṭāʾir?  [طَٰٓئِرَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it identifies the person's deeds, decreed fortune, a written record, knowledge, or imposed responsibility.
- 17:13/q07/p1 The person's good and evil deeds, which stay with their doer. — Mujāhid, Ḥasan, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, Farrāʾ, and many later exegetes give this gloss. Some include the deeds' consequences, happiness and misery; Qur'ān 99:5–6, 50:17–18, 82:10–14, 52:16, and 4:123 support preservation and recompense. Kur'an Yolu prefers deeds and obligations because the next clause names the record. - 17:13/q07/p3 The person's book or written record is attached to him. — Damghānī includes the record of answers to Munkar and Nakīr. Al-Ḥasan describes an open ledger around the neck at resurrection; fetal and newborn reports picture a prewritten leaf there. Ālūsī allows its literal manner if reports are sound. Ibn Qutayba says the plain deed and allotted-share glosses both need clarification before preferring a decreed good-and-evil share. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(الحسن,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، السدي، أبو عبيدة,الحسن); BURHAN; DURR-FULL(أنس,الحسن,الله,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,الحسن,حذيفة بن أسيد,مجاهد)]
- 17:13/q07/p4 The duty to obey divine law, which a person may fulfill or violate. — Qurṭubī reports this explanation; Kur'an Yolu renders it responsibility; Study Quran also mentions taklīf. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 17:13/q07/p5 The person's knowledge of what he has done. — Tustarī specifies awareness of good and evil actions. [1 notes: TUSTARI]

## 17:13/q08 Why is this allotment called a bird?  [طَٰٓئِرَهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Bird omen idiom versus arrows or marked slips assigning shares.
- 17:13/q08/p1 Arab omen taking from the flight and direction of birds supplied the metaphor for good or evil fate and deeds. — Ibn Qutayba says the omen's cause names its outcome (7:131). Wāḥidī cites Ḥassān and Kathīr yet calls augury false. Ṭabrisī distinguishes sāniḥ and bāriḥ, as do Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Kashshāf. Abū Zayd extends ṭāʾir to passing gazelles; examples include 36:18–19 and 27:47. Ibn Kathīr finds Ibn Luhayʿa's augury gloss very strange. [38 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(أهل المعاني); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المعاني); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أم العلاء الأنصارية); TABRISI(أبو زيد,الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو زيد,الكشاف,المجمع,المفردات); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:13/q08/p2 It evokes the arrow, lot, or marked slip by which a share falls to someone. — Abū ʿUbayda's Arab idiom and Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna's drawn arrow support this. Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests feathered arrows shot into portions as the source of the metaphor. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(أبو عبيد والعيني); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة والقتيبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:13/q09 How is ṭāʾir read?  [طَٰٓئِرَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: The received singular with alif versus ṭayr without alif.
- 17:13/q09/p1 Ṭayr without alif is also reported. — Reports name Ḥasan, Abū Rajāʾ, Mujāhid, Ibn Masʿūd, and Ubayy. Wāḥidī says this supports the deed interpretation; Itqān notes anomalous written forms. - 17:13/q09/p2 Ṭāʾir with alif is the received form. — Ibn Qutayba notes ṭāʾir can signify one bird and ṭayr a group, without a difference in sense here. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(الحسن)]

## 17:13/q10 What does the prophetic report say about augury and the attached bird?  [طَٰٓئِرَهُۥ · reports]
- 17:13/q10/p1 The Prophet denies contagion and bird omens, then says each person's bird is attached to his neck. — Ṭabarī transmits it; Ibn Kathīr gives Jabir reports, one described as connected. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also cites the shorter denial of omen. Ibn Kathīr calls Ibn Luhayʿa's augury gloss very strange. ## 17:13/q11 Why is the bird bound specifically in the neck?  [فِى عُنُقِهِۦ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the image draws on collars, visible marks, obligation idioms, or a literal record.
- 17:13/q11/p1 A collar, necklace, or shackle depicts an inescapable bond, honor, or shame. — Ṭabarī says the neck bears identifying marks, adornments and shame; Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn Kathīr and others cite Arabic usage and poetry by al-Aʿshā or a dove's collar. Good deeds adorn, sins disfigure. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the neck cannot be separated from oneself. Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts assigning deeds to the hand as the chief organ of acquisition. [31 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI(الحسن ومجاهد وأبو رجاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي,بعض أهل المعاني); DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(أهل المعاني); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشاعر); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(أسئلة المقحمة,حياة الحيوان); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الأعشى); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:13/q11/p2 A written decree or deed sheet is actually hung at the person's neck. — Mujāhid's newborn leaf, the fetal decree report, and al-Ḥasan's grave ledger offer literal images. Wāḥidī says the sheet need not be visible; Ālūsī accepts sound reports while leaving the manner unspecified. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(الحسن,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن,الله,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,حذيفة بن أسيد,مجاهد)]
- 17:13/q11/p3 It may allude to actual neck marks that identify a person's status or duty. — Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions marks for protected non-Muslims, Bashshār's poem on a neck inscription, and a camel's neck mark or bell. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(بشار)]

## 17:13/q12 How is ʿunuq read?  [عُنُقِهِۦ · readings]
- 17:13/q12/p1 The nūn of ʿunuq is also read quiescent. — Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān report it; Samīn calls it a common phonetic lightening, and Ālūsī also records it. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، الحسن، أبو رجاء، الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد والحسن وأبو رجاء)]

## 17:13/q13 Does naming the neck for a person affect emancipation formulas?  [عُنُقِهِۦ · law]
turns on: Whether neck and throat designate the whole person differently from hand or hair.
- 17:13/q13/p1 Saying a person's neck or throat is free emancipates them; saying hand or hair is free does not. — Ṭabrisī reports Abū Ḥanīfa's legal analogy from the neck representing the whole person. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو حنيفة)]
- 17:13/q13/p2 Either sort of expression emancipates the person. — Ṭabrisī reports al-Shāfiʿī's treatment of the expressions alike. [1 notes: TABRISI(الشافعي)]

## 17:13/q14 What reports or views accompany discussion of protection hung at the neck?  [عُنُقِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: Explanations of protective invocation range from discipline of the self to aid through spirits or divine power.
- 17:13/q14/p1 An invocation may be written and hung on the neck of a child unable to recite it. — Ibn ʿUmar taught the words to older children and hung a written copy on younger ones. Reports promise protection from Satan, fear, venomous creatures, or harm at a stopping place. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,النبي,خولة بنت حكيم,عبد الله بن عمر)]
- 17:13/q14/p2 Understanding refuge reveals human weakness and guards against the self's urges. — Rāzī calls the self the greater Satan. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 17:13/q14/p3 God's perfect words invoke aid of pure spirits against harmful spirits. — Rāzī cites the many angels occupying heaven and posits spirits in heavens, ether and air. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي)]
- 17:13/q14/p4 God's words signify His effective command and power, which protects from harm. — Rāzī gives this as an alternative to the spiritual account. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 17:13/q16 Where is the record kept, and what happens to it from life to resurrection?  [وَنُخْرِجُ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ · reports]
turns on: Whether a recording scroll is folded and reopened, a prenatal decree hangs on the neck, or emergence means revelation of a hidden record.
- 17:13/q16/p1 Two angels keep a ledger during life; it is folded at death, stays with the person in the grave, and opens at resurrection. — Ḥasan's account is widespread, drawing on 50:17; Abū al-Sawwār urges people to fill the open page as they choose. A separate report says God commands the angel to fold it at death. Wāḥidī and Rāzī allow bringing it from the grave. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); THALABI(أبو السوار العدوي,الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); JISHUMI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو السوار العدوي); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو السوار العدوي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]
- 17:13/q16/p2 The record is brought out by becoming visible after being hidden in worldly life. — Wāḥidī gives this alternative to removal from the grave; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God removes heedlessness, displaying an already prepared record. Rāzī's spiritual reading makes bodily life the concealment. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:13/q16/p3 An angel writes a person's traits, fate, provision, trials and deeds before birth, then the decree hangs at the neck. — The fetal report describes forty days of forming substance, a clot, then the angel's questions at four months about bodily form and happiness or misery. It records effects and deeds and hangs the decree until judgment. [6 notes: DURR-FULL(الله,الملك,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(حذيفة بن أسيد)]

## 17:13/q17 What morphology accounts for nukh'riju?  [وَنُخْرِجُ · grammar]
turns on: The analogical retention of Afʿala's hamza versus its regular deletion in the imperfect.
- 17:13/q17/p1 Afʿala verbs form imperfect yufʿilu and corresponding nufʿilu; the added hamza is normally dropped. — Sībawayh says al-Khalīl expected retention by analogy, but usage deletes a heavy, frequent extra hamza; Khaṭṭām and Laylā preserve it only by poetic necessity. Sībawayh contrasts Fāʿala, Faʿʿala and their prefixed t forms, participles and quadriliteral patterns; examples include qātala/yuqātilu, jarraba/yujarribu, and daḥraja. [22 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,خطام المجاشعي,ليلى الأخيلية)]

## 17:13/q18 What unusual outcome or reward does Ubayy's judgment report describe?  [يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ كِتَٰبًۭا · reports]
- 17:13/q18/p1 A leader to good first fears listed sins, then finds forgiveness and multiplied good; the honored person receives the book in his right hand and is adorned. — Ubayy says sins are inside the book, good deeds on its back; the person receives a crown, two garments, ornaments at every joint and Adam's stature of sixty cubits, then announces the same reward to companions, citing 69:19–23. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبي بن كعب)]

## 17:13/q19 What is the case and role of kitāban?  [كِتَٰبًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Direct object versus circumstantial state, specification, or nominative subject, depending on the verb reading.
- 17:13/q19/p1 Accusative as direct object of 'We bring out' or active 'he brings out'. — This is the common nūn construction. Ṭabrisī also allows specification. Abū Jaʿfar, Nāfiʿ and common readers are named. Fārisī says the book is direct object. - 17:13/q19/p2 Accusative as a state: the bird or deed emerges as a book. — Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, Kashshāf, Ṭabrisī, Bayḍāwī, Samīn and Ibn al-Jazarī describe this under yāʾ readings. Ibn al-Jazarī prefers it for the passive reading; Wāḥidī compares 81:10. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو جعفر,الحسن ومجاهد وابن محيصن ويعقوب); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر,قيل,يعقوب، والحسن، ومجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو جعفر,يعقوب); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر,ابن محيصن، مجاهد); NASHR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر,يعقوب ومجاهد وابن محيصن)]
- 17:13/q19/p3 Kitāb is nominative, as subject or passive subject in variant readings. — Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and a report of Ḥasan are named. Samīn calls Abū Jaʿfar's nominative in the passive construction awkward. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وعكرمة والضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر، الحسن، ابن محيصن، مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر,الحسن)]

## 17:13/q20 What is the book brought out at resurrection?  [كِتَٰبًۭا · referent]
turns on: A written register versus the soul's imprint or deeds themselves; an inward reading distinguishes two books.
- 17:13/q20/p1 The person's comprehensive record of good and bad deeds. — Ṭabarī says it lists worldly acts and assigned destiny; Ḥasan's angels write it. Ibn ʿĀshūr says everything small and great is itemized; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 18:49. The record can contain the grave's answers according to Damghānī. Biqāʿī says angels compare daily writing with the Preserved Tablet. [33 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); YAHYA-SALLAM(أبي بن كعب); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]
- 17:13/q20/p2 The soul bears impressions of actions that become legible when bodily concealment ends. — Rāzī says repetition makes dispositions, like water drops boring stone; at death the intellect reads the exposed impressions (50:22). Bayḍāwī presents this beside the written register, and Rāzī affirms the apparent reports as true too. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 17:13/q20/p3 The book consists of deeds themselves revealed in their actual reality. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 3:30 supports visible deeds rather than ordinary written symbols, and that the bird and book are one deed already attached before resurrection. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 17:13/q20/p4 There are two distinct books, one written by a person over time and one written for him in pre-eternity. — Sulamī reports this inward distinction, saying neither book is the other. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 17:13/q20/p5 One's speech, saliva, limbs, and joints compose the book. — Sulamī says a person dictates it to angels and the body testifies if he denies anything, citing 24:24. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 17:13/q21 What other reports concern deeds kept or recorded for a person?  [كِتَٰبًۭا · reports]
- 17:13/q21/p1 When a believer is ill, the usual deeds continue to be recorded until recovery or death. — Ibn Kathīr transmits the report from ʿUqba ibn ʿĀmir and grades its chain good and strong. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عقبة بن عامر)]

## 17:13/q22 What other book-and-scripture connections are drawn?  [كِتَٰبًۭا · links]
- 17:13/q22/p1 The worldly book of religious rulings grounds the accountability displayed in the Judgment record. — Itqān says Qur'ānic passages pair these two books, citing al-Kahf and al-Isrāʾ. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 17:13/q22/p2 Al-sijill is listed as a Persian word for a book. — Burhān notes the lexical borrowing. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 17:13/q23 How is the person said to encounter the book?  [يَلْقَىٰهُ · readings]

- 17:13/q23/p1 Yulaqqāhu, passive and doubled qāf: the person is handed or made to receive the book. — Reports name Ibn ʿĀmir, Abū Jaʿfar, al-Jaḥdarī, Ḥasan, and others; parallels include 76:11, 41:35 and 25:75. ## 17:13/q24 What is the syntax of 'he meets it, spread open'?  [يَلْقَىٰهُ مَنشُورًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause and 'spread open' are attributes or circumstantial descriptions, and why their order occurs.
- 17:13/q24/p1 'Spread open' is circumstantial, describing the book when encountered. — Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, Ṭabrisī and others give this parsing, including across the readings; Naḥḥās allows this or adjectival use. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 17:13/q24/p2 The encounter clause and 'spread open' both describe the book adjectivally. — Nasafī and others allow both. Samīn objects that this orders a less explicit adjective before a more explicit one; Itqān says the clausal modifier was advanced for the verse ending. [6 notes: NASAFI-FULL(الشامي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري، الشيخ، أبو البقاء); ITQAN(شمس الدين بن الصائغ)]
- 17:13/q24/p3 The encounter clause describes the book, while 'spread open' gives its circumstantial state. — Nasafī and Ālūsī report this conventional analysis; the open state obtains upon meeting. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL(الشامي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:13/q25 What does 'spread open' convey?  [مَنشُورًا · interpretation]
turns on: Readability and readiness versus the rhetorical purposes of announcement or public disclosure.
- 17:13/q25/p1 The record is unfolded, immediately visible and readable. — It is the opposite of folded, says Ibn ʿĀshūr; 81:10 compares the unfolding of scrolls. Ṭabrisī says the owner sees and reads it; even those illiterate in life read it at resurrection according to Qatāda. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو جعفر); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 17:13/q25/p2 Its openness hastens good news to the righteous and rebuke to evildoers. — Māwardī offers this explanation; Qurṭubī adopts it. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 17:13/q25/p3 Its openness publicly displays the person's good or evil deeds. — Māwardī gives this as an alternative purpose. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 17:13/q26 What does 'allotted fate' imply about decree and moral responsibility?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the fixed destiny dictates action independently, or foretells and incorporates a person's choices.
- 17:13/q26/p1 The person's freely acquired deeds determine felicity or misery within God's ordained pattern. — Jishumī argues against attributing human deeds solely to God; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 53:39–41 and 15:43,45, rejecting destiny independent of action. Asad takes 'destiny' chiefly as moral direction shaped by inclinations, actions and omissions; Study Quran allows foreknowledge of choices. [11 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:13/q26/p2 What God decreed in prior knowledge necessarily reaches the person. — Ibn Qutayba cites poets on destiny: unnamed poet, al-Qass, Ibn Aḥmar, al-Shammākh, al-Aʿshā, Abū Zubayd, al-Rāʿī and Afnūn. Rāzī argues that the fixed deed cannot fail; Qāshānī cites womb-fixed fortune, while Study Quran acknowledges a predestination reading. [19 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أبو زبيد,أفنون التغلبي,ابن أحمر,الأعشى,الراعي,الشاعر,الشماخ,القس); RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI-]

## 17:13/q27 How does the attached deed call people to account?  [* · interpretation]
- 17:13/q27/p1 Each person owns the deed and bears its consequence, which the open record makes undeniable. — Jishumī says awareness of recording aids the responsible person, urging obedience. Ibn ʿAṭiyya invokes the rejection of augury (Prophetic 'no contagion or ill omen'). Ṭabāṭabāʾī says book and bird jointly guarantee encounter with deeds. Suddī reports an unbeliever asking to audit himself and then being told to read; no soul bears another's burden. Al-Mufradāt glosses wizr as a burden used figuratively for sin. [13 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); TABRISI(أبو عبد الله); DURR-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI(المفردات); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 17:13/q27/p2 The fate and record are attached by decree, affirming God's comprehensive knowledge. — Ṭabarī uses the binding image of assigned destiny; Biqāʿī says even Israel's imposed scripture and decree cannot be escaped. The record exposes deeds and consequences. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 17:13/q28 How do surrounding verses lead into the individual record?  [* · links]
turns on: Different links stress warning, divine benefits, created time, or human haste.
- 17:13/q28/p1 After signs, guidance and warning, the record removes excuse and makes each person answer. — Rāzī says revelation completed the warning; Ibn ʿĀshūr says particular human deeds follow general 'everything' for emphasis (17:9–12). Kur'an Yolu similarly links faith, reward and punishment to self-accounting; Islāḥī describes the sūra section's invitation to the straight path and national punishment. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 17:13/q28/p2 Creation and the sun, moon, night and day serve worship; resurrection assesses the obedience enabled by them. — Rāzī cites 51:56; Jishumī calls night and day a wise blessing. Biqāʿī connects it to repeated inspection of creation (67:4). [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 17:13/q28/p3 God's provision of worldly and religious benefits calls people to serve Him, so their deeds are examined. — Rāzī gives this as another link. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 17:13/q28/p4 The rebuke of human haste leads to the record: rash desires obscure benefit and harm, but each deed remains for review. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this contextual link; al-Mufradāt adds that a verb for dispersal can mean hurrying. [2 notes: TABATABAI(المفردات)]

## 17:13/q29 How do inward readings understand the attached states and the opened book?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different inward accounts identify imposed states, pre-eternal fortune, or soul impressions.
- 17:13/q29/p1 Pre-eternal happiness, misery, states and deeds remain attached through life, death and resurrection. — Bursevī reports the Taʾwīlāt Najmiyya account; Qāshānī likens fortune fixed in the womb to a collar. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); QASHANI]
- 17:13/q29/p2 The book is the soul's hidden impress of actions revealed after death. — Rāzī describes attraction toward God as happiness and away from Him as misery; he affirms literal narratives alongside this spiritual sense. Ālūsī only refers to an earlier mystical interpretation, without restating it. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:13/q29/p3 States imposed by God demand patience in witnessing or etiquette in nearness. — Sulamī reports al-Naṣrābādhī's contrast between self-imposed and divinely imposed states. [1 notes: SULAMI(النصرآباذي)]

## 17:13/q30 What ancillary Arabic sayings and poetry illuminate fate, omens, and figurative language?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Each cited saying illustrates a separate idiom.
- 17:13/q30/p1 Arabic poetry portrays decree as unavertable, and treats misguidance as genuine leading astray. — Ibn Qutayba cites eight poets on fate, rejects glossing Labīd's 'whomever He misguides' as merely 'names astray', and notes iḍlāl can mean destruction, comparing 32:10. - 17:13/q30/p2 A camel's full lips and skin show feeding; 'food returned' means it reached the stomach. — Ibn Qutayba explains a proverb's physical signs of good feeding. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 17:13/q30/p3 Escaping 'at the little throatful of the chin' means surviving at the last instant. — Ibn Qutayba cites a Hudhalī poet on breath at the throat. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 17:13/q30/p4 A promiscuous woman's skirt 'causing consumption' depicts wealth exhausted by following her. — Ibn Qutayba identifies financial loss under a bodily image. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 17:13/q30/p5 A 'mountain goat crossing' signifies ill omen from either direction. — Ibn Qutayba notes the goat's passage, especially to the left; this also illuminates the omen idiom. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 17:13/q30/p6 A lowly person in rich pasture may boast, while abundant spring growth can cause hostility. — Ibn Qutayba explains ʿabd wa-khalā and a proverb about spoiled milk and bows, with a poet on enmity sprouting with grass. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 17:13/q30/p7 Other pastoral sayings describe lactation, tethers for young animals, and judging fatness from eating. — Ibn Qutayba glosses tarmīd, ribq, ranaq and afwāhuhā majassuhā. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 17:13/q30/p8 Nār in a saying about lineage is a cautery brand. — Ibn Qutayba notes its extension to anything branded with an iron. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 17:13/q31 What ancillary rhetorical examples are cited near discussion of the verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The examples concern poetic compression, omitted objects, or verse endings.
- 17:13/q31/p1 A pilgrimage poem exemplifies apt metaphor, economical wording and order that makes a scene immediately understood. — Jurjānī says its compressed completion of rites and wiping of corners indicate farewell circumambulation and departure. [3 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 17:13/q31/p2 Omitting understood objects can focus on actions and generalize their force. — Jurjānī analyses four omitted objects and examples of a mother being wearied and chambers that warm and shade; naming an object would narrow the assertion. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 17:13/q31/p3 A clausal modifier precedes a single-word modifier here for the verse ending. — Ibn al-Ṣāʾigh lists this among many rhyme adjustments such as fronting governed words, pronouns and later events, or altering final letters and vowels. [4 notes: ITQAN(شمس الدين بن الصائغ)]

## 17:13/q32 How is the strength of transmitted reports assessed?  [* · reports]
- 17:13/q32/p1 Reports lacking Qur'ānic, Prophetic or decisive rational support and having weak chains are omitted. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this of reports through Sunni and Shiʿi routes from the Prophet, ʿAlī, Salmān and others on these verses. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 17:13/q32/p2 The apparent resurrection reports are affirmed while spiritual interpretations are also permitted. — Rāzī calls affirming both the sound approach. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
