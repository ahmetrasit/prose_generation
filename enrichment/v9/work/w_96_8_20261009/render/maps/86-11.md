# 86:11: 14 questions

## 86:11/q01 What does al-samāʾ denote here?  [ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: Whether sky names the canopy itself or the clouds associated with its rain.
- 86:11/q01/p1 The familiar heaven or canopy above. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows the familiar sky; Abū Ḥayyān calls it the canopy; Ālūsī attributes it to the majority. Ibn Zayd's celestial bodies traverse this familiar sky. Elmalılı says it remains possible on the rain reading. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد,الجمهور); ELMALILI(İbn Zeyd)]
- 86:11/q01/p2 The rain-bearing clouds. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Elmalılı, and Ālūsī allow sky to signify cloud. Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid are cited for clouds containing or repeatedly bringing rain; Bayḍāwī's sea-water explanation also makes sky mean cloud. Elmalılı says rain may repeatedly fall from a cloud. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); ELMALILI(Mücâhid,İbn Abbas)]

## 86:11/q02 What is the sky's al-rajʿ?  [ٱلرَّجْعِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the return is precipitation and its gifts, visible celestial recurrence, the sky's own motion, returning angels, or a broader or inward restoration; Ālūsī and Elmalılı specifically dispute literal rotation of heaven.
- 86:11/q02/p1 Rain or water that returns repeatedly. — Most exegetes and lexicographers identify it as rain; al-Farrāʾ, Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and others stress rain after rain or its yearly recurrence. Abū ʿUbayda calls it water. Ibn Qutayba, Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Elmalılı and others cite al-Mutanakhil's Hudhalī sword simile; Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī and Elmalılı also cite al-Khansāʾ. Abū Ḥayyān prefers rain as standard usage fitting earth's fissure and vegetation; Wāḥidī and Rāzī prefer it among explanations. [76 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,عكرمة,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(أبو عبيدة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(أهل التفسير); THALABI(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس,المنخل الهذلي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس في رواية عطاء,الفراء,مقاتل وسعيد بن جبير وعكرمة عن ابن عباس ومجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة والضحاك,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الهذلي,غيره); TABRISI(أكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل اللغة,الخليل,المنخل,عامة المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخنساء,الشاعر,الشاعر الهذلي); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عطاء); BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد,الجمهور,الخنساء); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI(القمي,قيل); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن فارس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ, Ṭs); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 86:11/q02/p2 Clouds that repeatedly bring rain. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid describe rain-filled clouds or clouds raining and returning to rain. Māwardī and Ālūsī report the clouds themselves as the return; Ālūsī says God sends them from time to time. The Turkish commentary also includes rain-bearing clouds in a recurring water cycle. [16 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ, Ṭs)]
- 86:11/q02/p3 Provision, benefit, or good repeatedly bestowed from the sky. — Ṭabarī includes clouds and people's provisions; al-Ḥasan and Qatāda say the yearly provision sustains people and livestock, who would perish without it. Wāḥidī's scholars of meaning speak of repeatedly bestowed good. Ṭabrisī includes rain and sustenance; Elmalılı's report from Ḥasan includes water, plants and benefit. Qurṭubī and Rāzī report benefit more generally. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI(Hasan)]
- 86:11/q02/p4 The sun, moon, and stars repeatedly setting and rising or revisiting their stations. — Ibn Zayd explains their recurring appearance, direction, states and stations; some reports specify sun and moon, others add stars. ʿAbd al-Qāhir adds apparent retrograde movement of some stars. Biqāʿī includes night, day and seasons. Kur'an Yolu's translation chooses this reading; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the stars' rise after setting observable. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); JISHUMI(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(عبد القاهر الجرجاني); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); ELMALILI(İbn Zeyd); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Kesîr); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 86:11/q02/p5 The heaven or celestial sphere itself cycling back to its starting position. — Bayḍāwī and Biqāʿī describe return to a prior position; Ālūsī's reported version invokes exchange of apogee and perigee. Ālūsī objects that early Muslims distinguished heaven from the rotating sphere and that sphere-motion is philosophical. Elmalılı rejects literal motion for lack of proof and says old astronomy is unestablished. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(Kādî Beyzâvî); ALUSI-FULL-; ELMALILI-]
- 86:11/q02/p6 Angels returning to heaven with people's deeds. — Māwardī proposes this as an oath interpretation; Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report it. Elmalılı adds that angelic powers may have effects visible in recurrent events. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 86:11/q02/p7 The sky's return to a former state. — Māwardī transmits this from ʿIkrima without further specification. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 86:11/q02/p8 Returning vegetation after it withers and becomes mixed with soil. — Biqāʿī extends the recurrent return to plants after their decay; al-Khalīl also records spring vegetation as a sense of rajʿ, and Elmalılı cites the same lexical range. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل); ELMALILI]
- 86:11/q02/p9 A broad heavenly process of cyclical return and restoration. — Elmalılı deliberately synthesizes celestial cycles, sunrise and sunset, winds and clouds, and alternation of light and darkness, heat and cold, and moisture and dryness; he says all readings fit resurrection. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]
- 86:11/q02/p10 The spirit's return in a second creation. — Qāshānī offers an inward interpretation; Bursawī also pairs the spirit's sky with the body's earth that splits at death or upon their connection. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 86:11/q03 Why is rain or its benefit called al-rajʿ?  [ٱلرَّجْعِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the name points to observed recurrence, a proposed return of earthly water, or hope for its return.
- 86:11/q03/p1 Because rain or provision recurs repeatedly, often year after year. — The root denotes return and repetition according to Ibn Fāris; Jishumī describes renewed giving from state to state. Zajjāj, Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū Isḥāq, Ḥasan and others stress recurrence. Rāzī calls the rain sense metaphorical, not the literal name; al-Qaffāl likens it to an echo's returning sound. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also reports rain returning to earth. Bursawī specifies God brings it back after creating it. [14 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس في رواية عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيره); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ELMALILI(Zeccâc ve sâir eimme-i lügat); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن فارس)]
- 86:11/q03/p2 Because clouds or air take water from the seas or earth and return it as rain. — Rāzī presents this as an Arab belief; Bayḍāwī and Biqāʿī report the sea-to-cloud-to-earth explanation. Bursawī cites al-Rāghib for air returning taken water. Kur'an Yolu describes vapor rising and becoming rain. Ālūsī says the underlying supposition deserves scrutiny. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(من يقول); BURSEVI(الراغب,العرب); ELMALILI(Zeccâc ve sâir eimme-i lügat); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد)-]
- 86:11/q03/p3 As an optimistic name expressing hope for rain's return. — Rāzī and Bursawī report this explanation; Bursawī says rajʿ would then be an intransitive verbal noun. Abū Ḥayyān mentions naming rain rajʿ or irb in hope; Ālūsī reports an expectation alongside Ḥasan's annual sustenance. Elmalılı includes hoped-for return among linguistic derivations. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(بعض العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI(Zeccâc ve sâir eimme-i lügat)]

## 86:11/q04 What other Arabic senses and cognate names are given for rajʿ?  [ٱلرَّجْعِ · meaning]
turns on: Which attested associated sense or cognate rain-name is being discussed.
- 86:11/q04/p1 Rain is also called awb, with parallel names such as sabl and irb. — Qurṭubī cites a poem for awb; Abū Ḥayyān's poem uses return-derived names for clouds and rain; Samīn cites a second poem; Ālūsī notes awb and sabl as parallels. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشاعر); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 86:11/q04/p2 Rajʿ also denotes a lake, eddying water, benefit, and spring vegetation. — Elmalılı cites al-Qāmūs for this range; Qurṭubī attributes the spring vegetation sense to al-Khalīl; benefit is also reported as a possible sense of the oath phrase. [3 notes: ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل,قيل)]

## 86:11/q05 What is the morphology of dhāt al-rajʿ and rajʿ?  [ذَاتِ ٱلرَّجْعِ · grammar]
turns on: The notes address different morphological features of the phrase.
- 86:11/q05/p1 Dhāt is feminine and means possessing; rajʿ may function as a verbal noun used for rain. — Bursawī gives the feminine possessive construction. Ālūsī calls the rain sense a verbal noun used as a name for rainfall; Samīn calls the celestial reading a verbal noun for returning; Bursawī says the hopeful-name reading uses intransitive rather than transitive return. [4 notes: BURSEVI(بعض العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]
- 86:11/q05/p2 Rajʿān is an attested irregular plural of rajʿ. — Naḥḥās says analogy would instead yield arjuʿ or rujūʿ. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 86:11/q06 How is a stop on dhāt al-rajʿ read?  [ذَاتِ ٱلرَّجْعِ · readings]
- 86:11/q06/p1 Al-Kisāʾī is reported to stop with hāʾ on dhāt al-rajʿ. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports the same stop on dhāt al-ṣadʿ and other phrases beginning dhāt. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 86:11/q07 What is sworn by in this verse?  [وَٱلسَّمَآءِ ذَاتِ ٱلرَّجْعِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the oath object is the honored sky itself or its Lord.
- 86:11/q07/p1 God swears by the sky with its return, followed by the earth as a second oath. — Ṭabrisī calls it another oath reinforcing resurrection; Māwardī, Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān explicitly identify the sky as oath object, with Qurṭubī naming the earth a second oath. Rāzī places it after proofs of oneness and resurrection. Bursawī emphasizes their repeated benefits; Quṭb says the created scenes and their sounds convey force and decisiveness. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 86:11/q07/p2 The oath may be by the Lord of the sky. — Jishumī reports this as an alternative to swearing by the sky itself, honored for its power and blessing. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 86:11/q08 How do the sky and earth images work together?  [* · links]
- 86:11/q08/p1 Heaven gives or waters; earth opens and receives, producing vegetation and life like father, mother, and child. — Rāzī shifts from animal to plant creation and likens sky to father, earth to mother. Biqāʿī compares male watering and childbirth. Bursawī adds the plant as child; Elmalılı describes action above and receptivity below and reports Sufis calling heavens fathers and earthly realms mothers. Quṭb depicts rainfall and emergence as one life scene. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 86:11/q09 How do these images recall human creation earlier in the sūra?  [* · links]
- 86:11/q09/p1 Rain and emerging vegetation parallel effused reproductive water and life emerging from the womb or seed. — Elmalılı links rajʿ as rain with the effused water of 86:6, and rain fertilizing earth with water fertilizing a seed; he cites 36:36. Quṭb compares flowing rain and vegetation to reproductive fluid and an embryo emerging from womb darkness. Study Quran connects vegetation and human emergence in 86:6–8. [4 notes: ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]

## 86:11/q10 How does the returning sky support resurrection and judgement?  [* · links]
- 86:11/q10/p1 Recurring rain, growth, and celestial return show God's power to restore humans after death and the certainty of judgement. — Biqāʿī equates plants returning after decay with human restoration and says repeated processes prove the maker can restore the perished exactly. Elmalılı relates every rajʿ reading to 86:8 and the testing of secrets, and says humans return to the hereafter. Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects sky's return and earth's opening to emergence from graves. Asad says the pair displays creative and recreative power. Study Quran points to seasonal growth and 86:6–8. Iṣlāḥī says 11–14 make threatened Judgement certain and call for preparation; Quṭb says cosmic scenes remove lingering doubt. [10 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 86:11/q11 How does this oath lead to the affirmation of the Qur'an?  [* · links]
- 86:11/q11/p1 The cosmic oaths affirm that the Qur'an and its promised judgement are true and decisive. — Biqāʿī says preceding proofs of resurrection, requital and oneness establish God's speech as true and prompt the oath. Quṭb says any remaining doubt of the promise is met by a categorical declaration, with cosmic scenes as at 86:1; he stresses the expression's force. Kur'an Yolu says real manifestations of power attest the Qur'an as God's true speech. Iṣlāḥī says the judgement is no jest. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts the changing sky and earth with the unchanging sworn-to speech, grounded in fixed taʾwīl. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 86:11/q12 What does the sky–earth order reveal about God's action?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:11/q12/p1 Their recurring effects display divine power, knowledge, creative craft, and oneness. — Biqāʿī says restoration of what perishes demonstrates the maker's power and that preceding statements prove divine oneness. Bursawī says repeated oaths display perfect knowledge and power. Elmalılı locates life and return under God's creative and preserving power. Quṭb sees a stable, crafted order pointing to a maker unlike the work itself. Kur'an Yolu calls the events manifestations of God's power; Asad highlights creative and recreative power. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 86:11/q13 How do iyāb and awb compare with rujūʿ in the related lexical discussion?  [ٱلرَّجْعِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether iyāb is limited by time, intended destination, or the kind of returning subject.
- 86:11/q13/p1 Awb is voluntary return by a living being; Qur'anic iyāb and maʾāb usually concern creatures. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāghib defining maʾāb as its verbal noun and awwāb as one returning to God through repentance and obedience. She says rujūʿ usually applies to creatures too, but cites exceptions where affairs return to God: 11:123, 2:210, 3:109, 8:45, 22:86, 35:4 and 57:5. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]
- 86:11/q13/p2 Iyāb is return at night. — Al-Aṣmaʿī adduces poetry about an illness beginning at night. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الأصمعي); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو حاتم السجستاني)-]
- 86:11/q13/p3 Iyāb is return at any time, though often nocturnal. — Abū Ḥātim al-Sijistānī counters with a traveler, poetry and 88:25, while conceding common nighttime usage. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو حاتم السجستاني)]
- 86:11/q13/p4 Iyāb means return to an intended endpoint, unlike general rujūʿ. — Abū Hilāl al-ʿAskarī cites 88:25; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ suggests this distinction may explain Nāfiʿ's question about iyāb. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو هلال العسكري)]

## 86:11/q14 What distinctions about khashya and khushūʿ are recorded in the cited lexical discussion?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The notes compare different terms: fear and awe, then humility and submission.
- 86:11/q14/p1 Khashya is knowledge-grounded awe, unlike fear that may arise from force or terror. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says its Qur'anic forms concern worldly trial, including the unseen, Hour, Last Day, hardship, poverty, orphan loss and tyrannical wrong; when directed to a being, Qur'an directs it to God alone. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 86:11/q14/p2 Khushūʿ is sincere humility before majesty, unlike khudūʿ, which may be feigned submission. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says Arabs speak literally of a humble heart but figuratively of its submission; hypocrisy or fear may produce khudūʿ. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
