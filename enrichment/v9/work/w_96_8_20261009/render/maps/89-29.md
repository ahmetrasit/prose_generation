# 89:29: 18 questions

## 89:29/q01 How is the imperative adkhulī grammatically formed?  [فَٱدْخُلِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the imperative is inflected as a jussive, inherently uninflected, or governed by an understood lām.
- 89:29/q01/p1 Its omitted nūn marks the jussive imperative. — Al-Naḥḥās reports the Kufan analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الكوفيون)]
- 89:29/q01/p2 The imperative is uninflected, since it is neither a present-tense verb nor governed by a jussive particle. — Al-Naḥḥās reports the Basran objection to treating it as jussive. [1 notes: NAHHAS(البصريون)]
- 89:29/q01/p3 An omitted lām governs the imperative. — Al-Naḥḥās attributes this explanation to al-Farrāʾ. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء)]

## 89:29/q02 What does the fā before the command convey?  [فَٱدْخُلِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle explains return, orders the promised events, or makes the command an exhortation now.
- 89:29/q02/p1 It explains the preceding invitation to return. — Elmalılı calls the fā explanatory: entry specifies what return entails. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 89:29/q02/p2 It marks immediate spiritual admission at death, while bodily Paradise awaits resurrection. — Rāzī distinguishes the spiritual garden, following with fā, from bodily Paradise following with wa; al-Ālūsī says certainty can permit a future Garden event after fā. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI]
- 89:29/q02/p3 It can urge present effort and righteous deeds leading to inclusion among God's sincere servants. — Elmalılı takes the causative fā as a summons to join servants free of Satan's dominion in this life and the next. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 89:29/q03 What does entering among God's servants mean?  [فِى عِبَٰدِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'servants' identifies a righteous community, an unqualified company, bodies rejoined at resurrection, obedience, God's party, mercy, or Paradise with its people.
- 89:29/q03/p1 Join the company of God's righteous, believing, chosen, or near servants. — Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī and many others identify the company as righteous or believing. Al-Zajjāj, al-Baghawī and others specify chosen or obedient servants; al-Rāzī and Quṭb stress nearness. Al-Baghawī, Abū ʿUbayda and Study Quran invoke Solomon's prayer in 27:19; al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 29:9, and Ibn ʿĀshūr and Elmalılı connect 4:69. Al-Rāzī says purified souls together reflect one another like polished mirrors, raising joy and rank, and cites 56:90–91; al-Bayḍāwī preserves this near-servants explanation. Al-Ālūsī calls the mirror account speculative while affirming spiritual companionship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says complete surrender of being, acts and choices realizes servitude. Iṣlāḥī ties admission to the soul's success in the tests of favor and hardship, gratitude, patience and others' rights. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers righteousness from the honored possessive and pairing with Paradise. [73 notes: TUSTARI; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(آخرون,جمهور القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(أبو عبيدة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الطبري,بعض المفسرين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj,K); TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 89:29/q03/p2 Be with God's servants, without needing to supply a further qualifier. — Al-Suddī glosses fī as 'with.' Ibn ʿĀshūr says the preposition conveys real inclusion and so companionship. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues the tranquil soul promised Paradise is already righteous, so adding 'righteous' is unnecessary; she compares the concise 'servants' in 39:17 and 43:68. Burhān notes that fī can carry maʿa, 'with.' [13 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); BURHAN; DURR(السدي); DURR-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]

Al-Qurṭubī supports it from Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd; al-Ālūsī reports an ellipsis, 'bodies of My servants,' and notes that a generic plural can follow a singular soul. - 89:29/q03/p4 Enter God's obedience. — Muḥammad ibn Muzāḥim pairs God's obedience here with His mercy as the meaning of the Garden; al-Ḍaḥḥāk is also reported with the obedience explanation. [8 notes: TAB(بعض أهل العربية من أهل البصرة); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية من أهل البصرة); THALABI(الأخفش); QURTUBI(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين)]

[3 notes: THALABI(مقاتل والقرظي وأبو عبيدة); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 89:29/q03/p8 Join God's special servants who have realized essential unity. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward explanation. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 89:29/q04 How does fī work in 'among My servants'?  [فِى عِبَٰدِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition denotes company figuratively, actual containment in bodies, or the sense 'with.'
- 89:29/q04/p1 It marks figurative inclusion in a company; a literal container would normally follow dukhūl directly. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn contrast figurative entry into a crowd with direct-object entry into a real place; al-Ālūsī reports Abū Ḥayyān's analysis. Ibn ʿĀshūr instead calls the inclusion real while deriving companionship from it. Burhān catalogs combinations of physical and conceptual place and content, calling the mixed metaphor closest to the literal use. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 89:29/q04/p2 It marks actual location if the spirit enters the servants' bodies. — Al-Samīn says the bodily reading makes the locative relation literal; Abū Ḥayyān's figurative versus literal distinction explains why constructions differ. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN]
- 89:29/q04/p3 It can mean maʿa, 'with.' — Burhān explicitly cites this command as an instance; al-Suddī and al-Farrāʾ gloss entry as being with the servants. [6 notes: BURHAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); DURR(السدي); DURR-FULL(السدي)]

## 89:29/q05 How is 'My servants' read, and what follows from the variants?  [عِبَٰدِى · readings]
turns on: The transmitted plural and singular forms, whether the singular is collective or bodily, and expanded codex wordings; al-Ṭabarī appeals to reader agreement, while al-Naḥḥās contests al-Farrāʾ's list of readers.
- 89:29/q05/p1 The received majority reading is plural ʿibādī, God's servants. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Zajjāj prefer it on the agreement of authoritative or majority readers; it means the righteous servants. Ibn Kathīr favors the apparent plural and links return to divine judgment in 6:62 and 40:43, though one note attributes a contrary singular choice to Ibn Jarīr and calls it unusual. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān also name the majority. [11 notes: FARRA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA(جمهور القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وابن عباس وعكرمة والضحاك ومجاهد وأبو جعفر وأبو صالح والكلبي وأبو شيخ الهنائي واليماني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة والضحاك ومجاهد وأبو جعفر وأبو صالح والكلبي); IBNKATHIR+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 89:29/q05/p2 A variant reads singular ʿabdī, 'My servant.' — Al-Farrāʾ calls it Ibn ʿAbbās's alone, but al-Naḥḥās rejects exclusivity and names Mujāhid, ʿIkrima, Abū Jaʿfar and al-Ḍaḥḥāk. Other lists add Abū Shaykh, al-Kalbī, Ibn al-Sumayfiʿ, al-Yamānī, Ubayy, Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Abū ʿImrān and Abū Ṣāliḥ. Ibn Jinnī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī say the singular can be generic, presenting servants united in worship; Ibn Jinnī cites 59:14 and a Prophetic 'one hand' comparison. Al-Naḥḥās allows either each soul entering one servant or one standing for many. Abū Ḥayyān prefers generic equivalence with the plural; al-Biqāʿī says the generic can include few or many, yet also supports bodily return. Other readers take the singular as an individual soul entering its former body. ## 89:29/q06 When is the soul told to enter among the servants?  [فَٱدْخُلِى فِى عِبَٰدِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the surrounding address is heard at death, at resurrection, after reckoning, during the grave interval, or as exhortation in life; the sequence of return and entry is central.
- 89:29/q06/p1 At resurrection, sometimes after a return command heard at death. — Abū Ṣāliḥ distinguishes departure from the world at death from both entry commands on Resurrection. Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk locate the soul's return to the body then; al-Ṭabarī prefers resurrection because the next words command entry among servants and into the Garden. Al-Ālūsī reports ʿIkrima and al-Ḍaḥḥāk: nafs can mean person, rabb God, with an implied destination. Elmalılı says the Abū Ṣāliḥ reports can fit a unified command or each death as one's resurrection. [26 notes: TAB(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,الضحاك); TAB-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNABIHATIM(أبو صالح); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح); QURTUBI(ابن عباس والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس، ابن مسعود,الضحاك); DURR(أبو صالح); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); ALUSI(أبو صالح,عكرمة والضحاك); ELMALILI(Ebû Sâlih); TAB+]
- 89:29/q06/p2 At death, with immediate admission among close spirits or righteous servants. — Al-Ḥasan says God takes the believer's soul, both are pleased, and it enters Paradise among the righteous; Ibn Zayd's group sends the spirit to the sacred realm. Al-Rāzī relates fā to immediate spiritual bliss at death, while al-Ālūsī allows bodily Paradise after resurrection or temporary Garden enjoyment before the Hour. Al-Ālūsī cites a hadith of believers' souls in birds' crops in Paradise and an athar granting half the promised Garden at death. Ibn Kathīr says the address is heard at death and again at resurrection, like angelic good news at both moments. [19 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,زيد بن أسلم); IBNKATHIR; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; DURR(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن,زيد بن أسلم); ALUSI(ابن زيد وجماعة); ELMALILI]
- 89:29/q06/p3 After resurrection and completion of the reckoning. — Elmalılı considers this most apparent from 89:24 and the sequence; al-Ālūsī reports return to the Lord's promised Garden and righteous company after reckoning, answering the prior-residence objection by potential existence in Adam's loins in the Garden. He compares reports on maʿād in 28:85 and notes the disputed identity of Adam's Garden. [6 notes: ELMALILI; ALUSI; ELMALILI+]
- 89:29/q06/p4 After death but before resurrection, as the believer sees a Garden home and returns for grave questioning. — Al-Ālūsī transmits Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī: the soul reenters the body before Munkar and Nakīr question it; he supplies no grading. [2 notes: ALUSI(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 89:29/q06/p5 As a present exhortation to believers to persevere and join the righteous through good works. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a generic-soul exhortation set against the disbelievers; Elmalılı makes the fā a summons to righteous deeds in this life and the next. Al-Ālūsī reports a continuous inward reading, with potential Garden entry, but calls it unsound. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA(بعض العلماء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض العلماء); ELMALILI; ALUSI-]

## 89:29/q07 What is the return that leads into this command?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'return' is bodily restoration, departure at death, or arrival at divine reward or favor; some explanations supply an unspoken destination.
- 89:29/q07/p1 The spirit returns to its former body at resurrection. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk describe the restoration; al-Zajjāj's and Ibn Masʿūd's singular or body readings support it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports rabb as the soul's human owner, and al-Ālūsī gives the same analysis; al-Rāzī combines return to divine reward with reentry into the body. [11 notes: TAB(ابن عباس,الضحاك); IBNATIYYA(بعض العلماء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ELMALILI(İbn Abbas)]
- 89:29/q07/p2 The soul returns to God or departs the world at death; entry is later or follows immediately. — Abū Ṣāliḥ places return at departure and entry at resurrection; Ibn Zayd's group instead has immediate company of close servants in the sacred realm. [15 notes: TAB(أبو صالح); TAB-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNABIHATIM(أبو صالح); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو صالح); DURR(أبو صالح); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); ALUSI(أبو صالح,ابن زيد وجماعة); ELMALILI(Ebû Sâlih)]
- 89:29/q07/p3 The person returns to God's reward, promise, favor, or honorable command. — Al-Qaffāl treats the imperative as a report of the tranquil soul's return. Al-Ālūsī reports the promised Garden and company after reckoning, general favor from barzakh or before judgment, or God's treatment of her without distress and at an honored station; ʿIkrima and al-Ḍaḥḥāk allow an implied destination. Ibn Kathīr invokes return to judgment and divine presence in 6:62 and 40:43. [12 notes: RAZI(القفال); IBNKATHIR; ALUSI(عكرمة والضحاك)]

## 89:29/q08 Why are joining the servants and entering the Garden separate commands?  [فَٱدْخُلِى فِى عِبَٰدِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the separate clauses distinguish kinds or timing of reward, repeat a promise for effect, or reverse the order of one combined entry.
- 89:29/q08/p1 The first grants spiritual happiness through righteous company; the second grants bodily Paradise, completing the higher spiritual reward. — Al-Ālūsī puts spiritual reward first; al-Rāzī relates its immediacy to fā and the later physical Garden to wa. Al-Ālūsī also reports immediate enjoyment of a temporary Garden, citing believers' souls in birds' crops and half a promised Garden at death, while calling mirror-and-radiance speculation rhetorical. [8 notes: ALUSI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 89:29/q08/p2 The two commands restate the general glad tidings in detail, and repetition emphasizes Paradise and renews joy. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says detailing after summary and repeating 'enter' rather than sharing one verb secure the hearers' joy. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:29/q08/p3 The two clauses have an inverted sense: enter Paradise with God's servants. — Al-Thaʿlabī ascribes this arrangement to Muqātil, al-Quraẓī and Abū ʿUbayda. [1 notes: THALABI(مقاتل والقرظي وأبو عبيدة)]

## 89:29/q09 What does the possessive 'My servants' add to the address?  [عِبَٰدِى · rhetoric]
turns on: The honored relationship conveyed by God's possessive and the transition from the earlier third-person wording into first-person speech.
- 89:29/q09/p1 It honors the servants and marks them as righteous or chosen. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls the possessive an honor. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers righteousness from it and its pairing with 'My Garden'; Biqāʿī says they are worthy of attribution to God. Kur'an Yolu calls 'My servants' a supreme honor and cites divine pleasure greater than all blessings in 9:72. [5 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:29/q09/p2 It shifts from third-person reference to God's first-person speech. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the shift in both 'My servants' and 'My Garden.' If God voices the preceding address, 'your Lord' conveys closeness and honor; if angels voice it, God takes up their speech here. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:29/q10 Who voices these commands, and what kind of address are they?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether a divine declaration is implied from context, a new direct exhortation begins, or angels introduce God's first-person commands.
- 89:29/q10/p1 God speaks in an afterlife scene, with an implied 'God will say'; the commands announce and effect the promised outcome. — Elmalılı reports the majority's ellipsis and calls the address declarative and creative. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues the received sequence can reflect one revelation and the address answer the earth's crushing, with intervening matter parenthetical; he also allows direct divine speech after the warning. Kur'an Yolu says the direct address warmly extends the promise into this life. [4 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:29/q10/p2 God addresses believers directly at the sūra's end as a call they should heed. — Elmalılı finds a legislative divine address plausible; separation from preceding text highlights the tranquil souls as hearers. Ibn ʿĀshūr permits a new passage prompted by the polytheist's punishment. [2 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:29/q10/p3 Angels may voice the preceding address, with God taking up their speech at 'enter among My servants.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts this with direct divine speech; 'your Lord' is ordinary if angels speak, while God's first-person command begins here. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB(ابن عباس)]

## 89:29/q11 What events are reported in connection with this address?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different reports name Ḥamza, Khubayb or ʿUthmān as an occasion; other narrations concern Ibn ʿAbbās's funeral or the believer's grave, while al-Rāzī applies the wording generally.
- 89:29/q11/p1 It was revealed concerning Ḥamza ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. — Al-Rāzī reports this occasion without establishing it; Abū Ḥayyān also lists Ḥamza. Al-Rāzī insists the wording remains general regardless of a specific occasion. [3 notes: RAZI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 89:29/q11/p2 It was revealed concerning Khubayb ibn ʿAdī when he was crucified by Meccans. — Al-Rāzī says God answered Khubayb's prayer that his face turn toward the sacred city and no one could turn it away; one version says he faced Medina. Abū Ḥayyān also lists Khubayb. Al-Rāzī retains the wording's general force. [3 notes: RAZI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 89:29/q11/p3 It was revealed concerning ʿUthmān after he bought the well of Rūma for public drinking. — Al-Durr transmits a report from Ibn ʿAbbās about God's invitation to buy the well and the subsequent revelation; Abū Ḥayyān also lists ʿUthmān. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 89:29/q11/p4 At Ibn ʿAbbās's burial in Ṭāʾif, an unusual bird entered his bier and did not emerge, and an unseen speaker recited these verses at his grave. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's report appears in Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Durr; descriptions vary between bier and coffin and between recitation at the grave and after burial. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); DURR(سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 89:29/q11/p5 A believer rests in a spacious, illuminated grave, while an unbeliever is directed to torment. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr: Quranic or sunlike light illumines the believer's grave and the believer sleeps like a bride until awakened by a beloved; the unbeliever receives a foul coarse covering. [1 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص)]
- 89:29/q11/p6 Specific reports do not restrict the address to their named persons. — Al-Rāzī says the general wording governs beyond a particular cause, despite the Ḥamza and Khubayb reports. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 89:29/q12 What reward is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 89:29/q12/p1 Recitation during the ten nights brings forgiveness; recitation at other times brings light on Judgment Day. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī report the same Prophetic promise without grading it in these notes. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 89:29/q13 How does this verse close the sūra and relate to its other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: How the final promise relates to earlier warning and testing, and whether the closing verses are a later addition or part of the original sequence.
- 89:29/q13/p1 It adds glad tidings for the faithful after warning and punishment, encouraging good and deterring evil. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Quran joins promise to warning; he links this address to the preceding crushing of the earth and treats intervening material as parenthetical if the sequence is one revelation. Corpus Coranicum says the final positive afterlife ending answers the Hell scene in 89:23 and God's speech supersedes human responses to worldly allotment and threatened Hell. It calls verses 27–30 a later addition, whereas Ibn ʿĀshūr infers one original sequence from recitation and codex order. Iṣlāḥī sees the peaceful soul as passing the sura's tests of wealth, deprivation, gratitude, patience and others' rights. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:29/q13/p2 The peaceful life among the righteous resembles the ten nights, and Garden entry the festival dawn. — Elmalılı proposes this correspondence with the opening oath and Eid al-Adha imagery. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 89:29/q14 Should the two entry commands form one verse or be divided?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether semantic continuity outweighs an approximate end rhyme in assigning the verse boundary.
- 89:29/q14/p1 The two imperatives belong together, so the Kufan division between them is unsuitable. — Neuwirth argues their close relation requires keeping them together. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 89:29/q14/p2 The division is supported by an approximate rhyme between ʿibādī and jannatī. — Corpus Coranicum counters Neuwirth by allowing nonclassical vowel and consonant variation, as in other Quranic pairs in 89:15–16, 23–24 and 27–28. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth)-]

## 89:29/q15 What inward or spiritual lesson is drawn from entering among God's servants?  [فَٱدْخُلِى فِى عِبَٰدِى · inward]
turns on: Whether the command describes realized spiritual communion or can be applied as an ongoing earthly practice; al-Ālūsī rejects the latter as tafsīr.
- 89:29/q15/p1 It signifies realized servitude and communion with God's special servants. — Al-Qāshānī says the special servants realize essential unity. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the soul relinquishes independence and submits its being, actions and choices to God. Al-Rāzī likens purified souls to mirrors increasing one another's radiance and spiritual rank, citing 56:90–91; al-Ālūsī calls the mirror account rhetorical speculation. [6 notes: QASHANI; TABATABAI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-]
- 89:29/q15/p2 Throughout life, turn to God, join sincere servants by good works, and potentially enter the Garden. — Al-Ālūsī reports this inward application but says it is not a sound interpretation. Elmalılı separately urges worldly righteous effort through the causative fā. [1 notes: ELMALILI; ALUSI-]

## 89:29/q16 What other textual echoes or devotional uses accompany this phrase?  [* · links]
- 89:29/q16/p1 The request to be admitted among righteous servants echoes Solomon's prayer. — Muqātil and al-Baghawī cite 27:19; Study Quran joins it with 29:9. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 89:29/q16/p2 The word ʿabd is cross-referenced to 53:10. — Corpus Coranicum supplies the cross-reference without an additional explanation. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:29/q16/p3 The promise can be voiced as a prayer for the commentator and readers to return content, join sincere servants, and attain Paradise and God's vision. — Elmalılı closes his discussion with this supplication. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 89:29/q17 When is the believer promised Paradise?  [* · reports]
- 89:29/q17/p1 At death, gathering, and resurrection. — A report from Abū Usāma ibn Zayd from his father and Zayd ibn Aslam gives these three moments of good news, without choosing just one moment for the entry command. [3 notes: TAB(أبو أسامة بن زيد عن أبيه); IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]

## 89:29/q18 What is Paradise for the servants admitted to it?  [* · interpretation]
- 89:29/q18/p1 It is the eternal home and dwelling of the righteous and virtuous. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes this description to the majority. [1 notes: QURTUBI(الجمهور)]
