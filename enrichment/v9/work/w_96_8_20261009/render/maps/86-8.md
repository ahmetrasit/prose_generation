# 86:8: 14 questions

## 86:8/q01 Who does the pronoun in “indeed He” refer to?  [إِنَّهُۥ · referent]
- 86:8/q01/p1 God, the Creator. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Bursawī infer Him from the preceding creation; al-Samīn says no one else creates. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, Quṭb and Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise identify God. Al-Ālūsī says the earlier unnamed agent magnifies Him; Quṭb adds His care for humanity. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI]

## 86:8/q02 Why is the Creator indicated by a pronoun instead of named?  [إِنَّهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 86:8/q02/p1 The context makes the Creator identifiable while the pronoun directs the hearer back to their own maker. — Al-Biqāʿī says each person can recognize who formed them, making the same creator’s power to restore persuasive; an explicit divine name might be denied. Al-Ālūsī says leaving the earlier agent unspoken magnifies the sole Creator. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God’s identity is obvious. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 86:8/q03 How can this clause function as the answer to the opening oath?  [إِنَّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preceding intervening verses belong to the oath’s answer or stand parenthetically.
- 86:8/q03/p1 The oath’s answer can be any clause governed by inna. — Abū Ḥayyān gives this as one syntactic possibility. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 86:8/q03/p2 This very clause is the oath’s answer, with the intervening verses parenthetical. — Abū Ḥayyān gives this as another possibility. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 86:8/q04 What does rajʿ mean as a word?  [رَجْعِهِۦ · meaning]
- 86:8/q04/p1 Returning something to its original state. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives this lexical definition. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 86:8/q05 What is the return over which God has power?  [رَجْعِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: The referent of the suffix in rajʿihi—human or fluid—and whether return means resurrection, reversal of life, fluid restoration or retention, or a later return to earthly life.
- 86:8/q05/p1 Resurrecting the human after death. — Muqātil, al-Farrāʾ, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan and others give this interpretation. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Thaʿlabī as quoted by al-Qurṭubī favor it because 86:9 describes secrets tested on Judgment Day. Al-Naḥḥās calls it clearest, following Ibn Jarīr; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān call it clearest or stronger. Some describe recreating the decayed human, restoring the original state or bringing the person before God. Ibn Kathīr attributes resurrection to al-Ḍaḥḥāk. Al-Ālūsī rejects rival fluid and age readings. For why initial creation proves this, see the separate question below. [44 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة وقتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة والحسن وعكرمة); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL(الملوي); DURR-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة)+; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; NAHHAS(قتادة)+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI(الحسن وقتادة والجبائي)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 86:8/q05/p2 Returning emitted fluid to the urethra. — Mujāhid is repeatedly named; other reports name al-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿIkrima or both. Al-Ṭabarī’s general fluid-origin report can encompass this, and al-Naḥḥās says al-Ḍaḥḥāk gave a similar fluid explanation. Al-Zamakhsharī and others list this among alternative sites. Al-Ālūsī rejects the fluid reading. [21 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الضحاك,مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وعكرمة)-]
- 86:8/q05/p3 Returning the fluid to the backbone or loins from which it emerged. — Attributed chiefly to ʿIkrima, with al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Mujāhid and Ibn Abzā also reported. Some specify the father’s loins or the place of emergence, while Ibn Kathīr leaves that place unspecified. Al-Biqāʿī includes return to the man’s spine and woman’s breast region. Al-Ālūsī rejects the fluid reading. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل,عكرمة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(عكرمة ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك وعكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن أبزى,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وعكرمة)-]
- 86:8/q05/p4 Returning the human to the original fluid state. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk is repeatedly cited; some say before creation, while al-Biqāʿī describes bone, flesh and blood dissolving back into fluid. Al-Zamakhsharī lists return to the original state without specifying the mechanism. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); THALABI(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 86:8/q05/p5 Reversing a human through the stages of life. — Reports differ in detail: al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān trace old age through youth and childhood to semen; some mention only old age to youth. Al-Qurṭubī also reports old-to-young and young-to-old, and another old-age-to-childhood-to-sperm sequence. Ibn ʿAbbās likewise says an old man can become young and a young man old. Al-Biqāʿī adds adulthood, embryo, clot and drop, then the spinal and breast regions. Al-Ālūsī calls this unsound. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك,بعض أهل التأويل); NAHHAS(الضحاك); THALABI(مقاتل بن حيان); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(مقاتل بن حيان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك,الماوردي والثعلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)-]
- 86:8/q05/p6 Holding back the fluid so it does not emerge. — Ibn Zayd repeatedly gives this interpretation, explicitly comparing divine power to create from the fluid. Al-Naḥḥās ascribes a similar gloss to al-Ḍaḥḥāk. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(ابن زيد,الضحاك); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 86:8/q05/p7 Returning a resurrected person to earthly life. — Al-Māwardī says this is the return unbelievers will request; al-Qurṭubī repeats his possibility. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)]

## 86:8/q06 Why is “upon his return” placed before “able”?  [عَلَىٰ رَجْعِهِۦ لَقَادِرٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 86:8/q06/p1 To emphasize the particular return under discussion without limiting God’s power over other acts. — Bursawī gives this explanation of the order. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 86:8/q07 How does “upon his return” attach syntactically?  [عَلَىٰ رَجْعِهِۦ لَقَادِرٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase is governed by “able” directly or through its relative construction.
- 86:8/q07/p1 It attaches directly to “able.” — Al-Ālūsī presents this as one grammatical analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 86:8/q07/p2 It is understood through the relative pronoun governed by “able.” — Al-Ālūsī presents this as the alternative grammatical analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 86:8/q08 What does the emphatic wording of God’s ability convey?  [لَقَادِرٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 86:8/q08/p1 His power to restore the human is clear and without incapacity. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī call it plainly possible and unthwarted. Al-Ālūsī quotes al-Kashshāf’s gloss of emphatic capability as clearly powerful, says resurrection is the argument’s particular target, and takes the emphatic standalone sentence as answering an implied question because the proof is already clear. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف)]

## 86:8/q09 How does the following “day when secrets are tested” relate grammatically to this verse?  [رَجْعِهِۦ لَقَادِرٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Which expression governs “day,” and whether attaching it to ability would imply power limited to Judgment Day.
- 86:8/q09/p1 It qualifies the return or resurrection on that Day. — Al-Ṭabarī treats that Day as when the dead are restored, and al-Zajjāj and Ibn al-Jawzī use 86:9 to support the resurrection interpretation. Elmalılı prefers the day as modifying the return through a new sentence, since divine power is not confined to that day. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة); ELMALILI+; NAHHAS-]
- 86:8/q09/p2 An implied verb derived from “return” governs “day.” — Al-Zamakhsharī supplies an omitted governing verb on nonresurrection readings. Al-Burhān and al-Itqān say the day belongs semantically with the verbal noun but syntax bars separating that noun from its complement with an unrelated element; al-Itqān cites 40:10. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 86:8/q09/p3 “Able” may govern the day, since Judgment is singled out as the crucial warning. — Abū Ḥayyān answers Ibn ʿAṭiyya’s concern that this would limit God’s power: power is universal, although this Day is especially relevant. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ELMALILI-]
- 86:8/q09/p4 The following “helper” governs the day. — Al-Naḥḥās rejects Ibn Jarīr’s attachment to rajʿihi because it separates a relative construction from its antecedent with inna’s predicate; he assigns the day to nāṣir instead. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 86:8/q10 What argument from original creation establishes God’s power to resurrect?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:8/q10/p1 The One who first made a human from fluid can recreate that human after death. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, Elmalılı, Quṭb, Iṣlāḥī and Kur'an Yolu point to the first formation; al-Biqāʿī and Bursawī say recreation is even easier, while al-Qushayrī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say power over a thing entails power over its like. Al-Malawī describes fluid from spinal and breast regions reaching the testes and developing every forty days into a reasoning, speaking builder. Elmalılı cites the nine stages in 23:12–16 and says these verses begin with the immediately evident spurting fluid. Quṭb adds God's determination and governance. Study Quran cites 19:67, 22:5, 35:11 and 30:27, the last calling recreation easiest for Him. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الملوي); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 86:8/q11 How can the resurrected person be the very same person?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether personal identity depends on bodily matter or the preserved soul.
- 86:8/q11/p1 The preserved soul makes personal identity continuous when it joins a recreated body. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says identity resides in the soul, not bodily material. He notes the Angel of Death takes people (32:11), God takes souls in death and sleep (39:42), and angelic preservation can include writing deeds (45:29). Bodily similarity therefore poses no obstacle. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 86:8/q11/p2 Recreation cannot yield the identical person, because the second body is merely similar. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this objection to the argument from absolute divine power and rejects it by appealing to the preserved soul. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 86:8/q12 How does this verse connect the sūra’s creation and oversight themes to Judgment?  [* · links]
- 86:8/q12/p1 Human creation and preservation establish resurrection, exposure of secrets and recompense. — Al-Jishumī links divine power to hidden secrets being disclosed. Al-Biqāʿī sees the sūra as proclaiming the Qurʾān’s truthful promise of believers’ reward and unbelievers’ punishment; its name al-Ṭāriq signals the oath and its promised matter. He says the creation stages answer Arabs who denied resurrection and urges people to keep the recording angels’ record pleasing to God. Bursawī reports that creation displays power, sustenance generosity, death omnipotence and resurrection reward and punishment. Elmalılı says the condensed creation stages recall preservation and resurrection. Quṭb joins first creation to divine governance; Kur'an Yolu says afterlife deniers are urged to reflect on their own formation. Study Quran notes many pre-Islamic Arabs accepted God as Creator while denying resurrection, citing 18:37; 19:67–73; 22:5–6; 23:13–16; 35:11; 40:67; 76:2; and 80:18–22. [10 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 86:8/q13 How is this return connected with other Qurʾānic descriptions of returning to God?  [* · links]
- 86:8/q13/p1 It is the human return to God through death and resurrection, the last two stages of creation. — Elmalılı says it brings humanity before God and makes Him known, identifies death and resurrection as the eighth and ninth stages in al-Muʾminūn 23:15–16, and connects returning to God with 2:245; 10:56; 11:34; 28:70, 88; 36:22, 83; 41:21; and 43:85. [3 notes: ELMALILI]

## 86:8/q14 What other lexical senses appear in Damghānī’s excerpt?  [* · meaning]
- 86:8/q14/p1 Mashā can mean guidance, passing, or literal walking. — Damghānī lists these senses without further explanation in this excerpt. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
