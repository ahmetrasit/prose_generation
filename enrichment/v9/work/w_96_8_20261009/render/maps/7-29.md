# 7:29: 22 questions

## 7:29/q01 What does al-qist mean here?  [ٱلْقِسْطِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether qist is read as broad equity and balance, its foremost instance of monotheism, an exhaustive label for obedience, or an inward virtue; the root's derivation is also disputed.
- 7:29/q01/p1 Justice, fairness, truth, or the balanced and upright course in all affairs. — Muqatil, Zajjaj, Ibn Abi Hatim, Thalabi, Qushayri, Wahidi, Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabarsi, Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Bursawi, Alusi, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur, Islahi, Kuranyolu and Study Quran give justice/fairness. Zajjaj, Razi, Nasafi call it rationally recognizable good; Baydawi, Bursawi, Alusi, Elmalili and Ibn Ashur explain a mean between excess and deficiency, with Bursawi citing a report and Persian verse. Mawrdi and Abu Hayyan add truth/right. Jishumi relates it to direction toward truth, with 5:42 and 72:15; Tabarsi similarly contrasts inclination to truth with falsehood (60:8, 72:15). Alusi prefers Raghib's allotment derivation: qasata takes another's share unjustly, aqsata gives it fairly, against Tabarsi's inclination etymology; Tabatabai also transmits Raghib. Ibn Ashur gives monotheism, retaliation, beneficence, clothing and food as balanced examples (25:67). Islahi extends balance to belief, morals, worship, economics, politics and society. Kuranyolu includes truth in belief and conduct. [42 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(مجاهد والسدي); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,عطاء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد والسدي وأكثر المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء والسدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,غير واحد); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)+]
- 7:29/q01/p2 The testimony that there is no god but God, or monotheism. — Ibn Abbas supplies the shahada, Dahhak monotheism, and Razi cites 3:18; Abu Hayyan says all good begins with it. Ibn Ashur regards monotheism as a supreme instance of justice rather than its whole scope; Kuranyolu cites 31:13 against shirk. Razi makes the first command encompass knowledge of God's essence, acts, rulings and oneness; Study Quran links 3:18. [16 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس,الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); TABRISI(ابن عباس,الضحاك); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Abbas); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 7:29/q01/p3 A comprehensive name for every praiseworthy act of obedience and devotion. — Abu Muslim calls it all good/obedience; Tabarsi says this is the positive command after indecency is denied; Biqai specifies obligations, established by reason and transmission. Islahi says it underlies every religious directive. [6 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:29/q01/p4 Truthfulness. — Abu Uthman supplies this inward gloss. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان)]

## 7:29/q02 How does the command to justice answer the previous claim?  [أَمَرَ رَبِّى بِٱلْقِسْطِ · links]
- 7:29/q02/p1 God commands justice and good, refuting the claim that He commanded indecency, especially naked circumambulation and inherited pagan customs. — Zajjaj appeals to rational recognition that ugly acts cannot be just. Wahidi cites 7:28; Jishumi says the passage then moves to promise and threat. Razi says justice is good by qualities intrinsic to it, though he refers to his earlier answer on intrinsic good. Ibn Ashur says repeated 'say' emphasizes the denial, and commanding the contrary also implicitly forbids indecency. Tabatabai calls the command the opposite of the prior claim. Durr's Qatada says God neither commands nor approves disobedience. Kuranyolu rejects sanctifying inherited wrongs; Ibn Ashur specifically considers clothing and food. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم والقاضي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:29/q03 What does justice require in practice?  [بِٱلْقِسْطِ · interpretation]
- 7:29/q03/p1 It requires fairness toward God, others, and oneself, moderation in conduct, and worship of God alone. — Qushayri specifies duties, prohibitions and preferring nothing to God; fair dealing with people, sometimes relinquishing one's claim; and disciplining the soul. Ibn Ashur treats monotheism, measured retaliation, beneficence, clothing and food as examples. Islahi extends fairness through religious and public life and says the Creator's rights entail exclusive worship and accountability; absent recompense would be unjust. Kuranyolu rejects deifying ancestors and serving misguided leaders. Tabatabai rejects idolatry and blind imitation. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]

## 7:29/q04 How is the imperative 'establish' joined syntactically to 'my Lord commanded'?  [أَمَرَ رَبِّى بِٱلْقِسْطِ وَأَقِيمُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Which unspoken command or reporting verb licenses an imperative after the statement 'my Lord commanded justice'.
- 7:29/q04/p1 It coordinates with the implied imperative 'be just' or with an expanded verbal noun meaning 'that you be just.' — Ibn Atiyya, Samīn and Tabatabai derive 'be just' from the command to justice. Samīn prefers this, explaining that an can unpack a verbal noun with past, present or imperative (the verse about wearing a cloak and pleasing the eye illustrates present), whereas ma cannot join an imperative and kay takes only present. Abu Hayyan supplies an plus the imperative and rejects a merely nominal masdar particle. Qurtubi notes the ellipsis. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 7:29/q04/p2 An omitted 'say' introduces the imperative, either repeated independently or coordinated with the first 'say.' — Jishumi, Tabarsi, Razi, Abu Hayyan/Samīn on Zamakhshari, and Bursawi supply 'and say'; Bursawi avoids directly coordinating imperative and indicative. Wahidi's Jurjani says the Lord's command is speech, so the sense is 'say: God says, be just and establish.' Samīn also records attaching the imperative to the reported 'my Lord commanded' within the governing 'say.' [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الجرجاني); JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الجرجاني); BURSEVI]
- 7:29/q04/p3 An omitted 'and [He commanded] that you establish' links it to the previous command. — Jishumi supplies the conjunction and verbal noun. Alusi records this among several possible attachments. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:29/q04/p4 It coordinates with another implied imperative, such as 'turn toward God' or 'beware Satan.' — Abu Muslim connects to the preceding warning against Satan; Abu Hayyan and Samīn give an omitted 'turn' or 'approach' command. Alusi lists this proposal too. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:29/q05 What does 'establish your faces' direct worshippers to do?  [وَأَقِيمُوا۟ وُجُوهَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The force of 'face' and 'mosque': bodily qibla, wholehearted orientation, the mosque reached, congregation, universal prayer place, Meccan sanctuary, or inward station. Razi and Tabatabai give explicit textual and historical objections.
- 7:29/q05/p1 Face the Kaaba or divinely prescribed qibla in prayer wherever prayer occurs. — Mujahid explicitly includes church and other places. Muqatil includes mosque, church and synagogue; Ibn Atiyya calls qibla adherence required. Tabarsi's Jubbai glosses the prescribed direction and prayer times; Alusi says many call it obligatory. Razi prefers this because sincerity is expressed separately and rejects the duplicate sincerity reading. Tabatabai calls qibla reports an application and links 2:144; he rejects making it the primary Meccan sense because the Kaaba became qibla in Medina, though allows this verse's general command later specified. Study Quran cites 2:144 and 2:150. [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد والسدي وابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد,السدي,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); TABRISI(الجبائي وغيره,مجاهد والسدي وابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد والسدي وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والسدي وابن زيد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,زرارة وحمران ومحمد بن مسلم); STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI(المفسرون)-]
- 7:29/q05/p2 Turn wholly and uprightly toward God in worship, intention, and prayer, without other objects of devotion. — Al-Rabi treats 'face' as aim rather than organ; Baydawi, Nasafi, Zamakhshari, Alusi and Tabatabai stress undivided attention. Ibn Ashur compares facing an important sight without looking aside and explains 'establish' as firm action (2:3, 30:30); worship is reverent (hadith forbidding spitting before oneself). Abu al-Aliya combines exclusive devotion with facing the Sacred House. Ibn Kathir adds following messengers' law and freedom from shirk as the two conditions of acceptance. Elmalili calls for uniform communal orientation. Study Quran gives parallels at 2:112, 3:20, 6:79, 10:105, 30:30, 30:43 and 31:22. Tabatabai prefers wholehearted ritual orientation, but calls a mere sincerity gloss incomplete since the next clause repeats it. [32 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); THALABI(الربيع); MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع); TABRISI(الربيع); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الربيع بن أنس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الربيع,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI(المفسرون)-]
- 7:29/q05/p3 Pray at the mosque where the time of prayer catches you rather than wait for your own mosque. — Farra and Ibn Qutayba take the encountered mosque as sufficient; Dahhak says use any mosque if none is nearby. Ibn Jawzi says Ibn Qutayba prefers this; Tabarsi says Maghribi prefers it, and Alusi records Maghribi treating it as recommended but finds it remote. Razi objects that the wording requires orientation at every mosque, not a ban on leaving one for another. Tabatabai objects that multiple Muslim mosques do not fit the Meccan setting. [16 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والضحاك); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك وابن قتيبة); BURSEVI(الكلبي); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL(المغربي)+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة)+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI(المفسرون)-]
- 7:29/q05/p4 Attend a mosque for every prayer and pray with the congregation. — Mawardi and Tabarsi say most regard congregation as recommended, a minority obligatory; Alusi repeats the split. Abu Hayyan reports Tabrizi's narrower neighborhood congregation rule. Bursawi's Haddadi says obligatory, citing a hadith exempting those with an excuse; Bursawi gives congregation 27 degrees over solitary prayer and connects this to 17 obligatory plus 10 regular daily rakahs. Bursawi's Furūʿ favors the neighborhood mosque when its imam is learned and gives market workers day/night practice. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي,الماوردي); BURSEVI(الحدادي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI(المفسرون)]
- 7:29/q05/p5 Pray wherever one is; the whole earth can serve as a mosque. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan report this reading, and Mawrdi adds no delay until reaching a built mosque. Biqai extends orientation to every suitable place, time and circumstance. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:29/q05/p6 The command first addresses exclusive worship at pilgrimage sanctuaries, rejecting idols and naked ritual. — Ibn Ashur says the Meccan audience then had pilgrimage sanctuaries rather than later mosques; Hubal was on the Kaaba, Isaf and Naila at Safa and Marwa, and Manat at al-Mushallal. Believers are to continue observing the command. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:29/q05/p7 An inward discipline of uprightness at each spiritual station of prostration. — Junayd calls for guarding the secret, raising aspiration and contentment in God. Qashani restrains excess, deficiency and spiritual fluctuation; his four stations are obedience, annihilation in actions, annihilation in attributes and annihilation in essence, each with conditions and dangers. [6 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); QASHANI]
- 7:29/q05/p8 A report takes the established faces to be the Imams, which Tabatabai understands as congregational prayer leaders. — Al-Sadiq supplies the report; Tabatabai notes another possible sense follows. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 7:29/q06 What can 'face' and 'at every mosque' denote?  [وُجُوهَكُمْ عِندَ كُلِّ مَسْجِدٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether masjid functions as a place noun, time noun, or metonym for prayer or worship; whether wajh is bodily direction or whole-person attention.
- 7:29/q06/p1 Face can mean one's aim, attention, or whole being, beyond the physical organ. — Ibn Atiyya explains al-Rabi's aim rather than organ; Asad cites 3:20, and Ibn Ashur pictures full attention to an important sight. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الربيع); ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:29/q06/p2 Masjid denotes a place of prostration or a mosque, including any designated place of worship. — Farra and Tabarsi take the place; Ibn Ashur distinguishes general places set aside for worship from the later specific bounded Islamic prayer building, citing 5:2. Samīn notes the irregular kasra pattern where fatḥa is expected, with many analogues. Alusi says ʿinda can mean 'in' and sajda names prayer by a part. [10 notes: FARRA; TABRISI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:29/q06/p3 Masjid denotes the time of prostration or prayer, each prayer occasion. — Zajjaj and Wahidi's Abu Ishaq say each prayer time; Razi prefers the time-based sense over the place-based one; Jubbai, Zamakhshari, Samīn, Alusi, Elmalili and Bursawi allow it. Sujud may name the whole prayer, a part for the whole. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI(الجبائي وغيره); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 7:29/q06/p4 Here masjid can extend to every act of worship, not merely a time or place. — Asad compares 7:31; Qashani makes stations of prostration inward stages. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; QASHANI]

## 7:29/q07 What occasion or practical ruling is associated with praying at each mosque?  [عِندَ كُلِّ مَسْجِدٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether the wording imposes attendance or direction, and whether congregational prayer is recommended or obligatory.
- 7:29/q07/p1 People once refused other mosques in favor of their own; the verse tells them to pray where the time finds them. — Ibn Atiyya reports the occasion as preference for their own mosque and qibla. Farra, Dahhak, Ibn Qutayba and others urge the available mosque. Razi denies that the verse actually prohibits moving between mosques; Tabatabai says this presupposes multiple Muslim mosques absent in Mecca. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(الضحاك); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والضحاك); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك وابن قتيبة); BURSEVI(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI(المفسرون)-]
- 7:29/q07/p2 Congregational mosque prayer is recommended according to most, obligatory according to a minority. — Mawrdi, Tabarsi and Alusi give this split. Haddadi argues obligation with an exemption for those with an excuse who hear the call. Bursawi says mosque congregation better displays Islam's symbols for obligatory prayer and tarawih; home congregation is better than praying alone at home. The nearby mosque can be preferred when its imam is learned. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماوردي); BURSEVI(الحدادي,العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 7:29/q08 What does 'call upon Him' mean here?  [وَٱدْعُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether duʿa names worship or formal prayer broadly, literal petition, or confession of oneness.
- 7:29/q08/p1 Worship God, including ritual prayer and remembrance. — Thalabi, Baghawi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursawi, Ibn Ashur and others give worship. Razi prefers prayer because salat literally means supplication and its noblest acts are petition and remembrance; Bursawi says a particular act names the broader worship, whose gateway is humble need. Ibn Ashur cites 7:194, and Alusi says worship includes prayer. [16 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(commentators); RAZI-FULL+]
- 7:29/q08/p2 Supplicate God, asking Him alone after purifying one's religion. — Mawrdi, Ibn Jawzi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi report petition; Tabarsi holds humble petition after sincere religion; Study Quran says petitions commonly concern spiritual and material needs and Abu Hayyan adds exclusive devotion as a condition of response. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:29/q08/p3 Say the testimony of God's oneness and invoke no other deity. — Muqatil joins prayer and monotheism; Wahidi and Qurtubi take it as affirmation without partners. Abu Hayyan lists the shahada as a possible meaning. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية)]

## 7:29/q09 What is meant by sincerity in religion to Him?  [مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ ٱلدِّينَ · interpretation]
- 7:29/q09/p1 Devote worship and obedience purely to God, without idols, partners, hypocrisy, or ostentation. — Zajjaj and Baghawi gloss din as obedience/worship; Baydawi, Bursawi and Ibn Ashur stress obedience because all return to God. Tabarsi derives ikhlas from removing admixture; Ibn Ashur calls din obedience and explains the divine name al-Dayyan. Razi says sincerity covers both preceding commands because din embraces every devotion, comparing 98:5; Ibn Ashur likewise uses 98:5 to eliminate association. Nasafi says act seeking God's face; Qashani's first station avoids hypocrisy and his later stations see obedience as from and through God. Study Quran cites recurring formula at 10:22, 29:65, 31:32, 39:2, 39:11, 39:14, 40:14, 40:65, 73:8. Islahi says worship and obedience belong to God alone (72:18). [32 notes: TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); THALABI(الربيع); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:29/q10 How is 'sincere to Him in religion' construed grammatically?  [مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ ٱلدِّينَ · grammar]
- 7:29/q10/p1 Mukhlisin is a circumstantial accusative modifying the callers, and al-din is its object. — Ibn Atiyya and Samīn give the construction; Samīn allows lahu either to attach to mukhlisin or modify al-din circumstantially. Ibn Ashur links the circumstantial phrase to the pronoun in 'call/worship Him.' [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:29/q11 What inward states make invocation sincere?  [وَٱدْعُوهُ مُخْلِصِينَ · inward]
- 7:29/q11/p1 Conceal self-regard and ostentation, watch God continuously, and let all dealings be with Him alone. — Tustari advises private examination because only the sincere recognize showing off; outward conduct follows the Prophet, otherwise it is astray. Ruwaym ceases to see his own acts; Ibn Ata requires invocation free of affliction and defect; Muhasibi removes creatures from dealings with God; Abu Uthman forgets creatures while seeing the Creator; another definition joins vigilant watchfulness with forgetting self-interest. Qushayri extends God's presence to every act and omission. Qashani develops four stations, from dedicating action to God to seeing religion through God. [10 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان,ابن عطاء,بعضهم,حارث المحاسبي,رويم); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]

## 7:29/q12 What do 'began' and 'return' mean as words?  [بَدَأَكُمْ تَعُودُونَ · meaning]
- 7:29/q12/p1 To begin is to originate or perform first; to return is to perform again or restore. — Jishumi and Tabarsi distinguish first and second performance, with Jishumi calling God initiator/restorer and qualifying what can be restored. Abu Hayyan glosses badaʾa as originate/invent; Samīn says triliteral badaʾa and quadriliteral abdaʾa both occur (29:19–20), and with baʾ it can mean place first; Raghib records a rare abdaʾtu form, while abdaʾtu min ard means start departing. Samīn records badʾ as a chief counted first, with two anonymous poetic examples, one leader after his people died and one master not previously made master. Burhan says the triliteral is more eloquent but the paired quadriliteral yubdiʾ/yuʿid creates correspondence; beginning then return follows temporal order among Quranic pairs. [13 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الراغب); BURHAN]

## 7:29/q13 How is the comparison 'as He began you, you return' parsed?  [كَمَا بَدَأَكُمْ تَعُودُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical governor of kama, what comparison noun is omitted, and whether it attaches to the present command or the earlier emergence from earth.
- 7:29/q13/p1 The comparative kaf is accusative with 'you return,' comparing a second return or creation to the first. — Nahhas and Qurtubi give accusative comparison; Ibn al-Anbari treats kama as the fronted object of taʿudun. Samīn prefers an omitted verbal noun meaning 'a return like His beginning you' to an estimated 'emergence'; Ibn Ashur takes ma as infinitival, the fronting emphasizing the likeness; Bursawi also supplies an omitted verbal noun. Samīn parses ma as masdariyya. [9 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأنباري); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 7:29/q13/p2 An omitted noun 'creation' makes the comparison: He recreates creation as He first created it. — Abu Ali al-Farisi supplies khalq in both clauses, comparing 21:104, and rejects a literal comparison of people with beginning; Samīn prefers this analysis in its particular formulation. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); SAMIN-DURR(الفارسي)+]
- 7:29/q13/p3 The omitted verbal noun may be 'an emergence like His beginning you.' — Samīn reports Makki's alternative, but prefers 'a return' because it fits the explicit verb better. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(مكي); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 7:29/q15 Does the two-group return entail fixed and inescapable fate?  [كَمَا بَدَأَكُمْ تَعُودُونَ · theology]

Alusi transmits a Tirmidhi report from ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAs: two registers for Paradise and Hell are shown; companions are told to strive because final deeds decide the outcome. Qushayri describes matched original disposition, state and end. ## 7:29/q20 What other language or comparisons are adduced near this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited material ranges from comparison theory and grammatical shifts to lexical notes on neighboring text.
- 7:29/q20/p1 The comparison particle can express an intellectual likeness rather than bodily or sensory resemblance. — Jurjani's Prophetic 'green growth on dung' compares attractive beauty from bad upbringing with outwardly appealing growth rooted in corruption, not plant color, taste, smell or form. Honey's sweetness and bitterness analogize joy at agreement and distress at opposition. Calling a man 'sun' transfers eminence, rank, honor and fame; 'stars of guidance' likens Companions' teaching and example to celestial navigation. These are general analogies adduced in discussion of comparison. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(النبي)]
- 7:29/q20/p2 The verse illustrates a shift from past statement to imperative. — Burhan pairs 'my Lord commanded justice' with 'establish your faces' and cites a command after the permissibility of cattle as another example. [1 notes: BURHAN]
