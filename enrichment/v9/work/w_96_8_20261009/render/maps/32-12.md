# 32:12: 29 questions

## 32:12/q01 How does لو ترى frame the sight of the criminals?  [وَلَوْ تَرَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether لو is conditional or carries a wish, and whether a result must be supplied.
- 32:12/q01/p1 It is a conditional with an unspoken result. — Al-Zamakhshari calls it counterfactual; al-Samin reports the dispute with al-Shaykh. Abu Hayyan's poem illustrates conditional لو with an expressed answer. Al-Samin notes an analysis that the answer is implied by context; al-Burhan says omitted answers to لو and لولا are common. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN; JISHUMI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 32:12/q01/p2 It expresses a wish that the Prophet could see his enemies humbled. — Al-Zamakhshari makes this consolation for the harm they caused him. Al-Baydawi and al-Burhan also give the optative analysis. Abu Hayyan and Ibn Malik object that لو is not inherently optative and that even its wish use needs an answer; Ibn Malik supplies an omitted وددت. Al-Samin cites the poem about Kulayb; al-Alusi says its لو is conditional, while al-Khafaji allows a wish construction by usage. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مالك)-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن مالك,الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان وابن مالك)-]
- 32:12/q01/p3 Its condition and result are implicit in the surrounding wording. — Jishumi reports this further analysis. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 32:12/q02 Who is addressed by ترى?  [تَرَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether singular address singles out Muhammad, includes all viewers, or belongs to the earlier command to speak.
- 32:12/q02/p1 The address is general to anyone able to see. — Al-Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq take the singular Prophet address as including the community, citing 65:1. Al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, Abu Hayyan and al-Burhan compare an impersonal example about repaying kindness with harm. Al-Biqaʿi notes a possible shift from the criminals to the Prophet, but allows any observer. Al-Alusi says universal astonishment or the manifest nature of the scene motivates this usage; al-Itqan says its obviousness includes every viewer. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 32:12/q02/p2 The Prophet is addressed specifically. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Razi take the view as consolation after deniers hurt him. Al-Biqaʿi says the shift signals contempt for them. Islahi says he would see helplessness beyond what he now imagines. Study Quran reports this address while noting the shared effect of amazement. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 32:12/q02/p3 Muhammad is told to say the warning to a criminal. — Abu al-Abbas connects the sentence to the earlier قل and says its listener would regret past conduct; Abu Hayyan explains this link to يتوفاكم in 32:11. [3 notes: NAHHAS(أبو العباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العباس); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العباس)]

## 32:12/q03 What result is left unsaid after 'if you saw'?  [وَلَوْ تَرَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
- 32:12/q03/p1 One would see an astonishing, dreadful sight and take the utmost lesson. — Al-Zajjaj implies a lesson; al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, al-Burhan, al-Itqan and Bursawi supply wonder or dread. Al-Zamakhshari offers the worst imaginable state. Ibn ʿAtiyya and Jishumi say omission expands imagination and heightens terror. Al-Biqaʿi says the horror exceeds description; Islahi says the Prophet could not imagine the degree of helplessness now. [21 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; ZAJJAJ; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج)]

## 32:12/q04 What does ترى govern here?  [تَرَىٰٓ إِذِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether seeing takes an object recovered from what follows.
- 32:12/q04/p1 It needs no expressed object and governs the time إذ. — Al-Tabrisi compares 76:20; al-Nasafi calls إذ an adverb of ترى. Al-Alusi prefers intransitive ترى because it fits astonishment of any viewer. [4 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 32:12/q04/p2 Its object is omitted or supplied by the following scene. — Al-Tabrisi supplies 'the criminals when they lower their heads'; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi supply the criminals or their bowing from what follows إذ. [3 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 32:12/q05 How is the opening clause parsed?  [وَلَوْ تَرَىٰٓ · grammar]
- 32:12/q05/p1 It forms a nominal construction with a supplied predicate. — Al-Nahhas calls it a subject and predicate; al-Qurtubi says the predicate is supplied by the following construction. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 32:12/q06 Why does إذ introduce a future scene?  [إِذِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether إذ is figurative past usage for a certain future or replaces إذا.
- 32:12/q06/p1 The future event is expressed as past because its occurrence is certain. — Al-Wahidi compares 6:27; al-Zamakhshari says what God guarantees is certain. Al-Samin says لو shifts the present into past and cites أتى أمر الله (16:1); al-Biqaʿi explains the imperfect after a past-associated particle as inviting anticipation. Al-Alusi grounds the past idiom in God's eternal knowledge. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكواشي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:12/q06/p2 إذ stands in place of إذا. — Abu al-Baqa proposes substitution; al-Samin says it is unnecessary given the other account. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 32:12/q07 Who are المجرمون?  [ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite noun picks up 32:10, denotes all criminals, or specifies a subgroup.
- 32:12/q07/p1 They are the deniers of resurrection mentioned earlier. — Ibn ʿAtiyya cites the Hell threat and their later certainty; al-Nasafi and Bursawi identify those questioning recreation after dissolution in 32:10. Tabatabai appeals to the definite article and the verse's conclusion. Kuranyolu says context in 32:10–11 narrows the literal 'criminals/sinners' to unbelievers. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 32:12/q07/p2 They are the polytheists or unbelievers. — Yahya, al-Baghawi and Ibn Kathir call them polytheists; Ibn ʿAtiyya says unbelievers because of the threat of Hell. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 32:12/q07/p3 They are specifically the Meccan unbelievers. — Muqatil identifies them; al-Wahidi transmits him. [2 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 32:12/q07/p4 They are the whole class of criminals. — Al-Alusi gives the broader alternative to the previously mentioned deniers. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:12/q07/p5 They are people who sever what God commanded to be joined. — Al-Biqaʿi gives this characterization. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 32:12/q08 What does ناكسوا رؤوسهم describe?  [نَاكِسُوا۟ رُءُوسِهِمْ · interpretation]
- 32:12/q08/p1 They lower their heads in shame, regret, grief, humiliation and distress. — Yahya, al-Tabari, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Mawardi's transmitters, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqaʿi, Bursawi and al-Alusi give these shades. Al-Wahidi says a regretful person looks down in confused thought; al-Razi compares a servant facing the lord he wronged. Tabatabai gives the physical sense. Jishumi, al-Tabrisi and Bursawi explain naks as turning upside down; illness relapse has ḍamma, a reversed arrow kasra. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة,ابن عيسى,النقاش,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 32:12/q09 How does ناكسوا رؤوسهم work as a participial construction?  [نَاكِسُوا۟ رُءُوسِهِمْ · grammar]
- 32:12/q09/p1 The ongoing active participle loses its plural nun in annexation to its object without becoming definite. — Sibawayh says an ongoing participle governs like a present verb; loss of tanwin or plural nun eases pronunciation, putting the object in genitive. He cites this verse, other Qur'anic constructions (3:185, 54:27, 5:1–2, 5:95, 46:24), and poets including Zuhayr, al-Farazdaq, al-Zibriqan, al-Sulayk, al-Nabigha and al-Marrar. Al-Khalil's هو كائن أخيك illustrates the light form. Sibawayh distinguishes completed action and discusses genitive or accusative coordination, elision, long separation (6:96), and two-object order (14:47); Isa's Abu al-Aswad variant is poetic necessity. Al-Wahidi compares ثاني عطفه (22:9) and هديا بالغ الكعبة (5:95), referring to 4:97. [17 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]

## 32:12/q10 How is the head-bowing phrase read?  [نَاكِسُوا۟ رُءُوسِهِمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the first word is a participle or past verb.
- 32:12/q10/p1 The common reading has an active participle annexed to its object. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin contrast it with Zayd's verb reading. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 32:12/q10/p2 Zayd ibn Ali reads نكسوا رؤوسهم as a past verb and direct object. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin transmit it. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي)]

## 32:12/q11 What does عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ mean in the scene?  [عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ · interpretation]
- 32:12/q11/p1 They stand before God for reckoning and recompense. — Al-Mawardi, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi and al-Alusi specify judgment. Abu Hayyan says the wrongdoer feels acute shame before the one who will recompense him; al-Razi uses a servant who wronged his lord. Al-Biqaʿi describes the Lord as unique benefactor and governor. Tabatabai links the encounter to the meeting denied in 32:10. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 32:12/q12 How is their cry 'رَبَّنَا' introduced grammatically?  [رَبَّنَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether to supply a verb of saying, construe the utterance directly as a circumstance, or supply an appeal.
- 32:12/q12/p1 An unspoken 'they say' introduces their words. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjaj, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi and al-Burhan supply a speech verb. Al-Razi says its omission signals shame too intense for speech. Al-Samin calls the utterance circumstantial and says omitted قول is more usual than al-Zamakhshari's proposed appeal. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN]
- 32:12/q12/p2 The quoted plea itself is a circumstantial utterance governed by ناكسوا. — Al-Alusi prefers this account over supplying يستغيثون بقولهم. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 32:12/q12/p3 An unspoken 'they appeal for help by saying' introduces it. — Al-Samin attributes this to al-Zamakhshari and prefers the more common ellipsis of قول; al-Alusi contrasts it with his preferred circumstantial analysis. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 32:12/q13 What do they mean by 'we have seen and heard'?  [أَبْصَرْنَا وَسَمِعْنَا · interpretation]
turns on: What the objects of sight and hearing are, and whether the admission concerns worldly perception or the witnessed afterlife.
- 32:12/q13/p1 They now see the truth they denied and hear the messengers' truth or words. — Al-Tabari specifies punishment and the messengers; Ibn Isa says God's warning and their confirmation. Al-Wahidi says they see what they ignored and hear what they denied. The declaration comes too late to help, as Yahya, Qatada, al-Qurtubi and al-Durr stress. Al-Alusi describes seeing resurrection and hearing the messengers obediently; Ibn Kathir cites 19:38. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 32:12/q13/p2 They see God's promise or threat fulfilled and hear God confirm His messengers. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi give this pairing; al-Biqaʿi adds hearing God, angels and Fire. Al-Alusi instead specifies God's question to humans and jinn about messengers (6:130). [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:12/q13/p3 They see their sins and hear what is said about them. — Qatada says this comes when perception cannot benefit them. Al-Alusi gives a variant: they see the ugliness of deeds once thought good and hear angels say they return to the Fire. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:12/q13/p4 They were like blind and deaf people, but now truly see and hear. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Tabrisi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursawi and al-Alusi describe newly effective faculties or recognition of visible and audible signs; al-Irshad adds their claim that sound hearts can now understand and act. Al-Qurtubi says they perceived in life but failed to reflect. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 32:12/q13/p5 They confess that they had seen messengers and heard them in worldly life. — Al-Qurtubi says they also saw God's marvels, leaving no excuse; Abu Hayyan repeats this reading. Al-Alusi reports the messengers version. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 32:12/q13/p6 They confess to seeing creative signs and hearing revealed signs in worldly life. — Al-Alusi reports the proof-against-them reading and calls it weak. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 32:12/q13/p7 They see guidance and hear truth. — Al-Tabrisi gives this broad explanation; Tabatabai distinguishes direct vision from obedient hearing. [2 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI]
- 32:12/q13/p8 They now hear God's words and obey His command. — Ibn Kathir compares 19:38; Tabatabai also calls their hearing obedient. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 32:12/q14 Do أبصرنا and سمعنا require expressed objects?  [أَبْصَرْنَا وَسَمِعْنَا · grammar]
- 32:12/q14/p1 Their objects are omitted, or the verbs mean that they became seeing and hearing. — Al-Samin offers both analyses. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 32:12/q15 Why is sight mentioned before hearing?  [أَبْصَرْنَا وَسَمِعْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order foregrounds direct visual certainty or connects hearing to the promised action.
- 32:12/q15/p1 They heard of resurrection in life but now see it directly and gain certainty. — Al-Samarrai says hearing ordinarily leads because it is vital for instruction, has shorter range and develops earlier; a blind listener can be taught, while deafness impedes instruction. Here direct vision settles what they had heard and doubted. He compares the saying that news heard is unlike seeing oneself; his cave comparison (18:26) likewise illustrates contextual priority for sight. [11 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 32:12/q15/p2 Putting hearing last keeps it next to the promised righteous action that follows hearing. — Al-Alusi says most promised good action comes through hearing, so sight first avoids interrupting that link. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 32:12/q16 Where do they ask to be returned?  [فَٱرْجِعْنَا · interpretation]
- 32:12/q16/p1 They seek return to worldly life, the place of moral responsibility. — Yahya, al-Tabari, al-Mawardi, al-Tabrisi, al-Kashshaf, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, Bursawi, al-Biqaʿi and al-Alusi state this; al-Tabrisi calls it the world of obligation. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 32:12/q17 What good do they promise to do if returned?  [نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promised righteous action is general, specifically monotheistic confession, or faith with obedience.
- 32:12/q17/p1 They promise righteous deeds or obedience in earthly life. — Al-Tabari specifies obeying God; Tabatabai says they now recognize salvation needs faith and righteous deeds, but have lacked deeds. Al-Alusi and Bursawi connect the promised conduct to newly perceived signs. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 32:12/q17/p2 They promise to affirm God's oneness by declaring there is no god but God. — Al-Wahidi makes this the concrete deed sought. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 32:12/q17/p3 They mean faith and obedience. — Al-Nasafi gives this gloss. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 32:12/q18 How is صَٰلِحًا parsed?  [صَٰلِحًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative is an object or modifies an elided cognate noun.
- 32:12/q18/p1 It is a direct object or an adjective qualifying an omitted verbal noun. — Al-Samin records both grammatical possibilities. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 32:12/q19 What are they now certain of?  [إِنَّا مُوقِنُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which truth or condition their declaration of certainty specifies.
- 32:12/q19/p1 They are certain of resurrection and the coming reckoning. — Muqatil and al-Naqqash specify resurrection; al-Nasafi adds reckoning. Al-Wahidi's Ibn Abbas says they are certain of what they had denied; Ibn Kathir speaks of God's promise and meeting. The previous deniers in 32:10 explain the referent. [9 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(النقاش); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI]
- 32:12/q19/p2 They are certain that Muhammad and the messengers brought truth. — Yahya, al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi state this; Abu Hayyan transmits Yahya. Al-Tabrisi says there is no remaining doubt about any truth or the message. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); TABRISI]
- 32:12/q19/p3 They know God's oneness, exclusive worship, power over life and death, and resurrection. — Al-Tabari enumerates these objects of certainty. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 32:12/q19/p4 Their doubts have vanished, so they are now certain. — Al-Baydawi attributes removal of doubt to witnessing; al-Qurtubi says they had sensory evidence before but failed to reflect. Abu Hayyan mentions confusion on finally seeing and hearing. Bursawi and al-Alusi stress sound hearts and capacity to understand signs; al-Alusi allows إنا موقنون to explain the plea or resume without causal force. [11 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 32:12/q20 Is their declaration of certainty sincere?  [إِنَّا مُوقِنُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their words report genuine late knowledge or a strategic claim.
- 32:12/q20/p1 They have gained real certainty, but it comes too late to benefit them. — Ibn ʿAtiyya says their certainty shows they lacked it in life; al-Razi says faith and works count in earthly responsibility. Islahi says belief after seeing would defeat the purpose of intellect and moral awareness; Kuranyolu says the worldly trial has ended. Qatada's too-late perception supports this. Study Quran reports this as one reading. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 32:12/q20/p2 They only claim belief to escape punishment. — Al-Razi compares their denial of association in 6:23; Study Quran makes the same comparison. Al-Razi adds an alternative claim that they were punished for omitted good works rather than disbelief. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 32:12/q21 Why do they say 'we are certain' instead of adding 'we became certain' to the earlier verbs?  [إِنَّا مُوقِنُونَ · rhetoric]
- 32:12/q21/p1 The emphatic independent assertion shows firmness and eagerness for certainty. — Al-Alusi gives this as a possible reason for omitting وأيقنا after أبصرنا وسمعنا. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 32:12/q22 Why are their requests to return refused?  [فَٱرْجِعْنَا إِنَّا مُوقِنُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the answer stresses their predicted relapse, the ended trial, free choice, or previous warning.
- 32:12/q22/p1 If returned, they would resume forbidden acts and disbelief. — Sufyan cites 6:28; Ibn Kathir cites 6:27–29. Abu Hurayra's report says God's prior knowledge showed they would return to worse wrongdoing without repentance. Study Quran cites 6:28 alongside 35:37 and 23:100. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(سفيان); QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان الثوري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); STUDYQURAN]
- 32:12/q22/p2 Faith and righteous deeds benefit a person during earthly trial, before direct sight. — Al-Razi says afterlife faith and action are not accepted; al-Biqaʿi says valid faith precedes direct sight. Islahi says otherwise intellect and moral awareness would lose their purpose. Kuranyolu urges use of the opportunity before death, and Bursawi's Persian verses urge repentance while soul and tongue remain. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 32:12/q22/p3 A return would make guidance compulsory, whereas God gave them choice. — Al-Zamakhshari says they chose blindness over guidance, so punishment fell on those who chose blindness. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 32:12/q22/p4 God answers that their time and warning were already given and their plea is mere words. — Study Quran cites 35:37 and 23:100, plus 6:28, 40:11–12, for the refusals. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 32:12/q23 What does the verse imply about God's judgment and human acts?  [* · theology]
turns on: Which human acts and responsibility are relevant to recompense.
- 32:12/q23/p1 God judges His servants for their own acts, which refutes determinism. — Jishumi argues that judgment would be meaningless if God created every human act. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 32:12/q23/p2 Reward and punishment encompass acts of the heart as well as outward acts. — Jishumi states both sorts of acts deserve recompense. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 32:12/q23/p3 Only those who deserve it enter Hell, while every good deed is rewarded. — Abu Hurayra reports God's appointment of Adam at the scales: good exceeding evil even by an atom brings Paradise. Kuranyolu says many verses and hadith reject punishment for uncommitted sins and recognize reward for every good deed. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 32:12/q24 Whom does God forgive or punish?  [* · theology]
turns on: What further grounds, if any, identify those forgiven.
- 32:12/q24/p1 God forgives and punishes whom He wills; this passage leaves their identity unspecified. — Jishumi says other texts clarify whom. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 32:12/q24/p2 Repentance and divine mercy can lead to forgiveness. — Kuranyolu mentions valid repentance and similar means; Kaʿb reports that angels, prophets, martyrs and believers intercede, then Mercy intercedes for anyone who once remembered, feared, hoped in, or prayed to God, and such people leave the Fire. [2 notes: THALABI(كعب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 32:12/q25 What does the report of God's explanation to Adam say about punishment?  [* · reports]
- 32:12/q25/p1 God's decree against lying and His prior knowledge govern whom He punishes; Adam's scales show only wrongdoers enter Hell. — Abu Hurayra reports three divine explanations: without the decree God would have shown mercy despite prepared severity; 7:18 speaks of filling Hell with deniers and disobedient people; God punishes those He knows would return to worse conduct without repentance; an atom's excess of good leads to Paradise. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 32:12/q26 What inward reading is given to the criminals' bowed state?  [نَاكِسُوا۟ رُءُوسِهِمْ · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward state is an enduring bodily disposition or one eventually corrected.
- 32:12/q26/p1 Worldly people lowered their heads toward bodily desire after the primordial covenant. — Al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya says Satan beautified appetite; 7:179 compares them to cattle, with humans worse because they also transgress commands. They die and rise bowed, bewildered and ashamed, confessing too late. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 32:12/q26/p2 Some retain primordial light and eventually leave Hell after correction. — Al-Qashani says entrenched bodily dispositions veil them and pull them downward, but remaining light allows sight, hearing and desire for return; correction lasts according to how deeply dispositions are rooted. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 32:12/q27 How does this scene connect to the surrounding account of resurrection?  [* · links]
- 32:12/q27/p1 It shows deniers confronting the meeting and truth they rejected. — Al-Tabari links shame to past disobedience and 32:10. Al-Nasafi and Tabatabai identify 32:10's deniers; Tabatabai notes their encounter with the Lord echoes the denied meeting. Ibn Kathir compares their hearing with 19:38 and later self-reproach with 67:10. Study Quran compares confession after death in 19:38, 67:11 and 40:11. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 32:12/q27/p2 It belongs to the sūra's first-half movement through revelation, creation, resurrection and judgment. — Study Quran says Sūrat al-Sajdah's first half spans 32:2–14, including this scene. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 32:12/q28 Where was Sūrat al-Sajdah revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 32:12/q28/p1 It is Makkan. — Study Quran states this. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 32:12/q29 How is the ensuing rebuke about tasting punishment parsed?  [* · grammar]
- 32:12/q29/p1 The command to taste is spoken to them, and the phrase about forgetting is a genitive description of their day. — Al-Tabrisi gives this parsing of the response after their plea. [1 notes: TABRISI]
