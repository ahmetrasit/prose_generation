# 89:28: 20 questions

## 89:28/q01 Where is the soul told to return?  [ٱرْجِعِىٓ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكِ · interpretation]
turns on: The readings differ over whether 'return' addresses a departing spirit, a spirit rejoining its body, an honored arrival at God's reward, or a spiritual turning.
- 89:28/q01/p1 To its body at resurrection. — Ibn Abbas, Ikrima, al-Dahhak, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and others explain the return as re-entry into the body. Al-Tabari prefers it because 89:29–30 immediately command entry among the servants and into the Garden. Ibn Abbas's singular 'My servant' and Ibn Masud's 'body of My servant' support it (Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi); Sa'id describes souls returning from a river beneath the Throne. Muhammad ibn Ka'b instead locates bodily return after the believer sees Paradise at death, before Munkar and Nakir. - 89:28/q01/p3 To God at death, through the soul's departure from earthly life. — Abu Salih separates this departure from the later entry commands at resurrection. Al-Tustari describes an afterlife path; Ibn Zayd says the spirit returns to a sacred realm. Al-Farra also allows a return from worldly life. - 89:28/q01/p5 To God inwardly, by turning from worldly attachment or anxiety about deeds. — The people of indication and al-Bursawi call the soul away from settled worldly life. Al-Alusi rejects a further reading that applies all commands throughout earthly life. Al-Razi describes intellect rising through contingent beings and causes to the Necessary Existent. [5 notes: QASHANI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj, K)]
- 89:28/q01/p7 To the righteous human peers of the soul. — Ibn Kaysan glosses 'your Lord' as the soul's peers among God's righteous servants. [1 notes: THALABI(ابن كيسان)]

## 89:28/q02 When is this address made?  [ٱرْجِعِىٓ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes place the command at death, revival, judgment, several of these stages, or in the believer's present life.
- 89:28/q02/p1 At death. — Abu Salih reserves entry among the servants and into Paradise for resurrection; the report to Abu Bakr names an angel at death. Ibn Amr's account of a fragrant soul and a lit grave elaborates the departure. Muhammad ibn Ka'b locates a return to the body after a glimpse of Paradise and before questioning. ## 89:28/q03 How does the imperative 'return' function?  [ٱرْجِعِىٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the imperative is declarative promise, future command, or present exhortation.
- 89:28/q03/p1 It can state an already accomplished return or promised state. — Al-Farra and Kufan grammarians liken it to telling an existing tribesman to 'be Tamimi or Qaysi'; after the soul reaches reward the command reports that it is pleased and accepted. Ibn Ashur treats it as a promise qualified by the following two states. [9 notes: FARRA; TAB(بعض أهل العربية من أهل الكوفة); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية من أهل الكوفة); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:28/q03/p2 It is a future creative divine command supplied by an implied 'God will say'. — Elmalili reports the majority's ellipsis and contrasts it with a plausible direct legislative address whose separation from the preceding account makes the souls themselves hear it. [3 notes: ELMALILI]
- 89:28/q03/p3 It directly exhorts believers to keep striving or turn to God in life. — Ibn Atiyya makes the address a counterpart to the unbelievers; Elmalili commends voluntary return to God's decree in prosperity and hardship. Al-Alusi rejects extending all the commands in this way. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA(بعض العلماء); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ELMALILI; ELMALILI+; ALUSI-]

## 89:28/q04 Why say 'your Lord' in the address?  [رَبِّكِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether rabb identifies God, the soul's human owner, or an intimate mode of divine address.
- 89:28/q04/p1 It conveys intimate care and honor. — Al-Alusi notes its tenderness. Ibn Ashur says if God speaks it replaces a pronoun and honors the soul through the possessive relation; if angels speak, it is ordinary wording and God then takes up their speech. [4 notes: ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:28/q04/p2 It denotes the soul's human owner or body. — Ibn Atiyya cites singular 'My servant' and Ibn Masud's 'in the body of My servant'; Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi report this reading, and Ibn Ashur calls the resulting return literal. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA(بعض العلماء); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد بن حارثة وأبي بن كعب وابن مسعود)]
- 89:28/q04/p3 It denotes God as the destination or source. — Al-Biqa'i names the Creator who brought forth and nurtured the soul; Qutb describes a tender return to its source. [2 notes: BIQAI; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 89:28/q05 How is the rāʾ in 'return' pronounced?  [ٱرْجِعِىٓ · readings]
- 89:28/q05/p1 It remains emphatic. — Ibn al-Jazari in al-Nashr treats the preceding kasra as incidental and applies the same rule to ارجعون and ارجعوا. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 89:28/q06 What does 'pleased' mean here?  [رَاضِيَةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the satisfaction concerns God's Person and decree, bestowed reward, or the completed account.
- 89:28/q06/p1 Pleased with God's gift, reward, or eternal honor. — Muqatil connects it with deeds and God's recompense; Ibn Abbas with the promised reward; Ibn Ashur says the honor satisfies every desire. Al-Alusi prefers endless gifts over relief at a light accounting, which he calls weak. Biqa'i adds acceptance of favorable and bitter decrees in worldly life. - 89:28/q06/p2 Pleased with God and His decree in every circumstance. — Al-Hasan and al-Qushayri express mutual satisfaction; Tustari says contentment is through God. Al-Tabatabai connects trust with accepting decree and law; Islahi contrasts gratitude and patience with pride and despair in 89:15–16. Elmalili calls explicit contentment the soul's perfection; the Study Quran reports it as a foremost virtue and notes divine contentment may precede the soul's. - 89:28/q06/p3 Pleased with the ease and acceptance of its reckoning. — Al-Alusi reports this narrower gloss but considers it less persuasive than satisfaction with endless gifts. [0 notes: ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 89:28/q07 What does 'approved' mean here?  [مَّرْضِيَّةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: The notes vary in whether approval is keyed to deeds, faith, or reciprocal satisfaction.
- 89:28/q07/p1 Approved and pleasing to God for the soul's deeds, faith, or state. — Ibn Abbas relates approval to deeds, Tabarsi also mentions beliefs, and Ibn Ashur says divine approval promises further gifts beyond what already satisfied the soul. Biqa'i adds approval by creation; al-Zajjaj says purified. Elmalili ties approval to a sound heart and striving and says it depends on the soul's contentment. - 89:28/q07/p2 Accepted by God as the counterpart to her contentment with Him. — Al-Qashani says God's contentment comes first, invoking 5:119; Study Quran lists 5:119, 9:100, 58:22 and 98:8. Al-Alusi reports this gloss but questions its contextual fit. [7 notes: TUSTARI; QASHANI; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Aj, K,K)]

## 89:28/q08 How are the two adjectives formed and construed?  [رَاضِيَةًۭ مَّرْضِيَّةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: The notes distinguish circumstantial syntax, participial derivation, and figurative attribution.
- 89:28/q08/p1 Both are accusative circumstantial expressions. — Al-Alusi calls the first apparently prospective, although a simultaneous state is proposed; the second advances to the greater honor of divine approval, citing 9:72. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:28/q08/p2 Marḍiyyah is a passive participle meaning one approved of. — Ibn Ashur explains that deletion of its preposition and verbal connection makes the object a grammatical subject. Al-Zajjaj derives it from older marḍwah. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:28/q08/p3 Rāḍiyah can take a passive sense in an expression of rational attribution. — Suyuti gives 'a pleasing life' in 69:21 as 'a life pleased with', an example of attribution to something related to the actual agent. He says metaphor is more eloquent than literal usage; Zahiris and named jurists deny Qur'anic metaphor as akin to falsehood, an objection he rejects. [6 notes: ITQAN(الجمهور); ITQAN+; ITQAN(الظاهرية وابن القاص وابن خويز منداد)-]

## 89:28/q09 What does this wording imply about the soul's origin and return?  [ٱرْجِعِىٓ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكِ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'return' implies prior existence, literal bodily motion, or spatial direction toward God.
- 89:28/q09/p1 Souls existed before bodies, perhaps in a holy realm. — Ancient thinkers infer pre-existence and even eternity from 'return'; Razi says death-time address supports precedence but cannot establish eternity. Baydawi and Study Quran report the holy realm or Garden before creation. Al-Bursawi says the figurative return indicates prior creation; al-Alusi reports a realm of dominion. [10 notes: RAZI(من القدماء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI(من قال); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 89:28/q09/p2 Souls arise when bodies are ready; 'return' does not prove eternity. — Razi distinguishes mere precedence from eternity and allows a resurrection return to reward and body. Al-Alusi says most theologians, along with Plato and followers in his account, hold that creation coincides with bodily readiness. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI]
- 89:28/q09/p3 The immaterial soul's motion describes taking up and relinquishing bodily governance; a subtle body moves literally. — Razi states the two alternatives; Ibn Ashur likewise says the return is literal or metaphorical depending on the adopted interpretation. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:28/q09/p4 The phrase places God in a spatial direction. — Razi reports the corporealists' appeal to directional 'to' and rejects the inference by glossing it as judgment, reward, or grace. Al-Bursawi likewise invokes honor and nearness against the corporealists. [1 notes: RAZI(المجسمة); RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-; BURSEVI-]
- 89:28/q09/p5 The endpoint is God's judgment, grace, reward, or intellectual ultimacy rather than a spatial location. — Razi answers the corporealists with these nonspatial meanings and describes intellectual ascent through causes to the Necessary Existent; al-Bursawi describes promised honor and nearness. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI+; RAZI(المجسمة)-]

## 89:28/q10 Who speaks this address?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the divine address is voiced directly or through angels.
- 89:28/q10/p1 God addresses the soul. — Ibn Ashur says God may speak from the sacred presence; if so, 'your Lord' adds closeness and honor. Elmalili reports an implied future divine 'He will say' and also considers a direct present address. [8 notes: TAB(الضحاك); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:28/q10/p2 An angel or angels deliver the address. — Reports about Abu Bakr name an angel at death. Al-Farra places angels by recipients of books in their right hands; Ibn Ashur says 'your Lord' suits angelic speech and the next divine first-person command confirms it. ## 89:28/q12 What was reported at Ibn Abbas's burial?  [* · reports]
- 89:28/q12/p1 A mysterious bird entered his bier, and the verses were recited at his grave by an unseen voice. — Reports vary between bier and coffin and locate his death or funeral at Taif; the bird was not seen leaving. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); DURR(سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 89:28/q13 What happens to believers' souls at death according to the reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports depict admission to divine honor or a bodily return before grave questioning.
- 89:28/q13/p1 They are welcomed and promised Paradise; their graves are spacious and illuminated. — Ibn Amr describes a fragrant soul passing opened heavenly gates and angels' welcome, then a grave bright as sunlight or Quran; by contrast the unbeliever leaves foul and faces wrath and Hell. Other reports place the glad tidings at death, resurrection and gathering. [9 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); BAGHAWI(عبد الله ابن عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); DURR(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNASHUR(زيد بن حارثة)]
- 89:28/q13/p2 The soul sees its place in Paradise and returns to the body before Munkar and Nakir question it. — Muhammad ibn Ka'b makes the soul pleased by the reward seen and approved during the interim. [6 notes: DURR(محمد بن كعب القرظي); DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ALUSI(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 89:28/q14 About whom was the verse reportedly revealed?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports name distinct persons and events.
- 89:28/q14/p1 About Uthman after he bought and donated the well of Rumah. — The report in al-Durr says God invited its purchase for public drinking; al-Dahhak is named by Ibn Ashur. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR(الضحاك); IBNASHUR-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 89:28/q14/p2 About Hamza when he was killed. — Buraydah is named as transmitter. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(بريدة); IBNASHUR-FULL(بريدة)]
- 89:28/q14/p3 About Khubayb ibn Adi when Meccans crucified him. — Ibn Ashur reports this view without naming its source. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:28/q15 Was this passage revealed with the Meccan sūra or added later?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether continuity of recitation or reported occasions and compositional analysis determine the chronology.
- 89:28/q15/p1 The sūra is Meccan and the passage likely belongs to the same original sequence. — Ibn Ashur says the agreed Meccan classification and continuous codex sequence favor one revelation, with intervening material parenthetical; he allows Medinan placement by instruction or thematic fit if particular occasion reports are correct. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:28/q15/p2 The verses may be Medinan or a later insertion. — Al-Dani transmits a Medinan classification and reports about Uthman, Hamza and Khubayb imply later circumstances according to Ibn Ashur. Corpus Coranicum regards 23–24 and 27–30 as later additions to an earlier eschatological unit. [7 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:28/q16 How does this address connect with the preceding warning and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes variously connect the address to warning, the resurrection scene, worldly tests, or the oath.
- 89:28/q16/p1 It follows punishment with glad tidings for believers. — Ibn Atiyya contrasts punished souls with believers; Ibn Ashur says the Quran pairs promise and warning to encourage good and deter evil. Corpus Coranicum says the Garden ending answers Hell in 89:23 and God's direct address supersedes earlier negative speech. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:28/q16/p2 It continues the resurrection scene begun with the earth's crushing. — Ibn Ashur connects return of spirits to bodies with 89:21 and treats intervening lines as parenthetical; Elmalili sees the prior regret of 89:24 as context for a call after reckoning. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد بن حارثة وأبي بن كعب وابن مسعود); ELMALILI]
- 89:28/q16/p3 The soul completes the contrast with worldly arrogance and despair. — Bint al-Shati and Islahi connect wealth and deprivation to tests of gratitude, fulfilling others' rights and patient acceptance (89:15–16). Neuwirth relates the trusting soul to the oath's worship times and inner calm; Corpus Coranicum calls the ending a later antithesis. [9 notes: BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth)]
- 89:28/q16/p4 The sūra's ten nights prefigure the soul's bliss. — Elmalili compares its life among the righteous to ten nights before Eid al-Adha and Paradise entry to the dawn of the great festival. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 89:28/q17 What spiritual conduct does the address commend?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward lesson stresses lived obedience and acceptance or completion of spiritual ascent.
- 89:28/q17/p1 Exclusive trust, obedience and contentment with God's decree. — Elmalili describes knowing the First Source beyond unstable causes, sincere submission and freedom from ego and other attachments, citing 2:112, 2:207 and 90:8; he prays for his readers' return. Mujahid and another formulation emphasize certainty, humility, repentance and a heart responsive to God (8:2; 22:34–35; 39:54). Islahi commends gratitude, others' rights and patience. [13 notes: ELMALILI(Mücâhid); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; DURR(النبي)]
- 89:28/q17/p2 Ascent beyond the attributes to the divine Essence. — Al-Qashani says contentment with God follows God's contentment with the soul, citing 5:119; al-Bursawi says stability in ascent prevents regression to the commanding self. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Aj, K)]

## 89:28/q18 What literary effect does the direct address have?  [* · rhetoric]
- 89:28/q18/p1 It tenderly honors and reassures the believer. — Al-Alusi likens 'tranquil soul' to urging a known brave person and calls 'your Lord' tender. Ibn Ashur sees the possessive as intimacy; Kuran Yolu says direct address carries the afterlife promise into present life. Ibn Ashur calls the shift to first-person Paradise aesthetically fitting. [8 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 89:28/q19 What other Qur'anic and devotional connections are given?  [* · links]
- 89:28/q19/p1 The root of the two adjectives is compared with 101:7. — Corpus Coranicum directs readers to 101:7 for r-ḍ-y; Suyuti uses 69:21 to explain passive sense in a related adjective. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ITQAN]
- 89:28/q19/p2 Reciting al-Fajr has a reported reward. — Al-Baydawi reports forgiveness for recitation during the ten nights and a light on Resurrection for recitation at other times. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 89:28/q20 Who is addressed by the command to return?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether nafs here denotes the departing spirit or the human person/self.
- 89:28/q20/p1 The believer's spirit, separated from its body. — Elmalili distinguishes spirit from self or essence, and explains that at death the spirit departs its body toward its Lord. Abu Salih and Ibn Zayd locate the address at death. [4 notes: ELMALILI; TAB(أبو صالح); ALUSI(ابن زيد وجماعة)]
- 89:28/q20/p2 The human self or person, including souls that reenter bodies. — Elmalili notes the generic self or essence; al-Dahhak, Ibn Abbas and others describe resurrection. Ubayy's reading adds 'secure' and reads 'My servant' singular, and Ibn Abbas also reads the singular, supporting re-entry into a body. [6 notes: ELMALILI; NAHHAS(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA(بعض العلماء); DURR(أبيّ,ابن عباس); IBNASHUR]
