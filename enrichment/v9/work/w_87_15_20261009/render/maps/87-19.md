# 87:19: 23 questions

## 87:19/q01 What does ṣuḥuf mean here?  [صُحُفِ · meaning]
- 87:19/q01/p1 Written sheets or scrolls, hence revealed books or scriptures. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās and Kuran Yolu derive the plural from ṣaḥīfa; al-Bursawī distinguishes a single written sheet from a muṣḥaf, a collected set. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ṣuḥuf an irregular but more eloquent plural. Asad treats it as synonymous with kitāb and translates revelations. Corpus Coranicum suggests an Old South Arabian loan and observes early Qur'anic use for revealed texts or a heavenly archetype (81:10; 80:13-14; 74:52; 53:36-37), distinct from profane poetry; later 20:133 and 98:2 echo early passages, while kitāb and zubur become usual terms (78:29; 84:7,10; 26:196). [8 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 87:19/q02 How is ṣuḥuf pronounced?  [صُحُفِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the ḥāʾ takes ḍamma or sukūn; al-Ālūsī identifies the latter as Tamīm usage.
- 87:19/q02/p1 With ḍamma on the ḥāʾ, the majority reading. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies this as the majority pronunciation. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA]
- 87:19/q02/p2 With a silent ḥāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes it through Hārūn to Abū ʿAmr and also to al-Aʿmash; al-Ālūsī adds ʿIṣmah's transmission from Abū ʿAmr and calls it a Tamīm dialect. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; ALUSI(الأعمش وهرون وعصمة عن أبي عمرو)]

## 87:19/q03 Which scriptures are named by the scrolls of Abraham and Moses?  [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the genitive marks recipients or subjects, whether Moses' scrolls include the Torah, and whether Abraham had a distinct written revelation.
- 87:19/q03/p1 Revelations given to Abraham and Moses, with Moses' scripture understood as the Torah. — Muqātil says Abraham's scrolls have since been taken away. Al-Wāḥidī reports both a pre-Torah Mosaic scroll view and a Torah reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls Moses' many scrolls the Torah's books, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies al-Ṣādiq's tablets with the Torah, citing 7:145,150,154. Al-Qurṭubī says the meaning, rather than the exact wording, occurs in them. References to 53:36 appear in al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TAB+]
- 87:19/q03/p2 Abraham's ten scrolls and Moses' ten scrolls revealed before the Torah are meant. — Al-Ālūsī invokes Abū Dharr's 104-book report but leaves its authenticity to God; al-Wāḥidī also reports pre-Torah scrolls, and Study Quran gives the same allocation. This excludes the Torah from the Mosaic scrolls named here. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر); STUDYQURAN(Prophet); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 87:19/q03/p3 The names broadly evoke biblical scripture or tradition, rather than particular Abrahamic and Mosaic books. — Ahrens calls this a rough designation for the Old Testament or whole Bible, arguing that 53:36 onward paraphrases biblical material. Corpus Coranicum prefers a broad evocation through the two prominent figures; it observes that the Old Testament assigns Abraham no revealed book and judges specific apocryphal Abrahamic books unlikely. Islāḥī says Israelite prophetic writings survive in the altered Bible while Abraham's oral exhortations were later preserved by descendants. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Ahrens,Heck); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 87:19/q03/p4 The phrase means revealed writings about Abraham and Moses. — Heck reads the genitive objectively. Corpus Coranicum instead favors broad biblical evocation. [0 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Heck)-]

## 87:19/q04 How does the phrase relate grammatically to the earlier scrolls?  [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the named pair exhausts the preceding reference or illustrates the wider set of revealed scriptures.
- 87:19/q04/p1 It is an apposition specifying the earlier scrolls of 87:18. — Al-Naḥḥās calls ṣuḥuf an appositional substitute. Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe specification after the general phrase; al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī say initial vagueness magnifies the named scriptures. [11 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 87:19/q04/p2 Abraham's and Moses' books exemplify a teaching present in every prophet's scriptures. — Al-Rāzī gives this alternative and cites Abū Dharr's list of 104 books, including Abraham's ten. Al-Ḥasan says the teaching is in all God's books, and Kuran Yolu calls these two examples rather than limits. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 87:19/q05 What does calling these scrolls earlier mean?  [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · interpretation]
- 87:19/q05/p1 Earlier than the Qur'an, not necessarily the first scriptures ever revealed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr points to reports of scrolls given to messengers before Abraham and cites 53:50,56; a Bukhārī report preserves the saying about shame from earliest prophecy. [2 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 87:19/q06 How is Abraham's name read?  [إِبْرَٰهِيمَ · readings]
turns on: The presence of alif and yāʾ and the vowel on hāʾ; witnesses give differing details for some transmitted forms.
- 87:19/q06/p1 Ibrāhīm, with alif, yāʾ and kasra on hāʾ, is the common reading. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call it the majority form; Corpus Coranicum prefers it on Horovitz's argument that it parallels Ismāʿīl and occurs in Umayya b. Abī ṣ-Ṣalt, despite readings closer to biblical forms. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 87:19/q06/p2 Abū Rajāʾ omits both alif and yāʾ, with the hāʾ reported open or broken. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions omission; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report fatḥa and kasra variants; al-Ālūsī reports both and the full account also mentions Masūra's vowel variant. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA(أبو رجاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو رجاء، ابن الزبير، أبو موسى الأشعري، عبد الرحمن بن أبي بكرة); ABUHAYYAN(أبو رجاء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(أبو رجاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو رجاء)]
- 87:19/q06/p3 Ibrāhām, with a second alif in place of yāʾ, is read by Ibn al-Zubayr and Abū Mūsā throughout the Qur'an. — The transmitters agree on this form; al-Samīn describes it as two alifs. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن الزبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو رجاء، ابن الزبير، أبو موسى الأشعري، عبد الرحمن بن أبي بكرة); ABUHAYYAN(أبو موسى الأشعري وابن الزبير); SAMIN-DURR(أبو موسى وابن الزبير); ALUSI(أبو موسى الأشعري وابن الزبير); ALUSI-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري وابن الزبير)]
- 87:19/q06/p4 Mālik ibn Dīnār reads with an alif, open hāʾ and no yāʾ. — Reported by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī; the full al-Ālūsī note pairs this with another Ibn Khālawayh form. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN(مالك بن دينار); SAMIN-DURR(مالك ابن دينار); ALUSI(مالك بن دينار); ALUSI-FULL(مالك بن دينار)]
- 87:19/q06/p5 ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Abī Bakra reads with kasra on hāʾ and no yāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī say this occurs throughout the Qur'an; al-Samīn's version also omits the alif and calls the reader Ibn Abī Bakr. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA(عبد الرحمن بن أبي بكرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو رجاء، ابن الزبير، أبو موسى الأشعري، عبد الرحمن بن أبي بكرة); ABUHAYYAN(عبد الرحمن بن أبي بكرة); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الرحمن بن أبي بكر); ALUSI(عبد الرحمن بن أبي بكرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو رجاء)]
- 87:19/q06/p6 Ibn Khālawayh records a form with ḍamma on hāʾ. — The reports vary on the other letters: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn mention an alif; al-Ālūsī and his full account describe omission of both alif and yāʾ, Ibruhum. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN(ابن خالويه); SAMIN-DURR(ابن خالويه); ALUSI(ابن خالويه); ALUSI-FULL(ابن خالويه,مالك بن دينار)]

## 87:19/q07 Is the name Ibrāhīm foreign or Arabic?  [إِبْرَٰهِيمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the variant forms reflect adaptation of a foreign name or an Arabic root.
- 87:19/q07/p1 It is a foreign name adapted in Arabic. — Al-Ālūsī says the reading variants reflect Arabs' adaptation of foreign names and calls this the sounder view. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 87:19/q07/p2 It is Arabic, from barhama, intense looking. — Al-Kirmānī's Wonders offers this derivation; al-Ālūsī reports it while preferring foreign origin. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكرماني); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 87:19/q08 Why is Abraham named before Moses?  [إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Chronology and merit, or the relative prominence of admonition and law in the two scriptures.
- 87:19/q08/p1 Their historical and revelatory order, also precedence in merit, determines the sequence. — Al-Biqāʿī gives chronology and merit; al-Itqān and al-Burhān cite this pair as an example of chronological ordering that may combine with honor. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 87:19/q08/p2 Abraham's scrolls suit admonition more closely; Moses' law-focused book therefore comes last. — Al-Biqāʿī invokes Abū Dharr's hadith without grading it; he says Moses' book has fewer admonitions but forceful warnings, including curses against Torah violators and foretelling rejection of its good news about Muhammad. [4 notes: BIQAI(أبو ذر); BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:19/q09 How many revealed books or scrolls were there, and how were they apportioned?  [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Whether the ten additional sheets belong to Adam or Moses and whether Abraham receives ten or twenty.
- 87:19/q09/p1 A report counts 104: ten to Adam, fifty to Seth, thirty to Idrīs, ten to Abraham, plus four named books. — Abū Dharr's account names Torah, Gospel, Psalms and Furqān/Qur'an; al-Thaʿlabī adds that Idrīs was first to write with a pen. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes that another transmission instead gives Moses ten and omits Adam. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Abraham's ten without grading the transmission. [9 notes: THALABI(أبو ذر); KASHSHAF(أبو ذر); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو ذر); TABRISI(أبو ذر، النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI(أبو ذر); RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR(أبو ذر)]
- 87:19/q09/p2 A report counts 104: fifty to Seth, thirty to Idrīs, ten to Abraham, ten to Moses before the Torah, plus four named books. — This variant omits Adam's sheets and adds Moses' ten. Al-Ālūsī leaves authenticity to God; Study Quran notes a slightly different Shiite transmission. [6 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر); STUDYQURAN(Prophet,Shiite sources); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 87:19/q09/p3 Some Shiite accounts give Abraham twenty scrolls. — Study Quran records this as a variant alongside the ten-scroll reports. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Shiite sources)]

## 87:19/q10 What sayings are attributed to Abraham's scrolls?  [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ · reports]
turns on: These are complementary maxims within the reported scrolls, with variants in phrasing and detail.
- 87:19/q10/p1 They taught prudence: guard the tongue, know one's time, and attend to one's affairs. — Reported as a prophetic saying or transmitted aphorism by al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Ṭabrisī and others. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī add that speech counts as deeds and one should speak little except about what concerns one. [13 notes: KASHSHAF(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(إبراهيم); RAZI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI(الأثر); NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر)]
- 87:19/q10/p2 A ruler must protect the oppressed rather than hoard wealth, even when the oppressed is an unbeliever. — The Abū Dharr report addresses a powerful, tested, deceived ruler; God answers the oppressed person's plea even if unbelieving. Al-Ālūsī calls the Abrahamic scrolls collections of parables or maxims. [5 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر)]
- 87:19/q10/p3 The wise divide time among communion with God, self-accounting and reflection, and lawful personal needs. — Al-Qurṭubī mentions reflection on divine favor and food and drink; al-Ālūsī says meeting lawful needs refreshes the heart for other hours. [5 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر)]
- 87:19/q10/p4 Travel or striving is for livelihood, provision for the return to God, or lawful enjoyment. — Al-Qurṭubī gives three grounds for travel; the Abū Dharr reports in al-Durr and al-Ālūsī express the three aims as livelihood, afterlife provision and permitted pleasure. [5 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر)]

## 87:19/q11 What admonitions are attributed to Moses' scrolls?  [صُحُفِ وَمُوسَىٰ · reports]
- 87:19/q11/p1 They challenge those who rejoice despite death, trust a changing world, chafe at destiny, or fail to act despite reckoning. — Al-Qurṭubī includes weariness despite belief in decree; al-Ālūsī includes laughter despite certainty of Hell. Al-Durr and its full version present the items together as admonitions or lessons. [6 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر)]

## 87:19/q12 When were the named scriptures revealed?  [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The reported days for Psalms, Gospel and especially Qur'an differ; all reports place Abraham's scrolls on Ramadan's first night.
- 87:19/q12/p1 Abraham's scrolls came on Ramadan night one, Torah on six, Psalms on twelve, Gospel on eighteen, Qur'an on twenty-four. — Qatāda and a report in Ibn ʿAṭiyya give this sequence, except Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the Qur'an on fourteen; al-Ṭabrisī reports more generally that all scriptures came during Ramadan. [4 notes: TAB(قتادة); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI]
- 87:19/q12/p2 Abraham's scrolls came on Ramadan night one, Torah on six, Gospel on thirteen, Psalms on eighteen, Qur'an on twenty-four. — Wāthila transmits this sequence from the Prophet. [1 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(واثلة)]
- 87:19/q12/p3 Abraham's scrolls came on Ramadan night one, Torah on six, Psalms on twelve, Gospel on eighteen, Qur'an on fourteen. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this variant in both accounts. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 87:19/q13 How many prophets and messengers does the Abū Dharr report count?  [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · reports]
- 87:19/q13/p1 124,000 prophets and 313 messengers, with Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Shuʿayb and Muḥammad the four Arab prophets. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmit this as the opening of Abū Dharr's report about revealed books. [3 notes: THALABI(أبو ذر); TABRISI(أبو ذر، النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 87:19/q14 Which preceding teaching is said to be in the earlier scriptures?  [* · referent]
turns on: The scope of the preceding demonstrative: nearest verses, the whole sūra, its afterlife statement, or the Qur'an.
- 87:19/q14/p1 The teaching of 87:14-17: purification, remembrance and prayer, worldly preference, and the superior enduring afterlife. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the nearest antecedent; Ibn Kathīr calls that a strong choice and reports Qatāda and Ibn Zayd likewise. Al-Baghawī lists the four verses. The Prophet reportedly recited this passage when asked what remained from the scrolls; Ibn ʿĀshūr excludes the opening verses and could not establish that report's grade. Al-Biqāʿī includes glorification in the admonition and says its aim pervades every revealed law and cannot be abrogated. [19 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNKATHIR(قتادة وابن زيد); QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير)+; TAB+]
- 87:19/q14/p2 The whole sūra or its story is present in the earlier scriptures. — Ibn ʿAbbās says the sūra was copied from those scrolls; al-Suddī says it appeared just as revealed to Muhammad, a stronger claim than shared themes. Abū al-ʿĀliya says its story was there. Ibn Kathīr reports a related prophetic wording, this or all this, with al-Bazzār's limitation on a connected trustworthy route; one Ibn ʿAbbās version extends the material through the end of Sūra 53. [24 notes: TAB(أبو العالية,عكرمة,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,السدي); BAGHAWI(عكرمة والسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة والسدي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNKATHIR(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,ابن عباس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عكرمة); DURR(ابن عباس,السدي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,السدي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 87:19/q14/p3 Especially the assertion that the Hereafter is better and more enduring was in earlier books. — Qatāda says God's books agree on this (87:17), Ibn Zayd compares afterlife with first life, and Islāḥī calls it real life. [5 notes: TAB(ابن زيد,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:19/q14/p4 The entire Qur'an is attested in the earlier scriptures. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk identifies the reference as the Qur'an; Study Quran notes al-Ālūsī's connection to 26:196. [2 notes: THALABI(الضحاك); STUDYQURAN(Āl)]

## 87:19/q15 Is the earlier scripture's wording identical to this passage?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the assertion concerns shared meaning or the same revealed wording.
- 87:19/q15/p1 The same teaching or meaning occurs there, without requiring identical wording. — Al-Qurṭubī explicitly separates meaning from exact words. Ibn ʿĀshūr dignifies the enduring teaching through its older attestation, and al-Ṭabarī names its proximate content. [4 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB]
- 87:19/q15/p2 This sūra occurred in those scrolls as revealed to Muhammad. — Al-Suddī explicitly says as revealed; Ibn ʿAbbās says it was transcribed or copied. Other reports use broader whole-sūra wording without asserting verbatim identity. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي); DURR(ابن عباس,السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي)]

## 87:19/q16 What broader religious teaching does this shared scripture establish?  [* · theology]
- 87:19/q16/p1 Revelation has one divine source and a continuous core of faith, worship, morality and afterlife accountability. — Qutb locates the sūra's oneness, revelation and recompense in earlier scriptures: one creator perfects, apportions and guides, while details vary with eras. Asad speaks of common basic truths; Kuran Yolu cites 26:196; Islāḥī adds personal responsibility from 53:36-38 and says Muhammad revived Abraham's teaching after Ishmaelite neglect and innovations. Corpus Coranicum says the ancient scriptural agreement authorizes the rebuke, supported by the psalm-like opening; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the ending addresses deniers familiar with the two messengers. [16 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:19/q17 How does the ending connect to the sūra's opening and the next sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: These describe different, compatible connections made by the closing words.
- 87:19/q17/p1 The ending returns to opening glorification through purification, showing guidance and purpose. — Al-Biqāʿī joins 87:1 and 87:14: glorifying God and cleansing the soul are the sūra's central aim; guidance proves creation was not purposeless. He defines tazkiya as removal of impurities, transcendence, and acquiring divine qualities as possible. Qutb describes the cosmic hymn and the Prophet's promised preservation and ease; Study Quran traces the movement from glorification to contrasting hearers and earlier revelation. [6 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:19/q17/p2 Sūrat al-Ghāshiya explains this ending by affirming and depicting the afterlife. — Al-Biqāʿī points to 88:1, the fates of pious and wicked, and signs of God's power. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:19/q17/p3 Repetition of ṣuḥuf joins verses 18 and 19 by concatenation. — Corpus Coranicum compares early Qur'anic and pre-Islamic poetic chains that stretch syntax over short verses, citing 96:1-2,4-5,15-16; 106:1-2; 26:132-33; 40:36-37. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:19/q18 What other Qur'anic passages illuminate these scrolls?  [* · links]
- 87:19/q18/p1 Q 53:36-42 also names the scrolls and develops Abraham's fulfillment, personal responsibility, striving, recompense and return to God. — Ibn Kathīr cites 53:36-42 and Ibn ʿAbbās' link from 53:37 to the rule that no bearer bears another's burden. Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Asad and Corpus Coranicum also point to 53:36-37; the latter adds Moses' early Meccan account at 79:15-26. Al-Ālūsī says Moses' lineage was given earlier in his commentary. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM; ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:19/q18/p2 Q 26:196 likewise presents Qur'anic teaching as attested in earlier scriptures. — Kuran Yolu cites it for the single source of prophetic revelation; Study Quran invokes it for the entire-Qur'an interpretation. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Āl)]

## 87:19/q20 How was this sūra recited in prayer and with what response?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted occasions and responses associated with recitation.
- 87:19/q20/p1 The Prophet recited al-Aʿlā with other sūras in voluntary, Eid and Friday prayers. — ʿĀʾisha reports al-Aʿlā and al-Kāfirūn before witr, followed by al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq and al-Nās in witr. Qutb and Study Quran mention Eid and Friday use with al-Ghāshiya, sometimes on the same day; Study Quran also records a recommendation for evening prayer. A report from al-Nuʿmān concerns al-Ghāshiya on Friday with another sūra. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة); QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:19/q20/p2 The Prophet and ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAbbās said Glory to my Lord, the Most High when reciting it. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the Prophet loved the sūra and reports him saying Michael first uttered this praise. [3 notes: KASHSHAF(رسول الله,علي وابن عباس)]
- 87:19/q20/p3 It is recommended for recitation while prostrating. — Study Quran attributes this recommendation to the Prophet. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]

## 87:19/q21 When and where was this sūra revealed, and how long is it?  [* · sciences]
- 87:19/q21/p1 It is an early Meccan sūra of nineteen verses, often placed eighth after al-Takwīr. — Qutb calls it Meccan with nineteen verses; Study Quran reports the majority early Meccan chronology and another all-Meccan view based on possible reference to rituals before formal institution. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN(majority,others)]

## 87:19/q22 What does imāla affect in this passage?  [وَمُوسَىٰ · readings]
- 87:19/q22/p1 The rhyming endings from al-Aʿlā through Mūsā have imāla variants. — Ibn al-Jazarī says these were discussed earlier in his imāla chapter. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 87:19/q23 Can revealed wording disappear through abrogation?  [* · sciences]
- 87:19/q23/p1 God can cause recitation and script to be forgotten and discontinued, as happened to earlier scriptures; Qur'anic abrogation ended with the Prophet's death. — Abū Bakr al-Rāzī argues that God orders people to stop reciting and writing the text, so it fades; al-Burhān and al-Itqān preserve his argument. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو بكر الرازي); ITQAN(أبو بكر الرازي)]
