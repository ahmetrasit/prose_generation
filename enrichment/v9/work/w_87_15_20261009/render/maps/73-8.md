# 73:8: 8 questions

## 73:8/q01 What does remembering the Lord's name call for?  [وَٱذْكُرِ ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase calls for formulas, varied acts, directed deeds, moral recollection, or self-knowledge.
- 73:8/q01/p1 Persist in many forms of remembrance, worship and religious learning. — Al-Zamakhsharī includes glorification, unity, greatness, prayer, recitation and study, noting the Messenger filled his time with them. Al-Biqāʿī adds submission, supplication, learning and conduct; Elmalılı adds teaching and guidance. Iṣlāḥī counts preaching and reflection on inward and outward signs, without which remembrance is mere tongue exercise. Al-Kūrānyolu adds reliance on God's help and protection; Study Quran cites 29:45. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 73:8/q01/p2 Affirm God's oneness, exalt and magnify Him. — Maqātil adds sincerity; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī add magnification; al-Wāḥidī adds transcendence. Al-Biqāʿī says magnifying the name magnifies the Named and helps in both lives. [6 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 73:8/q01/p3 Begin prayer or Qurʾān recitation with the basmala. — Sahl says its blessing connects the worshipper to God and cuts other ties. Ibn Baḥr and Study Quran specify recitation; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report this as an interpretation. [9 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); THALABI(سهل); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(سهل); BURSEVI(بعضهم); TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 73:8/q01/p4 Call on God's names in supplication. — Al-Ṭabrisī says the names are used in worship and prayer; al-Qurṭubī says this makes prayer fruitful. Al-Bursawī cites the Prophetic promise of Paradise for enumerating the beautiful names. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī permits any name or Allah specifically. [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي); TABATABAI]
- 73:8/q01/p5 Direct one's work toward God's pleasure. — Al-Māwardī holds it; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī report it. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 73:8/q01/p6 Recall God's promises and warnings to obey and avoid disobedience. — Al-Māwardī allows this; al-Qurṭubī reports it. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 73:8/q01/p7 Remember verbally with the heart present, especially during night prayer. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the commands explain recitation with tartīl; prior awareness of God does not preclude a verbal command. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 73:8/q01/p8 Know and remember your own self as the Lord's 'name'. — Al-Qāshānī says self-knowledge prevents being left forgotten by God and leads toward perfection. [1 notes: BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 73:8/q02 When is remembrance commanded?  [وَٱذْكُرِ ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ · links]
turns on: Whether the command is tied to night recitation or extends through the day.
- 73:8/q02/p1 Continually through night and day in the customary sense. — Al-Ālūsī says customary continuity follows the specific times, while the Prophet already remembered God. Ibn ʿĀshūr finds it unrestricted despite coordination with night prayer, comparing 76:25. Al-Bursawī singles out dawn and before sunset as times of grace; Study Quran cites 29:45. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Aj); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 73:8/q02/p2 Remember during the day, using its free time if night worship was missed. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq give daytime as compensation. Al-Qurṭubī prefers al-Kalbī's daytime prayer reading as paired with the prior night instruction, comparing 25:62. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي)+]
- 73:8/q02/p3 The command describes night prayer in particular. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the imperatives as explaining tartīl and rejects the claim that prior awareness bars a fresh verbal command. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 73:8/q02/p4 Turn to worship when worldly duties are finished. — Ibn Kathīr connects this to 94:7 and cites Ibn Zayd; Study Quran reports it. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 73:8/q03 Why mention the Lord's 'name' in this wording?  [ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which feature is stressed: speech, names, person shift, or the Named.
- 73:8/q03/p1 It specifies spoken remembrance, which also carries inward thought. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says speech follows thought, comparing 7:205. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī joins utterance to the heart. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 73:8/q03/p2 It points to divine names, possibly Allah in particular. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly allows either; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī connect the names to supplication. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 73:8/q03/p3 The shift from first-person plural to third person awakens servitude. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the shift establishes the servant-Lord bond. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 73:8/q03/p4 Magnifying the name magnifies the One named. — Al-Biqāʿī links this to unity and sincerity, helping in both lives. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 73:8/q04 What does remembrance accomplish in the inward life?  [وَٱذْكُرِ ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ · inward]
turns on: Whether focus falls on experience, contemplative ascent, knowledge, resilience, or lasting awareness.
- 73:8/q04/p1 It brings nourishment, intimacy and life; heedlessness brings loss. — Dhū al-Nūn pictures celestial branches feeding and elevating hearts, cites a longing poem, and calls deprivation of remembrance the knower's punishment. Abū ʿUthmān contrasts heedlessness's loneliness with remembrance's intimacy. Iṣlāḥī says forgetting leaves the soul lifeless. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,ذو النون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 73:8/q04/p2 It advances from a spoken name to direct awareness of the One. — Al-Rāzī says the name yields to the Named (7:205); lordship's benefits still occupy the heart, while divinity (2:200) brings awe of might and self-sufficiency. Majesty and transcendence culminate in identity beyond likeness, composition, analogy, language and thought. God is source of manifestation yet hidden from intellect by overwhelming radiance. Al-Bursawī says manifestation makes remembrance and rememberer vanish. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 73:8/q04/p3 Persistent attention to a divine name yields affinity and divinely given knowledge. — Al-Bursawī's shaykh says divinely enabled attention to a metaphorical name yields affinity with its secret and spirit in proportion to effort. Al-Bursawī says purer readiness brings the true name's gift and spiritual and cosmic knowledge directly, through intermediaries, or both. [2 notes: BURSEVI(شيخي وسندي)]
- 73:8/q04/p4 Knowing God's attributes strengthens faith against Satan and crisis. — Iṣlāḥī says fresh knowledge makes one mountain-firm while weak comprehension makes faith baseless or fragile. Reflection gives remembrance substance. Al-Bursawī links it to 18:24 because forgetting and remembering are states of heart. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI]
- 73:8/q04/p5 God can grant uninterrupted awareness of Himself. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects treating perpetual remembrance as impossible or merely customary: God is never absent and can make Himself continuously known, not only to angels; compare 41:38 and 21:20. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 73:8/q04/p6 Glorifying, praising and magnifying God relieves need. — Al-Biqāʿī recounts that the Prophet directed Fāṭima to these remembrances at bedtime when she asked for a servant. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 73:8/q05 What does it mean to devote oneself to Him?  [وَتَبَتَّلْ إِلَيْهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether devotion specifies worship, renunciation, reliance, striving, prayer gesture, solitude, or direct orientation.
- 73:8/q05/p1 Turn exclusively to God in sincere worship, supplication and obedience, cutting off competing attachments. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās and many exegetes give sincerity; Qatāda specifies worship and supplication. Al-Farrāʾ leaves all but God's command and obedience. Ibn Qutayba derives the verb from cutting; Maryam's title and severed charity illustrate it. Al-Layth defines separation; poetry by Imruʾ al-Qays and Rabīʿa uses mutabattal of a monk. Al-Nasafī adds hoping for good from God alone; al-Biqāʿī describes progressively settled devotion. [69 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,ابن عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر الطبري,ابن زيد,مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الفراء,ربيعة بن مقروم,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن زيد,ابن عباس وغيره,عطاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(الفراء,الليث,المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الليث); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير,ابن عباس، ومجاهد، وأبو صالح، وعطية، والضحاك، والسدي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 73:8/q05/p2 Renounce worldly things and seek what is with God. — Zayd ibn Aslam's answer appears in al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī and al-Biqāʿī; al-Nasafī reports it as another view. [6 notes: THALABI(زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); RAZI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم)]
- 73:8/q05/p3 Rely wholly on God. — Shafīq is reported by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabrisī; Sufyān by al-Baghawī. Al-Kūrānyolu adds trust in divine protection. [4 notes: THALABI(شفيق); TABRISI(شقيق); BAGHAWI-FULL(سفيان); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 73:8/q05/p4 Exert oneself diligently in devotion. — Al-Ḥasan is reported by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī; Ibn Kathīr adds devotion to God. [3 notes: THALABI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 73:8/q05/p5 Raise the hands to God in supplication or qunūt. — Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī, Abū Jaʿfar, Abū ʿAbd Allāh and Abū Baṣīr give hand-raising and beseeching. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports variants of moving both index fingers, gesturing with one finger, or pointing while supplicating; he places these in qunūt and finds them apt if remembrance is verbal. [7 notes: THALABI(محمد بن علي); TABRISI(أبو بصير,أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله); TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت,أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله,بعضهم)]
- 73:8/q05/p6 Supplicate humbly. — Al-Māwardī expressly allows this explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 73:8/q05/p7 Withdraw into solitude. — Ibn ʿArafa gives solitude as an original Arabic sense; al-Layth notes avoiding men and marriage. Al-Qurṭubī prefers worship in context. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عرفة); WAHIDI-BASIT(اللبث); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 73:8/q05/p8 Turn to God Himself beyond reward, worship, knowledge or self-image. — Al-Rāzī says afterlife, worship or gnosis sought for themselves remain intermediaries. True servitude and knowledge are for the Worshipped and Known, exceeding explanation; he compares overwhelming love and distinguishes love of the beloved from desire to see the beloved. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and Abū al-Qāsim say one who reaches God cannot turn back. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI(أبو القيم)]
- 73:8/q05/p9 Withdraw from hostile rejecters toward mercy while continuing the mission. — Iṣlāḥī connects this to hurt from rejection; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet stays mindful and preaches, renewing his practice after Ḥirāʾ and revelation. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 73:8/q06 How does the command relate to the prohibition of tabattul?  [وَتَبَتَّلْ إِلَيْهِ · law]
turns on: Whether separation is monastic renunciation or a protective response to corruption.
- 73:8/q06/p1 The command concerns sincere devotion; the prohibition concerns leaving marriage or communal life. — Al-Qurṭubī contrasts Christian monasticism and cites 98:5 for sincerity and 5:87 against renouncing lawful pleasures. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn distinguish leaving gatherings or marriage; al-Bursawī cites 24:32 and distinguishes Maryam's and Fāṭima's titles. Ibn ʿĀshūr says food, sleep, family, marriage and perfume can serve religion. He reports the Prophet's love of women and perfume and a Ṣaḥīḥ report rejecting ʿUthmān ibn Maẓʿūn's request, saying permission would have led to castration. Ibn Jarīr notes the monastic use. [17 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر الطبري); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 73:8/q06/p2 In corrupt conditions, isolation and even celibacy may be preferable. — Ibn al-ʿArabī cites weakened trust and widespread unlawful wealth. Al-Qurṭubī separately allows remote sheep-tending during turmoil to protect faith. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 73:8/q07 Why does tabattal take the verbal noun tabtīlan?  [وَتَبَتَّلْ إِلَيْهِ تَبْتِيلًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form is a semantic substitute, cadence, paired morphology, means and goal, or divine agency.
- 73:8/q07/p1 Tabtīlan replaces expected tabattulan because tabattala has the sense of battala, 'cut off'. — Sībawayh says the meanings coincide. Al-Akhfash compares 71:17's nabāt for inbāt and a Wāfir poem with ittibāʿ after tatabbaʿa. Al-Wāḥidī invokes a near-synonymous noun; al-Samīn compares poetic inṭiwāʾ. Ibn ʿĀshūr says tabtīl is the noun of transitive battala, form V being reflexive; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it an absolute object. Al-Ālūsī licenses the accusative from the verb's sense; al-Suyūṭī generalizes the emphatic use of another verb's noun or a concrete noun. [20 notes: SIBAWAYH; AKHFASH; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ITQAN]
- 73:8/q07/p2 The choice preserves the sūra's verse-end cadence. — Al-Bayḍāwī also calls it allusive; al-Bursawī praises word order and sound. Al-Ālūsī points to the nabāt construction of 71:17. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 73:8/q07/p3 It joins gradual form V to abundant form II and a lasting devotional state. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares kassar and qattaʿa with simple verbs; Tafsīr al-Qayyim gives gradual effort to verb and intensification to noun. The verb marks renewal and the noun stability: begin manageably and grow. Later accounts call this naturally grasped yet inimitable eloquence. Al-Biqāʿī says both forms imply omitted counterparts; Ibn ʿĀshūr sees self-training. Al-Nasafī notes emphasis. [17 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR(التفسير القيم); SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 73:8/q07/p4 It names complete devotion as goal before active severing as means. — Al-Rāzī says active severing interrupts total absorption but must precede it, citing 29:69's promise to strivers. Elmalılı prefers this. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Râzî)+]
- 73:8/q07/p5 It signals that God brings about complete detachment through the servant's effort. — Al-Bursawī says effort depends on divine enabling. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 73:8/q08 How does the verse connect to its surroundings and the Prophet's mission?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether imperatives elaborate night recitation, broaden into mission, or prepare exclusive lordship.
- 73:8/q08/p1 It specifies remembrance and devotion as the highest acts of night vigil. — Al-Rāzī says the sequence commands vigil, gives the reason night is favored, then names its noblest acts. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says both imperatives explain tartīl. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 73:8/q08/p2 It extends night discipline into daytime remembrance and preaching. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is coordinated with 73:2 yet unrestricted, comparing 76:25, and notes 68:51's resistance to the Reminder. Al-Kūrānyolu says 73:1–7 prepare the Prophet to receive the message while these verses guide preaching. Iṣlāḥī connects it to endurance amid rejection. Al-Qurṭubī cites 25:62. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 73:8/q08/p3 It prepares the following affirmation of God alone as deity and world-Lord. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls rejection of associationism highest devotion and ḥanīfiyya. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says world-Lord prevents treating God as the Prophet's private deity like a pagan idol; al-Biqāʿī connects testimony of unity. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 73:8/q08/p4 It culminates a sequence of prophetic spiritual training. — An unnamed source in al-Sulamī describes discipline by commands and prohibitions, refinement through decree and power, dissolution of agency, then retreat to God. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
