# 87:6: 21 questions

## 87:6/q01 What does “We shall make you recite” mean?  [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether iqraʾa here is teaching recitation generally or listening for correction.
- 87:6/q01/p1 God will teach Muhammad the Qur’an and make him a reciter. — Baghawī and Thaʿlabī specify Gabriel’s recitation; Mālik, transmitted by Ibn Wahb in Qurṭubī, glosses it as teaching and retention. Bursevī cites Tāj al-maṣādir for keeping the Qur’an in the ear. Nasafī’s shorter note awkwardly says 'until he forgets,' while its full version says 'until he does not forget.' [14 notes: BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; THALABI(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 87:6/q01/p2 Iqraʾa can mean receiving a reading by listening so errors can be corrected. — Jishumī and Ṭabrisī also derive qirāʾa from gathering letters. Al-Majmaʿ, cited by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, gives the correction sense. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI(المجمع); TABATABAI-]
- 87:6/q01/p3 Reading is joining letters and words in measured recitation. — Al-Mufradāt, cited by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, excludes mere gathering or uttering a single letter. [1 notes: TABATABAI(المفردات)]

## 87:6/q02 How will God make the Prophet recite?  [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the divine making occurs through Gabriel’s instruction, inspiration, special memory, or an inward reading.
- 87:6/q02/p1 Gabriel conveys and recites the revelation, and God enables Muhammad to retain and deliver it. — Zajjāj says the unlettered Prophet teaches his companions without needing repetition. Wāḥidī reports Abū Isḥāq and Qatāda likewise. Bayḍāwī allows Gabriel’s recital or inspired reading. Bursevī includes revelation now and later. Ālūsī says divine attribution is mediated by Gabriel. Elmalılı names Gabriel the Trustworthy and Holy Spirit and cites 42:52; he specifies reciting from memory, not reading a text. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,قتادة); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:6/q02/p2 God inspires the Prophet to become a reader. — Rāzī relays Wāḥidī’s explanation. Bayḍāwī presents it alongside Gabriel’s recitation; Elmalılı says revelation makes him possess the Qur’an and recitation. Ālūsī reports Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq’s possibility that he could read writing without instruction though he did not write. [9 notes: RAZI(الواحدي); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABRISI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL(جعفر الصادق); ALUSI(جعفر الصادق); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:6/q02/p3 Gabriel repeats the revelation until the Prophet memorizes it. — Rāzī distinguishes repeated instruction from memorization after one hearing; Kashshāf connects repetition with his earlier haste and possible lapse. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 87:6/q02/p4 God expands or strengthens the Prophet’s heart so one hearing yields lasting memorization. — Rāzī contrasts this with repeated instruction. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/q02/p5 God gathers the Qur’an or its knowledge in his heart and eases its recitation and practice. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī say the Qur’an is gathered in his heart; Bursevī cites Kashf al-Asrār and 75:17 for heart and tongue. Rāzī connects comprehensive knowledge with easy action and 87:8. Iṣlāḥī cites 75:16–19 for collection, recital, following and explanation. [9 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:6/q02/p6 God enables exact recitation without addition, omission, or distortion. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the correction sense: no known case shows a misreading or forgotten revelation corrected by God. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 87:6/q02/p7 He reads the universal Qur’an of realities in his book of preparedness and Qur’anic intellect. — Qāshānī says he recalls this without forgetting. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:6/q03 What does the future prefix in “We shall make you recite” convey?  [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether sīn chiefly strengthens the pledge or points to its ongoing fulfillment.
- 87:6/q03/p1 It intensifies or confirms the divine promise. — Bursevī and Ālūsī report the emphatic sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a future marker confirms the coming act. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/q03/p2 It promises continuing and renewed recitation, including revelation yet to come. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reasons that the act had begun and the Qur’an would keep growing; Biqāʿī speaks of repeated instruction. Corpus Coranicum calls it a promise of further revelation. [8 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:6/q04 Why is God the first-person speaker who “makes recite”?  [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the question concerns the change of voice or the mediated attribution of recitation.
- 87:6/q04/p1 The shift to the majestic first-person plural suits direct divine good news and heightens its authority. — Corpus Coranicum calls the shift from third person singular iltifāt; Abdel Haleem says it dramatizes divine authority without implying redaction. Ibn ʿĀshūr says direct address fits the giver of glad tidings. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM(Abdel Haleem)]
- 87:6/q04/p2 God is credited with recitation because He brings about and commands the words recited through Gabriel. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this rational attribution; Ālūsī calls it metaphorical attribution through Gabriel. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 87:6/q05 How is the hamza of “We shall make you recite” treated in pausal readings?  [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · readings]
turns on: The extent of al-Akhfash’s substitution rule and the weight of written form versus transmitted pause practice.
- 87:6/q05/p1 The hamza may be replaced with yāʾ where the written form supports it, as in sanaqriʾuka. — Al-Dānī and the majority attribute broad substitution after kasra or ḍamma to al-Akhfash; Abū al-ʿIzz permits it for Ḥamza at pause. Al-Dānī favors matching the rasm. Ibn al-Jazarī says the rasm uses yāʾ here rather than customary wāw. [5 notes: NASHR(أبو الفتح فارس بن أحمد,الحافظ أبو عمرو الداني)]
- 87:6/q05/p2 Substitution is restricted to final radical hamza; medial cases use Sibawayh’s between-between easing. — Ibn al-Jazarī prefers what al-Akhfash’s own book permits and notes authorities who rejected his supposed broad method; al-Dānī combines substitution where rasm supports it with Sibawayh’s easing elsewhere. [3 notes: NASHR(الحافظ أبو عمرو الداني); NASHR+]
- 87:6/q05/p3 Substitution or easing varies with word boundary, region, vowel, and rasm. — Abū al-ʿAlāʾ permits substitution for a ḍamma hamza after kasra across or within words; Abū al-ʿIzz reports regional variation. Some non-Ḥijāzī Arabs use wāw, yāʾ or alif for final hamza by case. Ibn al-Jazarī distinguishes when rawm or ishām remains possible according to whose vowel governs replacement. [4 notes: NASHR(أبو الفتح فارس بن أحمد)]
- 87:6/q05/p4 The al-Kisāʾī method attributed through Khalaf has no practical basis. — Ibn al-Jazarī agrees with al-Dānī and rejects al-Shāṭibī’s allusion for lack of transmission or analogy. [1 notes: NASHR+]

## 87:6/q06 What does “forget” mean here in Arabic usage?  [تَنسَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical sense and vocalization of nisyān.
- 87:6/q06/p1 It is the opposite of remembering: known information fails to come to mind, briefly or for long. — Damghānī cites 18:63 and 18:73 and glosses this verse as lasting retention. Ṭabrisī calls it loss of meaning from the mind, like absent-mindedness, rather than simply loss of acquired knowledge. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows brief or long failure to recall. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNASHUR]
- 87:6/q06/p2 Nisyān in this sense has kasra on nūn and sukūn on sīn, unlike the similarly named anatomical term. — Ibn al-Jawzī calls the other, fully vowelled form the twin of the sciatic nerve. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 87:6/q07 What is the nature of forgetting?  [تَنسَىٰٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether forgetting is a created act or a privation of knowledge.
- 87:6/q07/p1 Forgetting is an act created by God. — Jishumī reports Abū ʿAlī; Ṭabrisī attributes the point to Abū ʿAlī al-Jubbāʾī. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي)]
- 87:6/q07/p2 Forgetting is the loss of ordinarily necessary knowledge, not an entity or act. — Jishumī reports Abū Hāshim in disagreement with Abū ʿAlī. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)]

## 87:6/q08 Is “you will not forget” a statement or a prohibition?  [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lā negates a predicted event or prohibits an avoidable cause of forgetting; the retained final alif also matters.
- 87:6/q08/p1 It is an asserted negation that functions as God’s promise of preserved memory. — Naḥḥās prefers exegetical support and says most linguists reject commanding involuntary forgetting. Damghānī cites 20:108 for assertive lā; Burhān describes its general negative use. Rāzī likens this to promising clothing so the addressee will not be naked; he says it brings stronger reassurance because forgetting is under God’s control. Ibn ʿĀshūr says report form serves a guarantee. [18 notes: NAHHAS; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+; IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير)+; NAHHAS+; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]
- 87:6/q08/p2 It is a prohibition: do not neglect recitation, review, or obedience lest you forget. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say review is within the Prophet’s power though forgetting itself is not. Samīn answers Makkī that prohibitions commonly target avoidable causes. Rāzī and Ālūsī report an alif retained for rhyme. Ālūsī’s preferred rescue is a report intended as prohibition of voluntary forgetting; Ibn Kathīr’s reported command exempts abrogated material. [18 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(آخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI+; NAHHAS-; RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 87:6/q09 Why is the final long vowel written or retained in “tansā”?  [تَنسَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form is explained by rhyme, orthography and pause, or a jussive retaining a weak letter.
- 87:6/q09/p1 It maintains the rhyme or regular verse endings. — This is especially invoked for a prohibitive jussive. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī compare the form al-sabīlā at 33:67; the shorter Bayḍāwī note cites 17:67. Abū Ḥayyān reports it but later rejects it as weak. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:6/q09/p2 The written form reflects the word’s original form or a pausal restoration, rather than proving prohibition. — Al-Marzūqī says a deleted alif is restored at pause. Qurṭubī appeals to the yāʾ in every codex and reading when preferring an actual exception. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام المرزوقي); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 87:6/q09/p3 A jussive can retain a weak letter. — Ālūsī reports this second prohibitive account but finds it unsatisfactory. [1 notes: ALUSI]

## 87:6/q10 What does “you will not forget” encompass?  [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the protected object is Qur’anic recitation, its practice, the law and sūras, or every remembered thing.
- 87:6/q10/p1 The Prophet retains the Qur’an God recites to him, so revelation can be conveyed faithfully. — Zajjāj cites 15:9 for divine preservation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says no verse is irretrievably lost; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses full delivery as revealed even as the Qur’an grows. Qutb says this removes the burden of guarding every letter and comforts the community. Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and Ālūsī point to exceptional retention despite illiteracy. Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifies no addition, omission or distortion. Ibn ʿAbbās reports an explicit assurance that God would ensure the Qur’an’s retention after the Prophet reviewed it out of fear of forgetting. Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ appeals to 87:6 and 18:24 to argue that God protected the Prophet from forgetting. [56 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); TAB; TAB-FULL(بعضهم); NAHHAS(مالك بن أنس); MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(الحسن وقتادة ومالك ابن أنس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب,كشف الأسرار); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(سعد بن أبي وقاص); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; DURR(ابن عباس)]
- 87:6/q10/p2 He does not abandon acting on revelation’s commands and prohibitions, except superseded practice or concessions. — Junayd says acting on commands and prohibitions is meant; Ibn Kaysān praises his answer in Sulamī and Qurṭubī. Māwardī uniquely connects the concession version to forbidding shirk. Al-Ālūsī calls this reading far-fetched. [16 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); SULAMI(ابن كيسان النحوي,الجنيد,محمد بن الحسن البغدادي); THALABI(الجنيد); MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الجنيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجنيد); QURTUBI-FULL(الجنيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI(Bazıları); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:6/q10/p3 The promise includes every object of forgetting, not only the recited revelation. — Biqāʿī explicitly includes anything else; Bursevī says the exception ranges over the broadest class of objects. Ālūsī reports perfect memory as a distinct view, then rejects it because of early transmitted interpretation and the consequential fāʾ. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI]
- 87:6/q10/p4 It guarantees retention of revealed law and sūras while also calling for continued attention and review. — These sources distinguish the verse from 75:16 and explain that neglecting review remains avoidable. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA(آخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 87:6/q10/p5 It concerns the truth heard by the Prophet. — Bursevī attributes this wording to al-Rāghib. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب)]

## 87:6/q11 What does the exception for what God wills allow?  [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the exception identifies an actual removal, ordinary lapse, deferred revelation, limited practice, or a rhetorical assertion of divine will.
- 87:6/q11/p1 God can remove abrogated recitation, often with its ruling, so it is forgotten or no longer practiced. — Ṭabarī explicitly prefers abrogation and withdrawal. Baghawī, Kashshāf, Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 2:106. Rāzī says it ceases to be recited in prayer then leaves memory. Qurṭubī prefers lifting recitation for a benefit, appealing to the normal force of an exception and the written yāʾ. Iṣlāḥī calls the removed directives temporary and says God signals their end. [19 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); TAB(قائلو هذه المقالة); TAB-FULL(قائلو هذه المقالة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما); BURHAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 87:6/q11/p2 God may cause actual forgetting as He wills, without specifying abrogation. — Qatāda repeatedly states this open exception. Qurṭubī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās through Abū Ṣāliḥ and Qatāda through Saʿīd. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the object of God’s choosing is omitted by normal Arabic usage. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNKATHIR(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/q11/p3 The exception is formal or rhetorical; no forgetting is intended or actually occurs. — Al-Farrāʾ compares 11:107 and ordinary oaths that qualify an intended complete gift. Al-Zamakhsharī adopts the empty-exception reading; Rāzī reports al-Kalbī that no forgetting occurred. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the exception preserves God’s unlimited power though He will not exercise it, citing 11:108. Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ argues from the Prophet's protection against forgetting that nansa'ha means defer or leave, rather than cause him to forget; he also invokes 18:24. [13 notes: FARRA; TAB(بعض أهل العربية); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); THALABI(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,الفراء وجماعة); TABATABAI(سعد بن أبي وقاص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 87:6/q11/p4 It allows a temporary lapse followed by recollection, without permanent loss. — Several cite the prayer omission and Ubayy’s query; Abū Ḥayyān instead cites ʿAbbād’s recitation reminding the Prophet of verses. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an ordinary human lapse for which God supplies a reminder. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/q11/p5 Forgetting may establish a practice for the community. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this to Ibn ʿAbbās and includes it among actual exceptions. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 87:6/q11/p6 God may defer a revelation that Muhammad has not yet recited. — Māwardī attributes this to Ibn ʿĪsā; Qurṭubī reports it among alternatives. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:6/q11/p7 Any forgetting is confined to minor etiquette or sunna, not a required ruling. — Rāzī’s reported argument is that loss of an obligatory ruling would damage the law. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/q11/p8 The exception concerns material outside the duty to convey, which he may forget before conveying. — Qushayrī adds that God knows the secret and the public. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 87:6/q11/p9 The exception reserves an awareness for his praiseworthy station at resurrection. — Qāshānī allows loss of awareness or forgetting now for that later station. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 87:6/q11/p10 It asserts God’s unrestricted will even within a firm promise or enduring law. — Qutb says the Qur’an repeatedly emphasizes this freedom. Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses God could cause forgetting though He does not. Rāzī reports al-Farrāʾ that preservation is divine favor rather than Muhammad’s own power, citing 17:86 and 39:65. [4 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 87:6/q11/p11 The qualification teaches saying “if God wills” about the future or keeps the Prophet attentive. — Rāzī cites 18:23–24 for the formula and separately reports vigilance over what might be excepted. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 87:6/q12 What prompted the reassurance about forgetting?  [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · reports]
turns on: Whether the earlier anxiety appeared as inward rehearsal, speaking ahead of Gabriel, or Gabriel’s direct reassurance.
- 87:6/q12/p1 The Prophet rehearsed revelation inwardly or repeatedly out of fear of forgetting. — Mujāhid emphasizes private inward rehearsal; Ibn ʿAbbās gives the fear and divine assurance. Durr cites a similar report from Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ through al-Ḥākim. Qutb attributes the repetition to love and responsibility for every letter. [19 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); NAHHAS(مجاهد); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR(ابن عباس,سعد بن أبي وقاص,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعد بن أبي وقاص); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:6/q12/p2 He began repeating a verse or moving his tongue before Gabriel had finished, fearing he would lose it; the verse relieved his haste. — Thaʿlabī attributes it to Mujāhid and al-Kalbī; Wāḥidī adds Muqātil. Ibn ʿĀshūr rates the Ibn ʿAbbās account authentic and says the Prophet then recited exactly as Gabriel did. Iṣlāḥī adds that he wished for the whole revelation at once and was told revelation follows a wise timetable. Suyūṭī’s Ibn ʿAbbās chain contains Juwaybir, whom he rates very weak. [17 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد والكلبي); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد ومقاتل والكلبي); QURTUBI(مجاهد والكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد والكلبي); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); DURR(ابن عباس)]
- 87:6/q12/p3 Gabriel himself assured the Prophet amid fear of forgetting, with an account mentioning forgotten or lifted nonlegal verses and Moses’ tablets. — Durr transmits this wider narrative from Ibn ʿAbbās, mentioning 87:7. Ibn ʿAbbās’s report specifies that Moses lost four of thirteen scrolls when his emerald tablets broke. [2 notes: DURR(ابن عباس)]

## 87:6/q13 What reports illustrate a lapse or omission after the promise?  [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports imply temporary recall failure, withdrawn revelation, or no actual lapse.
- 87:6/q13/p1 He omitted a verse in prayer; Ubayy wondered whether it was abrogated, and the Prophet said he had forgotten it. — Bursevī notes his answer followed Ubayy’s question; ʿĀʾisha reports this incident in Ibn ʿĀshūr. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 87:6/q13/p2 A man’s night recitation reminded him of omitted verses. — Ibn ʿĀshūr transmits ʿĀʾisha’s account. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 87:6/q13/p3 ʿAbbād’s recitation reminded him of verses in a sūra. — Abū Ḥayyān cites this for a temporary lapse. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 87:6/q13/p4 A stoning verse and a warning against disowning one’s fathers had once been revealed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿUmar for material no longer recited. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(عمر)]
- 87:6/q13/p5 After this verse the Prophet forgot nothing. — Al-Kalbī is cited by Rāzī; Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted through Abū Ṣāliḥ by Qurṭubī, who says until death, apart from what God willed. ## 87:6/q14 How does this promise relate to the passages against hurrying revelation?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the passages address the same moment of haste or different duties of preserving and reviewing revelation.
- 87:6/q14/p1 It belongs with 75:16–19 and 20:114: the Prophet need not hurry his tongue because God gathers and preserves revelation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-Qiyāmah came later, so 87:6 is the earlier pledge; Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers the order 87:6, 75:16–19, then 20:114. Iṣlāḥī says revelation supplied support against opposition and answers to objections, explaining the eagerness. [22 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA(الحسن وقتادة ومالك ابن أنس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); RAZI(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد ومقاتل والكلبي); ABUHAYYAN(الحسن وقتادة ومالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وقتادة ومالك); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 87:6/q14/p2 It is distinct from 75:16: here the law and sūras are preserved while review remains the Prophet’s duty. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report this alternative to the direct identification with al-Qiyāmah. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA(آخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 87:6/q15 What sign or miracle does this promise provide?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sign lies in the extraordinary memory, the fulfilled advance promise, or both.
- 87:6/q15/p1 An unlettered Prophet retains an extensive revelation accurately without normal study, writing, or repetition. — Rāzī calls this beyond ordinary capacity. Ālūsī places it beside Qur’anic inimitability and reports of unseen events. Bursevī says he received knowledge without writing and became a source of knowledge about writing, rules and crafts. Kuran Yolu says the miracle continues in communal reading and memorization. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الحسن وقتادة ومالك ابن أنس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:6/q15/p2 A prediction made early in the Meccan period and later fulfilled is itself foreknowledge and a sign. — Rāzī stresses the early timing; Biqāʿī counts the unlettered reading and fulfilled prediction as two signs. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 87:6/q16 How does this verse advance the sūra’s argument and sequence?  [* · links]
turns on: How divine guidance, the opening command, and the preceding sūra prepare the pledge.
- 87:6/q16/p1 After general creation and guidance, God now gives the Messenger special guidance through revelation and his mission. — Biqāʿī says divine attributes and acts prepare for prophethood and an exemplary Messenger, while revelation revives spirits by knowledge as God revives bodies. Ibn ʿĀshūr links God’s proportioning and guidance with equipping the Prophet to retain revelation and receive an easy law. Iṣlāḥī sees the same gradual development in vegetation and prophecy. [15 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:6/q16/p2 The promise follows the call to glorify because complete glorification involves reciting revelation and eases the Prophet’s fear. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the opening command implicit glad tidings now made explicit for full delivery. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/q16/p3 Worldly attachments obstruct the special glorification this promise serves. — Bursevī compares worldly pleasure, food and drink to quickly withered debris and says attachment veils a person’s possible perfection. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 87:6/q16/p4 God’s knowledge of hidden and manifest supports the pledge and grounds the exception. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God knows the Prophet’s concern and obedience; the shift to 'He knows' points to divine power. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI; BURSEVI]
- 87:6/q16/p5 The sūra steadies the Prophet amid rejection with preservation, ease, a gracious law, and reminders. — Qutb names oneness, revelation and afterlife recompense and cites earlier scriptures at 87:18–19. Ibn ʿĀshūr says confirmation of earlier scriptures comforts him. Iṣlāḥī says this begins a run of sūras addressing his preaching fears. [6 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:6/q16/p6 The preceding sūra’s decisive revelation and respite for deniers lead into this sūra’s direct divine promise. — Abū Ḥayyān cites 86:13. Biqāʿī says al-Ṭāriq alluded generally to denials now detailed; Iṣlāḥī says persistent deniers cannot escape God after temporary respite. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:6/q16/p7 The sūra’s structure refutes deficient views of God before turning to the Messenger and obedience. — Biqāʿī names haste, incapacity for resurrection, purposeless creation, false speech and imitable speech among the deficiencies denied. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:6/q17 What broader scriptural or literary parallels are noted for this verse?  [* · links]
- 87:6/q17/p1 The imperative opening followed by a future first-person plural revelation promise parallels 73:2–5. — Corpus Coranicum also refers readers to 96:1 for qaraʾa. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:6/q17/p2 The sūra’s message belongs to a longer account of revelation, afterlife, and earlier scriptures. — Qutb cites 87:18–19; Ibn ʿĀshūr says confirmation of earlier scriptures comforts the Prophet. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/q17/p3 Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ reads this promise alongside 18:24 to argue that nansa'ha means defer or leave rather than cause the Prophet to forget. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports Saʿd's appeal to both verses for the Prophet's protection from forgetting. [1 notes: TABATABAI(سعد بن أبي وقاص)]

## 87:6/q18 What bearing does the promise have on collecting the Qur’an?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Why a codex was unnecessary for the Prophet yet needed for the community.
- 87:6/q18/p1 The Prophet did not need a collected codex because God protected his memory; later collectors faced possible loss. — Burhān adds the exception for revelation God willed to abrogate. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 87:6/q18/p2 Collection proceeded in stages using written fragments checked against reciters. — Al-Ḥākim distinguishes collection with the Prophet, under Abū Bakr, and ordering sūras under ʿUthmān. Al-Muḥāsibī says the Prophet ordered writing on separate materials; Abū Bakr bound copies. Burhān says the community knew its miraculous composition through twenty years’ recitation and Zayd verified fragments against memorizers, fearing loss rather than additions. [4 notes: BURHAN(الحاكم,المحاسبي)]

## 87:6/q19 What abrogation relation is claimed for 87:6?  [* · sciences]
- 87:6/q19/p1 The passage beginning “We sent before you” was abrogated by this verse. — Suyūṭī also describes the sūra’s opening as travel-revealed and other abrogation examples concerning fighting, a judgment verse and the sword verse. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 87:6/q20 How is the report about the Prophet repeating Gabriel’s revelation assessed?  [* · reports]
turns on: The distinct transmissions and the grading each cited source provides.
- 87:6/q20/p1 One Ibn ʿAbbās chain is very weak because it contains Juwaybir. — Suyūṭī gives this assessment of al-Ṭabarānī’s report that the Prophet began repeating before Gabriel finished. [1 notes: SUYUTI-LUBAB]
- 87:6/q20/p2 An Ibn ʿAbbās report about lip movement is authentic. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the report together with 75:16–18. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 87:6/q21 What other interpretive details accompany the verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 87:6/q21/p1 A phrase about pasture not becoming debris when humans or animals use it is reported as an alternative nearby explanation. — Qurṭubī places this beside the deferred-revelation explanation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:6/q21/p2 An unrelated example distinguishes kinds of causal conditional answers. — Burhān reports grammarians who distinguish a causally following answer from one replacing an unstated consequence. [1 notes: BURHAN(بعضهم)]
- 87:6/q21/p3 The Prophet loved al-Aʿlā and recited it at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes with al-Ghāshiya on the same day. — Qutb says the sūra is Meccan and has nineteen verses, citing 88:1. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
