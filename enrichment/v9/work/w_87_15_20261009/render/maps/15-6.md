# 15:6: 15 questions

## 15:6/q01 Who spoke these words to the Prophet, and what occasion is reported?  [وَقَالُوا۟ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِى · reports]
turns on: The sources differ in whether they identify named Quraysh men or explain the wording by what the idolaters heard him say; neither account excludes the other.
- 15:6/q01/p1 Meccan or Quraysh unbelievers addressed Muhammad; a report specifies Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, Nawfal ibn Khuwaylid, and al-Walid ibn al-Mughira. — Muqatil names the four men; Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, al-Alusi, and Elmalili transmit that identification. Ibn Atiyya names the first two and others like them. Al-Qurtubi says the speakers then asked him to bring angels as proof. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); ELMALILI(Mukātil); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 15:6/q01/p2 A report says Makkan idolaters heard Muhammad say that God had sent the Reminder down to him, prompting this wording. — Study Quran transmits the report as an explanation of their phrasing; it does not identify individual speakers. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 15:6/q02 What is the Reminder here, and why is it called dhikr?  [ٱلذِّكْرُ · meaning]
- 15:6/q02/p1 It is the Qur'an, God's revealed message, named for recitation and remembrance. — Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi from Ibn Abbas, al-Nasafi, al-Dahhak through al-Durr, al-Alusi, Elmalili, and Study Quran explicitly identify it as the Qur'an; al-Damghani and Kur'an Yolu render it revelation. Elmalili says it recalls divine admonition; Ibn Ashur says it is recited and repeated, reminds of God and the Last Day, and preserves remembrance for later generations (21:10; 43:44). Ibn Ashur calls this a comprehensive Qur'anic name previously unknown to Arabs and distinguishes Qur'an as naming intended recitation; both name discourse received and recited, like poetry, sermons, stories, and fables. Kur'an Yolu lists remembering, memorizing, mentioning, and praising as lexical senses, warning/admonition/revelation, previous scriptures, and the Qur'an as wider Qur'anic uses (16:43; 21:7,105; 15:9), and cites Tirmidhi, Merits of the Qur'an, report 14, for dhikr as a Qur'anic name. Study Quran notes other scriptures are dhikr elsewhere (16:43; 21:2,7; 40:53) but cites 15:9; 7:2; 16:44; 20:3; 36:11 for this referent. Ibn Ashur also gives the basic senses verbal utterance and recollection. [19 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 15:6/q03 How is the descent clause read?  [نُزِّلَ ٱلذِّكْرُ · readings]
turns on: The reports vary in verb form, voice, and whether the alternative 'cast down' wording is a reading or a gloss that differs from the mushaf text.
- 15:6/q03/p1 The common reading has nuzzila, with a doubled consonant and passive voice. — Al-Samin labels it the reading of the generality. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 15:6/q03/p2 Zayd ibn Ali reads nazala, light and active. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin report this form as Zayd's reading. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي)]
- 15:6/q03/p3 Zayd ibn Ali reads nazzala actively, with al-dhikr raised as its subject. — Al-Alusi reports this form; its doubled consonant differs from the light active form reported by Abu Hayyan and al-Samin. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]
- 15:6/q03/p4 The wording 'the one to whom the Reminder was cast down' is reported as a reading or explanatory gloss. — Ibn Atiyya attributes it to al-A'mash; Abu Hayyan attributes it to Zayd ibn Ali and treats it as explanatory because its consonants differ from the written mushaf. Al-Alusi reports Abu Hayyan's preferred treatment as a gloss for the same reason; the transmitters named for the wording vary. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]

## 15:6/q04 Whose words are the address, and what does its apparent admission of revelation mean?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِى نُزِّلَ عَلَيْهِ ٱلذِّكْرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the entire quoted sentence belongs to the mockers, or God supplies the vocative while they utter only the charge.
- 15:6/q04/p1 The whole address is the unbelievers' sarcastic speech: 'the one to whom the Reminder descended' means according to his or his followers' claim, not their belief. — Ibn Abbas through al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi reasons that believers in the descent could not simultaneously call him mad. Abu Ali al-Farisi supplies the implicit 'according to you/your followers' and compares sarcastic titles at 44:49 and 43:49; al-Wahidi's reports from Zahra al-Yaman and Jarir illustrate repeating an epithet from its bearer's own self-assessment. Al-Farisi says Arabic can voice an alleged claim with this understood qualification. Ibn Atiyya compares calling an ignorant person a scholar unable to perform ablution. Al-Razi cites Pharaoh and Shuayb's people (26:27; 11:87), plus ironic good news (3:21); al-Nasafi cites the same parallels and calls the wording a deliberate reversal. Ibn Ashur says the vocative publicly spotlights the description, the relative clause makes claimed revelation the ground of mockery, and such a title follows its referent's claim rather than the speaker's belief. Ibn Kathir adds 'merely claims' revelation. Al-Alusi favors the related explanation that the extraordinary claim itself supplies their supposed evidence of madness. [30 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,ابن عباس عن عطاء,جرير,زهرة اليمن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabrisī); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 15:6/q04/p2 Only 'you are mad' is the unbelievers' speech; God supplies the address to clear the Prophet of the accusation. — Al-Alusi reports this from some interpreters but objects that it fits the next verse's rebuttal poorly, while allowing that the next verse could answer the unbelievers' implication. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 15:6/q05 What does the passive wording of 'sent down' imply?  [نُزِّلَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Al-Alusi gives two possible targets for the passive's denial: the claimed descent's agent or its occurrence upon Muhammad.
- 15:6/q05/p1 It distances the mockers from accepting revelation's divine source, as if the descent has no acknowledged agent. — Al-Biqai says their passive phrasing reflects denial of revelation itself; al-Alusi says it can suggest the claimed descent has no agent. Tabatabai says passive voice distances them from its alleged source, alongside the derisive title and charge. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 15:6/q05/p2 It directs denial to the claim that the Reminder descended upon Muhammad, rather than to identifying its sender. — Al-Alusi explicitly offers this as another implication of the passive. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 15:6/q06 Why is 'upon him' brought forward in the descent clause?  [عَلَيْهِ · rhetoric]
- 15:6/q06/p1 The order highlights their denial that a Reminder descends from God, whereas 43:31 challenges the chosen recipient after accepting the source of revelation. — Al-Alusi contrasts this wording with 43:31 to distinguish the objections. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 15:6/q07 What does majnun mean in this accusation?  [لَمَجْنُونٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether majnun denotes ordinary madness or a connection to jinn in the opponents' usage.
- 15:6/q07/p1 It is the ordinary accusation of madness; its root denotes covering or being covered. — Al-Wahidi gives verb forms for becoming or being made mad and explains dense, intertwined plants called majnun because their parts cover one another. The many reports of their calling Muhammad mad use this ordinary sense. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 15:6/q07/p2 In pagan usage it may mean a person connected with jinn, rather than simply an insane person. — Kur'an Yolu reports Ateş's proposed sense, alongside its account of pagan belief that jinn inspire poets. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ateş)]

## 15:6/q08 Was the charge of madness intended literally or as a taunt?  [إِنَّكَ لَمَجْنُونٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the opponents meant an actual diagnosis or used madness as figurative abuse.
- 15:6/q08/p1 It could be a metaphorical or derisive charge. — Kur'an Yolu reports commentators who allow metaphorical mockery; other commentators describe the whole address and accusation as sarcastic or derisive. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 15:6/q08/p2 They could have literally believed he was mad. — Kur'an Yolu reports this possibility from commentators; the faint-like state during revelation and the implausibility they perceived in his prophetic claim are given elsewhere as possible grounds. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 15:6/q09 What made the opponents call Muhammad mad?  [إِنَّكَ لَمَجْنُونٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: The proposed grounds differ: the apparent impossibility of the message, Muhammad's state during revelation, or a belief that jinn inspired what they called poetry.
- 15:6/q09/p1 They judged his claim of divine revelation and prophethood, and the teaching based on it, too implausible to be sane. — Al-Razi says they considered a true messenger from God impossible and his teaching beyond reason. Al-Baydawi says the claim of God's sending the Qur'an sounded like a madman's speech; al-Nasafi says the same. Ibn Kathir specifies his call to follow him and abandon inherited religion. Al-Biqai says they alleged a jinn confused him because he claimed God sent a Reminder. Al-Alusi prefers this explanation as best suited to the context, comparing someone deemed mad for an incredible assertion. Elmalili says they rejected his warnings and prophethood as irrational; Kur'an Yolu transmits al-Razi's version. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 15:6/q09/p2 His faint-like or absorbed state during revelation was mistaken for madness or jinn possession. — Al-Razi mentions other verses denying the charge (68:51–52; 7:184). Al-Alusi reports this explanation. Elmalili says they cited his absorbed state as supposed evidence; Kur'an Yolu transmits al-Razi's near-trance and jinn-possession formulation. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 15:6/q09/p3 They treated the Qur'an as poetry inspired by jinn rather than revelation from God. — Kur'an Yolu says pre-Islamic pagans thought poets received verse from jinn and applies that assumption to Muhammad's contemporaries, citing 21:5 and 37:36. Ateş's proposed sense of majnun as jinn-connected fits this conception. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ateş)]

## 15:6/q10 How does this saying develop the preceding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The links emphasize different earlier material: warnings and objections or the command in 15:3.
- 15:6/q10/p1 It presents the unbelievers' objections after threats and warnings, with insult taking the place of an answer. — Al-Razi says God turns from emphasizing threats to their objections to Muhammad's claim. Al-Jishumi says they answer the warning with mockery. Al-Biqai says the accusation substitutes for an answer to the preceding argument and exposes stubbornness (15:4–5). Al-Alusi says the passage depicts the Meccans' rejection of the bearer and the Book and the outcome awaiting them. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 15:6/q10/p2 It is conjoined to the command to leave them in 15:3, depicting deeper disbelief and denial beyond their indulgence and hopes. — Ibn Ashur reads 15:6 as continuing 15:3: leave them to disbelieve and utter every kind of denial and mockery. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 15:6/q11 How does the next verse respond to this accusation?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the following verse is read chiefly as the opponents' further demand or as a rebuttal relevant to the proposed division of speech.
- 15:6/q11/p1 After calling him mad, the opponents demand angels as evidence of his truthfulness. — Al-Qurtubi recounts their move from the insult in 15:6 to the angel demand in 15:7. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 15:6/q11/p2 The next verse rebuts the unbelievers' implied claim even if God supplied the vocative in this verse. — Al-Alusi allows that 15:7 could answer the implication but says it fits poorly if only 'you are mad' is their speech. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 15:6/q12 How is this taunt paralleled or answered elsewhere in the Qur'an?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited verses serve different roles: earlier parallels to the taunt and direct denials of the charge.
- 15:6/q12/p1 Pharaoh and other peoples similarly charged messengers with madness or used praise ironically. — Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, and al-Alusi compare Pharaoh's address to Moses (26:27). Al-Razi adds Shuayb's people's ironic praise (11:87) and ironic glad tidings of punishment (3:21); al-Nasafi cites those comparisons too. Elmalili invokes Pharaoh's accusation. Study Quran says possession was charged against Moses, Noah, and Muhammad and cites 23:70; 34:8; 44:14; 51:39,52; 68:51; 26:27; 54:9. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء)]
- 15:6/q12/p2 Other verses explicitly deny that Muhammad is mad. — Abu Ali al-Farisi points to the answer in 68:2, 'By your Lord's blessing, you are not mad.' Al-Razi cites 68:51–52 and 7:184 against the accusation. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي الفارسي); RAZI-FULL]

## 15:6/q13 What broader pattern or purpose does this mockery illustrate?  [* · interpretation]
- 15:6/q13/p1 The opponents repeat the mockery of earlier messengers and face their predecessors' fate; the sura consoles Muhammad while warning and offering hope. — Islahi states the repeated pattern and shared fate. Tabatabai says the sura addresses their mockery, dismissal of the Qur'an as mad rambling, and counsels patience and forbearance with warning and glad tidings. Ibn Ashur says they are to be left to their denial and mockery; al-Alusi frames the passage as rejection of both the messenger and Book with a coming outcome. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 15:6/q14 What report illustrates how the Prophet answered the polytheists' abuse?  [* · reports]
- 15:6/q14/p1 He told Aisha that God diverted their abuse because they insulted 'Mudhammam' while he was Muhammad. — Ibn Ashur reports the Prophet's statement to Aisha as an example of the abuse directed at him. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 15:6/q15 What happened when al-Hubab asked whether the Prophet's Badr position was revealed or tactical?  [* · reports]
- 15:6/q15/p1 The Prophet said it was strategy; al-Hubab advised securing the nearest water, blocking wells behind it, and filling a basin, and the Prophet approved and acted on the advice. — Ibn Hisham transmits al-Hubab ibn al-Mundhir's exchange; the report distinguishes a divinely fixed position from tactical judgment. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(الحباب بن المنذر بن الجموح)]
