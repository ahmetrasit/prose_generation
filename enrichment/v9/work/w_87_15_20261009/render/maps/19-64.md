# 19:64: 18 questions

## 19:64/q01 Who speaks the opening words?  [وَمَا نَتَنَزَّلُ · referent]
turns on: The occasion, singular address and connection with the preceding Paradise passage.
- 19:64/q01/p1 Gabriel speaks for himself and the angels to Muhammad. — Qatada, Mujahid and Ibn Abbas identify Gabriel after his delay. Razi cites the plural voice and singular addressee. Qurtubi, Tabrisī, Kuranyolu and Ibn Ashur say God directed Gabriel to give the answer; the latter supplies an implicit 'say'. Elmalili links Gabriel to the earlier Maryam story and its repeated taqi ending. Tabatabai compares a king's envoy: Gabriel's explanation remains God's message. [28 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(النبي); MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin genel kabulü); ELMALILI+; TABATABAI+]
- 19:64/q01/p2 The inhabitants of Paradise speak as they enter or settle there by God's command. — Ibn Bahr and Abu Muslim connect this to the prior Paradise description. Kashshaf says grace admits the pious as recompense for deeds. Samīn judges it closer than an omitted speech clause. Qadi objects that descent and a singular addressee fit Gabriel; Alusi and Tabatabai cite context, occasion and rabbuka against it. [15 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم وابن بحر,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم، ابن بحر); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو مسلم وصاحب الكشاف); STUDYQURAN(minority interpretation); SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي)-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 19:64/q01/p3 It continues the messenger's earlier words to Mary at 19:19. — Al-Naqqash's suggestion is called weak by Ibn Atiyya, remote by Alusi, and contextually disfavored by Tabatabai. [0 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش)-; SAMIN-DURR(النقاش)-; ALUSI-FULL(النقاش)-; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]

## 19:64/q02 What event led to this verse?  [وَمَا نَتَنَزَّلُ · reports]
turns on: The reports present different conversations and causes for Gabriel's delay.
- 19:64/q02/p1 Gabriel's visit was delayed; Muhammad missed him and asked why he did not come more often. — Ibn Abbas's exchange appears in Ahmad, Bukhari and Tirmidhi; Tirmidhi calls it hasan gharib and Ibn Ashur judges it the best-fitting report. Qatada, Mujahid and others describe the Prophet's distress and mutual longing. Ibn Hisham relates the return to al-Kahf's opening. Alusi allows repeated revelation or two encounters. [58 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); MUSNAD-AHMAD(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI(النبي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,جبريل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك وقتادة والسدي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عكرمة,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عكرمة,مجاهد); NASHR(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(Resûlullah); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); BUKHARI(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Abdullah b. - 19:64/q02/p2 The pause followed his promise to answer questions about the Cave, Dhul-Qarnayn and the Spirit without saying 'if God wills'. — Some reports say Quraysh consulted Jews. 18:23–24 teaches the qualification; pagans then claimed abandonment, linking 93:1–3. Biqai says the ordered answers across al-Kahf, al-Isra and Maryam show divine revelation: a human author could have answered the next day. - 19:64/q02/p3 An angel cited neglected grooming as the reason he stayed away. — Mujahid names nails, moustaches, knuckles and tooth-sticks. Ibn Anbari defines finger folds; Ibn Kathir gives an Ibn Abbas version. Bursevi cites washing joints and Qurtubi's point that hardened dirt can retain impurity and block water. [9 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,مجاهد)]
- 19:64/q02/p4 He delayed while Muhammad sought an answer about God's most loved and hated places. — The Prophet said he did not know until asking Gabriel; the delay made him fear displeasure. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 19:64/q02/p5 In Mecca, Khadija suggested God had abandoned the grieved Prophet during Gabriel's absence. — Al-Suddi links Gabriel's return with 93:2 and this verse. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(السدي)]

## 19:64/q03 How long did the delay last?  [وَمَا نَتَنَزَّلُ · reports]
turns on: The numbers vary in the reports.
- 19:64/q03/p1 Forty days or mornings. — Ikrima and Muqatil are cited. Ibn Kathir calls Ikrima's report unusual. A distinct report about a persistent beggar and al-Duha says forty mornings, but Nashr calls it isolated to Ibn Abi Bazzah and mu'dal. [12 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة وعكرمة والضحاك ومقاتل والكلبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); NASHR; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI(بعض المفسرين); STUDYQURAN]
- 19:64/q03/p2 Twelve nights. — Mujahid is named; the Prophet says the absence gave pagans room to speculate. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة وعكرمة والضحاك ومقاتل والكلبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN]

## 19:64/q04 What does tanazzul mean here?  [نَتَنَزَّلُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the form entails repeated descent.
- 19:64/q04/p1 Gradual or repeated descent at intervals. — Zamakhshari cites a poem and prefers repetition in context; Razi agrees. Biqai contrasts honored repeated descent with sudden punishment. Samīn notes the reflexive of form II. Bursevi and Tabatabai connect the interval to divine command. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 19:64/q04/p2 Descent generally, without necessary gradualness. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi cite poetic ordinary descent. Ibn Ashur separately suggests the form evokes exertion because leaving the angelic realm is unusual, comparing 97:4. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:64/q05 How is the opening clause read?  [نَتَنَزَّلُ بِأَمْرِ رَبِّكَ · readings]
turns on: Person and subject of the verb, and variant wording.
- 19:64/q05/p1 The majority read first-person plural nun: 'we descend'. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Samīn say Gabriel speaks for the angels. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]
- 19:64/q05/p2 Al-A'raj and others read third-person ya, with Gabriel as subject. — Ibn al-Sumayfi and Ibn Ya'mur have open ya; another account has al-A'raj with damma. Some make this God's report. Ibn Atiyya objects that the following first-person pronouns need quoted Gabriel speech. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعرج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السميفع وابن يعمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 19:64/q05/p3 In the ya reading, revelation or the Qur'an is the subject within Gabriel's speech. — Zamakhshari and Alusi assign the pronoun to revelation. Ibn Atiyya prefers God's appointed times for its descent; Samīn says an implicit 'saying' is required before the next words. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الأعرج); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 19:64/q05/p4 Ibn Masud reads 'except by your Lord's word' in place of 'command'. — The transmitted form is illa bi-qawli rabbik. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 19:64/q05/p5 Ibn Masud reads the ending as 'and your Lord has not forgotten you'. — The variant makes the reassurance to Muhammad explicit. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]

## 19:64/q06 What does descent only by God's command teach?  [إِلَّا بِأَمْرِ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether descent is outward visitation, Paradise entry or spiritual reception.
- 19:64/q06/p1 Angels have no independent power to descend or act; God sends them when wisdom requires. — Gabriel says he comes when sent. Qushayri lists aid, rescue, punishment and support as commissioned tasks. Razi denies any right to object to God's governance. Islahi says Muhammad should wait steadfastly for support, like a soldier for guidance; the point rebuts worship of angels as divine daughters and hopes for their intercession. Ibn Ashur compares 21:27; Tabatabai 66:6. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(النبي); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 19:64/q06/p2 On the Paradise reading, the pious enter or settle there only by God's grace and reward. — Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan and Alusi make this the meaning of the command. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم، ابن بحر)]
- 19:64/q06/p3 In an inward reading, angelic descent requires innate purity and durable acquired readiness. — Qashani says purification connects the soul to the higher assembly; 41:30 links angelic descent to steadfastness, and 26:222 shows enduring fitness for demonic descent. Combining both kinds of readiness signifies divine permission; emanation is continuous, so delay reflects absent readiness. [4 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:64/q07 What do the three regions before, behind and between designate?  [مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِينَا وَمَا خَلْفَنَا وَمَا بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: Time or space, its direction and bounds, and whether angels are described by human temporal categories.
- 19:64/q07/p1 The coming Hereafter is before, the past world behind, and the intervening time extends to Resurrection. — Tabari prefers future-before as dominant Arabic usage. Zajjaj specifies future reward and punishment. Abu Ishaq objects that the blasts have not yet been mentioned. Ibn Atiyya finds the reversed temporal mapping incoherent for angels. [24 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير,سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وقتادة ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(العوفي عن ابن عباس وابن جبير وقتادة ومقاتل وسفيان); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(العوفي عن ابن عباس، ابن جبير، قتادة، مقاتل، سفيان); TABATABAI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير والضحاك وقتادة وابن جريج والثوري)+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 19:64/q07/p2 The Hereafter is before and the world behind; the interval between the two trumpet blasts lies between. — Muqatil and Farra say the interval is forty years. Other reporters give the two-blast interval without a number. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الكلبي); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(جبريل); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وعكرمة وأبو العالية); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة ومقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وعكرمة وأبو العالية); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,قتادة); TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك والربيع,مقاتل)]
- 19:64/q07/p3 This world is before and the Hereafter behind; the two-blast interval lies between. — Farra, Rabi' and Abu al-Aliya use this order. Abu al-Aliya specifies the world until the first blast and the afterlife from resurrection. Ibn Atiyya objects it suits humans better than angels, though he says 'before' usually means earlier in time, like Torah before the Qur'an. [12 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,الربيع بن أنس); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية وعكرمة ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة والسدي والربيع بن أنس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية); TABATABAI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 19:64/q07/p4 Past worldly events are before, future worldly events and the Hereafter behind, with intervening time or the present between. — Ibn Jurayj spans both worlds; Abu Ali gives past, future and present. Ibn Jawzi says front and rear can be metaphorical. Samīn cites Zuhayr on yesterday, today and tomorrow; Burhan and Itqan say the three exhaust time. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جرير); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جريج); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 19:64/q07/p5 Before creation is before, after death or extinction behind, and lifetime or existence between. — Akhfash and Basran grammarians use creation and extinction as bounds. Ibn Jurayj extends the latter into everlasting afterlife. The middle varies as existence, earthly life or the passage from world to Hereafter. [19 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية من أهل البصرة); NAHHAS; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن القشيري,ابن جريج,الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج,الأخفش); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 19:64/q07/p6 Remaining lifetime or world-time is before, elapsed lifetime behind, and the present between. — Zamakhshari, Razi, Qurtubi and Tabatabai record this division; Baghawi also has future and past worldly life with the span of life between. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 19:64/q07/p7 After death is before, before creation behind, and life between. — Baghawi reports this reverse arrangement. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 19:64/q07/p8 All directions and locations surrounding angels, including destination, origin and current station. — Kashshaf says no movement through the King's realm occurs without leave. Ibn Atiyya specifies workspaces. Biqai includes above and below. Ibn Ashur reads four directions and extends the spatial image to beings, actions and times. [15 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري,صاحب الغينان); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 19:64/q07/p9 Heaven is ahead, earth behind, and the space between them in the middle. — Some say sky, earth and intervening air; Tabatabai lists the spatial reading. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب الغينان); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 19:64/q07/p10 Earth, the descent destination, is ahead; heaven, the origin, behind; air lies between. — Ibn Abbas through Ata and Baghawi use this direction. Wahidi's brief version says world and heavens; Tabatabai notes the reversal. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 19:64/q07/p11 The formula encompasses all times and places without specifying each region. — Yahya makes the middle worldly and otherworldly affairs. Kashshaf, Razi and Alusi generalize to divine dominion and knowledge. Asad says angels glimpse these regions without full comprehension, comparing 2:255. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(البغوي); ALUSI-FULL(البغوي); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ASAD-NOTES]
- 19:64/q07/p12 It encompasses angels' prior causes, their existence and their effects. — Tabatabai rejects eleven temporal and spatial mappings as unevidenced human categories projected onto angels; complete divine ownership explains their obedience. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 19:64/q07/p13 Inwardly, these are higher Power, lower Dominion and the intermediate stages where we dwell. — Qashani places all under divine mastery. The Najmiyya reading instead names eternal decree, everlasting governance and the span between pre-eternity and eternity. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 19:64/q07/p14 On the Paradise reading, they cover future Paradise, past world and the interval between. — Abu Muslim applies the temporal language to the pious entering Paradise. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم وابن بحر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم، ابن بحر)]

## 19:64/q08 What does 'to Him belongs' establish?  [لَهُۥ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِينَا وَمَا خَلْفَنَا وَمَا بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Sovereignty over actions or encompassing knowledge; several sources combine them.
- 19:64/q08/p1 God owns and governs every region and time, so angels act only at His command. — Ibn Ashur calls the lam ownership of governance and extends places metonymically to their contents. Tabatabai sees ownership as the reason for complete angelic obedience. Samarrai contrasts Maryam's ownership wording with 2:255's emphasis on knowledge. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 19:64/q08/p2 It especially declares God's exhaustive knowledge and preservation of affairs. — Baghawi glosses 'belongs' as 'knows'; Zajjaj and Nasafi link past, present and future knowledge to no forgetfulness; Bursevi compares 2:255. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(البغوي); ALUSI-FULL(البغوي); BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:64/q09 Why is 'that' singular?  [ذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
- 19:64/q09/p1 Arabic allows a singular demonstrative to refer back to two or more items together. — Ibn Anbari gives the rule; Qurtubi cites Zamakhshari and compares 2:68. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 19:64/q10 What does the denial of forgetfulness mean?  [وَمَا كَانَ رَبُّكَ نَسِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Literal divine non-forgetfulness, contextual non-abandonment, or Paradise recompense.
- 19:64/q10/p1 God has not forgotten or abandoned Muhammad; delayed revelation comes at the wise time. — Mujahid and Suddī identify the Prophet; 93:3 answers pagan claims. Alusi prefers non-abandonment as fitting the occasion, though others defend the literal sense. Ibn Atiyya says not to press for visits or fear pagans' delight. Biqai says neither the Spirit nor Muhammad's needs escape God; Ibn Ashur says the intensive form may answer a long delay. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); DURR-FULL(السدي); NASHR(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 19:64/q10/p2 God literally never forgets; His knowledge and governance are complete. — Zajjaj says He preserves every time; Nasafi includes every motion and stillness. Ibn Atiyya rejects 'abandons' as lexical sense: God can leave in other contexts, but forgetfulness is denied absolutely. Tabatabai says the clause completes the ownership rationale. Al-Rida treats divine 'forgetting' elsewhere as recompense for people neglecting God, citing 39:19 and 45:34. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام)]
- 19:64/q10/p3 God does not forget the deeds or promised reward of Paradise's righteous people. — Kashshaf and Alusi follow the alternative speakers; Abu Muslim takes the phrase as God's assurance of resurrection. Ibn Ashur permits a speaker addressing another or a divine coda. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم، ابن بحر); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:64/q10/p4 God withholds no due perfection; delayed revelation shows the recipient's readiness was lacking. — Qashani says He knows every capacity and grants completion when obtained. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:64/q11 What does the intensive nasiyyan add?  [نَسِيًّۭا · grammar]
turns on: Rhetorical intensity versus the logical scope of negating an intensive form.
- 19:64/q11/p1 It emphatically denies any divine forgetfulness. — Biqai says even occasional lapses are excluded, since a servant could leave unnoticed only under a master prone to prolonged lapse. Burhan describes fa'il as intensive or recurrent, contrasting qatil and jarih; Itqan catalogs nasiyyan as an intensive form. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:64/q11/p2 Denial of an intensive form alone does not logically deny the simple act, raising a question here. — Burhan raises the problem and cites end-rhyme suitability; Itqan calls nasiy and zalam apparent counterexamples. Alusi questions whether intensity favors the literal reading. [5 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:64/q11/p3 The negation remains valid even when the denied attribute is impossible for its subject. — Burhan rejects a requirement of possible attribution and a Hanafi inference that prohibition implies validity, citing 2:255 and 6:14. Itqan distinguishes impossible attributes from possible nonoccurrence. Burhan distinguishes truthful nafy from false jahd, though some equate them; he says negation targets what follows its particle and calls it half of speech. [7 notes: BURHAN(ابن الشجري,بعض العلماء); ITQAN(بعضهم)]

## 19:64/q12 What rule is drawn from matters Scripture leaves unmentioned?  [وَمَا كَانَ رَبُّكَ نَسِيًّۭا · law]
- 19:64/q12/p1 What God declares lawful or forbidden is so; unmentioned matters are pardoned, since His silence is no lapse. — Abu al-Darda reports the Prophetic saying and verse; Durr gives another through Jabir and an application to butter, cheese and fur. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الدرداء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 19:64/q13 How is this verse joined to the surrounding passage?  [وَمَا نَتَنَزَّلُ · links]
turns on: Fresh divinely placed speech or continuation of the Paradise passage.
- 19:64/q13/p1 It is a new answer placed after the preceding stories, with a contextual or implied speech cue. — Ibn Atiyya says waw joins unlike statements; Abu al-Baqa supplies 'angels say'. Razi compares speaker transitions at 2:117 and 3:51. Elmalili calls waw resumptive. Ibn Ashur says God had Gabriel speak where revelation had reached. Tabatabai says the envoy's explanation is a divine message and prefers that to weak occasion variants; Alusi considers a supplied introduction unnecessary. - 19:64/q13/p4 It answers hostile demands after the prophets' stories and prepares the command to worship. — Abu Hayyan connects 19:59 and 2:109 to hostile questioning. ## 19:64/q14 Why the repeated address 'your Lord'?  [رَبِّكَ رَبُّكَ · rhetoric]
- 19:64/q14/p1 It honors and reassures Muhammad of divine care and prepares the call to worship the one Lord. — Biqai says both arrival and delay belong to the beneficent Lord. Alusi says repetition signals care and gives the rationale; the following 'Lord of heavens and earth' is an omitted-subject predicate or apposition. Tabatabai says Gabriel's Lord is Muhammad's Lord, leading to 19:65. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 19:64/q15 How does the verse relate to al-Duha and other passages?  [* · links]
- 19:64/q15/p1 Its reassurance parallels al-Duha's denial that God forsook Muhammad after a pause. — Mujahid compares 93:3; some reports connect both revelations to one delay. Nashr's al-Dani explains Meccan takbir after al-Duha by that pause, while others followed the Prophet's later abandonment of it. Another forty-morning beggar report is mu'dal and isolated. ## 19:64/q16 What wider claims about revelation and verse order appear with this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 19:64/q16/p1 The Prophet fixed verse order as revealed, including answers to questions; the transmitted arrangement supports the Quran's inimitability. — Ibn Ashur cites memorization, scribal writing, Zayd's compilation, companion agreement, Malik and Ibn al-Anbari. Connection may be thematic or marked by particles without proving revelation order. A teacher advises testing whether adjacent verses complete each other; an insertion elsewhere resembles 19:64. [12 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الأنباري,بعض مشايخنا المحققين,مالك)]
- 19:64/q16/p2 The ordered sound patterns of sura-opening letters disclose divine knowledge beyond ordinary composition. — Baqillani says the openings balance classifications of Arabic letters, regardless of whether language is instituted or conventional. Alif-lam-mim spans articulation from farthest to lips. [4 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 19:64/q18 What further reported comparisons refine the before, behind and between language?  [مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِينَا وَمَا خَلْفَنَا وَمَا بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ · referent]

- 19:64/q18/p1 The Hereafter is before, the world behind, and the interval between blasts lies between. — Kashshaf specifies past worldly affairs, future Hereafter affairs and a forty-year interval; Tabari transmits Qatada's orientation. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TAB-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 19:64/q18/p2 This world is before and the Hereafter behind. — Yahya's Suddī says what lies behind the angels is worldly affairs from the vantage of the Hereafter.
