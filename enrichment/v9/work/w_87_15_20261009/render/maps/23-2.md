# 23:2: 29 questions

## 23:2/q01 How does this relative clause describe the believers?  [ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the listed attributes delimit believers or describe the fruit and grounds of faith.
- 23:2/q01/p1 It is an adjectival relative clause qualifying the believers. — Al-Naḥḥās makes الذين nominative as an adjective of المؤمنون and analyzes هم as subject and خاشعون as predicate within the relative clause; following clauses are parallel. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 23:2/q01/p2 The repeated relative clauses present rooted traits as grounds for success. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says each trait evidences rooted faith and is a cause of success, without exhausting all piety; he compares the condemned who failed in prayer, feeding the poor and belief in Judgment (74:42–46). Jishumī says the traits clarify who is a believer; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says living faith produces effects and success. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI; TABATABAI]
- 23:2/q01/p3 The traits restrict true believers, or clarify and praise them. — Ālūsī reports both analyses: restriction if the attributes distinguish true believers, or clarification and praise if they are branches of faith; he says some reports support restriction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:2/q02 How is ṣalāt read here?  [صَلَاتِهِمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether ṣalāt is taken as a verbal noun or a name for distinct prayers.
- 23:2/q02/p1 Most readers use the singular form. — Ṭabrisī reports the non-Kufan reading and ʿĀṣim's as singular. Abū ʿAlī treats it as a verbal noun. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل الكوفة غير عاصم)]
- 23:2/q02/p2 Kufan readers except ʿĀṣim use the plural form. — Ṭabrisī reports this reading. Abū ʿAlī explains the plural as naming distinct kinds of prescribed prayer, strengthened by its technical legal sense and additions to the original language. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل الكوفة غير عاصم)]

## 23:2/q03 Why is prayer singular here and plural at the passage's end?  [صَلَاتِهِمْ · rhetoric]
- 23:2/q03/p1 The singular denotes prayer as a genus; the plural denotes the several prayers to be guarded. — Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn, specifies five prayers, witr and regular sunnas or other optional services. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says humility applies uniformly to prayer as a kind, while maintenance concerns each obligatory prayer. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); TABATABAI]

## 23:2/q04 Why is prayer called 'their prayer'?  [صَلَاتِهِمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the possessive stresses benefit or the believers' close connection with prayer.
- 23:2/q04/p1 The worshippers own its benefit, while God needs nothing from it. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls prayer their provision and treasure; al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Ālūsī repeat the benefit contrast. Al-Biqāʿī says the ascription encourages guarding prayer as the bond between them and God. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 23:2/q04/p2 The possessive conveys close attachment to prayer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts this close relation with merely saying they are humble whenever they pray. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:2/q05 Why is 'in their prayer' placed before 'humble'?  [فِى صَلَاتِهِمْ خَٰشِعُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which force of the fronted phrase is intended: emphasis, connection, or restriction.
- 23:2/q05/p1 It emphasizes prayer and suits the verse-end pattern. — Al-Samīn cites emphasis and the end-rhyme shared with parallel verses. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the order gives care and priority to prayer as Islam's most important pillar. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:2/q05/p2 It connects prayer tightly to the believers and their faith. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording attaches humility closely to their own prayer. Ālūsī reports that prayer is brought near faith because prayer is itself called faith at 2:143. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:2/q05/p3 It restricts the claim to all of their prayers. — Ālūsī reports this as another explanation, against humility in only some prayers. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:2/q06 What does 'humble' convey as an enduring description?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 23:2/q06/p1 The nominal wording makes humility a lasting trait through prayer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts a verbal expression and notes its concise force; al-Sāmarrāʾī says it should persist in heart and limbs throughout prayer. He also reads 22:77–78's commands as stable believer qualities here. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:2/q07 What does khushūʿ mean in this prayer?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · meaning]

- 23:2/q07/p1 Humble submission and reverence before God. — Muqātil describes submission so complete one misses neighbors; Ṭabarī includes obedient performance of prescribed prayer, still limbs and avoiding forbidden things and refuses restriction to one sign. Zajjāj cites subdued voices at 20:108 for the root. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, Ibn ʿĪsā and later exegetes gloss humility and abasement. Islāhī adduces 20:108 and 57:16 for inward submission expressed in posture, recitation and prostration; Kur'an Yolu cites Ṭabarī's obedience and respect. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رباح); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(المفردات); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 23:2/q07/p2 Fear and awe within the heart. — Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda recur in early transmissions; Ibn ʿAbbās combines fear with stillness. Al-Kāshshāf and Bayḍāwī add the lowered gaze; Bursawī adds humility and gaze fixed on prayer. The Qurʾān Yolu translation is deep reverence. [26 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,الحسن); ZAJJAJ(الحسن وقتادة); THALABI(الحسن وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(bazıları); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

Al-Wāḥidī makes stillness the Arabic root sense and reduces lowering the eyes and person to it. Ibn al-Jawzī cites 41:39. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says bodily lowering shows a fearful heart. Al-Sāmarrāʾī adds abstention from unnecessary movement and distractions. [22 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الزهري); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,الزهري,مجاهد); THALABI(عمرو بن دينار); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزهري); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(عمرو بن دينار); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وإبراهيم والزهري); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمرو بن دينار); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم,الزهري); ITQAN; ELMALILI(bazıları); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a heart state that can exist outside prayer; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls attribution to limbs figurative and cites humbled voices at 20:108. Al-Mufradāt instead says khushūʿ usually shows in limbs and khudūʿ is more often inward. [10 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); JISHUMI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب، إبراهيم النخعي); QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI(bazıları); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI(المفردات)]

- 23:2/q07/p8 Heart and limbs together, with inner fear appearing outwardly. — Al-Māwardī reports heart and sight; al-Jishumī prefers outward submission with inward reverence. Al-Baghawī distinguishes khushūʿ in heart, body, sight and voice from merely bodily khudūʿ, while al-Layth locates it in sight and voice and calls soft ground khashʿa. Al-Rāzī and Elmalılı prefer inward surrender expressed by still lowered eyes; al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say the heart governs limbs. Al-Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Islāhī and al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise combine them. - 23:2/q07/p9 Exclusive inward attention to prayer and God. — Some define it as gathering one's full attention and turning away from everything else. Ibn Kathīr and al-Biqāʿī say emptying the heart and preferring prayer makes it rest and delight. Ṭabāṭabāʾī pictures a subdued person entirely occupied with an overpowering presence. Al-Qāshānī describes awe at the light of majesty. Ibn ʿĀshūr says prayer especially stirs the heart because it is intimate address. [9 notes: THALABI(بعضهم); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن كثير); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:2/q07/p10 Placing the right hand over the left. — Qatāda gives this concrete interpretation in al-Thaʿlabī; al-Ḍaḥḥāk is cited for it by Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī. [3 notes: THALABI(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 23:2/q07/p11 Repentance. — Al-Māwardī attributes the gloss to Ibrāhīm. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(إبراهيم)]
- 23:2/q07/p12 Reverence, sincere speech, certainty and focused aspiration. — The saying continued by al-Thaʿlabī gives these four conditions; Abū al-Dardāʾ is named in al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī. The position concerns reverence for the standing place and full collected attention. [4 notes: THALABI; NASAFI-FULL(أبو الدرداء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الدرداء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]
- 23:2/q07/p13 Sincere prayer for God without seeking a return. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports Abū Bakr al-Wāsiṭī's gloss. [1 notes: THALABI(أبو بكر الواسطي)]

## 23:2/q08 What happened to worshippers' gaze when this verse was revealed?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · reports]
turns on: The reports vary over the subject, earlier direction of gaze and certainty of the occasion.
- 23:2/q08/p1 The Prophet had looked skyward, then lowered his gaze to his prayer or prostration place. — Ibn Sīrīn and Abū Hurayra transmit the widely repeated account. Versions say he lowered his head or looked at the ground; al-Ḥākim authenticated Abū Hurayra's report. Ibn Kathīr calls Ibn Sīrīn and ʿAṭāʾ's reports mursal; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Kāshshāf state no grading. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن سيرين); ZAJJAJ(النبي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-ASBAB(أبو هريرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن سيرين وغيره); TABRISI(النبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو هريرة); RAZI-FULL(الحسن وابن سيرين); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن سيرين); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رباح); DURR-FULL(النبي ﷺ,محمد بن سيرين); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو هريرة، ابن سيرين); BURSEVI(النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ELMALILI(Hasan ve İbn Sîrîn); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); STUDYQURAN(R and Ṭ)]
- 23:2/q08/p2 The Prophet had looked around or right and left, then bowed his head and looked down. — A narrator other than ʿAṭāʾ and versions from Ibn Sīrīn mention looking around; one says he looked at something while praying and raised his gaze. Some accounts combine turning and upward gaze. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(غير عطاء); THALABI; DURR-FULL(محمد بن سيرين); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو هريرة، ابن سيرين)]
- 23:2/q08/p3 The Companions had looked upward, then lowered heads or gazes. — Ibn Sīrīn, Abū Hurayra and others transmit this; one version says they had also turned and then ceased looking around. Suyūṭī calls Ibn Sīrīn's report mursal. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(محمد بن سيرين); TAB-FULL(محمد بن سيرين); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن سيرين); DURR-FULL(محمد بن سيرين); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن سيرين); ELMALILI(Hasan ve İbn Sîrîn); STUDYQURAN(R and Ṭ)]
- 23:2/q08/p4 Muslims had looked sideways, then faced forward toward the qibla. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's report specifies looking forward, or toward the Kaʿba in the sanctuary. Hushaym's version says the Muslims faced their prayer. Al-Thaʿlabī combines skyward and sideways looking. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(هشيم); THALABI]
- 23:2/q08/p5 The connection of the Prophet's lowered gaze to this very verse was uncertain. — Ibn Sīrīn reports an unnamed informant who did not know whether this verse was the occasion; the practice of keeping the gaze on the prayer place remains in the report. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن سيرين)]

## 23:2/q09 Where should the gaze be directed during prayer?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether the norm is the prostration place or forward qibla and what counts as an invalidating turn.
- 23:2/q09/p1 Toward the place of prostration, within the prayer place. — Ibn Sīrīn and others give this practice; Ibn Kathīr transmits advice to close one's eyes if accustomed to looking around. Al-Kāshshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and others link it to reverent fear; Ibn ʿUmar adds awareness that God attends to the worshipper. Al-Nutaf dislikes looking skyward at the opening takbīr as a prohibited turning, though skyward looking outside prayer is acceptable for supplication. Ālūsī prohibits raising the eyes even for a blind person, citing Jābir ibn Samura's warning in Muslim, Abū Dāwūd and Ibn Mājah that sight may not return. [15 notes: THALABI(ابن سيرين وغيره); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن سيرين); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مسلم بن يسار وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن سيرين); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر); BURSEVI(النتف); ALUSI-FULL(جابر بن سمرة)]
- 23:2/q09/p2 Face forward toward the qibla; in Mecca look at the Sacred House. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the direction as qibla or ahead and specifies Kaʿba in the sanctuary. Kashifī distinguishes standing outside Mecca, with gaze at the prostration place, from Mecca, with gaze at the House. Study Quran notes that some worshippers look forward and slightly down. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); STUDYQURAN(R and Ṭ)]
- 23:2/q09/p3 A side glance without turning the neck need not invalidate prayer. — Al-Nasāʾī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās that the Prophet glanced right and left without twisting his neck; Kaʿb says the Prophet saw his glance but did not require repetition. Al-Qurṭubī points to his discussion of the direction faced at 2:144. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,كعب بن مالك)]

## 23:2/q10 Which movements and distractions conflict with humility in prayer?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · law]
turns on: How far particular bodily acts, glances and distractions infringe the verse's humility.
- 23:2/q10/p1 Turning, fidgeting and worldly distraction should be avoided. — Mujāhid says scholars disliked hand fidgeting, looking around and worldly thought. ʿĀʾisha reports turning as Satan's theft, in al-Bukhārī, Abū Dāwūd and al-Nasāʾī; Abū Dharr reports God's attention turns away when the worshipper turns. Abū Hurayra reports divine attention calling the person back, and Ālūsī's report allows an unavoidable turn only outside the obligatory part. ʿAṭāʾ prohibits fiddling with any limb; Abū Dharr's report says not to move pebbles because mercy faces the worshipper, cited by al-Tirmidhī; al-Ḥasan rebukes a pebble fidgeter seeking a heavenly bride. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); THALABI(أبو ذر,أبو هريرة,الحسن,عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو ذر,عائشة); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ذر,عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة,عائشة); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Aişe); BURSEVI]
- 23:2/q10/p2 Prayer manners encompass restrained clothing, posture, mouth, eyes and hands. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān list avoiding bunched clothing, bodily or clothing fidgeting, turning, stretching, yawning, closing eyes, covering mouth, trailing clothing, cracking or interlacing fingers, hands on hips and shifting pebbles; Ālūsī repeats much of this. Ālūsī calls hand-on-hip posture disliked, explaining the 'rest of the people of Hell' as a Jewish practice rather than actual rest in Hell (43:75). [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 23:2/q10/p3 Swaying should cease so limbs remain still. — Ālūsī calls swaying prohibited as a Jewish practice. Umm Rūmān reports Abū Bakr citing the Prophet's instruction not to sway like Jews and that still limbs complete prayer; Elmalılı repeats it. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أم رومان); ELMALILI(Ümm-i Rûman)]
- 23:2/q10/p4 Outward restraint is prescribed as the fruit of a humble heart. — The Prophet's response to the man playing with his beard is repeated widely: if his heart were humble, his limbs would be humble. Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes a version to an unnamed scholar; Ālūsī calls Abū Hurayra's version weak; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes no grading. Bursawī gives the general heart-to-limbs report. [11 notes: THALABI(رسول الله); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض العلماء); TABRISI(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي ﷺ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 23:2/q11 Is khushūʿ in prayer obligatory?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether the demand governs legal validity, the duty to pray, acceptance, or completeness.
- 23:2/q11/p1 Yes, it is obligatory. — Al-Jishumī asserts this; al-Baghawī says the later mention of guarding prayer shows both preservation and humility obligatory. Al-Rāzī grounds the duty in reflection on recitation (47:24, 73:4), remembrance (20:14), the ban on heedlessness (7:205), and the analogy to knowing what one says while intoxicated (4:43). Al-Qurṭubī and al-Taḥrīr judge this the correct view, locating it in the heart. [10 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التحرير)+; ALUSI-FULL(التحرير)+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 23:2/q11/p2 It is a meritorious completion of prayer. — The juristic alternative is reported by al-Qurṭubī and al-Taḥrīr; Ibn Ḥajar's commentary on al-Minhāj recommends inward and outward humility throughout prayer, including attention and avoiding purposeless motion. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التحرير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر,التحرير)]
- 23:2/q11/p3 Prayer without it still discharges the formal obligation, though acceptance and reward may fail. — Al-Rāzī answers the objection from juristic consensus by distinguishing legal discharge from acceptance, comparing a returned garment given courteously or contemptuously. Al-Ālūsī rejects making inward humility a validity component, although some jurists require it for some part; he cites sound reports for the loss of reward. Elmalılı links the inward root to valid intention and outward signs to manners. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says a non-praying person has not performed the pillar at all. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(المخالفون); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; RAZI-FULL+]
- 23:2/q11/p4 Heedless prayer is corrupt or earns only its understood portion. — Bishr al-Ḥāfī calls a prayer without khushūʿ corrupt, al-Ḥasan says it approaches punishment, and Muʿādh says deliberate notice of neighbors leaves no prayer. Theologians require inner intention to distinguish prostration to God from an idol. Al-Rāzī prefers the scrupulous view as safer and cites reports granting only a sixth or tenth, or only what is comprehended; ʿAbd al-Wāḥid claims consensus on the last point. Abū al-Layth adds accurate and reflective recitation. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو الليث,الحسن,المتكلمون,بشر الحافي,رسول الله,عبد الواحد بن زيد,معاذ بن جبل); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL+]

## 23:2/q12 Why must the heart attend to prayer's words and acts?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether inward attention is necessary for meaningful address and spiritual fruit.
- 23:2/q12/p1 Prayer is intimate address and meaningful remembrance, so heedless words and movements miss its purpose. — Al-Rāzī contrasts pilgrimage's disciplinary hardship with prayer, asks how 'guide us' (1:6) can be meaningful uncomprehended, compares an oath to thank someone recited without addressing them, and says bowing without awareness differs little from idolatrous motion. Prayer's central status, opening formulas and final salutation all call for attention; he records juristic disagreement on whom the final greeting addresses and the Fātiḥa behind an imam (1:1–7). Al-Ghazālī says heedless speech cannot be intimate address. Reports say such prayer yields only fatigue or no restraint from indecency. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL(الغزالي,بعض العلماء,رسول الله)]
- 23:2/q12/p2 Prayer's outward acts need inward servitude to gain spiritual force. — Kur'an Yolu says movement and recitation require awareness, and both outward form and inward content lead to salvation. Islāhī calls humility prayer's essence and says prayer without it has no effect. Al-Sāmarrāʾī calls it the soul of prayer and says humility combines gathering attention, reflection and fear with refraining from distraction. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:2/q13 What does sincere humble prayer accomplish?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which effects of humble prayer the notes emphasize.
- 23:2/q13/p1 It brings rest, delight, closeness and spiritual benefit. — Ibn Kathīr and al-Biqāʿī say a heart freed for prayer finds rest and delight. Anas reports that prayer was made the Prophet's eye's delight, alongside loved perfume and women; an Aslamī man and Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥanafiyya transmit the Prophet's call to Bilāl for rest through prayer. The poem cited by al-Qurṭubī says humble prayer joins submissive limbs, mercy and intimacy with God. Al-Qushayrī says awe and divine etiquette bring nearness. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس,رجل من أسلم,محمد بن الحنفية); BIQAI-FULL(أنس,ابن كثير,رجل من أسلم); QUSHAYRI]
- 23:2/q13/p2 It shapes conduct, restrains evil and supports success. — Al-Biqāʿī reports al-Rāzī that alert submission shapes dealings and service. Islāhī says awareness of God's company restrains vulgarity and futile action, and faith without prayer is barren. Kur'an Yolu names reverent prayer among salvation's conditions; Asad connects it with inner purity. Study Quran links the spiritual discipline to virtues beyond prayer and contemplation of God's qualities and human infirmity. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:2/q14 How are genuine and hypocritical humility distinguished?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · theology]
- 23:2/q14/p1 Genuine outward humility follows a believing, reverent heart. — The Prophet and Abū al-Dardāʾ warn against a humbled body with a hypocritical heart. Kur'an Yolu says a disbeliever cannot turn reverently to God, and outward worship without faith or respect is hypocrisy; the inner-outer mismatch corrupts character. Elmalılı links inner submission to perfected intention. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,النبي ﷺ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]

## 23:2/q15 How do inward readings describe the worshipper's humility?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · inward]
turns on: Which inward experience and outward signs each spiritual reading stresses.
- 23:2/q15/p1 Fear under God's gaze becomes purified outward obedience. — Tustarī makes inner fear the root, manifest in etiquette and movements purified of all besides God; al-Qāsim fears losing intimate communion, Abū Sulaymān describes the heart's abasement, and Fāris includes heart, limbs and aspiration. [4 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو سليمان,القاسم,فارس)]
- 23:2/q15/p2 Vision of divine gifts and high aspiration humble the exterior. — Al-Sulamī reports that recognition of the Real's gifts and regard humbles the outer self. Another saying plays on pride in refusing arrogance; another says limbs and aspirations avoid created defilement, with a couplet declaring even the lesser aim above time. [3 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 23:2/q15/p3 The self effaces in awe, unveiling and exclusive intimacy with God. — Al-Qushayrī describes inward bowing, melting and effacement; absence of the self, the nearest intrusive watcher, completes intimacy and brings joy. He calls distraction heedlessness, self-serving action excess and speech apart from God vain. Qāshānī describes the heart overtaken by majesty's light. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]
- 23:2/q15/p4 Each faculty is subdued and guarded against distraction. — Najmiyya, reported by Bursawī, maps outward humility to head, eye, ear, tongue, hands, bow, thoughts and feet; inwardly soul, heart, secret and spirit leave passing thoughts and created things for remembrance, love and disclosure. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 23:2/q16 What does the report about al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī illustrate?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · reports]
- 23:2/q16/p1 A person's color changes on preparing to stand before God. — Tustarī reports that al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī changed color after ablution because he was about to enter before the Lord of the Throne. [1 notes: TUSTARI(الحسن بن علي)]

## 23:2/q17 What do the Prophet's counsels to Muʿādh say about reverent believers?  [* · reports]
- 23:2/q17/p1 The Qurʾān guides vigilant believers through danger and toward reward. — Tustarī transmits the Prophet saying the Qurʾān restrains desire and ruin, believers strive for release and fear Hell's bridge; recording watchers see senses and limbs while piety, Qurʾān, fear, longing, prayer, fasting, charity, truth and modesty guide them. He also says he loves for Muʿādh what he loves for himself and conveyed Gabriel's message, hoping for joy on Judgment Day. [3 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 23:2/q18 What did ʿĀʾisha say these opening traits describe?  [* · reports]
- 23:2/q18/p1 They describe the Prophet's character. — Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī reports ʿĀʾisha asking for al-Muʾminūn to be recited through the verse on guarding prayer, then identifying that passage as the Prophet's character. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني); QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة)]

## 23:2/q19 What is reported about the opening ten verses and their revelation?  [* · reports]
- 23:2/q19/p1 Whoever upholds the first ten verses enters Paradise. — ʿUmar reports a bee-like sound during revelation, then the Prophet faced the qibla, raised his hands and prayed for divine favor before announcing the promise. Al-Baghawī notes a variant supplication from Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal, ʿAlī ibn al-Madīnī and others adding a request for contentment alongside divine pleasure. [3 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(عمر بن الخطاب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل وعلي بن المديني وجماعة,عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 23:2/q20 What reports predict the loss of khushūʿ?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · reports]
- 23:2/q20/p1 It is the first knowledge or quality to be taken away. — ʿUbāda ibn al-Ṣāmit says this; al-Tirmidhī graded one route ḥasan gharīb and al-Nasāʾī gives a sound route through ʿAwf ibn Mālik. Al-Qurṭubī records divided transmitter judgments on Muʿāwiya ibn Ṣāliḥ: trusted by Ibn Mahdī, Aḥmad and Abū Zurʿa and used by Muslim; acceptable for recording but not proof to Abū Ḥātim; Ibn Maʿīn differed; only al-Qaṭṭān criticized him. Ālūsī cites al-Ḥākim's sound grading for ʿUbāda and Ḥudhayfa, the latter pairing first-lost humility with prayer as the last remaining bond. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عبادة بن الصامت,معاوية بن صالح); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبادة بن الصامت); ALUSI-FULL(حذيفة,عبادة بن الصامت); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:2/q21 Why is humility named early and prayer named again at the end?  [* · links]
turns on: How the initial humility and final guarding of prayer are related.
- 23:2/q21/p1 Opening with humility and ending with guarding prayer honors prayer and its inner spirit. — Al-Ālūsī says the first position honors humility. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports Rūḥ al-Maʿānī that prayer without humility is a soulless body and suggests the opening names what is lost first, while the ending names what remains last. Abū Ḥayyān differentiates inward vigilance and bodily submission from regular timely prayer with purity, conditions and pillars. Al-Baghawī says both qualities are obligatory. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني)]
- 23:2/q21/p2 Humility here includes persistence in prayer and more. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 70:23's persistence with this broader absorption of heart and body throughout prayer. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:2/q22 Why is humility in prayer the first quality after faith?  [* · links]
- 23:2/q22/p1 Prayer is faith's foremost setting and expression. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says prayer especially stirs reverence as intimate address to God, so believers are praised for faith then humility in prayer. Islāhī calls prayer faith's leading manifestation, and al-Ālūsī says placing humility first honors it. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:2/q23 How does this verse fit neighboring passages and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Which neighboring passage or internal progression is being compared.
- 23:2/q23/p1 It begins a sequence in which lived faith yields success. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says active faith produces the enumerated effects; Islāhī traces faith through prayer, zakāh, restraint, fidelity and guarded prayer to lasting success. Study Quran says the sūra opens with virtues and righteous actions; its note also says affluent opposition to prophets recurs throughout, comparing 7:59–62 and 7:136. Jishumī places duties within trusts and covenants and sins under avoiding vain talk, so the list does not omit duties. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:2/q23/p2 It turns neighboring commands into stable character traits. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares commands to bow, prostrate, worship, pray and give zakāh at 22:77–78 with the qualities in 23:1–4. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 23:2/q23/p3 It emphasizes prayer's inner essence after an earlier stress on form. — Islāhī contrasts the preceding sūra's outward diligence with this sūra's humble inward prayer, and says al-Nūr follows by portraying faith's light or its absence across personal and collective life. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 23:2/q24 How does Qurʾānic usage contrast believers' humility with others'?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · links]
- 23:2/q24/p1 Believers show reverence in this life; wrongdoers show humiliation at judgment. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites believers' worship in 17:109, 3:199, 21:90, 2:45, 33:35 and 57:16, against humbled criminals and deniers in 88:1–4, 79:8–12, 70:43–44, 42:44–45 and 54:6–7; she calls the contrast rhetorical. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 23:2/q25 How is this verse linked to idle speech?  [* · links]
- 23:2/q25/p1 Speech away from God is vain, so humble prayer excludes it. — Al-Qushayrī calls creatures' speech without praise and extolling God vain, and attention to anything other than God estrangement. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 23:2/q26 What does abandoning prayer imply for faith?  [صَلَاتِهِمْ · theology]
- 23:2/q26/p1 A person who abandons it may be judged an unbeliever. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites a sound report calling abandonment disbelief and destruction of religion; some jurists deem the abandoner an unbeliever even after the two testimonies. He distinguishes this from prayer lacking humility, which still performs the pillar. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(unspecified,بعض الفقهاء)]

## 23:2/q27 Does khushūʿ apply outside prayer and to nonhuman things?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · meaning]
- 23:2/q27/p1 Yes; prayer is its most fitting human setting, while the word extends to voices and other things. — Ibn ʿĀshūr places it in the heart and says prayer is singled out because intimate address especially stirs it. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says humility can occur outside prayer and describe nonhuman things, citing humbled voices at 20:108. Al-Baghawī uses the same verse for the voice; al-Layth also associates the term with sight and voice and soft ground. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI(الليث)]

## 23:2/q28 What does the report through Ḥāritha say about truth?  [* · reports]
- 23:2/q28/p1 Every truth has a reality and every right thing a light. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this Prophetic saying through Ḥāritha ibn al-Nuʿmān from al-Kāfī and other sources. [1 notes: TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 23:2/q29 How does the heart's humility shape the worshipper's spiritual life?  [خَٰشِعُونَ · interpretation]
- 23:2/q29/p1 It belongs to inward worship and appears in the worshipper's gaze and bearing. — Ibn Kathīr reports al-Ḥasan that humility in believers' hearts led them to lower their eyes and demeanor. Kur'an Yolu names khushūʿ, together with taqwā, as Qurʾānic vocabulary for worship's inward spiritual dimension. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
