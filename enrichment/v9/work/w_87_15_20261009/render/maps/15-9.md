# 15:9: 24 questions

## 15:9/q01 Why does God say “We” with emphatic repetition?  [إِنَّا نَحْنُ وَإِنَّا · rhetoric]
- 15:9/q01/p1 It is a majestic plural and repeated emphasis asserting that God Himself reveals and preserves the Reminder. — Ibn Abbas identifies the plural with God alone; language scholars, al-Razi, and older scholars compare royal usage. Al-Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Baydawi, Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai, and Islahi connect the emphases to refuting mockery, excluding human or jinn authorship, and magnifying God and revelation; Ibn Ashur likens 63:1's use of an opponent's premise. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة,ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(بعض الأقدمين); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 15:9/q02 What is the grammatical role of naḥnu?  [نَحْنُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether naḥnu is parsed as a subject, emphasis of inna's noun, or a separating pronoun.
- 15:9/q02/p1 It is a subject whose predicate is nazzalnā, and that clause is the predicate of inna. — Al-Qurtubi gives the complete analysis. Al-Kawashi, al-Samin and al-Alusi accept subjecthood. Ibn Ashur calls the pronoun separating in the rhetorical analysis, while the others deny a technical separator because of the following clause or absence of two nouns. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الكواشي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 15:9/q02/p2 It is an emphatic pronoun for the noun of inna. — Al-Qurtubi calls it accusative emphasis; al-Samin and al-Alusi also allow emphatic status, rejecting separation as the technical parse. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 15:9/q02/p3 It serves as a separating pronoun that sharpens the affirmation. — Ibn Ashur uses this rhetorical description and compares 63:1; al-Qurtubi, al-Samin, al-Kawashi and al-Alusi explicitly dispute a technical separator analysis because the next expression is a clause rather than the required second noun. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; BURSEVI(الكواشي)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 15:9/q03 What does nazzalnā convey about revelation?  [نَزَّلْنَا · interpretation]
- 15:9/q03/p1 God Himself sent the Qur'an down gradually, through Gabriel to Muhammad. — Al-Kashshaf and Biqai specify Gabriel; Biqai excludes humans and jinn as authors. Tabatabai excludes Muhammad and angels as independent sources. Asad cites Zamakhshari on the verbal form and 2:23 for gradual revelation; Asad's translation says 'in stages'. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 15:9/q04 What does al-dhikr denote here?  [ٱلذِّكْرَ · referent]
turns on: Whether dhikr identifies the Qur'an as a text, highlights its admonition, or also conveys honor.
- 15:9/q04/p1 The Reminder is the Qur'an revealed to Muhammad. — Muqatil, al-Mawardi's Hasan and Dahhak, Wahidi's general exegetes, Damghani, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Alusi, Asad, Kuranyolu and Study Quran identify it. Kuranyolu ties it to the Book of 15:1; Study Quran to 15:6. Damghani cites 21:50 and 43:5, Elmalili cites 21:24 and 3:58 and notes the Qur'an's other names al-Hukm and ruh (42:52). Tabatabai argues the definite article points back to the previously mentioned Reminder, and rejects extending the promise to Torah and Gospel merely because they too are called remembrance. [17 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن والضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة المفسرين); DAMGHANI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 15:9/q04/p2 The word evokes admonition and the revealed reminder of duties and rulings. — Jishumi allows a reminder of obligations and rulings. Biqai reads admonition together with honor. Tabatabai says the Qur'an recalls God's names, acts, angels, messengers, law, resurrection and final destinies, and that all its teachings recall Him. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 15:9/q04/p3 The word also signifies honor for those who hold to the Book. — Jishumi cites 43:44; Biqai combines honor with admonition; al-Kashifi, quoted by Bursevi, says the Book brings worldly and otherworldly honor, citing 23:71. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 15:9/q05 What does al-dhikr do for its recipients?  [ٱلذِّكْرَ · inward]
- 15:9/q05/p1 It heals, clarifies, guides and guards those destined for felicity from disobedience. — Ibn Ata names healing, mercy, clarification and criterion. Sulami also transmits Ja'far's statement that God's preservation accompanies those for whom He wills good throughout life. Al-Mawardi says God keeps the Qur'an in hearts He wills good for, and removes it from hearts of those He wills evil for. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر); MAWARDI-FULL]

## 15:9/q06 To whom does lahu refer?  [لَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the preceding explicit noun or the implicitly understood recipient of revelation supplies the antecedent.
- 15:9/q06/p1 It refers to Muhammad: God protects him from harm, enemies and plots. — Al-Farra and other transmitters use this reading; Baghawi, Zamakhshari, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir and Alusi support it with 5:67. Ibn al-Anbari, as reported by Razi, says the recipient is understood from revelation, comparing 97:1; Wahidi's report of him instead favors the Qur'an. Elmalili adds jinn and Satan; Kuranyolu cites the Prophet's survival and successful spread of Islam and says this also indirectly preserves revelation. Qurtubi also reports a narrower protection from falsely claiming something about God or having such a claim made against him. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جرير); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ELMALILI(Ferrâ ve İbnü’l- Enbârî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı müfessirler); TABATABAI-]
- 15:9/q06/p2 It refers to the Reminder, the Qur'an. — Tabari and most later commentators take the nearest stated antecedent. Ibn al-Anbari as reported by Wahidi prefers it because revelation and revealed object already imply the recipient, comparing the unexpressed Qur'an in 97:1; Wahidi and Razi call it the apparent sense. Razi cites 41:42 and 4:82; Ibn Kathir invokes context; Tabatabai rejects the Prophet antecedent because 15:6's mockery turns on the Qur'an. Ibn Ashur calls the preservation clause parenthetic. Elmalili calls the Prophet reading sound but less apparent; Kuranyolu says both protections can be combined. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والأكثرون)+; ELMALILI(cumhur)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)+]

## 15:9/q07 How do the grammar and order of the preservation clause work?  [وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ لَحَٰفِظُونَ · grammar]
- 15:9/q07/p1 The nominal clause and active participle present an emphatic, continuing or future guarantee; the fronted object and lām strengthen it. — Alusi reports commentators who hear continuous miraculous protection. Tabatabai says the active participle normally points forward; Ibn Ashur says the lām reinforces the verb's force after its object and calls the waw parenthetic. Alusi says the paired clauses magnify God and revelation. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 15:9/q08 What does God's preservation of the Reminder encompass?  [لَحَٰفِظُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promise addresses textual integrity, heavenly keeping, continuing law, or retention in chosen people.
- 15:9/q08/p1 The Qur'an is kept intact against addition, omission, distortion, replacement and loss over time. — Qatada and Thabit specify Satan's inability to insert falsehood or remove truth; Tabari, Zajjaj, Baghawi, Qatada, Razi, Study Quran and Tabatabai invoke 41:42, while Razi and Study Quran also cite 4:82. Muqatil counters the charge of satanic teaching; Bursevi and Alusi include attacks, denial and mockery as ineffectual. Ibn Ashur includes non-vanishing and mass transmission from the Prophet's lifetime. Tabatabai limits the inference to changes that erase the text's character as remembrance: a repeated verse, dot or inflection with no such effect remains logically possible. Asad says early diacritic and vocalization variants usually change no meaning and mentions 85:22. Kuranyolu distinguishes protection during revelation from later preservation until Resurrection; Tabatabai includes order only where altered order destroys remembrance. [45 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة وثابت); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد وقتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة وثابت البناني); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(التبيان,المثنوي); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); STUDYQURAN]
- 15:9/q08/p2 God keeps the Reminder with Himself. — Mujahid's gloss is 'with Us'; Tabari and al-Durr transmit it. [3 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 15:9/q08/p3 Preservation includes its presence in the hearts and lives of those God chooses. — Ibn Ata says God preserves it in His friends' hearts and employs the elect's limbs in its service; al-Mawardi links retention and removal to God's will for good or evil; Ibn Atiyya reports storing it in people's breasts. [4 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 15:9/q08/p4 God keeps it until it is requited for at Judgment Day, or keeps its revealed law in force until Resurrection. — Al-Mawardi reports al-Hasan's requital reading; Alusi reports al-Hasan's understanding of continuing law. These reports vary in the future event emphasized. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 15:9/q09 By what means does God preserve the Qur'an?  [لَحَٰفِظُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the identified instrument is intrinsic inimitability, frustrated imitation, communal transmission, written compilation, diverted corrupters, or direct divine action.
- 15:9/q09/p1 Its miraculous composition exposes alien additions and omissions. — Razi's first account, Baydawi, Kashshaf and later commentators say changes disrupt its inimitable order and Arabic speakers recognize them. Study Quran likens arrangement to a city's walls. Alusi objects that whole suras could disappear without breaking the surviving text's inimitability, and prefers several divine means rather than this alone. Tabatabai uses present inimitability and coherent structure to test alleged extra suras: al-Khal' and al-Hafd, and the fabricated al-Wilaya, do not match Qur'anic speech. Burhan says judging eloquence requires trained literary taste, not formal logic or mere grammatical expertise. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف والكشف); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(another interpretation); TABATABAI(الروايات); BURHAN]
- 15:9/q09/p2 God appoints reciters, scholars and a community to memorize, teach, publicize and correct the text. — Razi says this continues through the period of religious obligation; even children correct a great elder's altered letter or dot. Ibn Atiyya reports memory in people's breasts. Bursevi's Bahr al-Ulum adds scholarship on wording, meaning, tafsir and readings. Ibn Ashur says mass transmission and memorization began during the Prophet's life. Study Quran cites traditional reciters' hearts; Islahi names the Ansar and people raised to accept and defend it. Kuranyolu adds modern writing and digital media as aids. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Muslims,another interpretation)]
- 15:9/q09/p3 God enabled the Companions' written collection and later copying as an instrument of preservation. — Wahidi, Razi, Alusi and Elmalili answer the objection that compilation would be unnecessary if God promised protection: the Companions' work was one of God's appointed means. Ibn al-Anbari instead says their collection improved access, memorization and recitation, though the promise meant it could not have been lost without it. Kuranyolu describes first memory, then memory and writing. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(قائل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 15:9/q09/p4 God guards it by His own power and knowledge, continuing its lesson beyond Muhammad's lifetime. — Biqai rejects the charge of a madman's confused speech by divine safeguarding; Bursevi cites the Mathnawi's assertion that the lesson will not die with Muhammad. Islahi emphasizes that intact preservation is God's responsibility. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المثنوي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 15:9/q09/p5 God prevents anyone from producing a rival text. — Razi gives this as a second account; Elmalili reports it as close to the composition argument. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون); ELMALILI]
- 15:9/q09/p6 God turns people away from corrupting it despite their ability to do so. — Bahr al-Ulum, reported by Bursevi, calls this ṣarfah and contrasts earlier scriptures that people did change. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم)]

## 15:9/q10 Why did earlier scriptures and the Qur'an have different histories?  [لَحَٰفِظُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the earlier change was textual or only interpretive, and whether preservation was entrusted to people or undertaken by God.
- 15:9/q10/p1 Earlier books were entrusted to human custodians and altered; God undertook the Qur'an's keeping Himself. — Qurtubi, Sufyan ibn Uyayna, Kashshaf, Nasafi, Nashr, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur use 5:44; Kashshaf and Nasafi mention scholarly envy and dispute. Ibn Ashur transmits Ismail ibn Ishaq's explanation through Qadi Iyad, Abu al-Hasan ibn al-Muntab's repetition and al-Muhamili's praise, and notes the parallel formulations may have arisen independently. Razi, Elmalili and Asad invoke the Qur'an's unique survival amid hostile efforts. [16 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); NASAFI-FULL; NASHR; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو الحسن بن المنتاب,القاضي إسماعيل بن إسحاق بن حماد المالكي البصري,سفيان بن عيينة); ASAD-NOTES]
- 15:9/q10/p2 Earlier scripture changed in interpretation rather than wording. — Ibn Atiyya reports this from Ibn Abbas but counters that Qur'anic passages show verbal change; he says even covering the stoning verse with a hand changes the wording's presentation. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

## 15:9/q11 What does the promise imply about the basmala at every sura's start?  [لَحَٰفِظُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether placing the basmala at each beginning counts as an added Qur'anic verse, and whether this promise settles its status.
- 15:9/q11/p1 It is a Qur'anic verse in each sura; otherwise its presence would violate preservation. — Wahidi's associates, Razi's associates, al-Qadi's associates in Alusi, and some reported by Kuranyolu argue that if Companions inserted the basmala they might equally have omitted text, undermining the Qur'an's authority. Burhan notes al-Shafi'i's proof for a basmala at each beginning while discussing transmission requirements. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحابنا); RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); ALUSI-FULL(أصحاب القاضي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); BURHAN(كثير من الأصوليين)]
- 15:9/q11/p2 The preservation promise does not establish that basmala inference. — Alusi derides the associates' argument; Kuranyolu calls it an unsound result of excessive concern with preservation. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أصحاب القاضي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 15:9/q12 What mode of transmission does preservation require?  [لَحَٰفِظُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the preservation and public proclamation guarantee extends from words to each verse's placement and order.
- 15:9/q12/p1 Qur'anic wording, verse placement and order must all be mass-transmitted; solitary material is not Qur'an. — Burhan says agreement covers wording and components and the verified Sunni position extends to placement and order, deriving it from preservation and universal proclamation. He contrasts the many legal theorists who require tawatur only for wording. Tabatabai reports this claimed consensus and mass transmission but disputes whether every verse's position is actually so transmitted. [6 notes: BURHAN; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,الجمهور,الزركشي,بعضهم)]
- 15:9/q12/p2 Mass transmission is required for wording, but not necessarily placement or order. — Burhan attributes this to many legal theorists, with al-Shafi'i's basmala argument as an example. Tabatabai says communal memorization secures the Qur'an generally but does not prove tawatur of every verse position; he denies evidence for claimed mass transmission of the current arrangement. [4 notes: BURHAN(كثير من الأصوليين); TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 15:9/q13 Does the verse prove that verbal Qur'anic speech is originated?  [لَحَٰفِظُونَ · theology]
- 15:9/q13/p1 It appears to support the origination of verbal Qur'anic speech. — Alusi reports this inference from proponents of originated verbal speech and says the verse appears to support them. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القائلون بحدوث الكلام اللفظي)]

## 15:9/q14 How did the first written collection take place?  [* · reports]
turns on: How the first codex was produced and whether the reports establish its completion under Abu Bakr.
- 15:9/q14/p1 After Yamama, Abu Bakr accepted Umar's proposal and had Zayd collect scattered written and memorized material. — Bukhari's Zayd report in Biqai and Tabatabai describes deaths of reciters, Abu Bakr's hesitation, palm materials, stones and memories; final al-Tawba verses were found with Khuzaima or Abu Khuzaima and the sheets passed to Umar then Hafsa. Yaqubi names a Quraysh-Ansar team consulting Sa'id ibn al-As; Abu al-Fida says palm stalks, hides and oral recitation. Umar required two witnesses in some reports; an interrupted Urwa report places Umar and Zayd at the mosque entrance. Al-Layth gives two just witnesses, Abu Khuzaima's doubled testimony and Umar's unsupported stoning verse excluded; Yahya ibn Abbad gives al-Harith ibn Khuzaima and Umar's decision to append two verses. Ubayy says 9:127 was not actually the last verse. Tabatabai distinguishes scattered collection from guaranteed present order. [13 notes: BIQAI-FULL(زيد بن ثابت); TABATABAI(أبو الفداء,أبي بن كعب,الليث بن سعد,اليعقوبي,زيد بن ثابت,عروة,يحيى بن عباد بن عبد الله بن الزبير,يحيى بن عبد الرحمن بن حاطب)]
- 15:9/q14/p2 Abu Bakr and Umar did not themselves complete a collected codex. — Ibn Sirin's sound-chain report says each died without collecting the Qur'an; Tabatabai juxtaposes it with first-collection accounts. [1 notes: TABATABAI(محمد بن سيرين)]

## 15:9/q15 Was the Qur'an gathered into one codex during the Prophet's lifetime?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether 'gathered' means individual memorization or written gathering, and whether that amounted to one ordered codex.
- 15:9/q15/p1 It was written and taught then, but one assembled codex came after his death. — Zayd's reports in Itqan and Tabatabai say the Qur'an was not collected into one thing at the Prophet's death, although al-Hakim reports Zayd arranging loose sheets before him. Al-Khattabi says expected abrogation of recitation or rulings delayed a single codex; the prohibition on writing other prophetic material does not conflict because Qur'anic material was written but not gathered and ordered by sura. Tabatabai reads earlier 'collection' as joining verses or similar suras, not binding today's codex. [7 notes: ITQAN(الخطابي,زيد بن ثابت); TABATABAI(زيد بن ثابت)]
- 15:9/q15/p2 Several Companions gathered or memorized the Qur'an during his lifetime. — Ibn Umar reports a nightly recitation curtailed by the Prophet. A hasan report names Muadh, Ubada, Ubayy, Abu al-Darda and Abu Ayyub; Ibn Sirin names Muadh, Ubayy and Abu Zayd plus a disputed fourth (Abu al-Darda, Uthman or Tamim); al-Sha'bi lists seven, one lacking two or three suras. Anas says four at the Prophet's death, with one version replacing Abu al-Darda by Ubayy; al-Mazari says collective memory suffices even if individuals knew parts. Tabatabai rejects dismissing Anas's wording without context, notes Zayd did not know all verses, and says even a nonliteral reading would mean four held most of the text, not today's complete order. Ibn Burayda calls Salim the first codex collector. [11 notes: TABATABAI(أنس,ابن بريدة,ابن سيرين,ابن عمر,الشعبي,المازري,زيد بن ثابت,محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 15:9/q16 What happened in Uthman's standardization?  [* · reports]
- 15:9/q16/p1 He unified disputed recitations by copying Hafsa's sheets, choosing Quraysh wording in disputes, circulating copies and removing other codices. — Biqai and Tabatabai give Anas's report of Hudhayfa's concern about Syrian and Iraqi forces, the four scribes Zayd, Ibn al-Zubayr, Sa'id and Abd al-Rahman, Quraysh dialect instructions, return to Hafsa and burning of other copies; Zayd found 33:23 with Khuzayma. Yaqubi reports boiling codices in vinegar or burning, perhaps sparing Ibn Masud, and a clash with him; his alternative triggers are rival family codices or Ibn Masud's and Hudhayfa's own call. Other reports describe children and teachers disputing, consulting eyewitness reciters, twelve scribes and deferred disputed passages; Ali's sound-chain statement says the group consented to avert schism. A report has Ubayy insisting on retaining al-Tawba's waw. Uthman explains al-Anfal and al-Tawba's adjacency without basmala by their related narratives and lack of final prophetic clarification. Tabatabai says the second gathering reconciled manuscript and reading differences. [17 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أنس,زيد بن ثابت); TABATABAI(أبي,أنس,أنس بن مالك,اليعقوبي,زيد,زيد بن ثابت,عثمان,علي,كثير بن أفلح)]

## 15:9/q17 Who determined the present order of suras and verses?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether reports of revelation and collection establish exact prophetic placement, and whether current order was transmitted at every point.
- 15:9/q17/p1 The Prophet, under Gabriel's direction, fixed the order; Companions merely bound what was already arranged. — Tabatabai reports the majority, al-Zarkashi, Abu Jafar ibn al-Zubayr, al-Baghawi and Ibn al-Hissar claiming revelation, consensus or tawatur fixed placement. Al-Baghawi says the Preserved Tablet's order passed to the lowest heaven and then piecemeal revelation; Ibn al-Hissar says prophetic directions specified each verse. Ibn Abbas reports the basmala marked sura completion or beginning, with one chain meeting the two Sahihs' standard and another called sound; Shiite reports from al-Baqir parallel them. Tabatabai rejects using descent reports to prove the current codex's order. [16 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر بن الزبير,ابن الحصار,ابن عباس,الباقر,البغوي,الجمهور,الزركشي,بعضهم)]
- 15:9/q17/p2 Companions judged where to place some separately revealed verses and ordered suras in the two collections. — Tabatabai points to the first collection's search for verses, late Meccan verses in Medinan suras and vice versa, and Uthman's moving al-Anfal and Bara'a between al-A'raf and Yunus; an Ibn Jubayr report instead lists Yunus as seventh of the long suras. He says Ali's codex followed revelation order while Ubayy's and Ibn Masud's differed. He reads Ibn Abbas's basmala reports as implying sequential original order, so current cross-period placement reflects later judgment. He rejects claimed consensus, tawatur and the idea of mere physical binding: reports establish wording but not exact placements; Zayd lacked some verses and Anas named only four collectors. His alternative remains compatible with preservation of divine qualities where order changes leave meaning intact. [27 notes: TABATABAI(أنس,ابن جبير,زيد بن ثابت,علي); TABATABAI+]

## 15:9/q18 What evidence establishes the present Qur'an's authenticity and limits?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether preservation is established by the Qur'an's present qualities and public history, or undermined by solitary collection and variant reports; and what each line of proof can actually exclude.
- 15:9/q18/p1 Its present divine qualities, public recitation and continuity establish the Qur'an's preservation. — Tabatabai says Muhammad historically preached and brought this Book; it retains an unmatched eloquence, no real contradiction (4:82), a challenge to humans and jinn (17:88), decisive speech (86:13-14), unified tawhid, guidance, light, truthful prophecy, and religious purity compared with the Testaments. The Prophet taught it publicly; readers and learners remained active even through Yamama; first oral and written transmission and agreed codex prevented its disappearance. Its qualities prove attribution wherever found, without first depending on tawatur. He cites hadith al-Thaqalayn, instructions to test reports against the Qur'an, and the Imams' use of today's text and directions to read it. Burhan calls the Qur'an internally consistent and says revelation and prophethood should be read by principles of preservation and the Prophet's truthfulness (53:3); he allows some ambiguous passages to remain undecidable. Study Quran invokes 41:42, 4:82, 18:1 and 75:17. [35 notes: BURHAN; TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم وأئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 15:9/q18/p2 Collection reports exclude additions and changes decisively, but alone exclude every omission only probabilistically. — Tabatabai cites Umar's alleged unwritten stoning verse and Ibn Masud's denial of al-Falaq and al-Nas; other Companions and mass-transmitted Imami reports affirm both suras. He says no proof of threefold tawatur for all aspects and allows only hypothetical loss of a repeated verse or harmless point or inflection without loss of the Qur'an's described qualities. He nevertheless holds the present text has the qualities of revelation. [8 notes: TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت,ابن مسعود,عمر)]
- 15:9/q18/p3 Textual alteration or omission occurred in the collection, at least in some passages. — Tabatabai reports positions of some Shia and Sunni hadith scholars and Hashwiyya alleging omission or reordering, generally not addition, and allegations of limited additions, deletions and changes. Proponents invoke many Shia and Sunni reports, different companion codices, variant readings, the uncertainty of a fallible collector, Ali's rejected codex and the Muslim precedent of earlier communities. One report claims more than 2,000 lost verses between al-Nisa passages; others say al-Tawba once equaled al-Baqara and al-Ahzab exceeded it by 200 verses. A report about Ibn Masud's omission of the two protective suras is also adduced. [13 notes: TABATABAI(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن مسعود,الروايات,عدة من أئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام عن النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,علي عليه السلام,عمر)]
- 15:9/q18/p4 The distortion arguments and reports do not prove textual corruption. — Tabatabai says consensus is only probable and depends on prophethood whose living proof is the Qur'an; using disputed traditions is similarly circular. The reports are mostly isolated, conflicting, weak or susceptible to insertion, including Israelite material; their alleged extra passages fail the Qur'an's style. A plausible interpolation possibility defeats even apparently sound chains. Ali's codex may have differed only in order; he made no recorded omitted-verse argument about his authority. Muslim imitation of earlier communities can mean sectarian division and corrupt interpretation, not identical textual histories. He rejects relying on ordinary reality alone as proof and treats Qur'anic qualities as the decisive test. [22 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 15:9/q19 How should reports alleged to show omitted or altered wording be read?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether a transmitted wording claims actual lost revelation or records paraphrase, application, devotion, weak variants or asserted recitation abrogation.
- 15:9/q19/p1 Many are explanation, application, devotional addition or paraphrase, not textual variants. — Tabatabai groups al-Sadiq's explanation of twisting commands, reports about Ali as occasion of revelation, Banu Tamim named in an occasion report, applications to Muhammad's family or to allegiance to Ali, and al-Rida's devotional 'Thus is my Lord' after al-Ikhlas among cases mistaken for lost words. Badr variants 'weak' and 'few' paraphrase 'humiliated'; a report objects to calling believers humiliated while the Prophet was among them. The Imams' use of today's wording supports explanatory readings of expressions like 'thus it descended'. [9 notes: TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام,الإمام,الرضا,الروايات,الصادق)]
- 15:9/q19/p2 Conflicting and weak alleged variant reports cannot establish omitted Qur'an. — Tabatabai cites incompatible stoning verse endings and divergent Throne Verse additions, rejects the view that agreement on the basic claim makes the variants probative, and notes fabrication in hadith books. He rejects both forgetting and textual alteration reports as conflicting with the Qur'an and resists 'abrogation of recitation': removing useful revealed speech would abandon remembrance, while the Qur'an's unrestricted descriptions as preserved, decisive, guidance and light forbid such a carve-out. Reports can at most leave limited omission uncertain on historical grounds. [16 notes: TABATABAI(الروايات); TABATABAI+]
- 15:9/q19/p3 They record real textual loss, change or abrogation of recitation. — Tabatabai reports alleged large omissions, Umar's unwritten stoning verse, variant companion codices and reports of forgetting. He says proponents distinguish abrogation of ruling and recitation from recitation-only abrogation but finds neither adequate to the Qur'an's own descriptions. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الروايات,عمر); TABATABAI-]

## 15:9/q20 How does this verse answer its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 15:9/q20/p1 It answers the mockery in 15:6 by affirming divine revelation and guaranteed preservation, while consoling the Prophet. — Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqai, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai, Kuranyolu and Islahi link it to the charge of madness or jinn speech. Ibn Ashur says the answer to the angel demand in 15:7 precedes this reply to 15:6 in reverse order; the second clause intensifies the rebuke and points forward. Biqai adds the Prophet's sound reason and trustworthy judgment as a counter to madness. Islahi says the Prophet is charged with conveying, not compelling acceptance; Quraysh's rejection should not distress him. Asad calls guidance through revelation the sura's main theme, and Tabatabai describes the sura's consolation, warning and glad tidings. [22 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف والكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 15:9/q21 What does the Qur'an's endurance show about its origin and the Messenger?  [* · theology]
- 15:9/q21/p1 Its enduring inimitability and fulfilled preservation promise attest divine origin and prophethood. — Kashshaf and Nasafi say human speech would admit additions and omissions. Razi calls nearly six centuries of fulfillment foreknowledge of the unseen and notes hostile efforts failed; Elmalili extends the observation to Hijri 1349, more than thirteen and a half centuries, distinguishing practical survival from the verse's intellectual miracle of prediction. Asad points to an unchanged text since the seventh century. Nashr says hostile eloquent Arabs could not rival even a verse, despite eight centuries of recitation; Tabatabai lists preservation among the Qur'an's predictive challenges. Ibn Ashur says a human composition would corrupt or contradict (4:82); the promise honors the Book, angers polytheists and announces religion's endurance. Nashr adds that its inexhaustible guidance meant no succession of prophets was needed, links 35:32's chosen inheritors to preservation, and transmits Anas's report of God's special people with trustworthy transmitters. Tabatabai calls the verse exceptionally rich in truths. [18 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; NASHR(النبي); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 15:9/q22 Can the preservation verse itself refute claims of textual corruption?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the challenged verse's integrity may be assumed when the very integrity of the received text is disputed.
- 15:9/q22/p1 Yes: its promise rules out additions, omissions and alterations. — Al-Qadi invokes the verse against some Imamis who allege alteration; Alusi recounts the same argument. Tabatabai instead builds a cumulative case from the current Qur'an's descriptions, including 41:41-42, and says it rules out alteration destructive of its status as remembrance. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي); TABATABAI]
- 15:9/q22/p2 As a stand-alone proof against that opponent, the argument is circular. — Razi says the objector could claim this very verse was added; Alusi adds that the amount's inimitability still needs proof. Tabatabai similarly says neither consensus nor authenticity of distortion reports can be settled without first proving the Qur'an as source, and favors the extant text's qualities. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القاضي); TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+]

## 15:9/q23 What happened when a scholar tested altered scripture copies?  [* · reports]
- 15:9/q23/p1 Torah and Gospel purchasers accepted altered copies, but Qur'an copyists detected and rejected the changes. — Al-Qurtubi transmits Yahya ibn Aktham's account: a Jewish scholar altered copies of each scripture, saw the contrast in buyers' reactions, and converted. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن أكثم)]

## 15:9/q24 What is reported about Ali's collection after the Prophet's death?  [* · reports]
- 15:9/q24/p1 Ali compiled a seven-part Qur'an and brought it on a camel. — Tabatabai reports Yaqubi's account of Ali's collection after the Prophet's death. His other notes say Ali's codex followed revelation order and may have differed from the official codex in placement rather than religious substance. [1 notes: TABATABAI(اليعقوبي)]
