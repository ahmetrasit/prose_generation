# 87:15/q11 Were verses 14–15 revealed in Mecca or Medina? [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the Eid-related closing verses share the opening's date.
- 87:15/q11/p1 The whole sūra, including these verses, is Meccan.
  reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī call this consensus; opponents invoke it against Eid and fitr applications. Qutb calls the sūra Meccan and says it has nineteen verses.
  holders (7 notes): THALABI; BAGHAWI(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الثعلبي); RAZI-FULL; ITQAN(بعضهم); QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: THALABI:v10p185/r1 BAGHAWI:87:15/r4 IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p91#4/r5 RAZI:87:15/r10 RAZI-FULL:v31p134/r4 ITQAN:v1p106/r2 QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r1
- 87:15/q11/p2 The opening is Meccan, but the closing verses about fitr alms and Eid came in Medina.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this from Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says reports calling the sūra Meccan can designate its opening; Study Quran reports the same family view.
  holders (3 notes): IBNASHUR(ابن عمر وابن عباس); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(family of the Prophet)
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:1/r8 TABATABAI:87:14/r1 STUDYQURAN:87:intro/r2
- 87:15/q11/p3 The Meccan date is stronger but not unanimous; a later legal realization remains possible.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī questions a claim of consensus and permits a later rule even if the sūra is Meccan.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL
  notes: ALUSI:87:15/r15 ALUSI-FULL:v30p109#2/r14

# 87:15/q12 How does this verse fit the sūra's surrounding argument? [* · links]
turns on: Which neighbouring lines and themes frame the promise of purification and prayer.
- 87:15/q12/p1 It promises success to those who respond to the Prophet's reminder, balancing the warning to those who avoid it.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum maps 87:10–11 to 87:12–15 as a chiasm and compares Q 92:5–21; it sees the messenger as exemplary to believers. Qutb and Iṣlāḥī connect the response to the reminder with divergent outcomes.
  holders (9 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r4 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r5 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r6 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r7 QUTB-ZILAL:87:10/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:87:15/r3 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p279/r4 ALUSI-FULL:v30p109#2/r19

  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī links it to 87:1 and an interwoven omission contrasting fire and purification. Corpus Coranicum says the warning implies fear, purification, invocation and prayer, returning to 87:1.

# 87:15/q13 What variant pronunciations affect words in this verse? [فَصَلَّىٰ وَذَكَرَ · readings]
turns on: The reported difference between verse-final and internal lām, and the noun's first vowel.
- 87:15/q13/p1 The final lām in fa-ṣallā may be softened at verse end, while nonfinal counterparts are pronounced emphatically.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī cites this preferred distinction for euphony against a preceding emphatic trigger; verse-final examples are 75:31, 87:15 and 96:10, contrasted with nonfinal yaslā at 87:12.
  holders (2 notes): NASHR; NASHR(أصحاب التفريق بين رؤوس الآي وغيرها)+
  notes: NASHR:v2p112/r6 NASHR:v2p112/r7
- 87:15/q13/p2 The noun for remembrance is pronounced with either kasra or ḍamma on its first consonant.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī records the two pronunciations.
  holders (1 notes): BIQAI
  notes: BIQAI:87:15/r2
sources: BIQAI = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; NASHR = Ibn al-Jazarī, d. 833
