# 87:15/q04 How does remembrance relate in time to prayer? [وَذَكَرَ فَصَلَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the coordinating fāʾ enforces chronological sequence.
- 87:15/q04/p1 Remembrance precedes and prompts prayer.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās says reversing the conjunction's order violates normal syntax. Abū Ḥanīfa insists the fāʾ has sequential force. Ibn ʿĀshūr says recollection naturally produces reverence and prayer.

# 87:15/q05 What does the wording prove about the opening takbīr's legal status? [وَذَكَرَ ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ فَصَلَّىٰ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse's remembrance is specifically the opening takbīr.
- 87:15/q05/p1 It proves the opening invocation is obligatory.
  reasons: The Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Nasafī tie the invocation to success. Al-Ālūsī reports the Ḥanafī argument that it stands between purification from shirk and prayer, both obligations.

# 87:15/q06 Is the opening takbīr outside prayer or a part of it? [وَذَكَرَ ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ فَصَلَّىٰ · law]

- 87:15/q06/p1 It precedes prayer as a distinct condition.

# 87:15/q07 May prayer be opened with any divine name? [ٱسْمَ رَبِّهِۦ · law]
turns on: Whether 'His name' is a legal specification of the prayer-opening formula.
- 87:15/q07/p1 Yes; the general expression permits any of God's names.
  reasons: The Kashshāf, Abū Ḥanīfa, al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī infer this from 'His name'; al-Qurṭubī notes a juristic dispute, and al-Ālūsī says this follows on the takbīr interpretation.

# 87:15/q08 What is the order and point of purification, remembrance, and prayer? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the order describes spiritual stages, means of purification, a practical charity sequence, or esoteric realization.
- 87:15/q08/p1 They trace belief's purification, knowledge or confession of God, then obedient bodily worship.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī describes removing false belief, knowing divine names and attributes, then humble service. Al-Ālūsī maps the sequence to heart, tongue and body; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a cleansed soul receives guidance readily. Al-Biqāʿī sees removal of defects and acquisition of perfections.
