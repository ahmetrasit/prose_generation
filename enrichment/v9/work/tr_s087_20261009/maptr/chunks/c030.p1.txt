IBNABIHATIM (عبد الله بن مسعود، علي، ابن عمر، عائشة): Ibn Masud calls dice non-Arab maysir and warns against them; Ali, Ibn Umar, and Aisha are reported similarly. IBNABIHATIM (علي بن أبي طالب): Ali says chess is a form of maysir. IBNABIHATIM (سعيد بن المسيب): Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab says a form of pre-Islamic maysir was selling meat for one or two sheep. IBNABIHATIM (القاسم بن محمد): Al-Qasim ibn Muhammad says backgammon is maysir whenever it distracts from remembrance of God and prayer. IBNABIHATIM (النبي): A report from the Prophet lists betting over pigeons, gambling, and throwing dice as three forms of maysir. SULAMI (author): Wine and gambling are described as matters of conjecture and deception. THALABI (author): Maysir is linked to yasara, meaning something became due; a yāsir is the lot-caster. Al-Nabigha and another poet use yāsir for a gambler. THALABI (مقاتل): Mujahid says it was named maysir because gamblers said the camel's price had become theirs; wealthy Arabs bought a camel and divided it into shares. THALABI (أبو عمرو): Abu Amr counts ten shares in the camel-gambling game. THALABI (طاوس ومجاهد وعطاء): They define maysir as anything involving gambling, even children's games with sticks and dice. THALABI (رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم): The Prophet warns against the two marked dice, calling them a form of Persian gambling. THALABI (علي): Ali calls backgammon and chess forms of maysir. THALABI (القاسم بن محمد): Al-Qasim ibn Muhammad says anything that distracts from remembrance of God and prayer is maysir. MAWARDI-FULL (author): Maysir means gambling and is connected to the expression yasara lī, meaning that a thing came easily or was attainable. WAHIDI-BASIT (ابن عباس): Maysir means gambling; Ibn Abbas says a pre-Islamic man wagered his family and property, which the loser surrendered. WAHIDI-BASIT (author): Maysir is a mafʿil from yasara, to become due; yāsir is one whose lot or wager becomes due, then the terms meant gambling and gambler. WAHIDI-WAJIZ (author): Maysir means gambling. JISHUMI (author): Maysir means gambling; proposed derivations connect it with something won by the arrows, with dividing the camel’s meat into shares, or with ease in arranging the joint purchase, all carried out as a lottery. BAGHAWI-FULL (author): Al-Maysir means gambling; it derives from yassara li, something becoming due, and the gambler is called yāsir or yasir. BAGHAWI-FULL (author): The verse covers all forms of gambling, down to children’s games with nuts and dice; Baghawī reports that Ṭāwūs, ʿAṭāʾ, and Mujāhid included them. BAGHAWI-FULL (علي): A report from ʿAlī classifies backgammon and chess as maysir. KASHSHAF-FULL (author): Maysir is a verbal noun for gambling, deriving either from ease because it takes wealth effortlessly or from yasar because it strips away wealth. KASHSHAF-FULL (ابن عباس): Ibn Abbas says that in pre-Islamic times a man would wager his family and wealth; the cited poem's ييسرونني compares what is done to the speaker with what gamblers do to a wagered person. KASHSHAF-FULL (النبي): A Prophetic report warns against two ill-omened games, described as the non-Arab form of gambling; no grading is stated. KASHSHAF-FULL (علي): Ali classifies backgammon and chess as gambling. KASHSHAF-FULL (ابن سيرين): Ibn Sirin says that anything involving a stake or risk is gambling. IBNATIYYA-FULL (المؤمنون): The people asking about wine and gambling are believers. IBNATIYYA-FULL (الأعشى): Al-Aʿshā’s verse praises the lottery’s provision of food to guests and the poor in winter, illustrating its social benefit. IBNATIYYA-FULL (محمد بن سيرين والحسن وابن عباس وابن المسيب وغيرهم): They classify every form of gambling as maysir, including dice, chess, and even children’s nut games. IBNATIYYA-FULL (لبيد): Labīd’s line says the lottery need not produce shameful acts, another poetic illustration of the practice’s benefits before the Qurʾānic judgment of its sin. IBNATIYYA-FULL (author): Two cited poetic lines use y-s-r language for the slaughter-and-lottery practice, supporting the derivation from slaughter. IBNATIYYA-FULL (author): The lottery used seven arrows with assigned shares and three blanks without shares: al-fadhdh, al-tawʾam, al-raqīb, al-ḥils, al-nāfis, al-musbil, al-muʿallā; al-manīḥ, al-safīḥ, and al-waghd were blanks. TABRISI (author): A further derivation connects maysir to ease, since people joined to make sharing the camel easier, though they did so by gambling. TABRISI (author): Maysir is gambling, named from yusr because the winner's share becomes due; the gambler is called yāsir because lots determine what is owed. TABRISI (ابن عباس وابن مسعود ومجاهد وقتادة والحسن): Ibn Abbas, Ibn Masʿud, Mujahid, Qatada, and al-Hasan say maysir means all gambling; Imami reports count children’s nut games as gambling too. IBNJAWZI-ZAD (ابن عباس وابن عمر والحسن وسعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وقتادة): Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn ʿUmar, al-Hasan, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Mujāhid, Qatāda, and others define maysir as gambling. RAZI-FULL (author): Maysir means gambling and is the verbal noun of yasara, as mawʿid and marjiʿ derive from their verbs; yassartuhu means I gambled it. RAZI-FULL (مقاتل): Muqatil derives maysir from ease, since it takes another's wealth effortlessly, or from yasar, affluence, because gambling can make one wealthy. RAZI-FULL (ابن عباس): Ibn Abbas says that in pre-Islamic times a man would wager his family and property. RAZI-FULL (ابن قتيبة): Ibn Qutayba derives maysir from apportioning: the camel is named for being divided, the butcher for portioning it, and gamblers are called yasirun because they divide its meat. RAZI-FULL (رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم): A Prophetic report warns against two dice as a form of Persian gambling. RAZI-FULL (ابن سيرين ومجاهد وعطاء): Ibn Sirin, Mujahid, and Ata regard any game involving a stake as gambling, even children's play with nuts. RAZI-FULL (علي): Ali classifies backgammon and chess as gambling. QURTUBI-FULL (الأزهري): Al-Azhari says maysir can name the camel gambled over because it is divided into shares; anything divided is described with the same root. QURTUBI-FULL (مالك): Malik divides maysir into amusement, including dice, chess, and entertainments, and gambling involving wagers. QURTUBI-FULL (علي بن أبي طالب): Ali calls chess “the maysir of the non-Arabs.” QURTUBI-FULL (مالك وجماعة العلماء): Malik and other scholars treat anything wagered upon as maysir. QURTUBI-FULL (سعيد بن المسيب): Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib said pre-Islamic maysir included selling meat for one or two live sheep; Mālik transmitted this in the Muwaṭṭaʾ through Dāwūd ibn Ḥuṣayn. QURTUBI-FULL (author): The lottery used eleven arrows; seven marked arrows had graded shares: al-fidh, al-tawʾam, al-raqīb, al-ḥalis, al-nafis or al-nafiz, al-misbal, and al-muʿallā. Al-Aṣmaʿī says their shares totaled twenty-eight portions. BAYDAWI-FULL (author): Maysir is a verbal noun like mawʿid; gambling is so called because it takes another’s wealth easily or strips away prosperity. NASAFI-FULL (author): The ruling includes all forms of gambling, such as backgammon and chess. NASAFI-FULL (author): Maysir is gambling, from يسر; it may be named for taking wealth easily without effort or for depriving someone of wealth. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (author): Maysir means gambling and follows the pattern maf'il from yasara, as maw'id derives from wa'ada; yasartu al-maysir means I gambled. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (author): The name maysir is variously derived from ease, affluence because gambling strips it away, something becoming due, or slaughtering and cutting up a camel. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (author): The poem's use of تيسرون and يسرت supports connecting maysir with gambling. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (علي): ʿAlī calls chess “the maysir of the non-Arabs.” ABUHAYYAN-FULL (القاسم): Al-Qāsim says anything that distracts from remembering God and prayer counts as maysir. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (author): Maysir first named pre-Islamic Arab gambling; in law it covers every form of gambling, whose prohibition is agreed. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (مالك): Mālik distinguishes amusement maysir, such as dice and chess, from gambling maysir, in which people wager against one another. SAMIN-DURR (author): Maysir means gambling and is formed on mifʿal from yusr/yasara-yaysiru. Alqama's line uses ييسرون and another poet's uses ييسرونني as usage evidence. SAMIN-DURR (author): One derivation takes maysir from ease, because winnings are easy to obtain; another derives it from wealth, since gambling strips a person of wealth. IBNKATHIR-FULL (author): Maysir means gambling. BIQAI-FULL (الحرالي): Harali calls maysir a pre-Islamic gambling institution intended to benefit the weak and to gain the spoils of victory. BIQAI-FULL (الأزهري): Al-Azhari derives maysir from dividing the staked camel into shares; yāsir first meant the butcher who apportioned its meat, then the gamblers who caused the slaughter. BIQAI-FULL (المجد الفيروزآبادي): Al-Fayruzabadi's dictionary defines maysir as playing with arrows, the camel staked in the game, backgammon, or gambling in general. BIQAI-FULL (author): Qimār is any wager based purely on uncertain risk; the author derives its name from the moon because a gambler's wealth waxes and wanes like the moon. BIQAI-FULL (مجاهد): Mujahid is quoted as defining maysir broadly as anything involving gambling, even children playing with walnuts. BIQAI-FULL (عبد الغافر الفارسي): Abd al-Ghafir al-Farsi describes maysir as pre-Islamic gambling over a camel or other property divided into shares, but notes that accounts differ and Muslim lexicographers lacked firm knowledge of its procedure. DURR-FULL (ابن عمر): Ibn Umar glosses al-maysir as gambling. DURR-FULL (مجاهد): Mujahid says al-maysir means gambling and derives its name from gamblers' phrase aysara jazuran, meaning to stake a camel. DURR-FULL (ابن عباس)
