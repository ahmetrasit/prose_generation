# 43:10: 15 questions

## 43:10/q01 How is ٱلَّذِى construed grammatically?  [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative noun continues the preceding description of al-ʿazīz or begins a sentence with an understood huwa.
- 43:10/q01/p1 It is the predicate of an omitted subject, understood as 'He who made.' — Al-Nahhas gives an omitted subject as one possibility. Ibn Ashur supplies huwa alladhi and, following al-Sakkaki's terminology, says subject omission accords with established usage; the relative pronoun is known through its clause and functions like a proper name. [3 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:10/q01/p2 It is a nominative adjective of al-ʿazīz in the preceding verse. — Al-Nahhas gives this as the other nominative analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 43:10/q02 Whose speech begins with ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ?  [ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ لَكُمُ · referent]
- 43:10/q02/p1 It begins God's own description after the report of the unbelievers' answer. — The author of al-Nazm, al-Razi, and al-Qurtubi note that unbelievers would have said 'for us,' whereas the verse says 'for you'; al-Razi adds that the later 'We revived' is divine speech and compares a listener adding praise of a mosque's builder after another speaker names him. Al-Wahidi says the new clause adds a true divine attribute to the one quoted from them; Ibn Atiyya calls it a fresh report; Abu Hayyan says it reminds people of earlier blessings; Bursevi calls it a fresh divine relative clause. Both speakers' descriptions concern the same God. Al-Qurtubi says the new declaration displays God's complete power. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 43:10/q03 What is the force of directly addressing people and repeating لَكُمُ?  [لَكُمُ لَكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 43:10/q03/p1 The address presses the rebuke and highlights God's benefits to people. — Al-Biqaʿi says the shift to direct address sharpens rebuke and recalls kindness that ought to bring submission. Al-Tabatabaʾi says it exposes the incoherence of admitting God alone creates while assigning governance elsewhere. Ibn Ashur says the two occurrences of lakum mark human benefit and distinguish this passage from 78:6–7, where vast creation argues for resurrection; he adds that the eleven second-person pronouns across four verses are expected address, not iltifat. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:10/q04 What does making the earth مَهْدًۭا convey?  [ٱلْأَرْضَ مَهْدًۭا · meaning]
- 43:10/q04/p1 God prepared the earth as a stable, comfortable, usable resting place for human life and movement. — Muqatil calls it a bed or floor; al-Tabari a spread bed to walk and tread on, citing 20:53, and reports al-Suddi's carpet; al-Mawardi a resting spread; al-Thaʿlabi a cradle; Ibn Atiyya a prepared spread for settling and movement; al-Razi cites stability, cultivation, construction, concealment of flaws of living and dead, and a child's cradle's comforts. Al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi stress rest and settlement; Ibn Kathir says people walk, stand, and sleep on it and that mountains fix it above flowing water against swaying. Al-Biqaʿi says it is firm and level though God could have made it quake or unstable on water. Bursevi defines mahd and mihad as prepared and smoothed, cites 2:22, and reports Bahr al-ʿUlum's sitting, sleeping, and turning in a bed. Al-Alusi says its immense size reconciles a leveled surface with a spherical body; Ibn Ashur says its flattened habitable surface is what benefits people here despite its spherical body, and cites 7:41. Al-Tabatabaʾi likens its nurturing to a child's cradle; Kuranyolu specifies walking, working, resting, and living; Study Quran reports ease of travel, agriculture, and habitation. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(كوفي وغيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 43:10/q05 How is the word مَهْدًۭا read?  [مَهْدًۭا · readings]
turns on: The transmitted readers and the presence or absence of the alif, with corresponding vowel patterns; the notes say the sense is shared.
- 43:10/q05/p1 Mahdan, without an alif after hāʾ. — Ibn Mujahid assigns this to ʿAsim, Hamza, and al-Kisaʾi in Ta Ha and al-Zukhruf; al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi call it the Kufan reading, and Ibn Atiyya also names Ibn Masʿud, Talha, and al-Aʿmash. Ibn al-Jazari specifies fatḥa on mīm and still hāʾ, and calls Ibn Mihran's report from Rawh erroneous. Al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur name ʿAsim; Ibn Ashur and Ibn Atiyya say its sense equals mihadan. Qiraat-ER presents mahdan as the form replaced by its variant. [8 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون); NASAFI-FULL(كوفي وغيره); NASHR(الكوفيون); ALUSI-FULL(عاصم); IBNASHUR-FULL(عاصم); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw)]
- 43:10/q05/p2 Mihādan, with an alif after hāʾ. — Ibn Mujahid assigns this to Ibn Kathir, Nafiʿ, Abu ʿAmr, and Ibn ʿAmir throughout the Qurʾan. Ibn Atiyya calls it the majority reading; al-Baydawi says non-Kufans read it; al-Nasafi agrees. Ibn al-Jazari specifies kasra on mīm, fatḥa on hāʾ, and an alif. Al-Tabrisi reports it as an alternative and points to Ta Ha for its explanation. Qiraat-ER says the form emphasizes bedding or prepared spreading; Ibn Atiyya says it means the same as mahdan. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(كوفي وغيره); NASHR(الكوفيون); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw)]

## 43:10/q06 What inward analogy is drawn from the earth as a resting place?  [ٱلْأَرْضَ مَهْدًۭا · inward]
- 43:10/q06/p1 Bodies house spirits as the earth houses bodies, and each habitation ends. — Al-Biqaʿi says bodily dwelling makes spirits fit for presence and travel and shows God's rule over the cosmos. He reports al-Qushayri's analogy between the earth's eventual ruin when souls' earthly term ends and bodily ruin when spirits depart. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(القشيري)]

## 43:10/q07 What does جَعَلَ mean in this clause?  [جَعَلَ · meaning]
- 43:10/q07/p1 It means making a thing one way rather than another. — Bursevi gives this sense while describing the clause as a fresh divine statement. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 43:10/q08 Why is جَعَلَ repeated before the paths?  [جَعَلَ وَجَعَلَ · rhetoric]
- 43:10/q08/p1 Its repetition stresses God's ability to provide means that ease difficult tasks. — Al-Biqaʿi connects the repeated verb with making hard undertakings easy. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 43:10/q09 Why is ٱلَّذِى not repeated before وَجَعَلَ?  [ٱلَّذِى وَجَعَلَ · rhetoric]
- 43:10/q09/p1 Both clauses concern the earth and form one imagined act. — Ibn Ashur explains the omission of a second relative noun on this basis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:10/q10 What are the سُبُلًۭا made through the earth?  [سُبُلًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether subul denotes travel routes in the terrain or broader means and resources for sustaining life.
- 43:10/q10/p1 They are passable roads or routes for travel. — Muqatil, Qatada as reported by ʿAbd al-Razzaq and al-Tabari, al-Suddi as reported by al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, and al-Tabatabaʾi give roads or routes. Al-Tabari says they ease travel between towns for livelihood and trade; al-Razi adds identifying markers and movement between regions. Ibn Kathir and al-Biqaʿi place routes through mountains and valleys; al-Biqaʿi notes that land might have been impassable. Bursevi includes worldly and religious travel and reports al-Raghib's definition of a path as easy to traverse. Kuranyolu names valleys, plains, and passes; Study Quran cites 21:31, 67:15, and 71:19–20 for navigable routes. Al-Thaʿlabi and al-Tabrisi connect them with finding destinations. [20 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 43:10/q10/p2 They are means of livelihood or resources meeting people's needs. — Al-Mawardi gives means of livelihood. Al-Qurtubi reports this beside the road interpretation. Kuranyolu derives a wider sense from subul as means or remedies: resources for varied needs. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:10/q11 What guidance is intended by لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ?  [لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance follows the roads to destinations, follows reflection and revelation to religious truth, recognizes God's favor, or finds subsistence; al-Tabatabaʾi prefers the immediate travel context.
- 43:10/q11/p1 People find their way to destinations on earthly journeys. — Muqatil says people recognize their routes. Al-Tabari says roads take them to lands, villages, and cities and that without roads they could scarcely leave their homes. Ibn ʿIsa, reported by al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi, and Hasan and Muqatil, reported by al-Wahidi, specify journeys. Al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Tabatabaʾi, Bursevi, and al-Alusi speak of reaching destinations. Al-Razi joins navigation with religious guidance. Al-Biqaʿi adds distant travel, useful aims, and routes that guide by repeated use. Study Quran says God facilitates earthly travel. Al-Tabatabaʾi explicitly prefers this sense over religious guidance. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عيسى); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(قيل)+]
- 43:10/q11/p2 People reach religious truth or knowledge of God's unity through reflection on His works. — Ibn ʿAbbas, reported by al-Wahidi, sees truth reached by reflecting on the provided land; Ibn Atiyya and al-Tabrisi offer reflection and consideration. Al-Qurtubi says created paths reveal God's power. Al-Razi combines this with practical navigation; al-Baydawi mentions the Creator's wisdom, Abu Hayyan reflection, and al-Biqaʿi the wisdom ordering the world. Bursevi and al-Alusi call divine unity the primary goal; al-Tabatabaʾi reports knowledge and exclusive worship of God as an alternative but prefers road guidance. Study Quran adds signs and revelation leading to truth and Paradise, citing 20:53, 2:53, 2:150, 7:158, 16:15, 21:31, 23:49, and 32:3. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 43:10/q11/p3 People recognize God's favor to them. — Saʿid b. Jubayr gives this explanation as reported by al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 43:10/q11/p4 People find their means of livelihood. — Al-Mawardi allows this sense of guidance; al-Qurtubi reports it as another explanation. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 43:10/q12 What does this verse show about God and His relation to people?  [* · theology]
- 43:10/q12/p1 God's beneficial ordering of the earth demonstrates His power and is evidence against idolatry. — Ibn Atiyya says these acts are blessings to humanity and proof against every disbelieving polytheist. Al-Baghawi treats them as works demonstrating God's existence. Al-Qurtubi says the divine declaration displays complete power. Ibn Ashur says the two relative clauses show exclusive power and beneficence. Al-Tabatabaʾi says creation entails governance and rebukes those who split the two. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 43:10/q13 How does this verse advance the surrounding argument?  [* · links]
- 43:10/q13/p1 After the admission that God creates, the passage points to His provision and governance and challenges the polytheists. — Al-Wahidi says the land-making clause adds God's true attribute to the one quoted from the unbelievers. Al-Baghawi says the account turns to God's works as proof of existence. Ibn Ashur says the proof that God alone created heaven and earth moves to proof that He alone supplies life's necessities, a resumption interrupting the transition to 43:15; he cites 43:9 and 43:15. Al-Tabatabaʾi says direct address rebukes a separation of creation from governance. Kuranyolu says the passage exposes contradictions and unsupported beliefs and indirectly calls to monotheism. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:10/q14 How does this verse relate to the description in Ṭā Hā 20:53?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes distinguish a shared subject and cross-reference from the precise change of verb between the two passages.
- 43:10/q14/p1 The earth and paths description parallels Ṭā Hā 20:53, where some commentators locate its explanation. — Al-Tabari cites 20:53 beside the earth as a spread bed and al-Suddi's carpet and roads. Al-Wahidi says this clause's explanation was given there; Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Tabrisi likewise refer back to that explanation, al-Tabrisi especially for mihadan. Asad cross-references the cradle image; Study Quran includes 20:53 among its guidance parallels. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:10/q14/p2 Ṭā Hā says wa-salaka lakum fīhā subulan where al-Zukhruf says wa-jaʿala lakum fīhā subulan. — Al-Burhan records the differing verbs in the parallel clauses. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 43:10/q15 How does this verse fit the sūra's overall theme?  [* · links]
- 43:10/q15/p1 It belongs to a coherent challenge to the Makkan idolaters' worldview. — Islahi says every part relates deeply to the central theme and the whole sura is harmonious. Study Quran says the sura answers Makkan idolaters' objections and addresses forms of disbelief; Kuranyolu says this passage exposes their contradictions and calls indirectly to monotheism. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
