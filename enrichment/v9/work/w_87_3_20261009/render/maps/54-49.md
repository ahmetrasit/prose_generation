# 54:49: 24 questions

## 54:49/q01 Why does God say 'We'?  [إِنَّا · rhetoric]
- 54:49/q01/p1 The plural pronoun denotes God alone. — Abu Ubayda cites the singular referents of a plural expression in 3:173 and the messenger's singular statement in 19:19. He also begins a distinct category of nouns whose singular and plural forms coincide, without giving its example here. [2 notes: MAJAZ]

## 54:49/q02 What does the emphatic opening contribute?  [إِنَّا · rhetoric]
- 54:49/q02/p1 It reinforces the assertion about creation by measure. — Ibn Ashur relates the force of inna here to its use in the prior statement about the criminals in 54:47; he also says the fronted object reinforces the assertion and that wisdom in recompense warrants emphasis. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 54:49/q03 How is kull read?  [كُلَّ · readings]
turns on: Whether the fronted noun receives accusative from an implicit verb or nominative as a sentence subject.
- 54:49/q03/p1 The established or majority reading has accusative kulla. — Al-Akhfash records nominative as well. Jishumi contrasts the common accusative with Abu al-Sammal's nominative. Ibn Atiyya calls the accusative the majority reading; Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Study Quran call it the common or communal reading, and Study Quran says all recitation schools use it. Razi calls it sounder; Nasafi calls the nominative anomalous. Abu Hatim and Abu al-Fath read with the community despite judging nominative stronger Arabic. [18 notes: AKHFASH; JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم); TABRISI(أبو السماك,محمد بن يزيد); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو السمال); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفتح,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN(most commentators)+]
- 54:49/q03/p2 Abu al-Sammal and others read nominative kullu. — Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisī, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Alusi report Abu al-Sammal's reading; Tabrisī calls it noncanonical and also reports anna with it. Ibn Atiyya and Samin mention some Sunni readers. Ibn Jinni and Abu al-Fath prefer its Arabic construction; Abu Hatim judges it best grammar while reciting accusative. Makki says Basran principles favor it, whereas Kufan principles favor accusative after inna. [17 notes: AKHFASH; JISHUMI(أبو السمال العدوي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم); TABRISI(أبو السماك); RAZI-FULL(ابن عطية); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو السمال); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفتح,ابن عطية,مكي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال وابن عطية وقوم من أهل السنة); STUDYQURAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال وأبو الفتح)+; TABRISI(ابن جني)+]

## 54:49/q04 How does kull shay'in relate syntactically to khalaqnahu?  [كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقْنَٰهُ · grammar]
turns on: The case, the understood governor, and whether khalaqnahu predicates creation or restricts shay' as an adjective.
- 54:49/q04/p1 Accusative kull is a fronted object resumed by the pronoun in khalaqnahu. — Al-Akhfash and the Basrans compare Abd Allah, I struck him; Sibawayh compares Zaydan darabtuhu. Jishumi calls it fronted object construction; Ibn Ashur says the explicit object before its pronoun and inna both reinforce the statement. [5 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); NAHHAS(سيبويه); JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 54:49/q04/p2 Accusative kull is governed by an omitted verb explained by khalaqnahu, so creation applies to every thing. — Zajjaj supplies 'We created every thing We created by measure.' Another grammarian and Tabari say the verb has priority after inna; Kufans similarly favor a verb. Razi compares 36:39 and 76:31 and cites 40:62 and 13:8 for an understood verb; his alternative reconstructs 'created' or 'measured' with restricted wording, which he says weakens the anti-Mu'tazilite inference. Qurtubi, Nasafi, Samin, Alusi, Biqai, and Ibn Ashur say accusative clarifies universal creation and avoids mistaking khalaqnahu for a modifier. Samin argues an adjective cannot govern or explain an earlier governor. Ibn Ashur adds that word order keeps bi-qadar beside its verb and avoids an unfinished predicate for inna. Tabari rejects the adjectival reading as yielding an ill-fitting 'We created it every thing.' [35 notes: TAB-FULL(غيره); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الكوفيون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قوم); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري,بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب الخفاجي)+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI(محمد بن يزيد)+; TABRISI(ابن جني)-]
- 54:49/q04/p3 Nominative kull is subject and khalaqnahu bi-qadar is its predicate. — Abu al-Fath and Ibn Jinni find this more idiomatic, like Zayd, I struck him; Ibn Jinni says inna changes the noun's case without changing the clause's structure and rejects al-Mubarrid's appeal to an implicit verb because a nominal clause's predicate is originally nominal. Abu Hatim calls it best grammar. Razi rejects the objection that an indefinite cannot start a useful clause: universal kull shay conveys information, unlike bare rajul qa'im. Samin places bi-qadar as predicate in another nominative analysis. Al-Radi says predicate and accusative readings still concern created things. [15 notes: AKHFASH; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم,أهل السنة); TABRISI(ابن جني); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال وابن عطية وقوم من أهل السنة,الرضي); STUDYQURAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال وأبو الفتح)+; TABRISI(ابن جني)+]
- 54:49/q04/p4 Nominative kull is subject, khalaqnahu qualifies shay', and bi-qadar is predicate: every thing God created is measured. — Al-Akhfash and Basrans allow an adjectival analysis. Qadariyya use it to leave acts attributed to human creation outside the assertion; Razi's reported objection cites 13:8 and contrasts 41:17. Samin says nominative may imply uncreated-by-God things outside the ruling. Al-Radi regards both nominative parses as restricted to created things, but al-Sayyid al-Sanad objects that the adjective leaves other created things unasserted. Tabari, Razi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Samin, Alusi, and Ibn Ashur argue against the adjective or its implication; Razi contrasts 51:49, which can be adjectival without this pronoun obstacle. [10 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(القدرية); RAZI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القدرية); SAMIN-DURR(القدرية); ALUSI-FULL(الرضي); STUDYQURAN; TAB-FULL-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أهل السنة)-; RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-; NASAFI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(السيد السند,الشهاب الخفاجي)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 54:49/q04/p5 The accusative has special force because inna favors a verb and foregrounds universal creation. — The Kufans contrast the dislocated object analogy. Al-Nahhas says accusative refutes deniers of created acts. Nasafi says the nominative can allow an adjectival parse; Samin and Abu al-Baqa say accusative excludes the implication of other creators. Al-Shihab objects but Alusi judges the accusative's clarity worth its ellipsis. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(غيره); NAHHAS(الكوفيون); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب الخفاجي)+; NASAFI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI(ابن جني)-]

## 54:49/q05 What does 'everything' include?  [كُلَّ شَىْءٍ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'everything' quantifies God's creations only or makes the creation of every thing its assertion, including deeds.
- 54:49/q05/p1 It spans created entities, properties, acts, and events, with no competing creator. — Ibn Ashur includes substances and accidents. Al-Qasim enumerates souls, deeds, traces, thoughts, breaths, moments, traits, lifespans, and livelihoods. Ibn Abbas includes good and evil and their ultimate felicity or wretchedness. Razi says human acts are things under the universal accusative parse; Sunni interpreters cite it against separate creators. Biqai includes the small and great and disbelievers' persistence. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); SULAMI(القاسم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أهل السنة); SAMIN-DURR(أهل السنة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 54:49/q05/p2 The phrase means all created things, excluding God and things proved uncreated or never created. — Razi says God, without beginning or measure, is outside the speaker's compositional scope rather than an exception to the predicate; he compares a person saying everyone in a house. Ibn Atiyya exempts the Quran and divine attributes on rational proof. Alusi excludes the infinitely many possible things that God never brought into being. Al-Radi says both readings refer to created things. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الرضي)]
- 54:49/q05/p3 The adjectival nominative reading restricts the assertion to things God created and leaves human acts or other things open. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Study Quran report Qadari and Mu'tazilite use of the restriction. Razi reports a nominative analysis that requires it but argues another subject-predicate analysis has stronger universal force. Al-Sayyid al-Sanad disputes al-Radi's claim that the readings convey the same coverage. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(القدرية); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القدرية); SAMIN-DURR(أهل الزيغ,القدرية); ALUSI-FULL(الرضي); STUDYQURAN; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-; ALUSI-FULL(السيد السند)-]

## 54:49/q06 What does khalaqnahu mean here?  [خَلَقْنَٰهُ · meaning]
- 54:49/q06/p1 It means deliberate bringing into existence, and can also convey making a distinct, clear reality. — Ibn Ashur says khalq literally denotes deliberate origination of entities and metaphorically producing clear meanings; he sees both senses here and cites 29:17. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 54:49/q07 What does bi-qadar mean?  [بِقَدَرٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether qadar denotes antecedent decree, precise proportion and function, or the specific allotted outcome in context.
- 54:49/q07/p1 It means creation according to prior determination, known and written before occurrence. — Tabari speaks of a measure God determined and decreed. Zajjaj, Wahidi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Tabrisī, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Alusi, Elmalili, and Study Quran mention the Preserved Tablet, often with each thing's state and time. Wahidi cites 54:52; Ibn Kathir says Sunni imams invoke this and sound reports against late arising Qadariyya. Mawridi adduces a rajaz line for an unavoidable prior decree. Razi cites 77:23 and poetry and compares deliberate measurement with an arrow accidentally landing somewhere; he rejects philosophers' natural necessity, since God could reverse sizes. Bursevi's Persian couplet invokes the dried pen and eternal Tablet. [30 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,بعض العلماء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(كثير من السلف); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarites,al-Zamakhsharī)]
- 54:49/q07/p2 It means each thing's fitting size, form, order, function, and allotted limit, according to wisdom. — Ibn Bahr says nothing is added or lacking. Al-Hasan and al-Rabi invoke 65:3; Tabrisī gives the tongue, hand, foot, eye, ear, and stomach their proper functions. Kalbi through Ibn Abbas and Tabrisī illustrate pairing woman and man, female and male animals, and gendered clothing. Razi describes essence, extension, countable atoms, finite qualities, and finite duration unlike God's eternity, citing 13:8. Ibn Kathir cites 25:2 and 87:1-3. Tabatabai distinguishes universal dependent possibility from particular causal limits: conditions, impediments, form, dimensions, states, and actions shape each being and guide its course, citing 15:21, 20:50, 87:2-3, and 4:126. Ibn Ashur says ordered laws serve wisdom; Hell is within the order. Study Quran cites Tabrisī's point that altered measures would defeat functions. [36 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,الكلبي عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الجبائي,الحسن); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,بعض العلماء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Zamakhsharī,al-Ṭabrisī,commentators favoring Muʿtazilite views)]
- 54:49/q07/p3 In this context the measured thing is the criminals' appointed punishment and entry into Saqar. — Muqatil and Wahidi connect the measure to decreed punishment. Study Quran allows this contextual sense; Elmalili says criminals cannot change sin's nature and miserable result. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 54:49/q07/p4 The measure particularly includes allotted duration and the timed course of judgment. — Islahi says everything has a duration fitted to its purpose, including the timing of punishment; nations receive respite for their capacities to unfold until proof is complete, and Quraysh receive that measured delay. Razi also includes each created thing's finite duration. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 54:49/q07/p5 Here measuring out concerns God's giving or withholding provision to creation. — Study Quran connects qadar to allocation of provision in the Quraysh dispute. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 54:49/q08 How are qadar and qadr vocalized and related?  [بِقَدَرٍۢ · readings]
- 54:49/q08/p1 Both qadar and qadr are read and share the sense of measuring or determination. — Kashshaf and Samin report both forms. Abu Hayyan reports both vocalizations. Ibn Ashur says the form with a moving dal is synonymous with the form with a quiescent dal. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قراءة غير مسماة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 54:49/q09 What is the force of the ba' in bi-qadar?  [بِقَدَرٍۢ · grammar]
- 54:49/q09/p1 It marks accompanying measure and attaches to 'created'. — Ibn Ashur treats the phrase as a stable adjunct acting like a second object because measure is the point. Tabatabai also says it attaches to 'created' and means accompaniment. Samin says it is governed by the verb in the accusative reading but acts as predicate of kull in one nominative reading. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR]

## 54:49/q10 How does qadar relate to qada'?  [بِقَدَرٍۢ · theology]
turns on: How each term relates to knowledge, will, causal necessity, recorded decree, and actual occurrence.
- 54:49/q10/p1 Qada' is God's knowledge and qadar His will or determining intention, so events follow will rather than natural necessity. — Razi rejects the philosophers' distinction and applies his account against polytheists; Abu Hayyan reports a related pairing of knowledge and will with power. Study Quran reports Razi's rejection of philosophical necessity. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)+; RAZI-FULL(الفلاسفة)-]
- 54:49/q10/p2 Qada' is the intended effect and qadar its necessary consequence. — Razi reports philosophers' analogy: fire's heat is followed by burning cotton or reeds. He rejects this account, saying sizes and effects answer to God's will. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفلاسفة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 54:49/q10/p3 Qada' is collective existence in the Tablet, while qadar is actual existence in the world when conditions arise. — Bursevi states this distinction beside reports of the prewritten decrees. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 54:49/q10/p4 Qada' is irreversible decisive existence, while conditional qadar can change before the full cause is assembled. — Tabatabai distinguishes a possible thing's measure from decisive judgment. Causes, preparations, and impediments shape outcomes; 13:39 and 2:106 describe erasing and confirming. Once all causes meet and impediments vanish, qadar is followed by qada. A report in al-Mahasin says God executes what He decisively decrees, which cannot be averted; Ali's moving from a leaning wall illustrates moving from one decree to another. For some beings whose measure only concerns possibility and need, the two coincide. He directs readers to his discussion at Surat al-Isra. [8 notes: TABATABAI(الإمام,علي)]
- 54:49/q10/p5 Qada' is pretemporal and pertains to God's uncreated Essence; qadar is temporal and concerns His action. — Study Quran attributes this account to Ash'arites. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarites)]

## 54:49/q11 What occasioned the revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported immediate interlocutors were Quraysh or Najran, and whether reports describe the first occasion or a later application.
- 54:49/q11/p1 Quraysh polytheists disputed predestination with the Prophet, and this passage was revealed. — Abu Hurayra's report appears through Ahmad, Tabari, Mawridi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Razi, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Suyuti, Alusi, Ibn Majah, Muslim, and Tirmidhi. The reported span varies among 54:48-49, 54:47-49, and 54:45-49; Wāhidi notes Muslim's transmission; Qurtubi and Tirmidhi call a route hasan sahih. Muhammad ibn Ka'b independently calls the passage a rebuke of decree-deniers. Tabari reports Ibn Abbas wondering whether the punished deniers belong to past or future. [32 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو هريرة); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,محمد بن كعب,محمد بن كعب القرظي); IBNHIBBAN(أبو هريرة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو هريرة); WAHIDI-ASBAB(أبو هريرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس عن عطاء,محمد بن كعب القرظي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو هريرة); RAZI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أكثر المفسرين,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة والنبي); NASAFI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNMAJAH(أبو هريرة); MUSLIM(أبو هريرة); STUDYQURAN; TIRMIDHI(أبو هريرة)]
- 54:49/q11/p2 A Najran delegation's claim that actions were theirs but lifespans God's prompted the verses through 54:49. — Abu Dharr reports their question about why written sins incur punishment, and the Prophet's reply that they would dispute with God on Judgment Day. Qurtubi records the same occasion. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو ذر); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ذر)]
- 54:49/q11/p3 The verses concern later members of the community who deny God's decree. — Wahidi and Ibn Kathir report a Prophetic application to future deniers without giving a grade; Ibn Kathir also cites Amr ibn Shuayb's grandfather via al-Bazzar, while Durr calls that route good-chain and says 'only' about deniers. Wahidi cites Abu Umama's attributed report about the Qadariyya. Nasafi reports Umar's oath; Ibn Abbas and Muhammad ibn Ka'b likewise identify deniers. Razi gives no grade to the attributed Prophetic report and examines whether 'community' means everyone addressed by Muhammad or believers alone. [17 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(أبو أمامة الباهلي); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي,محمد بن كعب القرظي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جد عمرو بن شعيب); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله)]

## 54:49/q12 Who are the Qadariyya implicated in this passage?  [* · referent]
turns on: Which view of agency the polemical label names, and whether the reported community includes later believers.
- 54:49/q12/p1 They are deniers of divine decree, including the polytheists who disputed it and later counterparts. — Wahidi includes the polytheists and members of this community who share their status. Ibn Hibban calls them opponents of the Messenger. Razi says the Quraysh context points to people assigning events to stars and denying God's power even to feed, citing 36:47; if 'community' means all people addressed by Muhammad, the prophetic label need not include Mu'tazila. If it means believers, he says the analogy to Magians notes a weaker argument without proving certain damnation. He finally describes deniers of God's power or believers in another power, assigning them error and Saqar. [6 notes: IBNHIBBAN; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 54:49/q12/p2 The label applies to opponents who say God does not create human obedience and sin. — Razi reports Jabriyya using the label this way. Ibn Atiyya says Qadariyya claim each person alone creates acts. Study Quran describes Qadarites as authors of acts and Mu'tazilites as creators of evil deeds to preserve divine justice. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(القدرية); RAZI-FULL(الجبري); STUDYQURAN(Muʿtazilites,Qadarites)]
- 54:49/q12/p3 The label applies instead to determinists who attribute human sins to God's decree. — Razi reports the Mu'tazilite retort that attributing adultery and theft to decree merits the label; he also records rival parties labeling Sunnis Qadariyya for affirming God's creation of acts. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أهل السنة,المعتزلي)]

## 54:49/q13 Does the verse establish that God creates and decrees human acts?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether kull asserts universal divine creation of acts, merely measures what God created, or leaves acquisition and choice compatible with decree.
- 54:49/q13/p1 Yes: all acts, including good and evil, are subject to God's knowledge, will, power, and decree. — Ibn Abbas says good and evil are decreed, with happiness the best good and wretchedness the worst evil. Sunni readers use the accusative's universal force; Razi argues human acts are things. Qurtubi rejects a split between human actions and divine lifespans and says humans have acquisition and effort enabled by God. Bursevi cites 4:78 and says reward and punishment remain tied to servants' acts. Elmalili says partial will itself falls under decree. Ibn Ashur says good and evil are within God's power while reverent speech may withhold attribution of evil. Study Quran reports Jabarite appeals to 76:30 and 81:29, but Ash'arites reject fatalism and cite 40:62 for God's creation of acts. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أهل السنة); SAMIN-DURR(أهل السنة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(أهل السنة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarite school of theology,Ashʿarites)]
- 54:49/q13/p2 People create their own acts, which the wording need not include under God's creation. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, and Samin report Qadari use of the adjectival nominative parse. Study Quran says Qadarites affirm freedom without limiting omnipotence and Mu'tazilites assign evil acts to humans to avoid attributing punished deeds to a just God. Samin mentions opponents who posit creatures outside God's creation. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(القدرية); RAZI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القدرية); SAMIN-DURR(أهل الزيغ,القدرية); STUDYQURAN(Muʿtazilites,Qadarites); NAHHAS-; QURTUBI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarite school of theology)-]
- 54:49/q13/p3 God creates acts, while humans choose among God-created options and acquire responsibility for them. — Qurtubi speaks of acquisition and effort through divine enabling; Study Quran attributes the acquisition account to Ash'arites and says their readers find divine wisdom and the Tablet in the verse. Bursevi holds that servants' good and evil acts receive reward or punishment while occurring by divine will. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(أهل السنة); STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarite school of theology,Ashʿarite theologians,Ashʿarites)]
- 54:49/q13/p4 The verse itself does not decide the technical dispute about acts. — Ibn Ashur says its scope and emphasis on wisdom in recompense permit different readings, though qadar has other proofs; he says its technical relation to causal means, obstacles, and human capacity is hidden. Razi says an alternative understood-verb analysis does not refute Mu'tazila. Study Quran observes that free-will leaning commentators emphasize precise wisdom rather than using this verse to prove their position. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(commentators favoring Muʿtazilite views)]
- 54:49/q13/p5 God determines every human act, including belief and evil, in a fatalistic account. — Study Quran attributes this to Jabariyyah with 76:30 and 81:29 and reports Ash'arite objection to Jabarite fatalism. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Jabariyyah); STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarite school of theology)-]

## 54:49/q14 Does divine decree cancel human action or excuse wrongdoing?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether determining an act entails approving it or removes the actor's capacity and accountability.
- 54:49/q14/p1 No: people should act, and voluntary choice itself falls within the measured order. — A man asked whether deeds begin anew or are settled; the Prophet told him to act because each is facilitated for the easier or harder path, citing 92:7 and 92:10. Elmalili says partial will and voluntary acts are within qadar. Tabatabai cites 80:19-20 for a measured human path still involving voluntary action; Study Quran reports Sufi views that freedom is God-created and will manifests divine power. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,النبي ﷺ عن سعد بن عبيدة وأبي عبد الرحمن السلمي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Sufi thinkers)]
- 54:49/q14/p2 Decree does not make sin good or remove accountability. — Biqai rebukes using God's will to excuse idolatry, citing 6:148, and says decree's inclusion of disbelievers' persistence does not imply divine approval. Elmalili says criminals cannot turn sin's nature or appointed misery into felicity and remain answerable in fire. Tabatabai explains punishment through the measured consequences of rejecting messengers. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 54:49/q15 What do linked reports say about God's prior writing of decrees?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported timing and form of the prior inscription.
- 54:49/q15/p1 God wrote creatures' decrees fifty thousand years before creating heaven and earth, when His Throne was over water. — Abd Allah ibn Amr's report appears in Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, and Bursevi; Ibn Kathir notes Muslim's transmission, Tirmidhi's hasan sahih gharib grade, Ibn Wahb's Throne detail, and 11:7. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 54:49/q15/p2 The first creation was the pen, ordered to write everything until Resurrection. — Ubada's deathbed instruction to his son says belief in good and bad decree is needed for faith and that what misses or strikes a person cannot have done otherwise; Ibn Kathir reports Tirmidhi's hasan sahih gharib grade. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبادة بن الصامت)]

## 54:49/q16 What practical teachings about decree do the cited reports give?  [* · reports]
turns on: The different teachings attached to reports about decree.
- 54:49/q16/p1 Everything, including incapacity and ability, occurs by decree. — Tawus heard Companions affirm it; Ibn Umar transmitted the prophetic wording. Ibn Kathir says Muslim alone gives the route through Malik. Ibn Abbas even includes resting one's hand on one's cheek. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(طاووس اليماني,عبد الله بن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر والنبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 54:49/q16/p2 Belief in good and bad decree is a condition of true faith. — Bursevi and Ibn Kathir report the prophetic enumeration with divine unity, prophethood, death or resurrection, and decree; Ibn Kathir gives Tirmidhi's preferred route and Ibn Majah's transmission. Study Quran cites the condition, and Ibn Kathir reports Ubada's deathbed instruction. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبادة بن الصامت,علي بن أبي طالب); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 54:49/q16/p3 Accept what God apportions and still seek His help and act. — Ibn Kathir calls the prophetic counsel sound: avoid helplessness and if-only regret that opens Satan's work. The Prophet told Ibn Abbas that no combined human benefit or harm exceeds the decree and that pens and scrolls are finished. Abu Hurayra reports that belief removes worry and grief. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة والنبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 54:49/q17 What do reports say about deniers of decree?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported sanction or eschatological consequence is in view.
- 54:49/q17/p1 They are the community's Magians, and believers should not visit their sick or attend their funerals. — Aisha's report is ungraded by Razi. Qurtubi attributes wording via Jabir to Ibn Majah; Ibn Kathir gives Ibn Umar's route through Ahmad and says none of the six canonical compilers included it. Durr and Alusi record further routes; Study Quran notes Ash'arite citation. Ibn Umar also wrote a Syrian correspondent cutting off contact after learning he disputed decree. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله والنبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarites)]
- 54:49/q17/p2 They have no share in Islam or the Prophet's intercession. — Anas's report says the Prophet disowns those claiming good and evil are in human hands and denies them intercession. Another report includes the Murji'a as a second group without share in Islam; Alusi says Tirmidhi grades an Ibn Abbas route hasan. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أنس بن مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس والنبي,ابن عباس وجابر والنبي); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 54:49/q17/p3 Ibn Umar dissociated from deniers, and their spending is not accepted until belief in decree. — Qurtubi cites Muslim for the dissociation, infers a judgment of unbelief, reports Ibn Umar's Uhud-sized gold oath, and compares 9:54 on rejected spending by unbelievers. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر)]
- 54:49/q17/p4 They face severe punishment or metamorphosis. — Wahidi reports Ka'b saying the Torah predicts their face-down dragging in Fire. Ibn Kathir records Ibn Umar's prophecy of metamorphosis among deniers and heretics, graded hasan sahih gharib by Tirmidhi. Ibn Abbas calls deniers the community's worst, bars visiting or praying for them, threatens physical punishment, and associates the coming error with denial of decreed good and evil. Alusi preserves an Ibn Abbas threat and his statement that adultery, theft, and wine drinking occur by decree. Abu Dharr's report calls disputants with God to account on Judgment Day. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو ذر,النبي عن عمر بن الخطاب,كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن عباس,عبد الله بن عمر); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 54:49/q18 Does creation by measure require divine wisdom or philosophical necessity?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether wisdom describes the benefits and order of God's act or constrains His act as an antecedent necessity.
- 54:49/q18/p1 Each created thing is proportioned for a wise purpose, including just recompense. — Al-Jubba'i says nothing is aimless and punishment fits deserts. Ibn Ashur connects ordered creation and Judgment through 13:8, 23:115, 15:85-86, and 44:38-40. Tabatabai says an act may be explained through creatures' benefits, as 5:82 and 2:61 illustrate, or through a fitting divine name, as 29:60 and 2:37 illustrate; the punishment here is an instance of a general measured rule. He says reasoning from regular effects, such as eating and drinking, avoids circularity. Study Quran reports precision and purpose stressed by Tabrisī and wisdom by Zamakhshari. [11 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(al-Zamakhsharī,al-Ṭabrisī)]
- 54:49/q18/p2 God's acts are not governed by an external rational rule or natural necessity. — Tabatabai says general rules are inferred from divine acts, not prior causes governing God, citing 21:23, 22:18, and 2:147. Razi rejects philosophers who make differences depend only on matter's receptivity, since God's will could change sizes and results. [2 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(الفلاسفة)-]
- 54:49/q18/p3 The present world is the best possible arrangement. — Bursevi reports Ghazali's argument that a better possible world left unrealized would imply miserliness or incapacity in God. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الغزالي)]

## 54:49/q19 How does the verse connect with the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether it directly explains the preceding punishment, opens the creation-and-command statement, or frames the surah's closing warning.
- 54:49/q19/p1 It gives the reason criminals are made to taste Saqar: they denied decree or meet a measured punishment. — Tabari calls the verse a threat to decree-deniers; Razi reports the common link with 54:48. Muqatil and Wahidi connect the appointed punishment to what precedes. Tabatabai says the criminals' rejection of prophetic guidance carries its measured outcome in 54:47-49. Ibn Ashur says the warnings culminate in purposeful recompense between 54:43 and 54:51; Elmalili says sin cannot be transformed into felicity. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 54:49/q19/p2 It begins a fresh declaration of God's creation, continued by the next verse on His single swift command. — Razi prefers this because 54:50 parallels creation and command in 7:54. He explains that the Prophet's citation of the punishment verses could end before the full final clause, just as partial verses in 4:29, 6:121, and 2:283 are cited. Nasafi and Ibn Ashur link measure to the next statement of swift command. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 54:49/q19/p3 The close warns Quraysh that the measured fate of earlier nations also awaits them. — Islahi says the closing section contrasts worldly humiliation, worse otherworldly punishment, and the God-fearing's reward. He says Quraysh's respite follows the measured rule applied to nations. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 54:49/q19/p4 The verse's measured creation and the next verse display the unconstrained speed and extent of God's power. — Kuran Yolu says no outside power determines God's will and neither time nor place confines Him. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 54:49/q20 What inward lesson is drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which devotional or metaphysical consequence the measured order yields.
- 54:49/q20/p1 Surrender to the settled measure brings trust, acceptance, and release from anxious planning. — Yusuf ibn al-Husayn says decree overcomes resolve; Bursevi's Persian couplet invokes the dried pen and counsels silence before the eternal Tablet. Study Quran reports Ibn Ajibah's appeal to 102:5 and 40:44 for accepting every decree and relief from sorrow. [3 notes: SULAMI(يوسف بن الحسين); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah)]
- 54:49/q20/p2 Finding God outweighs possession of every created thing. — Study Quran reports al-Maybudi's maxim: the one who finds God has everything, whereas someone deprived of Him has nothing despite possessing all. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(al-Maybudī)]
- 54:49/q20/p3 True freedom emerges through surrender of ego, while human choice itself manifests God-given capacity. — Study Quran reports Sufi readings relating the Divine Names al-Murid and al-Qadir to humanity's capacity, citing 2:31; will is divine power manifested and freedom arises through surrender. Ibn Arabi's saints, most capable of miracles, least use them because they accept God's will. Other Sufis identify a person's lot with an essence known before creation and relate freedom to the breathed Spirit in Adam, 32:9. Bursevi's Najmiyya reading measures all scientific and concrete being by original disposition and capacity, citing 20:50. [5 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿArabī,Sufi thinkers)]

## 54:49/q21 Can another agent determine a thing's measure?  [* · theology]
- 54:49/q21/p1 No agent other than God can impose the measure of a thing as it pleases. — Elmalili denies that any other agent can determine or necessitate the measure at will. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 54:49/q22 What follows for those who disbelieve in divine decree?  [* · theology]
- 54:49/q22/p1 They can be punished for denying it. — Al-Nahhas says the verse demonstrates punishment for disbelief in divine decree. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 54:49/q23 How is the report that all ability is decreed used against deniers?  [* · theology]
- 54:49/q23/p1 It refutes the Qadariyya. — Qurtubi explicitly presents the report as a refutation of the Qadariyya. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 54:49/q24 Why did this verse become central to debates over choice?  [* · theology]
- 54:49/q24/p1 The Prophet's dispute with deniers of decree made it a focus of the dispute over divine determinism and human choice. — Study Quran connects the debate with the reported prophetic encounter. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
