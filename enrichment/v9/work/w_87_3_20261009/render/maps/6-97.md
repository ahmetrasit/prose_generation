# 6:97: 22 questions

## 6:97/q01 What does jaʿala mean and how does it construe the stars?  [جَعَلَ ٱلنُّجُومَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether jaʿala has one object or a guidance complement serving as its second object.
- 6:97/q01/p1 It means created and takes the stars as its single object. — Ibn ʿAtiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr infer one object from the construction; al-Samīn calls this the apparent parse. Jishumī notes creation is one of four senses of jaʿl, alongside transformation, causing a state, and deeming, and compares 6:1. [7 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:97/q01/p2 It means made or appointed, with guidance functioning as an understood second object. — Ibn ʿAtiyya supplies 'guidance for you' from li-tahtadū. Al-Samīn defends this by treating the prepositional phrase as the second object, understood as 'established for your guidance.' The Shaykh objects that this omits an object of verbs like ẓanna. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 6:97/q02 Who is addressed by 'for you,' and what does it express?  [لَكُمُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether human use states the whole purpose of celestial creation.
- 6:97/q02/p1 All humans receive the stars as a benefit. — Ibn ʿAtiyya includes believers and unbelievers: the stars furnish proof against unbelievers and a lesson for believers. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the lām indicates purpose; Elmalılı stresses stars serve humans, not the reverse. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 6:97/q02/p2 Human benefit does not exhaust the divine purpose of each star. — Al-Balkhī cites varied sizes and courses and the life benefits of sun and moon; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says each celestial body is independently willed as well as woven into the world's interconnected benefits. [2 notes: TABRISI(البلخي); TABATABAI]

## 6:97/q03 How are the two purpose phrases 'for you' and 'so you may be guided' related?  [لَكُمُ لِتَهْتَدُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether guidance apposes a one-object creation clause or completes a two-object making clause.
- 6:97/q03/p1 The guidance phrase is a substitute or explication of 'for you,' with the governing verb understood again. — Al-Samīn treats li-tahtadū as a badal of lakum, compares 43:33, and says the purposive lām subordinates the verb after implicit an in Basran analysis. Al-Ālūsī calls it inclusion apposition. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the first lām of benefit from the second of wisdom, nearly an explicative substitute, comparing 5:114. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:97/q03/p2 The second phrase supplies the understood second object of 'made.' — Ibn ʿAtiyya's reading makes the stars 'guidance for you'; al-Samīn defends the complement as an adequate second object, against the Shaykh's objection about omitted objects. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 6:97/q04 What are the 'stars' in this verse?  [ٱلنُّجُومَ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'stars' names the ordinary night lights, a wider celestial class, or an inward referent.
- 6:97/q04/p1 They are visible celestial bodies, commonly understood apart from the sun and moon. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes small-looking lights in the night sky and excludes the moon; some exegetes reported by al-Ālūsī exclude both sun and moon by usage and the navigation context. Jishumī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Samīn derive najm from rising or emergence, also used of sprouting plants and horns; it may name stemless plants or specifically the Pleiades. Al-Samīn adds tanjīm as dispersal and scattered quiver 'stars.' Al-Ālūsī reports fixed and wandering classifications, seven traditional wanderers versus a later eighth called Herschel, al-Ṣūfī's 1,025 observed fixed stars or 1,022 without Coma, and six magnitudes with three grades each; he accepts classifications without objectionable religious implications and leaves the total to God. [10 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(المنجمون,بعض المفسرين,عبد الرحمن الصوفي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:97/q04/p2 They include the sun and moon. — Al-Ālūsī says this would give the general celestial benefit after their particular benefit in the preceding verse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:97/q04/p3 They signify the family of Muhammad. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this from the Tafsīr of ʿAlī ibn Ibrāhīm ibn Hāshim. [1 notes: TABRISI(علي بن إبراهيم بن هاشم)]
- 6:97/q04/p4 They signify the senses. — Al-Qāshānī takes the body's land as livelihood and the heart's sea as acquired knowledge, both navigated through the senses. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:97/q05 What guidance do the stars give in the darknesses?  [ٱلنُّجُومَ لِتَهْتَدُوا۟ ظُلُمَٰتِ ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance is practical navigation or an analogical or inward form of guidance.
- 6:97/q05/p1 They guide travelers along routes on land and sea, especially at night. — Muqātil calls them lights; al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās describe recovery from losing the road. Al-Rāzī and Study Quran specify when sun and moon are absent; 16:16 also speaks of landmarks and the star. Al-Ḥaddādī says travelers keep particular stars ahead, beside or behind; al-Biqāʿī notes fixed and moving stars and al-Bursawī their varied positions. Kuranyolu notes use before the compass and where modern aids fail. Arab poetry cited by Iṣlāḥī describes night routes and hours. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الحدادي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:97/q05/p2 They give figurative guidance through moral, intellectual, or spiritual darkness. — Al-Rāzī applies the figure to denial of divine agency and attributing bodily location to God, while admitting a move beyond literal wording but finding it worthy of the Qurʾan. Study Quran reads sun, moon, and stars as degrees of moral and spiritual illumination, citing Abraham's argument in 6:76–78. Elmalılı includes spiritual paths; al-Sulamī transmits Abū ʿAlī al-Jūzjānī's path to Paradise and night as a mount toward divine approval. [6 notes: SULAMI(أبو علي الجوزجاني); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:97/q05/p3 The senses guide through bodily livelihood and knowledge of the heart. — Al-Qāshānī reads land as the body, sea as the heart, and stars as the senses. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 6:97/q05/p4 The Prophet compares scholars with stars that guide people. — Anas transmits that when stars vanish guides may stray; the comparison extends the verse's travel image. [1 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 6:97/q06 What further uses do the stars have?  [ٱلنُّجُومَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse's named navigation is the only purpose or one among several; cited verses and reports enumerate others.
- 6:97/q06/p1 They adorn the heaven. — Al-Baghawī and Ibn ʿAtiyya cite 67:5; al-Rāzī cites 25:61, 37:6 and 85:1. Qatāda and other early authorities list adornment among three intended uses. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض السلف); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI]
- 6:97/q06/p2 They are missiles or guards against devils. — Al-Baghawī and Ibn ʿAtiyya cite 67:5; al-Qurṭubī cites 37:7 and 67:5. Qatāda and others include this in the three purposes. [10 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض السلف); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI]
- 6:97/q06/p3 They indicate qibla, night hours, prayer and fasting times, and other practical needs. — Al-Ṭabrisī specifies their light, risings and positions; al-Rāzī contrasts daytime sun and nighttime stars for qibla. Al-Biqāʿī adds prayer and fasting times; Iṣlāḥī cites Arab poets on stars as night clocks. Al-Ālūsī says navigation is selected by context while other benefits remain. [7 notes: TABRISI(البلخي); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:97/q06/p4 They display divine power, blessing, mercy and wisdom. — Jishumī includes religious and worldly benefit; al-Rāzī calls them a third class of signs. Al-Qurṭubī says the verse selects one benefit among many; Kuranyolu says their sustained cosmic order remains a sign even when people do not navigate by them. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:97/q07 What star knowledge may people learn or use?  [ٱلنُّجُومَ لِتَهْتَدُوا۟ · law]
turns on: The line between navigation and observation, fallible signs, and claims of prediction or causal power.
- 6:97/q07/p1 Learn enough observational astronomy for navigation and legitimate religious needs. — ʿUmar, Ibn ʿUmar, and a Prophetic report say learn navigation and then stop; ʿUmar analogizes useful genealogy for kinship and marriage. Mujāhid includes lunar stations. Al-Ālūsī permits constellations, stations and positions for religious benefit; Ibn Ḥajar calls timekeeping and qibla astronomy a communal duty. Elmalılı urges calculation, observation and mathematical astronomy; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes ancient Chaldean and Egyptian mapping of risings and motions. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر,عمر بن الخطاب,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر الهيتمي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:97/q07/p2 Predictive astrology and claims of independent celestial efficacy are forbidden or baseless. — Ibn ʿAtiyya calls extra astrological claims fabrication and unbelief; Qatāda rejects forecasts for birth or travel. Al-Biqāʿī quotes a comparison with magic and prophetic warnings; Ibn Ḥajar rejects certain forecasts of rain, snow, winds and prices, which God alone knows. Al-Ālūsī says warnings target divination, independent star effects and categorical forecasts, and that partial knowledge cannot produce certainty; he ultimately deems astrology useless. A rain report distinguishes crediting God from a lunar station; scholars distinguish star-caused rain as disbelief from fallible signs, whose wording is still disliked. The cited Kanz al-Yawāqīt compares uncertain celestial inference to diagnosis by pulse but says law censures it for weakening reliance on God. Iṣlāḥī notes Arabs' auspicious and ominous stars. [20 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض السلف); BIQAI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,صاحب كنز اليواقيت,قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر الهيتمي,ابن عباس,العلماء,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,زيد بن خالد الجهني والنبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(the Arabs)]
- 6:97/q07/p3 Observed conjunctions may be mentioned as fallible signs, without making stars causes. — Al-Ālūsī cites recurring conjunctions such as the Pleiades and Canopus; Ibn Ḥajar allows customary signs that may fail. Scholars say this avoids disbelief, though rain-attribution wording remains disliked. Al-Ālūsī interprets Ibn ʿAbbās's favorable words about calculation as possibly referring to this permissible empirical kind. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر الهيتمي,ابن عباس,العلماء)]
- 6:97/q07/p4 Reports prohibit looking at or discussing stars without stating an exception in their wording. — Al-Biqāʿī records prohibitions through ʿAlī, Abū Hurayra, ʿĀʾisha and Ibn Masʿūd, mentioning multiple routes; ʿAlī's report also addresses ablution, charity and donkey-horse breeding. Al-Ālūsī repeats reports from ʿAlī, Abū Hurayra and ʿĀʾisha, interpreting them as barriers to prohibited astrology. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة وعائشة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 6:97/q07/p5 Knowledge of magic or talismans is distinct from using them to harm. — The cited Kanz al-Yawāqīt calls harmful practice blameworthy while bare knowledge is not. It cites an authentic report of the Prophet's illness from a spell, Gabriel's disclosure, and its removal from under a stone in a well. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(صاحب كنز اليواقيت)]

## 6:97/q08 What does 'darknesses of land and sea' mean?  [ظُلُمَٰتِ ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the darkness is nighttime, route confusion, or figurative error.
- 6:97/q08/p1 Night darkness encountered on land and sea. — Stars appear at night, as Ibn ʿAtiyya notes. Al-Rāzī specifies the absence of sun and moon; al-Bursawī says the settings are named because navigation then needs stars. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the plural conveys intense darkness, with 2:17 as a parallel. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:97/q08/p2 Confusing or lost routes, and sometimes hardship, are called darkness. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās describe error or wandering off the road. Ibn ʿAtiyya includes hardships in places where the sun supplies navigation. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī explicitly call perplexing routes a metaphor. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:97/q08/p3 They can figure moral or intellectual error and misguidance. — Al-Ṭabarī includes error and misguidance, while Study Quran gives moral and intellectual darkness and al-Rāzī applies it to two theological errors. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 6:97/q09 Why is darkness attributed to land and sea?  [ظُلُمَٰتِ ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْرِ · rhetoric]
turns on: How to classify the nonliteral relation between the darkness and its setting.
- 6:97/q09/p1 The annexation associates darkness with where it occurs or where travelers experience it. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī treat night as attached to its setting. Al-Biqāʿī says land and water have their own darkness compounded by night. Ibn ʿĀshūr offers a locative reading or a weak-association lām relation, likening it to 'the planet of the awkward woman.' [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:97/q09/p2 The slight association is a figure of similarity. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Miftāḥ's account and compares the earth swallowing its water in 11:44. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:97/q09/p3 It is figurative attribution, because literal possession requires a fuller relation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Taftāzānī's view and suggests the analyses differ according to how near the weak relation is to ownership; he says the phrase itself suggests figurative attribution. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(التفتزاني)]

## 6:97/q10 Why is 'darknesses' plural?  [ظُلُمَٰتِ · rhetoric]
- 6:97/q10/p1 Arabic commonly uses the plural for intense darkness. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the earlier description of people left in darknesses at 2:17. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:97/q11 What does 'so you may be guided' mean?  [لِتَهْتَدُوا۟ · meaning]
- 6:97/q11/p1 To follow and recognize the right route indicated to a person. — Jishumī says God points toward right conduct and a person recognizes and follows it; al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās illustrate a lost traveler finding the road. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]

## 6:97/q12 What does 'We have detailed' mean?  [فَصَّلْنَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether detailing means verbal exposition or differentiation embodied in creation.
- 6:97/q12/p1 God explains and distinguishes signs point by point through evidence. — Jishumī says explanation proceeds one part at a time. Ibn ʿAtiyya adds clarification and division; Abū Ḥayyān agrees. Al-Sāmarrāʾī derives tafṣīl from separation and says the sequence of seeds, dawn, sun, moon and stars presents distinct matters. Study Quran compares 6:55, 6:98, 6:126, 7:32, 7:174, 9:11, 10:5 and 10:24. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:97/q12/p2 It can mean specifying the created signs in creation. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī offers created specification alongside exposition in words; al-Ālūsī likewise allows created signs as the referent. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:97/q13 Which signs have been detailed?  [ٱلْءَايَٰتِ · referent]
turns on: Whether āyāt primarily denotes recited statements, created realities, or evidentiary signs.
- 6:97/q13/p1 The signs include the created celestial sign and other created signs. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the definite article comprehensively. Al-Ālūsī includes created signs disclosing divine attributes; Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows specification through creation. Elmalılı lists land, sea, earth, heaven, plants, animals, atmosphere and celestial phenomena, invoking 18:109 for their inexhaustible science. Al-Qāshānī identifies spirit, heart and senses in his inward reading. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QASHANI]
- 6:97/q13/p2 They are recited signs that recount divine blessings, including stars. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as the other reading; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it exposition in words. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 6:97/q13/p3 Āyāt are proofs and arguments as well as signs. — Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Ṭabrisī call them proofs; Iṣlāḥī argues a sign proves what it signifies, chiefly divine unity but also resurrection and prophethood. Each sign has several aspects and leaves its referent for the inquirer to discern; al-Nasafī specifies oneness. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:97/q13/p4 In this sequence the stars' sign is cosmic, followed by human creation. — Al-Bursawī distinguishes the cosmic sign of 6:97 from 6:98's human sign; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds the growing system and its audience of believers in 6:99. [2 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 6:97/q14 Who are 'people who know,' and why are they singled out?  [لِقَوْمٍۢ يَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase identifies actual knowers, would-be knowers, or specifically skilled star users.
- 6:97/q14/p1 They recognize and benefit from the signs through knowledge and reflection. — Muqātil says they know God is one without partner. Al-Ṭabarī says they reflect, leave ignorance and knowingly obstinate wrongdoing; al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, Abū Ḥayyān and others distinguish them from people who ignore truth. Study Quran says they reason from sensory evidence to the unseen; Ibn ʿĀshūr says knowledge apprehends things as they are, citing two poets, and refers to belief in 6:99. Al-Biqāʿī says they infer unseen from observed; Elmalılı says intellectual lessons especially benefit the capable though signs guide everyone. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:97/q14/p2 It means people who want to know. — Iṣlāḥī says an action verb may stand for the intention behind that action. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:97/q14/p3 It especially names those skilled in calculation and navigation by stars. — Al-Suyūṭī in al-Itqān makes the scientific skill itself explain the ending. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 6:97/q15 Why does this verse end with 'know' while the human-creation verse ends with 'understand'?  [يَعْلَمُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording follows the relative obviousness of the sign or the special skill used with stars.
- 6:97/q15/p1 The stars' visible benefit takes ordinary knowledge; varied human creation needs deeper discernment. — Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī and Elmalılı compare 6:98 and its yafqahūn; Elmalılı adds insight into the self. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the series pairs stars with knowledge, human creation with deeper understanding, and plant growth with belief. Al-Zarkashī notes the tailored endings across the three Anʿām verses. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 6:97/q15/p2 The ending fits the learned skill of calculation and navigation. — Al-Itqān points to expert star use as the reason for yaʿlamūn. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 6:97/q16 What do the stars prove about God?  [ٱلنُّجُومَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the sign is read as general proof of oneness or developed into arguments about divine agency and transcendence.
- 6:97/q16/p1 They point to God's unique power, wisdom, mercy and governance, calling for worship and gratitude. — Jishumī, al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī point to divine power and beneficence; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the definite subject and predicate make God's sole agency restrictive even though the audience acknowledged creation. Iṣlāḥī argues service to humans rules out deifying stars and joins heaven and earth under one God; Elmalılı says stars are mercy's signs rather than managing deities. Al-Bursawī says blessings call for gratitude and cosmic and human signs address polytheists, believers and sinners differently; Kuranyolu notes the enduring celestial order. [10 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:97/q16/p2 Their varied properties imply specification by a voluntary divine agent. — Al-Rāzī argues the stars are alike as bodies yet differ in motion, place, brightness and size, so a choosing agent assigns the differences; this counters denial of divine voluntary action. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 6:97/q16/p3 Their finite, composed and changing nature rules out divinity and bodily attributes for God. — Al-Rāzī reasons that if bodily traits disqualify stars as gods, God transcends body, parts, limits, place and direction; he acknowledges the figurative step. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 6:97/q16/p4 The heavenly bodies obey and prostrate to God. — Study Quran cites 7:54 and 16:12 for their subjection and 22:18 and 55:5–6 for prostration. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 6:97/q17 What learning or response do the detailed signs call for?  [قَدْ فَصَّلْنَا ٱلْءَايَٰتِ لِقَوْمٍۢ يَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation]
- 6:97/q17/p1 Reflect on creation, infer its maker and respond with faith, obedience and gratitude. — Al-Ṭabarī urges leaving error; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the independent sentence records signs and removes unbelievers' excuses. Al-Bursawī calls for tawḥīd, direct witnessing, repentance and gratitude; the ordered worlds lead a rational person toward the Real, while heedlessness veils. Iṣlāḥī says intellectual and heartfelt signs answer Quraysh's demand for tangible marvels; obsession with the palpable can persist through miracles. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:97/q17/p2 Study the natural world and its many sciences. — Elmalılı urges botany, zoology, calculation, observation, meteorology and mathematical astronomy; he treats the created book as varied and inexhaustible, citing 18:109. [3 notes: ELMALILI]

## 6:97/q18 How does the verse connect with neighboring verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which adjacent or parallel passage supplies the relevant context.
- 6:97/q18/p1 It follows signs in dawn, night, sun and moon, adding stars as another useful celestial sign. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to night as repose in 6:96; Iṣlāḥī says it follows sun and moon. Study Quran says the cleaving and duality of 6:96–97 continue themes of 6:95. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the sequence of separate signs explains tafṣīl. - 6:97/q18/p2 The sequence moves from cosmic signs to the subtler human and growing signs. — Al-Biqāʿī sees a transition from earthly and heavenly dominion to comprehensive human creation, countering belief in choosing celestial bodies. ## 6:97/q19 What does the opening 'and' contribute?  [وَهُوَ · grammar]
- 6:97/q19/p1 It coordinates clauses without itself stating causation. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts wāw with fa, which commonly marks cause and result, illustrated by 'he studied, so he succeeded.' [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 6:97/q20 What report uses the verse's night and dawn language in prayer?  [* · reports]
- 6:97/q20/p1 A Prophetic prayer asks the maker of night repose and splitter of dawn for relief and strength. — Muslim ibn Yasār transmits requests for debt settlement, relief from poverty, and preservation of hearing, sight and strength for God's cause. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مسلم بن يسار)]

## 6:97/q21 What anecdote illustrates using the world for God's service?  [* · reports]
- 6:97/q21/p1 A youth rebuked Shahin's heedlessness, and Shahin repented after hearing how the world serves God's servants. — Al-Bursawī relates that Shahin ibn Shujāʿ al-Kirmānī met a youth riding a lion while hunting; an old woman brought sweet water. The youth said God made the world serve those who serve Him and enslave its servants. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 6:97/q22 What reports describe the history and social use of star knowledge?  [ٱلنُّجُومَ · reports]
turns on: The reports concern different historical claims rather than one shared origin.
- 6:97/q22/p1 Star knowledge was given to Adam. — Ḥumayd al-Shāmī says the stars were among Adam's knowledge. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(حميد الشامي)]
- 6:97/q22/p2 Astronomy originated with prophets, and Idrīs first taught it. — The author of the Book of Ornament also includes script among sciences received through revelation. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(صاحب كتاب الزينة)]
- 6:97/q22/p3 Arabs practiced astrology among several traditional arts. — A report through ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ḥafṣ lists soothsaying, physiognomy, augury, astrology and calculation; Islam removed soothsaying while the others persisted. Iṣlāḥī notes worship of springtime Sirius and auspicious or ominous stars. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله بن حفص); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(the Arabs)]
- 6:97/q22/p4 Stellar mapping was an ancient science of Chaldeans and Egyptians. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says charting risings, settings and motions guided astronomy's development. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
