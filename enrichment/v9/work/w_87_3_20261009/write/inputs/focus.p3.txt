# 87:3/q05 Is God said to compel or command misguidance? [فَهَدَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether directing to, mentioning, or decreeing error entails compelling or commanding it.
- 87:3/q05/p1 No; God commands and approves obedience while forbidding sin and misguidance.
  reasons: Qatāda says God neither compels nor commands sin and does not approve it, answering any reading of guidance that would make Him do so.
  holders (2 notes): RAZI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة)
  notes: RAZI:87:3/r13 RAZI-FULL:v31p127/r9
- 87:3/q05/p2 The verse's guidance can include a path to error or an implied act of leading astray.
  reasons: Mujāhid's reports say God guides toward good and evil or right and error; al-Farrāʾ proposes the implicit opposite. These sources do not all make the same claim about compulsion.
  holders (5 notes): MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; FARRA
  notes: MAWARDI:87:3/r1 MAWARDI-FULL:v6p251/r13 RAZI:87:3/r8 RAZI-FULL:v31p127/r4 FARRA:v3p256/r3
sources: FARRA = al-Farrāʾ, d. 207; MAWARDI = al-Māwardī, d. 450; MAWARDI-FULL = al-Māwardī, d. 450; RAZI = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606; RAZI-FULL = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606

# 87:3/q06 What does the fa in فَهَدَىٰ express? [فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:3/q06/p1 Guidance follows as the effect or completion of determination.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the fa linking creation and proportioning: each measured creature is directed to its allotted use. Al-Biqāʿī says creation needs proportion for balance, while ordainment needs guidance for completeness.
  holders (5 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:3/r2 IBNASHUR:87:3/r3 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p276/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p276/r5 BIQAI-FULL:v8p395#2/r3
sources: BIQAI-FULL = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393

# 87:3/q07 Who is the unspoken object of فَهَدَىٰ? [فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether general wording encompasses all created things or only beings capable of apprehending direction.
- 87:3/q07/p1 It is left general, then contextually limited to creatures able to apprehend direction.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr includes humans and animals with perception and will but excludes fruiting trees and seed production, which he calls determined rather than guided.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:3/r7 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p277/r2
- 87:3/q07/p2 The created object may be humans, humans and animals, or all creation.
  reasons: Study Quran reports these possibilities for 'created' in the preceding verse; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the corresponding human-specific reading of determination and guidance.
  holders (2 notes): STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR
  notes: STUDYQURAN:87:2/r1 IBNASHUR:87:3/r8
sources: IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; STUDYQURAN = Seyyed Hossein Nasr (ed.-in-chief), Caner K. Dagli, Maria Massi Dakake, Joseph E. B. Lumbard, Mohammed Rustom

# 87:3/q08 How are divine attributes linked by the conjunctions and relative pronoun? [وَٱلَّذِى قَدَّرَ فَهَدَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:3/q08/p1 The repeated relative pronoun and conjunction emphasize distinct divine predicates.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the repetition amplification. Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū al-Baqāʾ say conjunction makes each adjective independent; al-Burhān compares 68:10 and 57:3 and says conjunction suits attributes that differ widely. Al-Biqāʿī says wāw joins ordainment to proportion to establish both attributes together.
  holders (6 notes): BURHAN(الزمخشري وأبو البقاء); BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: BURHAN:v2p446/r1 BURHAN:v2p446/r2 BURHAN:v2p446/r3 BIQAI:87:3/r4 IBNASHUR:87:3/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p276/r5
sources: BIQAI = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393

# 87:3/q09 Do the perfect verbs describe only completed past acts? [قَدَّرَ فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:3/q09/p1 No; their perfect form is aspectual, and divine creation is ongoing and sustaining.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum points to the repeated growth and decay of pasture in verses 4–5 and compares 53:43–49, 96:1–5, and 95:4–5.
  holders (1 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:2-5:anmerkung/r1
sources: CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. (author line per sura in segments)

# 87:3/q10 What inward guidance does the verse describe? [فَهَدَىٰ · inward]
turns on: Which spiritual stage or disposition the guidance addresses.
- 87:3/q10/p1 Heedless people build the world, while worshippers prefer the afterlife.
  reasons: Al-Qushayrī contrasts the objects of their efforts.
  holders (1 notes): QUSHAYRI
  notes: QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r6
- 87:3/q10/p2 Ascetics recognize the world's passing nature and reject it.
  reasons: Al-Qushayrī gives this as another degree of guidance.
  holders (1 notes): QUSHAYRI
  notes: QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r7
- 87:3/q10/p3 Scholars contemplate created signs and remain with their inferences.
  reasons: Al-Qushayrī says they recognize God through examining His signs and creation.
  holders (1 notes): QUSHAYRI
  notes: QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r8
- 87:3/q10/p4 Seekers turn toward the glory of God's attribute and devote themselves to Him.
  reasons: Al-Qushayrī says they prefer Him and expend their effort seeking Him.
  holders (1 notes): QUSHAYRI
  notes: QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r9
- 87:3/q10/p5 Gnostics enter the sanctity of His description, watching and then witnessing Him.
  reasons: Al-Qushayrī portrays successive stages of awareness.
  holders (1 notes): QUSHAYRI
  notes: QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r10
- 87:3/q10/p6 Monotheists abandon familiar things and seek God alone in His sovereign sublimity.
  reasons: Al-Qushayrī names this as a distinct orientation.
  holders (1 notes): QUSHAYRI
  notes: QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r11
- 87:3/q10/p7 Knowers finally rest in incapacity before God beyond proof, explanation, vision, separation, and union.
  reasons: Al-Qushayrī locates the divine beyond these modes.
  holders (1 notes): QUSHAYRI
  notes: QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r12

  reasons: Al-Qāshānī says refinement brings the potential perfection forth.
  holders (1 notes): QASHANI
  notes: QASHANI:87:1-4/r3

# 87:3/q11 What reports illustrate divine determination and guidance? [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported incident is used to illustrate the point.
- 87:3/q11/p1 God decreed all creatures' destinies fifty thousand years before creating heaven and earth.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAmr reports the Prophet's statement; the report also says the Throne was over water. Bursawī cites its timing.
  holders (3 notes): IBNKATHIR(عبد الله بن عمرو); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)
  notes: IBNKATHIR:87:3/r3 IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p379/r4 BURSEVI:v10p313/r8
- 87:3/q11/p2 A very old blind snake found a healing plant and regained sight.
  reasons: Al-Kashshāf calls it a thousand-year-old snake; one version says fresh fennel, another fresh rue in a distant garden. Al-Qurṭubī repeats the fennel version. The plant names vary in the notes.
  holders (3 notes): KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI
  notes: KASHSHAF:87:3/r2 KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p739/r6 QURTUBI:87:3/r10
- 87:3/q11/p3 Animals show directed instinct in hunting, nest-building, and storing food.
  reasons: Bursawī names a weasel catching a mouse, spiders and bees making nests, and ants splitting damp seeds and drying them so they do not sprout.
  holders (2 notes): BURSEVI
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p314/r1 BURSEVI:v10p314/r2
- 87:3/q11/p4 A diving bird escaped a crow that kept stealing its fish by dragging the crow underwater.
  reasons: Bursawī attributes the observation to an unnamed observer.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI(بعضهم)
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p314/r3
- 87:3/q11/p5 A monkey exposed a seller who diluted milk by throwing the price of the added water into the sea.
  reasons: Bursawī reports this as a warning against dishonest sale.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p314/r4
- 87:3/q11/p6 A dog repeatedly exposed a well containing its murdered owner, leading to the killer's confession and execution.
  reasons: Bursawī transmits the story as an instance of animal guidance.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p314/r5
- 87:3/q11/p7 A palm tree weakened from attachment to another tree and recovered when bound to one of its branches.
  reasons: Bursawī presents the account as another instance of directed natural behavior.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p314/r6
sources: BURSEVI = İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127; IBNKATHIR = Ibn Kathīr, d. 774; IBNKATHIR-FULL = Ibn Kathīr, d. 774; KASHSHAF = al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538; KASHSHAF-FULL = al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538; QURTUBI = al-Qurṭubī, d. 671

# 87:3/q12 How does this verse support the call to glorify God and relate to the surrounding sūras? [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding passage or theological purpose supplies the connection.
- 87:3/q12/p1 Creation, proportion, determination, and guidance display God's power, wisdom, and worthiness of glorification.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī says they answer how the Lord is known and why He merits praise; Abraham's words in 26:78, Moses' reply in 20:49–50, and the first revelation in 96:1–4 use the same proof. The wonders are abundant and observable. Al-Qurṭubī's teacher says the clauses show divine exaltedness. Elmalılı says finite creatures imply the Lord's transcendence and grounds for trust amid disbelievers' schemes.
  holders (12 notes): RAZI
