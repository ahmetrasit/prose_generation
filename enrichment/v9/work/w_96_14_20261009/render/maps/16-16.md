# 16:16: 21 questions

## 16:16/q01 What are the signs (ʿalāmāt)?  [وَعَلَٰمَٰتٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the signs are terrestrial route markers, stars that have their own marking role, a reported sea creature, or a broader class; Wāḥidī invokes coordination with earth's features and the separate mention of the star.
- 16:16/q01/p1 Perceptible landmarks and route indicators, especially for daytime travel. — Yaḥyā says God places them along routes. Ibn ʿAbbās distinguishes daytime landmarks from night stars (Ṭabarī, Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyyah, Qurṭubī, Durr, Ālūsī). Ṭabarī includes trodden roads, mountains and stars; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah includes rivers and roads and favors this breadth. Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and Ālūsī add winds or even the smell of soil; Ālūsī connects masāfa with smelling. Bayḍāwī adds plains; Ibn Kathīr hills; Study Quran springs and wind vocabulary with 2:266, 17:69, 41:16, 51:41, 52:27. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God inspires people to establish or recognize markers for distances and safe routes. Wāḥidī derives ʿalāma from ʿilm; Jishumī, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī define a sign as a perceptible form conveying a hidden/intended meaning through writing, speech, gesture, appearance or convention. Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-ʿAwfī from Ibn ʿAbbās on the signs as landmarks. [39 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي,ابن عطية,ابن عيسى,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عيسى); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 16:16/q01/p2 Mountains in particular are the signs. — Muqātil, al-Kalbī and others identify mountains; Muqātil cites a mountain-like landmark comparison and 55:14. Muḥammad b. Kaʿb and al-Kalbī say mountains guide by day and stars by night (Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī). Wāḥidī favors mountains because the signs are coordinated with terrestrial creations and the following star is distinct; if they meant stars, he says, the wording would use 'by them.' Study Quran also cites mountains among wider landmarks. Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the mountain identification to Ibn al-Sāʾib and Muqātil. [18 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(الكلبي); THALABI(محمد بن كعب القرظي والكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي والقرظي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن كعب، الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الكلبي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الكلبي); DURR-FULL(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي); STUDYQURAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)+]
- 16:16/q01/p3 Stars themselves are the signs; some mark places and others guide. — Qatāda, Mujāhid and Ibrāhīm distinguish stellar markers from guiding stars (Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī). ʿAṭāʾ transmits this identification from Ibn ʿAbbās, though Wāḥidī rejects it for the terrestrial and grammatical reasons given above. Durr reports Ibrāhīm's markers in the sky and Mujāhid's division of markers and guides; Ibn Kathīr transmits Mālik's unusual formulation that the stars are the mountains. Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the division between stellar markers and guiding stars to Mujāhid, Qatāda, and al-Nakhaʿī. [18 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد وإبراهيم); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والنخعي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي ومجاهد); TABRISI(قتادة ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة، النخعي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النخعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مالك); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم,قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وقتادة والنخعي); WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 16:16/q01/p4 The signs are long, slender, colorful fish marking safe arrival near India. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports his father hearing an eastern scholar describe twisting fish in the Indian Ocean and reports several eyewitnesses; Abū Ḥayyān repeats the route from Yemen to India. Ālūsī calls this account far-fetched. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو القاضي محمد,أبي القاضي محمد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 16:16/q01/p5 The signs are mountain streams. — Al-Suddī is reported with this identification in Durr. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(السدي)]
- 16:16/q01/p6 The further signs include still undiscovered features of the earth. — Kuranyolu connects this wording with discoveries to come from physical geography, geology and biology. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 16:16/q02 How is ʿalāmāt governed in the sentence?  [وَعَلَٰمَٰتٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative follows the preceding construction, expresses the purpose of what was made, or depends on a supplied verb.
- 16:16/q02/p1 It is coordinated with the preceding created rivers and paths or landmarks. — Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī and Rāzī attach it to the preceding list. Islāḥī says Arabic leaves recoverable words unspoken in the path and landmark constructions and supplies them in translation. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 16:16/q02/p2 It is an accusative of purpose or verbal-noun-like indication: these things were made as signs for learning the routes. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says they teach people on every route, including mountains, rivers and roads; Abū Ḥayyān reports this analysis. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 16:16/q02/p3 An implicit verb governs it: God created signs for you. — Ṭabrisī reports this alternative supplied verb. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]

## 16:16/q03 How does the star clause connect to the signs clause?  [وَعَلَٰمَٰتٍۢ وَبِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Where the sentence ends after 'signs' and whether the following clause is syntactically new or coordinated.
- 16:16/q03/p1 The sentence ends at ʿalāmāt, and 'by the star they are guided' begins a new statement. — Thaʿlabī and Baghawī report this stop; Wāḥidī and Rāzī attribute it to al-Akhfash. Ṭabrisī and Qurṭubī report the same possibility. Samin says the prepositional phrase attaches to yahtadūn. [7 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي والقرظي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 16:16/q03/p2 Signs and stars are both parts of God's provision of guidance, with the star clause coordinated with creation or guidance before it. — Qurṭubī reports the reading that both are made for guidance. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the star clause coordinates with the creation clause but expresses God guiding people by stars at night, distinct from daytime markers. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:16/q04 What does the singular al-najm designate here?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ · referent]
turns on: Whether singular al-najm names the stellar class, a fixed named group, al-Jady alone, the Pleiades, or the pole star; the reported prophetic identification of al-Jady and Arabic usage are cited.
- 16:16/q04/p1 Stars as a class, expressed by a generic or collective singular. — Yaḥyā says the singular means the navigational group. Zajjāj compares singular dirham used of many coins; Wāḥidī says Zajjāj chooses the collective sense. Māwardī and Ṭabrisī specify fixed stars. Ibn Jawzī and Damghānī give the ordinary celestial sense; Ibn Jawzī also lists plant, emerging horn/tooth, Pleiades and stalkless plant as other Arabic senses. Abū Ḥayyān says the majority read it as a collective singular. Ibn ʿAṣfūr instead calls poetic singular for plural a metrical necessity, citing a star-disappearance line; Abū Ḥayyān supplies a parallel poem using singular-like 'throats'; Samin and Ālūsī transmit this argument. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah prefers the generic sense after mentioning other identifications. He also calls an earlier explanation weak, but its content is not preserved in this excerpt, so the object of his criticism cannot be identified. Ibn al-Jawzī reports al-Zajjāj's generic-singular explanation. [25 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وإبراهيم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عصفور,الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عصفور,العامة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عصفور); STUDYQURAN(Q, Z); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 16:16/q04/p2 Al-Jady alone, the especially fixed navigational star. — Māwardī gives its fixed central position. Reports through Ibn ʿAbbās identify the Prophet's answer as al-Jady and connect it with qibla and land/sea navigation (Māwardī, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī); Ālūsī says it should prevail if sound. Qurṭubī places the pole between al-Jady and the smaller Banāt Naʿsh. Abū Ḥayyān calls al-Jady the seventh star of the smaller Banāt Naʿsh, distinct from its lunar-station namesake. Ālūsī says astrologers use a diminutive to distinguish it from the zodiac sign, though another account calls that dialectal. Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-Māwardī's identification of al-Jady as the most fixed star at its center. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)+]
- 16:16/q04/p3 The Pleiades, Banāt Naʿsh, the two Farqads and al-Jady as a set of guiding stars. — Muqātil says these remain fixed year-round and work with mountains for land and sea navigation; Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī transmit al-Suddī's list. Names and spellings vary slightly, including Judayy/Jady and Furqad/Farqad. Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-Suddī's set: the Pleiades, the two Farqads, Banāt Naʿsh, and al-Jady. [10 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); THALABI(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي)]
- 16:16/q04/p4 Al-Jady and the two Farqads. — Farrāʾ identifies these; Wāḥidī says he chooses the view. Abū Ḥayyān says the Farqads are the first two stars of the smaller Banāt Naʿsh. Ibn al-ʿArabī says these conspicuous, fixed stars can guide ordinary travelers, unlike stars requiring expert knowledge of risings and settings. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah transmits al-Farrāʾ's identification of al-Jady and the two Farqads; Ibn al-Jawzī also attributes it to Ibn al-Sāʾib. [9 notes: FARRA; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)+]
- 16:16/q04/p5 The Pleiades alone. — Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report this alternative and cite a poem about its rising at harvest and bent vegetation; Abū Ḥayyān also cites a star guiding Euphrates crossings. Ālūsī says al-najm conventionally names the Pleiades and cites a seasonal-affliction report. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Q, Z)]
- 16:16/q04/p6 The Pleiades and Banāt Naʿsh. — Wāḥidī transmits this narrower list from al-Suddī. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي)]
- 16:16/q04/p7 The unmoving pole star or North Star. — Abū Ḥayyān says this star does not move; Samin and Study Quran report it among possible particular referents. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports an unnamed identification with the Pole Star, specified as unmoving. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); STUDYQURAN(Q, Z); IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيره)]

## 16:16/q05 How is al-najm read and how are its forms explained?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowels and whether the unusual form is a regular plural, a lightening or dialect, or a plural with its wāw omitted.
- 16:16/q05/p1 The common reading has the singular al-najm. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Abū Ḥayyān call this the majority reading; Samin says it may name one particular star or the generic class. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 16:16/q05/p2 A reading vocalizes both nūn and jīm with ḍamma as a plural. — Al-Ḥasan is named by Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Ṭabrisī; Samin names Ibn Waththāb, while Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī transmit differing attributions and forms. The analogies are suquf/saqf and ruhun/rahn. Abū Ḥayyān, Samin and Ālūsī favor a direct faʿul plural over derivation by deleting the wāw of nujūm. Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the reading with ḍamma on both nūn and jīm to al-Jaḥdarī. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن وثاب، الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وابن وثاب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجحدري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(ابن وثاب)+]
- 16:16/q05/p3 A reading has ḍamma on nūn and sukūn on jīm, a lightened plural or dialect form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah names Yaḥyā b. Waththāb; Rāzī reports it among al-Ḥasan's forms. Qurṭubī adduces a verse for shortening; Abū Ḥayyān, Samin and Ālūsī allow ease of pronunciation or an independent dialect. Ibn al-Jawzī also attributes the nūn-ḍamma, jīm-sukūn reading to al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Abū al-Mutawakkil, alongside al-Ḥasan and Yaḥyā b. Waththāb. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن وثاب، الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وابن وثاب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن والضحاك وأبو المتوكل ويحيى بن وثاب)]
- 16:16/q05/p4 The plural sounding form results from deletion of the wāw of nujūm. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Rāzī report this explanation; Samin compares a shortened plural and cites Abū al-Baqāʾ's khiyām/khaym analogy. Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī prefer treating the form as an ordinary plural. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 16:16/q05/p5 Mujāhid reads the full plural al-nujūm, retaining its wāw. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this reading from Mujāhid. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد)]

## 16:16/q06 What does 'they find guidance' mean here?  [يَهْتَدُونَ · meaning]
- 16:16/q06/p1 They know, recognize and discern the route or direction. — Muqātil contrasts this with 4:98, where not finding a path means not knowing the way. Damghānī glosses hadā as knowing/recognizing and cites 20:82 and 27:92; Ibn Jawzī cites recognition of landmarks and 21:31, 27:41 and 43:10. Burhān and Itqān gloss hudā as knowledge in this very phrase. [5 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 16:16/q07 For what travel or orientation do the stars guide?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause is limited to sea travel, covers journeys on both land and sea, also indicates prayer direction, or includes timekeeping; the neighboring sea passage and breadth of the blessing are cited.
- 16:16/q07/p1 They guide journeys by night on land and sea, especially where ordinary landmarks fail. — Ibn ʿAbbās distinguishes day markers and night stars (Ṭabarī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, Durr). Muqātil lists fixed stars plus mountains. Māwardī calls all journeys the majority view. Rāzī prefers land and sea as the broader blessing. Biqāʿī calls stellar guidance the clearest and most comprehensive, even by day. Bursevī stresses deserts and sea at night; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses sailors unable to moor each night. Study Quran says guidance spans times and places. Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-ʿAwfī from Ibn ʿAbbās on navigation by stars at night; he also reports travel guidance as one interpretation of the clause. [27 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL+]
- 16:16/q07/p2 The star guidance is specifically for sea travel. — Rāzī reports some commentators infer this restriction from the preceding discussion of the sea; Durr reports Ibrāhīm's stellar guidance at sea. Rāzī prefers the wider land-and-sea application. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم); RAZI-FULL-]
- 16:16/q07/p3 Stars indicate the qibla, including for travelers on land and sea. — Māwardī and Ṭabrisī report qibla as an alternative purpose; reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and the Prophet link al-Jady to qibla (Māwardī, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān). Wāḥidī, Thaʿlabī and Baghawī say roads and qibla; Rāzī argues route indicators can indicate qibla too. Study Quran cites this in unfamiliar territory. Ibn al-Jawzī reports finding the direction of prayer as the other interpretation of stellar guidance. [16 notes: THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Q, R); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 16:16/q07/p4 Stars also provide a measure of time. — Zajjāj includes marking years and reckoning among their purposes; Islāḥī says desert travelers measure time by them. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 16:16/q08 Who are 'they' in 'they are guided'?  [هُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the third person singles out Quraysh for their expertise or generalizes the guidance to all people; the shift of grammatical person is explained accordingly.
- 16:16/q08/p1 Quraysh, especially their trading travelers skilled in star navigation. — Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samin, Bursevī and Study Quran cite their journeys and skill; the shift from direct address makes their obligation to reflect, thank God or affirm tawḥīd especially strong. Ālūsī reports and develops this reading. Biqāʿī includes Quraysh as the first and most knowledgeable addressees while extending the statement outward. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 16:16/q08/p2 All travelers or earth's inhabitants, with a shift from direct address to third person. — Biqāʿī extends the address from Quraysh to Arabs and all inhabitants. Ālūsī reports the preceding addressees broadly. Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests the shift avoids repeating the second-person 'you find guidance' of the prior verses. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 16:16/q09 Why are 'by the star' and 'they' placed emphatically?  [وَبِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the marked word order mainly restricts the group or means of guidance, ranks star guidance, or serves cadence with emphasis.
- 16:16/q09/p1 Fronting and the detached pronoun restrict or emphasize star guidance for Quraysh in particular. — Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Bursevī connect the order with Quraysh's exceptional knowledge and debt of gratitude. Samin calls both frontings restrictive; Abū Ḥayyān cites Zamakhsharī's paraphrase 'by the star in particular.' [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 16:16/q09/p2 The fronting highlights the importance and superiority of stellar guidance. — Biqāʿī says other forms rank below star guidance; Ibn ʿĀshūr says sailors' reliance on stars makes both fronted elements important. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 16:16/q09/p3 Fronting serves the verse ending's cadence, while the detached pronoun adds emphasis. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī mention cadence; Ālūsī combines it with emphasis of 'they.' [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:16/q10 What purposes were the stars created to serve?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the list of divinely assigned uses is limited to three or includes calculation of years.
- 16:16/q10/p1 Adorning heaven, stoning devils, and guiding people are their three stated purposes. — Yaḥyā links the lamps and missiles to 67:5 and guidance to 6:97. Qatāda states the three purposes in Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī and Baghawī, adding that other purposes claimed without knowledge are baseless; Ḥassān b. Bilāl calls such attribution false, sinful and innovative. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(حسان بن بلال العنزي); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 16:16/q10/p2 Those functions also include marking years and reckoning. — Zajjāj lists adornment, stoning devils, guiding travelers, and marking years and calculation. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 16:16/q11 What may people learn or claim from the stars?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ · law]
turns on: The distinction drawn between practical observation of routes, direction and times and forecasts claiming knowledge of future events.
- 16:16/q11/p1 Learning stars for routes, qibla and times is permissible. — ʿUmar permits enough astronomy for roads and qibla, then advises learning genealogy for kinship (Bursevī). Ālūsī reports permission to learn what enables times, qibla and routes. Durr reports Mujāhid saw no harm in learning lunar stations; early authorities in Bursevī classify astronomy as the science of times. Al-Durr transmits Ibrāhīm's view that there is no harm in learning enough of the stars to navigate by them. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(إبراهيم,مجاهد); BURSEVI(بعض السلف,عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 16:16/q11/p2 Astrological prediction of future events is forbidden or unreliable. — Bursevī reports a prophetic saying that learning astrology takes a branch of sorcery; al-Ḥāfiẓ specifies foretelling from planetary motions and conjunctions, knowledge reserved to God. Bursevī says a prediction of destructive winds from a 581 AH conjunction failed, and reports a student's advice to avoid astrologers (and doctors). Qatāda and Ḥassān b. Bilāl reject claimed stellar purposes beyond the known uses. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(حسان بن بلال العنزي); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(الحافظ,النبي عليه السلام,تلميذ من شيخه)]

## 16:16/q12 Who was first said to study stars and calculation?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ · reports]
- 16:16/q12/p1 The prophet Idrīs. — Bursevī transmits this as an unnamed report. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قيل)]

## 16:16/q13 How should the qibla be found when its direction is uncertain?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether practical star bearings yield exact orientation or only an adequate general direction; the geographical objections concern changing meridians and uncertain coordinates.
- 16:16/q13/p1 Use available indications, including stars, other heavenly bodies, winds and terrestrial signs, and reason toward the likeliest direction. — Rāzī accepts the jurists' inference from route guidance and requires reasoning when signs appear. Qurṭubī distinguishes visible Kaaba, which must be precisely faced with the whole body, from the unseen Kaaba, whose direction is inferred from sun, moon, stars and winds. Ibn al-ʿArabī says ordinary people can use conspicuous fixed al-Jady and Farqads, while experts use risings, settings and north/south positions; he instructs placing the pole behind the left shoulder. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 16:16/q13/p2 Stars and other means establish the general qibla direction, but exact determination is difficult or impossible. — Ibn al-Bannāʾ says a star above Mecca does not keep the same bearing in another city, al-Jady's shoulder or side rule depends on an uncertain meridian, the midsummer zenith differs between cities, and even Indian-circle methods depend on uncertain latitude and longitude. Worshippers need only seek the direction. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو العباس أحمد بن البناء)]

## 16:16/q14 What follows if someone prays toward a wrong direction?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether the error reflects a failure to use available signs or is discovered after carrying out the required effort.
- 16:16/q14/p1 If signs were apparent and the person failed to reason, the prayer must be repeated or is invalid. — Rāzī calls such error negligence and requires repetition when it becomes clear. Qurṭubī says a person able to reason out the qibla who prays away from it has not prayed validly. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 16:16/q14/p2 If the error is discovered afterward, repeating within the prayer time is advised but not obligatory. — Qurṭubī says the original duty was fulfilled as commanded. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 16:16/q15 What should one do if no qibla signs appear?  [بِٱلنَّجْمِ هُمْ يَهْتَدُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether equal uncertainty permits selecting one direction or requires covering every possibility for certainty.
- 16:16/q15/p1 Choose any direction because none has evidence favoring it. — Rāzī reports this proposed rule when all directions are equal. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 16:16/q15/p2 Pray toward every direction to discharge the duty with certainty. — Rāzī prefers this rule. He analogizes an unknown missed prayer to making up all five; against the claim that only one is due, he argues that if all must be performed, all are obligatory. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 16:16/q16 What inward guidance is read in the star and signs?  [وَعَلَٰمَٰتٍۢ بِٱلنَّجْمِ · inward]
turns on: Which spiritual referents the physical signs symbolize and whether guidance is attributed directly to God or mediated through named figures.
- 16:16/q16/p1 The signs are landmarks of the path and stars of gnosis, yet God alone guides the seeker to Himself. — Al-Mālikī in Sulamī says no created sign or route reaches God apart from God; arrival and severance follow prior decree. [1 notes: SULAMI(المالكي)]
- 16:16/q16/p2 The star symbolizes the Messenger, and the signs symbolize the Imams or Ahl al-Bayt. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits Abū ʿAbd Allāh's interpretation and treats it as inner meaning, supported by a report naming the Prophet the star and the Ahl al-Bayt earth's signs and protection. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 16:16/q17 What wider point does this verse make in its passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources draw different connected lessons from the sequence: gratitude and reasoning, exclusive worship, or the placement of spiritual guidance.
- 16:16/q17/p1 Created navigational aids are among God's blessings and call people to reflection and gratitude. — Jishumī groups them with animals, trees and fruits and says reflection makes reasoning obligatory rather than uncritical imitation. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees evidence in beneficial creatures on Earth's surface; Asad says the passage returns to human intellectual potential, linking 16:4, 16:8, 16:14 and 2:30-33. Study Quran stresses guidance across time and place. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the earlier 'casting' images additions to a complete earth: mountains from seismic disturbance or perhaps celestial debris, rivers from rain, and roads/markers later; he cites analogous extension of 'casting' at 54:25. [7 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 16:16/q17/p2 Because these blessings are created by God, worship belongs to Him rather than deities that created none. — Islāḥī draws this implication from the created benefits; Ālūsī likewise links Quraysh's debt of thanks with tawḥīd. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:16/q17/p3 The placement of the spiritual-guidance verse avoids confusion and apparent contradiction with physical navigation signs. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says a later placement after the physical signs might seem more natural but the present placement has this clarifying effect. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 16:16/q18 What does the Arabic case contrast show about settled distinguishing traits?  [وَعَلَٰمَٰتٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the quality is established as a distinguishing mark or observed as an ongoing act.
- 16:16/q18/p1 Rafʿ presents knowledge and similar qualities as established virtues or distinguishing marks. — Sībawayh uses lahu ʿilm ʿilm al-fuqahāʾ with ḥilm, ʿaql and faḍl; lahu sharaf/dīn/fahm likewise describes stable qualities, while yatedayyan, yatasharraf and yatafahham describe unfinished ones. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 16:16/q18/p2 The accusative presents an action or quality observed while occurring, before it is settled. — Sībawayh contrasts accusative ʿilm for someone encountered while learning with praise for established knowledge; lahu ṣawt ṣawt ḥimār describes the sound made then, since sound is an action rather than a settled bodily trait. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 16:16/q19 What accompanying report describes God's prevailing power?  [* · reports]
- 16:16/q19/p1 Created things overcome one another until God creates death and declares His victory over it. — Zayd b. Aslam transmits a report he understood as prophetic; it also says mercy overcomes wrath and charity extinguishes sin. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(زيد بن أسلم)]

## 16:16/q20 What travel maxim is cited beside the route discussion?  [* · reports]
- 16:16/q20/p1 Take the road despite its turns, live in town despite hardship, and marry a virgin even if barren. — Bursevī quotes the anonymous maxim and glosses bārat as barren, ruined or worthless. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 16:16/q21 What signs of permission or prohibition are discussed with divine benefits?  [* · law]
turns on: The legal force of permission language, divine rebuke, mention of a favor, and a speaker's expression of personal avoidance.
- 16:16/q21/p1 Permission can be inferred from explicit allowance, removed blame, pardon, benefit, silence, or approval during revelation. — Burhān lists permission, conditional choice and other forms, mentions 16:5, and cites Jābir's report of withdrawal while Quran was revealed and Ḥassān's poetry before the Prophet as tacit approvals. [2 notes: BURHAN(جابر)]
- 16:16/q21/p2 Divine rebuke before an act signals its prohibition. — Burhān cites rebukes in al-Anfāl, al-Tawba, al-Aḥzāb, al-Taḥrīm and ʿAbasa and discusses Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām's treatment of rebuke. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 16:16/q21/p3 God cannot present a forbidden thing as a favor. — Burhān rejects explaining the favor as merely creating ability to endure it. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 16:16/q21/p4 A speaker's 'As for me, I do not do it' expresses dislike. — Burhān cites the Prophet's statement that he does not eat reclining. [1 notes: BURHAN]
