# 90:8: 10 questions

## 90:8/q01 What does the question 'Have We not made' convey?  [أَلَمْ نَجْعَل · rhetoric]
turns on: Ibn ʿĀshūr permits both assent and reproach; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ attends to the contrast in tense.
- 90:8/q01/p1 It calls for assent to the fact that God made these faculties. — Elmalılı spells out the affirmative answer: yes, We made them. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a confirmatory question. Corpus Coranicum compares the ʾa-lam question in lists of divine favors in 93:6–8 and 94:1–4. [4 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 90:8/q01/p2 It reproaches and rejects the person's boast. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question may reject the boast, especially the claim that no one saw him (90:7); the senses given by God make that boast untenable. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 90:8/q01/p3 Its past tense points to gifts already given before the person became arrogant. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts the past construction with alam against lan, which projects the person's deluded claim that no one will ever overpower him into the future. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 90:8/q02 What are the two eyes and what do they enable?  [عَيْنَيْنِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pair denotes only the physical organs or also the heart's sight; Elmalılı expressly gives both possibilities.
- 90:8/q02/p1 They are the two bodily eyes that give sight of the visible world. — Al-Dāmghānī explicitly calls them literal eyes. Al-Ṭabarī says they see God's proofs; al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī and others say they see visible things. Al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish the tongue's expression and the lips' covering of the mouth and help with speech, eating, drinking and blowing. Al-Bursawī adds sight from earth to heaven, recognition of benefit and harm, and viewing the world, Qurʾān and divine signs. He describes the eye as an image-receiving mirror: small pupil, quick movement, lids and lashes protect it; eyes sit high like a beacon lamp, are paired like sun and moon, have dark brows and pupils to reduce glare, and black lashes that strengthen vision. His report relates black clothing to glare from white marble and says antimony strengthens sight. He says movable pupils see without neck movement and eyes at one level prevent double vision. Al-Biqāʿī says they form symmetrically in the womb's three darknesses, with varied colors and an inexplicable power of sight. Quṭb emphasizes their precise construction; al-Rāzī points to anatomy books for the extraordinary properties of bodily organs. Ibn Jurayj leaves further benefits unspecified as 'and so on.' [26 notes: TAB; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; IBNKATHIR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; RAZI; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 90:8/q02/p2 The pair can also encompass outward sight and an inward eye of insight. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says the bodily eye sees signs of divine workmanship while the heart's eye sees loci of the unseen. Al-Wāsiṭī distinguishes an eye for created things from a special eye perceiving the Creator. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts sensory sight and moral insight, which open desire for perceived good and escape from evil. Al-Bursawī says both outer and inner eyes should be guarded and binocular sight is more complete. Elmalılı offers the visible pair or one visible eye with the heart's eye as possibilities. [5 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي); BIQAI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 90:8/q03 Why are the eyes singled out?  [عَيْنَيْنِ · rhetoric]
- 90:8/q03/p1 Sight is especially useful and directly answers the claim that no one sees the person. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both grounds: the eyes are the most useful of the senses, and their mention answers the preceding claim in 90:7. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 90:8/q04 What lesson follows from God giving these faculties?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw several complementary consequences from the gifts: gratitude, proof of divine power and knowledge, restraint, and moral choice.
- 90:8/q04/p1 The visible gifts call for reflection and gratitude to God. — Qatāda repeatedly calls the eyes, tongue and lips evident, abundant or recurring blessings that God uses to remind people to give thanks; al-Thaʿlabī says they compel gratitude. Al-Baghawī says the enumeration calls the person to reflect. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls them blessings that establish proof against him. Al-Biqāʿī says the intricacy of the eyes and their formation in the womb call for gratitude. Al-Nasafī calls the eyes a favor, and Ibn Jurayj leaves other benefits implicit. [18 notes: TAB(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); BAGHAWI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA; QURTUBI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); QUTB-ZILAL]
- 90:8/q04/p2 The maker of human perception can see, know, overpower, resurrect and reckon the person. — Abū Isḥāq and Ibn al-Jawzī invoke the eyes as signs of resurrection; al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran add reckoning. Al-Rāzī says the maker of rational heart and eloquent tongue from humble fluid can destroy creation and knows hidden things, removing excuse for denial or spending against religion. Al-Biqāʿī says the benefactor knows affairs and can bring benefit or harm, personally or through whomever He wills. Ibn ʿĀshūr invokes 67:14: the giver of knowledge cannot be ignorant or powerless. Al-Ḥasan frames the boast as a challenge to who will account for spending. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-QT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الحسن); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 90:8/q04/p3 Sight and speech are entrusted for lawful, accountable use. — The reported address commands closing lids and lips against forbidden sight and speech and restraining sexual desire. Al-Biqāʿī urges lawful perception and avoidance of what God forbids. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ stresses accountable sight and words rather than mere anatomy shared with animals. Iṣlāḥī says wealth has distracted addressees from seeing the needy around them. Quṭb contrasts the gift of sight with boasting and withholding what God supplied. Al-Bursawī says the outward and inward eyes should both be guarded. [12 notes: THALABI(رسول الله); BAGHAWI(الحديث); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI(أبو حازم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حازم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 90:8/q04/p4 These senses equip people for knowledge, moral discernment and responsible choice. — Al-Qāshānī says bodily faculties permit perfection, learning from lessons, asking and speaking before guidance to good and evil. Ibn ʿĀshūr presents a progression from senses that acquire knowledge, to speech that communicates it, to guidance that tests good and evil. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says innate discernment equips a human to bear the difficult trust. Kur'an Yolu links external knowledge to judgment of truth and falsehood and choice; it calls humans exceptional, responsible beings. Al-Ṭabarī specifically says the eyes see divine proofs. [6 notes: TAB; QASHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 90:8/q05 What does the reported divine address say about guarding the faculties?  [عَيْنَيْنِ · reports]
- 90:8/q05/p1 God gave coverings or means of restraint, so people should close their eyes, lips and sexual organs against forbidden use. — The Prophet's report in al-Thaʿlabī speaks of two coverings each for tongue, eyes and private parts. Al-Baghawī describes lips, eyelids and other bodily means; his fuller version addresses Adam's child. Abū Ḥāzim's report in al-Qurṭubī specifies lips, eyelids and protective coverings against forbidden speech, sight and desire. Al-Durr's reports command lawful use and closing eyes against the forbidden; one adds that humans cannot bear God's anger or withstand His vengeance. These are variants of the same moral address in the notes. [8 notes: THALABI(رسول الله); BAGHAWI(الحديث); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI(أبو حازم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حازم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 90:8/q06 How does this reminder answer the preceding claims and hardship?  [* · links]
turns on: Which nearby human claim or statement the divine question answers: being unseen, being unconquerable, hardship, unbelief, or spending.
- 90:8/q06/p1 The giver of eyes must see and know the person who imagines no one sees him. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī make the created eyes answer the claim about being unseen. Al-Rāzī connects it to 90:7 as evidence of divine power; al-Bayḍāwī says the favor clarifies that warning. Al-Ālūsī adds God's capacity to requite and account. Elmalılı asks whether the maker of sight could fail to see deeds. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates both seeing and overpowering claims to God's creation of perception and speech and cites 67:14. Al-Sāmarrāʾī calls the response direct. Study Quran draws accountability for the faculties' use. [12 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI; BAYDAWI; ALUSI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 90:8/q06/p2 The creator of the organs can overpower the one who claims nobody can overpower him. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī link this to 90:5: God made the eyes that see proofs and the organs that speak. Al-Rāzī's fuller excerpt says eyes, tongue, lips and guidance display divine power against the boast. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the maker of perception and speech cannot be powerless; his fuller note invokes 67:14. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 90:8/q06/p3 The faculties answer the account of human hardship by removing excuse during moral testing. — Al-Jishumī says the senses accompany the hardship of responsibility. Al-Ṭabrisī explicitly connects this reading with 90:4: God tests by commands after equipping humanity. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the senses and innate discernment prepare a human to bear the difficult trust. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BINTSHATI]
- 90:8/q06/p4 The wondrous capacities and guidance expose the unbeliever's own responsibility for unbelief. — Al-Jishumī reports this fourth connection: God made the body's capacities as evidence against the unbeliever and guided him, making his unbelief his own doing. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 90:8/q06/p5 The maker of his organs can hold the boastful spender to account. — Al-Ḥasan takes the boast as asking who can account for expenditure; al-Rāzī reports the answer as the One who created the person's organs. Al-Rāzī also says divine knowledge of hidden things leaves no excuse for spending against God's religion. [2 notes: RAZI(الحسن)]

## 90:8/q07 How do the eyes and other faculties lead into the two paths?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the following pair of paths denotes moral alternatives or the breasts that nourish a child; al-Qaffāl prefers the moral reading.
- 90:8/q07/p1 The two elevated paths are good and evil, made discernible to a morally equipped person. — Al-Rāzī glosses najd as an elevated road and likens clear proofs to a high visible way; he says most exegetes take the paths as good and evil. Abū Hurayra reports the Prophet saying these are the roads of good and evil and one must not prefer evil. Al-Qaffāl prefers this interpretation. Al-Qāshānī, Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and Kur'an Yolu relate sight, speech and moral awareness to distinguishing and choosing the paths; Kur'an Yolu adds true versus false and good versus bad. Al-Rāzī compares 76:2–3, where creation with hearing and sight is followed by guidance and grateful or ungrateful response. Ibn ʿĀshūr says senses acquire knowledge, speech communicates it and guidance distinguishes and tests good and evil. [9 notes: RAZI(أبي هريرة); QASHANI; BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI(القفال)+]
- 90:8/q07/p2 The two elevated paths are a child's two breasts, supplying life and provision. — Al-Rāzī reports this explanation from Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib; the two breasts are paths by which the child receives sustenance. [1 notes: RAZI(ابن عباس وسعيد بن المسيب)]

## 90:8/q08 How do the gifts prepare the later call to difficult deeds?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes explain different stages in the sura's ethical progression from equipped perception to difficult action.
- 90:8/q08/p1 The endowed person is responsible for taking the steep path and serving people in need. — Ibn al-Zubayr says people have eyes and means of reflection yet fail to save themselves, directing attention to the challenge in 90:11. Corpus Coranicum says rhetorical questions turn to a categorical judgment that the person endowed with insight and choice fails the steep path. Iṣlāḥī traces 90:8–17 from sight, speech and moral awareness through freeing slaves, feeding the poor, faith, patience and compassion, as remedies for greed and service to God. He also says the wealthy have failed to use their eyes to see the needy. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says sight and speech prepare the warning about moral consequences, especially of words. [5 notes: BIQAI(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI]
- 90:8/q08/p2 The hard path and service call for patience, then mercy and exhortation. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says choosing a path, climbing the ascent, freeing enslaved people, enduring famine and caring for orphans and the destitute all require perseverance. He says patience is mentioned first because of the earlier toil, ascent and two paths, and mercy follows the duties of freeing and feeding. Iṣlāḥī says exhortation is a believer's obligation and a virtuous deed remains incomplete without it. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 90:8/q09 How is this verse arranged within the passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes identify either the local stanza pattern or a parallel passage as the organizing connection.
- 90:8/q09/p1 It belongs to a second triplet of gifts within two rhymed triplets of polemical questions. — Corpus Coranicum says the second stanza resumes the introduction's statement about humanity; repeated third-person questions bind two triplets. The second triplet lists sight, speech and moral direction, recalling creation in 90:4; a rhyme shift accents the contrast with human self-importance. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 90:8/q09/p2 The parallel account of creation joins senses to guidance and the possibility of gratitude or ingratitude. — Al-Rāzī explicitly compares 76:2–3, which describes human creation with hearing and sight followed by guidance to two paths and a grateful or ungrateful response. [1 notes: RAZI]

## 90:8/q10 What incidental excerpt material accompanies the verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The two notes preserve different incidental content in the supplied excerpts and do not dispute one another.
- 90:8/q10/p1 One excerpt supplies only the verse text, without an explanation of it. — The al-Rāzī full-text segment contains the wording about eyes, tongue, lips and two paths but no commentary on those verses. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 90:8/q10/p2 A lexical heading lists other senses of ghishiya without examples here. — Al-Dāmghānī's excerpt also names elevation or riding over, drowsiness and darkness as senses of ghishiya, but does not exemplify them in the passage. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
