# 26:218: 12 questions

## 26:218/q01 How is 'the One who sees you' construed grammatically?  [ٱلَّذِى يَرَىٰكَ · grammar]
- 26:218/q01/p1 The relative clause admits nominative, accusative, or genitive construction. — Al-Samīn gives nominative as predicate of an omitted subject, accusative as praise, and genitive as adjective, apposition, or explicative apposition. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 26:218/q02 What does 'sees you' mean?  [يَرَىٰكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'seeing' is glossed simply as perception or specified as sight together with knowledge.
- 26:218/q02/p1 God perceives the Prophet. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly glosses yarāka as 'perceives you.' [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 26:218/q02/p2 God sees him with sight and knowledge. — Al-Biqāʿī specifies both sight and knowledge as the Prophet leaves his bed's comfort for God's pleasure. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:218/q03 What does God's seeing the Prophet convey?  [يَرَىٰكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which implication of divine seeing is drawn: personal care and acceptance, sufficiency for reliance, or vigilance over intentions.
- 26:218/q03/p1 God attends to, cares for, accepts, and aids the Prophet. — Ibn Kathīr cites 52:48 for divine attention and care; Ibn ʿĀshūr says God sees the Prophet turn to Him and accepts his devotion, also comparing 52:48. Iṣlāḥī says God accounts for his night prayer, supplication, and efforts for companions and aids him. Al-Bayḍāwī relates God's awareness of his deserving state to promised power against enemies and help for allies, reassurance, and reliance. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 26:218/q03/p2 God's encompassing knowledge secures benefit and averts harm for one who relies on Him. — Al-Biqāʿī says the description after the command to trust establishes divine sufficiency; Bursawī says God's seeing explains the promised mercy and grounds the Prophet's reliance. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 26:218/q03/p3 God's awareness of every intention inspires watchfulness of hidden states. — The Najmī interpretations, quoted by Bursawī, say God sees intention and resolve in every undertaking, turning the Prophet from witnessing creatures toward vigilance over inward states. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 26:218/q04 What does 'when you rise' refer to?  [حِينَ تَقُومُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether rising is restricted to prayer (and which prayer or setting), describes physical posture, extends to every state, or denotes the Prophet's religious mission; several appeal to the following phrase about movement among prostrators.
- 26:218/q04/p1 The Prophet rises for prayer. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Kalbī, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, and many exegetes identify prayer; al-Ṭabrisī includes setting out from bed or seat and prayer alone or in a congregation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls prayer the apparent sense. Al-Wāḥidī says prayer-specific readings stress prayer's importance. Asad objects that restricting the verse to prayer is too narrow; Iṣlāḥī notes other unspecified exegetical alternatives. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(أكثر المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ASAD-NOTES(Other commentators)-]
- 26:218/q04/p2 He rises specifically for night prayer or tahajjud. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī specify the night vigil; Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite established religious or Qurʾānic usage. Bursawī adduces the merit and persistence of night prayer. The Study Quran says many considered it obligatory in Mecca before 73:20 made it voluntary. Iṣlāḥī finds the following movement among prostrators in 26:219 a strong indication. Elmalılı allows night vigil as the special case of prayer. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(one interpretation); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 26:218/q04/p3 He prays alone or rises into private retreat. — Muqātil and al-Ḥasan specify solitary prayer; al-Ḥasan explains 'standing' as leaving others and reaching solitude. Yaḥyā's report contrasts prayer alone here with congregational prayer in the next phrase and attributes one of those two views to al-Ḥasan or Qatāda. Al-Ṭabrisī describes rising unseen at night; Abū Ḥayyān reports private retreat. Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-Ḥasan's shorter 'when alone.' [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 26:218/q04/p4 God sees him wherever he is and in every state or activity. — Mujāhid's wording is 'wherever he is'; Qatāda adds standing, sitting, and all conditions. Ikrima includes standing and sitting among people; al-Wāḥidī extends the sense to every action he rises to and explicitly prefers it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows all activities while favoring prayer's apparent sense; Asad prefers Mujāhid's breadth over a prayer restriction. Al-Ṭabrisī and others transmit both broad and prayer-related readings. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو مجاهد,قتادة); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ASAD-NOTES(Mujahid); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 26:218/q04/p5 He rises from his bed, seat, or place. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says bed or sitting place; Abū al-Jawzāʾ says his place. Al-Biqāʿī highlights leaving bed after sleep and its comfort for God's pleasure. Al-Ṭabrisī combines physical rising from bed or seat with prayer. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو الجوزاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 26:218/q04/p6 God sees the Prophet in the successive postures of prayer. — ʿIkrima names standing, bowing, and prostration, while Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, and another ʿIkrima report include sitting and relate the next phrase to movements through prayer. Yaḥyā reports this as one account of the two phrases; al-Thaʿlabī's Mujāhid combines the postures with God's seeing him wherever he is. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); THALABI(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,قتادة)]
- 26:218/q04/p7 He rises to lead congregational prayer. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī place his movement among worshippers in the standing, bowing, prostration, and sitting of a congregation. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ prefers this as the apparent meaning; the Study Quran also reports it. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(some); RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(another interpretation); TABATABAI+]
- 26:218/q04/p8 He rises for night worship and checks his companions' worship. — Al-Zamakhsharī links the sight of his night worship to attentive inspection of companions and preparation for the afterlife; al-Rāzī offers this as one interpretation. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's picture of discreet inspection of worship and inner states. Iṣlāḥī includes his efforts to share night prayer's blessings with companions. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:218/q04/p9 He rises to call, warn, or act for the religious mission. — Al-Baghawī transmits rising to summon people to Islam; al-Ṭabrisī gives rising to warn and deliver the message. Elmalılı adds enjoining right and exalting God's word. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; ELMALILI]
- 26:218/q04/p10 He rises and moves among worshippers while managing religious affairs. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī describe God's unfailing sight of the Prophet whenever he rises or moves among worshippers in the conduct of religious affairs. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 26:218/q04/p11 He rises to fight the polytheists. — Al-Māwardī proposes that the next phrase then concerns his affairs among Muslims and the religious rulings he establishes. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 26:218/q05 What report describes the Prophet visiting his companions after night prayer ceased to be obligatory?  [حِينَ تَقُومُ · reports]
- 26:218/q05/p1 He toured their homes and heard their remembrance and recitation humming like hornets or wasps. — Al-Zamakhsharī gives the account after the night obligation ceased; al-Rāzī repeats it as anonymous and supplies no grading. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's account as devotional murmuring and adds that the Prophet checked their worship and inner states. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 26:218/q06 How did the Prophet maintain night prayer?  [حِينَ تَقُومُ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe distinct circumstances: praying seated when able to continue and making up a missed vigil.
- 26:218/q06/p1 He never omitted it, praying seated when ill or tired. — Bursawī transmits ʿĀʾisha's report of the Prophet's persistent night prayer and seated prayer during illness or fatigue. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عائشة)]
- 26:218/q06/p2 If he missed it through illness or another cause, he made up twelve cycles during the day. — Bursawī attributes the report to Muslim. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 26:218/q07 What is the special merit of night prayer?  [حِينَ تَقُومُ · interpretation]
- 26:218/q07/p1 It is the best prayer after the obligatory prayer and merits making up when missed. — Bursawī quotes a prophetic report calling it the best prayer after obligatory prayer; he says tahajjud exceeds other voluntary prayers in merit, so it is made up although ordinary supererogatory prayers are not. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 26:218/q08 How does the verse fit its surrounding discourse?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the wording to the preceding reliance and mercy, the adjoining mention of prostrators, the sūrah's opening, or a parallel account of prayer.
- 26:218/q08/p1 The description after the command to rely on God grounds reliance in His care and support. — Al-Bayḍāwī connects awareness of the Prophet's state to promised victory and help for allies, reassuring him; al-Biqāʿī grounds divine sufficiency in encompassing knowledge that secures benefit and averts harm; Bursawī says seeing explains the promised mercy. Al-Zamakhsharī moves from divine mercy to the Prophet's night worship and attention to companions. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 26:218/q08/p2 The pairing shows care for Muslims alongside care for their Messenger as part of his blessing. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes this as the effect of the compact pairing of the Prophet's standing and his movement among prostrators. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:218/q08/p3 The concluding discourse returns to the sūrah's opening warnings and signs. — Iṣlāḥī says Qurʾānic warnings are true, signs of punishment can come, and earlier prophetic histories already provide signs for the stubborn Quraysh. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:218/q08/p4 The Prophet's vigil has a parallel in Gospel accounts of Jesus praying and urging disciples to pray during trial. — Iṣlāḥī cites Jesus praying on a mountain, urging wakefulness, and repeatedly waking disciples to seek protection. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:218/q09 What inward rising and divine vision are described?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward reading traces stages of resurrection or calls for sustained awareness of divine watchfulness.
- 26:218/q09/p1 God accompanies and preserves the Prophet through successive spiritual resurrections. — Al-Qāshānī names the minor resurrection, the intermediate primordial state, and the major resurrection in unity and steadfastness. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 26:218/q09/p2 Awareness of divine watchfulness calls for steadiness in absence and presence. — Al-Wāsiṭī says one never escapes the watch of divine power; the Najmī interpretations quoted by Bursawī say God's sight of intention draws attention from creatures to hidden states. [2 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 26:218/q10 What does reliance on God require or consist of?  [* · inward]
turns on: The descriptions emphasize knowledge, faith, dependence, certainty, exclusive hope, or servanthood; two reports differ on whether reliance's reality can be captured through description.
- 26:218/q10/p1 Outwardly it follows knowledge; inwardly it puts certainty into practice. — Al-Kattānī distinguishes outward, inward, and true realization of reliance. [1 notes: SULAMI(الكتاني)]
- 26:218/q10/p2 It accompanies faith and grows with it through discipline, patience, and God's aid. — Abū al-Ḥusayn al-Rāzī says seeking reliance entails preserving faith, disciplining the self by its rules, practicing patience, and seeking divine help. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو الحسين الرازي)]
- 26:218/q10/p3 It is the heart's dependence on God within the covenant of faith. — Ibn ʿUyayna identifies this inward dependence as part of faith's covenant. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عيينة)]
- 26:218/q10/p4 Its reality cannot be known through description alone. — An unnamed authority calls reliance part of the covenant of faith but denies that description alone discloses its reality; Abū Sulaymān al-Dārānī contrasts his own access through its defining quality. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); SULAMI(أبو سليمان الداراني)-]
- 26:218/q10/p5 It can be known through its defining quality. — Abū Sulaymān al-Dārānī says this in express contrast to the claim that its reality eludes description. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو سليمان الداراني); SULAMI(بعضهم)-]
- 26:218/q10/p6 It is strength grounded in dependence and certainty. — Reported from an unnamed authority by al-Sulamī. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 26:218/q10/p7 It abandons fear and hope in anyone besides God. — Reported from an unnamed authority by al-Sulamī. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 26:218/q10/p8 It subjects the self to servanthood and fixes the heart and gaze on God. — An unnamed authority places the heart with lordship and directs one's whole gaze to God. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 26:218/q11 Does the adjoining description of movement among prostrators support believing ancestors?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether 'movement among prostrators' may refer to ancestral transmission, whether 'father' in 6:74 can be shifted to uncle or maternal grandfather, and whether the pure-loins report can outweigh the Qurʾānic wording.
- 26:218/q11/p1 Yes: it can describe the Prophet's soul passing through a line of believing ancestors. — Al-Rāzī reports the Rāfiḍa's inference from movement among prostrators. They cite the ungraded report of transfer through pure loins and wombs and 9:28's description of unbelievers as impure. To meet 6:74's naming of Āzar as Abraham's father, they say 'father' can mean uncle, citing Ishmael as father of Jacob's sons in 2:133 and the Prophet's calling al-ʿAbbās father; or maternal grandfather, citing Jesus among Abraham's descendants in 6:84–85. Al-Rāzī objects that this departs from the apparent sense, combines possible senses of an ambiguous term, and sets a solitary report against the Qurʾān. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الرافضة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL-]
- 26:218/q11/p2 No: the ancestry inference fails against the apparent wording and cannot be established by that report. — Al-Rāzī retains the apparent 'his father Āzar' in 6:74; he rejects making one ambiguous expression carry all possible senses at once and says the solitary pure-loins report cannot outweigh the Qurʾān. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 26:218/q12 What other readings of 'when you rise' are reported without detail?  [حِينَ تَقُومُ · interpretation]
- 26:218/q12/p1 Exegetes offer various other possibilities, but this note does not specify them. — Iṣlāḥī acknowledges unspecified exegetical alternatives before preferring the tahajjud reading. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Exegetes)]
