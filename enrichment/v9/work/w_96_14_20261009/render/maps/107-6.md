# 107:6: 22 questions

## 107:6/q01 Who are those described as showing off?  [ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the public prayer identifies the historical hypocrites, depicts a general moral trait, or addresses the custodians; Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the first to a Medinan dating.
- 107:6/q01/p1 They are hypocrites who present themselves as worshippers, praying before people but neglecting prayer in private. — Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others identify hypocrites; Ibn ʿAbbās and Mālik connect them with 4:142. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās say public Islam protected blood and families. Al-Nasafī says they disbelieve prayer obligatory; another account says they merely join, bow and rise without seeking God. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes this referent conditional on a Medinan setting and compares the apparent fear of hypocrites in 9:64. Al-Qurṭubī cites ostentation as evidence for the identification. Their absence of private prayer, reward-expectation and regret varies by account. [41 notes: TUSTARI; TAB(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الحسن,الضحاك); NAHHAS; MAWARDI(علي وابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(علي، ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); TABRISI(علي، ابن عباس); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI(ابن عباس ومجاهد والإمام مالك,بعضهم); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:6/q01/p2 The condemnation extends to anyone who displays a deed without sincere intent for God, including professing Muslims. — Al-Māwardī gives this as an alternative and cites God's rejection of work for another; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the usage extends beyond hypocrisy. Al-Biqāʿī cites Ibn al-Zubayr: these traits may occur among Muslims without making every bearer a denier; he compares hadiths about traits of hypocrisy and sin during fornication. Al-Zamakhsharī says some scholars exhibit ostentation. Study Quran says religious hypocrisy is not restricted to Muslims. [9 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 107:6/q01/p3 They are the custodians who perform prayer to impress others. — Iṣlāḥī calls their prayer a theatrical enactment and attributes their refusal to spend or lend to miserliness. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 107:6/q02 What does their showing off mean and seek?  [يُرَآءُونَ · meaning]
turns on: The notes distinguish the general motive of human approval, the specific public/private pattern, and enhancement of an otherwise performed act; al-Rāzī explicitly contrasts a hypocrite with a show-off.
- 107:6/q02/p1 They display prayer or other apparently pious deeds to be seen, praised, admired, esteemed or rewarded by people rather than worshipping God sincerely. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī oppose human notice to intention for God; al-Wāḥidī cites 4:142. Al-Qurṭubī calls worldly gain and status in hearts the root motive; al-Biqāʿī adds favors from onlookers. Bursawī calls seeing a figurative route to praise or recognition. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls it an outward state unlike the inner one and cites Qur'anic uses about giving and worship at 2:264, 4:38, 4:142 and 8:47. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī, Sayyid Quṭb, Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran stress reputation or approval; Iṣlāḥī says such prayer has no inner effect and increases callousness. Al-Sulamī says show-offs seek standing, not an inspiration that turns them from notice. Ibn al-ʿArabī, cited by al-Qurṭubī, lists ostentation in religious appearance, ascetic clothing, pious speech, and embellished worship or charity. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(علي بن أبي طالب); TAB-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNABIHATIM(علي بن أبي طالب); SULAMI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:6/q02/p2 Their show consists especially in praying when observed and abandoning prayer when unobserved. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Kalbī contrast public and private conduct; al-Biqāʿī explains that seeking present approval removes their reason to pray when alone. Ibn Zayd says prayer is not even their usual conduct; al-Ḥasan adds lack of regret when it is missed. [22 notes: FARRA; TAB(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الحسن); MAWARDI(علي وابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(علي، ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); TABRISI(علي، ابن عباس); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:6/q02/p3 They outwardly improve prayer or display humility and piety absent from the heart. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes this show-off from a hypocrite who hides unbelief, or from one who never prays privately; Bursawī repeats the contrast and defines it as seeking worldly gain. Al-Qurṭubī says even a sinner may pray so people call him a worshipper. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; QURTUBI]

## 107:6/q03 Why is the verb a reciprocal form?  [يُرَآءُونَ · grammar]
- 107:6/q03/p1 The person shows deeds to an audience and the audience returns praise or admiration. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī explain murāʾāh or riyāʾ as reciprocal display; Ibn ʿĀshūr says no simple form is attested because the act entails repeated display. [8 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR]

## 107:6/q04 How is يُرَآءُونَ read?  [يُرَآءُونَ · readings]
turns on: The shortened variants differ in hamza doubling; Abū Ḥayyān offers transitivity for the doubled form and easing or alif loss for the undoubled form.
- 107:6/q04/p1 The majority read yurāʾūna, the present of rāʾā on the fāʿala pattern. — Abū Ḥayyān explicitly attributes this to the majority. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 107:6/q04/p2 Ibn Abī Isḥāq and Abū al-Ashhab read a shortened form with doubled hamza. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report it; Abū Ḥayyān analyzes the strengthened hamza as making the verb transitive like raʾā/arā, with imperfect and plural resembling yuṣallī/yuṣallūn. Their rendering of the reader's name and the vowel varies. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن أبي إسحاق وأبو الأشهب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق وأبو الأشهب); ABUHAYYAN(ابن أبي إسحاق والأشهب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق والأشهب); ALUSI(ابن إسحق والأشهب)]
- 107:6/q04/p3 Another report from Ibn Abī Isḥāq has the shortened form without doubled hamza. — Abū Ḥayyān explains this either by lightening a heavy doubled hamza or by unmotivated deletion of the alif; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī report the variant. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن أبي إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق وأبو الأشهب); ABUHAYYAN(ابن أبي إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); ALUSI(ابن إسحق)]

## 107:6/q05 Why place هُمْ before the verbal predicate?  [هُمْ يُرَآءُونَ · rhetoric]
- 107:6/q05/p1 The explicit, fronted pronoun strengthens the assertion that they show off. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies fronting of the subject before the verbal predicate as emphasis. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 107:6/q06 Why repeat ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ in this description?  [ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ · grammar]
- 107:6/q06/p1 Repeating the phrase permits a verb to follow a verb instead of coordinating a verb directly with a noun. — Bursawī transmits the explanation of Burhān al-Qurʾān. [1 notes: BURSEVI(برهان القرآن)]

## 107:6/q07 When does visible worship count as ostentation?  [يُرَآءُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether optional status itself defines condemned riyāʾ or the performer's purpose does; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ disputes the former restriction.
- 107:6/q07/p1 Visibility alone does not make worship ostentatious; the intention to win praise does, while sincere public duties or acts shown to encourage others are acceptable. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī and Bursawī recommend public obligations as Islamic signs that avert suspicion or blame and private voluntary worship as the usual course, except when it encourages imitation. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī cite Prophetic wording about not concealing obligations, without a grading in al-Nasafī. Ibn Kathīr says sincere work remains sincere if its performer later enjoys being seen; his two-reward report has a strange route, middling Saʿīd b. Bashīr, rare transmission from al-Aʿmash, and a parallel called gharīb by al-Tirmidhī, with other routes mursal. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects that restricting condemned riyāʾ to optional works is unnecessary: publicly fulfilling a duty or setting an example is not plausibly condemned. [28 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(الكشاف); BINTSHATI+]
- 107:6/q07/p2 The condemnation of showing off should be limited to optional worship; required public duties and examples are exempt. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ attributes this restriction to some commentators and rejects the need for it, though she agrees that sincere public obligations and examples are not condemned. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين); BINTSHATI-]

## 107:6/q08 What incident illustrates suspicion of public ostentation?  [يُرَآءُونَ · reports]
- 107:6/q08/p1 An observer saw a man prolong a thanksgiving prostration in a mosque and said it would have been better at home. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī transmit the anecdote as suspicion of ostentation or reputation-seeking; al-Rāzī says embarrassment must not stop voluntary worship. [5 notes: KASHSHAF(بعضهم); KASHSHAF-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(بعضهم)]

## 107:6/q09 What do the transmitted sayings say about sincerity and ostentation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports address distinct outcomes and cases: mixed intentions, sincere small deeds, subtle danger, punishment, permissible pleasure after sincere prayer, and public duties.
- 107:6/q09/p1 God accepts only acts wholly for Him and rejects worship shared with another intended recipient. — Tustarī transmits a command to sincere work, rejecting division between God and kinship; al-Māwardī transmits God's saying that work for another associates a partner with Him and is unneeded by Him. [3 notes: TUSTARI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI(النبي); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 107:6/q09/p2 Sincere intention makes even little work sufficient. — Tustarī reports the Prophet's instruction to Muʿādh. [1 notes: TUSTARI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 107:6/q09/p3 Ostentation is extremely hidden, like a black ant on black ground in a dark night. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī report the Prophetic simile; al-Rāzī states no grading. They and Bursawī say avoiding it is difficult except with disciplined sincerity. [9 notes: KASHSHAF(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله); RAZI(النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام); RAZI-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI]
- 107:6/q09/p4 A Hell valley from which Hell seeks refuge four hundred times daily awaits ostentatious reciters, givers, pilgrims and fighters. — Ibn Kathīr transmits the report from Ibn ʿAbbās, including those going out in God's path. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 107:6/q09/p5 God will expose and humiliate one who publicizes deeds for human notice. — Ibn Kathīr transmits a Prophetic report through ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr, and notes another route through an unnamed man via Aḥmad; Ibn al-Mubārak calls a related Abū Hurayra report a good hadith for show-offs. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR(ابن المبارك,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن المبارك,عبد الله بن عمرو عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 107:6/q09/p6 A person pleased when his private prayer is seen receives two rewards, for secret and public worship. — Ibn Kathīr calls one Abū Hurayra route gharīb, rates Saʿīd ibn Bashīr middling, and notes the rarity of his report from al-Aʿmash; al-Tirmidhī calls another route gharīb and notes mursal alternatives. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 107:6/q09/p7 God's obligatory duties should be openly declared. — Al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī cite Prophetic wording, including wa-lā ghumma fī farāʾiḍ Allāh; al-Nasafī gives no grading. [3 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 107:6/q10 How does showing off relate to heedlessness in prayer?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sahw names failures of timing or form, loss of prayer's moral purpose, or deliberate private abandonment; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ expressly rejects timing and bodily-distraction readings.
- 107:6/q10/p1 It is a further defect in prayer alongside neglect of time or attentive performance. — Al-Baghawī transmits Qatāda's indifference to prayer, Mujāhid's treating it lightly, Abū al-ʿĀliya's late prayer and incomplete bowing/prostration, and an account lacking hope or fear; al-Ḥasan adds no regret over missed prayer. Al-Rāzī says prayer must be free of missing its time, distraction during it, and ostentation. Ibn Kathīr gives late ʿAṣr without calm or reverence, perhaps for show, alongside the hypocrites of 4:142. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو العالية,الحسن,قتادة,قيل,مجاهد); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 107:6/q10/p2 Showing off itself reveals neglect of prayer's purpose: conscious worship of God that should shape conduct and care for others. — Sayyid Quṭb says sincere prayer requires presence before God; Iṣlāḥī says it needs pure intention, yields gratitude and spending, and links prayer with spending as two pillars of sharīʿah. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reads ʿan as heedlessness of purpose: standing before God, restraining wrongdoing, humility and care for needy people, citing 90:11–17. She rejects treating forgetfulness, missed prayer or time, fidgeting, or yawning as the point because even punctual worship can feign humility for benefit. Kur'an Yolu stresses inward faith, intention, sincerity and reverence and cites 1:5 and 3:64 for divine unity; Study Quran contrasts 70:23 and 70:34. [11 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]
- 107:6/q10/p3 Their heedlessness is deliberately abandoning prayer in private while corrupting its inward quality in public. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts material abandonment with inner corruption; Ibn ʿĀshūr says sahw literally is inadvertent forgetfulness but here sarcastically denotes deliberate neglect, paralleling 'forgetting' associates and heedlessness of signs in al-Aʿrāf and Yūnus; genuine forgetfulness is excused. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 107:6/q11 What does the warning teach about showing off, sincere worship and faith?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes distinguish the religious gravity of ostentation from whether every person displaying one trait can be identified as a denier or hypocrite.
- 107:6/q11/p1 Ostentation corrupts devotion and resembles association of partners with God; true prayer requires sincerity. — Tustarī calls it hidden shirk of conduct, distinguishing it from unforgivable shirk concerning God's essence; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī call it a branch of unbelief. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says prayer for people is not accepted. Iṣlāḥī says another motive defeats prayer's purpose; Kur'an Yolu connects sincerity to tawḥīd. [9 notes: TUSTARI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 107:6/q11/p2 The vices expose denial of judgment and call believers to avoid such traits, though an individual Muslim's lapse does not necessarily prove unbelief. — Al-Biqāʿī says denial of resurrection and recompense is the root of contempt for sacred matters and these vices; he cites Ibn al-Zubayr's caution that Muslims may display them. Al-Rāzī frames the severe threat as a problem because these three sins alone do not make a Muslim a hypocrite. Sayyid Quṭb says religious life joins inner worship and social care and yields cooperation and mercy. [8 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI]

## 107:6/q12 How does this clause connect to the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: How the consequential fāʾ and descriptive clauses attach the worshippers to the earlier denier, especially if the orphan-pusher is taken as a particular person.
- 107:6/q12/p1 It continues the warning to inattentive worshippers and joins ostentation to withholding aid. — Al-Nasafī joins the clauses to the preceding woe; Sayyid Quṭb identifies the careless worshippers by both later traits. Corpus Coranicum says the emphatic woe opens a vice catalogue and verses 4–6 target ritual neglect; its cross-correspondence sets religious omissions in verses 1 and 4–6 against social omissions in verses 2–3 and 7. Iṣlāḥī treats ostentation and miserliness as causes of devotionless prayer. [6 notes: NASAFI; QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 107:6/q12/p2 The consequential fāʾ escalates from denial and orphan-abuse to prayer-neglect, ostentation and withholding, identifying one group of deniers. — Al-Bayḍāwī says prayer is religion's pillar, ostentation a branch of unbelief and zakāt its bridge; al-Ālūsī adds customary loans. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the causal fāʾ makes show-praying hypocrites part of the deniers; al-Ālūsī transmits a causal reading from prior coordination attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Mālik. Al-Zamakhsharī's syntax, reported by Abū Ḥayyān, joins fa-dhālika to the denier, elides the answer to araʾayta, and treats the woe's descriptive plural as standing for a generic singular pronoun. [6 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 107:6/q12/p3 The later vices can form a prompted digression if the orphan-pusher is one named person. — Al-Ālūsī reports the Kashf author's objection that later prayer-neglect and ostentation would ill fit as further labels of a named orphan-pusher; al-Ālūsī treats denial of religion as the sūra's principal concern and compares a rebuke at 5:91. [2 notes: ALUSI(صاحب الكشف)]
- 107:6/q12/p4 Calling them 'those who pray' instead of using a pronoun exposes bad treatment of God and people. — Al-Bayḍāwī identifies this as the effect of the overt descriptive phrase. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 107:6/q12/p5 The final ostentation and withholding show that even lesser visible good does not overcome their greed. — Al-Biqāʿī says they can show off with some smaller good yet cannot give even a trivial benefit; he adds that the best people most benefit God's dependents. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 107:6/q13 How does the clause connect with other Qur'anic passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources draw different intertextual links rather than disagreeing over one exclusive parallel.
- 107:6/q13/p1 The hypocrites' displayed prayer recalls 4:142 and other passages on hypocrisy or ostentation. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran cite 4:142; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ lists 2:264, 4:38, 4:142 and 8:47; Abū Ḥayyān points back to his treatment of 2:264. Study Quran directs readers to 63:1–8 on hypocrisy; al-Qurṭubī directs them to 2:271 on disclosed charity. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:6/q13/p2 True faith's established prayer and spending in 8:4 counter this sūra's showy prayer and refusal to give. — Al-Biqāʿī makes the prayer-and-giving connection explicit. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:6/q13/p3 Al-Kawthar answers the four vices: giving, prayer, exclusive devotion and sacrifice counter stinginess, neglect, ostentation and withheld aid. — Al-Biqāʿī and al-Suyūṭī map the four oppositions, citing 108:1–2; Elmalılı sees al-Kawthar as condensing prior sūras, especially from al-Ḍuḥā, in an account of Muḥammad's rank. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI]

## 107:6/q14 Is there a comparison outside the Qur'an?  [* · links]
- 107:6/q14/p1 Matthew 6:5 likewise depicts hypocrites praying publicly to be seen. — Rudolph draws the comparison in Corpus Coranicum. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Rudolph)]

## 107:6/q15 What revelation setting is assigned to this verse's sūra or final section?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether references to hypocrisy require a Medinan setting and whether evidence supports a later ending; Asad rejects the split date.
- 107:6/q15/p1 The whole seven-verse sūra is Meccan, possibly early in the mission after al-Takāthur. — Al-Jishumī reports Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima and Qatāda; Asad dates it to the early mission, probably after sūra 102, and rejects a Medinan final section for lack of historical or textual evidence. Study Quran reports this as the majority view; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows it. [5 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة، قتادة); ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES(some commentators)+]
- 107:6/q15/p2 The whole sūra is Medinan. — Al-Jishumī reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Wāqidī. Sayyid Quṭb leans to this because hypocrisy and ostentation were unfamiliar in Mecca; Study Quran allows it because political advantage made hypocrisy possible in Medina. [4 notes: JISHUMI(الضحاك، الواقدي); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; QUTB-ZILAL+]
- 107:6/q15/p3 The first section is Meccan and the section from 'woe to those who pray' onward is Medinan. — Al-Jishumī and al-Burhān attribute this to Muqātil; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran report a split view. Sayyid Quṭb allows later verses appended for thematic continuity, while Asad disputes the Medinan ending. [5 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); BURHAN(مقاتل بن سليمان); QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES(some commentators)-]

## 107:6/q16 About whom were the last three verses reportedly revealed?  [* · reports]

[2 notes: IBNASHUR(هبة الله بن سلامة); IBNASHUR-FULL(هبة الله بن سلامة)]

## 107:6/q17 What reports discuss reciting this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 107:6/q17/p1 A report says recitation brings God's forgiveness to a person who pays zakāt. — Al-Jishumī transmits this through Ubayy and supplies no grading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(النبي)]

## 107:6/q18 How is the sūra's composition used to relate this warning to later events?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Al-Biqāʿī uses either the sūra's twenty-five words or those words plus the basmala to generate different dates.
- 107:6/q18/p1 Its twenty-five words point to year twelve AH and Abu Bakr's defeat of apostates and zakāt-refusers, exposing earlier worship as show. — Al-Biqāʿī counts from the start of prophethood and names Banū Ḥanīfa at Yamāma, al-Nujayr in Kinda and al-Aswad al-ʿAnsī in Ṣanʿāʾ as defeated; he says the woe befell showy worshippers invited to pray by their Prophet who turned away. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:6/q18/p2 The twenty-five words plus the four-word basmala point to year sixteen AH and the clearing of polytheists, Christians and Magians from the peninsula. — Al-Biqāʿī says most converted and others departed to Byzantine lands; he rejects pronoun or letter counts as unnecessary once religion was manifest. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 107:6/q19 Does the verse end after يُرَآءُونَ?  [يُرَآءُونَ · sciences]
- 107:6/q19/p1 In Damascus, Mecca and Medina counts it does not end here; verses 6–7 can form one unit and yield two symmetrical triads. — Corpus Coranicum cites Neuwirth's formal comparison with the short woe and longer vice catalogue in 83:1–3; it acknowledges the rhyme potential at yurāʾūn. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 107:6/q20 What inward reading is given to showing off?  [يُرَآءُونَ · inward]
- 107:6/q20/p1 The person is veiled from God by creatures or attributes deeds and spiritual states to the dark ego. — Al-Qāshānī describes the creaturely veil; Bursawī gives the ego attribution. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 107:6/q21 How does the sūra's opening frame the later warning?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the later vices intensify the opening rebuke or describe a separately appended but joined passage.
- 107:6/q21/p1 After recounting favors to Quraysh, the sūra marvels at their denial despite security and provision. — Al-Jishumī relates the opening to their denial of the Prophet and his prophethood. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 107:6/q21/p2 The later neglect, ostentation and withholding make religious pretenders more blameworthy than open deniers who mistreat the needy. — Elmalılı contrasts the groups' conduct. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 107:6/q21/p3 Even where final verses are later revelation, their wording is shaped to fit the earlier sūra. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the consequential fāʾ as a clue to such a joining. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 107:6/q22 Which act do the cited authorities say they show off through?  [يُرَآءُونَ · interpretation]
- 107:6/q22/p1 They show off through their prayers. — ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib gives this explanation in reports preserved by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Suyūṭī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī.
