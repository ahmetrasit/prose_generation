# 53:40: 24 questions

## 53:40/q01 What does سعيه mean?  [سَعْيَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether striving names outward work or its inward intention.
- 53:40/q01/p1 A person's work, deeds, or effort. — Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba gloss saʿy as work or deed. Bursevī links the sense to 92:4, where efforts differ, and says these teachings are in the scrolls. [3 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; BURSEVI]
- 53:40/q01/p2 A person's intention. — Al-Warrāq cites the report that people are raised according to their intentions. [1 notes: THALABI(أبو بكر الوراق)]

## 53:40/q02 How does سعيه clarify the preceding ما in 53:39?  [سَعْيَهُۥ · links]
- 53:40/q02/p1 It supports reading ما as a verbal noun denoting the work accomplished. — Rāzī prefers this reading; a verbal noun can denote its object, just as خلق الله can mean God's created thing. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 53:40/q03 How is وأن سعيه connected with the preceding scriptural statements?  [وَأَنَّ سَعْيَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the coordination keeps verse 40 inside the earlier scriptures or introduces a further statement.
- 53:40/q03/p1 It coordinates with the earlier أن and remains among the teachings in the scriptures. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya traces the coordination to the understood أن in verse 38; he says the majority read fatḥa while Abū al-Sammāl reads kasra from verse 42 onward. Al-Samīn allows genitive, nominative, or accusative case for both وأن clauses. Ibn ʿĀshūr says emphatic أن fits this consequence of a person's possessing only his own effort. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:40/q03/p2 It begins Qur'anic commentary on the earlier scriptural statement rather than remaining within it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr alternatively coordinates it with relative ما under the preposition bi in verses 36–37: the people were not told this truth. Islāhī says extensions grounded in the original statement begin at verse 40 and turn the discourse toward Quraysh. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:40/q04 Does أن require an omitted pronoun of شأن here?  [وَأَنَّ سَعْيَهُۥ · grammar]
- 53:40/q04/p1 No; سعيه itself serves as the noun of أن. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this also removes any need to reduce أن. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:40/q05 What time does سوف mark?  [سَوْفَ · grammar]
- 53:40/q05/p1 It marks a future event, usually understood as distant. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the usual force of سوف. Elmalılı says the effort's fruit need not appear immediately, yet the effort is not lost. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 53:40/q06 How is يُرى read and grammatically construed?  [يُرَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Whether an active verb can take an implied object pronoun without leaving أن improperly construed.
- 53:40/q06/p1 The passive reading means that effort is shown or seen. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī derive it from 'I showed him the thing'; al-Ālūsī also gives that derivation. Al-Samīn prefers visual perception on the passive because interpreting it as knowing needs an omitted second object. Al-Zajjāj allows an active construction but prefers this reading. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 53:40/q06/p2 An active reading is permissible with an implied object pronoun referring to effort. — Abū Isḥāq, al-Khalīl, Sībawayh and associates, Muḥammad ibn Yazīd, and al-Zajjāj permit it as سوف يراه. Al-Mubarrid says أن necessitates the omitted pronoun because the clause would otherwise have two governors; the Basran analogy is إن زيدا ضربت. Al-Samīn says only أن governs سعيه, while يرى governs the pronoun, so this is not double government by تنازع or اشتغال; he rejects calling the pronoun far-fetched. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,الخليل,محمد بن يزيد); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج,المبرد); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 53:40/q06/p3 The active reading is forbidden or weak. — The Kufans say أن and يرى would both govern سعيه. Abū al-Baqāʾ calls the active fatḥ reading anomalous and weak because it leaves the noun of أن without a predicate. Al-Zajjāj calls analogous إن زيدا سوف أكرم weak unless a pronoun is supplied or the construction changed to إن زيدا سأكرم. Al-Samīn disputes the alleged double government and the objection to an implied pronoun. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الكوفيون); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق)-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 53:40/q07 What does it mean that effort will be seen?  [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether seeing denotes direct presentation of deeds, knowledge of them, their recompense, or a visible embodiment.
- 53:40/q07/p1 The deeds or effort themselves will be displayed and witnessed at the Resurrection. — Muqātil says worldly deeds appear in the Hereafter. Al-Zajjāj says God already knows them, but the servant sees them on his balance. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Wāḥidī include good and bad deeds; al-Baghawī and al-Nasafī specify the scale. Al-Burṣawī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı add the record. Rāzī says disclosure brings joy to believers and grief to unbelievers. Ibn Kathīr compares 9:105; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites 3:30 and 99:6–8 for deeds made present. [23 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:40/q07/p2 The person's effort will be made known or understood. — Ibn Qutayba glosses 'will be seen' as 'will be known'; al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī transmit him. Asad says God makes people understand their life deeds. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes seeing as a metaphor for certain, widespread public knowledge and compares 7:49. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 53:40/q07/p3 What is seen is the effort's recompense. — Al-Jishumī transmits this as a second view of Judgment Day; al-Qurṭubī says God shows a person the recompense. Al-Ṭabrisī explains seeing the deeds as being repaid for them. Rāzī calls a figurative reading in which one sees the reward of service before a king remote: verse 41 separately promises full recompense. [3 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL-]
- 53:40/q07/p4 A past deed may itself return in a visible, even beautiful, form. — Rāzī reports a righteous deed shown in beautiful form and argues that every existent can be seen and God can restore what ceased to exist. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows embodied visible deeds or identification by signs, citing 18:49, the believers' light in 66:8, and a banner assigned to each traitor. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:40/q07/p5 God may be the one who sees the effort. — Rāzī cites 9:105 for this alternative. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:40/q08 By whom will the striving be seen?  [يُرَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the passive points chiefly to the doer, other witnesses, or intentionally leaves the observer open.
- 53:40/q08/p1 The person who performed it will see it. — Al-Zajjāj places it in the servant's balance; al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Asad, and Study Quran likewise emphasize the person's sight or understanding. Al-Ṭabrisī says the servant sees and receives full repayment. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:40/q08/p2 God and other witnesses, as well as or besides the performer, will see it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names God and those present; Rāzī allows God as viewer and compares 9:105. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the passive suggests someone besides the performer. Study Quran links 9:105, where God, the Messenger, and believers see deeds. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:40/q08/p3 The passive leaves the viewer unspecified. — Al-Biqāʿī says being seen itself brings pleasure or shame regardless of who sees it. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 53:40/q09 How are efforts displayed or identified at reckoning?  [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the display is through scales, written records, visible forms or signs, or public disclosure.
- 53:40/q09/p1 They are visible on the balance. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī reporting al-Zajjāj, al-Nasafī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı locate the deeds on the scales. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 53:40/q09/p2 They are seen written in the person's record. — Al-Jishumī reports this view; Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı mention record and scale together. [4 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 53:40/q09/p3 They may appear as embodied deeds or as identifying signs. — Rāzī reports righteous deeds in beautiful form and says God can restore what has ceased. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 18:49 and 66:8, the light going ahead of believers, and the Prophetic report of a banner for every traitor. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي)]
- 53:40/q09/p4 Their truth may be established through public knowledge. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the exchange in 7:49 and calls seeing a metaphor for certain, widespread knowledge. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:40/q10 Why is the viewer left unnamed by the passive verb?  [يُرَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the passive chiefly universalizes the effect of exposure or points to additional observers.
- 53:40/q10/p1 The fact of being seen itself produces joy or shame whoever sees. — Al-Biqāʿī explicitly links the unspecified viewer to pleasure for good effort and shame for evil effort. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:40/q10/p2 The passive implies an observer besides the person who performed the deed. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ draws this inference from the passive form. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 53:40/q11 What is the effect of showing people's deeds?  [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · interpretation]
- 53:40/q11/p1 It honors and gladdens the good, and rebukes, grieves, or shames wrongdoers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says public presentation honors good people and rebukes wrongdoers; Rāzī says believers rejoice, perhaps before everyone, while unbelievers grieve. Al-Biqāʿī says being seen itself gives pleasure or shame. Al-Ālūsī calls it glad tidings and rebuke. Ibn ʿĀshūr speaks of good reputation or public disgrace. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:40/q12 How does seeing effort relate to its full recompense in the next verse?  [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · links]
turns on: Whether 'seen' already means reward or precedes the distinct recompense of verse 41.
- 53:40/q12/p1 Display comes first; full repayment follows. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Ṭabrisī make the sequence explicit. Al-Naḥḥās, Ibn Kathīr, Islāhī, Kur'an Yolu, and Study Quran couple presentation with just recompense. Al-Ṭabrisī takes the following pronoun as referring to effort: the effort itself is repaid, with lasting reward for obedience. Rāzī says the next verse's separate mention of repayment argues against taking seeing as reward. [10 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:40/q12/p2 Seeing can itself mean receiving the recompense. — Al-Jishumī reports that what appears on Judgment Day is reward; al-Qurṭubī says God shows the person's recompense. Rāzī rejects this as remote because verse 41 separately addresses recompense. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 53:40/q13 What is the referent and syntax of the repayment pronoun in verse 41?  [سَعْيَهُۥ · links]
- 53:40/q13/p1 It refers back to effort, which is repaid in full. — Al-Ṭabrisī assigns the pronoun to effort. Rāzī reports a parsing in which جزى takes two objects, or three with a preposition that can be omitted; he cites 76:12. [2 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL]

## 53:40/q14 Why and how may the fullest reward be delayed?  [سَوْفَ · links]
turns on: Whether the future wording points to postponed completion of recompense or an added supreme reward.
- 53:40/q14/p1 Full reward comes later, although reward can start at death. — Rāzī says 'then' may indicate delay in recompense or simply advance the discourse. He answers the concern about a righteous person's wait: reward starts at death and reaches completion in Paradise. Elmalılı likewise says effort's fruit need not be immediate and will not be lost. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 53:40/q14/p2 The 'most complete' reward may be an added reward beyond Paradise: seeing God. — Rāzī prefers this reading because an unrestricted superlative calls for what exceeds every otherwise complete reward; he compares 10:26. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 53:40/q15 What does this promise teach about deeds and accountability?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is comprehensive judgment or the worth of honest personal effort.
- 53:40/q15/p1 Every good and bad deed is accounted for and justly repaid, however small. — Al-Naḥḥās says both kinds of deeds are seen and repaid; Ibn Kathīr specifies good for good and evil for evil. Islāhī says even tiny good deeds count and no small evil is ignored; Kur'an Yolu says every freely chosen deed is brought forward and judged with complete justice. Study Quran says deeds testify to a person's life. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:40/q15/p2 Honest effort and earning by one's own labor have value before God. — Kur'an Yolu draws this teaching from the verses. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:40/q16 How do the neighboring statements address the fears and hopes of righteous people and sinners?  [* · links]
- 53:40/q16/p1 The wording leaves sinners hope of forgiveness and reassures the righteous about what they earn. — Rāzī says bearing no other's burden does not exclude God's forgiving one's own burden. For the righteous, 'only what he strives for' affirms receipt of what is earned; phrasing it as 'not what he did not strive for' would not. This balance expresses mercy preceding wrath. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:40/q17 What inward lesson is drawn from a person seeing their striving?  [سَعْيَهُۥ يُرَىٰ · inward]
turns on: Whether seeing effort exposes dependence on grace, removes self-credit, or discloses stages of the inward path.
- 53:40/q17/p1 One sees one's striving is unfit for God and survives only through divine grace. — Tustarī and Sahl say the servant learns what the striving merits; without the Lord's grace, it would perish or cause the servant to perish. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); BURSEVI(سهل)]
- 53:40/q17/p2 Striving is not a means of earning reward. — Al-Wāsiṭī says this of the Prophet's striving in al-Sulamī, and Bursevī reports the principle without that limitation. [2 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); BURSEVI(الواسطي)]
- 53:40/q17/p3 Recognition of a work's defects makes one dislike credit for it. — Abū Ḥafṣ says one disowns attribution of deficient work to oneself and dislikes being recalled for it. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص)]
- 53:40/q17/p4 The path of striving moves through beginning, middle, and end, with distinct gifts at the end. — Al-Warrāq says annihilation is rewarded with witnessing and subsistence with bodily nurture, including lawful provisions deferred on the path. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الوراق)]
- 53:40/q17/p5 Striving has four inward levels, each with its own result. — Bursevī describes the ego's purification from violations and natural conformity, yielding gardens; the heart's removal of darkness and veils, yielding abandonment of worldly desire, pleasure, adornment, wealth, and rank; the secret's polishing through divine attributes, yielding their manifestations; and the spirit's essential theophanies, yielding extinction of ego and subsistence in oneness. [4 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:40/q18 What report illustrates the public exposure of wrongdoing?  [يُرَىٰ · reports]
- 53:40/q18/p1 God will expose before creation one who publicizes something his brother dislikes. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr both transmit the Prophetic report; it illustrates the disgrace of a public display. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي)]

## 53:40/q19 What report supplies a visible sign of betrayal at Judgment Day?  [يُرَىٰ · reports]
- 53:40/q19/p1 A banner will be raised for each traitor, identifying the betrayal. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the Prophetic hadith as an example of efforts identified by signs. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي)]

## 53:40/q20 What is reported of the Prophet when he recited these verses?  [* · reports]
- 53:40/q20/p1 He recited the istirjāʿ formula and became subdued. — Al-Durr transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 53:40/q21 What incident is reported as the setting for this passage?  [* · reports]
- 53:40/q21/p1 A report links it to ʿUthmān stopping his donations after his foster brother promised to bear his sins. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this from Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, al-Kalbī, and other exegetes. ʿAbd Allāh ibn Abī Sarḥ received a camel; the account says ʿUthmān left his post at Uḥud, then resumed his former practice. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس والسدي والكلبي وجماعة من المفسرين)]

## 53:40/q22 How does this passage fit the sūra's movement toward earlier scripture?  [* · links]
- 53:40/q22/p1 The passage turns from reflection on creation to enduring teachings in the scriptures of Moses and Abraham. — Study Quran describes the third section's transition and its perennial truths. Islāhī says reliance on descent from those prophets for Paradise is condemned and attention goes to their teachings and sacrifices. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:40/q23 What do the cited literary examples show about figurative images?  [* · rhetoric]
- 53:40/q23/p1 A single image can support several different comparisons. — Al-Jurjānī says a sparking flint can suggest generosity, sharp intelligence, success, or reaching an aim; failure to spark can suggest miserliness, dullness, or fruitless effort. The moon can suggest fame, distinction, honor, elevation, completion, and decline. He cites Abū Tammām's growing noble child, al-Buḥturī's rising honor, an unnamed poet's life cycle, Ibn Bābak's vizier's partial royal authority after Fakhr al-Dawla appointed Abū ʿAlī, and Abū Bakr al-Khwārizmī's wealthy versus poor host. [8 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 53:40/q24 What determines whether a word sounds apt in a literary context?  [* · rhetoric]
- 53:40/q24/p1 A word's merit comes from its fit with neighboring words, not from its isolated sound or meaning. — Al-Jurjānī says أخدع works in al-Ḥamāsa and al-Buḥturī but burdens a line by Abū Tammām; شيء works in ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa and Abū Ḥayya but weakens al-Mutanabbī's line about the revolving heavens. If merit inhered in a word alone, its effect would not change between settings. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
