# 90:7: 12 questions

## 90:7/q01 How is أَيَحْسَبُ read?  [أَيَحْسَبُ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowel of the sīn in the two occurrences.
- 90:7/q01/p1 The sīn has kasra. — A man of Banū ʿĀmir reports hearing the Prophet recite it so (Thaʿlabī); QIRAAT-ER calls this the canonical imperfect stem vowel and says the root and sense are retained. [2 notes: THALABI(رجل من بني عامر عن أبيه); QIRAAT-ER]
- 90:7/q01/p2 The sīn has fatḥa. — The man of Banū ʿĀmir who prayed behind the Prophet reports both paired questions with yaḥsab opened (al-Durr). [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رجل من بني عامر)]
- 90:7/q01/p3 The sīn has ḍamma. — Al-Qurṭubī reports a Prophetic reading with ḍamma at both occurrences. [1 notes: QURTUBI(النبي)]

## 90:7/q02 Why use أَن لَّمْ and the past negation?  [أَن لَّمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether lam points to witnessed past action or serves as a certain future negation in the alternative 'find' interpretation.
- 90:7/q02/p1 The past negation matches his earlier boast and covers deeds already done. — Al-Tibyān says lam suits the past-tense claim that he spent and destroyed wealth; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts past surveillance with the preceding lan about future power and says past wording also suits senses already given to him. Al-Nashr notes that open an lam is written as separate words throughout the Qurʾān, including here. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(التبيان); BINTSHATI; NASHR]
- 90:7/q02/p2 The past-form negation can express certainty about a future encounter. — Al-Ālūsī reports the interpretation that lam has the force of lan: God will find the man on Judgment Day and reckon with him. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 90:7/q03 How is يَرَهُ pronounced and formed?  [يَرَهُۥٓ · readings]
turns on: Different transmitted treatments of the hamza, rāʾ, and final hāʾ.
- 90:7/q03/p1 The hamza of underlying yarʾahu is omitted after its vowel moves to the rāʾ. — Al-Naḥḥās explains that the hamza then becomes quiescent beside another quiescent consonant. ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān adds that the rāʾ's new vowel is incidental, so the quiescent hamza is deleted before the following consonant. [2 notes: NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان)]
- 90:7/q03/p2 The rāʾ is read quiescent. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to al-Ḥasan and his full text to al-Aʿmash; both explain it as avoidance of successive vowel movements. The attributions differ. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش)]
- 90:7/q03/p3 The final hāʾ is read quiescent. — Ibn Jinnī reports al-Aʿmash and refers to his earlier discussion of this hāʾ. Ibn Mujāhid says al-Kisāʾī alone transmitted this from Ḥamza and no other transmitter did. Al-Nashr places an lam yarahu among exceptions to regular letter rules, with twelve letters in twenty places. [3 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الأعمش); IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي عن حمزة); NASHR]

## 90:7/q04 Why can an imperfect verb portray a past act?  [يَرَهُۥٓ · rhetoric]
- 90:7/q04/p1 An imperfect makes an important past event vivid as though it unfolds before listeners. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī illustrates the Arabic usage with a Companion's account of killing Abū Rāfiʿ, where fa-aḍribuhu uses the imperfect for the central blow. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(الصحابي الذي قتل أبا رافع)]

## 90:7/q05 What can أَحَدٌ mean as a word?  [أَحَدٌ · meaning]
- 90:7/q05/p1 Aḥad has the sense of wāḥid, one, and may designate God. — Ibn al-Jawzī says aḥad and wāḥid begin the number sequence; he cites Ibn Fāris equating them and deriving 'became isolated' from istaḥada. Al-Dāmghānī says aḥad can designate God; Ibn al-Jawzī also cites this Qurʾānic application and these neighboring al-Balad questions. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,بعض المفسرين); DAMGHANI]

## 90:7/q06 Who is the one thought not to see him?  [أَحَدٌ · referent]
turns on: Whether aḥad points to God, other human observers including Muḥammad, or the recording angels.
- 90:7/q06/p1 God is the witness. — Mujāhid, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ, Muqātil, al-Suddī, al-Kalbī, and other early authorities are cited for God seeing the person or his spending. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports al-Kashshāf's allowance that the watcher is God. The source accounts vary between deeds, amount spent, and inner motive. [22 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس,مقاتل); BAGHAWI(الكلبي,سعيد بن جبير وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي,سعيد بن جبير وقتادة); IBNKATHIR(مجاهد وغيره من السلف); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(السدي,الضحاك); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف); MUQATIL; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 90:7/q06/p2 Other people can witness his expenditure or discern that his boast is false. — Ibn Shajara says no one among people saw his spending; Ibn ʿĀshūr says some people can recognize the lie and cites Zuhayr's line that character becomes known despite attempts to hide it. [6 notes: MAWARDI(ابن شجرة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(زهير)]
- 90:7/q06/p3 Recording angels watch and count his deeds. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the generic reading of al-insān under which angels watch and count deeds until Judgment; Abū Ḥayyān says watchers record and preserve deeds throughout life. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN]
- 90:7/q06/p4 Muḥammad may know what he spent. — Al-Thaʿlabī offers this alongside God as the referent; the boastful man had not spent all he claimed. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 90:7/q07 Who is the person addressed by the pronoun?  [يَرَهُۥٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the human is generic or identified with one of several Quraysh figures; the al-Ḥārith report supplies an expiation occasion.
- 90:7/q07/p1 It concerns a human being generally, especially the boastful disbeliever. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a generic reading of al-insān. Al-Kashshāf's full text calls him a Quraysh notable who disbelieves in Resurrection and mistreats believers; Abū Ḥayyān also reports an unnamed Quraysh magnate. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports al-Kashshāf's grammatical possibility that the pronoun refers to the human being. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف)]
- 90:7/q07/p2 He is Abū al-Ashadd, also named Abū al-Asad Usayd ibn Kilda. — Al-Kashshāf describes a strong man who challenged others to remove him from a stretched hide, leaving only footprints; Abū Ḥayyān calls him Abū al-Asad Usayd ibn Kilda, places him on an ʿUkāẓ leather mat, and says he could be moved only in pieces. Names and physical details vary. [2 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN(قيل)]
- 90:7/q07/p3 He is al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān report this identification. [2 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN(قيل)]
- 90:7/q07/p4 He is al-Ḥārith ibn ʿĀmir ibn Nawfal. — Muqātil as reported by al-Thaʿlabī says the passage was revealed about him after expiations for a sin; he complained that conversion cost him his wealth in expiations and expenses. Abū Ḥayyān says he sought the Prophet's expiation rulings and boasted of vast sums spent on liabilities. [2 notes: THALABI(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN(قيل)]

## 90:7/q08 What does the question say about his claim to have spent wealth?  [أَيَحْسَبُ أَن لَّمْ يَرَهُۥٓ أَحَدٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the boast is wholly or partly false, or reflects real but morally flawed expenditure; some explanations allow both.
- 90:7/q08/p1 His boast is false: he spent nothing, or less than he claimed, and observers know the truth. — Al-Farrāʾ says he claimed spending in opposition to Muḥammad but lied; al-Kalbī's versions say either nothing was given or the claimed amount was overstated. God sees the amount or lack of spending according to al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, and others. Ibn ʿĀshūr says some people recognize the lie; Zuhayr's verse says hidden character becomes known. Elmalılı prefers the broader warning reading because it can include this lie. [27 notes: FARRA; TAB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; BAGHAWI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي); ELMALILI(Kelbî); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(زهير); TABRISI(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي، الفراء)+]
- 90:7/q08/p2 He really spent, but did so for display, hostility, waste, or personal obligations; God saw his expenditure and motive. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī emphasize ostentation and reputation; al-Farrāʾ and al-Ālūsī allow hostility to the Prophet; al-Qāshānī and Bursawī say corrupt intention turns seeming virtue into vice. Elmalılı warns of punishment and regret for wasteful display or enmity. Iṣlāḥī says claimants rehearse personal and collective expenses to excuse miserliness; they should have thanked God and aided the needy. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿAntara's boast of spending while preserving honor as an example of pre-Islamic valuation. God knows both spending and its purpose. [21 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ELMALILI+]
- 90:7/q08/p3 God saw and counted what was actually spent and will replace it. — Muqātil explicitly adds replacement for the spender, beyond the bare statement that God knows the sum. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 90:7/q09 What mistaken assumption does the rebuking question expose?  [أَيَحْسَبُ أَن لَّمْ يَرَهُۥٓ أَحَدٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the denial concerns observation, public exposure and judgment, God's power over him, or resurrection itself; Māwardī distinguishes a threat from refuting denial.
- 90:7/q09/p1 He assumes he escapes God's sight and knowledge of acts and intentions. — Al-Biqāʿī extends the point to private and public conduct and hidden desires; Sayyid Quṭb and al-Sāmarrāʾī include spending motives. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says seeing is more specific than knowing and covers details of all creatures' actions; the paired questions invoke God's power and knowledge. [11 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; DURR-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس,مقاتل)]
- 90:7/q09/p2 He assumes his action will never be exposed, judged, or punished. — Māwardī calls this a threatening reading, as against the earlier readings that refute a denial. Al-Ḥasan says the man wants to reckon his own spending and asks who can hold him to account, while God can reckon with him. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Ṭabarī connect sight to divine reckoning. [13 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); ELMALILI+]
- 90:7/q09/p3 He thinks no one can overpower him. — Mujāhid is reported with this explanation in Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr, connecting this wording with the paired challenge about divine power. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 90:7/q09/p4 He supposes there is no Resurrection or later retribution. — Al-Kashshāf's full text describes the Quraysh notable's confidence despite his strength and mistreatment of believers; al-Sāmarrāʾī states the assumption that God cannot punish or requite him. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 90:7/q10 What account of wealth and life is expected at Judgment?  [أَيَحْسَبُ أَن لَّمْ يَرَهُۥٓ أَحَدٌ · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in the listed subjects of questioning and whether they add body, knowledge, family love, or ostentatious giving.
- 90:7/q10/p1 People will be asked how they earned wealth and where they spent it. — Qatāda is repeatedly cited, sometimes adding that God already saw the person. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr is paired with him by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī; al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Study Quran transmit the same point. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI(سعيد بن جبير وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وقتادة); RAZI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 90:7/q10/p2 A servant's feet will not move until questioned about life, body, and wealth's source and use. — Abū Dharr's version lists lifespan, body, earnings and where wealth was spent; Ibn ʿAṭiyya's Prophetic report similarly includes life, body, and wealth. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(أبو ذر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 90:7/q10/p3 A servant's feet will not move until questioned about life, wealth, knowledge and its practice. — The versions in al-Ālūsī and Study Quran list lifespan, wealth's source and spending, and knowledge and how it was used; al-Ālūsī's shorter citation calls the hadith ungraded. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(النبي); STUDYQURAN(Hadith)]
- 90:7/q10/p4 The questioning includes love for the Prophet's family as well as life, wealth, and knowledge or deeds. — Al-Thaʿlabī's report via Ibn ʿAbbās includes life, wealth, knowledge practiced, and love for the Prophet's family; Ibn Khurja says he heard it only from al-Haytham. Al-Ṭabrisī lists lifespan, wealth, deeds and family love. Study Quran notes a Shiite version adds the family-love question. [3 notes: THALABI(رسول الله); TABRISI(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Shiite version)]
- 90:7/q10/p5 A claimant to charity and zakat can be condemned for giving to gain a generous reputation. — Abū Hurayra's report says the servant claims to have spent and given zakat but is told he sought the name 'generous' and is sent to the Fire. [1 notes: QURTUBI(أبو هريرة)]

## 90:7/q11 How does the wording of the question expose the boast?  [أَيَحْسَبُ أَن لَّمْ يَرَهُۥٓ أَحَدٌ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the cited rhetoric explains the direct interrogative after the boast or the broader imagined description in its context.
- 90:7/q11/p1 It elaborates the earlier spending claim and rebukes confidence that the lie will pass unnoticed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the question an explicative supplement to 'I spent much wealth'; some people recognize a false boast. He cites Zuhayr on hidden character becoming known and says the interrogative implies divine knowledge of the boaster's inner state. His fuller text calls the claim a supposed pre-Islamic virtue and cites ʿAntara's poem on spending while preserving honor; he describes it as circumstantial to human hardship. [7 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(زهير)]
- 90:7/q11/p2 The question speaks as if an imagined, unknown description were already familiar. — Al-Jurjānī says a description of a man whose property is shared by nearly everyone does not report a known generous man or compare a gradable trait. It makes the listener imagine a precisely matching case; this mode constructs the unknown as familiar, often introduced by the relative alladhī. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 90:7/q12 How do surrounding verses and the sūra frame this question?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes make different links: divine faculties and judgment, structural parallels and self-sufficiency, or the spending claim against the sūra's difficult moral path.
- 90:7/q12/p1 The paired questions and the ensuing gifts establish God's power to see, judge, and requite. — Al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī say eyes, tongue and lips or the two eyes recall divine favor and prove power to account and resurrect. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 67:14: the creator of perception and speech knows and judges acts. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the eyes directly answer the assumption of being unseen; al-Tibyān says the creator who gave strength is more powerful and knows deeds. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ pairs future power and past surveillance across the human's full course and invokes 96:6–8 on arrogance, creation and return. Elmalılı also links gifts to power, knowledge and divergent souls. [11 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(التبيان); BINTSHATI; ELMALILI]
- 90:7/q12/p2 The repeated questions bind the stanza and challenge imagined independence from God. — Corpus Coranicum says the second stanza resumes the introduction's human theme in two rhymed triplets, with repeated third-person polemical questions and matching openings and rhyme. It compares the self-sufficiency theme with 104:1–3, 80:5, 92:8 and 96:7 and the questioning with 75:3 and 75:36. Al-Biqāʿī treats concealment as a fourth carnal desire and says unfulfilled desires drive hardship; Iṣlāḥī describes conceit about self-earned status that breeds indifference to God and the Hereafter. [8 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; BIQAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 90:7/q12/p3 The spending boast is set against the harder moral paths and service to others. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says spending alone falls short of difficult servitude and solidarity with believers. Iṣlāḥī says the boasters should thank God and help the needy, while their accounts of personal and collective expenses excuse miserliness. He glosses mālan lubadā as much wealth. Al-Thaʿlabī reports three vocalizations of lubadan: Abū Jaʿfar doubles bāʾ as a collective of labid and rākiʿ; Mujāhid rounds both lām and bāʾ with light bāʾ, with heavy-matter and reclining-man usages; others have ḍamma on lām and fatḥa on light bāʾ as plural of heap or a non-derived singular. Al-Sāmarrāʾī calls najd a raised hard path matching the sūra's hardship, unlike easy sabīl; most commentators call the two paths good and evil, and a Prophetic report cautions against preferring evil, which he judges closer. Guidance shows the paths without guaranteeing the good destination, as 22:4 illustrates. [11 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; THALABI(أبو جعفر,مجاهد); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(أغلب المفسرين,الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
