# 96:14/q07 What warning or lesson does God's seeing convey? [أَلَمْ يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize requital, the immediate rebuke for forfeited guidance, the denial of practical knowledge, or vigilance.
- 96:14/q07/p1 God observes the wrongdoing and will punish or fully requite it; this deters sin and encourages obedience.
  reasons: Al-Tabari, al-Baghawi and others address Abu Jahl's forbidden prayer. Al-Razi includes resurrection and atom-small acts that escape no divine knowledge; al-Tabrisi says obedience and avoidance of sin follow. Ibn Ashur calls seeing a metonym for punishment. Al-Biqaʿi pairs punishment of misguiders with aid to the obedient.

# 96:14/q08 How broadly does the warning apply to forbidding worship? [* · law]
turns on: Whether a restriction targets worship itself or a separate wrong or protected interest, and how far the specific occasion generalizes.
- 96:14/q08/p1 It applies beyond Abu Jahl to anyone who obstructs prayer or other obedience, including distraction from worship.
  reasons: Al-Razi extends the threat to everyone preventing obedience; al-Alusi extends it to verbal or practical prevention of other worship. Bint al-Shati says a specific occasion does not confine general wording. Kur'an Yolu prefers a universal application to tyrants obstructing religion's goodness, truth and justice.
  holders (9 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+
  notes: RAZI:96:14/r2 RAZI-FULL:v32p22#3/r2 ALUSI:96:14/r4 ALUSI:96:14/r39 ALUSI-FULL:v30p186/r4 ALUSI-FULL:v30p186/r7 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r3 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:9/r2
- 96:14/q08/p2 Preventing prayer in a usurped place or disliked time is outside this warning because the objection is to an accompanying wrong.
  reasons: Al-Razi and al-Alusi distinguish forbidding the transgression from forbidding prayer itself.
  holders (4 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL

- 96:14/q08/p3 A master or husband may limit voluntary worship for a protected interest, with God's permission.
  reasons: Al-Razi gives a slave's night prayer or voluntary fast and a wife's retreat as examples; the aim is welfare, not hostility to worship.

  reasons: Al-Alusi reports Ali's fear of this threat when people prayed before Eid: he stated what he saw the Prophet do. Abu Hanifa likewise answered Abu Yusuf with the proper formula and prostration rather than an explicit ban.

# 96:14/q09 What happened when the opponent tried to stop the Prophet praying? [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted accounts differ on names, manner of prevention and the prayer scene; chronology is used to test the Abu Talib story.
- 96:14/q09/p1 Abu Jahl threatened to trample the Prophet but recoiled from a trench of fire, terror and wings.
  reasons: Abu Hurayra's report includes his oath by al-Lat and al-Uzza; the Prophet said angels would seize him limb by limb. Al-Baghawi's version is uncertain whether the revelation clause belongs to Abu Hurayra's narration or elsewhere; al-Alusi says the passage through the sura was then revealed.
  holders (2 notes): BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI(أبو هريرة)
  notes: BAGHAWI-FULL:v8p479#2/r11 ALUSI:96:14/r2
- 96:14/q09/p2 Abu Jahl came on the Prophet praying and forbade him; he also verbally repeated his prohibition.
  reasons: Ibn Abbas ties the incident to the passage through the lying forelock. Al-Alusi says al-Tirmidhi authenticated the verbal warning, also transmitted by Ahmad and others; Qatada says the passage concerned the threatened trampling.
  holders (3 notes): SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI(ابن عباس)
  notes: SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p232#2/r3 DURR-FULL:v8p565/r3 ALUSI:96:14/r10
- 96:14/q09/p3 The forbider was Umayya ibn Khalaf, who stopped Salman from praying.
  reasons: Al-Hasan supplies the names; al-Alusi deems it unlikely because Salman converted in Medina while the sura is Meccan, though he says the general warning would cover Umayya if true.
  holders (1 notes): KASHSHAF(الحسن); ALUSI(الحسن)-
  notes: KASHSHAF:96:14/r6 | against: ALUSI:96:14/r3
- 96:14/q09/p4 The prayer was Zuhr, also reported as the first congregational prayer with the Prophet, Abu Bakr and Ali.
  reasons: Al-Alusi attributes these scene details to Abu Hayyan.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI(أبو حيان)
  notes: ALUSI:96:14/r5
- 96:14/q09/p5 Abu Talib brought Ja'far to pray beside the Prophet and praised and protected him.
  reasons: Al-Alusi reports Abu Hayyan's story, then questions it: the Mi'raj and obligatory prayer are dated after Abu Talib's death by Ibn Hazm, Ibn Faris and al-Suddi, at one year, fifteen months and seventeen months before Hijra respectively. A competing al-Zuhri chronology puts the Mi'raj five years after the mission, favored by Nawawi and Qurtubi, with objections noted.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-

- 96:14/q09/p6 The forbidding may have been physical prevention, revealing the tyrant's limited power.
  reasons: Al-Alusi reports this explanation of the wording.
