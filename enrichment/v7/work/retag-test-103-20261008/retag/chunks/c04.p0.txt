# Notes

## 11:114
[MUJAHID:v1p308#5/r1] verses 11:114 | Mujahid says the evening portion of night is an hour at the night prayer, al-isha. «عن مجاهد ﴿ وزلفا من الليل ﴾ قال ساعة من الليل صلاة العتمة»
[MUQATIL:11:114/r1] verses 11:114 | The prayers at the two ends of the day are dawn, al-ula, and afternoon; the night prayers are sunset and evening. «{ وأقم الصلاة } ، يعني وأتم الصلاة، يعني ركوعها وسجودها، { طرفي النهار } ، يعني صلاة الغداة، وصلاة الأولى، والعصر،»
[MUQATIL:11:114/r2] verses 11:114 | The five prayers erase sins provided major sins are avoided; this was revealed about Abu Miqbal, also named Amir b. Qays al-Ansari of Banu al-Najjar. «{ إن الحسنات } ، يعني الصلوات الخمس { يذهبن السيئات } ، يعني يكفرن الذنوب ما اجتنبت الكبائر، نزلت في أبى مقبل،»
[MUQATIL:11:114/r3] verses 11:114 | Abu Miqbal confessed to the Prophet that he had made advances to a woman but had not had intercourse; the obligatory prayer he prayed with the Prophet expiated it. «ثم عمد الرجل، فصلى المكتوبة وراء النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، فلما انصرف النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، فقال له»
[MUQATIL:11:114/r4] verses 11:114 | The verse's prayer reminder echoes God's command to Moses to establish prayer for remembrance in 20:14. «ثم قال { ذلك } الذي ذكره من الصلاة طرفي النهار وزلفى من الليل من الصلاة، { ذكرى للذاكرين }»
[NAHHAS:v2p306/r2] verses 11:114 | طرفي النهار is a time adverb; readings vary on زلفا; حسنات may mean prayers, while repentance removes sins. «نصب على الظرف وحذفت النون للإضافة وكسرت الياء لالتقاء الساكنين»
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r1] verses 11:114 | The two ends of day mean morning and evening: dawn prayer, and afternoon prayer after noon, which includes noon and afternoon; the night periods near day's end are sunset and night prayers. «﴿ وأقم الصلاة طرفي النهار ﴾ غدوة وعشية ﴿ وزلفا من الليل ﴾ وساعات من الليل جمع زلفة»
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r2] verses 11:114 | The accusative dual 'ends' functions adverbially because the words are annexed to times, as in phrases giving a whole or half day. «وانتصاب طرفى النهار على الظرف لانهما مضافان إلى الوقت»
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r3] verses 11:114 | The five prayers expiate sins committed between them; alternatively good deeds generally erase sins, as in the prophetic instruction to follow an evil deed with a good one. «أن الصلوات الخمس يذهبن الذنوب وفى الحديث أن الصلوات الخمس تكفر»
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r4] verses 11:114 | The Prophet also recommends saying 'Glory be to God, praise belongs to God, there is no god but God, and God is greatest.' «أو سبحان الله والحمد لله ولا إله إلا الله و الله أكبر»
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r5] verses 11:114 | That may refer to the instruction beginning 'remain steadfast' and what follows, or to the Qur'an as admonition for those who take heed. «إشارة إلى فاستقم فما بعده أو القرآن»
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r6] verses 11:114 | The verse was revealed about ʿAmr ibn Ghaziyya al-Anṣārī, a date seller who entered a woman's home and kissed her, then repented; the Prophet said his joining the afternoon prayer expiated it, and that the rule applied to everyone. «نزلت فى عمرو بن غزية الأنصارى بائع التمر قال لامرأة فى البيت»
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r7] verses 11:114 | The Prophet asks whether the man prayed the afternoon prayer with them, calls it an expiation, and says this applies to all people, not him alone. «قال عليه السلام هل شهدت معنا العصر قال نعم قال هى كفارة لك»
[QASHANI:11:114-115/r1] verses 11:114 | The five senses distract the heart with bodily forms and veil it from divine presence; five prayers close those sensory doors, open the heart to God, focus attention, and admit light. «لما كانت الحواس الخمس شواغل تشغل القلب بما يرد عليه من الهيئات الجسمانية»
[QASHANI:11:114-115/r2] verses 11:114 | Prayer's incoming light removes the darkness and residue of sensory distractions, meaning that good deeds remove evil deeds. «وهذا معنى قوله: { إن الحسنات يذهبن السيئات } وقد ورد»
[QASHANI:11:114-115/r3] verses 11:114 | A prophetic report says one prayer expiates what lies between it and the next, provided major sins are avoided. «إن الصلاة إلى الصلاة كفارة ما بينهما ما اجتنبت الكبائر»
[QASHANI:11:114-115/r4] verses 11:114 | Praying at both ends of the day lets gathered presence and light persist through other times and remove their fragmentation, sustaining continual prayer. «لينسحب حكمها ببقاء الجمعية واستيلاء الهيئة النورية في أوله إلى سائر الأوقات»
[QASHANI:11:114-115/r5] verses 11:114 | Natural powers dominate at night, drawing the soul to bodily management and sleep and away from spiritual perception; wakefulness and prayer refine and illuminate them. «لما كانت القوى الطبيعية المدبرة لأمر الغذاء سلطانها في الليل»
[QASHANI:11:114-115/r6] verses 11:114 | These prayer times and removal of sins remind those who remember their state of clarity, gathered attention, intimacy, and spiritual taste. «تذكير لمن يذكر حاله عند الحضور مع الله في الصفاء والجمعية»
[QIRAAT-ER:11:114:7#1/r1] verses 11:114 | The vowel shift from zulafan to zulufan changes the internal pattern of the plural without changing meaning. «Vowel shift: zulafan → zulufan (ḍamma on lām) — plural of zulfa with different internal vowel pattern»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r1] verses 11:114 | Most early interpreters identify the good deeds that erase sins as the five daily prayers. «ذهب جمهور المتأولين من الصحابة والتابعين رضي الله عنهم أجمعين إلى أن الحسنات هاهنا هي»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r10] verses 11:114 | On the view that a zulfah is the first hour after sunset, the night portion means the nightfall prayer, according to Ibn Abbas. «وقال قوم: الزلفة أول ساعة من الليل بعد مغيب الشمس فعلى هذا يكون المراد بزلف»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r11] verses 11:114 | Al-Hasan interprets the night portion as sunset and nightfall prayers. «وقال الحسن: المغرب والعشاء. وقيل»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r12] verses 11:114 | Al-Akhfash says the phrase means night prayer without specifying which prayer. «وقال الأخفش: يعني صلاة الليل ولم يعين»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r2] verses 11:114 | Mujahid says the good deeds include saying glory and praise to God and affirming His oneness and greatness; Ibn Atiyya treats this as an example. «وقال مجاهد: الحسنات قول الرجل سبحان الله والحمد لله ولا إله إلا الله والله أكبر»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r3] verses 11:114 | Ibn Atiyya says Mujahid's list is illustrative; the wording is general about good deeds but specific to lesser sins, as long as major sins are avoided. «قال ابن عطية: وهذا على جهة المثال في الحسنات، والذي يظهر أن اللفظ عام في»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r4] verses 11:114 | The occasion of revelation supports the majority: an Ansari, identified by some as Abu al-Yusr ibn Amr and by others as Abbad, had an intimate encounter short of intercourse. «قلت: سبب النزول يعضد قول الجمهور نزلت في رجل من الأنصار، قيل: هو أبو اليسر»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r5] verses 11:114 | In a Tirmidhi report from Abdullah, a man confesses to intimacy short of intercourse; after reciting the verse, the Prophet says it applies to everyone, not only that man. Tirmidhi grades it hasan sahih. «روى الترمذي عن عبد الله قال: جاء رجل إلى النبي»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r6] verses 11:114 | Another Tirmidhi report from Ibn Masud concerns a forbidden kiss; the Prophet says the verse applies to the questioner and whoever acts on it from his community, and Tirmidhi grades the report hasan sahih. «وخرج أيضا عن ابن مسعود أن رجلا أصاب من امرأة قبلة حرام فأتى النبي»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r7] verses 11:114 | Mujahid and Ibn Muhaysin also read زلفى, like قربى. «وقرأ مجاهد وابن محيصن أيضا «زلفى» مثل قربى»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r8] verses 11:114 | The remaining readers use زلف with an open lām, like غرفة and غرف. «وقرأ الباقون «وزلفا» بفتح اللام كغرفة وغرف»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r9] verses 11:114 | Ibn al-Arabi says zalaf means hours and zulfah is its singular. «قال ابن الأعرابي: الزلف الساعات، واحدها زلفة»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r1] verses 11:114 | Abu al-Yusr recounts kissing a woman who came to buy dates; after he confessed to Abu Bakr and Umar, he told the Prophet, feared Hell, and received the verse. The Prophet said it was for everyone; al-Tirmidhi calls this report hasan gharib and notes Qays ibn al-Rabi was weakened. «وروي
" عن أبي اليسر قال: أتتني امرأة تبتاع تمرا فقلت: إن في البيت تمرا أطيب»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r2] verses 11:114 | An alternative report says the Prophet turned away from the man, then after the afternoon prayer Gabriel brought the verse; the Prophet asked whether he had prayed with them and called the prayer an expiation. «أعرض عنه، وأقيمت صلاة العصر فلما فرغ منها نزل جبريل عليه»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r3] verses 11:114 | Another report says the Prophet told the man to perform four cycles of prayer after reciting the verse. «لما تلا عليه هذه الآية قال له: «قم فصل أربع ركعات»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r4] verses 11:114 | Al-Tirmidhi al-Hakim transmits from Ibn Abbas that no good deed answers and reaches an old sin more quickly than a recent good deed. «وخرج الترمذي الحكيم في «نوادر الأصول» من حديث ابن عباس عن رسول الله»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r5] verses 11:114 | The verse and reports show that an unlawful kiss or touch does not incur the fixed penalty. «دلت الآية مع هذه الأحاديث على أن القبلة الحرام واللمس الحرام لا يجب فيهما الحد»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r6] verses 11:114 | Ibn al-Mundhir is said to choose that no fixed penalty or discretionary punishment applies when a man and woman are found in one garment, using this report as evidence. «وقد يستدل به على أن لا حد ولا أدب على الرجل والمرأة وإن وجدا في»
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#4/r1] verses 11:114 | The Quran names prayer's postures and recitation, but gives its outline; the Prophet explained times, units, required conditions, sunnah, and virtues, as commanded in 16:44. «وهذا كله مجمل أجمله في كتابه، وأحال على نبيه في بيانه فقال جل ذكره:
وأنزلنا»
