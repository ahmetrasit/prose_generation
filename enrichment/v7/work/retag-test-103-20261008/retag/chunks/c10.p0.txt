# Notes

## 12:42
[ZAJJAJ:v3p111/r4] verses 12:42 | Some define biḍʿ as three to five years; Quṭrub extends it to seven; al-Aṣmaʿī, which the author calls correct, says three to nine.  «قال بعضهم: البضع ما بين الثلاث إلى الخمس»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p111/r5] verses 12:42 | Al-Aṣmaʿī derives biḍʿ from cutting off a piece and says it denotes a part of a number below ten, from three through nine.  «وقال الأصمعي وهو القول الصحيح: البضع ما بين الثلاث إلى التسع»

## 18:25
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p402#2/r1] verses 18:25 | Qatada says Ibn Masud's reading includes وقالوا, making the people the ones who state the sleepers stayed three hundred years and nine; the command to say God knows best supports this reading. «عن قتادة في قوله تعالى ﴿ ولبثوا في كهفهم ﴾ قال في حرف ابن مسعود وقالوا ولبثوا يعني أنه قاله الناس»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r4] verses 18:25 | The three hundred plus nine years are God’s report of the youths’ sleep in the cave until their discovery; Mujāhid sees this as specifying the ‘counted years’ of 18:11. «الظاهر أن قوله ولبثوا الآية إخبار من الله تعالى بمدة لبثهم نياما»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r5] verses 18:25, 18:26 | After giving the exact duration, God commands the Prophet to say that God knows best: this is certain truth because God knows the unseen of heaven and earth. «ولما تحرر هذا العدد بإخبار من الله تعالى أمر نبيه أن يقول قل الله أعلم»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r7] verses 18:25 | The suggestion that the additional nine means hours, days, months, or years, disputed by Israelites, is far-fetched: an unspecified addition after a specified number inherits the same unit. «وهذا بعيد لأنه إذا سبق عدد مفسر وعطف عليه ما لم يفسر»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r8] verses 18:25 | Al-Naqqāsh says the three hundred are solar years, with nine added because Arabs reckon lunar years, aligning the two calendars. «وحكى النقاش أنها ثلاثمائة شمسية، ولما كان الخطاب للعرب زيدت التسع»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r9] verses 18:25 | Qatāda and Maṭar al-Warrāq say لبثوا is a report from the Israelites, citing ʿAbd Allāh’s codex; in the common reading it continues the people’s reported claims. «وقال قتادة ومطر الوراق: لبثوا إخبار من بني إسرائيل»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r1] verses 18:25, 18:26 | One view says the Prophet is told to return knowledge of the duration to God, refuting others; Abū Ḥayyān prefers that the phrase belongs to the disputants, as the context indicates and because the matter is among God’s hidden knowledge. «قيل: هو من قول المتنازعين في أمرهم وهو الصحيح»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r2] verses 18:25 | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says مائة with tanwīn is apposition or بدل بيان; others call it explanatory or a specification, and al-Zamakhsharī calls it apposition to ثلاثمائة. «قال ابن عطية: على البدل أو عطف البيان. وقيل: على التفسير والتمييز»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r3] verses 18:25 | Abū al-Baqāʾ reports allowing مائة as a substitute for مائة understood as ‘hundreds,’ but Abū Ḥayyān says Basrans reject apposition and that مائة normally takes a singular genitive, not plural سنين. «فأما عطف البيان فلا يجوز على مذهب البصريين»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r4] verses 18:25 | These readers omit tanwīn from مائة and attach it to سنين, using a plural where singular is usual; Abū Ḥātim harshly criticized the reading. «مائة بغير تنوين مضافا إلى سنين أوقع الجمع موقع المفرد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r5] verses 18:25 | Abū ʿAlī says مائة is usually followed by a singular but may also be followed by a plural. «وقال أبو علي: هذه تضاف في المشهور إلى المفرد، وقد تضاف إلى الجمع»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r6] verses 18:25 | Ubayy and ʿAbd Allāh’s codex read سنة in the singular. «وقرأ أبي سنة وكذا في مصحف عبد الله»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r7] verses 18:25 | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk reads سنون with wāw, supplied as a predicate: ‘they are years.’ «وقرأ الضحاك: سنون بالواو على إضمار هي سنون»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r8] verses 18:25 | Al-Ḥasan and a narration from Abū ʿAmr pronounce تسعا with open tāʾ, like عشر. «وقرأ الحسن وأبو عمرو في رواية اللؤلؤي عنه تسعا بفتح التاء»
[AKHFASH:v2p429#2/r2] verses 18:25 | سنين may be a substitute for ثلاث or for مئة; it is genitive if it explains مئة and accusative if it explains ثلاث. «على البدل من {ثلاث} ومن "المئة" أي: لبثوا ثلاث مئة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#2/r1] verses 18:25 | The statement that the youths stayed three hundred years and nine more is an independent sentence explaining the earlier summary that God kept them asleep for a number of years; Mujāhid is cited for this explanation, which several scholars choose. «وهي جملة مستأنفة مبينة كما قال مجاهد لما أجمل في قوله تعالى ( فضربنا على آذانهم في الكهف سنين عددا )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r2] verses 18:25 | The nine years may reconcile the solar-year count of the People of Scripture with the lunar-year count of Arabs, and this explanation is attributed by some to ʿAlī; al-Ālūsī details astronomical objections to the claimed nine-year difference. «فقيل هو الإشارة إلى أنها ثلثمائة بحساب أهل الكتاب واعتبار السنة الشمسية وثلثمائة وتسع بحساب العرب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r3] verses 18:25 | Al-Khafājī says the two counts are explicit in context: three hundred by the People of the Book's reckoning, then nine extra by the questioners' reckoning; the difference is approximate because the remaining fraction is below half a year. «وبحث فيه الخفاجي بأن وجه الدلالة فيه ظاهر لأن المعنى لبثوا ثلثمائة سنة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r4] verses 18:25 | Al-Ṭībī proposes that the sleepers nearly awoke after three hundred years but something kept them sleeping nine additional years; al-Ālūsī objects that the verse would then need to say their sleep increased. «وقال الطيبي في توجيه العدول : إنه يمكن أن يقال : لعلهم لما استكملوا ثلثمائة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r5] verses 18:25 | Al-Ālūsī rejects the nine-year sleep-extension proposal because it would require an unexpressed meaning, that their sleep itself grew stronger for nine years. «وتعقب بأن هذا يقتضي أن يكون المراد وازدادوا نوما أي قوى نومهم في تسع سنين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r1] verses 18:25 | Some say the People of the Book disagreed over the sleepers' duration as they did their number; the verse resolves the disagreement, and the nine-year phrase confirms the answer like the exception in the account of Noah's thousand years. «وقال أيضا : يجوز أن يكون أهل الكتاب قد اختلفوا في مدة لبثهم كما اختلفوا في عدتهم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r2] verses 18:25 | Another view says the sleepers briefly awoke and then returned to sleep, explaining the increase; al-Ālūsī considers this closer than the solar-versus-lunar reckoning explanation. «وقيل إنهم انتبهوا قليلا ثم ردوا إلى حالتهم الأولى فلذا ذكر الازدياد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r3] verses 18:25 | A view attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās treats the duration statement as the People of the Book's quoted speculation, not God's assertion; he argues that a true duration would not be followed by saying God knows best, and cites the prior reports as conjecture. «ونسب ذلك إلى ابن عباس فقد أخرج ابن أبي حاتم وابن مردويه عنه رضي الله تعالى عنه أنه قال»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r4] verses 18:25 | Al-Ālūsī doubts this attribution: Ibn ʿAbbās accepted the seven-sleepers view even though it too is followed by God's knowing their number, so the same wording cannot distinguish rejection here. «فقد صح عنه القول بأن عدة أصحاب الكهف سبعة وثامنهم كلبهم مع أنه تعالى عقب القول بذلك بقوله سبحانه ( قل ربي أعلم بعدتهم )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r5] verses 18:25 | Ibn Masʿūd's reading says that people said they remained in the cave, supporting the possibility that the duration is quoted speculation; al-Ālūsī still says the following divine statement does not by itself signal rejection. «نعم قرأ ابن مسعود ( قالوا لبثوا كهفهم ) وهو يقتضي أن يكون من كلام الخائضين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p253/r1] verses 18:25 | On the quoted-speech interpretation, al-Khafājī assigns the pronoun in “they increased” to the People of the Book: some said three hundred, others added nine; al-Ālūsī rejects this as weak. «وقال الخفاجي : إن ضمير عليه لأهل الكتاب بخلافه على الأول ويظهر فيه وجه العدول»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p253/r2] verses 18:25 | Al-Ālūsī rejects the suggestion that the nine is unspecified as to years, months, days, or hours: a following unqualified count takes the unit of the preceding explicit number. «وقيل إنه تعالى ما قال ( وازدادوا تسعا ) كانت التسع مبهمة لا يدري أنها سنون أم شهور»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p253/r3] verses 18:25 | The accusative “nine” is the one object of the verb “increased”; although Abū al-Baqāʾ says “increase” can take two objects, its derived form here takes one, as usage reported by al-Rāghib also supports. «ونصب ( تسعا ) على أنه مفعول ( ازدادوا ) وهو مما يتعدى إلى واحد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p253/r4] verses 18:25 | Abū al-Baqāʾ and Ibn al-Ḥājib parse “years” in the common reading as an accusative substitute for “three hundred”; al-Zamakhsharī instead calls it an explanatory apposition, which the Sea commentary rejects for Basran grammar. «فقال أبو البقاء وابن الحاجب : هو منصوب على البدلية من ( ثلثمائة )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p253/r5] verses 18:25 | Some prefer explanatory apposition because substitution would seem to make the number itself unintended; a report says listeners asked whether the three hundred meant days, months, or years, after which “years” was revealed. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows either parsing. «وادعى بعضهم أنه أولى من البدلية لأنها تستلزم أن لا يكون العدد مقصودا»
