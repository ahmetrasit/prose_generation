# Notes

## 11:114
[BIQAI-FULL:v3p587/r2] verses 11:114 | Ibn Abbas regards the verse as belonging to the Meccan and Medinan portions of the surah; the cited report supports that view. «وهذا الحديث يؤيد قول ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما : إن هذه الآية من هذه السورة المكية المدنية»
[BIQAI-FULL:v3p587/r3] verses 11:114 | The preceding exhortation, warning, consolation, and instruction in the remedy for misery form a great reminder for those able to attend, reflect clearly, and understand deeply. «الأمر العالي الرتبة الذي تقدم من الترغيب والترهيب والتسلية وتعليم الداء والدواء»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r1] verses 11:114 | Sins are impurities and acts of obedience purify; ablution washes away sins with its drops, so some jurists considered used ablution cloth impure and disliked praying in it. «أن الذنوب كلها نجاسات والطاعات مطهرات وبماء أعضاء الوضوء تتساقط الأوزار»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r2] verses 11:114 | A report has God tell Moses that each drop of ablution water used by Aḥmad and his community earns a garden as broad as the sky. «يتوضأ أحمد وأمته كما أمرتهم وأعطيهم بكل قطرة»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r3] verses 11:114 | Ḥāfiẓ's Persian couplet praises prayer and devoted appeal purified by tears and heart's blood. «خوشا نماز ونياز كسى كه ازسردرد بآب ديده»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r4] verses 11:114 | The highest good deed is knowledge of God, following monotheism, and opposing the lower self; remembrance purifies souls and hearts, strengthens obedience, and defeats Satan's schemes. «العلم بالله ، وطريقه التوحيد ، وخلاف هوى النفس»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r5] verses 11:114 | When companions ask whether ‘There is no god but God’ is a good deed, the Prophet says it is the best of good deeds. «لا إله إلا الله من الحسنات قال»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r6] verses 11:114 | The verse signals constant remembrance, worship, and obedience by day and night, with time for human needs, daytime work, and sleep to restore tired faculties for worship. «وفي الآية إشارة إلى إدامة الذكر والطاعة والعبادة في الليل والنهار»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r7] verses 11:114 | In the inward reading, light from good deeds, remembrance, and watchfulness at day edges and night approaches erases the darkness of time spent on bodily needs. «أي أن أنوار الحسنات وهي الأعمال الصالحة والذكر والمراقبة»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r8] verses 11:114 | The luminous spirit would be lost through attachment to the dark body unless sacred good works nurture it, lift it toward spirituality and divine unity, and dispel bodily darkness. «تعلق الروح النوراني العلوي بالجسد الظلماني السفلي موجب لخسران الروح»
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r9] verses 11:114 | He compares this nurture to a seed that would be lost unless water made one seed into seven hundred, with God multiplying further as He wills. «كما أن إلقاء الحبة في الأرض موجب لخسران الحبة»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r1] verses 11:114 | One reading assigns the afternoon edge of day to noon and afternoon prayers, and the night approaches to sunset and night prayers, covering all five daily prayers. «الظهر والعصر لأن ما بعد الزوال عشى»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r10] verses 11:114 | The Prophet compares five daily prayers to bathing five times in a river at one's door, which leaves no dirt behind. «لو أن نهرا بباب أحدكم يغتسل منه كل يوم خمس مرات»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r2] verses 11:114 | Another reading takes the day's ends as morning and sunset and night approaches as night prayer plus tahajjud, obligatory for the Prophet; it cites the night vigil command. «وفسر بعضهم طرفي النهار بالصبح والمغرب وزلف الليل بالعشاء»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r3] verses 11:114 | Other interpretations include witr according to Abū Ḥanīfa, or night prayer, witr, and tahajjud together, matching the plural ‘approaches.’ «أو الوتر على ما ذهب إليه أبو حنيفة أو مجموع العشاء والوتر والتهجد»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r4] verses 11:114 | A similar passage is read as morning before sunrise, noon and afternoon before sunset, and sunset and night prayers at night. «أي : بصلاة الصبح [ق : 39-79]»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r5] verses 11:114 | Good deeds, especially the five prayers, erase consequences of minor sins rather than undoing sins already committed; prayers, Fridays, and Ramadans expiate between them when major sins are avoided. «على الإطلاق لا سيما الصلوات الخمس»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r6] verses 11:114 | Prayer also restrains people from committing sins, as another verse says it keeps one from indecency and wrongdoing. «إن الصلوة تنهى عن الفحشاء والمنكر»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r7] verses 11:114 | Abū al-Yusr was tempted by a woman buying dates; he drew her home, embraced and kissed her but stopped before intercourse when she warned him to fear God, then regretted it. «أن أبا اليسر الأنصاري كان يبيع التمر فأتته امرأة»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r8] verses 11:114 | Abū al-Yusr confessed to Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, who advised concealing the sin and repenting, but he could not keep silent and went to the Prophet. «فأتى أبا بكر رضي الله عنه فأخبره»
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r9] verses 11:114 | The Prophet told him to wait for God's command; after the afternoon prayer the verse came, and prayer expiated his act for everyone. «فلما صلى صلاة العصر نزلت هذه الآية»
[DURR-FULL:v4p480/r10] verses 11:114 | Mujahid identifies the day prayers as dawn, noon, and afternoon, and the night intervals as sunset and night prayers. «عن مجاهد في قوله {وأقم الصلاة طرفي النهار} قال: صلاة الفجر وصلاتي العشاء يعني الظهر والعصر»
[DURR-FULL:v4p480/r11] verses 11:114 | Mujahid also glosses the night intervals as successive hours, referring to the late night prayer. «عن مجاهد في قوله {وزلفا من الليل} قال: ساعة بعد ساعة يعني صلاة العشاء الآخرة»
[DURR-FULL:v4p480/r12] verses 11:114 | Ibn Abbas preferred delaying the night prayer and would cite “and at intervals of the night.” «عن ابن عباس أنه كان يستحب تأخير العشاء ويقرأ {وزلفا من الليل}»
[DURR-FULL:v4p480/r8] verses 11:114 | Ibn Abbas identifies the two ends of the day as sunset and dawn prayers, and the night intervals as the late evening prayer. «عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما في قوله {وأقم الصلاة طرفي النهار} قال: صلاة المغرب والغداة {وزلفا من الليل}»
[DURR-FULL:v4p480/r9] verses 11:114 | Hasan identifies the ends of the day as dawn and afternoon prayers, and the night intervals as sunset and night prayers; he says the Prophet called the latter the two night intervals. «عن الحسن في قوله {وأقم الصلاة طرفي النهار} قال: الفجر والعصر {وزلفا من الليل} قال: هما زلفتان»
[DURR-FULL:v4p482/r1] verses 11:114 | The Prophet applies the prayer instruction to everyone, not only the man in the preceding account. «فقرأ عليه {وأقم الصلاة طرفي النهار} الآية فقال معاذ بن جبل: يا رسول الله أله وحده أم للناس كافة»
[DURR-FULL:v4p482/r2] verses 11:114 | Abu al-Yusr says he kissed a woman who came to buy dates, confessed to Abu Bakr and Umar, then told the Prophet, who reproached him; the verse was revealed and applied generally. «عن أبي اليسر قال أتتني امرأة تبتاع تمرا فقلت: إن في البيت تمرا أطيب منه»
[DURR-FULL:v4p482/r3] verses 11:114 | A man who asked for punishment was told after praying with the Prophet that he was cleansed of his sin like a newborn; the verse was revealed then. «عن أبي أمامة رضي الله عنه أن رجلا أتى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال: يا رسول الله أقم في حد الله»
[DURR-FULL:v4p482/r4] verses 11:114 | Mu'adh reports a man asking about intimate contact with a woman short of intercourse; the verse was revealed in response. «عن معاذ بن جبل قال: رجل إلى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال: ما ترى في رجل لقي امرأة لا يعرفها»
[DURR-FULL:v4p483/r1] verses 11:114 | In Mu'adh's account, the Prophet prescribed careful ablution and prayer and said the instruction applies generally to believers. «فقال له النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم توضأ وضوءا حسنا ثم قم فصل قال معاذ: فقلت يا رسول الله»
[DURR-FULL:v4p483/r2] verses 11:114 | A man confessed to touching a woman whose husband was away on campaign; Umar insisted the revealed prayer instruction applied to all believers, and the Prophet agreed. «عن ابن عباس قال جاء رجل إلى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال: إن امرأة جاءت تبايعني»
[DURR-FULL:v4p483/r3] verses 11:114 | Another report says a man confessed to having done something with a woman short of intercourse, after which the prayer verse was revealed. «عن ابن عباس قال: جاء رجل إلى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال: إني نلت من امرأة»
[DURR-FULL:v4p483/r4] verses 11:114 | A man who desired a woman tried to approach her while she bathed in a pool, but his organ became limp; the Prophet prescribed four prayer cycles, and the verse was revealed. «عن ابن عباس أن رجلا كان يحب امرأة فاستأذن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم في حاجة»
[DURR-FULL:v4p483/r5] verses 11:114 | A date seller led an Anṣari woman home and pressured her, then regretted sexual contact short of intercourse; the Prophet prayed with him, explained the five prayers named in the verse, and said its cleansing applies to everyone. «عن بريدة قال جاءت امرأة من الأنصار إلى رجل يبيع التمر بالمدينة وكانت امرأة حسناء جميلة»
[DURR-FULL:v4p484#2/r1] verses 11:114 | The Prophet says the prayers expiate sins committed between them, citing the verse. «عن أبي مالك الأشعري قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم جعلت الصلوات كفارات لما بينهن»
[DURR-FULL:v4p484#2/r2] verses 11:114 | The Prophet says each prayer removes the sins that came before it. «عن أبي أيوب الأنصاري قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم كل صلاة تحط ما بين يديها من خطيئة»
[DURR-FULL:v4p484#2/r3] verses 11:114 | Uthman reports that successive prayers after ablution erase sins from the interval since the previous prayer; he identifies the verse's good deeds as these acts and the enduring good deeds as declarations of remembrance. «عن عثمان قال: رأيت رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يتوضأ ثم قال من توضأ وضوئي هذا»
