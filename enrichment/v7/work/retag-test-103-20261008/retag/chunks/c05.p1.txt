[TAB-FULL:v12p626/r2] verses 11:114 | Qatada reports that a man who kissed a woman came to the Prophet saying he was ruined, and the verse was revealed in response. «عن قتادة، " أن رجلا أصاب من امرأة قبلة، فأتى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال: يا نبي الله هلكت»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#4/r3] verses 11:114 | A report identifies the two ends of the day as sunset and dawn prayers and the night periods as the late evening prayer. «وفيه عن جرير عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام قال»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#4/r4] verses 11:114 | A report from Zurara connects the verse to the five daily prayers; al-Tabataba'i says it suggests the day's two sides mean periods before and after noon. «وفي التهذيب بإسناده عن زرارة عن أبي جعفر عليه السلام في حديث في الصلوات الخمس اليومية»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#4/r5] verses 11:114 | A report explains that a believer's night prayer removes sins committed during the day; the author notes parallel transmissions in several collections. «قال صلاة المؤمن بالليل تذهب بما عمل من ذنب النهار»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#4/r6] verses 11:114 | In a report narrated by Salman, the Prophet shakes a dry branch and says that good ablution followed by the five prayers sheds a Muslim's sins like falling leaves. «هكذا فعله رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم وأنا معه تحت شجرة»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#5/r1] verses 11:114 | Ali recounts a man confessing a sin; after the man prayed with the Prophet following good ablution, the Prophet told him the prayer expiated the sin. «عن الحارث عن علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام قال كنا مع رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#5/r2] verses 11:114 | The confession story circulates through many Companion routes with variations; al-Tirmidhi reports it from Abu al-Yusr, who experienced it. «والرواية مروية بطرق كثيرة عن ابن مسعود وأبى أمامة ومعاذ بن جبل وابن عباس»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#5/r3] verses 11:114 | When Ali asks which verse gives Muslims most hope, he dismisses several proposed forgiveness verses and identifies the command to establish prayer. «عن أبى حمزة الثمالى قال سمعت أحدهما عليهما السلام يقول إن عليا عليه السلام أقبل على الناس»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#5/r4] verses 11:114 | The report says five prayers wash away sins like a river cleans dirt, leaving the worshiper cleansed as at birth after each prayer. «إنما منزلة الصلوات الخمس لأمتي كنهر جار على باب أحدكم»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#6/r1] verses 11:114 | The prayer-as-river simile is also transmitted in Sunni collections from Companions including Abu Hurayra, Anas, Jabir, and Abu Sa'id. «وقد روي المثل المذكور في آخر الحديث من طرق أهل السنة عن عدة من الصحابة كأبي هريرة وأنس وجابر»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#6/r2] verses 11:114 | Abu Abdillah warns that angels record each deed; small sins should not be dismissed, and a good deed can erase an old grave sin, as the verse teaches. «عن إبراهيم الكرخي قال كنت عند أبي عبد الله عليه السلام فدخل عليه رجل من أهل المدينة»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#6/r3] verses 11:114 | Asked whether charity and pilgrimage from officials' earnings erase the sin, Abu Abdillah says one sin does not cancel another, but a good deed cancels sin. «عن سماعة بن مهران قال سأل أبا عبد الله عليه السلام رجل من أهل الجبال»
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#6/r4] verses 11:114 | A report says a Muslim who performs ablution and obligatory prayer well is forgiven for sins committed since the preceding prayer. «عن أبى أمامة قال قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم»
[TABATABAI:11:114-116/r1] verses 11:114 | Good deeds, especially prescribed prayers, remove traces of sins from believers' souls; this teaching serves as a reminder for those who remember God. «تعليل لقوله وأقم الصلاة وبيان أن الصلوات حسنات واردة على نفوس المؤمنين»

[TABRISI:11:113-116/r18] verses 11:114 | Establish prayer by completing its acts of bowing, prostration, and other obligations. «أي أدها وائت بأعمالها على وجه التمام في ركوعها وسجودها»

[TABRISI:11:113-117/r3] verses 11:114 | Al-Sadiq advises a visitor to account for each day and night because a noble angel records his public and hidden acts; he should neither dismiss a sin nor belittle a good deed. «واعلم أن معك ملكا كريما موكلا بك يحفظ عليك ما تصنع ويطلع على سرك الذي تخفيه من الناس»
[TABRISI:11:113-117/r4] verses 11:114 | Al-Sadiq says a good deed can catch and remove even a great old sin forgotten by its doer, after it has been recorded, illustrating that good deeds erase sins. «أما إنها لتدرك الذنب العظيم القديم المنسي عند عامله فتجتذبه وتسقطه وتذهب به»
[TABRISI:11:113-117/r5] verses 11:114 | Abu Hamza al-Thumali reports that Ali asked which Quranic verse gives the greatest hope; one person proposed a verse, but Ali said it was good, not the one he meant. «أية آية في كتاب الله أرجى عندكم»
[TABRISI:11:113-117/r6] verses 11:114 | In the report from Abu Hamza al-Thumali, a second respondent cites the promise to forgive one who does evil or wrongs himself; Ali again says it is good but not the sought verse. «قال: حسنة وليست إياها وقال بعضهم»
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r1] verses 11:114 | Ali reports that the Prophet called the prayer command in this verse the most hopeful verse in the Book. «رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يقول " أرجى آية في كتاب الله»
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r2] verses 11:114 | The Prophet compares the five daily prayers to bathing five times in a river: prayer removes sins as washing removes bodily dirt. «إن أحدكم ليقوم من وضوئه فتساقط عن جوارحه الذنوب»
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r3] verses 11:114 | One interpretation is that persistence in good deeds leads a person to abandon evil deeds, as though the good deeds remove them. «الدوام على فعل الحسنات يدعو إلى ترك السيئات فكأنها يذهبن بها»
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r4] verses 11:114 | A second interpretation identifies the good deeds as repentance, which removes sins by cancelling their punishment; the author says there is no disagreement that repentance cancels punishment. «المراد بالحسنات التوبة فإنها تذهب السيئات بأن تسقط عقابها»
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r5] verses 11:114 | The reminder is an admonition for whoever reflects on the teaching that good deeds remove evil deeds. «فيه تذكار وموعظة لمن تذكر به وفكر فيه»
[TABRISI:11:114/r1] verses 11:114 | Other glosses say to perform prayer evenly or to keep performing it continuously. «وقيل: معناه اعملها على استواء. وقيل: أدم على فعلها»
[TABRISI:11:114/r2] verses 11:114 | Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zayd identify the two ends of day as dawn and sunset prayers and the night intervals as the first hours of night, including the final evening prayer. «صلاة العشاء الآخرة والزلف أول ساعات الليل عن ابن عباس وابن زيد»
[TABRISI:11:114/r3] verses 11:114 | They say noon and afternoon prayers are omitted either because they are obviously daytime prayers or because they fall near the day's latter end after noon; 17:78 specifies the sun's decline, meaning noon, and this is the report from Abu Ja'far. «وترك ذكر الظهر والعصر لأحد أمرين إما لظهورهما في أنهما صلاتا النهار»
