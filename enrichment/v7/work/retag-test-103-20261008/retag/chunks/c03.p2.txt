[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:11:114/r1] verses 11:114 | Day runs from dawn to sunset and night from sunset to dawn; the day’s two edges are dawn and sunset. The prayer at the second edge is variously identified as noon and afternoon before sunset, or sunset and night prayers after it. «Gündüzün iki tarafından maksat, geceyle birleşen iki tarafı, yani başı ve sonu olup tan yerinin ağardığı ve güneşin battığı zamanlardır»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:11:114/r2] verses 11:114 | Zulef, plural of zulfa, means the night’s early hours near day and refers here to the night prayer; because the verse gives no form or exact times, it stresses prayer at the indicated periods without detailing them, as Shawkani notes. «“Gündüze yakın saatler” diye tercüme ettiğimiz zülef kelimesi ise zülfenin çoğulu olup gecenin gündüze yakın olan ilk saatlerini ifade eder»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:11:114/r3] verses 11:114 | Some hold that this verse sets the times for all obligatory prayers; the mutawatir Sunna supplies their specific times and manner. «Bu âyetin bütün farz namazların vakitlerini belirlediği kanaatinde olanlar da vardır»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:11:114/r4] verses 11:114 | The verse calls for worship and abundant good deeds to remove or forgive evils, especially prayer, and its closing words address those who turn toward Qur’anic guidance. «Âyet, kötülüklerin ortadan kalkması veya bağışlanması için ibadetlerle iyiliklerin çokça yapılmasının gereğine işaret etmektedir»

[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p508/r11] verses 11:114 | Abu Musa Tahman identifies the man as Abu al-Yusr al-Ansari; Ibn Abis calls him Amr ibn Ghaziya, and Muqatil calls him Amir ibn Qays, all Ansaris. «أبو اليسر الأنصاري»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p508/r2] verses 11:114 | Zulaf is the plural of zulfa, a station or degree, meaning hours or periods of night; Muzdalifa is named as a stop after Arafat or because Adam drew near to Eve there. «والزلف جمع زلفة , والزلفة المنزلة , فكأنه قال ومنازل من الليل , أي ساعات من الليل»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p508/r3] verses 11:114 | The phrase may denote the late evening prayer (Ibn Abbas and Mujahid) or sunset and evening prayers (al-Dahhak and al-Hasan, who transmit it from the Prophet). «وفي معنى { زلفا من الليل } قولان : أحدهما : صلاة العشاء الآخرة , قاله ابن عباس ومجاهد .»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p508/r4] verses 11:114 | Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, Ibn Masud, and al-Dahhak interpret the good deeds as the five daily prayers. «في هذا الحسنات أربعة أقاويل : أحدها : الصلوات الخمس , قاله ابن عباس والحسن وابن مسعود والضحاك .»
