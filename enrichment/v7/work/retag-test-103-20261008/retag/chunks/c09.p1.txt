معت قتادة، يقول: " البضع: ما بين الثلاث إلى التسع»
[TAB-FULL:v13p177/r3] verses 12:42 | Mujahid likewise defines bid' as three to nine years. «عن إسرائيل، عن منصور، عن مجاهد، " {بضع سنين} [يوسف: 42] قال: ما بين الثلاث إلى التسع»
[TAB-FULL:v13p177/r4] verses 12:42 | Ibn Abbas defines bid' as fewer than ten years. «قال ابن جريج، قال ابن عباس: " {بضع سنين} [يوسف: 42] دون العشرة»
[TAB-FULL:v13p177/r5] verses 12:42 | Al-Farra says bid' is used with tens, from ten through ninety, as an odd number within that range, but not with a hundred or thousand. «وزعم الفراء أن البضع لا يذكر إلا مع العشر، ومع العشرين إلى التسعين»
[TAB-FULL:v13p177/r6] verses 12:42 | Al-Tabari holds bid' ranges from three to nine and is never below three, following observed Arabic usage. «والصواب في البضع من الثلاث إلى التسع إلى العشر، ولا يكون دون الثلاث»
[TABATABAI:12:22-34#31/r3] verses 12:24, 12:42, 12:50 | A report says Joseph stumbled three times: when he desired her and was imprisoned, when he asked the cupbearer to mention him and forgot his Lord, and when he said the brothers were thieves. «أخرج أبو الشيخ عن ابن عباس قال عثر يوسف عليه السلام ثلاث عثرات حين هم بها»
[TABATABAI:12:22-34#31/r4] verses 12:24, 12:42, 12:50 | The report conflicts with verses describing God’s election and purification of Joseph and Satan’s lack of access; its alleged grave sins cannot fit his status as a truthful benefactor, and similar reports are unreliable. «والرواية تخالف صريح كلامه تعالى حيث يذكر أن الله اجتباه وأخلصه لنفسه»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42#2/r4] verses 12:42 | The al-Qummi report is textually disordered: it makes the two men royal attendants assigned to guard Joseph, which conflicts with the Qur’an’s description of them as his prison companions. «أقول وفي الرواية اضطراب لفظي»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42#2/r5] verses 12:42 | A report in al-Ayyashi identifies Joseph’s lord in the request to remember him as the Aziz. «وفي تفسير العياشي عن سماعة عن قول الله»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42/r2] verses 12:42 | The pronouns in the request to remember Joseph and in the statement that he remained in prison refer to Joseph; he asked the man he expected to survive to mention his case to the king. «راجعة إلى يوسف أي قال يوسف للذين ظن هو أنه سينجو منهما»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42/r3] verses 12:42 | Here zann can mean conviction rather than uncertain conjecture, as in the Qur’anic description of those who expect to meet their Lord. «إطلاق الظن على مطلق الاعتقاد وله نظائر في القرآن كقوله تعالى الذين يظنون أنهم ملاقوا ربهم»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42/r4] verses 12:41, 12:42 | The view that Joseph interpreted the dreams only by conjecture conflicts with his declaration that the matter was settled and God’s statement that He taught him interpretation. «أنه صرح لهما بعلمه في قوله»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42/r5] verses 12:42 | A possible alternative makes the saved prisoner, rather than Joseph, the subject of the pronoun in the phrase about expecting rescue; the author accepts it if context allows. «إلى الموصول أي قال يوسف لصاحبه الذي ظن ذلك الصاحب»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42/r6] verses 12:42 | The pronouns in the forgetting clause refer to the surviving cupbearer: Satan made him forget to mention Joseph to the king, so Joseph stayed imprisoned for several years. «أي فأنسى الشيطان صاحبه الناجي أن يذكره لربه»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42/r7] verses 12:42 | The alternative that Satan made Joseph forget God and that Joseph was punished for seeking help from a person contradicts the text’s protection of the sincere and wrongly treats ordinary use of means as reliance. «فمما يخالف نص الكتاب فإن الله سبحانه نص على كونه عليه السلام من المخلصين ونص على أن المخلصين لا سبيل للشيطان إليهم»
[TABATABAI:12:41-42/r8] verses 12:42, 12:45 | The later statement that the cupbearer remembered Joseph after a lapse of years confirms that the cupbearer, not Joseph, was the one who forgot. «قرينة صالحة على أن الناسي هو الساقي دون يوسف»
[TABATABAI:12:42/r1] verses 12:42 | A report attributed to the Prophet says Joseph would not have remained imprisoned so long had he not uttered the request to remember him to the king. «لو لم يقل يوسف الكلمة التي قال ما لبث في السجن»
[TABATABAI:12:42/r2] verses 12:42 | A parallel report attributed to the Prophet says, God have mercy on Joseph, had he not said remember me to your lord he would not have remained imprisoned so long. «عن ابن المنذر وابن أبى حاتم وابن مردويه عن أبي هريرة»
[TABATABAI:12:42/r3] verses 12:42 | Similar blame of Joseph’s request is also transmitted from Ikrima, al-Hasan, and others; the author gives no grading for these reports. «وروى مثله عن عكرمة والحسن وغيرهما»
[TABATABAI:12:42/r4] verses 12:42 | A report says God reminded Joseph of favors, rebuked him for seeking a creature’s help, and ordered him to remain imprisoned for several years; the author classifies it with reports contradicting the Qur’an. «قال الله ليوسف ألست الذي حببتك إلى أبيك وفضلتك على الناس بالحسن»
[TABATABAI:12:42/r5] verses 12:42, 12:79, 12:80 | Another report counts Joseph’s request to the king among three alleged lapses, alongside saying the brothers were thieves and saying he had not betrayed them; it also has Gabriel rebuke him. «ومثلها ما في الدر المنثور عن ابن مردويه عن ابن عباس قال عثر يوسف عليه السلام ثلاث عثرات»
[TABATABAI:12:42/r6] verses 12:42, 12:79, 12:80 | The author rejects these reports as imputing explicit lies to the truthful Joseph and says God’s text clears him of such accusations. «وفي الرواية نسبة الفرية والكذب الصريح إلى الصديق عليه السلام»
[TABATABAI:12:42/r7] verses 12:42 | The reading that Joseph was punished for asking another person for help contradicts the text’s declaration that he was among God’s sincere servants, and using ordinary means does not imply reliance on them. «والله سبحانه يبرئه من هذه المفتريات بنص كتابه»
[TABRISI:12:42/r1] verses 12:42 | The majority and al-Jubbaʾi take ظن as certain knowledge received by revelation, as in the verse about expecting to meet one's account; this is the author's preferred reading. «هذا قول الأكثرين واختيار الجبائي»
[TABRISI:12:42/r10] verses 12:42 | The lesson is to rely on and seek help from God in hardship, although seeking help from other people is also permissible. «ترغيب في الاعتصام بالله تعالى والاستعانة به دون غيره عند نزول الشدائد»
[TABRISI:12:42/r2] verses 12:42 | Qatada takes ظن in its ordinary sense of conjecture, since Yusuf could not verify that the prisoner's dream report was true. «قال قتادة للذي ظنه ناجيا لأنه لا يحكم بصدقه فيما قصه من الرؤيا»
[TABRISI:12:42/r3] verses 12:42 | Yusuf asked the escaped cupbearer to mention that he was unjustly imprisoned to his master, the king. «أي اذكرني عند سيدك بأني محبوس ظلما»
[TABRISI:12:42/r4] verses 12:42 | The author first explains Satan's making Yusuf forget as Yusuf forgetting to mention God and instead asking a human for help, when reliance on God was more fitting. «يعني أنسى الشيطان يوسف ذكر الله تعالى في تلك الحال حتى استغاث بمخلوق»
[TABRISI:12:42/r5] verses 12:42 | An alternative takes the forgotten remembrance as the cupbearer's failure to mention Yusuf to the king; the author paraphrases this as Satan making him forget Yusuf before his master. «عن الحسن ومحمد بن إسحاق والجبائي وأبي مسلم وعلى هذا فتقديره فأنساه الشيطان ذكر يوسف»
[TABRISI:12:42/r6] verses 12:42 | The stay of several years is specified as seven years in reports from Ibn Abbas, ʿAli ibn al-Husayn, and Abu ʿAbd Allah. «أي سبع سنين عن ابن عباس وروي ذلك عن علي بن الحسين ع وأبي عبد الله»
[TABRISI:12:42/r7] verses 12:42 | A report from the Prophet marvels that Yusuf sought help from a creature instead of the Creator; another says his request to be remembered prolonged his stay. «عجبت من أخي يوسف ع كيف استغاث بالمخلوق دون الخالق»
[TABRISI:12:42/r8] verses 12:42 | In a report from Abu ʿAbd Allah, Gabriel asks Yusuf who gave him his beauty, his father's love, the caravan, rescue from the well, and protection from the women; Yusuf answers that God did, and is told to remain in prison for turning to a creature. «جاء جبرائيل ع فقال: يا يوسف من جعلك أحسن الناس»
[TABRISI:12:42/r9] verses 12:42 | Seeking human help to avert harm is permitted and can even be required; the Prophet sought help from the Emigrants and Helpers, so the reports describe a temporary reproach for departing from Yusuf's usual reliance on God, not a prohibition. «الاستعانة بالعباد في دفع المضار والتخلص من المكاره جائز غير منكر ولا قبيح»
