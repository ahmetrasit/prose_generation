[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r4] verses 11:114 | The majority read zulafan with an open lām. «وقرأ الجمهور: وزلفا بفتح اللام، وطلحة وعيسى البصرة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r5] verses 11:114 | These readers use a damma on the lām, as though the word were a singular noun. «وطلحة وعيسى البصرة وابن أبي إسحاق وأبو جعفر: بضمها كأنه اسم مفرد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r6] verses 11:114 | They read the lām quiescent; a further transmission from them has zulfa on the fuʿlā pattern, a feminine singular meaning a station or degree. «وقرأ ابن محيصن ومجاهد: بإسكانها، وروي عنهما: وزلفى على وزن فعلى»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r7] verses 11:114 | The plural readings treat the word as successive stations or as a collective noun; zulafā is equivalent to one zalfa. «فزلف جمع كظلم، وزلف كبسر في بسر، وزلف كبسر في بسرة، فهما اسما جنس»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r8] verses 11:114 | The apparent syntax coordinates the night approaches with the two daytime boundaries as another bounded period. «والظاهر عطف وزلفا من الليل على طرفي النهار، عطف طرفا على طرف»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r9] verses 11:114 | Al-Zamakhsharī explains zulaf as the part of the day near nightfall; another interpretation makes it night prayers of devotion and coordinates it with الصلاة instead. «وقال الزمخشري: وقد ذكر هذه القراآت وهو ما يقرب من آخر النهار من الليل»
[AKHFASH:v1p391/r3] verses 11:114 | The yāʾ in طرفي النهار is vocalized because it meets a following still consonant; still consonants are usually given kasra, as in صاحبي السجن. «وقال {طرفي النهار} فحرك الياء لأنها ساكنة لقيها حرف ساكن»
[AKHFASH:v1p391/r4] verses 11:114 | زلفا is a plural of زلفة, with زلفات and زلف also used as plural forms. «وقال {وزلفا من الليل} لأنها جماعة تقول "زلفة" و"زلفات" و"زلف"»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r1] verses 11:114 | Ṭalḥa, Ibn Abī Isḥāq, and Abū Jaʿfar read zuluf with a rounded lām; Mujāhid and Ibn Muḥayṣin read it with a silent lām, and a variant has zulfa, a synonymous form. «وقرأ طلحة وابن أبي إسحاق وأبو جعفر ( زلفا ) بضم اللام»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r2] verses 11:114 | Good deeds expiate sins and remove liability, not sins as entities; another view says their records are erased. The view that they prevent sinning is remote and contrary to reports. «أي يكفرنها ويذهبن المؤاخذة عليها وإلا فنفس السيئات أعراض وجدت فانعدمت»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r3] verses 11:114 | The apparent scope is obligatory and voluntary good deeds, though some restrict it to obligations. Reports about affirmation matching angels, fasting ʿArafa, and gratitude after eating or dressing support voluntary acts as expiators. «والظاهر أن المراد من الحسنات ما يعم الصلوات المفروضة وغيرها من الطاعات»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r4] verses 11:114 | The occasion is reported as Abū al-Yusr regretting he kissed a woman; after waiting for revelation, the Prophet said prayer atoned for it. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, and Ibn al-Musayyib also transmit this view. «أبا اليسر من الأنصار قبل امرأة ثم ندم فأتى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r5] verses 11:114 | Those who identify good deeds as obligatory prayers likely cite one important instance in the general category; the occasion does not restrict the verse. «والظاهر أن ذلك منهم إقتصار على بعض مهم من أفراد ذلك العام وسبب النزول لا يأبى العموم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156/r1] verses 11:114 | Establish the prescribed prayers fully; other reported meanings are to maintain them regularly or perform them at the start of their times. «أي المكتوبة ومعنى إقامتها أداؤها على تمامها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156/r2] verses 11:114 | The two ends mean the beginning and end of the day. Zulf are hours near the day; al-Layth and Thaʿlab say the first part of night, while Abū ʿUbayda, al-Akhfash, and Ibn Qutayba say any night hour, supported by al-ʿAjjāj’s verse. «فإنه من أزلفه إذا قربه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156/r3] verses 11:114 | Zulf is coordinated with the two ends, and min al-layl qualifies it. End prayers are variously dawn and afternoon, dawn and sunset, or dawn then noon and afternoon; Abū Ḥayyān favors dawn and afternoon, while noon as an endpoint is questioned. «وهو عطف على ( طرفي النهار ) و ( من الليل ) في موضع الصفة له»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156/r4] verses 11:114 | Most identify the night prayers as sunset and evening; a report via al-Ḥasan from Ibn ʿAbbās specifies ʿatama. Another reading makes zulf night prayers drawing near to God, perhaps the Prophet’s night prayer or evening and witr. «والمراد بصلاة الزلف عند الأكثر صلاة المغرب والعشاء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p157/r1] verses 11:114 | Most take ‘sins’ as minor sins and say major sins require repentance, citing Muslim’s report through al-ʿAlāʾ that five prayers expiate the interval if major sins are avoided. «والمراد بالسيآت عند الأكثرين الصغائر لأن الكبائر لا يكفرها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p157/r2] verses 11:114 | Al-Balqīnī says 4:31 concerns avoiding major sins throughout life, whereas the prayer report concerns each day; al-Samhūdī objects that lifelong avoidance entails daily prayer, then discusses multiple expiators retaining their rewards. «وأجاب البلقيني بأن ذلك غير وارد لأن المراد بالآية أن تجتنبوا في جميع العمر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p157/r3] verses 11:114 | Al-Ālūsī rejects a lifelong reading of 4:31: the reward for avoiding major sins at each time expiates minor sins committed then, as al-Qāḍī and al-Ghazālī support. «والظاهر أن المراد من ذلك أن ثواب إجتناب الكبائر في كل وقت يكفر الصغائر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p157/r4] verses 11:114 | Al-Ghazālī says restraint expiates only when someone has ability and desire to commit a major sin; abstaining from incapacity or lack of desire does not earn that effect. «إجتناب الكبيرة إنما يكفر الصغيرة إذا إجتنبها مع القدرة والإرادة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p157/r5] verses 11:114 | Al-Nawawī says that when an expiatory act coincides with major sins but no minor sins, one may hope that the major sins are lightened. «أنه صادف فعل المكفر كبيرة أو كبائر ولم يصادف صغيرة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p158/r1] verses 11:114 | Al-Ālūsī reconciles the evidence by making avoidance of major sins a limit on expiating all interval sins, not a condition for any expiation: prayers expiate minors and all interval sins when majors are avoided. «ما اجتنبت الكبائر في الخبر ليس قيدا لأصل التكفير بل لشمول التكفير سائر الذنوب التي بين الصلوات الخمس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p158/r2] verses 11:114 | Ibn Ḥajar says avoiding major sins entails performing the five prayers, since abandoning prayer is major; al-Ālūsī says this argument merits examination. «وعلى تقدير ورد السؤال فالتخلص عنه سهل وذلك لأنه لا يتم إجتناب الكبائر إلا بفعل الصلوات الخمس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p158/r3] verses 11:114 | Al-Muḥibb al-Ṭabarī records two views: the majority make avoidance of major sins a condition; another treats it as an exception, so expiation excludes major sins, which he calls stronger. «إختلف العلماء في أمر تكفير الصغائر بالعبادات هل هو مشروط بإجتناب الكبائر على قولين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p158/r4] verses 11:114 | Al-Zarkashī reports disagreement over requiring repentance for expiation; al-Baḥr says the requirement is explicit among rigorous legal theorists. «ذكر الزركشي أنهم إختلفوا في أن التكفير هل يشترط فيه التوبة أم لا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p158/r5] verses 11:114 | Al-Subkī says acceptance of repentance from unbelief is certain by grace, but acceptance for sins is disputed; other authorities differ over whether its expiation is presumptive or certain. «والمنقول عن السبكي أنه قال : إن قبول التوبة عن الكفر مقطوع به تفضلا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p158/r6] verses 11:114 | Al-Nasafī, Ṣadr al-Sharīʿa, and other Sunni scholars allow punishment for minor sins even when major sins are avoided, citing 4:48 and 18:49; the Muʿtazila deny it based on 4:31, which may concern unbelief. «أن العقاب على الصغائر جائز الوقوع سواء إجتنب مرتكبها الكبائر أم لا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p159/r1] verses 11:114 | The author of al-Dhakhāʾir argues that some good deeds expiate minor and major sins, citing reports promising forgiveness of past and future sins; Ibn al-Mundhir leans this way. «وذهب صاحب الذخائر إلى أن من الحسنات ما يكفر الصغائر والكبائر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p159/r2] verses 11:114 | Critics call broad expiation ignorance and agreement with the Murjiʾa, arguing repentance would be pointless; al-Ālūsī replies that expiation differs from Murjiʾism and repentance remains obligatory for new and recurring delay sins. «إن الكبائر والصغائر تكفرها الطهارة والصلاة لظاهر الأحاديث»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p159/r3] verses 11:114 | Past expiation does not remove the ongoing duty to repent, just as a sleeper whose prayer time passed must still make it up; the Abū Hurayra report is specific and cannot be extended by analogy. «ولا يلزم من تكفير الله تعالى ذنوب عبده سقوط التكليف بالتوبة التي كلف بها تكليفا مستمرا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p159/r4] verses 11:114 | Repentance for minor sins is obligatory according to al-Ashʿarī, with al-Juwaynī and al-Anṣārī reporting consensus; ʿIzz al-Dīn ibn ʿAbd al-Salām says delay incurs renewed sin over time. «التوبة من الصغائر واجبة على ما نقل عن الأشعري وحكى إمام الحرمين وتلميذه الأنصاري الإجماع عليه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p160/r1] verses 11:114 | The reminder benefits those who heed admonition, singled out because they are the ones who benefit. «أي عظة للمتعظين وخصهم بالذكر لأنهم المنتفعون بها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p160/r2] verses 11:114 | Al-Zamakhsharī says ‘that’ refers to the counsel about uprightness, avoiding tyranny and inclination toward wrongdoers, and establishing prayer at the named times; Abū Ḥayyān prefers prayer alone. «والإشارة إلى ما تقدم من الوصية بالإستقامة والنهي عن الطغيان والركون إلى الذين ظلموا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p160/r3] verses 11:114 | Al-Ṭabarī takes the reference as the sūrah’s commands and prohibitions; others identify the Qurʾān, or prayer and repentance. «إشارة إلى الأوامر والنواهي في هذه السورة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p168#2/r10] verses 11:114 | Al-Wasiti says the lights of acts of obedience remove the darkness of acts of disobedience. «قال الواسطي : أنوار الطاعات تذهب بظلم المعاصي»
