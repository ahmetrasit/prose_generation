## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v1p215#2/r1] author | holds | The heavens and earth are created with marvels that human minds cannot fully comprehend; the heavens are plural because they differ in kind or have separate orbits, while earths share the substance of soil.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#2/r2] ابن التمجيد | reports | In the philosophers' account, each heaven's convex surface touches the concave surface above it, except the ninth sphere; authorities disagree whether the space above it is void.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#2/r3] author | holds | Night and day alternate, each following the other, and vary in length, darkness, and light.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#2/r4] author | holds | Ships stay afloat despite their weight and the water's lightness, and move back and forth with a single wind; الفلك is a collective singular with feminine agreement.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#2/r5] author | holds | The ships carry useful goods: merchants profit from transport, and recipients benefit from what arrives.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#2/r6] author | holds | The rain's origin is the direction above; 'heaven' can mean the celestial sphere or anything elevated, including clouds, and the phrase introduces water as a type.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r1] author | holds | Rain revives the land with plants, flowers, and trees; its death is the loss of crops and leaves as dryness takes hold.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r10] عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص | reports | Ibn ʿAmr counts eight winds: four bring mercy, while the barren and violent winds bring punishment on land or sea.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r11] author | holds | Clouds are a divinely governed kind that moves quickly through the air; the noun is singular though treated collectively, and can be understood by singular or plural agreement.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r2] ابن الشيخ | reports | The land's new beauty and growth are likened to an animal's life because both bring freshness, splendor, and increase.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r3] author | holds | Creatures are spread across the land after rain revives it, since fertility and rain sustain their growth and life.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r4] author | holds | Wind rotation includes changes in direction, warmth, force, and effects; another reported interpretation distinguishes winds of mercy from winds of punishment.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas calls wind and water God's greatest armies and links the name الريح to its refreshing effect on people.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r6] وكيع الجراح | reports | Wakiʿ says that without wind and flies the world would become foul-smelling.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r7] شريح القاضي | reports | Shurayh says each wind either heals someone ill or makes someone healthy ill.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r8] بكر بن عباس | reports | Bakr says the four winds prepare every cloud drop: the east wind stirs it, the south gathers it, the west fertilizes it, and the north disperses it.
[BURSEVI:v1p215#3/r9] author | holds | The four primary winds come from the cardinal directions; winds between their bearings are called nakbāʾ because they veer from the primary courses.
[BURSEVI:v1p216/r1] author | holds | The cloud's suspension itself is wondrous: if light it should rise, and if dense it should fall.
[BURSEVI:v1p216/r2] author | holds | The indefinite plural 'signs' magnifies their number and greatness; the delayed predicate permits the emphatic lām, and rational observers read these signs with minds and hearts.
[BURSEVI:v1p216/r3] author | holds | People who reflect on these phenomena infer God's immense power and wisdom, recognize Him as creator, and affirm His oneness.
[BURSEVI:v1p216/r4] author | holds | The verse rebukes polytheists who demanded a confirming sign: these changes already suffice, so their failure to recognize them shows poor judgment.
[BURSEVI:v1p216/r5] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet warns that woe awaits whoever recites this verse without taking it to heart; the author explains the image as failing to consider it.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r1] أبو الضحى | reports | Al-Bayhaqi transmits an interrupted report that the Quraysh had 360 idols around the Kaaba; on hearing the verse they demanded a recognizable proof, and it was revealed in response.
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r2] author | holds | Al-Ālūsī says the polytheists' extreme ignorance kept them from accepting the comprehensive proof already implied by the verse's two descriptions.
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r3] author | holds | The heavens are plural because people benefit from all their parts, including starlight, whereas the earth is singular because only the one earth they observe benefits them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r4] أبو حيان | reports | Abū Ḥayyān says earth is not pluralized because the plural is heavy and would oppose the pattern; some singulars have no Qurʾanic plural and some plurals no singular.
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r5] بعض المحققين | reports | A scholarly view says the seven heavens are distinct personal layers, whether touching as philosophers hold or separated by journeys of five hundred years in reports; differences in their effects indicate different natures. | mentions 2:29, 41:12
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r6] author | holds | Night and day may mean their alternation, each succeeding the other, or their internal variation in length, increase and decrease, darkness and light.
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r7] author | reports | Night is mentioned before day either because it was created first or because it is more noble.
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r8] author | holds | The phrase about ships is coordinated either with the creation of the heavens or with night and day, not with the word heavens alone.
[ALUSI-FULL:v2p30/r9] author | holds | Fulk is used for both singular and plural; its vowel may be original as in qufl or incidental as in asad, and the syntax can reveal number. | mentions 36:41, 10:22

[ALUSI-FULL:v2p31/r2] author | holds | In bimā yanfaʿu al-nās, mā can be a verbal noun, meaning by their benefiting people, or a relative pronoun, meaning what benefits them; a descriptive mā poorly fits the argument.

[ALUSI-FULL:v2p31/r4] author | reports | The rain clause is coordinated with ships; its later placement despite its broader benefit may give it extra emphasis, while another account explains the ship mention as a route to knowledge of the sea's wonders.
