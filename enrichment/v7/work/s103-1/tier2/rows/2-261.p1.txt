## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p338#2/r1] author | holds | God multiplies the reward for whoever He wills, not every giver, because givers' circumstances differ; alternatively, He may increase the seven hundredfold further for those who merit it.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p338/r1] author | holds | The parable omits a genitive: either the givers' expenditure is like a seed, or the givers are like someone sowing a seed; God truly causes growth, though it is attributed to seed, earth, or water as causes.

[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p338/r3] author | holds | The image is found in millet and sorghum, and wheat stalks can sometimes branch to that yield in fertile fields; even if unattested, a hypothetical image is valid.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r1] author | holds | The verse presents spending in God’s cause as a noble example and urges it; it concerns voluntary spending, and God’s cause includes all obedient acts benefiting Muslims and the community, especially jihad.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r10] author | holds | Sunbula derives from the crop’s sending out its contents and has the plural sanābil.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r11] author | holds | The syntax elides a possessive: the likeness is of the spending of those who give, or of the owner of a grain; al-Ṭabarī takes the hundred grains as conditional on finding such an ear.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r12] الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk glosses “a hundred grains in each ear” as each ear producing a hundred grains; al-Ṭabarī treats this as equivalent to his own reading, but Ibn ʿAṭiyya says that is not required by al-Ḍaḥḥāk’s wording.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r13] بعضهم | reports | Some readers place the accusative on “a hundred grains,” construing it as the object of “it produced.”
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r2] author | holds | Grain is a generic term for what people cultivate and eat, especially wheat; al-Mutalammis’s poem illustrates the word.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r3] المتلمس | reports | Al-Mutalammis uses ḥabb to mean grain, eaten by weevils in the village.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r4] author | holds | A wheat ear can contain a hundred grains, though other crops may produce more; the example simply uses this figure.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r5] author | holds | Other Qur’anic passages promise tenfold reward for good deeds; spending in jihad earns seven hundredfold, as a sound report explains.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r6] طائفة | reports | One group takes “God multiplies for whom He wills” to restate and reinforce the seven hundredfold, with no increase beyond it.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r7] طائفة من العلماء | reports | Another group says God may multiply the reward beyond seven hundredfold.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r8] ابن عباس | reports | A report attributes to Ibn ʿAbbās a multiplication up to two million, but Ibn ʿAṭiyya says its chain is not established.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p355#2/r9] ابن عمر | reports | Ibn ʿUmar reports that when this verse came down, the Prophet asked for more reward for his community; 57:11 and then 39:10 followed, after he asked again. | mentions 57:11, 39:10

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:2:261/r1] author | holds | Nabat refers to plants or vegetation; anbata is to make something grow, and related forms name adolescence, the poppy tree, and other growth.
[TABRISI:2:261/r2] author | holds | Sunbula follows a pattern related to sending something down; a grain-bearing ear is so called because its contents are covered like a hanging curtain.
[TABRISI:2:261/r3] author | holds | The parable can compare charitable spending with a grain, or compare the spenders with a sower; سبيل الله includes jihad and all forms of righteousness in the general view transmitted from Abu Abd Allah and chosen by Abu Ali al-Jubba'i.
[TABRISI:2:261/r4] author | reports | A narrower view restricts spending in God's path here to jihad; on that view other devotional acts receive tenfold reward rather than this multiplication.
[TABRISI:2:261/r5] author | holds | The image promises seven hundredfold reward, with God able to multiply even further or to grant this increase to whomever he wills.
