## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v6p4066#2/r1] author | holds | Sukr has four lexical senses: an intoxicating drink; food or flavor; stillness or quiet; and the verbal noun for becoming confused or blocked, including blocked water channels.
[JISHUMI:v6p4066#2/r2] الشاعر | reports | The cited line ‘they made the best people's grapes sukr’ uses sukr to mean food or flavor.
[JISHUMI:v6p4066#2/r3] الشاعر | reports | The cited phrases use sukr for stillness, including a calm night, a wind that has settled, and the eye of a slaughtered camel.
[JISHUMI:v6p4066#2/r5] author | holds | The pronoun in منه refers to an omitted ‘thing’: from the fruits people take an intoxicant; رزقا حسنا may be coordinated with it, as Abū Muslim says, or supplied by a separate verb.
[JISHUMI:v6p4071/r3] مشايخنا | holds | They infer that a cooked drink is permissible: since the verse counts sukr as a blessing but wine is forbidden, sukr here must be cooked drink; an abrogation claim is unnecessary when reconciliation works.
