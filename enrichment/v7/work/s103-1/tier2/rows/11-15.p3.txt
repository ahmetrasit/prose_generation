## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p310#2/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas says ostentatious people receive in this world the reward for good deeds done solely for worldly gain, such as fasting, prayer, or night vigil, but their work is void in the Hereafter; Mujahid and al-Dahhak also report this view.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p310#2/r2] أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas and Hasan say these verses were revealed about Jews and Christians.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p310#2/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid and others identify the people addressed as those who act for show.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p310#2/r4] قتادة | reports | Qatada says those whose goal and intention are worldly receive their good deeds' reward here, then arrive with no rewarded good deed; believers are rewarded both here and in the Hereafter. | mentions 17:18, 17:19, 17:20, 17:21, 42:20

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v3p511#2/r7] author | holds | Those whose deeds seek worldly life and adornment receive their full worldly recompense, such as status and wealth, without reduction; their bodies, souls, and religions are still impoverished in both worlds.
[BIQAI-FULL:v3p511#2/r8] author | holds | This worldly recompense explains why punishment is withheld during their period of respite despite their wrongdoing.

## DURR-FULL: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[DURR-FULL:v4p405/r6] أنس | reports | Anas says the promise about those seeking worldly life and its adornment was revealed concerning Jews and Christians.

[DURR-FULL:v4p405/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas glosses the desired life as its reward and adornment as its wealth; God fully grants health, family joy, money, and children, a rule he says was abrogated by 17:18. | mentions 17:18
[DURR-FULL:v4p407#2/r1] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet describes three groups among his community: sincere worshippers enter Paradise, while those who worship for worldly gain or to be seen are sent to Hell.
[DURR-FULL:v4p407#2/r2] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says hypocritical people will see Paradise's scent and palaces, then be denied its reward and taste painful punishment because they performed before people but disregarded God in private.
[DURR-FULL:v4p407#2/r3] سعيد بن جبير | reports | The worldly seeker receives the reward of his deed here but nothing in the Hereafter; Sa'id compares this to the verse about usury not growing with God. | mentions 30:39
[DURR-FULL:v4p407#2/r4] قتادة | reports | Qatada says whoever makes worldly life his sole concern is repaid good deeds here but has no good in the Hereafter; a believer may be rewarded in both worlds, without being wronged.

[DURR-FULL:v4p407/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says even prayer, fasting, and night worship can be done to obtain worldly benefit; its seeker receives that benefit, but the deed is null and he loses in the Hereafter.
[DURR-FULL:v4p407/r3] سعيد بن جبير | reports | Sa'id ibn Jubayr defines the person as one who acts for worldly gain without seeking God.
[DURR-FULL:v4p407/r4] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says the verse was revealed about the polytheists.
[DURR-FULL:v4p407/r5] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid identifies the people described as those who show off in worship.
[DURR-FULL:v4p407/r6] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says the first judged include a Qur'an reciter, a wealthy donor, and a slain fighter; each is condemned because he acted for public praise and is sent to Hell. Al-Tirmidhi grades the report hasan.

[DURR-FULL:v4p408/r2] ميمون بن مهران | reports | A person's standing with God is reflected in their deeds; the believer may be rewarded in both worlds, while the unbeliever receives worldly reward only.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p23#2/r1] author | holds | The verse describes those whose good-looking deeds seek worldly life and its adornments, such as health, security, wealth, children, and status, with كَان suggesting persistent intent and little concern for the afterlife.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p23#2/r2] author | prefers | They receive the full recompense of their deeds in this life; the phrase omits a genitive meaning their rewards, rather than using deeds metaphorically for rewards.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p23#2/r3] author | holds | The verb نوف includes the sense of conveying, which explains its construction with إلى even though it normally takes a direct object.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p23#2/r4] طلحة بن ميمون | reports | Ṭalḥa ibn Maymūn reads يوف with yāʾ and the verb attributed to God; Zayd ibn ʿAlī uses a light form, and a passive reading raises أعمالهم.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p23#2/r5] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan reads نوفي without the final yāʾ in the jussive, explained through Arabic deletion patterns or the weakened force of a distant conditional response.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p23#2/r6] الفَرّاء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ says كان is extra and accounts for the jussive response; Abū Ḥayyān objects that the actual conditional verb يريد would then have to be jussive.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p24/r1] author | holds | لا يبخسون means their worldly recompense is not reduced; the pronoun most naturally refers to worldly life, though another view refers it to the deeds.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p24/r2] author | holds | The wording presents full recompense as payment of a right and non-reduction as avoiding injustice, although the recipients have no claim to it; the promise remains governed by God's wise will. | mentions 17:18
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p24/r3] الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib defines bakhs as an unjust reduction, which heightens the denial of any shortfall.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p24/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās is quoted by al-Naḥḥās as saying that the verse of worldly recompense abrogated the present verse; al-Ālūsī says abrogation of reports is impossible and treats the statement as loose usage.

## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:11:15/r1] author | holds | The verse concerns people whose sole intention is worldly life and its adornment, and who work toward that end.
[ELMALILI:11:15/r2] author | holds | God fully repays such people’s deeds in this world: their effort is not shorted or delayed, and they receive at least the value of their work. This does not mean every desired outcome is granted, since the promise concerns deeds, not wishes.
[ELMALILI:11:15/r3] author | holds | People are created to compete and be tested in deeds, and every act yields a good or bad consequence; the author links this to the promise that effort will be seen. | mentions 53:40
[ELMALILI:11:15/r4] author | holds | A diver may fail to find the pearl sought but still accomplish the dive, learning the skill or suffering an accident; the true fruit is the outcome God assigns to the act.
[ELMALILI:11:15/r5] author | holds | Because those whose highest aim is transient worldly life have exhausted their deeds’ reward within this life, their recompense cannot extend into the everlasting hereafter.
