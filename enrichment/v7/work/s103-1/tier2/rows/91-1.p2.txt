## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v10p340#3/r1] author | holds | The sun's duha is its spreading light when it rises, its power becomes manifest, and its radiance extends over the earth.
[BURSEVI:v10p340#3/r2] author | holds | In the inward reading, the sun is the spirit and its light spreads through the body and shines upon the self.
[BURSEVI:v10p341#2/r1] author | holds | Although the conditional sense of idha seems to require an answer and an operator, the author reads it causally in these oaths: swear because the moon follows, the day reveals, and the night covers; al-Qamus also allows a present-time meaning, “when.”
[BURSEVI:v10p341#2/r7] author | prefers | The oath wa's are conjunctive and share the implied verb, avoiding the problem of coordinating words governed by different agents or multiplying oaths with one answer.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p140/r1] ابن عباس | reports | A report from Ibn Abbas, authenticated by al-Hakim, explains duha as the sun's light when it has risen and its dominion appears.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p140/r2] author | holds | Strictly, duha is when the sun moves visibly away from the eastern horizon; its successive times are called dhuwa, duha, then extended duhaʾ. Attributed to the sun, it figuratively means its shining.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p140/r3] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad reportedly derives duha from ḍaḥḥ, the sun's light, and treats the alif and wāw in related forms as reversed from a second ḥāʾ.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p140/r4] أبو حيان | rejects | Abu Hayyan suspects that this derivation was falsely attributed to al-Mubarrad, since the two forms have different roots and one is not derived from the other.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p140/r5] author | reports | A reply says the claim was not about small-scale derivation; Alusi remarks that even a novice knows the difference.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p140/r6] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil explains duha as the sun's heat, by reference to its necessary effect; he also says it means the whole day, which Alusi considers a remote interpretation for this oath.

## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:91:1/r1] author | holds | Duha literally describes the sun rising above the visible horizon and its light spreading; by extension it names the morning period, with dahwa, duha, and daha marking successively later hours.
[ELMALILI:91:1/r2] müfessirîn | reports | Because the word is attached to the sun, exegetes interpret its duha as the sun’s radiance as it shines.
[ELMALILI:91:1/r3] author | holds | The oath first invokes the sun in all its properties and then especially its light when it appears.
[ELMALILI:91:1/r4] author | holds | The sun drives major movements and transformations in the celestial system and life, both at rising and setting; its light makes the physical world visible and reveals its Creator’s care and power.
[ELMALILI:91:1/r5] author | holds | Heat and light are its clearest benefits; both are felt especially at duha, and the sun’s heat connects to the preceding surah’s fire while light is what first strikes the eye. | mentions 90:20
[ELMALILI:91:1/r6] author | holds | Light presents external forms to conscious sight; through reflection between the world and the self, together with other senses, it develops spiritual understanding and guides recognition of God.
[ELMALILI:91:1/r7] author | holds | This illumination can lead purified souls to return contentedly to God and attain success under the call to enter among His servants and Paradise. | mentions 89:28, 89:29, 89:30
[ELMALILI:91:1/r8] author | holds | Light becomes fully meaningful in conscious perception, joining external and internal experience; it models how awareness links people to what lies beyond them, while creatures without consciousness have no concept of sun or light.
[ELMALILI:91:1/r9] author | holds | The wording draws attention to the sun’s illuminating and guiding role, then moves through changing light and darkness, the heavens and earth, and the soul’s inspired good and evil toward purification and success.

## QUTB-ZILAL: Sayyid Quṭb, d. 1386 AH
[QUTB-ZILAL:91:1/r1] author | holds | This short Meccan surah’s unified rhyme and rhythm frame its central truth: the human soul’s innate capacities, the person’s role in shaping it, and responsibility for its destiny; the Thamud story models failure to purify it. | mentions 91:7, 91:8, 91:9, 91:10, 91:11, 91:12, 91:13, 91:14
[QUTB-ZILAL:91:1/r2] author | holds | God’s oath gives cosmic scenes and the soul special worth and invites reflection on their meaning; creation and the human heart communicate through an innate, wordless spiritual language.
[QUTB-ZILAL:91:1/r3] قول مروي | reports | A reported interpretation takes al-duha to mean the entire daytime.
[QUTB-ZILAL:91:1/r4] author | prefers | He sees no need to leave the nearby, specific meaning of the bright morning hours, whose distinctive quality carries its own significance.

## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p365/r1] author | holds | The surah is usually called al-Shams; al-Bukhārī's title quotes its first verse, a name preferred to prevent confusion with al-Takwīr, and al-Itqān does not list it among surahs with multiple names.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p365/r2] author | holds | The surah is Meccan by agreement, twenty-sixth in revelation after al-Qadr and before al-Burūj; most regions count fifteen verses, while Meccans count sixteen.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p365/r3] author | holds | The surah warns idolaters that they may soon suffer the punishment that struck Thamūd for shirk and defiance of their messenger.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p365/r4] author | holds | Its majestic oaths draw evidence of God's unique divinity from the artistry of the sun, moon, heavens, earth, and the soul's paths toward guidance or ruin.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p366/r1] author | holds | The oath confirms the threat of destruction implicitly conveyed by the news; each waw following a pause is an oath particle, and the sun, moon, sky, earth, and human soul display God's wise and powerful creation.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p366/r2] author | holds | The dawn brightness, moon following the sun, daylight, and night are all examples of the fine order God established.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p366/r3] author | holds | Ḍuḥā is when the sun rises above the eastern horizon and its rays appear, at roughly the height that looks like a spear's length.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p366/r4] author | holds | The oath is prepared by noting that purifying the soul leads to success, while neglecting its reform leads to immorality and loss.
