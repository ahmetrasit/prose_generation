## TAB: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB:101:8/r1] author | holds | Those whose good deeds weigh little have the Hawiya as their abode, where they fall headfirst in Hell.
[TAB:101:8/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatāda says the Hawiya is the Fire, the sinners' refuge; the phrase can also be an Arabic exclamation for a person in dire trouble.
[TAB:101:8/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatāda explains هوت أمه as a familiar Arabic phrase used when someone faces a severe predicament.
[TAB:101:8/r4] الأشعث بن عبد الله الأعمى | reports | Al-Ashʿath describes believers' spirits welcoming a dead believer from worldly grief; asked about the dead, they say they were taken to their mother, the Hawiya.
[TAB:101:8/r5] أبو صالح | reports | Abū Ṣāliḥ says they fall headfirst into the Fire.
[TAB:101:8/r6] قتادة | reports | Qatāda also says the person falls into the Fire headfirst.
[TAB:101:8/r7] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd explains that the Fire is called the person's mother because it is his refuge and the place to which he returns. | mentions 3:151
[TAB:101:8/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the Fire is made his mother because, with no other home, it shelters him as a woman shelters her child.

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v10p274#3/r5] author | holds | For the one whose scales are light, his destination and home are Hell.
[THALABI:v10p274#3/r6] قتادة | reports | He says hāwat ummuh is a familiar Arabic expression used when someone falls into a severe predicament.
[THALABI:v10p274#3/r7] بعضهم | reports | Some interpret ‘his mother’ as his head, meaning the people fall headfirst into Hell; Qatada and Abu Salih adopted this interpretation.
[THALABI:v10p274#3/r8] أنس بن مالك | reports | He reports an angel weighing each person’s deeds; a heavy scale brings a public proclamation of lasting felicity, while a light one brings lasting misery.

## QUSHAYRI: al-Qushayrī, d. 465 AH

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:101:7-9/r1] author | holds | The balance has a pan and a tongue on which written records of good and bad deeds are weighed; Qurtubi discussed it under earlier surahs and in his Remembrance book.
[QURTUBI-FULL:101:7-9/r2] author | reports | One interpretation says there is a single balance in Gabriel's hand weighing human deeds, expressed in plural form.
[QURTUBI-FULL:101:7-9/r3] author | reports | Another interpretation posits multiple balances, supported by a poet's line about each event having its balance.
[QURTUBI-FULL:101:7-9/r4] عبد العزيز بن يحيى | reports | Abd al-Aziz ibn Yahya takes the balances as proofs and arguments, citing a poet who says he had a balance for every disputant.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v10p386/r1] التأويلات النجمية | reports | The person whose weighed qualities are divine attributes and godly character traits is at rest from the consequences of those qualities.
[BURSEVI:v10p386/r10] author | holds | Both groups commit sins, though the first has fewer and outweighed sins and the second has many; the trial remains, so the Prophet tells ‘Ali to follow a bad deed with a good one, in keeping with God's forgiving name.
[BURSEVI:v10p386/r11] author | holds | God's scale reverses ordinary scales: rising and elevation are heaviness, while falling is lightness; weighty deeds are enduring good works, and valueless light deeds are passing sensual pleasures.
[BURSEVI:v10p386/r2] author | holds | Scales are light when a person has no good deed of weight or when sins outweigh good deeds.
[BURSEVI:v10p386/r3] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Mas‘ud says one extra good deed leads to Paradise, while one extra bad deed leads to Hell.
[BURSEVI:v10p386/r9] التأويلات النجمية | reports | The inward reading makes the origin of those with light scales an abyss of veiling and a hot fire of ignorance, blindness, ego, passion, Satan, and the world.

## ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p421/r4] author | holds | Only deeds will help: God weighs them in a just balance, granting eternal bliss when good deeds outweigh evil and Hell when the scales tip the other way.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p425/r1] author | holds | The balance gives weight only to truth, meaning good deeds; the light-scaled lose because they denied God’s revelations. | mentions 7:8, 7:9
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p425/r2] author | holds | The relative singular man in the scale descriptions denotes a plural group.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p425/r4] author | holds | Those whose evil deeds have no weight face eternal Hell; any good deeds are nullified by ill intent and heretical belief, and the next verse identifies the abyss as blazing fire.
