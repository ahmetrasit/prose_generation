## FARRA: al-Farrāʾ, d. 207 AH

[FARRA:v2p72/r5] author | holds | The genitive reading أعمالهم كرماد is grammatically possible, though the author has not heard it in recitation; poetry illustrates an implied preposition and a fronted noun.
[FARRA:v2p73/r1] author | holds | عاصف may describe the day because wind occurs then, just as a day may be called cold or hot; a poem similarly calls days cloudy or sunny.
[FARRA:v2p73/r2] author | holds | Alternatively عاصف الريح may omit الريح because it was already mentioned; if عاصف modifies wind alone, it may still take the day's case by proximity.

[FARRA:v2p73/r5] author | prefers | The author prefers رفع المتين; the cited poem shows a genitive word attracted to a neighboring noun despite its syntactic role.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v13p622/r1] بعض نحويي البصرة | reports | A Basran grammarian supplies an omitted phrase, “among what We recount is the likeness of the disbelievers,” comparing the construction to 13:35. | mentions 13:35
[TAB-FULL:v13p622/r2] بعض نحويي الكوفيين | reports | Kufan grammarians make the deeds the subject of the simile, with the meaning “the deeds of those who disbelieved are like ashes”; they discuss parallel word order and case in 39:60 and 2:217. | mentions 39:60, 2:217, 13:35
[TAB-FULL:v13p622/r3] author | holds | The verse compares disbelievers’ worldly deeds, which they thought were for God, to ash blown away; their mixed idolatrous works cannot benefit them at Judgment because they were not done purely for God.
[TAB-FULL:v13p622/r4] author | holds | The far-off error is the disbelievers’ deeds: they were done without guidance or sound direction, far from the straight path.
[TAB-FULL:v13p622/r5] author | holds | عاصف is grammatically attached to “day,” describing it by the wind that occurs in it, like calling a day hot or cold.
[TAB-FULL:v13p623/r1] author | holds | The wording may mean “on a stormy-wind day,” with الريح omitted because it was already mentioned; poetry likewise omits a noun after يوم.
[TAB-FULL:v13p623/r2] author | holds | Alternatively, عاصف describes الريح but follows the case of the nearby word يوم; Arabic poetry and the phrase “a ruined lizard’s burrow” show case attraction.
[TAB-FULL:v13p623/r3] ابن جريج | reports | Ibn Jurayj says the wind carried the ashes on the stormy day.
[TAB-FULL:v13p623/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says those who worshiped other than God find none of their deeds useful at Judgment, just as wind makes ashes uncontrollable.

## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH

[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p128#2/r3] author | reports | In في يوم عاصف, the day is described as stormy because the wind occurs on that day, like calling a day cold or hot.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p128#2/r4] author | reports | Alternatively, في يوم عاصف means a day of stormy wind, with الريح omitted because wind was already mentioned.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p128#2/r5] author | reports | A third grammatical explanation keeps عاصف as an adjective of the previously mentioned wind, but its later position makes it follow the case of اليوم.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p128#2/r6] author | reports | They cannot obtain any reward in the afterlife for their good works in this world because disbelief nullified those works.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p128#2/r7] author | reports | Alternatively, they cannot benefit in the afterlife from worldly possessions acquired through their sins.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p128#2/r8] author | holds | Their error is called far-reaching because death makes it impossible to repair or compensate for it.
