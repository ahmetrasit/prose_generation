## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p148#3/r1] أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي وابن عباس | reports | Al-Sulami and others, and in a report from Ibn Abbas, take al-husna as the statement “there is no god but God”; another view includes any truth-bearing word.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p148#3/r2] عكرمة وجماعة وابن عباس | reports | Ikrima and a group, also reported from Ibn Abbas, take al-husna as the good religion of Islam.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p148#3/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid identifies al-husna as Paradise; another view takes it as recompense in general.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p148#3/r4] author | prefers | Alusi prefers a comprehensive reading: giving signifies worship, taqwa includes all good actions and avoiding sins, and belief in al-husna means faith in tawhid and all required beliefs.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p148#3/r5] أبو قحافة | reports | A report authenticated by al-Hakim says Abu Quhafa questioned Abu Bakr for freeing weak slaves instead of strong men who could protect him; Abu Bakr said he acted for God's sake, and the verses were revealed. | mentions 92:19, 92:20, 92:21
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p148#3/r6] عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿud reports that Abu Bakr bought and freed Bilal from Umayya ibn Khalaf; the surah's opening verses were then revealed. A similar occasion is related for Abu al-Dahdah. | mentions 92:1, 92:4
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p148#3/r7] author | holds | Mentioning belief after taqwa singles out the specific after the general and maintains the rhyme; another suggestion is that listening to learn monotheism is part of giving obedience and avoiding shirk part of taqwa, but Alusi rejects it.

[ELMALILI:92:5-11#2/r1] author | holds | The second reason for mentioning giving and taqwa first is that effects and recompense follow their causes and deeds; al-husnā is their resulting goal.
[ELMALILI:92:5-11/r10] author | holds | The believer expects the afterlife to be better, remains optimistic about good and pessimistic about evil, and accepts hardship for virtue because it ends in happiness.

[ELMALILI:92:5-11/r5] author | reports | Interpretations of al-husnā include the best trait such as faith or ihsan, the best word such as tawhid or the Qur'an, the best religion of Islam, or the best recompense and Paradise.
