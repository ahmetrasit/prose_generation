## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:2:261-274#7/r7] علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام | reports | Al-Durr al-Manthūr reports ʿAlī explaining good earnings as gold and silver, and earthly produce as grain, dates, and everything subject to zakat.
[TABATABAI:2:261-274#7/r8] البراء بن عازب | reports | Al-Barāʾ reports that the verse was revealed about some Anṣār who hung poor clusters of dates for the hungry Ahl al-Ṣuffa; after the warning, they brought their good dates instead. Al-Tirmidhī's report is authenticated.
[TABATABAI:2:261-274#8/r1] الصادق عليه السلام | reports | A report in al-Kāfī says some gave the worst dates, jaʿrūr and maʿāfāra, as zakat from good palms; the Prophet told them not to estimate those palms or bring their produce, and explained accepting such fruit would mean overlooking its defects.
[TABATABAI:2:261-274#8/r2] أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Another report says people who acquired ill-gotten wealth in the pre-Islamic period wanted to donate it after Islam, but God required them to give from the best of their earnings.
[TABATABAI:2:261-274/r1] author | holds | The connected themes suggest this passage was revealed as one unit: spending grows seven hundredfold or more, sincere giving bears fruit, ostentation and reminders or harm ruin reward, good wealth should be given, the poor detained in God's path are recipients, and the reward is great.
[TABATABAI:2:261-274/r2] author | holds | The passage orders its teaching around purpose (giving for God, not people), manner (without reproach or injury), quality (good rather than bad wealth), recipient (the needy constrained in God's path), and reward.
[TABATABAI:2:266-267/r10] author | holds | The ending either tells donors to consider that God praises good giving despite His wealth, or that a rich and praiseworthy God should not be approached with an unworthy gift.
[TABATABAI:2:266-267/r7] author | holds | Tayammum means intending, khabīth is the opposite of good, and the grammatical circumstantial clauses attach respectively to the subjects of 'intend' and 'spend'.
[TABATABAI:2:266-267/r8] author | holds | The exception means that people would accept such poor produce only while overlooking its defects, or while bearing the discomfort of doing so.
[TABATABAI:2:266-267/r9] author | holds | God requires good earnings, not the worthless property one would reluctantly accept; giving the latter looks like disposal rather than generosity and cultivates neither affection nor virtue.
