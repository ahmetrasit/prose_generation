## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:64:1-10#3/r16] author | holds | God will gather people for the Day of Gathering, the Day of Mutual Loss, when who gained and who lost becomes clear.
[ELMALILI:64:1-10#4/r1] author | holds | Taghābun derives from gabn: losing or deceiving in a transaction or judgment, by selling or buying below or above value; mutual deception becomes manifest.
[ELMALILI:64:1-10#4/r10] author | prefers | The author prefers a definition broad enough to include both reciprocal and one-sided deception: the day real profit and loss are realized.
[ELMALILI:64:1-10#4/r11] author | holds | Calling it mutual loss ironically addresses worldly people consumed by love of wealth who aim to deceive others; only the greatest final gain and loss are truly decisive.
[ELMALILI:64:1-10#4/r12] author | holds | Whoever believes in God and does good for that day has sins covered and enters eternal gardens; the greatest triumph is safety and supreme divine pleasure.
[ELMALILI:64:1-10#4/r2] author | holds | The source distinguishes gabn in commerce, pronounced with a silent bāʾ, from gaban in judgment, with both consonants vocalized.
[ELMALILI:64:1-10#4/r3] Râgıb | reports | Raghib defines gabn as a concealed imbalance in dealings and says the Day of Mutual Loss reveals whether spiritual transactions gained or lost. | mentions 2:207, 9:111, 3:77
