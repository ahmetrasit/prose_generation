## MUJAHID: Mujāhid b. Jabr, d. 104 AH
[MUJAHID:v2p770/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the best form is the most upright creation, the return to the lowest is decrepit old age, and the reward of the faithful is undiminished.
[MUJAHID:v2p770/r2] ابن عباس | reports | The report says a believer who reaches decrepit old age continues to receive the reward of good deeds from youth and health; old-age actions do not harm him and sins after decrepitude are not recorded against him.
[MUJAHID:v2p770/r3] ابن عباس | reports | A second report from Ibn Abbas adds that the believer is not held accountable for sins committed after returning to decrepit old age.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v24p514/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas recounts that some people in the Prophet's time became senile; when asked about their lost understanding, God excused them and preserved the reward earned before their minds failed.

[TAB-FULL:v24p517/r2] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says a person who recites the Quran is not returned to decrepit old age, and reads the phrase as senility so severe that one no longer knows after having known.

[TAB-FULL:v24p517/r4] author | holds | Al-Tabari allows a disconnected exception: even after being returned to the lowest state, believers who did righteous deeds retain their reward.
[TAB-FULL:v24p519/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says a believer who becomes unable to continue a righteous practice in old age keeps receiving its reward until death.
[TAB-FULL:v24p519/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says obedience maintained throughout youth earns the same reward after the believer grows senile, without liability for acts committed after losing reason.
[TAB-FULL:v24p519/r3] إبراهيم | reports | Ibrahim says a believer who reaches decrepit age receives the reward of the best work he did in youth and health.
[TAB-FULL:v24p520/r1] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says God pays the believer's reward or deed and does not hold him to account when old age returns him to weakness.
[TAB-FULL:v24p520/r2] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says senility does not harm an elderly person if God ends his life upon the best deeds he used to do.
[TAB-FULL:v24p520/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatadah says a person who reaches old age after doing good receives the same reward he had while able to work.
[TAB-FULL:v24p520/r4] مجاهد | reports | Another interpretation takes the descent as Hell and exempts believers from that fate; Mujahid glosses the exception as those who believe. | mentions 103:3
[TAB-FULL:v24p520/r5] الحسن | reports | Hasan reads the exception after Hell as parallel to the exception of believers from loss in Surat al-Asr. | mentions 103:2, 103:3

[TAB-FULL:v24p522/r3] إبراهيم | reports | Ibrahim also understands the reward as uncounted.

[TAB-FULL:v24p522/r5] author | prefers | Al-Tabari prefers ‘not diminished,’ meaning their reward remains as it was in health and youth; he connects the word to manīn, something weak, and cites poetry to show the gift has no shortfall or error.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
