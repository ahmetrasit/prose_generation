[ALUSI-FULL:v3p19#2/r1] الكسائي والفراء وأبو علي | reports | Most grammarians, including al-Kisāʾī, al-Farrāʾ, and Abū ʿAlī, take kāf as a noun meaning 'like,' governed by an omitted 'have you seen'; the preceding 'have you not seen' signals the omission.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p19#2/r2] الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash treats the kāf as extra, yielding a direct coordination with the earlier person who disputed Abraham.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p19#2/r3] author | reports | Other grammarians analyze the coordination semantically, as though the text said 'Have you not seen the one who argued—or the one who passed?'; another view makes it Abraham challenging his opponent, which al-Ālūsī finds weak because of the distance and ellipsis.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p19#2/r4] author | holds | Direct coordination of the relative clause with the previous relative would require a preposition before kāf; as a noun it rarely accepts one, and as a particle it cannot.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p19#2/r5] author | rejects | A proposed distinction says 'have you not seen' introduces the object of wonder while 'have you seen' introduces its likeness; al-Ālūsī rejects it because both expressions occur with and without comparison in Arabic and Qurʾanic usage.
