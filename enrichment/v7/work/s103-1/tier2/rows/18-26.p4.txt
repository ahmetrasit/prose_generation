## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p301#2/r1] author | reports | If 18:25 is God’s own duration report, 18:26 ends disputes among the People of the Book; if it quotes them, the Prophet is told to defer knowledge to God as at 18:22. | mentions 18:25, 18:22
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p301#2/r2] author | holds | The unseen of heavens and earth includes all entities and conditions hidden from people; the fronted ‘to Him’ restricts this knowledge to God, against claims to know the sleepers’ history.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p301#2/r3] author | holds | The paired exclamations marvel at God’s comprehensive knowledge of unseen sights and sounds, which no one shares.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p301#2/r4] author | holds | The denial that they have any guardian besides Him invalidates the polytheists’ claimed divine protectors through a general negative construction.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p301#2/r5] author | holds | The denial of partners in God’s judgment rebuts their claim that their idols share His sovereignty.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p301#2/r6] الجمهور | reports | Most readers use third-person indicative yushriku, whose pronoun returns to God in the preceding phrase.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p301#2/r7] ابن عامر | reports | Ibn ʿĀmir reads the verb in second person and jussive after prohibitive lā, addressing the Prophet, his community, or any listener.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p301#2/r8] author | holds | Ibn Ashur closes the sleepers’ story here and remarks that exegetes have transmitted many fabricated reports about it.

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:18:26#2/r1] author | prefers | The pronoun in to them most plausibly refers to the Cave youths, though it could refer to rational beings or everyone in heaven and earth.
[TABATABAI:18:26#2/r2] author | holds | The verse gives a general proof that God knows their duration because He owns and perceives all unseen things, plus a specific proof because He alone directly governs them.
[TABATABAI:18:26#2/r3] author | holds | The two causal proof clauses are detached rather than conjoined because each explains why God knows their duration.
[TABATABAI:18:26#3/r3] author | rejects | A report’s claim that the duration in 18:25 is the People of the Book’s statement rejected by 18:26 is incompatible with the surrounding passage and its eloquent structure.
[TABATABAI:18:26#4/r1] author | reports | Reports differ over whether the youths died or slept again and whether the three hundred and nine years came from God or the People of the Book; the broadly shared account is that monotheists fled a coercive polytheist king and slept in a cave.
[TABATABAI:18:26/r1] author | holds | God’s greater knowledge is asserted because people disputed the duration; His ownership of the unseen in heaven and earth means nothing escapes Him.
[TABATABAI:18:26/r2] author | holds | The phrase He is most seeing and hearing is an exclamatory expression of perfect perception, completing the argument for His knowledge rather than repeating it.
[TABATABAI:18:26/r3] author | rejects | The lam means exclusive ownership, not merely possession of knowledge; the alternative makes the seeing-and-hearing phrase redundant and wrongly implies other beings know all the unseen.
[TABATABAI:18:26/r4] author | holds | The first clause denies another guardian with independent authority; the following clause denies any partner in God’s judgment, including delegated administration or adjudication.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#32/r10] author | holds | The cave story circulated in detailed reports from al-Qummī, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Ibn Isḥāq, Wahb ibn Munabbih, and al-Nuʿmān ibn Bashīr, among others.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#33/r1] author | holds | The transmitted accounts disagree even on major features, while reports about dates, rulers, lineage, appearance, names, and the title أصحاب الرقيم diverge still more.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#33/r2] author | holds | One cause is the story’s prominence among People of the Book: Quraysh learned it from them, and its Christian images and monuments spread it widely, so communities reshaped it to fit their beliefs.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#33/r3] author | holds | A second cause is Muslims’ broad collection of outside reports, especially through converts such as Wahb ibn Munabbih and Kaʿb al-Aḥbār; later transmitters treated such material as prophetic reports.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#33/r4] author | holds | The Qurʾān selects key lessons that serve guidance instead of supplying a complete historical account; it is a book of guidance, not history.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#34/r1] author | holds | The Qurʾān omits names, ancestry, birth, upbringing, occupations, social standing, dates, rulers, cities, persecutors, and the dog’s identity because guidance does not depend on them; historians and early exegetes filled these gaps, producing divergent accounts.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#36/r1] أهل الرواية والتاريخ | reports | Most historians date the story between Jesus and Muḥammad; it is absent from the two Testaments, though some Muslim reports say Quraysh heard it from Jews, while Christians preserved it extensively.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#36/r2] author | holds | Christian accounts resemble Ibn Isḥāq’s report from Ibn ʿAbbās in some respects but differ from Muslims on the number of sleepers, whether they had a dog, and the length of their sleep.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#38/r4] author | prefers | After centuries as a ruined, forgotten site, Jordanian excavation uncovered Rujayb; the author considers its archaeological signs and some Muslim reports the strongest fit for the Qurʾānic cave.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#39/r1] author | holds | Amman stands on the site of Philadelphia, a major and beautiful city before Islam that remained under Roman control from the early second century until the Muslim conquest.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#39/r2] author | reports | Other proposed locations include a cave on Mount Qāsiyūn near Damascus, one at Petra, a Scandinavian cave with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and a cave near Nakhchivan.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#39/r3] author | prefers | No evidence identifies any proposed cave as the Qurʾānic one; the Scandinavian and Caucasus claims conflict with the story’s Roman setting and the limits of Roman power.

[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p457/r4] author | holds | The expressions about God's seeing and hearing intensify praise: nothing escapes Him, so no one can answer better than God.
