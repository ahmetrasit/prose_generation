## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73#2/r2] author | holds | The wording حتى زرتم المقابر sounds like a completed event; the verse mocks the thought that merely outnumbering rivals has any benefit.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73#2/r3] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report urges grave visits because they remind people of death; here the rivals visited graves because of hard hearts and immersion in worldly love.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73#2/r5] author | reports | On the wealth reading, visiting graves is a metaphor for dying; Jarir's line uses visiting graves to mean Abu Malik died.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73#2/r6] author | reports | Al-Razi raises two objections: visitors leave while the dead remain, and the past-tense wording seems incompatible with future death.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73#2/r7] author | reports | Replies say the dead too must leave for judgment; the address may concern people near death, earlier generations on whose path the living follow, or disbelievers on Judgment Day after their graves are visited.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r10] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن ابن مسعود | reports | The Prophet first forbade grave visits, then permitted them because they make people renounce the world and remember the afterlife; Ibn Mas'ud's report is in Ibn Majah.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r5] author | holds | Visiting graves means death: people become visitors to their graves and return from them to their final home in Paradise or Hell.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r6] author | reports | Another interpretation says boasting lasted until people counted the dead; another treats the verse as a warning about seeing God's punishment in the graves.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r7] author | holds | Maqabir is the plural of maqbara in either vowelled form; qubur is the plural of qabr, with additional poetic and lexical forms.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r8] author | holds | The paired verb forms distinguish burying someone from ordering that person buried; Qurtubi notes that the issue was discussed in Surah Abasa.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r9] author | holds | This is the Qur'an's only mention of graves; visiting them is a powerful cure for a hard heart because it recalls death and the afterlife, shortening worldly hopes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#3/r1] author | reports | The continuation of the Muslim report says grave visits remind people of death.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#3/r2] بريدة | reports | Al-Tirmidhi transmits from Burayda that grave visits remind people of the afterlife and grades the report Hasan Sahih.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#3/r3] أبو هريرة | reports | A report in al-Tirmidhi says the Prophet cursed women who frequently visit graves; the chapter also cites Ibn Abbas and Hassan ibn Thabit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#3/r4] أبو عيسى | reports | Abu Isa grades the report about women who frequently visit graves Hasan Sahih.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#3/r5] بعض أهل العلم | reports | Some scholars say the curse came before the Prophet later permitted grave visits, after which the permission included women as well as men.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#3/r6] بعضهم | reports | Others restrict the disapproval to women because they are thought to have less patience and greater grief.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#3/r7] author | holds | Men's grave visits are agreed upon, while women's visits are disputed; Qurtubi permits older women and allows women to go when separated from men.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#3/r8] author | holds | Where a mixed gathering risks temptation, visits are unlawful: either sex may be distracted by the other's presence and leave sinful rather than rewarded.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#4/r2] author | reports | The report transmitted by Ibn Abbas says direct experience is not like hearing about an event.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#4/r3] author | holds | A visitor should attend with a present heart, seeking God, repairing the heart, or benefiting the dead with Qur'an and prayer; merely touring graves is an animal-like activity.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#4/r4] author | holds | Visitors should avoid walking or sitting on graves, greet the cemetery and the known deceased, and face the deceased as they would a living person they address.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH

[BAYDAWI-FULL:102:1-2/r3] author | holds | Alternatively, worldly competition in wealth and children distracts people until death and burial; visiting graves is a metaphor for dying.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:102:1-4/r2] author | holds | Competition over wealth, children, and ancestry can extend even to boasting about long-dead ancestors’ bones, driven by imagination and concealing illusion.
[QASHANI:102:1-4/r3] author | reports | Alternatively, the rivalry continues until death: people spend their whole lives in it and never notice what would save them.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r1] الجمهور | reports | The surah is Meccan according to all exegetes, while al-Bukhārī calls it Madinan.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r10] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya criticizes people who embellish and raise graves with stone and marble; Abū Ḥayyān replies that he had not seen the lavish Egyptian cemeteries and their wasted wealth.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r2] الكلبي ومقاتل | reports | Al-Kalbī and Muqātil say the rival clans of Sahm and ʿAbd Manāf counted their living notables, then their dead; ʿAbd Manāf had more living, but Sahm more dead from pre-Islamic times.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the passage concerns Jews who claimed their tribes outnumbered other tribes.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r4] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says it concerns a clan among the Anṣār.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r6] author | reports | The author calls the use of visiting graves for counting the dead a sarcastic metaphor that poorly fits the ordinary sense of visiting; another reading is that rivalry consumed life until death.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r7] author | reports | An Arab and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz understood the wording literally: visitors leave rather than remain, so the people must be raised again.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r8] author | reports | Another interpretation is that they repeatedly visited graves to outdo one another in honoring and remembering the dead.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p535/r9] الرسول | reports | The Prophet first prohibited grave visits, then allowed them for taking admonition rather than boasting and rivalry.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p536/r1] author | holds | The author criticizes devotees who boast of visiting saints' tombs, preserve lengthy shrine stories without knowing purification duties, and exploit popular generosity for money and status.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p536/r2] author | holds | He adds that people with some learning imitate the popular shrine storytellers to gain wealth, prestige, and hand-kissing, and prays for God's guidance to obedience.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p472#2/r1] author | holds | Love of worldly life, its pleasures, and its adornments distracts people from seeking the afterlife until death brings them to the graves.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p472#2/r2] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report attributed to the Prophet says rivalry distracted people from obedience until death came; Ibn Kathīr gives no grading here.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p472#2/r7] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The dead person is followed by family, wealth, and deeds; family and wealth return, while deeds remain.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p473/r4] ابن بريدة | reports | Ibn Burayda reports that the sūra was revealed about Banū Ḥāritha and Banū al-Ḥārith boasting over living and dead members of their tribes at the graves.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p473/r5] قتادة | reports | Qatāda says people boasted that their tribe was more numerous than another, while their members kept dying until all became grave-dwellers.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p473/r6] author | prefers | Ibn Kathīr prefers that visiting the graves means reaching them and being buried there, citing the Prophet's reply to a sick man who said fever would send him to the graves.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p473/r7] علي | reports | ʿAlī reports that the Muslims continued to doubt punishment in the grave until this sūra was revealed; Ibn Kathīr later cites al-Tirmidhī calling the report gharīb.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p474/r1] عمر بن عبد العزيز | reports | ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz reflects that graves are only a visit, and a visitor must return to a home; Ibn Kathīr explains the destination as Paradise or Hell.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p474/r2] author | holds | Ibn Kathīr explains ʿUmar's “home” as the person's eventual home in Paradise or Hell.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p474/r3] author | reports | An unnamed Bedouin hears “until you visit the graves” and declares that the Resurrection has happened today, since a visitor will leave that place for another.
