## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#3/r6] الحسن | reports | Ibn Abi Hatim and Abu al-Shaykh transmit from Hasan that when the command to stand firm was revealed, the believers girded themselves and the Prophet was no longer seen laughing.
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#3/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says no verse was harder on the Prophet than the command to stand firm; the report explains his saying that Hud and al-Waqi'a made his hair gray.
[TABATABAI:11:109-119#3/r8] author | holds | The familiar wording is Hud and its sisters; because al-Waqi'a lacks a parallel command, the author thinks the shared reference may instead be the Resurrection.
[TABATABAI:11:112#2/r1] author | rejects | Istiqama here is full and steady performance of servitude, not merely moderation between extremes; the following ban on transgression targets excess and clarifies the command.
[TABATABAI:11:112#2/r2] author | holds | Transgression means crossing the limits of human servitude through pride and refusal to submit, as earlier peoples did; the verb evokes water exceeding its bounds.
[TABATABAI:11:112#2/r3] author | holds | And those who repent coordinates with the hidden subject of stand firm: believers who returned from shirk through faith; the Prophet is singled out to honor his office. | mentions 2:285, 66:8
[TABATABAI:11:112#2/r4] author | holds | As you were commanded applies specifically to the Prophet, because the earlier command to establish religion's direction addressed him alone. | mentions 30:30
[TABATABAI:11:112-113/r1] author | holds | The verse commands steadfastness in monotheism and servitude, requires believers to stand firm, and warns them not to cross God's limits because He sees their deeds.
[TABATABAI:11:112-113/r2] author | holds | The severe address singles out the Prophet and follows accounts of earlier nations' destruction, intensifying the warning. | mentions 17:74, 17:75
[TABATABAI:11:112/r1] author | holds | Standing signifies a person's stable, empowered state; the image extends to anything established so that it can produce its full effects, including justice, law, prayer, testimony, and religion.
[TABATABAI:11:112/r2] author | holds | Iqama sets something upright so all intended effects remain available; istiqama seeks and sustains that complete functioning, as with a straight road or a person perfecting an undertaking.
[TABATABAI:11:112/r3] author | holds | The command means steadfastly fulfilling religion and its right, maintaining monotheism in inward and outward life, as explained by earlier commands to face religion sincerely. | mentions 41:6, 41:30, 30:30
[TABATABAI:11:112/r4] صاحب روح المعاني | reports | Alusi suggests as commanded may refer to another, unrecited revelation and calls istiqama a comprehensive middle path between excess and neglect.
[TABATABAI:11:112/r5] author | rejects | He rejects an appeal to an unmentioned revelation: the Qur'an had already commanded the Prophet to establish himself upon religion, so no hidden instruction is needed.
