## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:18/r1] author | holds | They seem awake because their eyes are open or they turn frequently, although they sleep; God turns them to keep the ground from consuming the sides touching it.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:18/r2] author | reports | The action is also read with a yāʾ and God as subject, or as the verbal noun describing their turning.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:18/r3] author | reports | The dog may have been a stray that followed and was divinely made to speak, or a shepherd's dog; the reading كالبهم supports an owner of a dog.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:18/r4] author | holds | The participle basit depicts the dog's past state, while waṣīd means the cave's yard, entrance, or threshold.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:18/r5] author | holds | An observer would flee in terror because divine awe enveloped the sleepers, their bodies were imposing and eyes open, or the place was desolate.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:18/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas warned Muawiya that God had barred even a better man from looking at the sleepers; Muawiya sent men anyway, and a wind burned them.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:18/r7] الحجازيان | reports | The Hijazi readers double the consonant in لملئت for emphasis; Ibn Amir, al-Kisa'i, and Yaqub read رعبا with a heavy consonant.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r1] author | holds | The verse's recitation of taḥsabuhum with fatḥa on the sīn is attributed to the Syrian readers.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r2] author | holds | They seem awake because their eyes are open while they are actually asleep.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r3] author | reports | One report says they were turned twice each year; another says once on the day of ʿĀshūrāʾ.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r4] author | holds | The dog is described with an active participle for a past scene, since an active participle does not govern when it has past meaning.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r5] author | reports | Al-waṣīd means either the courtyard or the threshold.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r6] author | holds | Farāran is an accusative cognate accusative because the sense of turning away is fleeing.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r7] author | holds | The Hijazi reading doubles the lām in “they would be filled” for emphasis; the Syrian and ʿAlī readings vocalize the ʿayn with ḍamma.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r8] author | holds | Ruʿb is fear that fills the chest; their terrifying appearance may come from divinely bestowed awe or from long nails, hair, and huge bodies.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#3/r9] ابن عباس | reports | When Muʿāwiya wanted to enter the cave during a Roman campaign, Ibn ʿAbbās warned that even someone better than him would flee; men who entered were killed by a wind.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:18:18/r1] author | holds | The youths seem awake because their eyes, senses, and voluntary animal movements are active, but truly sleep in heedlessness; God turns them between good and virtue and evil and nature.
[QASHANI:18:18/r2] author | holds | Their dog represents the self, its forelegs the powers of anger and desire; it remains awake by the body's threshold, with stronger nobler anger on the right and weaker baser lust on the left.
[QASHANI:18:18/r3] author | holds | An unready observer would flee from their abstract realities and luminous stature, or from their discipline; even an initiate may flee their unity-station in awe of divine majesty.
