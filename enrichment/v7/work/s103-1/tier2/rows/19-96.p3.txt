[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p544/r3] author | reports | The Christians' mistaken view made humanity eternally sinful and salvation impossible unless God's son atoned for believers' sins by sacrifice.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p544/r4] author | reports | The Jews presumed their prophetic ancestry secured them from Hell, or that any punishment would last only briefly before mercy forgave them.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p544/r5] author | reports | Arabian idolaters denied the reality of Judgment but expected God's angel-daughters to secure them through intercession if it occurred.

## KURANYOLU-TEFSIR: Hayreddin Karaman, Mustafa Çağrıcı, İbrahim Kâfi Dönmez, Sadrettin Gümüş
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:19:96/r1] author | holds | After describing the fate of polytheists, the passage describes believers who do good; invoking al-Rahman highlights abundant mercy.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:19:96/r2] author | holds | God creates love for believers: He loves them, makes His other servants love them, and develops brotherhood and affection among believers.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:19:96/r3] Hz. Peygamber | reports | The Prophet said that when God loves a servant He makes Gabriel, heavenly beings, and people on earth love that person; variant reports say the reverse for one God dislikes.

## STUDYQURAN: Seyyed Hossein Nasr (ed.-in-chief), Caner K. Dagli, Maria Massi Dakake, Joseph E. B. Lumbard, Mohammed Rustom
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r1] commentators | reports | Commentators usually treat wudd, “love,” as synonymous with ḥubb, the Quran’s more usual word for love. | mentions 2:165, 5:54
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r10] some Sufis | reports | Some Sufis understand the ordained love as unveiling divine secrets, linking it to a hadith qudsi in which God loves the servant who draws near through supererogatory acts.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r11] al-Qushayrī | reports | Al-Qushayrī cites a related hadith qudsi in which the servant draws near until he loves God and God loves him.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r2] author | holds | The common interpretation is that God grants believers and righteous people love in this world, especially among other believing servants.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r3] al-Sulamī | reports | Al-Sulamī says righteous people are beloved by fellow Muslims, a manifestation of God’s love for them.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r4] al-Rāzī | prefers | Al-Rāzī favors the interpretation that righteous believers are loved by others.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r5] some commentators | reports | An alternative interpretation is that God grants righteous believers what they themselves love; its proponents note that believers may be hated by disbelievers while some disbelievers are widely loved.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r6] the hadith | reports | A hadith supports the preferred view: when God loves a servant, He tells Gabriel to love that person, then Heaven and earth are moved to love them.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r7] some commentators | reports | Some say the verse consoled ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, who worried about leaving his companions in Makkah.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r8] Shiite commentators | reports | Shiite commentators understand the ordained love as love of and spiritual attachment to ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib; they hold only those who love ʿAlī to be true Muslim believers.
[STUDYQURAN:19:96/r9] the Prophet | reports | A Shiite report says the Prophet told ʿAlī to ask God for a covenant and for love in believers’ hearts, after which this verse was revealed.
