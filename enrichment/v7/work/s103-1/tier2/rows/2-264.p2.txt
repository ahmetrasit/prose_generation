## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44#2/r1] أصحابنا | rejects | Al-Razi's school reads لا تبطلوا as a command not to perform charity in a null form: if the original motive was other than God, no valid reward ever arose to be removed later.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44#2/r2] أصحابنا | holds | If old and new merits do not conflict, the new event cannot erase the old; if they do conflict, the new event has no priority, and prevention is easier than reversal.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44#2/r3] أصحابنا | holds | A later event cannot erase what existed only in the past, what exists now without contradiction, or what has not yet come into existence.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44#2/r4] أصحابنا | holds | Explaining the prior merit's disappearance by the arrival of the later event is circular, since the later event requires the prior resistance already to have disappeared.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44#2/r5] أبو هاشم | reports | Abu Hashim's mutual-balancing account has each event erase some of the other; if both resulting absences occur, the causes must simultaneously exist and not exist, an impossibility.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44#2/r6] أبو علي الجبائي | rejects | Abu Ali al-Jubba'i says the later punishment alone removes prior reward; al-Razi objects that the earlier good then provides no benefit or protection, contrary to 99:7 and justice. | mentions 99:7
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44/r1] القاضي | reports | Al-Qadi says the prohibition clarifies that taunting and harm cancel the reward, not the already completed act of charity; the verse compares this cancellation with a clearer case.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44/r2] author | holds | The first parable compares the taunting donor to a show-off who is also an unbeliever, whose reward's failure is clearer than the donor's.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44/r3] author | holds | The smooth stone is the unbeliever, the thin soil is the apparent donation, and the downpour is unbelief or taunting that washes away the deed's reward.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p44/r4] الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubba'i argues that reward and punishment cannot both be deserved because each is an eternal pure benefit or harm; otherwise the same outcome would be both just punishment and unjust denial of reward.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p45/r1] author | holds | If a minor sin cancels only some equal parts of reward, selecting those parts without a distinguishing cause is arbitrary; if it cancels all reward, that is rejected by agreement, so minor sins cancel none.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p45/r2] author | holds | If a major punishment exceeds prior reward, one part cannot be singled out to cancel it without cause; if every part independently cancels it, multiple independent causes produce the same effect, which is impossible.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p45/r3] author | holds | Good and blameworthy consequences can coexist without erasing each other: a servant who saves his master from an enemy deserves praise but also blame for exposing the master's property to theft.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p45/r4] author | holds | A later act cannot alter the causal force of an earlier act in the past; absent such an effect, the earlier entitlement remains, and the past is stronger than a later event.
