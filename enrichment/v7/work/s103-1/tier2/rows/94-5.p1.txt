## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p776/r1] author | holds | The promise reassures Muhammad, whom the Meccan polytheists mocked for poverty, that God's great gifts mean he should not despair of further favor; ease would come soon after the hardship believers faced.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p776/r2] author | holds | Though مع means accompaniment, the ease follows hardship after a short interval; it is presented as already alongside hardship to console and strengthen hearts.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p776/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas and Ibn Masud say one hardship cannot overcome two eases; the statement is also transmitted as a Prophetic report.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p776/r4] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet once came out laughing and said that one hardship would not overcome two eases.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p776/r5] author | holds | The second clause may repeat the first for emphasis, or make a second promise; the definite hardship is the same one, while the indefinite ease can be a different portion, yielding two eases. | mentions 52:11
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p776/r6] author | reports | The two eases may be victories in Muhammad's time and the caliphs' time, or ease in this world and the next, as with the two good outcomes of victory and reward. | mentions 9:52
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p776/r7] author | holds | The indefinite form magnifies the ease; Ibn Masud's codex has the phrase once, but the Prophetic saying can still take two eases as the ease of both abodes.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:94:4-6/r6] author | reports | Ease also means victory over the Meccan opponents until they submit to the truth willingly or under compulsion.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:94:5-6/r1] author | holds | The first promise says that hardship comes with relief, namely ease, abundance, or wealth.
[QURTUBI-FULL:94:5-6/r10] author | prefers | Qurtubi says the Prophet was poor and mocked for it; God consoled him, promised worldly ease, and fulfilled it through conquests and generous wealth before his death.
[QURTUBI-FULL:94:5-6/r11] author | holds | The first promise concerns immediate worldly relief and was fulfilled by the conquests of Hijaz and Yemen, abundance, large camel gifts, generous grants, and a year's provision for his family.
[QURTUBI-FULL:94:5-6/r12] author | holds | Although especially fulfilled for the Prophet, this worldly favor may also reach some of his community if God wills.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:94:4-6/r2] author | holds | Hardship includes constriction, burdens, people’s error and abuse; ease includes relief, removal, and guidance. The verse counsels hope, with indefinite يسر magnifying relief and مع emphasizing close succession.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:94:5-7/r1] author | holds | The two hardships are veiling by creation from God and then by God from creation; their eases are disclosure of the Essence with sainthood and divinely given expansion with prophethood.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r1] author | reports | The common reading has sukūn in all four occurrences of ‘‘usr’ and ‘yusr’; Ibn Waththab, Abu Ja‘far, and ‘Isa read them with ḍamma, whose status as original or lengthened form is disputed.
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ‘Abbas explains the repeated definite ‘hardship’ and indefinite ‘ease’ with the saying that one hardship will not overcome two eases; a report also attributes the saying to the Prophet, who came out laughing.
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r3] author | holds | Arabic normally uses the definite article on a repeated noun when it refers to the same man or messenger, but an indefinite repeated noun refers to a different one; the author applies this to the verse’s one hardship and two eases. | mentions 73:15, 73:16
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r4] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari calls Ibn ‘Abbas’s interpretation a hopeful reading that takes God’s promise in the fullest sense; alternatively, the second sentence either repeats the first for emphasis or independently promises another ease after hardship. | mentions 77:15
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r5] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says the hardship remains one whether its definite article is referential or generic; indefinite ‘ease’ covers part of its class, and an independent second promise can name another ease.
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r6] أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa likewise says the two definite hardships are identical and the two indefinite eases are separate, deriving the saying about one hardship and two eases.
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r7] الزمخشري | reports | Since ‘ma‘a’ normally means accompaniment, al-Zamakhshari says the ease follows hardship quickly enough to be figuratively concurrent, consoling and strengthening hearts.
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r8] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari explains the indefinite ‘yusran’ as magnifying the ease; Ibn Mas‘ud’s codex has it only once.
[SAMIN-DURR:94:5/r9] الزمخشري | reports | Explaining the Prophet’s report despite Ibn Mas‘ud’s single ‘yusran’, al-Zamakhshari takes the magnified ease to include the ease of both abodes, thus amounting to two.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p432/r1] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says heavenly aid comes in proportion to burdens and patience in proportion to affliction, in a report transmitted from Abu Hurayrah.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p432/r3] ابن دريد | reports | Ibn Duraid quotes Abu Hatim al-Sijistani's poem: when every avenue appears closed, divine relief arrives at the point of despair and hardship is followed by near deliverance.
