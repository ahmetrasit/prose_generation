## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:17:23-39#3/r10] أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Jaʿfar recounts that the Prophet gave away an ounce of gold, then lacked anything for a petitioner; God taught him that giving all one has leaves one destitute.
[TABATABAI:17:23-39#3/r7] أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Even lawful giving becomes waste when a person gives away all his money and is left with nothing.
[TABATABAI:17:23-39#3/r8] أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Al-Qummi transmits Jaʿfar’s gloss that al-maḥsūr means unclothed; another report in this passage uses it for poverty.
[TABATABAI:17:23-39#3/r9] أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Ajlan reports Jaʿfar giving dates to successive beggars and recounting how the Prophet gave a woman his shirt; God taught measured spending, and al-iḥṣār is poverty.
[TABATABAI:17:23-39#4/r1] أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Ibn Sinan reports that Jaʿfar demonstrated the two expressions by closing his hand and then opening his palm.
[TABATABAI:17:28-30/r3] author | holds | The hand chained to the neck is a metaphor for miserly withholding; spreading it fully is spending everything and keeping nothing, so the verse strongly rejects both stinginess and excess.
[TABATABAI:17:28-30/r4] author | holds | After spending everything, one sits blamed by oneself and others, exhausted and unable to meet ordinary needs, or stripped bare and unable to face or interact with people.
[TABATABAI:17:28-30/r5] author | reports | Another interpretation makes blame the result of withholding and regret the result of extravagance; the author says this reading and the equation of all spending with waste are not established.
[TABATABAI:17:28-30/r6] author | rejects | Spending all one owns for God or on deserving recipients is not necessarily waste or corruption, so the verse does not imply regret for money that was not misused.
