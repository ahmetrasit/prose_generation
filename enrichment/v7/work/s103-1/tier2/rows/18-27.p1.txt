## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r1] author | holds | The passage from this verse through the Moses-and-Khidr story forms one discourse responding to Quraysh leaders who demanded that poor believers be expelled before they would believe.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r2] author | holds | God rejects the leaders' demand and makes the governing instruction steadfast recitation of the revealed Book and adherence to it, disregarding objections and obstinacy.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r3] author | holds | The command to recite includes both reading the Book and following its guidance in practice.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r4] author | holds | The statement that none can change God's words may support forbidding analogical qualification of an explicit text, since the command requires acting on its apparent wording.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r5] أبو مسلم الأصفهاني | reports | Abu Muslim al-Isfahani's position would make the verse preclude abrogation as well.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r6] author | holds | Abrogation is not textual alteration: a ruling remains valid until its abrogator arrives, which functions as its endpoint.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r7] author | reports | The commentators agree that multahad means a refuge or place of recourse.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r8] أهل اللغة | reports | Lexicographers connect multahad to lahd and ilhad, meaning to incline; mulhid is one who inclines away from religion. | mentions 16:103
[RAZI-FULL:v21p97#2/r9] author | holds | The verse means that apart from God there is no refuge in explanation and guidance.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27#2/r1] author | reports | One chronology places the youths in the cave before Jesus, with their awakening in the interval between Jesus and Muhammad.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27#2/r2] author | reports | Another says they lived before Moses, who mentioned them in the Torah, prompting the Jews’ question to the Prophet.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27#2/r3] author | reports | A third view places their cave entry after Jesus; the author leaves which chronology is correct to God.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27/r1] author | reports | The command to recite may conclude the Cave story: follow the Qur’an, for God’s words cannot be changed and His account of the youths will not fail.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27/r2] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari interprets the unchangeable words as God’s threats against sinners who oppose His Book.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27/r3] author | holds | If the Prophet does not follow the Qur’an and opposes it, he will find no refuge apart from God.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27/r4] author | holds | Multaḥad derives from inclination: one who seeks refuge has turned toward the place sought.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27/r5] القشيري أبو نصر عبد الرحيم | reports | Al-Qushayri identifies this verse as the end of the Cave Companions’ story.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27/r6] ابن عباس | reports | When Muawiya wanted to uncover the cave, Ibn Abbas objected that God had barred someone better than him, citing the warning that an observer would flee; a wind drove out the men sent in. | mentions 18:18
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:27/r7] الثعلبي | reports | Al-Thaʿlabi also relates an elaborate report in which the Prophet sends Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, and Ali to the youths by a wind; they enter, convey his greeting, and the youths accept Islam before returning to sleep until the Mahdi and Resurrection.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p165/r6] author | holds | After revealing the cave story, God commands the Prophet to recite all revelation; no one can alter His words, either because only He can change them or because His reported truths remain fixed while some commands are abrogated.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p165/r7] author | holds | The command and warning about unalterable words allude to disputants changing and distorting the cave story; ملتحد is a refuge one turns toward.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p151#2/r1] author | holds | The Messenger is commanded to recite and convey God's Book; its words cannot be changed, distorted, or reinterpreted away.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p151#2/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid glosses a refuge as a place of shelter; Qatada glosses it as a protector or master.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p151#2/r3] ابن جرير | reports | Ibn Jarir explains that if Muhammad failed to recite what was revealed, he would have no refuge from God; conveying the message is reinforced by the command in 5:67 and the warning in 28:85. | mentions 5:67, 28:85

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v5p183#2/r1] author | holds | Recite the revelation as worship and a means of nearness, act according to it, and learn its secrets; do not heed requests for another Qur'an or a change.
[BURSEVI:v5p183#2/r2] author | holds | Tilāwa is Quran recitation that follows and adheres to it, as in study and regular litanies; qirāʾa more broadly means voicing letters.
[BURSEVI:v5p183#2/r3] author | holds | No one can alter God's words; the general statement is qualified by the verse about replacing one revelation with another.
[BURSEVI:v5p183#2/r4] author | holds | The Qur'an and its rulings never change by addition or loss and are preserved in memory; people change across ages as knowledge and practice revert to ignorance and abandonment.
[BURSEVI:v5p183#2/r5] إبراهيم بن أدهم | reports | He found a stone promising benefit if turned over; its reverse said that someone who fails to practice known knowledge cannot seek what he does not know.
[BURSEVI:v5p183#2/r6] author | rejects | He condemns the innovating Ilhāmiyya, who abandon learning and the Qur'an as a supposed veil and substitute poems for the Qur'an of the spiritual path.
[BURSEVI:v5p183#2/r7] الكمال الخجندي | reports | His couplet contrasts a heart weary of hearing the Qur'an with falsehood weary of God's true speech.
[BURSEVI:v5p183#2/r8] إبراهيم الخواص | reports | He names five remedies for the heart: reflective Quran reading, an empty stomach, night prayer, dawn supplication, and company with the righteous; indulgence in desire leaves spiritual illness without refuge.
