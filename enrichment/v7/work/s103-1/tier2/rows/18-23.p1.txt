[ALUSI-FULL:v15p247#2/r1] author | holds | The prohibition covers promising to do anything one resolves in the future; ghadan primarily means the next day but the active participle already conveys futurity, which the phrase reinforces.

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:18:23/r1] author | reports | A report says Jews suggested three questions to Quraysh as a test of prophethood: the cave companions, Moses and his youth, and Dhul-Qarnayn.
[TABATABAI:18:23/r2] author | holds | Unlike the Dhul-Qarnayn account, the cave story does not begin with an explicit question formula, though the closing command about tomorrow hints at the question. | mentions 18:83
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#21/r3] author | holds | The instruction applies to anyone who plans and announces a future action; all beings, acts, and effects belong to God, who may determine them without challenge.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#21/r4] author | holds | Causes and agents have no independent efficacy: God empowers them and permits their effects, or withholds power by willing otherwise.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#22/r1] author | holds | The Quran repeatedly says that human acts and natural processes occur by God's permission, including cutting trees, calamities, plant growth, death, faith, and a messenger’s obedience. | mentions 59:5, 64:11, 7:58, 3:145, 10:100, 4:64
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#22/r2] author | holds | A believer should recognize that God grants ownership and power, rely on Him when resolving to act, and qualify future promises by God's permission or lack of contrary will. | mentions 3:159
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#22/r3] author | holds | The verse does not forbid attributing actions to people, which the Quran often does; its exception corrects the claim that people act independently of God's will and permission. | mentions 10:41, 28:55
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#23/r1] author | prefers | The exception modifies the circumstances or times of the statement: say you will act tomorrow only while attaching God’s will, meaning permission for the action.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#23/r2] الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash reads an omitted verb say in the exception, making this etiquette that avoids falsehood or broken promises if prevented; the author rejects it as needless strain that changes the meaning.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#23/r3] author | reports | Some exegetes read the phrase as an object of divine will, restricting it to obedient acts; the author rejects this legislative reading as unsupported.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#23/r4] author | rejects | The legislative interpretation lacks evidence: God’s will is not used this way in the Quran, which does use exceptions of creative will. | mentions 18:69
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#24/r1] المعتزلة | reports | Another Muʿtazili interpretation makes the exception modify the act rather than the statement, with God preventing an independently caused act; the author rejects it because Quranic usage accepts that construction. | mentions 6:80, 7:89, 6:111
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#24/r2] author | rejects | A proposal that God must first authorize disclosure of what He has informed the Prophet requires an unsupported implied object and otherwise imposes an unknowable duty.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#24/r3] author | rejects | A final proposal treats the exception as eternal, meaning never state a future intention; this conflicts with Quranic passages attributing future actions to the Prophet and others. | mentions 11:108, 10:41, 18:83
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#24/r4] author | holds | The Quran itself presents Moses, Shuʿayb, Ishmael, and the Prophet as speaking of future acts conditionally on God’s will, supporting the ordinary permission reading. | mentions 18:69, 28:27, 37:102, 48:27
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#27/r1] أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | The Prophet promised answers the next day without saying if God wills; revelation stopped for forty days, causing grief and doubt while Quraysh mocked, then Gabriel explained revelation comes only by God’s permission.
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#32/r8] علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام | reports | An account from ʿAlī says a person swearing by God may make an exception for forty days, recalling the Prophet’s delayed answer to Jewish questioners after he omitted “if God wills.”
[TABATABAI:18:9-26#32/r9] author | holds | Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī defines thunyā as an exception and says related reports suggest a decisive oath; whether an exception can qualify an already binding oath is left to jurisprudence.
