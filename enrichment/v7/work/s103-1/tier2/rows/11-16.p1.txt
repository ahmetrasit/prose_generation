## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r1] author | holds | The opponents claimed to dispute with Muhammad to establish truth, but Rāzī says envy and refusal to follow him were their real motives.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r2] author | holds | The verse resembles 17:18 and 42:20, which distinguish those seeking worldly life from those seeking the harvest of the hereafter. | mentions 17:18, 42:20
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r3] author | reports | One view restricts the verse to disbelievers because its ending about hell and nullified deeds cannot describe believers generally; the general opening is narrowed to those seeking only worldly adornment.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r4] الأصم | reports | Al-Aṣamm says the disbelievers who deny resurrection are intended, since they desire only worldly happiness.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r5] author | reports | Another view identifies hypocrites who joined the Prophet's campaigns to seek spoils without belief in afterlife reward.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r6] أنس | reports | A third view, transmitted from Anas, identifies Jews and Christians.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r7] القاضي | reports | The Qāḍī's chosen reading concerns people who do good deeds for worldly praise: social benefits such as charity and public works benefit others, but worship without proper intention is not obedience.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p159/r1] القاضي | reports | On the Qāḍī's view, the deeds meant are good works that a disbeliever can validly perform.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p159/r2] author | reports | A second view takes the wording generally, including believers who act for reputation and disbelievers who seek worldly reward; the hell clause is difficult unless it means punishment for those corrupted acts.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p159/r3] أبو هريرة | reports | Abū Hurayra transmits a prophetic report that the Qurʾān reciter, wealthy donor, and martyr are condemned because each sought public praise rather than God.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p159/r4] أبو هريرة | reports | After Abū Hurayra recited this report to Muʿāwiya, the narrator says Muʿāwiya wept and then affirmed the truth of the verse.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:16/r1] author | holds | The warning indicates eternal punishment; since believers do not remain eternally in Hell (4:116), it applies when the hypocrite dies in disbelief. | mentions 4:116
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:16/r2] قيل | reports | An alternative says they enter Hell for a specified number of days, then leave through intercession or a divine release.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:16/r3] author | holds | The verse threatens loss of faith, especially through ostentation, which is a form of shirk as explained under al-Nisa and later under al-Kahf.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:16/r4] أبو حاتم | reports | Abu Hatim treats the wording as involving an omitted pronoun; al-Nahhas says no deletion is needed because باطل is verbal-noun usage, meaning their work is void.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:16/r5] أبي وعبد الله | reports | Ubayy and Abdullah read وباطلا ما كانوا يعملون, taking ما as extra and the sense as they practiced falsehood.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:16-17/r1] author | holds | Those seeking only the world have only Fire in the afterlife; their deeds yield no reward because they lacked sincerity, were not rightly performed, or were confined to worldly aims.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:16-17/r2] author | holds | The two statements explain the preceding threat: their work has no afterlife reward, and what they did was itself invalid; one reading makes batilan the object of ya'malun.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p149#2/r1] author | holds | Such people have only the Fire in the afterlife; their work earns no reward there because they intended this world and received what they sought.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p149#2/r2] author | holds | Their deeds are intrinsically futile because they were done without a proper purpose; work with no valid aim merits no reward.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:11:16-17/r1] author | holds | Such people face the afterlife's fire because worldly veils frustrate the heart's aptitude and cause distress from ill-fitting acquisitions; their outwardly good deeds vanish because they intended worldly gain.
[QASHANI:11:16-17/r2] عليه السلام | reports | A prophetic report that actions are judged by intentions supports the nullification of deeds performed for worldly ends.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p132/r4] author | holds | Restricting the Fire to these people most clearly indicates unbelievers; if ostentatious believers are included, it means the Fire is their due, though God may forgive them, as Ibn ʿAbbas and Ibn Jubayr apparently hold.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p132/r5] author | reports | The pronoun in ما صنعوا فيها apparently refers to the afterlife, where deeds come to nothing, but it may refer to worldly life; ما may be relative or a verbal noun.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p132/r6] author | holds | باطل reinforces the stated nullification; it may begin a new clause or be a second predicate, with corresponding analyses of ما كانوا.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p132/r7] زيد بن علي | reports | Zayd ibn ʿAli reads وبطل as a past verb; Ubayy and Ibn Masʿud read وباطلا in the accusative, parsed as an object of يعملون with ما extra, though the syntax is disputed. | mentions 43:20
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p134/r1] author | holds | Invalid deeds deserve no reward because they were not done with a sound intention.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16#2/r1] author | reports | Al-Zamakhshari's emphatic ما analysis is illustrated with poetry: وحديث ما على قصره and لأمر ما جدع قصير أنفه.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16#2/r2] author | reports | The same use of ما had been discussed under the expression مثلا ما بعوضة, cited here as الزمر 39:9. | mentions 39:9
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r1] author | reports | In وحبط ما صنعوا فيها, فيها may modify حبط and refer to the afterlife, or modify صنعوا and refer to worldly life as in 11:15. | mentions 11:15
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r2] author | reports | ما صنعوا may be a relative pronoun with an omitted object pronoun, or a verbal noun meaning their making has perished.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r3] الجمهور | reports | The majority read باطل in the nominative; one analysis makes it a fronted predicate and ما كانوا يعملون its subject, with ما either relative or verbal noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r4] مكي بن أبي طالب | reports | Makki makes باطل the subject and ما كانوا يعملون the predicate; the author says this is possible only as an error and criticizes Makki for giving no other analysis.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r5] author | reports | A third nominative parse coordinates باطل with the preceding predicates, taking ما كانوا يعملون as its subject; Zayd ibn Ali's verbal reading supports it.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r6] زيد بن علي | reports | Zayd ibn Ali reads وباطل as a past-tense verb coordinated with حبط.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r7] أبي وابن مسعود | reports | Ubayy and Ibn Masud read باطلا in the accusative; Makki says this is also how it appears in their codices.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r8] مكي وأبو البقاء وصاحب اللوامح | reports | They take accusative باطلا as the object of يعملون with an extra ما, allowing the predicate of كان to precede it. | mentions 34:40
[SAMIN-DURR:11:16/r9] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari also allows ما to be emphatic and Baṭilan to mean utterly false, or باطلا to be a verbal noun.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p310#2/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas says ostentatious people receive in this world the reward for good deeds done solely for worldly gain, such as fasting, prayer, or night vigil, but their work is void in the Hereafter; Mujahid and al-Dahhak also report this view.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p310#2/r2] أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas and Hasan say these verses were revealed about Jews and Christians.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p310#2/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid and others identify the people addressed as those who act for show.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p310#2/r4] قتادة | reports | Qatada says those whose goal and intention are worldly receive their good deeds' reward here, then arrive with no rewarded good deed; believers are rewarded both here and in the Hereafter. | mentions 17:18, 17:19, 17:20, 17:21, 42:20

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v3p512/r1] author | holds | Those people receive only Fire in the afterlife after taking their full reward in this world; their works collapse because they lack a sound foundation and are empty forms.
[BIQAI-FULL:v3p512/r2] author | holds | The author defines adornment as beautification by clothing, jewelry, or appearance; full recompense as delivering a right completely; and the nullification of work through the image of a spoiled stomach.
