## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:57:20/r1] author | holds | The verse teaches detachment from worldly life, whose play, amusement, adornment, boasting, and accumulation include homes and mounts as well as money and children.
[MUQATIL:57:20/r2] author | holds | Worldly life resembles rain-fed pasture that delights unbelievers, then yellows and becomes dead debris; so does the world for one who prefers it.
[MUQATIL:57:20/r3] author | holds | The Hereafter has severe punishment and God's forgiveness and approval for believers; worldly life is only a passing, deceptive enjoyment.

## TUSTARI: Sahl al-Tustarī, d. 283 AH
[TUSTARI:57:20/r1] author | holds | Worldly life is a sleeping soul and the afterlife is an awake soul.
[TUSTARI:57:20/r2] author | holds | The path to salvation begins with knowledge, bears fruit in resisting appetite and avoiding prohibitions, then disciplines the soul to fulfill commands in purity; this makes worship easy and leads to the stations of worshippers and their spiritual tasting.
[TUSTARI:57:20/r3] author | reports | A story says Abraham, thirsty in a hot desert, asked an Abyssinian herdsman for water; the man struck a rock and water sprang out.
[TUSTARI:57:20/r4] الله | reports | God says He would have removed the heavens and earth at the herdsman’s request because the man wanted nothing in either world but God.
[TUSTARI:57:20/r5] عامر بن عبد القيس | reports | Amir ibn Abd Qays says he gave up women and collecting water, while accepting sleep and food as unavoidable needs to manage as best he could.

## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH
[ZAJJAJ:v5p127#2/r1] author | holds | Yahīj means that the crop begins to dry and turn yellow; ḥuṭāman means shattered, broken, and gone, illustrating worldly life’s passing.
[ZAJJAJ:v5p127#2/r2] author | reports | Riḍwān is also read riḍwān; ʿĀṣim is reported to have transmitted both the ḍamma and kasra forms, meaning forgiveness for God’s friends and punishment for His enemies.
[ZAJJAJ:v5p127/r1] author | reports | The kāf in kamathali is nominative as an adjective; the clause likens worldly life and its ornaments to rain and its growth.
[ZAJJAJ:v5p127/r2] author | reports | Alternatively, the kāf is nominative as a predicate after another predicate: worldly life and its adornment are like rain whose growth amazes people.
[ZAJJAJ:v5p127/r3] author | reports | Al-kuffār can mean cultivators: if even farmers are impressed by the crop, who know it well, its growth is exceptionally beautiful.
[ZAJJAJ:v5p127/r4] author | reports | Alternatively, al-kuffār means those who disbelieve in God; they are more impressed by worldly adornment than believers.

## SULAMI: al-Sulamī, d. 412 AH
[SULAMI:v2p310#3/r1] ذو النون | reports | The world is blameworthy, especially for those who seek it, rely on it, boast in it, or urge others toward it; denouncing it can itself become a practiced trade.
[SULAMI:v2p310#3/r2] أبو بكر الوراق | reports | The world is a house of trial, toil, and desire; whoever exhausts his worldly aims is safe from everything.
[SULAMI:v2p310#3/r3] أبو بكر الوراق | reports | The world is what passes away, while the afterlife remains and never perishes.
[SULAMI:v2p310#3/r4] سهل | reports | The root of worldly life is ignorance; its branches include bodily pleasures and possessions, and its fruits are sins, punishment, hard-heartedness, and love of sin.
[SULAMI:v2p310#3/r5] عمر المكي | reports | God describes worldly life through both praise and blame, using invitation, warning, longing, and caution, as in this verse's depiction of play and amusement.
[SULAMI:v2p310#4/r1] ابن عطاء | reports | Whatever distracts a servant from the afterlife is worldly life; for different people that distraction may be property, trade, status, work, company, or personal desire.
[SULAMI:v2p310#4/r2] ذو النون | reports | Seekers should not pursue the world; if they do, they should not love it, since it is only provision for a journey whose resting place is elsewhere.
[SULAMI:v2p310#4/r3] ابن عطاء | reports | Worldly governance relies on power and planning, religious governance on obeying commands and prohibitions, and seeking God on disavowing one's own power.
[SULAMI:v2p310#4/r4] سهل | reports | The world is a sleeping self, while the afterlife is an awakened self.
