## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:259-260/r1] author | holds | The passerby was ʿUzayr, and the ruined town was Iliya; its buildings had collapsed over their roofs.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:259-260/r2] author | holds | ʿUzayr wondered how God could restore the town after its destruction, so God chose to show resurrection through him.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:259-260/r3] author | holds | He arrived on a donkey carrying a small vessel of juice and a basket of figs; God put him to sleep, took his soul for a century, then revived him.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:259-260/r4] author | holds | His food and drink remained unchanged for a hundred years as evidence of the elapsed time, while his donkey lay dead with visible bones.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:259-260/r5] author | holds | The wāw in “so We may make you a sign” is extra; he was a sign because he returned youthful with black hair while his grandsons were gray.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:259-260/r6] author | holds | “Nunshizuhā” means reviving the bones; another reading, “nunshizuhā,” means raising them from the ground, since elevation is called nushūz.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:259-260/r7] author | holds | After seeing the donkey reassembled and clothed with flesh, ʿUzayr knew by direct observation what he had previously known only unseen.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p317/r1] author | reports | The verse continues the preceding account of Abraham's disputant; another reading treats it as a separate question asking whether one has seen both men.

[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p317/r3] وهب بن منبه | reports | Wahb identifies him as Armiya ibn Hilqiya, from Aaron's tribe, and says he was al-Khidr.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p317/r4] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says the passerby was an unbeliever who doubted resurrection.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p317/r5] author | reports | Reported locations for the village include Jerusalem, the holy land, Dayr Sabir Abad, Muslim Badh, Dayr Hiraql, a village destroyed with its fleeing inhabitants, and an "grape village" two farsakhs from Jerusalem.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p317/r6] author | holds | Khawiya can describe a fallen or an empty house; the village lay collapsed on its roofs, with roofs falling first and walls then falling onto them.
