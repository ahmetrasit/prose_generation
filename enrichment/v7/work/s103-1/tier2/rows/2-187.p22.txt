[BIQAI-FULL:v1p351/r2] الحرالي | reports | Al-Harali says God pardoned violations caused by human disposition, then eased the law to fit this community's nature; reports of one Emigrant and one Ansari show the mercy reached both groups.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p351/r3] عائشة | reports | Aisha told the Prophet that his Lord hastened to satisfy his desire, illustrating the special easing granted to the community through him.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p351/r4] الحرالي | reports | The Prophet told Ali to ask God to make truth turn with him; the author also says wise leaders adapt commands to people's dispositions, like directing the brave to fight and restraining the coward.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p351/r5] النبي | reports | The Prophet considered forbidding ghila, then refrained after learning that Persians and Romans practiced it without harm to their children; law should account for different human natures.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p352/r1] الحرالي | reports | Forgiveness erases the trace of wrongdoing and hints that purification such as expiation might otherwise have been due; Ramadan fasting elevates the community beyond earlier separated days and nights.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p352/r2] الحرالي | reports | Nighttime intercourse becomes obedience to God's command rather than mere appetite; 'what God prescribed' may mean offspring or a lawful place for sex, and some scholars favored marriage for the fasting person at night.

[BIQAI-FULL:v1p352/r4] author | holds | Making dawn's appearance the endpoint of permitted food and sex requires watching for dawn and supports intending the fast before it; intercourse continuing to dawn shows that ritual bathing may wait until daytime.
