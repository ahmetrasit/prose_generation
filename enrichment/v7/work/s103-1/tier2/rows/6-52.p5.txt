## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r1] author | holds | After commanding warning of the non-pious, God commands drawing the pious near and forbids their dismissal.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r2] author | holds | Calling on God morning and evening means continuous worship, or specifically dawn and afternoon prayers, or all five prayers; the Shami reading has the stated form of 'morning.'
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r3] author | holds | Seeking God's face means sincerity; 'face' can denote a thing's essence and reality.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r4] author | reports | Revelation followed the chiefs' demand to dismiss poor believers Bilal, Suhayb, Ammar, and others; the Prophet refused, they requested alternate days and a written agreement, then the poor moved aside as Ali was called to write. The Prophet threw away the paper and embraced them.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r5] author | holds | The believers' account is theirs alone and cannot transfer to the Prophet; the source compares the statement with a verse about Noah's reckoning. | mentions 26:113
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p324#3/r6] author | holds | The consequence clause answers the negation, while the next clause answers the prohibition; alternatively, being unjust is caused by dismissing them.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:6:52-53/r1] author | holds | The dawn and dusk callers are complete people of unity; warnings benefit neither them, lost in God, nor those whose hearts are hardened.
[QASHANI:6:52-53/r2] author | holds | Their constant worship is presence of heart, witnessing of spirit, and orientation of the secret to God alone through eternal love, without seeking reward or fearing punishment.
[QASHANI:6:52-53/r3] author | holds | They seek God's Essence rather than changing attributes or intermediaries, having witnessed the annihilation of means and even of their own selves.
[QASHANI:6:52-53/r4] author | holds | They have no intermediary relation of account through the Prophet and, absorbed in God, do not involve themselves in his mission, aid to Islam, or opposition to disbelief.
[QASHANI:6:52-53/r5] author | holds | Expelling them distracts their uninterrupted presence and makes the Prophet unjust; the following trial is that veiled people see only poverty and outward appearance and scorn their hidden rank. | mentions 70:23

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p496/r1] author | holds | This segment reproduces Quranic verses 6:36–52 without commentary.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p497/r4] author | holds | Ṭard means expulsion accompanied by humiliation; ṭarīd means an expelled person, and the author notes tribal names derived from these terms.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p520/r4] سعد بن أبي وقاص | reports | Saʿd ibn Abi Waqqas says the verse was revealed about six poor believers, including himself, Ibn Masʿud, Suhayb, ʿAmmar, al-Miqdad, and Bilal, after Quraysh demanded their expulsion.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p520/r5] خباب بن الأرت | reports | Khabbab says the verse concerned weak believers whom the Prophet taught morning and evening; elite visitors al-Aqraʿ and ʿUyayna objected to sitting with them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p520/r6] author | rejects | The author doubts this version because the verse is Meccan while those elites were not warned until Medina.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p520/r7] خباب بن الأرت | reports | A related report says the Prophet would rise and leave the poor believers, after which the instruction to remain with those who call on God was revealed. | mentions 18:28
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p520/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Al-ʿAwfi's report from Ibn ʿAbbas says nobles would believe if the poor were moved back so they could pray behind the nobles; expulsion meant moving them from the row, not the gathering.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p520/r9] author | holds | Despite variations in the reports, their common point is that Arab notables asked the Prophet to send the poor believers away, prompting the verse.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r1] author | holds | After commanding warning for the unpious, God honors the pious and forbids their expulsion, describing their outward and inward sincerity in worship.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r11] الفراء | reports | The familiar form غدوة is treated as a proper noun and diptote; al-Farra reports a dialect that uses it for the morning of the same day.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r2] author | holds | Calling on their Lord means asking, seeking refuge, and directing supplication and desire to Him.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r3] author | prefers | Morning and evening stand for continual worship, like saying 'praise God morning and evening'; they may also be singled out because they are busy hours, making remembrance then especially telling.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r4] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan and Muqatil identify the phrase with the two daily prayers practiced in Mecca, morning and evening.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r5] قتادة | reports | Qatada and Mujahid in one report identify the phrase as the dawn and afternoon prayers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r6] ابن عمر | reports | Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid in another report, and Ibrahim identify the phrase with the five prayers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r7] بعض القصاص | reports | Some storytellers take it as people gathering with the believers morning and evening; Ibn al-Musayyib, ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Abi ʿAmra, and others object that the verse concerns congregational prayers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r8] أبو جعفر | reports | Abu Jaʿfar interprets it as reciting and learning the Qur'an.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p521/r9] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says it means worship; Ibrahim in another report says remembrance of God; al-Zajjaj says monotheistic, sincere supplication and worship.
