## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH

[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p291#2/r17] author | reports | Alternatively, God clothed them in awe that turned people's eyes away until their appointed term, keeping intruders from them.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p291#2/r18] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says that during a Roman naval campaign with Muawiya, Muawiya wanted to enter the cave; Ibn Abbas said God had prevented someone better than him. A party sent inside was driven out by a wind.

## QUSHAYRI: al-Qushayrī, d. 465 AH
[QUSHAYRI:v2p212/r3] author | holds | The youths were outwardly present to observers but inwardly absorbed and effaced in what was unveiled to them of the Real; in reality another acted through them.
[QUSHAYRI:v2p212/r4] author | holds | Turning them from side to side describes God's sheltering care, more complete than a mother's tenderness and more precious than a father's mercy.
[QUSHAYRI:v2p212/r5] author | holds | The knowers of unity appear outwardly differentiated but inwardly witness unity, and their actions unfold without affectation.
[QUSHAYRI:v2p212/r6] author | holds | A lover loves those associated with the beloved; the dog is remembered alongside the youths through the Resurrection. | mentions 18:22
[QUSHAYRI:v2p212/r7] التفاسير | reports | A reported version says the shepherd would not send away the dog because he had raised it; another says God made the dog speak and refuse to leave its caretaker.
[QUSHAYRI:v2p212/r8] author | holds | The dog's lowly value and impure qualities did not harm it because it kept company with the youths.
