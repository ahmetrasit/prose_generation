## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says one prisoner was the king's food steward and the other his cupbearer; the king, al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan, suspected them of poisoning him and imprisoned them.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r10] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says the prisoners called Joseph a benefactor because he visited their sick, consoled the grieving, and made room for cramped inmates.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r11] author | reports | Other explanations say Joseph encouraged patience and promised reward, was skilled in knowledge, accepted excuses, or put others' rights before his own.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r12] ابن إسحاق | reports | Ibn Ishaq says they called Joseph a benefactor in the hope that he would interpret their dreams for them.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says the prisoners told Joseph they loved him; Joseph asked them not to love him because the love of his aunt, father, and al-Aziz's wife had brought him tribulation, and he wanted love only from his Lord.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r3] author | holds | They are called fatayan because each was a slave, and slave may mean a young man regardless of age.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r4] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid and Ibn Ishaq say the prisoners had true dreams and Joseph's interpretations came true; Abu Hurayra transmits the Prophetic maxim that the truest dreams belong to the most truthful speakers.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r5] أبو هريرة | reports | Through Muhammad ibn Sirin, Abu Hurayra reports the Prophet saying the most truthful dreams belong to the most truthful speakers.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r6] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud and al-Suddi say the prisoners invented dreams to test Joseph, then claimed they were joking after his answer; he replied that the matter they asked about had been decided.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r7] أبو مجلز | reports | Abu Mijlaz says one prisoner, the one who was crucified, had lied in his dream, while the other had spoken truthfully.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r8] author | holds | Yakhmur here means grapes, either named for what their juice becomes or because Omanis call grapes khamr; Ibn Masud read anaba, grapes.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p35/r9] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says people of Oman call grapes khamr; Ibn Masud read the phrase as 'I see myself pressing grapes.'
