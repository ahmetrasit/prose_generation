[ALUSI-FULL:v15p212#2/r13] author | holds | The question concerns counting the duration as discrete units, dividing it into years and reaching a numerical count, not measuring its continuous extent.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p212#2/r14] author | reports | An alternative takes amad as the duration's endpoint: the count may concern the full 309 years or the final, three-hundred-ninth year, with the latter counting the number's encompassed ranks.

[ALUSI-FULL:v15p213#2/r3] author | reports | One objection is that comparatives from non-triliteral verbs are not regular; grammarians variously allow them, forbid them except as anomalies, or allow them when the hamza is not causative.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p213#2/r4] سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh apparently permits forming a comparative from non-triliteral verbs without restriction.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p213#2/r5] أبو علي | reports | Abu Ali forbids such formation except for anomalous forms, which cannot serve as a productive pattern.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p213#2/r6] ابن عصفور | reports | Ibn Asfur distinguishes causative hamza, which blocks comparative formation, from other hamza; here the hamza is not causative.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p213#2/r7] author | reports | A second objection asks how the comparative can govern amadan as a direct object; supplying an omitted verb merely repeats the text, while other parses distort the sense.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p213#2/r8] أبو حيان | reports | Abu Hayyan treats amadan as a duration governed by a dropped preposition: the original would mean “for a duration”; ma explicates an otherwise vague word. | mentions 2:106, 35:2
