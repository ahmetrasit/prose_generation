[ALUSI-FULL:v15p253/r5] بعضهم | reports | Some prefer explanatory apposition because substitution would seem to make the number itself unintended; a report says listeners asked whether the three hundred meant days, months, or years, after which “years” was revealed. [ALUSI-FULL:v15p253/r6] الزجاج | reports | A parsing of “years” as a numeral specification is criticized because it seems to make the stay nine hundred years: the specification should count one item per hundred, and years are plural; al-Ālūsī replies that plural specification follows the underlying collective usage.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p253/r7] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj also allows “years” to modify “hundred” in the genitive, semantically describing the whole number as in an Antarah verse about forty-two milk-camels; the passage continues its grammatical defense in the next segment.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p254/r1] أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī explains the poetic construction by taking “milch-camel” collectively, allowing its plural adjective to describe the whole numbered group; a collective plural in singular form can specify a numeral, as in “twenty persons.”

[ALUSI-FULL:v15p254/r3] author | holds | The plural genitive is possible though uncommon: the plural ending may function like a replacement for the singular's deleted final consonant, and the Quranic reading itself suffices as Arabic evidence despite the lack of another attested example.

## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:18:24-26/r2] author | holds | Three hundred years plus nine gives the lunar equivalent of three hundred solar years.
[ELMALILI:18:24-26/r3] author | reports | Some commentators hold that the duration is God's own statement.
[ELMALILI:18:24-26/r4] İbn Mes‘ud | reports | A report from Ibn Masud takes the duration as a quotation of those disputing the count, with an omitted “they said.”
[ELMALILI:18:24-26/r5] author | reports | Other commentators prefer connecting the duration to the statement of those who said they would build a mosque over the sleepers.
[ELMALILI:18:24-26/r6] author | prefers | Elmalılı prefers a fresh, resumptive reading: the victorious Cave party remained hidden in their cave for 309 years, until their side prevailed.
[ELMALILI:18:24-26/r7] author | holds | He connects their emergence with Christians overcoming the idolatrous Romans at the beginning of the fourth century, which fits a hiding period of more than three hundred years.
[ELMALILI:18:24-26/r8] author | holds | Their long hidden victory foreshadows that Muhammad's mission and Islam would prevail sooner and more swiftly and beautifully, a triumph that began after the Hijra.
