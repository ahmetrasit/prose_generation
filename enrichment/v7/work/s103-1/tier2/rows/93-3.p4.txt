## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:93:2-4#2/r1] author | holds | Tevdīʿ first means a departing guest's gracious farewell and prayers, then came to mean simply leaving; here it means leaving because farewell cannot literally be attributed to God.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4#2/r2] author | holds | The revelation's occasion supports reading the verb as abandonment: God did not leave the Prophet out of anger, but through continuing mercy and care.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4#2/r3] author | holds | Abandonment can express either grace or chastisement; the contrast of forenoon and night hints that the Prophet remains under divine mercy, while the denial of hatred excludes wrath.

[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r11] author | holds | Night can symbolize life's suffering, death's grief, cessation of revelation and knowledge, and spiritual constriction; here the focus is respite during that darkness or the joy that comes when revelation resumes.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r12] author | holds | Duha expresses the bright, youthful joy of life and revealed knowledge, while the settled night signals rest before a promised renewal; this relation fittingly introduces the assurance that the Lord has not abandoned Muhammad.

## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p394#2/r1] author | holds | The oath rebuts the idolaters' claim that revelation stopped when the Prophet did not recite the Quran at night for several days; the warning targets their insinuation, since the Prophet himself had no doubt.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p394#2/r6] author | holds | The denial of abandonment answers the oath; a negative oath response is not prefixed with لام.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p395/r1] author | holds | Farewell is a traveler's greeting; here it metaphorically means separation after connection, comparing interrupted speech to departure, although the attribution is only figurative because God has no ordinary human contact with people. It denies that God stopped revelation.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p395/r2] author | holds | The added denial of hatred rejects both mockeries: some said the Lord had left the Prophet and others said He hated him; the coordinated clause shares the oath-response syntax.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p395/r3] author | holds | قلي and قلى, with the stated alternative vowels, mean intense hatred; the surah's opening explains the setting of the idolaters' remarks.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p395/r4] ابن عباس وابن جريج | reports | They report a second interruption of revelation lasting about fifteen days, after which idolaters said Muhammad's Lord had abandoned and hated him, prompting the verse.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p395/r5] author | holds | Revelation paused twice: first before al-Mudaththir or al-Muzzammil, after two or three surahs, when the Prophet feared revelation had ended and then saw Gabriel on a chair between heaven and earth; this early pause, perhaps forty days, went unnoticed by idolaters.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p395/r6] author | holds | The second pause followed roughly eight more surahs, making this the eleventh in revelation order, consistent with its chronological placement.
