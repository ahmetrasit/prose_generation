## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p245#2/r3] author | holds | Deniers say worldly life alone exists: parents die and children live; time, long life, and the alternation of night and day destroy them, without knowledge.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p245#2/r4] أبو هريرة | reports | A divine report forbids saying “woe to time”: God is Time, sends night and day, and may seize them at will.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p245#2/r5] أبو هريرة | reports | The Prophet forbids cursing time and calling grapes karm, saying God is Time and karm is the Muslim man.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p245#2/r6] author | holds | Arabs blamed time for calamities; because God is the real agent, cursing time ultimately curses God, so they were forbidden.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p87/r1] author | holds | The words we die and live describe the human kind collectively: one group dies while another continues to live.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p87/r2] author | holds | Their claim that time destroys them treats the long passage of time, in which afflictions unfold, as the killer; God says they have no knowledge and only conjecture.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p87/r3] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd reads the claim as only a passing eon destroying them.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p87/r4] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid glosses dahr as time; Abū Hurayra reports that the pre-Islamic Arabs blamed night and day, while the Prophetic prohibition on reviling time means God causes what they attribute to time.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p87/r5] النبي عليه السلام | reports | The Prophet says not to revile time; in another report God says that the son of Adam abuses time, though God holds night and day in His hand.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:45:22-24/r8] author | holds | Those denying resurrection say there is only worldly life, with no return or reckoning; their claim that time alone causes death reflects ignorance of the Creator and rests on conjecture.
[TABRISI:45:22-24/r9] author | reports | The phrase we die and live may mean that the order is reversed, that people die and their children live, or that some die while others live.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p363/r1] author | holds | Dahr refers to the succession of night and day; the deniers have no knowledge and state this only as a conjecture. | mentions 23:37
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v7p363/r2] author | reports | The Prophet said not to curse time, for God is the one who causes people to die, not the passage of time as they imagine.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v27p232#2/r1] المشركون | reports | The deniers of resurrection challenge the claim by demanding that their dead forefathers be brought back to testify that resurrection is real.
[RAZI-FULL:v27p232#2/r2] author | rejects | The challenge is weak: failure to occur now does not prove impossibility, just as our nonexistence before birth did not make our later existence impossible.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r1] أبو علي الثقفي | reports | His poem tells one who reproaches time that time is commanded and ends at God's command, contrasting the unbeliever's multiplying wealth with a poor believer's growing faith.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r10] author | rejects | Some later Muslims who cannot publicly deny resurrection recast it as bodily death and afterlife rewards as images seen by souls; Qurtubi calls them more harmful than open disbelievers because they disguise truth and mislead.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r11] author | reports | Another interpretation takes “we die and live” to mean that memories and traces survive, a life of remembrance.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r12] author | reports | A further view alludes to transmigration: when a person dies, the spirit enters inert matter and lives through it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r2] عبد الله بن عمر | reports | He rebukes his son Salim for speaking often of time and quotes a poem saying time neither perpetrates nor brings calamity; God raises an agent and reverses fortunes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r3] الأعشى | reports | Abū ʿUbayd cites al-Aʿshā's poem: God retains fidelity and reckoning, while blame belongs to the person.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r4] عمرو بن قميئة | reports | His poem describes being struck by time's unseen arrows, illustrating how the Arabs attributed events to time.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r5] أبو هريرة | reports | The cited Prophetic report continues with God's statement that the son of Adam harms Him; the excerpt uses an ellipsis for the rest.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r6] أبو عبيد | reports | In debate with an atheist, Abū ʿUbayd argues that people did not curse God through all time; rather, Arabs blamed time for events, as al-Aʿshā's poem illustrates.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r7] author | holds | Arabs habitually blamed time during calamities and attributed events to it, although God alone is the true agent.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r8] author | holds | The phrase “they have no knowledge” uses min as an added particle; they say this from doubt and speak only by conjecture.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24#2/r9] author | holds | Among polytheists are people who acknowledge a creator but deny resurrection, and people who remain uncertain rather than rejecting it decisively.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r1] author | holds | The speakers deny the afterlife, resurrection, and recompense.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r10] قطرب | reports | Quṭrub says only death destroys them and cites Abū Dhuʾayb's poem about fate and time.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r11] عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima interprets “nothing destroys us but time” as “nothing destroys us but God.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r12] أبو هريرة | reports | The Prophet reports the pagan claim that night and day kill, followed by God's statement that He turns night and day and holds the affair; Qurtubi identifies the wording as Bukhari's, also transmitted by Muslim and Abū Dāwūd.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r13] أبو هريرة | reports | The Prophet also forbids saying “O woe to time,” since God is al-dahr; the report is in al-Muwaṭṭaʾ.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r14] author | reports | Some scholars infer from the hadith that al-dahr is a divine name; others say it rebuts Arabs who treated time as an agent, since God causes the events they blame on time.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r2] الكلبي | reports | He reads “we die and live” as the parents dying while their children live.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r3] قراءة | reports | An alternate recitation vocalizes the nūn of naḥyā with ḍamma.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r4] author | reports | Another explanation is that some people die while others live.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r5] ابن مسعود | reports | His reading reverses the word order to “we live and die.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r6] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid interprets al-dahr as the years and days.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r7] قتادة | reports | Qatāda glosses al-dahr as a lifetime, equivalent in meaning to years and days.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r8] قراءة | reports | An alternate reading adds the wording dahr yamurr, time that passes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:45:24/r9] ابن عيينة | reports | Ibn ʿUyayna says the pagan Arabs thought time itself killed, gave life, and caused death; the verse came down about them.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:45:24/r1] author | holds | ما هي refers either to life itself or the present state; the speakers claim there is only this worldly life.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:45:24/r2] author | holds | نموت ونحيا may describe humans as previously dead drops then alive, individuals dying while descendants live, alternating deaths and lives, or death and life with no afterlife; the words may also imply transmigration, held by many idolaters.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:45:24/r3] author | holds | The claim that time alone destroys them attributes events independently to time and celestial motions; it may deny resurrection as well, or both claims, without knowledge or evidence.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:45:24/r4] author | holds | They merely conjecture, relying on inherited belief and rejecting what they have not perceived.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p133/r1] author | reports | “We die and live” may mean parents die while their children live, some people die while others live, or lifeless semen becomes alive; it may also simply deny any life after worldly death.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p133/r2] author | reports | Another view reads the statement as transmigration: a person dies and the soul then inhabits a lifeless body and lives there.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p133/r3] author | holds | They claimed that days and nights cause death, denying the Angel of Death and God's command to seize souls, and blaming events on time.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p133/r4] author | reports | The Prophet said not to curse time, for God—not time—is the bringer of events.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p133/r5] author | holds | Their claim rests on conjecture and guesswork, not knowledge or certainty.
