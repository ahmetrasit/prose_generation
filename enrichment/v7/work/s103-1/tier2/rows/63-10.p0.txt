## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:63:9-11/r2] author | holds | Before death, hypocrites should spend their wealth; at death one asks for a short delay to give alms and obey God's commands.
[MUQATIL:63:9-11/r3] author | holds | Aṣṣaddaq means purify my wealth by almsgiving; being among the righteous means being among believers, as in 9:75. | mentions 9:75

## FARRA: al-Farrāʾ, d. 207 AH
[FARRA:v3p159/r4] author | holds | وأكن is jussive by returning to the conditional verb as if the ف in فأصدق were absent; Abdullah's reading وأكون returns to the overt verb and is accusative.
[FARRA:v3p159/r5] author | holds | The common accusative is allowed even without written واو because Arabic sometimes omits that letter, as in manuscript spellings such as سليمن and فقلا.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v22p671/r1] author | holds | Believers should spend before death; the dying person asks for a brief reprieve to give alms and obey God by fulfilling obligations.
[TAB-FULL:v22p671/r2] بعض أهل التأويل | reports | Another interpretation of becoming righteous is to perform the pilgrimage to the Sacred House.
[TAB-FULL:v22p671/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says a person who dies without paying zakat or performing Hajj asks to return; he reads “give alms” as zakat and “be righteous” as Hajj.
[TAB-FULL:v22p671/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says Hajj is due when one has a mount to carry them and provisions to get there.
[TAB-FULL:v22p673/r1] الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk describes a believer dying with wealth but unpaid zakat, unperformed Hajj, and unpaid dues to God; he asks to return, but death is not delayed.
[TAB-FULL:v22p673/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās similarly says such a believer asks to return in order to give charity and zakat before the appointed death.
[TAB-FULL:v22p673/r3] سفيان | reports | Sufyān glosses the requested alms and righteousness as zakat and Hajj.
[TAB-FULL:v22p673/r4] author | holds | Most regional readers recite wa-akun with jussive mood, coordinated with the implied jussive after fa-asaddaq; Ibn Muḥayṣin and Abū ʿAmr read wa-akūna in the subjunctive, coordinated with the subjunctive fa-asaddaq. Both readings are valid.

## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH
[ZAJJAJ:v5p177/r3] author | reports | Tafsīr says anyone who neglected zakat or pilgrimage asks for another chance when facing death.
[ZAJJAJ:v5p177/r4] author | reports | The reading fa-aṣṣaddaq wa-akun makes the first verb a response to lawlā (“if only you delayed me”); akūn is jussive by coordination with that response’s syntactic position and conditional sense.
[ZAJJAJ:v5p177/r5] author | reports | The reading fa-aṣṣaddaq wa-akūna follows the overt form of the first verb, with both verbs in the same mood.

## IBNMUJAHID: Ibn Mujāhid, d. 324 AH
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p635/r12] ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي | reports | These readers use jazm in وأكن and omit its wāw; Abū ʿAmr reads وأكون with wāw.

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r1] قيل | reports | The wording indicates that what God calls provision is lawful provision.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r10] author | holds | Aṣaddaq derives from ataṣaddaq, with tāʾ assimilated into ṣād because they are close within one word.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r11] الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk reports Ibn ʿAbbās's reading wa-azakkā.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r12] غيره | reports | Another explanation says being righteous means performing obligations and avoiding prohibitions, that is, being righteous among the righteous.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r2] author | holds | Fa-aṣṣaddaq is the response to the wish introduced by laula.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r3] author | holds | Wa-akun is coordinated to the position before fa, not to the words after it; Arabic often coordinates to underlying position.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r4] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan, Ibn Muḥayṣin, and Abū ʿAmr read wa-akūna in the accusative; a similar reading is attributed to Ubayy and Ibn Masʿūd, but it differs from the canonical written text.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r5] محمد بن يزيد | rejects | He rejects an appeal to the spelling of kalimn, since Qur'anic analogues yakūnu, takūnu, and nakūnu retain wāw.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r6] غيره | reports | Another grammarian says the wāw in kalimn was omitted because it was already represented in the preceding spelling sequence; ordinary speech needs no such device.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r7] سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh says without fa the verb would be jussive as a response to a question carrying a wish; poetry illustrates it.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r8] سيبويه | reports | He cites a poem as evidence for coordination to underlying syntactic position.
[NAHHAS:v4p436/r9] author | holds | The Qur'anic wa-yadharhum may be jussive on the position after fa, or nominative by following the overt form. | mentions 13:27

## FARISI-HUJJA: Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, d. 377 AH
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p293/r1] أبو عمرو | reports | Abū ʿAmr alone reads وأكون with wāw; he coordinates the verb with the visible, subjunctive form فأصدق.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p293/r2] الباقون | reports | The other readers read وأكن without wāw, coordinating it with فأصدق's underlying jussive position as the response to an implied condition. | mentions 7:186
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p293/r3] author | holds | Both syntactic attachments are defensible: one follows the underlying jussive position and the other the expressed wording; the verse means, 'Delay me and I will give charity.' | mentions 7:186

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v9p323/r2] author | reports | The particle la in the dying person's plea may be extra, expressing a wish, or may mean 'why not?' as a question.
[THALABI:v9p323/r3] author | holds | The person's request to give charity means giving zakat; 'being among the righteous' means believers according to Muqatil and other commentators.
[THALABI:v9p323/r4] author | reports | Another view says the righteous here are pilgrims and that the verse concerns believers rather than hypocrites.
[THALABI:v9p323/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says a person who dies with wealth but unpaid zakat, or who could afford pilgrimage but omitted it, asks at death to return and give charity, meaning to perform Hajj.
[THALABI:v9p323/r6] author | reports | Abu Amr and Ibn Muhaysin read 'wa-akūna' with a waw and subjunctive nūn as a response to the wish or question; Abu Amr says the codex omitted the waw for brevity.
[THALABI:v9p323/r7] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra cites codices of Abd Allah and Ubayy with 'wa-akūna' and a waw as support for that reading.
[THALABI:v9p324/r1] أبو عبيد | prefers | Abu Ubayd prefers the jussive 'wa-akun' because it is in Uthman's codex, used by most readers, and grammatically coordinates with the underlying position of 'fa-asaddaq'; an old poetic line illustrates this.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p477/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says spending from what God provided means paying the wealth's alms.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p477/r6] أبو إسحاق | reports | Abu Ishaq says the warning urges continuous remembrance and generosity before death makes the person certain of what is coming and asking to be delayed.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p477/r7] author | holds | The dying person asks for a short extension of his term so that he can give alms and pay zakat.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r1] عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | Ata transmits Ibn Abbas's argument that these people were not believers, since a believer does not ask to return to life; only an unbeliever does.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r10] author | reports | A further poetic example carries the clause forward according to the position after a suppressed فاء.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r11] author | prefers | Abu Amr's وأكون follows the spoken surface rather than the underlying meaning, which the author considers preferable because it is more explicit and closer in wording.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r2] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says anyone dying without pilgrimage or alms asks for another chance, and he reads وأكن من الصالحين as pilgrimage.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas defended his reading that being among the righteous means making pilgrimage by reciting the verse and saying أحج.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r4] أبو إسحاق | reports | Abu Ishaq explains the plea as would that You delay me.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r5] author | holds | The jussive reading وأكن follows the syntactic position of فأصدق as a conditional response, while وأكون follows the surface wording.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r6] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad says وأكون follows the preceding wording because فأصدق answers a wish expressed by لولا أخرتني, while the jussive follows the underlying position that would occur without the فاء.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r7] أبو علي | reports | Abu Ali explains the jussive as coordinated with the position of فأصدق: postponement is an implied condition, as in أخرني أصدق.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r8] سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh's poetic examples illustrate grammatical government by an expression's underlying syntactic position; one example has نصب الحديدا after the position of بالجبال.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p478/r9] لبيد | reports | Labid's line likewise places معدا in the accusative according to the underlying position, because من is treated as extra.

## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:63:9-11/r2] author | holds | Spending means paying zakat before death prompts a request for delay to give charity and become righteous, here glossed as performing pilgrimage; anyone falling short in zakat or pilgrimage asks to return at death.
