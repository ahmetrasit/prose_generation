## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r10] author | holds | The wording that they abide in Hell plainly establishes the unbelievers' everlasting punishment.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r11] صاحب الكشاف | reports | Al-Kashshaf analyzes “abiding in Hell” as an apposition, a second predicate of “those,” or a predicate with an omitted subject.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r18] المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazila argue that the verse assigns punishment to bad deeds, which would be unjust if God created human acts.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r19] author | rejects | Al-Razi answers that an unmotivated sin would be accidental, not voluntary, and so could not merit punishment; if motivated, its cause was not the person's act without an infinite regress, making the resulting act compelled and undeserving of reward.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r2] author | holds | After the Trumpet comes accounting and the division between the blessed, whose scales are heavy, and the wretched, whose scales are light.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r3] author | reports | Some infer that everyone must belong either to the saved or the damned, ruling out people with no entitlement to reward or punishment and those whose two are equal.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r4] author | reports | One interpretation treats the scales as a metaphor for justice.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r5] author | reports | A second takes the scales as good deeds: deeds with value win, while weightless deeds like a mirage leave their doer in Hell. | mentions 24:39
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the scales are plural of weighed things: righteous deeds valued by God; “no weight” means no worth. | mentions 18:105
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r7] author | reports | A third interpretation is a real scale with a tongue and two pans, weighing good deeds in beautiful form and evil deeds in ugly form.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas explains losing oneself as being cheated because the believers inherit the lost person's place.
[RAZI-FULL:v23p107/r9] author | reports | Another explanation is that the damned cannot benefit from themselves because they are trapped in punishment.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH

[SAMIN-DURR:23:103/r3] الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh calls this an unusual بدل: only the phrase في جهنم replaces an implied استقروا, as the result of losing oneself is settlement in Hell.
