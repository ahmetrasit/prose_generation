## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:28-29#2/r1] أبو ذؤيب | reports | Abu Dhuʾayb's verse of the night traveler leaning on an elbow is cited as evidence for مرتفق meaning a place or posture of leaning.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#3/r4] الزجاج | reports | The declaration ‘the truth is from your Lord’ may mean that the revelation is true and came from God rather than from the Prophet himself.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#3/r5] author | reports | Another gloss says the proof has appeared, truth is clear, and doubt has been removed.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#3/r6] author | holds | ‘Let whoever wishes believe and whoever wishes disbelieve’ is a threat, not permission: each person chooses what harms or benefits himself, not God.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#3/r7] author | holds | The imperative form works as a threat because the person threatened is effectively being ordered to bring humiliation on himself.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#4/r1] ابن عباس | reports | The fire's سرادق is a wall of fire surrounding the condemned; Qatada alternatively takes it as the smoke and flame reaching them before the fire, while Abu Muslim treats it as an image of encirclement on every side. | mentions 77:30
[TABRISI:18:28-29#4/r2] author | reports | Those suffering thirst and heat who beg for relief are given water like molten metal; Ibn Masʿud identifies it as melted lead, copper, or brass.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#4/r3] author | reports | Other reported explanations of مهل are oil scum that burns the scalp when approached, oil dregs, pus and blood, liquid at extreme heat, or black water in a wholly black Hell; authorities include Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Saʿid ibn Jubayr, and al-Dahhak.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#4/r4] author | holds | The water scorches and cooks faces as it nears them; it is called relief only because the text pairs it with the word for being given relief.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#4/r5] مجاهد | reports | The fire is a wretched place to lean or gather in; Mujahid derives مرتفق from companionship and assembly.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#4/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas and ʿAṭāʾ gloss مرتفق as a lodging or settled abode.
[TABRISI:18:28-29/r5] author | holds | The word فرط means exceeding the truth and going beyond proper limits; سرادق is the surrounding tent or cloth enclosure.
[TABRISI:18:28-29/r6] رؤبة | reports | Ruʾba's line about a canopy of glory is cited to attest that سرادق means an enclosing pavilion or canopy.
[TABRISI:18:28-29/r7] author | holds | The word مهل can mean the thick residue of oil, molten copper, or another melted metal; مرتفق is a place to lean on, from leaning on one's elbow, and may also suggest a place of benefit.

[RAZI-FULL:v21p101#2/r1] author | reports | One account of the passage's order says it answers wealthy leaders who conditioned belief on expelling the poor: acceptance benefits them, refusal harms them, and social status is irrelevant.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p101#2/r2] author | reports | A second account identifies the truth from God as the command to keep company with poor believers and disregard worldly leaders.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p101#2/r3] author | reports | A third account connects the passage to the freedom to believe or disbelieve and God's refusal to expel believers to attract unbelieving elites.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p101#2/r4] author | rejects | The objection that expelling the poor would sacrifice a small harm to them to prevent the greater harm of unbelief is rejected: belief conditional on avoiding the poor is ugly hypocrisy.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p101#3/r1] المعتزلة | reports | The Mu'tazila take the invitation to believe or disbelieve as explicit proof that faith, disbelief, obedience, and sin are left to human choice.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p101#3/r2] author | holds | Razi argues that actions require intention and choice, but every intention cannot depend on an earlier one indefinitely; the chain must end in a will God creates necessarily in a person.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p101#3/r3] author | holds | When the decisive will exists, the action follows necessarily; when it is absent, the action does not occur, leaving humans compelled in the form of choosers.
