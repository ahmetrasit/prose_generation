[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p363#2/r1] author | holds | These verses draw people toward charity by exposing Satan's whispering and hostility, recalling God's reward and gift of wisdom, and urging reflection on spending and vows, whether public or hidden.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p363#2/r2] author | holds | An unrestricted promise normally denotes good, but here the promise is explicitly attached to the unwelcome prospect of poverty; fahshāʾ covers all ugly acts and all sins.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:2:268/r1] author | holds | Faqr means need, opposite of wealth; related forms describe poverty, the back vertebrae, a calamity that breaks them, and a mount lowered for riding.
[TABRISI:2:268/r2] author | holds | Waʿd, promise, can refer to good or evil when qualified, but when unqualified or left vague it denotes good; waʿīd is specifically a threat of evil.
[TABRISI:2:268/r3] علي بن عيسى | reports | ʿAlī b. ʿĪsā glosses faḥshāʾ as sins; the miser is called fāḥish because refusing guests and petitioners is offensive, as illustrated by poetry of Ṭarafa and Kaʿb.
[TABRISI:2:268/r4] author | holds | Satan frightens people that giving to good causes or giving their best wealth will make them poor; one view specifies payment of zakat.
[TABRISI:2:268/r5] author | reports | Satan's command to faḥshāʾ means sins and abandoning obedience, or spending from inferior wealth; that is disobedience and can sever ties when the wealthy withhold from needy kin and neighbors.
[TABRISI:2:268/r6] author | holds | God promises that giving the best wealth will cover sins and pardon punishment, replace the gift with better provision, and increase His bounty.
[TABRISI:2:268/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās divides the promises into two from God—pardon and increased provision—and two from Satan—fear of poverty and an order to sin.
[TABRISI:2:268/r8] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd, with a similar report from Abū ʿAbd Allāh, describes one prompting from Satan and one from the angel: Satan urges poverty and sin, while the angel urges giving and forbids sin.
[TABRISI:2:268/r9] author | holds | God's breadth means that giving does not harm Him or diminish His stores; He knows who deserves a gift.

[RAZI-FULL:v7p56#2/r3] author | holds | Faqr and faqr are two forms for poverty; its root is breaking the backbone, illustrated by Ṭarafa's line, and al-Kashshāf notes two vocalized readings.

[RAZI-FULL:v7p57/r4] author | prefers | The hereafter's promise deserves trust over Satan's worldly poverty threat: the afterlife is certain, God's forgiveness is guaranteed, hoarded wealth may be unusable, worldly pleasure ends, and afterlife benefits are lasting and pure.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p58/r1] author | holds | مغفرة means erasure of sins, as in Q 9:103; its indefinite form and the phrase منه magnify it because the giver's greatness magnifies the gift. | mentions 9:103
[RAZI-FULL:v7p58/r2] author | reports | The exceptional forgiveness may turn sins into good deeds, make the donor an intercessor for other sinners, or include afterlife realities beyond present human understanding. | mentions 25:70
[RAZI-FULL:v7p58/r3] author | prefers | فضل can mean worldly recompense, but al-Rāzī prefers social goodwill: people helped by a generous person pray for and assist him, opening worldly good; he also explains psychological and spiritual benefits of giving.
