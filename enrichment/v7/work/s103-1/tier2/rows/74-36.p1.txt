## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p336/r3] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj reads nadhiran as an active participle in the circumstantial accusative from the pronoun in innaha.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p336/r4] author | rejects | The proposed attachments to the opening command, its later warning command, or al-kubar and its pronoun are unsound; Abu al-Baqaʾ’s circumstantial reading from the whole clause’s implied sense is acceptable.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p336/r7] أبو رزين وابن زيد | reports | Abu Razin identifies the warner as God with an omitted verb; Ibn Zayd identifies him as Muhammad, likewise supplied by an omitted verb.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r1] author | reports | One analysis makes ‘nadhiran’ an accusative specification of ‘ihda’, which conveys greatness; it means warning just as nakir means denunciation, as in the example ‘one of the women in chastity’. 
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r10] ابن أبي عبلة، أبي بن كعب | reports | The common reading has ‘nadhiran’ in the accusative; Ibn Abi ‘Abla and Ubayy ibn Ka‘b read it nominative. If Hell is intended it can be a second predicate or predicate of omitted ‘hiya’; if God or the Messenger is intended, an omitted ‘huwa’ is understood.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r11] author | holds | ‘Lil-bashar’ may be an adjective or the object of ‘nadhir’; the preposition li- is extra, strengthening the governor.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r2] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra’ treats ‘nadhiran’ as a verbal noun meaning warning, governed by an omitted verb.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r3] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj takes ‘nadhir’ as an active form with a passive sense and a circumstantial accusative from the pronoun in ‘innaha’.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r4] author | reports | Other proposed governors make it circumstantial from ‘ihda’ understood as magnificent, from the subject of ‘qum’ at the sura’s opening, or a verbal noun governed by ‘anذir’ there.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r5] author | reports | Further analyses make it circumstantial from ‘al-kubar’ or its pronoun, from ‘li-ihda’ per Ibn ‘Atiyya, or an accusative with an omitted ‘I mean’. 
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r6] author | reports | It may instead be governed by an omitted ‘call’ with God intended, or by ‘call’ or ‘convey’ with the Messenger intended; another analysis supplies ‘it became a warning’. 
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r7] author | reports | Additional circumstantial analyses attach it to the pronoun in ‘kubar’ or ‘huwa’ in the statement that none knows the Lord’s hosts but Him.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r8] أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa treats it as an object of purpose, governed by the verbal meaning in ‘al-kubar’; the author calls that analysis distant.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:36/r9] أبو جعفر | reports | When ‘nadhiran’ is circumstantial from a feminine noun, its masculine form is explained by an implied ‘possessing warning’ and a relational sense, according to Abu Ja‘far.
