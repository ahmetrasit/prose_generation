## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8#2/r1] author | reports | موازين may be the plural of ميزان, the instrument: one scale for each person according to exegetical reports, or scales corresponding to numerous deeds; the state is expressed through its place.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8#2/r2] author | reports | Alternatively, موازين is the plural of موزون and refers to the deeds being weighed.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8/r1] author | holds | On the first parse, الوزن is the subject and يومئذ is its predicate; the genitive clause after إذ is omitted and its tanwin compensates for it, as the majority hold against al-Akhfash.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8/r2] الجمهور | reports | The majority treat the tanwin in يومئذ as compensation for the omitted genitive clause after إذ; al-Akhfash disagrees.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8/r3] مكي | reports | If يومئذ is the predicate, الحق may be an adjective of الوزن, an omitted predicate answering what the weighing is, or—unusually—an appositive to the pronoun within the adverb.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8/r4] author | reports | An alternative makes الحق the predicate and يومئذ either a time adverb governed by الوزن or, very weakly, a broad object.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8/r5] أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa rejects treating الحق as an adjective after يومئذ because he sees an interruption between the relative and its clause.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8/r6] author | rejects | The relative clause is already complete before الحق, so the adjective is not separated from its clause; that adjective parse would instead leave the subject without a predicate.
[SAMIN-DURR:7:8/r7] author | rejects | Detailed hypothetical rearrangements of الحق and يومئذ, and discussion of an unattested accusative reading, are unnecessary because the Qur'an's wording is fixed.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p389#2/r1] ابن عباس | reports | One view weighs deeds themselves, which God turns into bodies; al-Baghawi reports this from Ibn Abbas, with traditions portraying Qur’an, surahs, and righteous deeds as visible forms.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p389#2/r2] author | reports | A second view weighs the record of deeds; the report of the card bearing There is no god but God outweighing ninety-nine scrolls is graded sound by al-Tirmidhi.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p389#2/r3] author | reports | A third view weighs the person: a very heavy man may weigh less than a gnat’s wing, while Ibn Masud’s thin legs outweigh Mount Uhud. | mentions 18:105
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p389#2/r4] author | prefers | Ibn Kathir sees no conflict: deeds, their records or forms, and the people who performed them may all be weighed at different times.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v3p389/r1] author | holds | The scales on Judgment Day weigh deeds with perfect justice; God wrongs no one, and the heavier scale brings success while the lighter one brings ruin. | mentions 21:47, 4:40, 101:6, 101:7, 101:8, 101:9, 101:10, 101:11, 23:101, 23:102, 23:103

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH

## DURR-FULL: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r1] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In a dialogue, the Prophet defines faith as belief in God, angels, books, messengers, Paradise and Hell, the balance, resurrection, and divine decree.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r10] ابن عباس | reports | The balance has a tongue and two pans; heavy scales bring success, while light scales mean losing oneself and one's home in Paradise for wronging God's signs.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r2] مجاهد | reports | The weighing is justice; heavy scales mean good deeds, and the transmitted gloss for light scales also says good deeds.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r3] عبد الله بن العيزار | reports | A caller beside the balance announces each person's lasting felicity or misery according to whether the scales are heavy or light.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r4] السدي | reports | Deeds themselves are weighed.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r5] وهب بن منبه | reports | Only the endings of deeds are weighed: God grants a good ending to the one He wills good for and an evil ending to the one He wills evil for.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r6] الحارث الأعور | reports | Truth feels heavy to people of truth as it is heavy on the balance, and light to people of falsehood as it is light there.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r7] الحسن | reports | Hasan says the balance has a tongue and two pans.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r8] كعب | reports | The balance is placed between two trees near Jerusalem.
[DURR-FULL:v3p417/r9] حذيفة | reports | Gabriel oversees the balances: an oppressor's good deeds are transferred to the victim, or the victim's sins to the oppressor if the oppressor has no good deeds.
[DURR-FULL:v3p418/r1] قتادة | reports | Asked whether people remember their families at resurrection, the Prophet says not at the balance, when records fly, or at the bridge.
[DURR-FULL:v3p418/r2] ابن عباس | reports | One more good deed than bad leads to Paradise and one more bad than good to Hell; equal deeds place a person among the people of the Heights, and a single grain can tip the balance.
[DURR-FULL:v3p418/r3] علي بن أبي طالب | reports | A person whose outward state outweighs the inward has a light balance; one whose inward outweighs the outward has a heavy balance.
[DURR-FULL:v3p418/r4] جابر | reports | The Prophet says good and bad deeds are weighed; whichever side prevails determines entry to Paradise or Hell.
[DURR-FULL:v3p418/r5] أنس | reports | An angel announces a person's lasting felicity or misery according to whether the balance tips heavy or light.
[DURR-FULL:v3p418/r6] عائشة | reports | The Prophet says people do not remember one another while the balance is placed, until each knows whether it is heavy or light.
[DURR-FULL:v3p418/r7] عائشة | reports | The Prophet adds that people do not remember one another as records scatter until they know where each book lands, citing 69:19. | mentions 69:19
[DURR-FULL:v3p418/r8] عائشة | reports | At the bridge over Hell, hooks and thorns detain whom God wills until they know whether they will escape.
