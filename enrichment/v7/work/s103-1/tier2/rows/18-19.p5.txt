## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:19/r1] author | holds | Their awakening is a second sign of God's power, teaching them about resurrection and prompting gratitude and certainty.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:19/r2] author | holds | Thinking they had slept a day or part of one reflects a sleeper's inability to measure time; alternatively, they entered in the morning and awoke at noon, then reconsidered after seeing their hair and nails.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:19/r3] author | reports | The line saying their Lord knows best may be one party correcting the others, or an objection by the others to their estimate.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:19/r4] author | holds | Their silver waraqa may be coined or uncoined; variant readings lighten, double, or assimilate its consonants, and taking food shows that trusting God still permits provision.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:19/r5] author | holds | The city is Tarsus; azka food means lawful and tasty, or more abundant and cheaper.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:19/r6] author | holds | The messenger should deal gently to avoid being cheated or act discreetly to avoid recognition, and must do nothing that alerts anyone.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r1] author | holds | As God made them sleep, He awakened them too, displaying His power over both sleep and resurrection.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r10] author | reports | Some scholars, longing for the Sacred House, said travel requires only packed provisions and trust in the Merciful.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r11] author | holds | In “which of them,” the أهلها is elided, as in “ask the town”; azkā can mean more lawful, wholesome, plentiful, or cheaper.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r12] author | holds | Ṭaʿāman is a specification (tamyīz), and the envoy should obtain provisions from the food he finds.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r13] author | holds | He should act gently in the purchase so he is not cheated, or remain discreet so no one recognizes him.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r14] author | holds | The envoy must not cause anyone to discover them, even unintentionally; the source calls him the cause of their being noticed.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r2] author | holds | Their questioning helps them learn what happened, reflect on God's power, increase certainty, and give thanks for His favor.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r3] author | holds | The questioner was their leader, who asked how long they had stayed.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r4] author | holds | Their answer of a day or part of one rests on predominant conjecture and shows that reasoning and acting on such conjecture are permissible.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r5] author | reports | Some answered that God knew best after signs or inspiration showed them the stay had been long and its exact duration was known only to Him.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r6] author | reports | A report says they entered in the morning and woke after noon, then judged from their long nails and hair that the sleep had lasted a long time.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r7] ابن عباس | prefers | Ibn ʿAbbās inferred that the sleepers were seven from the singular questioner, the plural answer, and a further plural reply.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r8] author | holds | One of them was sent, namely Tamlīkha; wariq is silver, whether coined or uncoined, and several readers pronounce its rāʾ still.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p7/r9] author | holds | The city is Tarsus; taking provisions for travel is proper for those who trust God, unlike relying on chance or empty claims of reliance.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:18:19#2/r1] author | holds | The purest food means the city's best knowledge, free of excess, disputation, dialectic, grammar, and empty appearances that cannot strengthen the soul; knowledge nourishes the heart. | mentions 88:7
[QASHANI:18:19#2/r2] author | holds | Be discerning and choose a verified pure-hearted, upright, complete guide, not a superficial pretender who claims to teach what he lacks; companionship yields insight.
[QASHANI:18:19#2/r3] author | holds | Alternatively, act discreetly so ignorant people do not learn their situation or faith; if the youths symbolize spiritual faculties, the envoy is thought, city their gathered faculties, and the best food intellect rather than illusion, imagination, or sensation.
[QASHANI:18:19/r1] author | holds | Their raising is genuine spiritual awakening: they discuss latent meanings and realities of their capacities to actualize them; Sufis call this first awakening wakefulness.
[QASHANI:18:19/r2] author | holds | The question of their duration repeats an earlier interpretation; the correct group leaves it to God, then sends one with silver to the city for insight and completion.
[QASHANI:18:19/r3] author | holds | Silver signifies innate first principles that yield truths and divine knowledge; the city is a place of gathering or the city of knowledge, whose gate is ʿAlī.
[QASHANI:18:19/r4] عليه السلام | reports | A prophetic saying calls himself the city of knowledge and ʿAlī its gate.
[QASHANI:18:19/r5] author | holds | One envoy suffices because full perfection does not depend on everyone learning; the highest knowledge of one person can teach and awaken the rest. | mentions 9:122
