[SAMIN-DURR:68:25/r3] author | reports | An unnamed poet pairs hard with anger, supporting the sense of fury; the source says the two words are coordinated because their wording differs.
[SAMIN-DURR:68:25/r4] author | reports | Hard may mean withholding or scarcity: camels give little milk and a year little rain; Abu Ubayd and al-Qutaybi report this usage.
[SAMIN-DURR:68:25/r5] author | holds | The word has several vocalized forms and derivatives, including ‘hardan’ and ‘harid’; Arabic also describes a lion as hard and lions as hawarid.
[SAMIN-DURR:68:25/r6] الأصمعي | reports | Hard may mean isolation or withdrawal; the verb can mean to withdraw, and a solitary star is called kawkab hard. Al-Asma‘i identifies the usage as Hudhayli.
[SAMIN-DURR:68:25/r7] author | reports | Another lexical view is intention or direction: ‘harada yaḥrudu hardaka’ means to intend your direction; a cited verse illustrates the expression.
[SAMIN-DURR:68:25/r8] author | holds | The verse has been interpreted according to all the listed senses of hard.
[SAMIN-DURR:68:25/r9] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says Hard is the proper name of the owners’ particular garden.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p196#2/r1] author | holds | They thought they had power over their orchard and could carry out their plan.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p196/r4] author | holds | The phrase “on resolve” can mean strength and severity; other reported meanings are earnestness, anger, hostility toward the poor, or the name of their town, with the last rejected as far-fetched.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p196/r5] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid explains “on resolve” as earnestness.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p196/r6] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima explains “on resolve” as anger.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p196/r7] الشعبي | reports | Al-Sha‘bi explains the phrase as resolve against the poor.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p196/r8] السدي | rejects | Al-Suddi says Harad was the name of their village, but Ibn Kathir dismisses this as a distant interpretation.

[BIQAI-FULL:v8p105/r6] author | reports | Ḥard can mean aim, intense anger, firm insistence, haste, stinginess, or lack of good, as in a season without rain or a camel withholding milk.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p105/r7] author | holds | They thought themselves capable of harvesting because they had made no exception; such certainty about a future act presumes a power no creature has.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r1] أبو عبيد وغيره | reports | They gloss حرد as prevention, drawing on camels whose milk dries up and a year with little rain and fertility.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r2] author | holds | The prepositional phrase is fronted before قادرين for exclusivity and rhyme: they set out able only to deprive, despite being able to benefit the poor.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r3] author | reports | On one reading, they sought to deprive the poor but received deprivation themselves; another says their garden was barren, so they lost its benefits.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r4] author | reports | Alternatively حرد attaches to غدوا and means the garden's barrenness, punning with حرث; capable is mock reversal of their plan to reap and deny the poor.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r5] أبو نصر أحمد بن حاتم | reports | Abu Nasr reports حرد with fatḥa as anger and cites a line describing horses filled with rage; they can only anger one another.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r6] سفيان والسدي | reports | Sufyan and al-Suddi transmit the interpretation that the brothers could only anger one another, as in their later reproaching each other.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reports حرد as intention and speed: they hurried toward the garden believing they could reap it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r8] الأزهري والسدي | reports | Al-Azhari calls حرد the name of their village, and al-Suddi gives it as the garden's name; the author doubts this was intended.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p31#3/r9] author | reports | Other glosses make حرد mean their isolation from the poor or their restriction of the poor from the charity their father gave.

[ELMALILI:68:7-33#5/r10] author | holds | Ḥard is read as forceful prevention and despotism: they set out early with power only for greed and anger.
[ELMALILI:68:7-33#5/r11] author | holds | Collective speed and despotism can be more powerful and dreadful than an individual's.

## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p84/r1] author | holds | وغدوا على حرد قادرين is a circumstantial clause with an implicit قد, describing their state as they set out.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p84/r2] author | holds | Repeating غدوا in the circumstantial clause makes their ill-fated early departure striking; Ibn al-Qays's verse similarly turns back to the night already described.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p84/r3] امرؤ القيس | reports | The quoted verse about spending the night awake illustrates the rhetorical return to an already-mentioned night.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p84/r4] author | reports | حرد can mean prevention, forceful purpose or speed, or anger.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p84/r5] author | holds | The word's selection has a rhetorical subtlety because its different meanings attach aptly either to قادرين or to غدوا.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p84/r6] author | reports | If حرد modifies قادرين, the fronted phrase restricts them to being able to withhold good or fruit, not help anyone; calling this ability is ironic. | mentions 2:264, 75:4
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p84/r7] author | reports | If حرد means speed or resolve, it modifies غدوا and describes their energetic haste; قادرين then conveys their mistaken confidence that they could accomplish their aim.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p84/r8] author | holds | This reading foreshadows their failure, confirmed by their response on seeing the ruined garden and by the preceding calamity. | mentions 68:19, 68:26
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p85/r1] author | reports | If حرد means anger, the phrase means they could do nothing but rage at the poor, having failed to gather the fruit they sought; the fronting restricts their capacity to anger.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p85/r2] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says حرد is the name of their village or orchard; Ibn Ashur suspects this is a contrived explanation drawn from the verbs اغدوا and غدوا.

## BINTSHATI: ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419 AH
[BINTSHATI:v2p63/r4] author | holds | Ḥard means withholding or preventing, with an added sense of aversion, as in a year withholding yield or a camel withholding milk.
[BINTSHATI:v2p63/r9] author | reports | Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture.
