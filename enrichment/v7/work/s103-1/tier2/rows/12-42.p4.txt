## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:12:42/r1] author | reports | If Joseph's expectation that one would be saved came from reasoning, it illustrates human effort; if from revelation, then 'thought' means certain knowledge.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:12:42/r2] author | holds | Joseph asked the saved cupbearer to mention his situation to the king so that he could be released.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:12:42/r3] author | reports | The pronoun in 'Satan made him forget' may refer to the cupbearer, whom Satan made forget to mention Joseph; the source can be attributed through association or refer to mentioning the Lord's affairs.

[BAYDAWI-FULL:12:42/r6] author | holds | Seeking people's help in hardship is generally acceptable but does not suit the station of prophets; bid' means a span from three to nine and comes from cutting.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:12:41-42#2/r1] author | holds | Asking the cupbearer to remember him means seeking stable spiritual existence through love; intoxicated love raises spirit to unity and heart to spirit, but both remain veiled in divine light rather than annihilated.
[QASHANI:12:41-42#2/r2] author | holds | Remaining at that level can produce ego and transgression; Satanic illusion makes Joseph forget annihilation because he seeks the spirit's station and retains a remainder of self.
[QASHANI:12:41-42#2/r3] عليه السلام | reports | A prophetic report says God have mercy on Joseph: had he not asked the cupbearer to remember him, he would not have remained imprisoned for years.
[QASHANI:12:41-42#2/r4] author | holds | Alternatively, illusion forgets the love-messenger when love dominates in bewildered witnessing; the lover forgets creation and self in unity until annihilation ends and divine life restores differentiation.
[QASHANI:12:41-42#2/r5] author | holds | The prison's duration is the spiritual journey; the later visions of cattle and grain symbolize changes in soul and bodily qualities during seclusion and return.
[QASHANI:12:41-42/r4] author | holds | When both faculties function through God rather than themselves, confinement ends and subsistence begins, though a trace of ego remains.
