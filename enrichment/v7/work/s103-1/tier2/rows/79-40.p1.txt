## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:79:34-41/r5] author | holds | The one fearing the Lord’s station ascends to heart and witnesses divine governance, restrains the self from desire through fear or awe, and reaches paradise according to rank.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p401/r3] author | holds | Fear of the Lord's station means fear of standing before Him for judgment; the possessive phrase magnifies that awe.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p401/r4] ابن عباس | reports | He says the person feared God when he was about to sin and stopped himself.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p401/r5] author | holds | Restraining the soul means restraining its desires, usually blameworthy; Sahl says only prophets and some truthful believers escape desire.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p401/r6] بعض الحكماء، عمران الميرتلي، الفضيل | reports | A wise saying advises opposing desire to reach right judgment; Imran's poem warns against obeying it; al-Fudayl calls resisting desire the best deed for both groups.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p317#2/r5] author | holds | Fearing one’s Lord means fearing to stand before Him and face His judgment, and restraining desire by returning oneself to obedience.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p308#s079/r1] author | holds | The surah’s purpose is to establish resurrection and the gathering, displaying humanity’s final fate after the preceding surahs’ full exposition; its oaths show the matter has reached its decisive climax.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p320/r4] author | holds | Fear of standing before the Lord means fearing the encounter or its place and time, through awareness of His kindness and majesty; such fear presupposes belief in resurrection.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p320/r5] author | holds | The soul must be restrained from every desire, since the Fire is surrounded by appetites and religious law opposes what the lower self wants.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p320/r6] الرازي | reports | Al-Rāzī defines blameworthy desire as appetite that conflicts with religious commands.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p320/r7] الجنيد | reports | Al-Junayd says that when the self opposes its desire, its illness becomes its cure.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p320/r8] author | holds | The rhetorical arrangement uses transgression to imply the opposite of restraint and restraint to imply rejection of worldly preference.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v10p254#2/r1] author | holds | Fearing the Lord’s station means fearing to stand before the Master for judgment on the great Day; such fear follows knowledge of creation and return.
[BURSEVI:v10p254#2/r2] author | reports | Maqam may be a verbal noun for standing, a place where servants stand for judgment and recompense, or an emphatic insertion.
[BURSEVI:v10p254#2/r3] author | holds | Fear is paired with transgression because it is the first motive for obedience, followed by hope and then love; these suit ordinary people, the elect, and the most elect, respectively.
[BURSEVI:v10p254#2/r4] author | holds | Restraining the self from desire means refusing natural appetites and worldly ornament because their consequences are harmful; hawa is desire without a religious warrant.
[BURSEVI:v10p254#2/r5] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet said his greatest fear for the community was desire and long hope: desire turns people from truth, while long hope makes them forget the afterlife.
[BURSEVI:v10p254#2/r6] بعض الكبار | reports | A great master says the seven desires listed in 3:14 reduce to worldly play and amusement, then to desire itself; escaping desire frees one from every bond and barrier. | mentions 3:14
[BURSEVI:v10p254#2/r7] سهل | reports | Sahl says only prophets and some, not all, of the truthful escape desire; one is safe by imposing proper discipline on oneself.
[BURSEVI:v10p254#2/r8] author | holds | Bursevi describes the human as an isthmus between Divine and created, angelic and animal realities; from the higher aspect, a person restrains the lower self, as the Prophet addresses his human aspect from his angelic or unitive station.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#4/r1] author | reports | مقام ربه may mean the servant's standing before his Lord on the Great Calamity and the day deeds are recalled, with the possessive construction compared to رقود حلب.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#4/r2] author | reports | Alternatively the phrase means whoever fears his Lord, with مقام treated as extra; this rhetorically establishes fear of God, discussed at length in al-Rahman.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#4/r3] author | holds | Restrain the self from destructive desire by patience, preferring good works, and refusing to be deceived by worldly pleasure and ornament.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#4/r4] ابن عباس ومقاتل | reports | Ibn Abbas and Muqatil describe a person who considers a sin, recalls standing before God in judgment, fears that meeting, and abandons the sin.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#4/r5] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib explains the name هوا by the way desire carries its follower toward weakness in this world and the abyss in the next.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#4/r6] بعض الحكماء | reports | A maxim says that one finds the right answer by looking at one's desire and opposing it; al-Fudayl calls opposition to desire the best deed.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#4/r7] أبو عمران الميرتلي | reports | Abu Imran al-Mirtalli's poem warns that obeying desire or a stubborn self leads a person to ruin.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#5/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the two verses concern the brothers Abu ʿAziz and Musʿab: one was tyrannical and worldly, while Musʿab feared God and restrained desire.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#5/r3] author | reports | Musʿab protected the Prophet at Uhud and died pierced by arrows; the Prophet said he entrusted him to God and recalled his two cloaks and golden sandal strap.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#5/r4] الكشاف | reports | Al-Kashshaf says Musʿab killed his captured brother Abu ʿAziz at Uhud; the account says their mother was exceptionally wealthy.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p36#5/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Another report from Ibn Abbas connects the two verses to Abu Jahl and Musʿab; another says the first verse concerned al-Nadr and his son al-Harith.

## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p88/r4] author | holds | The warning and glad tidings follow the proof of resurrection because judgment follows resurrection; the wise prepare for lasting reward rather than transient pleasure.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p92/r2] author | holds | The blame concerns choosing worldly life over the afterlife; worldly benefits that do not cost one the afterlife are not blameworthy, as the counsel to Qarun shows. | mentions 28:77
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p92/r3] author | holds | The two clauses are parallel: fearing the Lord counters transgression, while restraining the self from desire counters preferring worldly life.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p92/r4] author | holds | Restraining one's self personifies it as another who urges sin; this rhetorical self-separation is illustrated by Urwa ibn Udhayna's verse about heart and conscience.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p92/r5] عروة بن أذينة | reports | His verse illustrates the inner dialogue by which the heart appeals to conscience to restrain the self's impulses.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p92/r6] author | holds | Hawa means what the soul desires, especially appetites or anger contrary to truth and complete benefit; since such desire is unguided, ‘without guidance’ in 28:50 is emphatic. | mentions 28:50
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p92/r7] author | holds | The generic definite article in al-hawa denotes the class of desire.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p93/r2] author | holds | ‘The station of his Lord’ figuratively conveys divine majesty and awe, naming the place of standing for what is associated with it. | mentions 14:14, 55:46
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p93/r3] author | holds | The paired descriptions of remembering and the Garden use a collect-then-divide rhetorical structure; ‘the self’ has the same generic definite sense as ‘the abode.’

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:79:1-41#3/r1] author | holds | God's station as Lord includes the attributes entailed by lordship, such as knowledge, absolute power, dominance, mercy, and wrath; it is the source of forgiveness for believers and punishment for deniers. | mentions 20:81, 20:82, 15:49, 15:50
[TABATABAI:79:1-41#4/r1] author | holds | The station feared is God's lordship, the source of mercy for faithful people and painful punishment for those who deny and disobey.
[TABATABAI:79:1-41#4/r12] جعفر الصادق | reports | Al-Sadiq says that knowing God sees, hears, and knows one's deeds restrains a person from wrongdoing; the author says this supports his interpretation of fearing God's station.
[TABATABAI:79:1-41#4/r13] علي | reports | Ali warns that following desire turns people away from truth, while long hopes make them forget the afterlife.
[TABATABAI:79:1-41#4/r2] قيل | rejects | One view says it is the servant's standing before God on Judgment Day to answer for deeds; the author dismisses this reading.
[TABATABAI:79:1-41#4/r3] قيل | reports | Another reading treats “fearing his Lord's station” as an emphatic insertion meaning simply fearing his Lord, like the phrase “honor his abode.”
[TABATABAI:79:33-41/r6] author | holds | The passage divides people into two exemplary classes, introducing Hell's people first because the address targets polytheists; transgression and preferring worldly life define the former, fear and restraint the latter.
[TABATABAI:79:38-41/r2] author | holds | The verse describes restraining the self from desire rather than never acting on it, since ignorance and weakness may cause a sin without arrogance and God is forgiving. | mentions 53:31, 53:32, 4:31
[TABATABAI:79:38-41/r3] author | holds | The two classes are unbelieving wrongdoers and faithful, pious people; the fate of the weak and those mixing good and bad deeds is left to God, who may forgive
