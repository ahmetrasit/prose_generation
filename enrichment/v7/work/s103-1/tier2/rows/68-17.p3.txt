## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:68:1-33#5/r10] أبو جعفر | reports | Abu Jaʿfar says Meccans were tested by famine just as the garden owners were; their garden was al-Ridwan in Yemen, nine miles from Sanaʿa.
[TABATABAI:68:1-33#5/r11] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas tells of a father who always gave each rightful person a share, then five sons who planned to keep an unusually abundant harvest from Muslim poor; four agreed and the moderate brother objected.
[TABATABAI:68:17-21/r3] author | holds | The garden episode is a warning to the Prophet's deniers: like its owners, they arrogantly rely on wealth, children, and apparent causes without gratitude, then face sudden punishment, useless possessions, regret, and repentance too late.
[TABATABAI:68:17-21/r4] author | holds | If these verses were revealed with the preceding passage, their warning applies to the deniers generally and recalls the surprise defeat at Badr as worldly proof that apparent causes fail.
[TABATABAI:68:17-21/r5] قيل | reports | Reports say the passage was revealed during a famine that struck Mecca after the Prophet prayed for years of hardship like Joseph's; the garden owners then parallel Mecca, though the final mutual blame does not fit easily.
[TABATABAI:68:17-21/r6] author | holds | Balāʾ can mean a test or the affliction itself; sarm is cutting fruit, istithnaʾ is excluding some from a general rule or saying if God wills, and a night visitor is a punishment arriving at night.
[TABATABAI:68:17/r1] author | holds | The surah consoles the Prophet after the polytheists accuse him of madness, promises reward and honors his great character, forbids compliance and compromise with them, and commands patience for God's judgment.
[TABATABAI:68:17/r2] author | holds | The surah's overall context is Meccan; the author considers it plausible that the seventeen verses about the garden owners were revealed in Medina.

## BINTSHATI: ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419 AH
[BINTSHATI:v2p39/r1] author | holds | The surah is early Meccan except verses 17–33 and 48–50, and is commonly placed after al-ʿAlaq as the second revelation; despite ordering disputes it reflects the first revelation’s atmosphere. | mentions 96:1
[BINTSHATI:v2p63/r2] author | prefers | The apparent story is that the garden owners planned to harvest everything at dawn and leave nothing; the author rejects the gloss that they merely omitted saying in shāʾ Allāh.
[BINTSHATI:v2p63/r9] author | reports | Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture.
[BINTSHATI:v2p65/r3] author | holds | The plural pronoun in we tested them refers to the deniers and every oath-breaking, contemptible person.
[BINTSHATI:v2p65/r4] أبو حيان | reports | One interpretation compares Quraysh's trial to the garden owners' punishment by citing the seven-year famine, when Quraysh ate hides.
[BINTSHATI:v2p65/r5] أبو حيان | reports | Another interpretation compares the garden owners' planned deprivation of the poor with Quraysh's vow before Badr to kill the Prophet and his companions, followed by their defeat. | mentions 3:123
[BINTSHATI:v2p65/r6] author | rejects | The Badr interpretation reads later history into the passage, since Badr occurred about five years after this surah; the warning addresses tyrants generally. | mentions 3:123

[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p419/r3] author | holds | The orchard parable warns Quraysh that present peace and blessings do not last: God can remove them suddenly and leave people to face the consequences.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p431/r2] author | holds | The orchard parable exposes the hollowness of Quraysh leaders’ authority: God can remove it instantly, after which their grief and repentance will be useless.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p431/r3] author | holds | The plural pronoun ‘them’ refers to the previously described people collectively, confirming that the portrait is of Quraysh leadership as a whole rather than one individual.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p431/r4] author | holds | The definite article in ‘the orchard’ is generic in parables, painting a complete situation rather than identifying a particular orchard or historical owners in Yemen or Sana.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p431/r5] author | holds | The owners confidently swore they would harvest the next morning because they believed their orchard had survived every weather change and was secure from calamity.

[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:68:17/r1] author | holds | The garden parable warns Meccan polytheists who failed to thank God for blessings; a pious owner shared harvest with the poor, but his sons planned to exclude them and a night calamity destroyed the crop.
