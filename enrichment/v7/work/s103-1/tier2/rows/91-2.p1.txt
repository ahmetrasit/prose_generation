## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v31p171/r1] author | holds | The sura urges obedience and warns against disobedience; oaths by beneficial created things prompt reflection and gratitude because divine oaths weigh heavily on the heart.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p171/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra explains imala in endings with roots from waw by their resemblance to words beginning with ya; forms such as tala and daha can shift toward ya in verbs.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p171/r6] author | holds | Full non-imala is also sound because many Arabs do not incline these alifs toward ya, and letters that historically shifted from waw or ya do not always reveal the shift in pronunciation.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p171/r7] author | holds | Hamza's mixed approach is also good: only alifs derived from ya warrant imala, while tala, daha and similar forms derive from waw.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p172/r10] author | reports | A further view says the moon follows in apparent size and its relation to worldly interests, with a special astronomical affinity to the sun.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p172/r6] عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | The moon follows the sun by remaining visible after its setting in the first half of the lunar month.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p172/r7] قتادة والكلبي | reports | Qatada and al-Kalbi say the moon follows the sun by setting after it on the new moon night.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p172/r8] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra understands following as taking light from the sun.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p172/r9] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says the moon follows the sun when it becomes full and nearly matches its brightness on the white nights.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p173#2/r2] القفال | reports | Al-Qaffal says all four oaths are really about the sun through its midday light, the moon's borrowing its light, its emergence in daytime and its concealment at night.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:2/r1] author | holds | The moon follows the sun when it becomes visible after sunset; talaw means to follow.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:2/r2] قتادة | reports | He says this occurs on the night of the new moon, when the moon is seen after sunset.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:2/r3] ابن زيد | reports | In the first half of the month the moon follows the setting sun by rising afterward; in the second half it follows by setting after it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:2/r4] الفراء | reports | He says the moon takes some of the sun's light.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:2/r5] الزجاج | reports | Another view says the moon resembles the sun in light and brightness when full and round.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:91:1-4/r2] author | holds | The moon follows the sun’s rising early in the month, its setting at full moon, or the sun’s cycle of form and light.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p341#5/r1] author | holds | The moon follows the sun in brightness and light during the first half of the month, taking over its light.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p484/r7] author | holds | The Meccan surah swears by parts of the upper and lower worlds and by the soul as an instrument of reflection; it moves from disbelievers' afterlife to their worldly destruction and eventual Fire.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p485/r10] الزجاج وغيره | reports | Al-Zajjāj and others gloss the phrase as becoming full and round; the moon follows the sun in light and magnitude as no other planet does.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p485/r11] author | reports | One account says that in the month's second half the sun sets as the moon rises, while in the first half the moon sets after the sun.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p485/r12] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says the moon rises at sunset, drawing on the sun's light during the first half of the month.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p485/r6] الحسن والفراء | reports | Al-Hasan and al-Farrāʾ say the moon habitually follows the sun because it receives its light from it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p485/r7] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says the moon follows the sun throughout the month, rising after it in the first half and setting after it in the second.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p485/r8] ابن سلام | reports | Ibn Sallām limits the following to the first half: the moon takes the sun's place and rises after it sets.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p485/r9] قتادة | reports | Qatada takes the referent to be the full moon: the sun sets and the moon rises.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p486/r3] author | prefers | For eloquence, all the pronouns through يغشاها should refer to the sun; this matches the preceding description of day revealing it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p486/r5] القفال | reports | Al-Qaffāl summarizes the oaths as four solar aspects: daytime brightness and activity, the moon taking its light, full appearance, and disappearance at night.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p487/r1] أصحابنا | reports | The author's colleagues interpret سبحان من سخركن as a proper name, with mā infinitival and temporal.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#2/r1] author | reports | The Shaykh invokes the Sibawayh poem as a case of coordinating a genitive plus nominative pair with another pair; the author says this concedes the disputed coordination, though the poem has an additional complication.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#2/r2] الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil says the last two wāws in the oath sequence coordinate nouns, while the first is like the prepositional wāw and the tā’ of swearing. | mentions 92:1, 74:33, 74:34, 91:2, 92:1
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#2/r3] ابن كيسان | reports | Ibn Kaysan permits an explicit oath verb with the wāw, as in ‘I swear by God that Zayd is standing’; the author says this objection does not bind al-Zamakhshari’s position.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#2/r4] الشيخ | holds | The Shaykh identifies the deeper puzzle as finding a governor for a future ‘idha’ after an oath: the present oath verb, an implied event, the oath object itself, and an implied circumstantial all face objections. | mentions 53:1, 74:33, 74:34, 92:1
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r1] author | rejects | The Shaykh’s preference that conjunction does not itself govern does not compel al-Zamakhshari, who chooses the alternative grammatical doctrine.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r2] author | rejects | The author insists that the construction does involve two governors: oath wāw governs the genitive and the oath verb governs the time adverb, with both sets of complements coordinated.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r3] author | holds | The Sibawayh poem cited by the Shaykh acknowledges coordination across the two governors, even though the Shaykh relies on Sibawayh’s authority.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r4] author | rejects | Mentioning Ibn Kaysan’s permission to state an oath verb does not refute al-Zamakhshari, because the latter need not share Ibn Kaysan’s view.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r5] author | holds | The Shaykh’s example with a second time phrase preceding the coordinated noun does parallel the verse’s governors, though it raises a separate issue of repeating the construction.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r6] author | rejects | Al-Khalil’s description as ‘disfavored’ supports prohibition rather than rebutting al-Zamakhshari; the Shaykh has either softened the term or misunderstood it.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r7] author | rejects | An omitted construct such as ‘rising of the star’ can be the governor; the author says an oath now may refer to an object that will exist later, so the tense mismatch is no objection.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r8] author | holds | A deleted governor before ‘idha’ is possible as a prospective circumstantial; the oath verb still governs it, and the circumstantial may be understood prospectively.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#3/r9] author | holds | If the sworn object is a physical entity, one can supply an event, making the temporal phrase a circumstantial attached to that event; Ibn al-Hajib addressed the question and the author notes extended debate.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3/r2] author | holds | The ‘idha’ clauses raise a syntactic problem: a conditional needs an answer, while a purely temporal phrase needs a governor; the oath verb is present-time and cannot straightforwardly govern a future time phrase.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3/r3] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari answers that an oath wāw suppresses its verb and stands for both the verb and preposition; following coordinating wāws can therefore govern the same accusative and genitive pattern.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3/r4] الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh rejects the claim that a conjunction governs by standing in for a governor; he says the governor of the coordinated element is the same as for its antecedent, though he does not insist on that point against al-Zamakhshari.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3/r5] الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh says the verse coordinates two nouns with matching case roles, rather than coordinating across two governors, and offers ‘passing by Zayd standing and ‘Amr sitting’ as the pattern.
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3/r6] author | reports | The Shaykh cites a line in Sibawayh that coordinates a genitive and a nominative with another genitive and nominative as a parallel construction.
