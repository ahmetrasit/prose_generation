## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:7:6-9/r2] author | holds | Mīzān means comparing two things to reveal their measure and extends metaphorically to poetic meter and measured speech; truth is putting things where wisdom requires, while weight is downward force and lightness upward force.
[TABRISI:7:7-8/r10] author | prefers | The author prefers the metaphorical justice reading, followed by the literal scale, since Arabs call apt words and deeds “weighed”; he also considers the literal view supported by transmitted reports and the apparent wording.
[TABRISI:7:7-8/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and al-Balkhī understand the balance as justice in the afterlife, where no one is wronged.
[TABRISI:7:7-8/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, and al-Jubbāʾī say God sets up a real balance with a tongue and two pans to weigh good and bad deeds.
[TABRISI:7:7-8/r5] عبد الله بن عمر | reports | One proposed account is that the records of deeds themselves are weighed, reported from ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿUmar and others.
[TABRISI:7:7-8/r6] الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī offers the possibility that signs of good and bad deeds appear in the balance's pans and are seen by people.
[TABRISI:7:7-8/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Another view from Ibn ʿAbbās says good deeds are shown in a beautiful form and bad deeds in an ugly form.
[TABRISI:7:7-8/r8] عبيد بن عمير | reports | ʿUbayd b. ʿUmayr says the believer and unbeliever themselves are weighed; a physically huge man may weigh less than a gnat's wing.
[TABRISI:7:7-8/r9] أبو مسلم | reports | Abū Muslim reads weighing as the believer's high standing and the unbeliever's humiliation, like the declaration that they will have no weight in 18:105. | mentions 18:105
[TABRISI:7:8-9/r1] author | holds | The poetic example speaks of measured, sound speech, including indirect expression; weighing may mean that something stands in the mind as equal to its comparison.
[TABRISI:7:8-9/r2] author | holds | Mawāzīn is plural because each kind of worship may have its own scale, or each scale may correspond to a category of a person's deeds; a report says prayer is a scale.
[TABRISI:7:8-9/r3] author | holds | Those whose scales are heavy succeed by winning God's reward.
