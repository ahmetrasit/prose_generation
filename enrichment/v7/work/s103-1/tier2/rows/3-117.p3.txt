## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r1] author | prefers | فيها صر may be a full subject-predicate clause, or فيها alone may modify ريح with صر as subject; the latter is preferred because simple adjectives are the norm.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r2] author | reports | صر may mean severe burning cold, as the cited line shows; it may also mean صرصر, a cold wind, as Laylā al-Akhyaliyya’s verse shows.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r3] ليلى الأخيلية | reports | Laylā al-Akhyaliyya’s verse uses صرصر for a cold wind.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r4] author | holds | صر derives from tightening and knotting, as in صرة; أصر على means to persist.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r5] الزجاج | reports | Some say صر is the sound of fire in the wind, from صر الشيء يصر صريرا; al-Zajjāj endorses this gloss.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r6] author | holds | If صر means cold or sound, its location in the wind is straightforward; if it describes wind like صرصر, an omitted noun or figurative containment is needed, and the latter is remote.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r7] author | holds | أصابت is an adjective of ريح, not صر, since ريح is feminine and صر masculine; the prepositional description comes first because it is nearer a simple adjective.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r8] author | holds | ظلموا modifies قوم; ظلمهم may refer to the crop owners, whose sins caused destruction, or to the spenders.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#2/r9] ابن عطية | prefers | Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers the spenders as referent because the crop owners appear only in the comparison, not to explain or correct their wrongdoing.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#3/r1] author | holds | The light لكن is adversative; fronted أنفسهم is an object for exclusivity, stressing that wrongdoing’s harm falls on the wrongdoers, and the order also suits rhyme.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#3/r2] author | holds | Some read لكنّ with gemination, making أنفسهم its subject and يظلمون its predicate with omitted object يظلمونها to preserve the verse ending.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#3/r3] author | reports | Another parse gives the particle an omitted pronoun of affair as its subject, with أنفسهم as the fronted object; grammarians disagree whether omission is limited to poetic necessity.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#3/r4] author | reports | The cited line إن من يدخل الكنيسة يوما supports allowing an omitted particle-name beyond necessity, though some restrict the construction to poetry.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#3/r5] السكائي | reports | Al-Sukkāʾī is credited with parsing the hadith as إنّه من أشد الناس عذابا يوم القيامة.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117#3/r6] author | reports | A competing analysis treats من as extra, yielding إن أشد الناس; Basrans generally reject extra من here except al-Akhfash.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117/r1] author | holds | ما may be a relative noun with object suffix omitted or a verbal noun; كمثل ريح is predicate, though comparing spending to wind seems odd because the intended image concerns the crop.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117/r2] الزمخشري | prefers | Al-Zamakhsharī resolves the mismatch as a compound comparison: the whole scene is compared with the whole scene, not individuals one by one.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117/r3] ابن عطية | prefers | Ibn ʿAṭiyya sees two paired comparisons with one member omitted from each pair because the parallel implies it; he calls this the height of eloquence and inimitability.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117/r4] author | prefers | A third solution supplies a deleted مضاف; adding مهلك before ريح is clearer than before ما ينفقون because it avoids directly comparing the spent object to wind.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117/r5] الشيخ | reports | Al-Shaykh’s estimate may also require a second omitted مضاف before ريح, yielding a comparison of two acts of destruction.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:117/r6] author | holds | If ما is a verbal noun, the verse compares spending that yields no benefit to such a wind, an abstract act compared with a perceptible thing.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p138#2/r10] author | holds | The comparison has two paired images: wind destroying the crop images spending's harm, while the ruined crop images wealth that yields no return beyond loss and effort.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p138#2/r11] author | holds | The analogy uses sensible wind damage to depict hidden, abstract wasted spending, and visible crop ruin to depict the intelligible loss of wealth after toil.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p138#2/r12] author | holds | The verse uses ellipsis by implication: the likeness of spending is omitted first because the wind supplies it, and the field is omitted second because spending supplies it.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p138#2/r13] author | holds | God does not wrong either group: disbelievers spent outside His prescribed way, while field owners failed to protect their crops through obedience; the verse suggests obedience can protect crops from calamity.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p138#2/r14] author | holds | The disbelievers' self-wrong remains their own even if their spending seems to spite believers: believers ultimately prevail, making that injury void and an added punishment for the spenders.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p138#2/r8] author | holds | Their spending, whether meant as worship or not, bears no fruit because they omitted sincerity, the condition by which it is accepted.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p138#2/r9] author | holds | The icy, destructive wind striking a field owned by people who wronged themselves through unbelief images their wasted spending and wealth, bringing toil and harm in both worlds.
