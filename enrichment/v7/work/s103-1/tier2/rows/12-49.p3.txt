## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255#3/r1] author | holds | عام and سنة both mean year, but عام often suggests ease and fertility whereas سنة often suggests hardship and famine; the positive term signals the changed conditions from the preceding years.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255#3/r2] ابن عباس ومجاهد والجمهور | reports | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and most exegetes explain يغاث الناس as receiving rain, from the triliteral root غاث.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255#3/r3] author | reports | Another explanation derives it from relief: God will succor them by lifting the hardships pressing upon them, using the fourth-form verb from غوث.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255#3/r4] author | holds | They will press products normally squeezed, such as grapes, sugarcane, olives, and sesame; explicit mention matters because rain does not ensure these crops as directly as grain, or because this special good news suits the king's own circumstances and makes his reading's second-person form apt.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255#3/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas interprets the verb as milking; this can derive from pressing because milking squeezes the udder to release milk.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255#3/r6] author | holds | Repeating فيه can signal different times or kinds of benefit, enumerate the year's blessings, or emphasize that the preceding drought's rain was negligible beside this year's; the fronting of the phrase marks the focus, with rhyme also possible for the second occurrence.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r1] جعفر بن محمد والأعرج وعيسى البصرة | reports | Ja'far ibn Muhammad, al-Aʿraj, and ʿIsa of Basra read يعصرون as passive; ʿIsa also transmits a second-person passive reading, 'you will be delivered' from hardship, matching 'people will receive rain'.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r10] زيد بن أسلم | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam reports Joseph began testing the coming famine by preparing two portions for each man; when one man ate all of his, Joseph said that was the first day of the hard years.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r11] البلخي | reports | Al-Balkhi uses the counselors' dismissal of the king's dream as confused dreams to challenge the rule that dreams occur according to their first interpretation; Al-Alusi replies with the hadith that a dream hangs on a bird's wing until interpreted, and says dismissing it as meaningless may not count as interpretation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r12] ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-Arabi restricts the dream rule to visions admitting several possible meanings: one interpretation is made and then comes to pass.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r13] author | reports | The passage is also cited as evidence that an unbeliever can have a true dream; interpreters are advised not to ask at sunrise, sunset, or night, and some say a dream seen in sleep is its own interpretation. Al-Alusi finds many such claims opaque.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r2] أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu Ubayda and others take the active form as pressing in the sense of rescue; al-Bahr gives a related lexical sense of relief and cites Abu Zubayd's poem about Uthman as a refuge for the distressed.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r3] ابن المنير | reports | Ibn al-Munir understands the verb as receiving rain from a cloud whose time to be squeezed has come, with the preposition omitted or the verb carrying the sense of raining.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r4] الجوهري | reports | Al-Jawhari says عصر القوم means that a people received rain, which makes the reading apparent without a verbal supplement.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r5] النقاش | reports | Al-Naqqash transmits a doubled form يعصّرون with a passive yā and kasra on ṣād, conveying abundance or intensity.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r6] زيد بن علي | reports | Zayd ibn Ali reads تعصرون with a doubled consonant from an assimilated form of تعتصرون; it can mean squeezing fruit or being rescued.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r7] قتادة وابن عباس | reports | Qatada, as transmitted by Ibn Jarir and others, and Ibn Abbas say God granted Joseph knowledge of this year's details by revelation; Al-Alusi treats revelation as the apparent source.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r8] author | holds | Joseph ends his answer to the king with the glad news; unlike his answer to the prisoners, where the prediction came first, this placement at the end highlights his excellence.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p256/r9] بعضهم | rejects | A non-revelatory view says Joseph inferred that drought naturally ends in abundance or that God sends ease after hardship; Al-Alusi rejects it because the prophecy would be vague, especially in readings where mutual relief is described, which only revelation could establish.

[TABATABAI:12:43-57#2/r2] الرضا عليه السلام | reports | A report says Joseph stockpiled the harvests, sold food during famine first for money, then valuables, livestock, servants, property, and land, and finally for people’s freedom; he then freed the Egyptians and restored their possessions and kingship.
[TABATABAI:12:43-57#2/r3] author | holds | The author omits many similar reports about the famine economy because most do not directly serve the interpretation of these verses.
[TABATABAI:12:46-49/r10] author | reports | Yughath may mean that people are helped through relief from hardship, or that they receive rain; the next phrase about pressing makes the rain sense more apt.
[TABATABAI:12:49#2/r6] author | holds | The final year is inferred from the repeated seven: after the seven difficult years people need not exert themselves to manage sowing and reserves.
[TABATABAI:12:49#3/r1] author | holds | Joseph shifts from addressing the group directly to saying people will receive rain or aid and press produce, indicating they will no longer need to manage their livelihoods.
[TABATABAI:12:49#3/r2] صاحب المنار | reports | The Manar author argues that the unusually specific final-year forecast cannot come from the dream and must come from revelation.
[TABATABAI:12:49#3/r4] author | holds | Rain, fodder growth, or milking livestock best fits the imagery of fat and lean cows, explaining the specific mention of rain and pressing.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r1] author | prefers | The rain sense of yughath fits the reference to pressing; the claim that aid is more natural is unpersuasive except under a passive reading of yaʿsirun, they are rained upon.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r2] author | rejects | An objection that Egypt’s fertility depends on Nile flooding rather than rain fails because the floodwaters are supplied by rain in Sudan.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r3] لسان العرب | reports | The lexicon also defines ghayth as vegetation grown from rainwater, a sense that fits the following mention of pressing.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r4] author | holds | Pressing can mean extracting juice or oil from fruit and plants, and some interpret it as milking livestock.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r5] author | holds | The final year brings crops, rain, or divine relief and abundant produce or milk; the phrasing implies plentiful blessing for people and livestock.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r6] البيضاوي | reports | Al-Baydawi treats the final fertile year as glad tidings beyond the interpretation of fat and lean cows; Joseph may have known it through revelation, the expected end of drought, or God’s custom of easing hardship.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r7] صاحب المنار | reports | The Manar author says the final year’s exceptional abundance is extra information not entailed by the dream and therefore known to Joseph through revelation.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r8] author | rejects | The author says interpreters wrongly treat the dream as a forecast of seven fertile then seven barren years; Joseph instead states the required policy for surviving the anticipated famine.
[TABATABAI:78:13-14/r8] جعفر الصادق | reports | Al-Sadiq reads yuʿṣirūn in the rain year as “they are rained upon,” citing 78:14; the author identifies the verb as passive and al-muʿṣirāt as clouds that rain. | mentions 78:14
