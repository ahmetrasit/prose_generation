## SUYUTI-LUBAB: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p234#2/r1] ابن بريدة | reports | Ibn Burayda reports that the Ansar tribes Banu Haritha and Banu al-Harith competed by boasting about their living members, then went to the graves and boasted of the dead; the surah was revealed.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r1] الكاشفي | reports | Al-Kashifi says the tribes went to the cemetery and counted the graves of their own notables to compare numbers.
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r2] author | holds | They continued competing until they counted the dead and boasted in their dead; visiting graves is a euphemism for mentioning the dead and a sarcastic inversion of the visit's purpose.
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r3] الطيبي | reports | Al-Tibi says grave visitation was legislated to recall death, renounce love of worldly life, and abandon boasting; these people instead made it a cause of hardness and deeper worldly attachment.
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r4] author | holds | The verse is a declarative rebuke, and on this reading the endpoint “until” is included in the distraction.
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r5] author | reports | Another reading says worldly competition in wealth and children distracted them until they died and were buried; grave visitation then means death after wasted lives and neglect of the afterlife.
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r6] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet says a person owns only what he consumed, wore out, or gave in charity from his wealth.
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r7] عمر بن عبد العزيز | reports | ‘Umar ibn ‘Abd al-‘Aziz says graves are a place one visits and must leave, returning either to Paradise or the Fire.
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r8] author | holds | Because a visitor departs rather than staying, the wording hints at resurrection and warns against worldly life while urging preparation for death and the afterlife.
[BURSEVI:v10p387#2/r9] author | reports | A Persian poem warns that death will raid and one must leave the world; an unenslaved heart travels to the next world with ease.

[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#3/r12] عمر بن عبد العزيز | reports | Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz says every visitor must return to either Paradise or Hell, also suggesting a short stay in the graves.

[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#3/r16] author | holds | The author condemns boasting over graves and the associated abuses of some ignorant Sufis, while recognizing grave-visiting for admonition as prescribed.

## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r10] Katâde | reports | Qatada says rival clans claimed superiority and seniority while their people kept dying until all became inhabitants of graves.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r11] author | holds | The verse's apparent sense best suits numerical boasting; other vain rivalries are implied, and the following kalla rebukes them.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r12] author | reports | A second reading says desire for wealth, children, and numbers consumed the whole lifespan until death, leaving no effort for the afterlife; Ahtal and Jarir use grave-visiting for death.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r13] Ahtal | reports | Ahtal's verse uses visiting a grave as an expression for dying.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r14] Cerîr | reports | Jarir likewise says Abu Malik visited the graves, meaning he died.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r15] Alî | reports | Ali reports that they remained uncertain about grave punishment until this surah was revealed, yielding the sense until you taste the punishment of the grave.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r16] author | holds | On the grave-punishment reading, worldly hoarders awaken when they enter the grave, leaving transient possessions but carrying their burden; in both death readings takathur ends at burial.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r17] author | reports | The past form zurتم can convey a certain future, include those already dead, depict dying groups, or be construed as a time phrase meaning until the visit.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r18] Ebû Müslim | reports | Abu Muslim says God will address unbelievers with this surah on Resurrection Day, but the next clause about certain knowledge fits an address in this world or the grave better.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r19] A‘râbî | reports | An Arab listener swore that the people would be raised for judgment because a visitor departs rather than remaining at the visited place.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r20] Ömer b. Abdülaziz | reports | Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz says every visitor must leave for either Paradise or Hell, and the image also suggests the dead do not remain long in graves.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r7] author | reports | The first interpretation, tied to the tribal reports, is that people were distracted into counting and boasting of the dead; grave visits should instead awaken reflection and prompt deeds that weigh heavily.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r8] Keşşâf | prefers | On the first report, visiting graves is a metaphor or metonymy for boasting by counting the dead; Kashshaf prefers this reading.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r9] author | reports | A second interpretation takes the visits literally, following the report in which the groups actually went to the graves.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r1] author | holds | The endpoint clause may attach to takathur or ilhā: people multiplied until visiting graves, were distracted into grave-boasting, or stayed distracted until death or grave-punishment.
