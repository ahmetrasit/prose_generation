## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:2:263/r1] author | holds | A kind reply and forgiveness toward a poor person who asks but receives nothing are better than charity followed by injury or reproach.
[MUQATIL:2:263/r2] author | holds | God has no need of people's charity and is forbearing, delaying punishment for those who reproach and hurt its recipient.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v4p658/r1] author | holds | A kind word or prayer for a Muslim brother, together with excusing and concealing his difficult circumstances, is better than charity followed by injury.
[TAB-FULL:v4p658/r2] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says it is better to keep one’s wealth than give it and then reproach or harm the recipient.
[TAB-FULL:v4p658/r3] author | holds | God is independent of people’s alms and forbearing, delaying punishment for those who remind recipients and hurt them.
[TAB-FULL:v4p658/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas glosses the Rich as complete in wealth and the Forbearing as complete in forbearance.

## IBNABIHATIM: Ibn Abī Ḥātim al-Rāzī, d. 327 AH
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p516#2/r1] النبي | reports | A report reaching Amr ibn Dinar says the Prophet declared that no charity is dearer to God than a kind word, citing this verse.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p516#2/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatada says God knew some people would remind others of gifts, disliked it, and therefore commended kind speech and forgiveness over charity followed by harm.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p516#2/r3] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi explains “God is self-sufficient” as meaning that God has no need of your charities.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p516#2/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says God informed His servants of His forbearance, compassion, generosity, vast mercy, and forgiveness.

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v1p333/r2] author | holds | قول معروف may have an omitted predicate; مغفرة is a subject and خير its predicate, meaning a forgiving act beats hurtful charity.

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v2p259/r10] author | holds | A kind, gracious answer to a petitioner is “known good”; al-Kalbī defines it as a sincere prayer for one's brother in his absence, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk as speech reconciling people.
[THALABI:v2p259/r11] محمد بن جرير | reports | Muḥammad ibn Jarīr explains forgiveness as the donor's overlooking the poor person's need-related conduct; al-Kalbī and al-Ḍaḥḥāk also call it pardon for an insult.
[THALABI:v2p259/r12] author | holds | Because a poor person may be distressed by refusal and answer harshly or complain, the giver is urged to pardon him; this is better than a gift followed by harm.
[THALABI:v2p259/r13] author | holds | Harm after charity includes reproaching the petitioner for asking, complaining about him, finding fault, or other hurtful remarks.
[THALABI:v2p260/r1] author | holds | God has no need of human charity; He could enrich everyone, but gave wealth to test the rich in gratitude and left the poor to be tested in patience. | mentions 16:71
[THALABI:v2p260/r2] author | holds | God is forbearing because He does not hasten punishment for someone who reproaches or harms with a gift.
[THALABI:v2p260/r3] عبد الرحمان السليماني | reports | A report from ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulaymānī, client of ʿUmar, attributes to the Prophet advice not to interrupt a petitioner and to answer with dignity, gentleness, a small gift, or gracious refusal.
[THALABI:v2p260/r4] بشر بن الحرث | reports | Bishr ibn al-Ḥārith recounts a dream in which ʿAlī said that a poor person's confidence in God is even better than a rich person's charitable kindness.

## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p337/r10] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet warns that one who reminds others of what he gave will not be addressed, regarded, or purified by God on Judgment Day and will suffer painful punishment.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p337/r6] author | holds | Good speech replaces reproach and harm: one should kindly draw near when giving or pray for the asker when refusing.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p337/r7] author | reports | Forgiveness may mean pardoning the asker's offense, being kept from sin, declining to give (Ibn Bahr), or concealing the poor person's state without exposing it.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p337/r8] ابن بحر | reports | Ibn Bahr interprets forgiveness in this context as leaving the charity and withholding it.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p337/r9] author | holds | The harm after charity may be reminding the recipient of the gift or taunting them for poverty; good speech is better than such charity either for the recipient or with God.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p411/r1] author | holds | قول معروف is kind, pleasing speech and a gracious response to a petitioner; ʿAta says it can be a kind promise, and generally it is accepted rather than rejected by people.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p411/r2] ابن جرير | reports | He interprets مغفرة as concealing the petitioner's need.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p411/r3] عطاء والحسن | reports | They interpret it as forgiving a petitioner who speaks rudely after being refused; pardon is better than charity followed by reproach or hurt.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p411/r4] author | holds | A poor person may react sharply to refusal without a gift, so God commands pardon and teaches that this is better than giving and then hurting the petitioner.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p411/r5] author | holds | God needs no human charity; He invites people to give so He can reward them. Ghani is the opposite of need.

## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH

[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:261-263/r5] author | holds | Kind speech and pardon are better than charity followed by reproach; God needs no human charity and patiently delays punishment for those who boast of it.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p326/r1] author | holds | A kind reply to a petitioner, or a kindly promise, is 'recognized speech'; al-Kalbi says it can be a prayer for a fellow believer in their absence, and al-Dahhak links it to reconciliation.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p326/r2] الكلبي والضحاك | reports | Al-Kalbi and al-Dahhak say forgiveness means overlooking the wrongdoer; it can also mean forgiving a poor person's rude persistence when refused.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p326/r3] author | holds | A kind word and forgiveness are better than charity followed by hurtful reminders or reproach.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p326/r4] author | holds | God needs no one's charity and, being forbearing, does not hasten punishment of one who reminds and hurts recipients.

## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p340/r1] author | holds | A good word means a courteous, gracious reply.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p340/r2] author | holds | Forgiveness may mean excusing an irritating petitioner, God's forgiveness earned by a gentle refusal, or the petitioner's forgiving the refusal.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p340/r3] author | holds | The indefinite subject can be predicated of as ‘better’ because its descriptive qualification makes it specific.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v1p340/r4] author | holds | God has no need of a giver who reminds and harms, and His forbearance delays immediate punishment; this restraint signals displeasure and threat.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p357/r1] author | holds | Kind speech—prayer, reassurance, and hope in God’s reward—and pardon for a needy person’s faults are better than a gift that is outwardly charity but inwardly empty; kind speech earns reward, while harmful giving does not.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p357/r2] المهدوي وغيره | reports | Al-Mahdawī and others supply an implied “more fitting” in the syntax: a good word is more fitting, and pardon is better.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p357/r3] author | rejects | Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects the supplied wording because it dulls the rhetorical force; the omitted wording should be understood as present in meaning.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p357/r4] author | holds | Here forgiveness means overlooking a needy person’s defect and difficult circumstances.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p357/r5] النقاش | reports | Al-Naqqāsh says pardon can mean forgiving a petitioner who speaks harshly after being refused.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p357/r6] author | holds | God has no need of such a donor’s gift and patiently gives possible offenders time.
