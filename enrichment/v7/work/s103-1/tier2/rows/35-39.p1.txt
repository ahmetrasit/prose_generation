## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:35:39-41/r1] author | holds | The closing section argues for God's sole lordship from human succession on earth and His sustaining the heavens and earth, denies lordship to idols, rebukes broken oaths and plotting, and warns that God delays but does not fail to punish.
[TABATABAI:35:39-41/r2] author | holds | Each human generation succeeds its predecessor and gains comparable use of the earth; this succession is bound to procreation and creation, so it is a form of governance inseparable from creation.
[TABATABAI:35:39-41/r3] author | holds | Because God creates humanity and makes generations successors, He alone governs them; even opponents concede that God is the Creator, so He is their Lord.
[TABATABAI:35:39-41/r4] author | holds | Disbelief harms the disbeliever by bringing divine contempt and personal loss, replacing human happiness with misery in the journey to the next life.
[TABATABAI:35:39-41/r5] author | holds | The text says contempt is 'with their Lord' because it comes from God, while loss is the person's own consequence of changing faith and happiness into disbelief and misery.
[TABATABAI:35:39-41/r6] author | holds | The wording 'increase' reflects human nature as open to development: faith increases perfection and nearness to God, while disbelief increases contempt and loss.
[TABATABAI:35:39-41/r7] author | holds | Divine love and hatred are attributes of action, not intrinsic qualities: love is mercy extending toward someone and hatred is its withdrawal.
