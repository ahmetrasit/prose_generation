## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:12:42/r1] أكثرون، الجبائي | prefers | The majority and al-Jubbaʾi take ظن as certain knowledge received by revelation, as in the verse about expecting to meet one's account; this is the author's preferred reading. | mentions 69:20
[TABRISI:12:42/r10] author | holds | The lesson is to rely on and seek help from God in hardship, although seeking help from other people is also permissible.
[TABRISI:12:42/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatada takes ظن in its ordinary sense of conjecture, since Yusuf could not verify that the prisoner's dream report was true.
[TABRISI:12:42/r3] author | holds | Yusuf asked the escaped cupbearer to mention that he was unjustly imprisoned to his master, the king.
[TABRISI:12:42/r4] author | prefers | The author first explains Satan's making Yusuf forget as Yusuf forgetting to mention God and instead asking a human for help, when reliance on God was more fitting.
[TABRISI:12:42/r5] الحسن، محمد بن إسحاق، الجبائي، أبو مسلم | reports | An alternative takes the forgotten remembrance as the cupbearer's failure to mention Yusuf to the king; the author paraphrases this as Satan making him forget Yusuf before his master.
[TABRISI:12:42/r6] ابن عباس، علي بن الحسين، أبو عبد الله | reports | The stay of several years is specified as seven years in reports from Ibn Abbas, ʿAli ibn al-Husayn, and Abu ʿAbd Allah.
[TABRISI:12:42/r7] رسول الله | reports | A report from the Prophet marvels that Yusuf sought help from a creature instead of the Creator; another says his request to be remembered prolonged his stay.
[TABRISI:12:42/r8] أبو عبد الله | reports | In a report from Abu ʿAbd Allah, Gabriel asks Yusuf who gave him his beauty, his father's love, the caravan, rescue from the well, and protection from the women; Yusuf answers that God did, and is told to remain in prison for turning to a creature.
[TABRISI:12:42/r9] author | holds | Seeking human help to avert harm is permitted and can even be required; the Prophet sought help from the Emigrants and Helpers, so the reports describe a temporary reproach for departing from Yusuf's usual reliance on God, not a prohibition.
