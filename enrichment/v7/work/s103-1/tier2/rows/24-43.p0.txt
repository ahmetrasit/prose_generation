## FARRA: al-Farrāʾ, d. 207 AH
[FARRA:v2p256#2/r1] author | holds | الودق means rain, and يصيب به means He punishes with it.
[FARRA:v2p256#2/r2] author | holds | The mountains may be actual formations of hail in the sky, with من omitted before the material; alternatively they mean mountain-sized quantities of hail, like two houses' worth of straw.
[FARRA:v2p256#2/r3] author | holds | When من introduces material or measure, removing it makes the following noun accusative, as in عدل ذلك صياما and ملء الأرض ذهبا. | mentions 5:95, 3:91
[FARRA:v2p256#2/r4] أبو جعفر | reports | Abu Ja'far read يذهب بالأبصار in the description of the lightning.
[FARRA:v2p256/r1] author | holds | بينه can be singular after سحاب because the word is singular in form but collective in meaning; a collective such as نخل likewise permits بين with one noun.
[FARRA:v2p256/r2] author | holds | بين normally requires two or more referents; a single place-name works only when it denotes a group or a compound settlement, as in the poem about Hूमل and عراد.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v17p337/r1] author | reports | One interpretation takes the phrase literally: God sends hail from mountains in the sky, with the mountains themselves made of hail.
[TAB-FULL:v17p337/r2] author | reports | Another interpretation takes the phrase as an amount of hail as large as mountains falling from the sky, just as “two houses of straw” means an amount of straw filling two houses.
[TAB-FULL:v17p339/r1] author | holds | “Sanā” is the shortened noun for the intense light of the cloud's lightning, nearly blinding whoever sees it.
[TAB-FULL:v17p339/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas glosses the lightning's sanā as its light.
[TAB-FULL:v17p339/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada describes it as the flash of lightning that nearly takes sight away.
[TAB-FULL:v17p339/r4] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says the lightning's brightness can take away sight.
[TAB-FULL:v17p339/r5] author | reports | Most readers pronounce “yadhhab” with an open yāʾ, while Abū Jaʿfar reads it with a ḍamma as a passive verb.
[TAB-FULL:v17p339/r6] author | prefers | The open-yāʾ reading is preferred by consensus and Arab usage: “went with it” takes a preposition, while the causative “made it go” normally takes a direct object.

## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH
[ZAJJAJ:v4p49#2/r1] author | holds | The phrase means hail-bearing clouds like mountains, or an amount of hail as great as mountains, as in mountains of wealth.
[ZAJJAJ:v4p49#2/r2] author | holds | A lightened reading of ينزل is allowed; Abu Jaʿfar alone read يذهب بالأبصار, grammatically weak though the yāʾ form with ḍamma is allowed.
[ZAJJAJ:v4p49#2/r3] author | holds | Sana is lightning's light; readings distinguish برق as the amount of lightning from برقة as one flash, like an action once versus its measure.
[ZAJJAJ:v4p49/r1] author | holds | Yuzjī means drives, yualif joins scattered clouds into one, and rukāman piles clouds together; wadq is rain.
[ZAJJAJ:v4p49/r2] author | prefers | The reading خلاله is broader and better than خلله; khilāl is a plural like mountains from mountain.
[ZAJJAJ:v4p49/r3] author | holds | Cloud may be plural or collective singular; بينه can refer to the whole cloud, as a region name can contain multiple places.

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v3p141/r1] author | holds | Bayn normally relates two things, but baynahu is possible for clouds as a collective containing many clouds or segments.
[NAHHAS:v3p141/r10] أبو جعفر | reports | He reads yudhhib with a damma on yāʾ; Abu Hatim calls this ungrammatical, following al-Akhfash’s analogy that one says dakhala bi-l-madkhal, not udkhila.
[NAHHAS:v3p141/r11] أبو حاتم | reports | He argues that bāʾ alternates with the hamza of transitivity; the author says that view is clear, though extra bāʾ is also attested elsewhere. | mentions 22:25

[NAHHAS:v3p141/r3] امرؤ القيس | reports | The verse ‘between al-Dukhūl and Ḥawmal’ uses bayn with one place-name that contains many sites, supporting the collective explanation.
