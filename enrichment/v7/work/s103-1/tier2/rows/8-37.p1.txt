## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v4p2921/r6] author | holds | Tamyīz is separating or distinguishing two things; khabīth is what is poor or bad, opposite ṭayyib, which is good and may mean pleasant or lawful.
[JISHUMI:v4p2921/r7] author | holds | Rukām is a mass stacked layer upon layer, like piled clouds, sand, or mud; the verb means placing one thing over another.
[JISHUMI:v4p2921/r8] author | holds | The singular masculine pronoun in فيجعله refers back to khabīth; أولئك is plural because it refers to الذين كفروا at the opening.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p356/r1] author | reports | One view identifies the evil with disbelievers and the good with believers, destined respectively for Hell and Paradise.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p356/r2] الكلبي | reports | Al-Kalbi interprets the evil and good as wicked deeds and righteous deeds, with their respective recompenses in Hell and Paradise.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p356/r3] author | reports | A further view distinguishes spending for Satan’s cause from spending for God’s cause.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p356/r4] author | holds | The evil is piled together, as a dense mass of clouds is gathered.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p356/r5] author | holds | The losers are the disbelievers who spent their wealth, losing their trade because they bought punishment in the Hereafter.

## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p207#3/r10] author | holds | On the main reading, the lām attaches to ‘they will be gathered’; under the alternative, it attaches to ‘it becomes regret.’ The reading ليميز is lightened.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p207#3/r7] author | holds | The disbelievers among them are gathered to Hell, since some others accepted Islam well.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p207#3/r8] author | holds | The verse distinguishes the unbelieving group from the believers and heaps the vile together until they are piled up, as in the image of piled crowds. | mentions 72:19
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p207#3/r9] قيل | reports | Alternatively, it separates the ill-gotten money spent against the Prophet from the good money spent by Abu Bakr and Uthman to support him, then consigns the former to Hell with its owners. | mentions 9:35

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r1] ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر | reports | These readers pronounce ليَميز with an open yāʾ and kasra on mīm, from ماز; a rare reading انمازوا and Abu Zayd's line illustrate the verb's form and its passive-like separation.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r2] حمزة والكسائي | reports | They read ليميز with a prefixed ḍamma, open mīm, and doubled yāʾ, from ميّز meaning to separate; 67:8 uses تميز for things separating. | mentions 67:8
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r3] ابن عباس والسدي | reports | They identify the vile as disbelievers and the good as believers.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r4] author | holds | On that interpretation, the purpose clause attaches to the disbelievers being gathered: God distinguishes them by gathering them together and casting them into Hell; losers are those whose effort failed and who ended there.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r5] ابن سلام والزجاج | reports | They identify the vile as the wealth polytheists spent obstructing God's path and the good as believers' spending in God's path.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r6] author | holds | On this view, the purpose clause attaches to their being overcome: despite their spending, the disbelievers are defeated, so God distinguishes the groups by abandoning the vile and supporting the good.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r7] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says God will bring out on Resurrection Day the wealth that was charity or an act of devotion, then order the rest thrown into the Fire.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r8] الحسن | reports | Hasan says the disbelievers are punished through that wealth; al-Zajjaj compares this to the Qur'anic threat that wealth will brand its owners. | mentions 9:35
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p526/r9] author | holds | On both interpretations, piling the vile together means collecting, joining, and compacting its scattered parts; ركم means to make dense, as in سحاب مركوم and Dhu al-Rumma's verse.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p527/r1] أبو علي | reports | Abu Ali explains يجعل الخبيث as يلقي, 'casts'.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p527/r2] author | holds | Under the wealth interpretation, the losers are hypocrites among the disbelievers; the word loss suits them both financially and in other respects.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:8:36-37#2/r1] author | holds | One reading of separating the bad from the good distinguishes unbelievers' spending from believers' spending, then piles all the unbelievers' expenditures together and punishes them in Hell as in 9:35. | mentions 9:35
[TABRISI:8:36-37#2/r2] أبو مسلم | reports | Abū Muslim says God distinguishes unbelievers from believers: in this world through victory, triumph, recognized names, and special rulings, and in the next through reward and Paradise.
[TABRISI:8:36-37#2/r3] author | reports | Another interpretation has the bad people piled atop one another in Hell, packed together like a heap of clouds; the phrase “make it in Hell” means He admits them there.
[TABRISI:8:36-37#2/r4] author | holds | These people lose themselves because they traded spending their wealth in disobedience for punishment in the afterlife.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p355#2/r1] ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر | reports | They read limayiza without gemination; Hamza and al-Kisa'i use a geminated form, and both are Arabic variants.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p355#2/r2] ابن الأنباري | reports | The lām in limayiza connects to the earlier “they will spend it” phrase.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p355#2/r3] ابن جرير الطبري | reports | Alternatively, the lām connects to the phrase about gathering them to Hell.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p355#2/r4] ابن عباس عن ابن أبي طلحة والسدي ومقاتل | reports | One interpretation is distinguishing the people of bliss from misery, or believers from unbelievers.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p355#2/r5] أبو صالح عن ابن عباس | reports | Another interpretation is distinguishing good deeds from bad deeds.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p355#2/r6] ابن زيد والزجاج | reports | Another interpretation separates spending for God's cause from spending for Satan's cause.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p356/r1] الزجاج | reports | Rakama means piling one thing on another; the resulting heap is called rukam.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p356/r2] author | reports | If “the bad” means unbelievers, they are piled on one another in Hell.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p356/r3] author | reports | If “the bad” means their wealth, one view says it is thrown into fire to punish its owners, as in the warning of 9:35. | mentions 9:35
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p356/r4] author | reports | Another view says wealth they revered in life is thrown into fire to show its humiliation, as with the sun and moon.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v15p129/r1] مقاتل والكلبي | reports | Muqatil and al-Kalbi say the spending passage was revealed about twelve leading Quraysh who financed the army at Badr.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p129/r2] سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد | reports | Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Mujahid say it concerned Abu Sufyan financing war against Muhammad at Uhud; al-Kashshaf reports he hired two thousand Abyssinians and other Arabs and spent forty uqiyyas, each of forty-two mithqals.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p129/r3] author | holds | The disbelievers spent to block others from following Muhammad, God's path, even if they did not understand their own purpose in those terms; the spending ultimately became regret because it failed and they were defeated, as 58:21 promises. | mentions 58:21
[RAZI-FULL:v15p129/r4] author | holds | The verse specifies those who remained disbelievers because some of the original spenders later became Muslims; its wording appears to restrict their gathering to Hell, and Razi says their spending brings only worldly regret and frustration plus severe punishment in the afterlife.
[RAZI-FULL:v15p129/r5] author | reports | One interpretation says God separates the evil unbelieving party from the good believers, then piles the evil together like the crowded mass in 72:19; those refers to the evil party. | mentions 72:19

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:8:37/r1] author | reports | The impure and pure may mean disbelievers and believers, or corruption and soundness; the purpose clause can attach to gathering, victory, or the spending which becomes a regret.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:8:37/r2] author | holds | Hamza, al-Kisa'i, and Yaqub read the verb as tamyiz, which is more intensive than simple separation.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:8:37/r3] author | reports | The impure are heaped together until crowded upon one another, or the spending is joined to the disbelievers to increase their punishment like hoarded treasure.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:8:37/r4] author | reports | The final reference points either to the impure faction or to the spenders, who lose themselves and their wealth completely.
