One reading concerns Adam: forty years passed while he was a clay body, not remembered or known; alternatively, all people were nonexistent before becoming remembered.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:76:1-10#4/r1] ابن عباس | reports | A transmitted chronology places al-Insan among the surahs revealed in Medina, after al-Rahman.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#4/r2] عكرمة والحسن بن أبي الحسن البصري | reports | Their reported sequence puts the opening of al-Insan among the revelations in Medina.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#4/r3] علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali reports that the Prophet described each surah's reward in revelation order and likewise places al-Insan in the Medinan sequence.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#4/r4] author | reports | The Prophet's reported total is 114 surahs, 6,236 verses, and 321,250 letters; only the fortunate learn the Quran and God's friends maintain its recitation.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#4/r5] ابن عباس | reports | The report adds that a surah's opening revealed in Mecca was written there, with later additions made in Medina.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r1] author | rejects | The author rejects the sectarian argument that a Medinan narrative proves this surah is fabricated because the surah is Meccan; he says he clarifies the truth against hostility to the Prophet's family.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r10] سعيد الحداد | reports | Abu Ja'far distinguishes being mentioned in divine knowledge from being mentioned in creation.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r2] author | holds | The question means “has indeed come” or “did there not come”; it is a reportative question about a time when the human was not mentioned.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r3] الزجاج | reports | The human existed as dust and clay but was not a mentioned thing until the spirit was breathed into him.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r4] الجبائي | reports | The gentle rhetorical question makes an unbeliever recognize that he once did not exist and therefore has a creator who brought him into being.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r5] الحسن وقتادة وسفيان والجبائي | reports | They identify the human as Adam, the first person given that name.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r6] أبو مسلم | reports | Abu Muslim takes “the human” generically, referring to humankind as a class.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r7] author | reports | One view says Adam's clay body lay for forty years before the spirit was breathed into it, unmentioned in heaven and earth.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r8] ابن عباس | reports | A report from Ibn Abbas says Adam's creation was completed after 120 years.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#5/r9] زرارة | reports | Abu Ja'far explains that the human was a thing but was not mentioned.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#6/r1] عبد الأعلى مولى آل سام | reports | Abu Abd Allah gives the same interpretation as the preceding report: the human was a thing but not mentioned.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#6/r2] حمران بن أعين | reports | Abu Ja'far says the human was a determined thing but had not yet been brought into formed existence.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#6/r3] author | holds | These reports support that the nonexistent is known and can be called a thing, even when it is not mentioned.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#6/r4] author | holds | If “human” means humankind, it describes a person unknown before birth, then developing in the father's loins and mother's womb until birth.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#6/r5] author | reports | Another interpretation says scholars were unknown, then God made them renowned through knowledge during life and after death.
[TABRISI:76:1-10#6/r6] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | After hearing the verse, Umar wished Adam had remained as he was, without children who would be tested.
[TABRISI:76:1-10/r9] author | holds | Dahr is the passage of night and day; its plurals include adhur and duhur.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p427#2/r1] الفراء | reports | Hal means qad here: the verse reports that a time did come, rather than asking a question; interrogative hal can also prompt an admission.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p427#2/r2] الجمهور | reports | The human is Adam, and the time was the forty years when he was shaped from clay before the spirit was breathed into him.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p427#2/r3] ابن عباس وابن جريج | reports | Alternatively, al-insān means all people and the time is their development as a sperm-drop, clot, and morsel of flesh.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v8p427#2/r4] author | holds | The human was something, but was not yet mentioned or known.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1-2/r1] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil proposes reordered syntax: a period passed when the human was unremembered because humanity was created after all animals and no animal afterward.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1-2/r2] author | reports | Another view means Adam’s descendants and the nine months of pregnancy, when the embryo is an insignificant clot and morsel.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1-2/r3] أبو بكر | reports | Abu Bakr wished the period before Adam was remembered had continued, so people would not be born and tested.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1-2/r4] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | Umar likewise heard someone recite the verse and said, “If only it had finished.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r1] الكسائي والفراء وأبو عبيدة | reports | These grammarians take hal as qad; al-Farra says it may mark denial or a report, and here it is a report, while another view treats it as a question meaning “it came.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r10] محمد بن الجهم عن الفراء | reports | Ibn Jahm transmits al-Farra’s view that Adam existed but was not yet remembered.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r11] قتادة ومقاتل | reports | Some take the negation literally: Adam did not yet exist or have a name among creatures; Qatada says humanity was created recently and no creature came after it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r2] قتادة والثوري وعكرمة والسدي | reports | They identify the human in the verse as Adam, a view also transmitted from Ibn Abbas.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says Adam lay between Mecca and Taif for forty years before the spirit was breathed into him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Another report gives forty years each as clay, dark mud, and shaped clay, completing Adam after 120 years; Ibn Masud adds forty years as earth, totaling 160.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r5] ابن عباس | reports | A report from Ibn Abbas says the period’s length is unknown.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Al-Dahhak transmits from Ibn Abbas that “not remembered” means Adam was neither in heaven nor on earth.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r7] الفراء وقطرب وثعلب | reports | They say Adam was an unrecognized shaped body of earth and clay until the spirit entered him, after which he became known and remembered.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r8] يحيى بن سلام | reports | Yahya ibn Sallam says Adam was not remembered among creation, although he was remembered by God.
[QURTUBI-FULL:76:1/r9] القشيري | reports | Another interpretation takes remembrance as rank and honor, which Adam gained when God made him a vicegerent and entrusted him with what the heavens and mountains declined. | mentions 43:44

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:76:1-2/r1] author | holds | هل أتى is a confirmatory, expectant question and means ‘indeed’; its origin is أهل, supported by a poetic line.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:76:1-2/r2] author | holds | A finite stretch within unbounded time passed when the human was not yet known as a human, like an element or drop; the clause describes either الإنسان or حين. Human may be generic or Adam before his descendants.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p302#2/r1] author | holds | The human is Adam, who spent forty years as shaped clay before the spirit was breathed into him; his name and intended role were unknown.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p302#2/r2] author | holds | “A time passed over him” cannot describe nonexistence, and “not mentioned” is a circumstantial accusative describing the human as then unknown.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:76:1-2/r1] author | holds | The human existed in divine knowledge and in reality through the eternal spirit, but was unmentioned among people while in the unseen world, unknown to those in the visible world.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p357/r10] author | holds | ‘The human’ most likely means humanity: the interval is nonexistence or embryonic development through successive stages until a person can be addressed, with no renown during that period.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p357/r11] author | reports | Another view identifies the human as Adam and the interval as the time before his spirit was breathed into him.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p357/r12] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas says Adam remained clay for forty years, then shaped clay for forty, then stinking mud for forty, completing his creation in 120 years.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p357/r3] الجمهور | reports | The majority consider the surah Meccan; Mujahid and Qatada call it Medinan, while Hasan and ʿIkrima say it is Medinan except the command not to obey a sinner or unbeliever.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p357/r6] author | holds | The surah’s connection to the previous one is evident and needs no elaboration.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p357/r7] ابن عباس وقتادة | reports | They take hal here to mean qad (‘already’), although hal with a nominal clause cannot mean qad and with a verb it usually marks a direct question.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p357/r8] author | reports | The explanation connects hal to an original ahl form with its hamza elided; a poem asking ‘Did they see us?’ is offered as evidence.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p357/r9] أبو بكر وعمر | reports | When the verse was recited to Abu Bakr, or according to another report Umar, he wished the state of being uncreated and unaccountable had lasted.
