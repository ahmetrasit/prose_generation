## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:79:42-44/r3] الراغب | reports | Raghib says dhikrā means frequent remembrance and is more emphatic than dhikr; the construction asks what knowledge the Prophet could gain from repeatedly mentioning the Hour.
[TABATABAI:79:42-44/r4] author | prefers | Alternatively, dhikrā is the heart's apprehension of a thing's true nature; the Prophet cannot know the Hour's reality and timing by frequent remembrance, which better fits the context.
[TABATABAI:79:42-44/r5] قيل | rejects | Some say the Hour's remembrance is unrelated to the Prophet's mission, which is to warn those who fear it; another says the question is rebuked because his mission is already linked to the Hour and cites a report that he and the Hour were sent close together. The author finds these readings contextually weak and strained.
[TABATABAI:79:42-44/r6] قيل | rejects | Another view takes the phrase as part of the polytheists' question about what the Prophet knows from remembering the Hour; the author says the context does not suit it.
[TABATABAI:79:46#2/r6] عائشة | reports | A report graded sound by al-Hakim says the Prophet was repeatedly asked about the Hour until these verses were revealed, after which he was not asked; the author says the context does not fit this as a reply to the Prophet's own question.
