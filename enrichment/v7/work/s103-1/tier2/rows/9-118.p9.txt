## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#2/r2] author | holds | Takhalluf means leaving something behind; rahb means spaciousness, so ‘the earth despite its breadth’ describes their distress even in a wide world.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#2/r3] author | holds | The verses address distinct groups and kinds of repentance—some in the first group had not sinned, while the three laggards had—but the syntax joins them into one statement revealed together.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#2/r4] author | holds | The chief purpose is to describe God’s pardon of the three laggards; mention of the Prophet, Emigrants, and Helpers reassures them by placing all groups on equal terms after pardon.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#2/r5] author | holds | The repeated repentance follows an overview-then-detail structure: God’s repentance is first stated for everyone, then specified for each group and finally for the three so that they may repent.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#3/r4] author | holds | The three were socially shunned even by their families, grieved continuously, and became certain that repentance was their only refuge; God then guided them to repent and forgave them.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#4/r3] author | holds | Because God’s tawba precedes their repentance, it means the first tawba, guidance that enables them to repent.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#4/r4] author | holds | Calling God compassionate and merciful supports the enabling-guidance reading, since the verse names no attribute explicitly denoting acceptance.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#4/r5] author | holds | The pronoun in “so that they repent” refers to the three left behind; they repent after God guides them, and God then accepts and forgives.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#4/r6] author | holds | Although the wording alone does not state acceptance, the story is established by transmission and the verse’s broad tawba language and final names make rejection of their repentance impossible; 4:17 confirms acceptance. | mentions 4:17
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#5/r1] author | rejects | The claim that “so that they repent” means God merely made repentance easy is a weak interpretation.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#5/r2] author | rejects | He rejects interpreting their repentance as a return to their pre-sin condition.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#5/r3] author | rejects | He also rejects taking the pronoun as referring to all believers, who would repent after hearing that God accepts repentance.
[TABATABAI:9:111-123#5/r4] author | holds | In this context, zann conveys certainty by contextual implication, not by the word’s lexical meaning.

## ASAD-NOTES: Muhammad Asad, d. 1412 AH
[ASAD-NOTES:9:117#n155/r1] author | holds | Although the phrase can mean 'those left behind,' Asad renders alladhina khullifu as those who became morally altered or corrupt, based on its figurative Arabic sense and support from major philologists including al-Asma'i. | mentions 9:83
[ASAD-NOTES:9:117#n155/r2] classical commentators | reports | Classical commentators identify the three as Ka'b ibn Malik, Mararah ibn al-Rabi', and Hilal ibn Umayyah, who abstained from Tabuk and were ostracized until revelation; Asad accepts their historical failure but rejects restricting the verse to them.
[ASAD-NOTES:9:117#n155/r3] author | prefers | Asad takes 'the three' to mean three groups: those excused after equivocal reasons, those who stayed without permission and later repented, and those whose cases were deferred and who repented after Tabuk. | mentions 9:43, 9:46, 9:90, 9:102, 9:105, 9:106
[ASAD-NOTES:9:117#n156/r1] author | holds | Beyond the historical case, the verse applies to believers who stray temporarily, recognize their corruption—spontaneously or after rebuke—and sincerely repent.
