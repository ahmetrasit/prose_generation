## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:91:2-3#2/r1] author | reports | The Shaykh invokes the Sibawayh poem as a case of coordinating a genitive plus nominative pair with another pair; the author says this concedes the disputed coordination, though the poem has an additional complication.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v10p341#2/r1] author | holds | Although the conditional sense of idha seems to require an answer and an operator, the author reads it causally in these oaths: swear because the moon follows, the day reveals, and the night covers; al-Qamus also allows a present-time meaning, “when.”
[BURSEVI:v10p341#2/r2] author | holds | Day is the sun's light erasing the earth's shadow and night-darkness; it reveals the sun as daylight spreads, so the act of revealing is figuratively attributed to the day because it occurs then.
[BURSEVI:v10p341#2/r3] author | holds | The text may also mean that day reveals the darkness, world, or earth, though these objects are omitted because they are understood.
[BURSEVI:v10p341#2/r4] author | holds | In the inward reading, day is the spirit's dominant light and power, bringing the spirit fully into view like the sun at midday.
[BURSEVI:v10p341#2/r7] author | prefers | The oath wa's are conjunctive and share the implied verb, avoiding the problem of coordinating words governed by different agents or multiplying oaths with one answer.

## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:91:3/r1] author | holds | Tajliyah means to make manifest, clarify, or polish; the pronoun refers to the sun, and the phrase describes clear daylight without cloud or haze.
[ELMALILI:91:3/r2] author | holds | Daylight is made the agent that unveils the sun even though it is the sun's own light reflected on earth, a noteworthy rhetorical reversal.
