## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p158/r1] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud reports that Quraysh leaders asked Muhammad to expel Suhayb, Khabbab, Bilal, Ammar, and other poor Muslims, promising to follow him if he did.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p158/r2] خباب بن الأرت | reports | Khabbab says Uyayna ibn Hisn and al-Aqra ibn Habis asked the Prophet to remove poor enslaved believers while Arab delegations visited, so they would not be seen with them.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p158/r3] خباب بن الأرت | reports | In Khabbab's account, the Prophet agreed and summoned Ali to write their demand, but revelation came; he set aside the page, turned to the poor Muslims, and drew near to them.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p158/r4] ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbari says the Prophet considered prioritizing wealthy leaders to attract their people, intending good rather than contempt for the poor, but God forbade it.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p162/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says calling on God morning and evening means performing the obligatory dawn and afternoon prayers; Hasan, Mujahid, Qatada, and al-Dahhak similarly define it as prescribed prayer.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p162/r2] إبراهيم | reports | Ibrahim offers remembrance of God at the two ends of the day as one meaning, but also reports that the phrase means the five obligatory prayers, as all exegetes hold.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p162/r3] ابن عامر | reports | Ibn Amir reads al-ghuduwa with waw; Abu Ali favors the common al-ghadāh because it can be indefinite and take the article, while ghuduwa is ordinarily a definite proper time-name.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p162/r4] أبو علي | reports | Abu Ali says the manuscript's waw does not establish that reading, since ṣalāh is also written with waw for alif; Sibawayh treats ghuduwa and bukra as names for times, with Yūnus reporting their definite use without nunation.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p162/r5] أبو علي | reports | For Ibn Amir's reading, Abu Ali cites al-Khalil's permission to say 'today, ghuduwa and bukra' like ḍaḥwa, and time-names sometimes take the article when construed as generic.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p162/r6] author | holds | The author explains that a proper name becomes shareable and generic when stripped of its individual reference, making forms such as 'the Zayds' possible; the discussion cites Ibn Mayada.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p167/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the poor worshippers seek God's reward and act for His pleasure without associating anything with Him.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p167/r2] أهل المعاني | reports | One interpretation takes 'His face' as God's obedience and the direction or path He commanded people to follow; al-Zajjaj says it testifies to sincere intentions. | mentions 2:115
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p167/r3] أبو إسحاق | reports | The other interpretation uses 'face' as an honorific expression: they seek God alone; Abu Ishaq gives this explanation at 18:28. | mentions 18:28
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p168/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas takes the reckoning pronouns to refer to the polytheists: the Prophet is not accountable for them, nor they for him; God alone rewards His allies and punishes His enemies.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p168/r2] author | reports | On Ibn Abbas's reading, the Prophet must not court the polytheists by excluding poor believers; the believers' reward is not theirs to give.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p168/r3] author | prefers | Most exegetes take the pronouns to refer to the poor worshippers; this best fits the explicit object of 'expel them'.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p168/r4] الحسن | reports | One explanation says the Prophet is not responsible for their actions or account of their actions; Ibn Abbas also reports this meaning.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p168/r5] أهل المعاني | reports | The story resembles Noah's people calling his followers the lowly: the prophets defer their deeds and livelihood to God, and the poor believers' humble trades do not disgrace the Prophet. | mentions 26:111, 26:112, 26:113
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p170/r1] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says the repeated reckoning clause confirms parallel phrasing, though the second phrase could be omitted.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p170/r2] أهل المعاني | reports | A second view concerns provision: neither the Prophet's nor the poor believers' livelihood depends on one another, because God provides for all; he should let them draw near.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p170/r3] ابن عباس | reports | The warning against becoming one of the wrongdoers means harming oneself through disobedience and losing reward; ظلم can mean diminution in Arabic and in Quranic usage.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v8p170/r4] ابن زيد | prefers | Ibn Zayd calls expelling the poor injustice to them: they did not deserve it, so the author calls this a sound interpretation.

## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:6:52-53/r1] author | reports | The verse concerns poor believers after Meccan chiefs told the Prophet to remove them so the chiefs could sit with him and believe.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:6:52-53/r2] author | holds | Calling on God morning and evening means performing the obligatory prayers; seeking his face means seeking his reward.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:6:52-53/r3] author | holds | The Prophet is not responsible for the poor people's provision, nor are they responsible for his; God provides for all, so he should keep them near.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:6:52-53/r4] author | holds | Expelling these believers would wrong them.

## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v3p2244#2/r1] ابن عامر | reports | Ibn ʿĀmir reads بالغدوة والعشي with wāw and a ḍamma on ghayn in both this verse and al-Kahf; the other readers have alif and fatḥa.
[JISHUMI:v3p2244#2/r2] ابن أبي عبلة | reports | Ibn Abī ʿAbla reads the plural forms بالغدوات والعشيات; the original wāw turns into alif after fatḥa on the preceding dāl.
[JISHUMI:v3p2244#2/r3] author | holds | Ṭard means banishing, exclusion, or expulsion.
[JISHUMI:v3p2244#2/r4] author | holds | Wajh can mean the familiar bodily face or the essence/aspect of a thing, as in saying this is the wajh of an opinion.
[JISHUMI:v3p2244#2/r7] author | holds | The prohibition طرد is jussive, with kasra to separate adjacent vowelless consonants; فتطردهم can answer the negation in ما عليك من حسابهم من شيء.
[JISHUMI:v3p2244#2/r8] أبو مسلم | reports | Abū Muslim parses فتطردهم فتكون as consequences of the prohibition: do not expel them, or you become unjust by expelling them.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p145#2/r4] author | reports | Salman and Khabbab say the verse came after al-Aqra, Uyayna, and their elite group asked the Prophet to dismiss poor believers such as Bilal, Suhayb, Ammar, and Khabbab, whose coarse wool clothes they despised.

[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p145#2/r6] author | reports | The Prophet dropped the sheet, summoned the poor believers, and honored them with God's message of mercy; he thereafter sat close to them and waited for them before leaving. | mentions 18:28
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p145#2/r7] author | reports | The Prophet told them he was grateful not to die before being commanded to remain patient with members of his community, saying their life and death were with them. | mentions 18:28
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p146/r1] الكلبي | reports | Al-Kalbi says the petitioners proposed separate days or a single gathering where the Prophet would face them and turn his back on the poor; God revealed the prohibition against dismissing the poor worshippers.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p146/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says Quraysh claimed they would follow Muhammad if not for Bilal and Ibn Umm Abd; the verse followed.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p146/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says morning and evening worship means the dawn and afternoon prayers; he also reports that it can mean all five prayers after elite men asked the poor to pray behind them.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p146/r4] سعيد بن المسيب | reports | Said ibn al-Musayyib rejects reading the phrase as a session for public storytellers; he says it refers to the prayer they had just completed.

[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p146/r6] author | holds | Seeking God's face means seeking God through obedience; Ibn Abbas says they seek God's reward.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p146/r7] author | holds | The Prophet is not responsible for the worshippers' account and they are not responsible for his; their livelihood is not his burden, nor is his provision theirs.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p146/r8] author | holds | The phrase 'so you would drive them away' answers the preceding negation; 'then you would be among wrongdoers' answers the prohibition, respectively.
