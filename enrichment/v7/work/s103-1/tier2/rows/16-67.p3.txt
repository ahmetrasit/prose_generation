[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#3/r7] author | holds | The first two reports used by the Ḥanafīs are weak; stronger prophetic reports state that every intoxicant is forbidden and that what intoxicates in large quantity is forbidden in small quantity, with al-Nasāʾī defending the transmitters' reliability.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#4/r1] author | holds | The third report is authentic but the Prophet gave the changed-smelling nabīdh to a servant because he disliked its odor, not because it intoxicated; the same concern explains the report about Zaynab's honey and maghāfīr.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#4/r2] عطاء | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported by ʿAṭāʾ, Ṭāwūs, and Mujāhid to say that what intoxicates in quantity is forbidden in small quantity; this is also related by Qays ibn Dīnār and is his legal opinion.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#4/r3] author | rejects | The report through ʿAbd Allāh ibn Shaddād conflicts with the larger group and established prophetic teaching, so it cannot support the opposing position.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#4/r4] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | ʿUmar's nabīdh meant a non-intoxicating drink; al-Nasāʾī reports it had been turned into vinegar, and another report says ʿUmar punished a drinker if the drink proved intoxicating.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#4/r5] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | In a sermon, ʿUmar says wine's prohibition covers drinks made from grapes, honey, dates, wheat, and barley, and defines wine by its effect on the intellect.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#4/r6] إبراهيم النخعي | reports | Al-Nasāʾī says Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī was the first to permit intoxicating nabīdh; al-Qurṭubī calls this a learned scholar's error and says no opinion overrides the Sunna.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#4/r7] ابن المبارك | reports | Ibn al-Mubārak says the only reliably transmitted concession for intoxicants was from Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#4/r8] الطحاوي | reports | Even if reports of al-Ṭaḥāwī or al-Thawrī permitting it are sound, they cannot override the Sunna; al-Ṭaḥāwī elsewhere says the community agrees that fermented, foaming grape juice is wine and that permitting it is unbelief, while date-wine is disputed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#5/r1] الطحاوي | reports | Al-Ṭaḥāwī argues that the report calling wine a product of date palm and grape was not acted upon as applying the prohibition to date wine; nevertheless, the jurists analogize intoxicating date and raisin infusions and thus every intoxicating drink.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#5/r2] الطحاوي | reports | Al-Ṭaḥāwī accepts the report that every intoxicant is forbidden but says scholars differ whether it means the whole category that can intoxicate or only a drink that actually causes drunkenness.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#5/r3] author | holds | Al-Qurṭubī concludes that al-Ṭaḥāwī's own reasoning makes every intoxicating drink prohibited.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#5/r4] عائشة | reports | ʿĀʾisha says wine is forbidden for its consequence rather than its name, so every drink with the same consequence is forbidden.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#5/r5] ابن المنذر | reports | Ibn al-Mundhir says the Kufans brought defective reports; disputes must return to Qur'an and Sunna, and a successor's drinking is either mistaken interpretation or a sin requiring repentance.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67#5/r6] قيل | reports | An alternative interpretation treats the fruits as evidence for resurrection: their Creator can resurrect, regardless of whether wine is lawful, and mentioning its use does not itself imply prohibition, as in 2:219. | mentions 2:219
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67/r1] الطبري | reports | Al-Ṭabarī supplies an omitted mā after dates and grapes, with minhu signaling the ellipsis; another view supplies a generic thing, while the preferred parse takes minhu as referring to the mentioned produce without deletion.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67/r2] author | holds | Alternatively, the fruit phrase may coordinate with cattle to mean there is a lesson in dates and grapes, or with the preceding min to mean drinks are also provided from fruit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the verse preceded wine's prohibition: sukr means wine, while good provision is lawful food and drink from the two trees; Ibn Jubayr, al-Nakhaʿī, al-Shaʿbī, and Abū Thawr agree.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67/r4] قيل | reports | One view takes sukr as vinegar in Abyssinian and good provision as food; another takes it as sweet lawful juice that may later ferment and become forbidden when intoxicating.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67/r5] ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabī calls Ibn ʿAbbās's view strongest and offers either pre-prohibition timing or a contrast between people's unlawful use and lawful use; he concludes the Meccan verse was abrogated by the Medinan wine ban.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67/r6] author | prefers | Al-Qurṭubī says no abrogation is needed if sukr means vinegar or sweet juice, leaving the verse decisive and in force.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:67/r7] ابن مسعود | reports | Most commentators, including Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿUmar, Abū Razīn, al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, Ibn Abī Laylā, and al-Kalbī, define sukr as forbidden wine and good provision as what God permits; language authorities likewise use it for wine or intoxicants, with poetry attesting sukr.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:67/r1] author | holds | The first clause has an omitted verb meaning that people are given the juice of dates and grapes; the following clause resumes the explanation, with its pronoun referring to the omitted juice or fruit.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:67/r2] author | holds | The good provision includes dates, raisins, syrup, and vinegar.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:67/r3] author | holds | If revealed before wine's prohibition, the verse indicates disapproval of it; if afterward, it combines reproach with a reminder of blessing.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:67/r4] قيل | reports | Sugar is interpreted as nabidh, a fermented drink.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:67/r5] قيل | reports | Sugar is also said to mean taste or flavor, supported by a poetic use describing grazing on people's reputations.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:67/r6] قيل | reports | Another interpretation is food that removes hunger, with provision then referring to its sale proceeds.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:67/r7] author | holds | The sign benefits people who use reason to examine the evidence.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262/r1] author | holds | The phrase is attached to an omitted 'We give you drink' from date-palm and grape fruits, meaning their juice; the omission is supplied by the preceding verb.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262/r2] author | holds | The clause about making sugar explains the drinking or repeats minhu for emphasis; the pronoun returns to the omitted juice.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262/r3] author | holds | Sukr means wine, named by its verbal noun; alternatively, the verse predates wine's prohibition and is abrogated, or it combines a reproach with a blessing.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262/r4] أبو حنيفة وأبو يوسف | reports | They hold that nabīdh from grapes, raisins, or dates boiled until two-thirds evaporate and then fermented remains lawful until it intoxicates; they argue from this verse, a prophetic report, and many reports.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262/r5] author | holds | The good provision includes vinegar, syrup, dates, raisins, and other wholesome products.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p262/r6] author | holds | The signs in these fruits are evidence for people who reason.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p557/r2] author | holds | من ثمرات most naturally attaches to تتخذون; repeating من is emphatic and the singular pronoun can stand for an omitted عصير, the fruit collectively, or an understood antecedent.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p557/r3] author | reports | Other parses connect the phrase to نسقيكم, coordinate it with livestock as a sign, or supply an omitted ما; the author rejects al-Ṭabarī's proposed deletion as unacceptable to Basran grammarians and notes al-Zamakhsharī's omitted noun analysis.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p557/r4] author | holds | سكر means wine in Arabic, supported by poetry; al-Zamakhsharī derives it as a verbal noun from سكِر.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p557/r5] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿUmar, Abū Razīn, al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, al-Shaʿbī, al-Nakhaʿī, Ibn Abī Laylā, al-Kalbī, Ibn Jubayr, Abū Thawr, and most exegetes take السكر as wine; the verse is Meccan, before wine's Medinan prohibition, and thus abrogated on this reading.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p557/r6] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says God mentions wine as a blessing before its prohibition.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p557/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says السكر means vinegar in the Ethiopian language; another view takes it as sweet, lawful juice named for what it may become if left to ferment.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p557/r8] أبو عبيدة | reports | Abū ʿUbayda says السكر means taste and al-Ṭabarī chooses this sense, citing usage and a poetic witness continued in the next passage.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p558/r1] author | reports | The cited verse uses سكرا for something savored; another interpretation treats wine metaphorically as fermented from human faults.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p558/r2] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj rejects Abū ʿUbayda's meaning of taste as inconsistent with Arab usage and most commentators; views such as vinegar, sweet juice, or non-intoxicating drink avoid abrogation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p558/r3] أبو حنيفة | reports | Another view defines النبيذ as grape, raisin, or date juice boiled until two-thirds evaporate, then fermented; Abū Ḥanīfa permits it until intoxication.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p558/r4] author | holds | If السكر is wine, the verse is abrogated; without that reading it is not. If no abrogation is assumed, the verse combines reproach for forbidden drink with favor for lawful vinegar, date, raisin, or syrup.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p558/r5] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī permits treating السكر as a kind of رزق حسن, a coordination of qualities; the author observes that ordinary coordination suggests distinct items.
