## BINTSHATI: ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419 AH
[BINTSHATI:v1p24/r1] ابن قيم الجوزية | reports | The prevailing earlier view treats Qur'anic oaths as glorifying what is sworn by, since created things are great signs.
[BINTSHATI:v1p24/r2] author | rejects | Reading the night oath as glorification can require strained interpretations because night can also evoke loneliness, death, graves, and estrangement.
[BINTSHATI:v1p24/r4] الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh likens night to divine majesty through its fearful stilling power, while also noting its benefits for bodily and mental rest.
[BINTSHATI:v1p24/r5] author | rejects | The author finds this search for grandeur in night forced by an assumed rule that every oath must glorify its object.
[BINTSHATI:v1p25/r1] author | rejects | Earlier interpreters explain night in general, although the verse specifically qualifies it as the night when it settles.
[BINTSHATI:v1p25/r2] author | holds | The author argues that interpreters conflate glorification with the wisdom of creation; every created thing has wisdom, which alone does not establish an oath's intended grandeur.
[BINTSHATI:v1p25/r3] author | prefers | The oath's wāw can serve a rhetorical purpose beyond glorification: strongly directing attention to familiar sensory realities that prepare the reader for unseen meanings.
[BINTSHATI:v1p25/r4] author | holds | The quality selected for the oath suits its context; any impression of glorification strengthens the attention rather than defining the device's main purpose.
[BINTSHATI:v1p26/r1] author | prefers | The daily alternation from bright duha to a quiet night is a sensory image that prepares for the unseen pause in revelation, without suggesting that heaven abandoned earth.
[BINTSHATI:v1p26/r2] author | holds | The sensory contrast between light and darkness can clarify unseen guidance and truth or misguidance and falsehood.
[BINTSHATI:v1p26/r3] فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري | reports | They compare alternating night and day, which wax and wane by wisdom, to revelation being sent and withheld according to divine purposes.

[BINTSHATI:v1p27/r3] الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh says the Prophet's distress during the pause was relieved by renewed revelation: duha represents life-giving first revelation, while quiet night restores strength for later work.
[BINTSHATI:v1p27/r4] author | rejects | The author's sensory analogy is nearly lost in Abduh's account of night as rest and preparation for work.
[BINTSHATI:v1p27/r5] ابن قيم الجوزية | reports | Ibn al-Qayyim comes close to matching the bright duha after darkness with revelation after its pause, but frames the oath as proof of God's lordship, wisdom, and mercy.
[BINTSHATI:v1p28/r1] author | rejects | The author says Ibn al-Qayyim's appeal to doubts about God's lordship misses the stated occasion, which concerns a claim that God abandoned Muhammad.
[BINTSHATI:v1p28/r2] بعض المفسرين | reports | Some interpret the order of duha then night as preferring life until believers reach maturity, avoiding despair first and then fear of divine plotting.
[BINTSHATI:v1p28/r3] author | rejects | The author finds no clear connection between the oath and its answer in the accounts of al-Tabari, al-Zamakhshari, and Abu Hayyan, and notes they do not compare it with God's explicit denials of oaths.
[BINTSHATI:v1p28/r4] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari reports disagreements over whether duha means the whole day or one daytime hour, and whether saja means night arriving, departing, settling, or becoming still.
[BINTSHATI:v1p29/r1] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari selects daylight, understood as visible sunlight, for duha and stillness among its people for saja.
[BINTSHATI:v1p29/r2] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari defines duha as the early day when the sun rises and shines, while also reporting that it can mean the whole day; saja is settled darkness or quiet people and sounds.

[BINTSHATI:v1p29/r4] الفراء وابن الأعرابي | reports | Al-Farra says saja means that night darkened and rested, while Ibn al-A'rabi says its darkness intensified.
[BINTSHATI:v1p29/r5] النيسابوري | reports | Al-Nisaburi allows saja to mean that people become still, a figurative attribution to night.
[BINTSHATI:v1p29/r6] الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh defines duha as sunlight in the day's prime and saja as the quiet and inactivity of night-time inhabitants.
[BINTSHATI:v1p29/r7] author | holds | Although the words can carry several lexical senses, the rhetoric of this particular setting admits only the single sense that best fits it.
[BINTSHATI:v1p31/r1] author | prefers | The appointment of people at duha in 20:59 and its use opposite evening in 79:46 show that duha means a specific daytime period, not the whole day; the same reading fits the sun's duha in 91:1. | mentions 79:46, 20:59, 91:1
