## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:65:7-10/r1] author | holds | Wealthy and constrained people each spend according to their means; God charges only what He has granted, consoling the poor and promising ease soon and in the afterlife.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p200/r7] author | holds | Because divorce involves aversion, husbands may slander former wives to discourage suitors; repeated calls to piety press them to obey the law, respect rights, avoid harm, and pay maintenance, earning expiation and reward.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p202#2/r1] author | holds | A well-off or constrained husband should spend according to his capacity on divorced women and nursing mothers; no one is required to spend beyond what God has given.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p202#2/r2] author | holds | The apparent addressee is the husband, making this a basis for the father's duty to maintain a child rather than the mother's.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p202#2/r3] محمد بن المواز | reports | Muḥammad ibn al-Mawwāz says both parents contribute to a child's maintenance in proportion to their inheritance shares.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p202#2/r4] author | reports | He cites a report in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī in which a son says, “My father, support me; to whom will you leave me?”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p202#2/r5] author | reports | Most read لينفق with the imperative lām; Abū Muʿādh reports a purpose lām with subjunctive, governed by an omitted “we prescribed this.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p202#2/r6] author | reports | Most read قدر with a light dāl; Ibn Abī ʿAbla reads it doubled.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p202#2/r7] author | holds | The promise that God will make ease after hardship means a person whose provision is restricted will receive openings in provision; it is not limited to poor people at the time or poor husbands.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p153#2/r1] author | holds | A child’s father or guardian should spend according to his means; God does not burden a person beyond what He gives, as another verse says. | mentions 2:286
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p153#2/r2] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | Umar tested how Abu Ubayda would use a thousand dinars; seeing that he then wore better clothes and ate better food, Umar said Abu Ubayda had rightly applied this verse.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p153#2/r3] أبو مالك الأشعري | reports | A Prophetic report compares three people giving one tenth of their very different wealth and says their rewards are equal; Ibn Kathir calls this route unusual.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p154/r1] author | holds | God promises relief after hardship and does not break His promise, as the two verses in al-Sharh repeat. | mentions 94:5, 94:6
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p154/r2] أبو هريرة | reports | In a report, a destitute wife reassures her hungry returning husband that provision is coming; her oven fills with goat meat and the mill turns. The Prophet said the mill would have continued had she left it alone.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p154/r3] أبو هريرة | reports | A second report tells of a poor couple whose mill and cooking vessel fill after the wife prays; the Prophet says the untouched mill would have kept turning until Resurrection.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p35/r1] author | holds | Fear of poverty is no excuse to withhold support: the well-provided should be generous, while the constrained must give according to their means and trust God.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p35/r2] author | holds | “Restricted in provision” means God has limited a person to necessities; the passive wording teaches proper reverence in speaking of God’s action.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p35/r3] author | holds | Support is required for nursing women and others whom God obligates, and spending only a portion of what He gave does not consume the whole gift.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p35/r4] author | holds | God may replace support from capital, household goods, or sale of property when income is unavailable; the Prophet exemplifies simplicity and ease.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p35/r5] author | holds | God does not burden a soul beyond what He has provided; the verse may imply that a person given a duty to spend receives means to meet it.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p35/r6] author | holds | God promises ease after hardship, though not necessarily after every hardship; no one remains restricted throughout every circumstance of life.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p35/r7] القشيري | reports | Al-Qushayrī says expecting relief marks those of intermediate spiritual rank: they rise above despair but have not reached contentment whether means are present or absent.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p35/r8] author | holds | The promise was plainly fulfilled for the Companions, who gained Arabia, Persia, and Byzantium and their treasures; its promise remains enduring.
