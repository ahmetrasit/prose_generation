## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251#2/r1] author | holds | The concluding sentence is independent; its demonstrative marks the loss as remote and immense, while the emphatic pronoun, definite noun, intensive form, and mubīn underscore its totality and horror.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251#2/r2] author | holds | The form khusrān is more emphatic than khusr, and describing it as manifest leaves no greater kind of loss beyond it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r1] author | holds | The command to worship whatever they wish conveys intense anger: since they would not stop, they are told to proceed so punishment can come upon them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r10] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan explains the family as the houris; al-Ālūsī says this reading is somewhat remote.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r11] author | holds | The statement does more than define complete losers: it identifies them as those addressed by the preceding command, either directly or as the foremost members of its category.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r2] author | holds | The losers are those fully ruined, having squandered themselves and what they needed to secure.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r3] author | holds | On the main interpretation, their families are followers whom they misled; they ruin themselves and those followers by choosing unbelief.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r4] author | holds | The loss occurs when they enter Hell, having exposed themselves and their followers to everlasting torment.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r5] author | reports | Another view takes the Day of Resurrection as the point when their loss becomes manifest and is fully realized.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r6] author | reports | A further view takes family as followers generally: if they are in Hell, the losers lose themselves; if in Paradise, they are irretrievably separated from them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r7] author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī objects that separation from the Paradise-bound would be no meaningful loss if reunion would bring no benefit to the loser.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r8] قتادة، مجاهد، ميمون بن مهران | reports | Qatāda, Mujāhid, and Maymūn ibn Mihrān say everyone has a family prepared in Paradise if they obey God; unbelievers lose that family.
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p251/r9] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says they lose the Paradise-dwellers prepared for them had they obeyed God, thereby depriving those companions too.

## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p358/r1] author | holds | The command to confront the polytheists addresses those who pressed the Prophet to abandon his call and follow their religion; context identifies the two groups addressed.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p358/r2] مقاتل | reports | Muqātil reports that Quraysh asked the Prophet what led him to this religion and urged him to follow the faith of his fathers and leaders.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p358/r3] author | holds | Repeating ‘say’ and the declaration of worshipping God alone reinforces 39:11: the two formulations each imply exclusivity and together establish it three times, preparing the warning that follows. | mentions 39:11
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p358/r4] author | holds | The fāʾ introduces a rhetorical consequence, and ‘worship whatever you wish besides Him’ is a leave-it-to-you challenge showing indifference; their choice rests on whim, not evidence. | mentions 18:29
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p360/r1] author | holds | After the apparent leave-taking, the Qur'an adds admonition indirectly, speaking of the addressees as absent while intending them.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p360/r2] author | holds | The emphatic opening and definite ‘the losers’ make the phrase a hyperbolic restriction: complete losers are those who lose themselves and their families.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p360/r3] author | holds | The contextual target is the polytheists under debate; the relative clause replaces direct address to warn that they will lose themselves and their families at Judgment. | mentions 39:7, 39:15
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p360/r4] author | holds | Losing oneself pictures people causing their own punishment while imagining they are securing bliss, like a merchant whose capital is ruined; the wording also occurs in 7:9. | mentions 7:9
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p361/r1] author | holds | They lose their families by leading spouses and children into disbelief, so neither they nor their relatives benefit or help one another in the next life. | mentions 80:37, 66:6
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p361/r2] author | holds | The concluding ‘that is the manifest loss’ draws the result: losing one's dearest things makes their loss the greatest and clearest, emphasized by the verbal noun al-khusrān.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v23p361/r3] author | holds | The demonstrative, attention particle, and separating pronoun heighten and rhetorically restrict the judgment of their loss.

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:39:13-15/r10] author | holds | Loss is the wasting of capital, and khusrān is more emphatic; losing oneself means entering ruin so completely that the capacity for happiness is lost.
[TABATABAI:39:13-15/r11] author | holds | The idolaters lose themselves through unbelief and their families by leading their close relations into unbelief and associationism.
[TABATABAI:39:13-15/r12] بعضهم | reports | Family may mean the spouses and servants God would have prepared for each person in Paradise had they believed; the author finds this more fitting. | mentions 23:101
[TABATABAI:39:13-15/r13] author | prefers | The Paradise-family reading fits better because worldly kinship and social ties are severed on the Day of Resurrection. | mentions 23:101
[TABATABAI:39:13-15/r14] author | holds | This is manifest loss because worldly wealth or status loss is temporary and replaceable, while loss of the self is eternal and cannot be replaced.
[TABATABAI:39:13-15/r9] author | holds | The order to worship whatever they wish besides God is a threat: it will not benefit them, and they will suffer the consequence of turning away.
