## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:63:9-11/r1] author | holds | The address calls those who acknowledge faith hypocrites; they must not let wealth and children distract them from obligatory prayer, since abandoning it makes them losers.

## TUSTARI: Sahl al-Tustarī, d. 283 AH
[TUSTARI:63:9/r1] author | holds | Wealth and children must not distract from performing obligations on time; anyone diverted from God’s remembrance and service by worldly desire and then pleased with worship is deceived, except one God takes.
[TUSTARI:63:9/r2] سلمان | reports | Salman said he did not weep from fear of death or greed for the world, but because the Prophet had instructed them to keep only a traveler’s provision; his possessions were modest bedding and utensils.
[TUSTARI:63:9/r3] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet’s instruction was that one’s worldly provision should be no more than a traveler’s supplies.
[TUSTARI:63:9/r4] سلمان | reports | Salman advised Saʿd to remember God when he intends, when he judges, and when he swears.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v22p670/r1] author | holds | Wealth and children must not cause believers to neglect remembrance of God; the verb means to distract or divert someone from something.
[TAB-FULL:v22p670/r2] author | holds | The verb’s meaning is illustrated by Imruʾ al-Qays’s line about distracting a woman from her child with amulets.
[TAB-FULL:v22p670/r3] الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk interprets remembrance of God here specifically as the five daily prayers.
[TAB-FULL:v22p673/r1] الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk describes a believer dying with wealth but unpaid zakat, unperformed Hajj, and unpaid dues to God; he asks to return, but death is not delayed.

## IBNABIHATIM: Ibn Abī Ḥātim al-Rāzī, d. 327 AH
[IBNABIHATIM:v10p3357#3/r1] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Ibn Abbas transmits the Prophet's warning that a person able to perform pilgrimage or owing zakat who fails to act will ask to return at death; he supports it with this sura's warning about wealth and children distracting from God.

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v4p434/r15] author | holds | The command means wealth and children must not distract from God's remembrance; tulhikum derives from causing someone to forget and be distracted.
[NAHHAS:v4p434/r16] author | holds | A poem uses alhaytuhā for distracting a nursing woman from her child; those who do this lose mercy and reward.

## SULAMI: al-Sulamī, d. 412 AH
[SULAMI:v2p329#2/r1] سهل | reports | Wealth and children must not distract from performing obligatory worship at its earliest time; any worldly attraction that diverts from remembrance and service brings loss.

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH

## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH

[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p18/r2] الضحاك | reports | It means God's obligations, including prayer and other duties.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p18/r3] الكلبي | reports | It means obeying God through jihad.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p18/r4] author | reports | It may mean fearing God when He is remembered.

## QUSHAYRI: al-Qushayrī, d. 465 AH
[QUSHAYRI:v3p321/r13] author | holds | Do not neglect religion for wealth or children: prioritize God's due and He will manage worldly needs; your obligation is to God, while He guarantees your provision.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p477/r1] عطاء | reports | Ata identifies remembrance of God as His obligations, including pilgrimage and almsgiving.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p477/r2] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak identifies remembrance of God as the five daily prayers.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p477/r3] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil identifies remembrance as the prescribed prayer and says this verse and the next address hypocrites who professed faith.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v21p477/r4] author | holds | The verse says that one who lets wealth and children distract him does so by his own action, since wealth itself does not distract someone who resists its temptation.

## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:63:9-11/r1] author | holds | Wealth and children must not distract from God's remembrance, meaning the five daily prayers; those distracted from prayer are losers.

## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v10p6945/r8] author | holds | Anything that occupies someone and makes them neglect another matter has distracted them; wealth is named as the cause, as in expressions where an effect is attributed to its cause.
[JISHUMI:v10p6945/r9] author | holds | The term ajal means a period or fixed term, while ājil is the opposite of immediate.
[JISHUMI:v10p6946/r5] أبو مسلم | prefers | Dhikr of God here most aptly means all forms of obedience.
[JISHUMI:v10p6946/r6] أبو علي | reports | Another view restricts remembrance to the five daily prayers; other views take it as remembrance at all times or gratitude, patience, and acceptance in every condition.
[JISHUMI:v10p6946/r7] author | holds | One should not neglect God in hardship or blessing, since His beneficence continues in every condition.
[JISHUMI:v10p6946/r8] author | holds | Those distracted lose God's reward and mercy.
[JISHUMI:v10p6948/r4] author | holds | The command not to let wealth distract people shows that their acts originate from them; otherwise it would be God distracting them, and the text also says they are the losers.

## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p545#2/r1] author | holds | Wealth distracts through its management, pursuit of growth and profit, trade, and enjoyment; children distract through affection, care, and providing for their needs during life and after one's death.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p545#2/r2] author | holds | Believers already know that wealth and children are slight beside what God has, so remembrance of God must take precedence over them.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p545#2/r3] author | holds | Being occupied with worldly life at the expense of religion makes people lose their trade by exchanging the lasting good for a paltry, passing thing.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p545#2/r4] author | reports | One interpretation identifies remembrance of God with the five daily prayers.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p545#2/r5] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan interprets remembrance as all religious duties, effectively obedience to God.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p545#2/r6] author | reports | Another interpretation identifies remembrance with the Qur'an.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p545#2/r7] الكلبي | reports | Al-Kalbi interprets remembrance as fighting alongside the Messenger of God.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p315#2/r1] author | holds | Ilhāʾ is distraction through pleasure and appetite; remembrance of God includes prayer, monotheism, supplication, and every obligatory or recommended act, which Ibn ʿAṭiyya considers the stronger view over limiting it to prescribed prayer.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p315#2/r2] الضحاك وعطاء وأصحابه | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿAṭāʾ, and their companions limit remembrance here to the prescribed prayer.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:63:11#2/r3] author | holds | Wealth and children must not distract believers from the five prescribed prayers.
[TABRISI:63:11#2/r4] أبو مسلم | reports | Alternatively, remembrance of God covers all forms of obedience.
[TABRISI:63:11#2/r5] author | reports | Another interpretation is remembering God's gifts with gratitude, meeting affliction with patience, and accepting His decree, in both hardship and blessing.
[TABRISI:63:11#2/r6] author | holds | Whoever is distracted by wealth or children loses God's reward and mercy.
[TABRISI:63:11/r7] author | holds | Infidād means dispersal; silver is named for its distribution in the prices of purchased things, and anything distracting one from another thing is called a diversion.
[TABRISI:63:11/r8] عمرو بن كلثوم | reports | His line about a poem distracting the sons of Jusham from every virtue supplies an example of the verb ‘to distract.’
[TABRISI:63:11/r9] امرؤ القيس | reports | His line about distracting a pregnant woman and nursing mother also witnesses the meaning of ‘to distract.’
