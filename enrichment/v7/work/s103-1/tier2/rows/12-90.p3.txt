## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r1] author | holds | Qunbul retains the final ياء of يتقي in pause and liaison; other readers delete it, as expected for a jussive.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r10] author | holds | The conditional result is linked through the generality of المحسنين, an omitted منهم, or أل replacing that pronoun.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r2] author | reports | The strongest account is a dialect retaining weak letters in this environment; poetic evidence includes Qays ibn Zuhayr and two other verses.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r3] سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh says the jussive deletes the underlying vowel; the weak letter follows its deletion to distinguish moods.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r4] author | reports | An objection says the jussive marker already distinguishes mood; the reply is that retained ياء can make زرني أعطيك ambiguous.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r5] ابن السراج | reports | Ibn al-Sarraj instead says the jussive directly affects and deletes the weak letter.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r6] author | reports | A second account treats من as relative and يتق as indicative; it is challenged by the jussive ويصبر.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r7] author | reports | The jussive ويصبر is explained by successive vowels, with Abu Amr's readings at 3:160 and 2:67 as parallels. | mentions 3:160, 2:67

[SAMIN-DURR:12:90#2/r9] author | reports | The author allows a reverse account—conditional من keeps ياء by resemblance to relative من—but finds it odd that a distant العامل affects يتق and not nearby ويصبر.
