## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:74:35/r1] author | reports | The pronoun in ‘innaha’ may refer to Hell, the Resurrection, the warning, or be a pronoun of the matter; the Shaykh’s Resurrection interpretation has an unmentioned antecedent and a gender problem.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:35/r2] نصر بن عاصم، ابن محيصن | reports | The common reading has ‘li-ihda’ with hamza; Nasr ibn ‘Asim and Ibn Muhaysin, with a report from Ibn Kathir, read ‘li-hada’ without it, an exceptional form not suitable as a productive analogy.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:35/r3] author | holds | The exceptional form may be explained by changing hamza to alif and deleting the alif when two vowelless letters meet; regular easing would instead place the hamza between hamza and alif.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:35/r4] author | holds | ‘One of the greatest calamities’ means one of the great disasters; the cited verse by Ibn al-Mu‘alla witnesses the phrase, while kubar is the plural of kubra.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:35/r5] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ‘Atiyya calls kubar a plural of kabira, but the author suspects that this is an error.
[SAMIN-DURR:74:35/r6] الزمخشري | reports | The sentence may answer the oath beginning ‘by the moon’, or explain ‘kalla’ while the oath is an emphatic parenthesis; the author rejects the second as forced and contrary to the apparent syntax.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p130#3/r1] author | reports | The clause may answer the oath; alternatively كلا rebukes the denier, and the clause explains the rebuke while the oath is parenthetical or has an implied answer.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p130#3/r2] author | holds | The pronoun refers to Saqar; الكبر is the plural of الكبرى, analogized to other feminine forms ending in alif.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p130#3/r3] author | rejects | Ibn Atiyya's claim that الكبر is the plural of كبيرة is mistaken.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p130#3/r4] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari understands Saqar as one of the greatest calamities, among many large calamities; another reading makes it uniquely one of the greatest.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p130#3/r5] author | prefers | The interpretation that many great calamities exist and Saqar is one of them better fits the context and hints at further tribulations.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p130#3/r6] صاحب التيسير | reports | A further report takes Saqar as one of the seven levels of Hell; the author says this is not persuasive.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p130#3/r7] في البحر | reports | Al-Bahr offers the warning or affairs of the afterlife as possible antecedents for the pronoun; another proposal refers it to the Hour without a stated antecedent.
[ALUSI-FULL:v29p130#3/r8] نصر بن عاصم وابن محيصن ووهب بن جرير عن ابن كثير | reports | They omit the hamza in إحدى, an irregular but light deletion of the hamza.

## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p320/r1] author | holds | The repeated البشر in this passage and لواحة للبشر is complete paronomasia. | mentions 74:29
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p320/r2] author | holds | كلا is a rebuke and rejection, usually responding to a claim by the same or another speaker; the cited passages illustrate this function. | mentions 26:61, 26:62, 19:77
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p320/r3] author | reports | كلا may be moved ahead to reject the preceding claim about the parable, with the intervening material parenthetical and the oath beginning a new statement; this permits a pause after كلا.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p321/r1] author | reports | The opening waw may introduce an oath that concludes the preceding rejection of the claim about Hell's guards; إنها لحدى الكبر then explains the rejection, with its pronoun referring to Saqar. | mentions 74:26
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p321/r2] author | reports | Alternatively, the oath begins the following statement, and إنها لحدى الكبر is its response; the pronoun again refers to Saqar, the greatest of terrors. | mentions 74:26, 74:31
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p321/r3] author | holds | The three oath clauses repeat emphasis, conventionally three times, and swear by a great created object and two great signs of God's power.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p321/r4] author | holds | Moon, departing night, and brightening dawn are apt oaths because their lights appear amid darkness, evoking guidance and misguidance and the Qur'an's light. | mentions 74:31
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p321/r5] author | holds | The night departs as dawn approaches, while dawn begins to show its light.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p321/r6] author | holds | إذ and إذا are time adverbs functioning circumstantially for night and dawn; the ordered transition from darkness to light recalls Islam removing disbelief, as in 14:1. | mentions 14:1
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p322/r1] author | holds | The sentence is used like a proverb; إحدى means uniquely prominent among the greatest calamities, not merely one item in a group. | mentions 74:11
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p322/r2] author | reports | The comparison with one among the notable men and the proverb إحدى حظيات لقمان illustrates distinction and prominence. | mentions 74:11
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p322/r3] نافع وحمزة وحفص ويعقوب وخلف | reports | These readers have إذ أدبر, with إذ and a past-tense form: the oath is by the recurring moment when night has departed.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p322/r4] ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو وأبو بكر عن عاصم والكسائي وأبو جعفر | reports | These readers have إذا دبر, with a future-time adverb and a plain verb meaning departed, parallel to إذا أسفر; both readings are sound.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p322/r5] author | holds | الكبر is an irregular broken plural of الكبرى; the form treats the word as the name of a great calamity rather than a feminine adjective, unlike عظمى.

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:74:35-38/r1] author | holds | The passage clears the Quran of the accusation against it: it is one of God's greatest signs and warns all humanity, freeing those who follow it from deeds that bind them to Saqar.
[TABATABAI:74:35-38/r3] author | holds | The author notes another possible function for kalla beyond the two readings just reported.
[TABATABAI:74:35-38/r5] بعضهم | reports | The usual view takes the pronoun in indeed it is one of the greatest as referring to Saqar, one of the greatest calamities, and makes the clause the oath's answer.
[TABATABAI:74:35-38/r6] author | prefers | The author allows the pronoun to refer instead to the Quran: the rebuke answers the claim that it is magic or human speech, and the Quran is among the greatest divine signs and a warning.
[TABATABAI:74:35-38/r7] بعضهم | reports | Another analysis makes the clause a reason for the rebuke and treats the oath as an emphatic parenthesis with an omitted answer, or kalla as indicating the answer.
