## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r1] author | holds | God created people from nothing and takes their souls when their appointed lifespans end.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r2] علي | reports | Ali defines the worst age as seventy-five years.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada gives ninety years.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r4] قطرب | reports | Qutrub gives eighty years.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra says old people may lose their earlier ability to reason.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r6] ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba says severe old age can leave someone unable to know anything after once knowing about the world.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r7] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says some grow senile and become ignorant after being learned, demonstrating God's power to move people from knowledge to ignorance.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r8] عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says this does not describe Muslims, who gain honor, reason, and knowledge with long life.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p467#2/r9] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says whoever recites the Qur'an is not returned to the worst age.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v20p60#2/r1] author | holds | After signs in animals, God points to human life stages: growth, youthful plateau, slight decline in middle age, and major decline in old age; the transitions show God acts as the mover, not nature.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p60#2/r2] الطبائعيون | reports | Naturalist physicians say the body begins from semen and menstrual blood, both hot and moist; heat consumes their moisture and hardens the developing organs and tissues.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p60#2/r3] الطبائعيون | reports | They say early life has excess moisture that permits growth until about 30 or 35; youth follows when moisture only suffices to preserve innate heat, lasting until about forty.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p61#2/r1] author | rejects | Naturalists say heat dries innate moisture, the loss of moisture weakens heat, and the two decline until death; Razi objects that nutrition could replace moisture and that their account circles through mutually dependent causes.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p61/r1] الطبائعيون | reports | Naturalists say decline begins when moisture no longer suffices to preserve innate heat: middle age is a subtle decline to sixty, old age an obvious decline to 120.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p61/r2] author | rejects | Razi argues prenatal heat transformed much more moisture into organs than it does after maturity; if heat alone caused development, it should dissipate moisture even faster in the mature body, contrary to observation.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p61/r3] author | rejects | Innate heat in an adult cannot be merely the small amount present in semen, yet if it grows with the body, strength and health should rise forever; aging therefore is not explained by nature alone.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p62/r1] author | rejects | Razi concludes naturalists' account entails circular reasoning and is false; the stages of aging instead come from the all-powerful, wise, merciful creator, as illustrated by Q 77:20-24. | mentions 77:20, 77:21, 77:22, 77:23, 77:24
[RAZI-FULL:v20p62/r2] author | holds | The rational argument confirms that God creates humans, causes death, and causes the shift from youth and health to old age, weakness, and loss of knowledge; these are not effects of nature.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p62/r3] author | holds | God's knowledge and power explain these changes: nature cannot distinguish benefit from harm, whereas the world's creator knows benefits and can secure them and avert harms.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p62/r4] author | holds | Arḏal al-ʿumur means the most wretched and weakest stage of life; the wording is linked to Q 26:111. | mentions 26:111
[RAZI-FULL:v20p62/r5] المفسرون | reports | The commentators say God created people from nothing and takes them at the ends of their appointed lives.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p62/r6] author | reports | Razi raises two views on whether the verse's return to the most decrepit age includes Muslims or is restricted to disbelievers, but this excerpt does not give the views.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r1] علي | reports | ʿAlī identifies the “most decrepit age” as seventy-five years.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatāda gives ninety years as the age meant by the phrase.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r3] السدي | reports | Al-Suddī interprets the phrase as senility.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r4] author | prefers | Rāzī prefers long age: senility is loss of reason, which the verse presents as the end caused by reaching decrepit age; equating them makes cause and end identical.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r5] author | reports | A second view excludes Muslims, since long life only increases their honor with God; Rāzī supports it with the exception for believers in the cited verse. | mentions 95:5, 95:6
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r6] عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima says a person who recites the Qurʾān will not be returned to decrepit age.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās explains “God is knowing” as knowledge of what He does to His allies and enemies.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r8] author | holds | The verse establishes both a freely acting Creator and resurrection: God first brought humans from nothing and can restore them after death.
[RAZI-FULL:v20p63/r9] author | holds | The alternation of life and death, and of childhood ignorance, adult knowledge, and old-age loss of reason, makes a second return to life and intellect possible.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:70/r1] author | holds | Ardhal al-ʿumur means the worst and lowest age, when strength and intellect decline into senility and the like.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:70/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās calls it the lowest stage of life, when a person becomes like a child without reason.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:70/r3] أنس بن مالك | reports | Al-Bukhārī reports from Anas that the Prophet sought refuge from laziness, cowardice, senility, and miserliness; a report from Saʿd adds seeking refuge from being returned to the lowest age.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:70/r4] author | holds | Not knowing after knowledge means returning through extreme old age to childhood and forgetting matters once known.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:70/r5] قيل | reports | One view says this loss of knowledge does not happen to a believer because a believer's knowledge is not taken away.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:70/r6] قيل | reports | Another reading of the meaning is that the person no longer acts after knowing; action is expressed as knowledge because it depends on knowledge, and age affects action more.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:70/r7] author | holds | The argument addresses deniers of resurrection: the One who returns a person to this condition can cause death and then bring him back to life.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:70/r1] author | holds | God takes people at differing appointed times; the most decrepit age is senility, when strength and reason decline as in childhood.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:70/r2] قيل | reports | Some identify the decrepit age as ninety-five years; others say seventy-five.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:70/r3] author | holds | Old age returns a person to childhood-like forgetfulness and poor understanding; God's knowledge encompasses lifespan and His power can take the vigorous young while leaving the frail old.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:70/r4] author | holds | The wide differences in lifespans show they result from a wise, powerful determiner rather than natural dispositions alone.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p263/r1] author | holds | God takes people's souls; the most decrepit age is variously set at seventy-five, eighty, or ninety.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p263/r2] author | reports | The purpose of extreme old age may be that a person forgets what was learned, or ceases to acquire further knowledge.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p263/r3] author | holds | God knows the wisdom of transforming people from their prime toward decline or from life toward extinction, and can change whatever He wills.
