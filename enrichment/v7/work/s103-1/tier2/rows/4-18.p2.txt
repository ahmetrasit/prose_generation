## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:4:18/r1] author | holds | Last-minute repentance by sinners and death in disbelief are treated alike to stress that repentance at that point is not accepted.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:4:18/r2] قيل | reports | Another interpretation identifies the first group as sinful believers, the next as hypocrites whose disbelief and misconduct are compounded, and the final group as disbelievers.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:4:18/r3] author | holds | The verse confirms their repentance is not accepted and says their painful punishment has been prepared; i'tadad means preparation from 'atad, equipment, and may derive from a form of a'adda.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p212/r1] author | holds | Repentance is not accepted once the dying person sees the causes of death and the angel of death: responsibility has ended, and the act is compelled rather than chosen.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p212/r2] author | holds | Acceptance is reward and promise, and can only apply to a voluntary act.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p212/r3] author | holds | The phrase about those who die disbelieving coordinates grammatically with the earlier relative clause: there is no repentance for either group.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p212/r4] سعيد بن جبير | reports | Saʿid ibn Jubayr assigns the first verse to believers, the middle one to hypocrites, and the last to unbelievers.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p212/r5] author | holds | Some codices write the phrase with two lām letters; the following demonstrative is a subject whose predicate is that painful punishment has been prepared.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p563/r6] author | holds | The verse denies acceptance to the sinner who delays repentance until despair at death, and to the person who dies in disbelief; Pharaoh is an example of the first. | mentions 10:90, 40:85
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p564/r1] الزمخشري | reports | He equates the deathbed with the afterlife because both lie beyond the point of choice and religious responsibility.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p564/r2] المعتزلة | reports | They say knowledge of God becomes necessary through witnessing afterlife terrors, removing the basis for religious obligation and choice.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p564/r3] author | rejects | The author says approaching death does not itself end responsibility: Israelites were resurrected and held accountable after death, and death’s pain is no greater than other severe pain.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p564/r4] author | holds | Near-death distress could even help a person repent; God may accept at some times and withhold acceptance at others by sovereign choice.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p564/r5] الربيع | reports | He says the verse originally concerned Muslims and was superseded by the statement that God may forgive lesser sins, leaving believers’ fate to divine will. | mentions 4:48
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p564/r6] author | rejects | The author says no abrogation need be claimed: 4:18 does not say that no sinner without accepted repentance can be forgiven; he also notes that reports are not normally abrogated. | mentions 4:48
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p564/r7] author | holds | The two coordinated groups are distinct: ‘nor’ negates the rule separately for each group, and the phrase ‘while they are unbelievers’ belongs to those who die in that state.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p565/r1] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī gives two readings of those doing sins: disbelievers, because the next clause says they die as unbelievers, or Muslim grave sinners, with unbelief used as severe threat language. | mentions 3:97, 9:54
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p565/r2] author | rejects | The author calls al-Zamakhsharī’s options contradictory: his earlier explanation requires two groups, but this discussion collapses them into one or treats unbelief figuratively.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p565/r3] author | rejects | He says the strained interpretation serves Muʿtazilite doctrine by guaranteeing punishment for grave sinners and violates syntax and the apparent sense.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p565/r4] author | holds | The present tense ‘they do’ signals persistence in sins until death; ‘I repent now’ is repentance only upon seeing death and is therefore defensive, not accepted.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p565/r5] author | reports | Another reading makes ‘I repent now’ conditional rather than decisive, which is why it is not accepted.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p565/r6] author | holds | ‘Repentance is not for them’ means their repentance is not accepted, not that they cannot repent; the second group literally dies in disbelief and cannot repent afterward in this world.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p566/r1] author | reports | ‘They die’ may mean they approach death, just as ‘death came’ can refer to its signs; then unbelief, like repentance, is not accepted at that point.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p566/r2] author | reports | ‘Those’ may refer to both groups, who share a prepared punishment though one punishment ends and the other lasts forever, or only to the nearest-mentioned unbelievers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p566/r3] author | reports | The first reading warns the sinner who delays repentance until death by pairing him with an unbeliever; the second ties the threat to dying in disbelief.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p566/r4] author | holds | The apparent mention of punishment already prepared indicates that Hell is created, a topic the author says he discussed earlier.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p566/r5] الزمخشري | reports | He says the parallel wording of a prepared threat and God’s promise to accept repentance shows both outcomes will certainly occur.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p566/r6] author | rejects | The author says this subtly imports the Muʿtazilite claim that grave sinners without repentance must certainly be punished; the nearest antecedent may instead be unbelievers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p566/r7] author | holds | Even if the threat includes unrepentant Muslim sinners, it does not require punishment: God may punish or forgive them under the decisive verse about lesser sins. | mentions 4:48

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:4:18#2/r1] author | holds | الذين is genitive and coordinated with الذين يعملون: repentance is unavailable to late penitents at death and to those dying disbelievers; the first group means hypocrites.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:18#2/r2] أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ makes الذين nominative as a subject and أولئك and what follows its predicate, treating the preceding lām as emphatic.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:18#2/r3] author | rejects | That parse fails against the written form: an emphatic lām before the article would be للذين, but the text has ولا الذين with the negative particle.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:18#2/r4] author | holds | أولئك is a subject and أعتدنا its predicate; it may refer to the nearest group alone or both groups, and أعتدنا means ‘prepared.’
[SAMIN-DURR:4:18/r1] author | prefers | حتى begins a new conditional clause setting the endpoint for persistent sin: when death comes, they say they repent; it does not govern إذا as a preposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:4:18/r2] author | rejects | A prepositional حتى fails because it would govern a conditional clause, which cannot be governed from before; Ibn Mālik’s examples at 39:71 and 10:22 are discussed, as is 4:6. | mentions 39:71, 10:22, 4:6

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v2p238/r1] author | holds | A disbeliever who dies in disbelief gains nothing from remorse or repentance and cannot ransom himself even with earth’s weight in gold.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v2p238/r2] ابن عباس وأبو العالية والربيع بن أنس | reports | Ibn Abbas, Abu al-Aliya, and al-Rabi ibn Anas say the verse concerns people who die in polytheism.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v2p238/r3] أبو ذر | reports | The Prophet says repentance or forgiveness remains possible until the barrier falls, meaning the soul leaves while the person is associating partners with God.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p227/r12] author | holds | Repentance is not accepted from persistent sinners who do not return soon and wait until death reaches the throat; foregrounding the person facing death creates suspense and fear.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p227/r13] author | holds | A dying sinner's claim to repent now may be spoken aloud, as Pharaoh did, or held in the heart; the verse sharply urges haste.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p227/r14] author | holds | The same ruling covers those who die as unbelievers, literally or figuratively, without repenting, including at the death-rattle.
[BIQAI-FULL:v2p227/r15] author | holds | The verse groups persistent sin and unbelief to deter sinning, because withdrawal from sin becomes difficult after committing it; both face painful punishment, while unrepentant believers remain subject to God's will.
