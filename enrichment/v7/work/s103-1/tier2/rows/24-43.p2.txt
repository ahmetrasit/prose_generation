## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v7p5217/r2] ابن عباس، الضحاك | reports | The common reading خلاله means the cloud center; Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak give خلله, the plural of خلل.
[JISHUMI:v7p5217/r3] أبو جعفر | reports | Abu Jafar reads يذهب with a passive-causative form; the other readers use ذهب يذهب, which the author prefers because the verb is transitive here.
[JISHUMI:v7p5218/r1] author | holds | Khalal is the gap between two things; hail is named from cold or from scraping the earth; sana with long vowel means elevation and short sana means sight.
[JISHUMI:v7p5218/r4] author | holds | The pronouns in ثم يؤلف بينه ثم يجعله refer to سحاب as a masculine word, and its feminine form is non-literal.
[JISHUMI:v7p5218/r5] author | holds | In من السماء من جبال فيها من برد, the first من marks origin, the second a part, and the third explains kind.
[JISHUMI:v7p5224/r1] author | prefers | Everything indicates God's unity, transcendence, and justice; this is preferable to restricting glorification to some beings or treating it as spoken praise only.
[JISHUMI:v7p5224/r3] author | holds | The heavens and earth, birds in flight, clouds, rain and hail, and the alternation of night and day show God's unity and perfect power, knowledge, life, hearing, and sight.
[JISHUMI:v7p5224/r4] author | holds | Rainfall follows the choice and purposes of a living, willing God, contrary to natural philosophers.
