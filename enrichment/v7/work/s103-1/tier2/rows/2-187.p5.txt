## WAHIDI-ASBAB: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p48/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says fasting initially barred food and marital relations from the night prayer until the next night; some Muslims, including ʿUmar, broke that restriction and complained.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p50/r1] البراء بن عازب | reports | Al-Barāʾ reports that before the concession, people could eat and have relations after breaking the fast only until they slept; Qays b. Ṣirma fainted after sleeping hungry, and ʿUmar also reported a lapse.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p50/r2] البراء | reports | In a second version, one who slept before eating could not eat that night or the next day; Qays b. Ṣirma slept hungry after asking his wife for food, and the revelation brought great relief.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p52/r1] سهل بن سعد | reports | Sahl b. Saʿd says some tied white and black threads to their legs and ate until they could distinguish them; the phrase “from dawn” clarified that the verse meant night and day.
[WAHIDI-ASBAB:v1p52/r2] القاسم بن محمد | reports | Al-Qāsim b. Muḥammad says the first fasting rule ran from one evening to the next; sleeping barred food and marital relations, until God granted relief after ʿUmar and Ṣirma’s cases.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p596/r1] المفسرون | reports | At the beginning of the fasting obligation, intercourse was forbidden on fasting nights and eating or drinking after the last evening prayer; God then permitted all of it until dawn.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p596/r2] author | holds | The singular ليلة الصيام stands in place of the plural nights; al-Abbas ibn Mirdas supplies a poetic parallel.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p596/r3] الليث | reports | Rafath means intercourse, though its root meaning is obscene speech, as the line cited by al-Zajjaj shows.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p596/r4] أبو إسحاق | reports | Rafath is a comprehensive word for everything a man desires from a woman.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p596/r5] عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | Rafath means intercourse.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p596/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Because God uses modest euphemism, Qur'anic references to touching, contact, consummation, entering, and rafath mean intercourse.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p596/r7] الزجاجي | reports | No Arabic term was originally exclusive to intercourse; frequent use can make a word effectively literal, as with مباضعة, whose root means cutting flesh.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p596/r8] الزجاجي | reports | Other common intercourse expressions—غشيها, وطئها, قربها, and بطنها—originally denote other actions; even فرج is a euphemism.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r1] author | rejects | He rejects the claim that ناك ينيك نيكا is an explicit, literal intercourse term: it too is metaphorical, since Arabs say drowsiness “possessed” an eye to mean it clung to it.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r2] author | holds | The root of that expression means sticking or persisting; he postpones discussion of nikah to 2:221. | mentions 2:221
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r3] أبو عبيدة | reports | Rafath ila your wives means reaching or being intimate with them.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r4] الأخفش | reports | The preposition إلى governs rafath because the verb is used with the sense of الإفضاء.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r5] author | holds | Clothing covers the body; spouses are each other's clothing because their bodies join in intercourse and each is like the garment the other wears, as al-Jaʿdi's poem illustrates.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r6] author | holds | Arab usage also calls a wife clothing, bedding, waist-wrapper, mother of children, dwelling, or shelter; cited poetry uses izar for wives and includes أم بيت and ربضا.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r7] الربيع | reports | He glosses the spouses as bedding for one another: she is a bed and he a coverlet.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r8] ابن زيد | reports | He takes the image as intercourse: each spouse shields the other from people's sight; the source says this position is distinctive to humans, with a report that humans and crocodiles, and perhaps bears, mate belly-to-belly.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p598/r9] ابن عباس | reports | He interprets clothing as mutual comfort or repose; Mujahid and Qatada share this view.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p601/r1] author | holds | The spouses mingle through cohabitation; either spouse can scarcely endure separation from the other.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p601/r2] author | reports | Another explanation is that each spouse protects the other from what is forbidden; a prophetic report says marriage secures two-thirds of one's religion.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p601/r3] author | holds | The singular لباس after plural هن is used as a verbal noun; the sense is that they are mutually clothed or joined to one another.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v3p601/r4] author | holds | The forms خانه and اختانه mean to fail or betray; a sword that misses its stroke has betrayed, and fortune betrays when it turns worse.
