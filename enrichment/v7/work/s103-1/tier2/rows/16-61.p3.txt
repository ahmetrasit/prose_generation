[TABATABAI:16:41-64#4/r1] author | rejects | Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the Abū Hurayra report conflicts with the Qurʾān and Sunna on prophetic infallibility; it cannot mean punishment for merely leaving the preferable, since that incurs no punishment.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r1] author | holds | The reading that animals exist for people’s use is supported by the statement that God created everything on earth for you. | mentions 2:29
[TABATABAI:16:61/r10] author | rejects | The author rejects the rain-withholding explanation because the wording gives no evidence for it.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r11] author | holds | The delay clause supplies the omitted sense that God does not punish immediately; an appointed term means an individual’s death, a nation’s extinction, or the end of humankind at the Trumpet.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r2] author | reports | On the human-only reading, continued punishment would leave no moving human: most would perish for their wrongdoing, while the rare infallible prophets and Imams would never be born because their parents and ancestors had already perished.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r3] author | holds | Other answers defending the general reading of creature as all animals and humans are not reproduced because the author sees no benefit in listing them.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r4] author | rejects | The verse does not prove that every person who perishes is personally sinful or that prophets lack immunity: it predicts universal extinction under immediate punishment, while some could perish through their ancestors’ deaths.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r5] author | reports | One proposed defense limits people to wrongdoers because the verse says for their wrongdoing, excluding the infallible from the outset.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r6] author | rejects | The author rejects limiting people to wrongdoers: then only wrongdoers would die, leaving no reason for the verse to say that all creatures on earth would perish.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r7] author | reports | Another response broadens wrongdoing to include leaving the preferable, a breach of an indicative rather than binding command that may occur from prophets, as in Adam’s prayer. | mentions 7:23
[TABATABAI:16:61/r8] author | rejects | He rejects that broadening because the context concerns shirk and violations of binding commands, and the promised appointed term has the tone of threat rather than a reference to leaving the preferable.
[TABATABAI:16:61/r9] author | reports | A third answer says God may withhold rain because of human wrongdoing, destroying animals and righteous people too; for them the calamity is a trial and greater reward.
