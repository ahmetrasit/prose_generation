## ASAD-NOTES: Muhammad Asad, d. 1412 AH
[ASAD-NOTES:68:14#n12/r1] author | holds | They planned without saying 'if God wills'; this is the parable's first lesson and links it to the question about their wealth in vv.14-15. | mentions 68:14, 68:15

## BINTSHATI: ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419 AH
[BINTSHATI:v2p63/r2] author | prefers | The apparent story is that the garden owners planned to harvest everything at dawn and leave nothing; the author rejects the gloss that they merely omitted saying in shāʾ Allāh.
[BINTSHATI:v2p63/r9] author | reports | Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture.

## ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p432/r1] author | rejects | Although exegetes unanimously take the phrase to mean they omitted ‘if God wills,’ Islahi rejects that reading: the construction is unsuitable, and istithnāʾ is not explicit enough without contextual evidence.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p432/r2] author | prefers | He reads istithnāʾ literally: they swore to take all the fruit and leave none for the poor, rejecting a customary charitable gleaning practice also stated in Leviticus.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p432/r3] author | holds | The oath about leaving nothing emphasizes the same stinginess as Quraysh leaders’ forbidding good, especially Abū Lahab and his associates.

## KURANYOLU-TEFSIR: Hayreddin Karaman, Mustafa Çağrıcı, İbrahim Kâfi Dönmez, Sadrettin Gümüş
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:68:17/r4] Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu | reports | Most commentators say the garden owners resolved what they would do the next day without saying 'if God wills.'
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:68:17/r5] bazı yorumlar | reports | An alternative interpretation is that they made no provision for the poor's share.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:68:17/r6] author | holds | When intending future action, people should say inshaallah because human will and power alone cannot make an event occur; God's will is also necessary. | mentions 18:23, 18:24, 31:34
