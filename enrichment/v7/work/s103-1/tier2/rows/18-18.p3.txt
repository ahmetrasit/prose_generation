## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p503/r6] author | holds | The verse narrates a past condition with present-tense verbs for vividness; ayqaz is the plural of yaqiz, an awake person.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p503/r7] أهل التفسير | reports | Commentators say their eyes were open while they slept, making an observer think they were awake.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p503/r8] author | holds | The author also suggests they seemed alert because they were carefully preserved and had not acquired the usual relaxed appearance of sleepers; open eyes would make this still clearer if reliably transmitted.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504#2/r1] المهدوي والزجاج | reports | Al-Mahdawi and al-Zajjaj suggest their frightening hair and nails caused the terror, but the author calls this far-fetched.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504#2/r2] author | prefers | God preserved their sleeping appearance as a sign: their clothes and bodies did not change, while the awakened youth noticed only changes to the land and buildings.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504#2/r3] أبو حاتم | reports | Abu Hatim says both pronunciations of ruʿban, with a silent or rounded ʿayn, are valid dialect forms.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r1] author | holds | The author argues that the verse presents the turning as a separate report, not as the reason observers thought the youths were awake.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r10] أبو الفضل الجوهري | reports | A preacher, as heard by Ibn Atiyya's father, said that loving virtuous people brings blessing, citing the dog remembered in the Qur'an because it accompanied good people.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r11] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari reports unnamed views that the dog was a cook among the youths or one of the youths stationed as a lookout; the author says the human reading is weakened by the extended arms, a dog's familiar posture.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r12] أبو عمر المطرز | reports | Abu Umar al-Muṭarriz reports a reading kalibahum, which may mean a human sentry lying low with arms extended or the dog itself.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r13] author | holds | The active participle bāsit is used with past meaning because the verse depicts a scene; wasid means the cave threshold or its site, with courtyard, door, and ground also reported, and the author prefers threshold.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r14] ابن عباس ومجاهد وابن جبير | reports | They gloss wasid as a courtyard; Ibn Abbas also says door and Ibn Jubayr also says earth.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r15] author | holds | Most readers pronounce law iṭṭalaʿta with kasra on wāw, while some use ḍamma; the lam in lamilʾta is geminated by one group to intensify the sense, but plain form is more common.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r16] المخبل السعدي | reports | Al-Mukhabbal al-Saʿdi's verse attests the intensified form of the verb meaning to be filled.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r2] author | prefers | The author reports al-Hasan's alternate reading of the turning phrase, prefers Abu Hatim's syntax over Ibn Jinni's, and treats the turning as a separate reported action.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r3] أبو عياض | reports | Abu Iyad says the sleepers were turned over twice a year.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r4] فرقة | reports | One group says they were turned once every seven years.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r5] فرقة | reports | Another group restricts the turning to the final nine years.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the turning protected them from the ground, and sunlight would have burned them; without turning, earth would have consumed them.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r7] author | rejects | The author says avoiding feeding, sunlight, and contact with the ground through such a long sleep is more wondrous than simply preserving them from sun and earth; the reports are conjectural.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r8] author | holds | Commentators generally attribute the turning to God's command and the angels; the author allows instead that God enabled the living youths to turn without waking.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p504/r9] author | reports | Most commentators say the dog was real and belonged to a hunter among them; another account has a shepherd and his dog join the youths.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:17-18#2/r3] author | holds | The active participle bāsiṭ governs ‘his forelegs’ despite referring to the past, because the narrative depicts the scene as present.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#2/r5] author | holds | The wāw in iṭṭalaʿtum may have kasra, which is preferred between two vowelless consonants, or ḍamma by assimilation to wāw; ḍamma is favored when the next syllable is also ḍamma.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#2/r6] author | holds | Firāran is a verbal noun because wallayta means ‘you fled’; raʿban is a specification, as in ‘filled with fear’ or ‘the vessel filled with water.’
[TABRISI:18:17-18#3/r10] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās reports that they were turned once each year.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#3/r11] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās and most commentators say the youths fled their ruler by night, met a shepherd with a dog, and the dog followed them in their faith.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#3/r6] الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī and others say the youths appeared awake because their eyes were open and they breathed as though about to speak, though they did not.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#3/r7] قيل | reports | Alternatively, they turned over just as awake people do.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#3/r8] author | holds | God turned them periodically from one side to the other so the earth would not consume them and their clothes would not decay from lying on one side.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#3/r9] أبو هريرة | reports | Abū Hurayra reports that they were turned twice each year.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r1] كعب | reports | Kaʿb gives another account: after repeatedly driving the dog away, the youths heard it say it loved God’s friends and would guard them, then it slept beside them.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r10] قيل | reports | Alternatively, the eerie setting terrified people who came to the cave, and unbelievers sealed its entrance to kill the youths; God made this a protection for them.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r11] author | rejects | The suggestion that long nails and hair caused the terror is unsound, since the youths later said they had slept only a day or part of one. | mentions 18:19
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r12] ابن عباس | reports | Saʿīd ibn Jubayr reports Ibn ʿAbbās telling Muʿāwiya during a Roman campaign that no one, even someone better than him, was permitted to uncover the youths; when Muʿāwiya sent men into the cave, God sent wind that drove them out.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r2] مقاتل | reports | Muqātil says the dog was yellow.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the dog was spotted and named Qiṭmīr.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r4] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan’s commentary says the dog remained there for 309 years without food, drink, sleep, or rising.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r5] author | holds | Bāsiṭ dhirāʿayh means laying both forelegs flat on the ground, like a lion lying down.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Waṣīd means the cave’s courtyard according to Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and Qatāda; others say the entrance, the recess entrance, or the threshold.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r7] الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī places the dog by the entrance of the recess, not the cave entrance, reasoning that searchers would otherwise have entered or found it; shepherds later moved the stones and used the opening as a livestock enclosure.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r8] عطاء | reports | ʿAṭāʾ says waṣīd is the door’s threshold.
[TABRISI:18:17-18#4/r9] author | holds | Anyone who looked into the cave would flee in terror; God kept people away until the appointed time and protected the sleepers from predators and other harm.
[TABRISI:18:17-18/r5] أبو الحسن | reports | Abū al-Ḥasan considers the lighter lummilt more idiomatic, since people usually say ‘you filled me with fear’ rather than ‘fear filled me.’
[TABRISI:18:17-18/r6] author | reports | Poetry by Imruʾ al-Qays and al-Aʿshā supports the lighter form, while al-Mukhabbal al-Saʿdī supplies an example of the intensified form.
[TABRISI:18:17-18/r7] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan’s variant reads ‘and their turning’ as an active verb, with an understood verb such as ‘you see’ governing it.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r1] الزجاج | reports | A person who is alert is called يقظ or يقظان; the plural is أيقاظ, while رقود means sleepers.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r10] ابن عباس عن ابن أبي طلحة وسعيد بن جبير ومجاهد والضحاك وقتادة | reports | One view makes الوصيد the cave's courtyard; the words الوصيد and الأصيد are dialectal variants, also meaning enclosure or courtyard.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r11] الفراء | reports | The Hijaz says الوصيد, while Najd says الأصيد; both are dialectal words for an enclosure or courtyard.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r12] عكرمة عن ابن عباس والسدي | reports | A second view identifies الوصيد as the door; Ibn Qutayba understands the dog to lie at the door and cites a poem.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r13] العوفي عن ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير ومجاهد | reports | A third view takes الوصيد to mean the ground or open earth.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r14] عطاء | reports | A fourth view understands الوصيد as the door threshold.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r15] ابن قتيبة | prefers | He prefers threshold: the root describes closing a door against its sill, and this permits the dog to be at the cave entrance whether inside or outside. | mentions 96:8
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r2] الفراء | reports | The singular of أيقاظ is يقظ; he also gives يقظ as its singular form.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p118#2/r3] ابن السائب | reports | They look awake because their eyes are open while they sleep.
