## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p325/r3] author | holds | The Prophet is consoled to endure their denials; glorifying God before sunrise and sunset means the dawn and afternoon prayers. In an authentic report, Jarir says the Prophet compared seeing God to seeing the full moon and urged guarding those prayers.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p325/r4] النبي | reports | The Prophet said that no one who performs prayer before sunrise and before sunset will enter the Fire; Muslim transmits the report from Umara ibn Ruwayba.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p325/r5] النبي | reports | A report from Ibn Umar says the lowest person in Paradise sees a kingdom stretching a thousand years, while the highest in rank sees God twice each day.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p326/r1] author | holds | The night intervals mean its hours and the night prayer; some instead interpret them as the sunset and night prayers. The day's ends correspond to the night intervals.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p326/r2] author | holds | The hope of contentment recalls God's promise to give until the Prophet is pleased in 93:5. | mentions 93:5
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p326/r3] النبي | reports | In an authentic report, God asks the people of Paradise if they are satisfied; after they cite His unmatched gifts, He promises them His pleasure and says He will never be displeased with them.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p326/r4] النبي | reports | Another report says that after people of Paradise recall that God whitened their faces, weighted their scales, saved them from the Fire, and admitted them to Paradise, the veil is lifted and seeing God proves a greater gift, called the increase.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r1] author | holds | The Prophet is to endure mockery and other hostile speech; prayer and remembrance lift the soul toward spiritual excellence and help sustain patience.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r2] author | holds | Tasbīḥ with praise means worshipping God alone through prayer and remembrance, declaring him free of imperfection while affirming every perfection.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r3] author | holds | Before sunrise means dawn prayer; before sunset can mean both afternoon and noon prayers, while night hours indicate sunset and night prayers.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r4] author | holds | Night worship is especially excellent because hearts are settled and people are quiet and alone with God, though its greater difficulty makes it more meritorious.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r5] author | holds | Repeating the command for dawn and afternoon prayers highlights their special merit; the wording also encompasses voluntary prayers throughout day and night.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r6] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Jarir ibn ʿAbd Allah reports that the Prophet compared seeing God to seeing the full moon and urged guarding the prayers before sunrise and sunset; he then recited this verse. The author says it is in both Ṣaḥīḥs.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r7] author | prefers | The author prefers reading the prayer times as dawn and afternoon before sunrise and sunset, sunset and night during some night hours, and dawn, noon, and afternoon across the day's four endpoints.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r8] author | holds | On this preferred reading, the first and last endpoints are dawn and afternoon prayers and the middle endpoints preparation for prayer and prayer itself; the prepositions mark night portions but are omitted for the active daytime.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p57/r9] author | holds | The wording laʿalla keeps the worshipper hopeful rather than secure: God may please the Prophet with the triumph of his religion and fulfillment of all he hopes for in both lives.
