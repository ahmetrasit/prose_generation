## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143#2/r1] author | reports | Some scholars say the sleepers' eyes remained open to air so they would decay more slowly; a poet compares this to a wolf sleeping with one eye watchful.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143#2/r2] author | reports | Some early authorities say God turned them from side to side twice a year; Ibn Abbas says the ground would have consumed them if they had not been turned.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143#2/r3] ابن عباس | prefers | Al-Wasid most correctly means the courtyard or threshold at the entrance, though Ibn Abbas also says door and another view says ground; the word is also used as asid, compare 104:8. | mentions 104:8
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143#2/r4] ابن جريج | reports | Ibn Jurayj says the dog lay at the entrance guarding the youths, in keeping with its nature.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143#2/r5] author | holds | The dog sat outside because angels do not enter a house with a dog, image, ritually impure person, or unbeliever, according to reports; the dog shared the youths' sleep and blessing, showing the benefit of keeping company with the righteous.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143#2/r6] author | prefers | The dog was probably one youth's hunting dog; another view says it belonged to the king's cook, who joined their faith.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143#2/r7] الحسن البصري | reports | A report transmitted by Ibn Asakir from al-Hasan gives the cave dog the name Qitmir and lists other traditional names and locations, without Ibn Kathir endorsing those details.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p144/r1] شعيب الجبائي | reports | Shu'ayb al-Jubba'i reportedly called the dog Hamran, another claimed name.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p144/r2] author | rejects | Claims about the dog's color have no value, evidence, or need and are forbidden speculation about the unseen.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p144/r3] author | holds | God placed such awe on the sleepers that anyone who saw them would flee in terror, protecting them from approach or touch until their appointed sleep ended according to God's wisdom and mercy.
