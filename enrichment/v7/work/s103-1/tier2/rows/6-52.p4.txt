## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v12p193/r1] عبدالله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud reports that Quraysh leaders asked Muhammad to dismiss poor believers such as Suhayb, Khabbab, Bilal, and Ammar so they might follow him; he refused, then agreed to separate meeting times in hope they would convert.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p193/r2] author | reports | After the leaders demanded a written agreement and Umar suggested trying their request, this verse descended; the Prophet threw away the page, Umar apologized, and Salman and Khabbab said it concerned them.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p193/r3] سلمان وخباب | reports | Salman and Khabbab say the Prophet then sat with them until his knees touched theirs; after 18:28 he stopped rising before them and praised God for being commanded to stay with them in life and death. | mentions 18:28
[RAZI-FULL:v12p193/r4] الطاعنون في عصمة الأنبياء | reports | Critics cite the incident as sin: the Prophet expelled poor believers despite the prohibition, became one of the wrongdoers, contradicted Noah's refusal to expel believers and the command to follow prophets, sought worldly adornment, and later said God rebuked him. | mentions 11:29, 6:90, 18:28, 20:131
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194#2/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas, Hasan, and Mujahid take calling upon God at dawn and evening to mean the prescribed dawn and afternoon prayers; another explanation treats the two ends of the day as shorthand for keeping all five prayers.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194#2/r2] إبراهيم | reports | Ibrahim takes الدعاء to mean remembrance: the poor believers remember God at the two ends of the day.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194#2/r3] المجسمة | reports | The anthropomorphists cite wanting God's face and related verses such as 55:27 as evidence that God has bodily parts; Razi answers that God's complete oneness in 112:1 rules out composition. | mentions 55:27, 112:1
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194#2/r4] author | holds | Face is either an honorific way to refer to God Himself, like face of an argument, or a metonym for love and seeking approval because a lover wants to see the beloved's face; compare 2:115. | mentions 2:115
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194#2/r5] author | reports | One reading makes the pronouns in the account clause refer to the disbelievers: the Prophet need not bear their account or worry whether the proposed meeting leads them to Islam, since God guides and governs them.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194/r1] author | holds | Razi says Muhammad did not dismiss them from contempt for poverty; he set separate meeting times to win Quraysh, whose conversion mattered greatly, and any mistaken judgment in this effort is forgiven.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194/r2] author | holds | Calling their dismissal wrongful means the poor deserved honor; expelling them from that gathering wronged them by leaving the more fitting and excellent course, not by violating a duty. The other criticisms likewise concern what was preferable.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194/r3] author | reports | Ibn Amir reads بالغدوة with waw and a damma on ghayn, also in 18:28; the other readers have the common form بالغداة with alif and a fatha. | mentions 18:28
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194/r4] أبو علي الفارسي | reports | Al-Farisi favors the common reading because ghadāh is an indefinite noun that can take the definite article, while ghudwah is a proper name; spelling ṣalāh with waw despite its alif sound shows orthography proves nothing.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p194/r5] سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh says ghudwah and bukrah can function as generic names like Umm Hubayn; Yunus reports Abu Amr leaves them uninflected when used definitely, supporting the common reading, while al-Khalil permits them like ḍuḥwah, supporting Ibn Amir.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p195/r1] author | prefers | The more apparent reading refers the account pronouns to the poor worshippers, since the pronoun in the warning about expelling them certainly refers to those believers and the nearby pronouns should match.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p195/r2] author | reports | The disbelievers accused the poor of joining for food and clothes rather than faith; if so, Muhammad need only judge appearances, while their hidden account belongs to them, as no bearer carries another's burden. | mentions 6:164
[RAZI-FULL:v12p195/r3] author | holds | Both clauses are needed to express one reciprocal point: neither Muhammad nor the poor will be held responsible for the other's account. | mentions 6:164
[RAZI-FULL:v12p195/r4] author | reports | A second interpretation is that Muhammad bears no responsibility for their livelihood and they bear none for his; God provides for both, so he should let them remain rather than expel them.
[RAZI-FULL:v12p195/r5] author | holds | The episode resembles Noah's exchange with his people over his low-status followers: 26:111-113 describes laborers and craftsmen, and their account belongs to God. The expulsion clause answers the denial; being unjust may mean wronging himself or the poor who deserved welcome. | mentions 26:111, 26:112, 26:113

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#2/r1] خباب | reports | Khabbab’s Ibn Majah report says al-Aqra‘ and ‘Uyayna sought a private session because Arab delegations might see them with poor believers; the Prophet agreed before revelation forbade dismissing them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#2/r2] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | After revelation, the Prophet sat with the poor companions and did not rise until they chose to leave.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r1] سعد | reports | Sa‘d reports that Quraysh rejected following six poor companions, including Sa‘d, Ibn Mas‘ud, Suhayb, ‘Ammar, al-Miqdad, and Bilal; revelation answered their demand.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r2] author | holds | ‘You are not responsible for their account’ means their judgment and provision belong to God, as do the Prophet’s; the first min is partitive and the second emphatic extra.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r3] author | holds | Since God alone provides and judges, the Prophet must receive and sit with them; expelling them to accommodate people of lesser faith would be injustice.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r4] author | holds | The warning is instruction for others, not evidence that the Prophet actually expelled anyone; it resembles the conditional warning against shirk though he would never commit it. | mentions 39:65
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r5] author | holds | The syntax makes ‘then you would expel them’ a result of the preceding negation and ‘so you would be unjust’ a result of the prohibition; the clauses can also be reordered.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52#3/r6] author | holds | The verse and report forbid elevating people for status or fine clothing and despising those who are obscure or poorly dressed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r1] المشركون | reports | The polytheists objected to sitting with poor believers such as Salman, Suhayb, Bilal, and Khabbab, and asked that they be dismissed; the Prophet considered it but did not expel them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r2] author | holds | The Prophet inclined toward their request only in hope of converting them and their people, thinking it would neither harm nor diminish the poor companions; revelation forbade the contemplated expulsion.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r3] سعد بن أبي وقاص | reports | In Muslim’s report, Sa‘d and five other companions were present when the polytheists asked the Prophet to dismiss them, and revelation came after he reflected on the request.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r4] ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن | reports | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and al-Hasan interpret morning and evening invocation as keeping the obligatory prayers in congregation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r5] author | reports | Other explanations take the invocation as remembrance and Qur’an recitation, or as prayers at daybreak and evening for guidance and forgiveness.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r6] author | reports | ‘Seeking His face’ can mean sincerely seeking God’s obedience, or seeking God Himself, who is described as possessing the Face. | mentions 55:27, 13:22
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r7] author | holds | Morning and evening are specified because people are usually occupied then, so one devoted to worship during busy hours is still more devoted in free time.
[QURTUBI-FULL:6:52/r8] author | holds | The Prophet later obeyed the command in 18:28 to remain with these worshippers, not looking away to seek worldly display. | mentions 18:28
