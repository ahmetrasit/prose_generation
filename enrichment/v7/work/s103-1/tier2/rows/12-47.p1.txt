## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r1] author | holds | Joseph interprets the seven fat cows and green ears as seven fertile years, and the lean cows and dry ears as seven drought years.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r2] author | holds | Dāʾban means successive years; grammatically it is a verbal noun used nonliterally because sowing means persevering in the customary way.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r3] قيل | reports | Two alternative parses take dāʾban as a circumstantial accusative, meaning persistently, or as an adjective describing the seven years as continuous.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r4] أبو حاتم | reports | Abu Hatim reports Yaʿqub's hamza-vowelled reading dāʾaban and derives it from daʾiba; al-Naḥḥās says Arab linguists recognize only daʾb.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ explains the vowel on the hamza as permissible because it is a guttural consonant; the root sense is habit, supported by a line using ka-daʾbika.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r6] author | holds | Leaving harvested grain in its ears protects it from pests and preserves it longer, as was practiced in Egypt.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r7] author | holds | They should extract only what they need to eat; the explicit second clause is a command, while the first appears to report but may also command sowing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r8] author | holds | The verse grounds legal public interests: preserving religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property; securing them is benefit, losing them is harm, and averting harm is benefit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:47/r9] author | holds | The revealed laws guide people to worldly interests so they can know and worship God and attain afterlife happiness; this care is divine grace, not an obligation owed by God, according to Sunni authorities.
