## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p93#2/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada says خُلِّفوا means they were left behind from the Tabuk expedition; Ka'b himself rejects this, explaining that they were left pending acceptance of their excuses, not because they failed to join the campaign.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p93#2/r4] author | prefers | Ibn Atiyya prefers 'left pending their excuse' because the constriction of the earth marks the endpoint of that decision, not the fact they had stayed behind from the campaign.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p93#2/r5] الجمهور | reports | The majority read خُلِّفوا with ḍamma on خ and a doubled lām; other readers offer several vocalized forms, including المخلفين and خالفوا.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p93#2/r6] أبو جعفر | reports | Abu Ja'far says that if the word meant simply 'left behind' in the passive sense, the three would have had no sin.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p93#2/r7] author | holds | بما رحبت means 'with its spaciousness,' with ما as a verbal noun; their selves narrowing is a metaphor for grief and distress filling them, and ظنوا means they knew with certainty.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p93#2/r8] author | holds | Because the passage counts blessings, it first names God's action in turning toward them so that they could repent; when recounting wrongdoing, the order instead begins with the wrongdoer, as in 61:5, to stress culpability. | mentions 61:5
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p94/r1] author | holds | The episode's full explanation depends on the detailed hadith of the three in the cited books; their sin was grave because the law expects greater diligence from people of standing, who serve as examples and evidence against hypocrites and critics. Ka'b was from al-Aqaba and his companions from Badr.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p94/r2] الأوزاعي | reports | In a letter to al-Mansur Abu Ja'far, al-Awza'i says kinship to the Prophet increases one's duty to God and obedience, and makes people's censure stronger when one acts against it; al-Tanukhi's verse likewise says a great person's fault is great.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p94/r3] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | One route says the Prophet was at Umm Salama's home on the night their repentance was revealed; when she offered to summon them, he said the crowds would keep them awake all night.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:9:117-118#2/r2] author | reports | Khullifū means they were left behind and stayed; khālafū means they opposed and therefore stayed back.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#2/r4] author | holds | The earth narrowing despite its breadth describes extreme remorse; ẓann here means certainty, as in Duraid b. al-Ṣimma's poem.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#3/r1] author | reports | The three are Kaʿb b. Mālik, Murāra b. al-Rabīʿ, and Hilāl b. Umayya, who stayed back from the expedition through laxity rather than hypocrisy, regretted it, and were shunned for fifty days until God accepted their repentance.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#3/r9] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid says the three were left behind from acceptance of repentance after other people, including hypocrites, had been pardoned, like the group deferred to God's decision in 9:106. | mentions 9:106
[TABRISI:9:117-118#4/r1] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda say the three were left behind from the Tabūk campaign; the Ahl al-Bayt reading “opposed” means they acted contrary to the Prophet, and if they had merely been left behind they would not merit blame.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#4/r2] author | holds | Their world became constricted despite its breadth, describing the furthest degree of remorse; the Prophet had ordered people not to speak or sit with them while their repentance was delayed.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#4/r3] author | holds | Their ostracism was not a rejection of repentance: they were still commanded to repent, and God delayed revealing acceptance to intensify the trial and reform them and others against repeating the offense.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r1] author | holds | The constriction of their souls is hyperbole for such grief that they seemed to have nowhere to hide themselves.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r10] author | reports | Another coherence reading says that after declaring His sovereignty and that none can help apart from Him, God immediately demonstrates mercy by accepting believers' repentance.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r2] author | reports | An alternative explains their constriction as the tightness of their breasts from the worry within them.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r3] author | holds | They became certain that no refuge could protect them from God except God, and that repentance alone could save them from His punishment.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r4] author | holds | God made repentance easy for them, and they then repented.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r5] author | reports | An alternative reads their repentance as a return to their former condition before the sin.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r6] author | reports | Another reading says God accepted the three men's repentance and disclosed it to the Prophet so believers would repent, knowing God accepts repentance.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r7] الحسن | reports | Hasan says they shed no blood, took no property, and severed no kinship; they lagged behind the Prophet's expedition for property, family, or rest, then regretted it and repented.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r8] author | holds | Al-Tawwab means the One who accepts repentance repeatedly, and al-Rahim describes His compassion toward His servants.
[TABRISI:9:117-118#5/r9] أبو مسلم | reports | Abu Muslim links the verse to the earlier praise of those who repent: God now shows acceptance of their repentance and approval for following the Prophet in hardship.
[TABRISI:9:117-118/r2] علي بن الحسين | reports | The common reading says the three were left behind; ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn, Abū Jaʿfar, and Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq read “they differed/opposed.”
[TABRISI:9:117-118/r3] عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima, Zirr b. Ḥubaysh, and ʿAmr b. ʿUbayd read the verb with a light consonant and fatḥa on khāʾ and lām, meaning left in the rear.
