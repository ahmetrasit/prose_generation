The imperfect yadhdhakkar indicates repeated renewal of remembrance, while the verbal noun shukūr suggests gratitude can occur at once; arāda is repeated because the two forms do not coordinate smoothly.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v19p66/r2] الجمهور | reports | The majority read yadhdhakkar with a doubled open dhāl, from yata-dhakkaru by assimilation of tāʾ into dhāl.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v19p66/r3] حمزة وخلف | reports | Ḥamzah and Khalaf read yadhkur with a still dhāl and rounded kāf; it has the same meaning, though the doubled form implies greater effort, and both acts suit night and day.

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:25:41-62/r6] الصادق عليه السلام | reports | Al-Faqih transmits al-Sadiq's instruction to make up what was missed at night during the day and what was missed by day at night, citing the alternating night and day.
[TABATABAI:25:62/r2] author | holds | Khilfa means something that replaces another; night and day succeed one another and each can serve for remembrance or gratitude when the other has passed.
[TABATABAI:25:62/r3] author | holds | Remembrance means returning to innate proofs of God's unity and attributes, culminating in faith; gratitude is speech or action praising His gifts, including worship and good works.
[TABATABAI:25:62/r4] author | holds | Alternating night and day is a favor: one may recover faith, worship, or a good deed missed during one period in the other.
[TABATABAI:25:62/r5] author | holds | The verse links back to the sun and moon: though the arrogant are kept from ascending to God's presence, servants can still approach and receive guidance by alternating daylight and moonlit night.
[TABATABAI:25:62/r6] بعضهم | reports | Some interpret remembrance as the obligatory prayer and gratitude as the voluntary prayer; al-Tabataba'i says the verse can fit this reading but does not require it.

## ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p356/r4] author | holds | Verses 61-77 teach that the world's nights and days suffice as reminders for anyone willing to remember and give thanks; the humble servants of God contrast with the arrogant Quraysh, who are warned of consequences for disregarding God's favor.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p390/r1] author | holds | Reminder addresses the intellect and gratitude the heart; signs throughout creation sustain both, but benefiting from them depends on intention, which God tests without coercing human choice.
