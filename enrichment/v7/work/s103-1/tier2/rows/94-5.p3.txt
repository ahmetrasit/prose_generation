## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p407/r7] author | holds | It promises ease whenever hardship comes, urging the Prophet to endure the mission's burdens and seek God's help.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r1] author | holds | The verse promises that God will make each difficulty facing the Prophet easy; with signals ease follows closely after hardship or its first signs.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r2] author | holds | Because hardship and ease are opposites, their literal simultaneity is impossible; maʿa is metaphorical for ease's near arrival after hardship.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r3] author | holds | This reading reconciles the verse with the general promise that God will bring ease after hardship in al-Talaq; the intervals can vary.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r4] author | holds | The definite article in hardship refers to the Prophet's known hardship, with al functioning like a genitive replacement in a Kufa grammarian analysis; this keeps the surah focused on his honor.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r5] author | holds | God's promise to make the Prophet's difficulties easy continues the earlier promise, “We shall ease you toward ease.”
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r6] author | holds | Inna marks the news as important. The fa remains because it supplies the resumptive consequence as well as causation, which inna alone would lose.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r7] author | holds | The indefinite “ease” magnifies the relief, which overwhelms hardship; the promise may extend to the community because the Prophet's trials concern the mission and Muslims.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r8] الحسن | reports | A report through Yunus and Maʿmar from al-Hasan attributes to the Prophet the glad tidings that ease has come and one hardship cannot overcome two eases; Ibn Ashur takes this as including the community.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r9] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | In the Muwatta, Abu Ubayda wrote Umar about Roman forces and fears; Umar replied that God gives a believing servant relief after hardship and one hardship cannot overcome two eases.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r1] عائذ بن شريح | reports | Aʿidh ibn Shurayh reports from Anas that the Prophet said if hardship entered a rock, ease would enter after it and drive hardship out; then the two verses were revealed.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r2] البزار | reports | Al-Bazzar says he knows no transmitter of this Anas report except Aʿidh ibn Shurayh.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r3] أبو حاتم الرازي | reports | Abu Hatim al-Razi judged the Aʿidh ibn Shurayh hadith weak, as quoted by Ibn Kathir.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r4] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Jarir transmits a similar report from Ibn Masʿud as a Companion statement, not as a prophetic report.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r5] author | holds | The ease promise may be a parenthesis between the Prophet's raised reputation and the command to resume striving, teaching that no hardship lacks some accompanying ease; otherwise people would perish.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r6] author | holds | God's gentleness prevents hardship from being unrelieved, as reflected in the statement that if God punished people for wrongdoing He would leave no creature alive.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reports the divine saying that God created one hardship and two eases, so hardship cannot overcome both.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r8] author | holds | Hardship is difficulty in obtaining a desired goal; ease is the opposite, obtaining it readily without effort.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p415/r1] author | rejects | The rule that a repeated definite noun is identical and an indefinite one different does not establish “two eases”: it concerns an indefinite later made definite by the covenant article, in another sentence.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p415/r2] أبو علي الحسين الجرجاني | reports | Abu Ali al-Husayn al-Jurjani had already refuted that grammatical argument in al-Nazm; al-Kashshaf also rejects it, and Ibn Hisham calls the rule mistaken.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p415/r3] author | holds | The second sentence emphasizes the judgment that ease accompanies hardship; “two eases” is a symbolic dual for ease's preponderance, not a count derived from definiteness.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p415/r4] author | holds | The dual can suggest repeated abundance: as “look twice” in al-Mulk means look repeatedly, and expressions such as labbayk use repetition that implies strength.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p415/r5] author | rejects | The “one hardship, two eases” interpretation is not derived from the definite article on hardship or the indefinite form of ease.

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:94:2-5/r7] author | holds | The definite article on hardship is generic rather than exhaustive, and ease follows hardship in sequence rather than coexisting in time; the underlying rule is the changing, impermanent nature of events.
[TABATABAI:94:2-5/r8] author | reports | Al-Zamakhshari calls the initial fa explanatory and reads the passage as consolation through a gracious promise.

## ASAD-NOTES: Muhammad Asad, d. 1412 AH
[ASAD-NOTES:94:intro/r1] author | holds | Ash-Sharh was revealed almost immediately after Ad-Duha and appears to continue it directly.
[ASAD-NOTES:94:intro/r2] Ta'us ibn Kaysan and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz | reports | They regarded Ad-Duha and Ash-Sharh as one surah and recited them in prayer without a second basmala; Asad cites Razi.

## BINTSHATI: ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419 AH
[BINTSHATI:v1p68/r3] author | holds | The fa links ease causally to the prior gifts of expanded breast, removed burden, and raised mention; inna and repetition reinforce reassurance and remove doubt.
[BINTSHATI:v1p68/r4] author | holds | Repetition in short surahs need not be rhetorical excess when the occasion calls for reinforcing assurance.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r1] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says ma'a makes relief follow hardship soon, so that the coming ease is treated as if accompanying it for comfort.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r2] النيسابوري | reports | Al-Nisaburi likewise says nearness makes the time of ease seem continuous with hardship and strengthens hope.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r3] الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh says ma'a secures hope that relief must come, as though already with hardship.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r4] author | prefers | The author agrees that ma'a signals accompaniment but rejects the simile and the generalization to believers: the context chiefly reassures the Prophet, and the ease is joined to hardship.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r5] الفراء والزجاج | reports | They argue that repeated definite al-usr refers to one known hardship, while the indefinite yusr presents two distinct eases; a report from the Prophet says one hardship cannot overcome two eases.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r1] أبو حيان | reports | Al-Bahr reports the view that one hardship comes with two eases because hardship is definite and ease indefinite.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r2] الجرجاني | rejects | Al-Jurjani warns that repeating a sentence about a horse with a sword does not prove the horse has two swords.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r3] النيسابوري | reports | Al-Nisaburi explores generic or definite hardship and repeated or new sentences, concluding that a sound hadith would require one hardship and two distinct eases; without it, several readings remain possible.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r4] author | rejects | The author finds the competing grammatical scenarios needlessly complex; some commentators also identify the two eases as worldly and afterlife relief or as conquests and afterlife reward.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r1] author | prefers | The second verse confirms the first to strengthen the Prophet's inward certainty in God's gifts, rather than introduce a separate promise.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r2] author | prefers | Al-usr is definite by reference to the Prophet's distress at facing entrenched idolatry, while indefinite yusr leaves the relief open rather than setting limits on it.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r3] author | holds | Usr denotes the most severe hardship and struggle, with Arabic usage for intense physical constriction and Qur'anic descriptions of the Day's hardship and other acute trials. | mentions 54:8, 74:9, 25:26, 92:10, 65:6, 65:7, 9:117, 18:73
[BINTSHATI:v1p72/r1] author | holds | Yusr is the opposite of hardship; Arabic also uses it for wealth and for something prepared or achieved without difficulty, conveying relief and release here. | mentions 65:7, 2:185, 2:280, 74:9, 92:7, 92:10, 2:196, 73:20, 54:17, 44:58
