## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:18:45-46/r1] الراغب | reports | The second parable depicts worldly life’s quickly vanishing beauty; hashīm is fragile vegetation broken to pieces, and dharw means scattering, though one gloss says bringing it and taking it away.
[TABATABAI:18:45-46/r2] author | holds | Rain makes the earth’s plants fresh and beautiful, then they become broken fragments scattered by winds, illustrating how worldly adornment quickly vanishes.
[TABATABAI:18:45-46/r3] author | holds | Saying the rain “mixed with it” emphasizes water’s role in forming vegetation; rain is named because other water sources begin with it, and “became” does not restrict the change to morning.
