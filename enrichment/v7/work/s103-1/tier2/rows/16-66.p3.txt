[QURTUBI-FULL:16:66#5/r1] author | holds | The preceding discussion concludes that milk is blessed and that asking for an increase points to fertility, goodness, and abundant blessing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:16:66/r1] author | holds | The cattle here comprise camels, cows, sheep, and goats; they are a lesson demonstrating God's power, unity, and greatness.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:64-66/r10] author | holds | The first مِن is partitive, while the second marks origin or the location from which drawing begins; خالص means pure of blood color, dung odor, or coarse matter, and سائغ means easy to swallow.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:64-66/r11] author | reports | The milk description is also read سيغا, with doubled or light consonant.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:64-66/r4] author | holds | Livestock offer an instructive sign that carries one from ignorance to knowledge; the pronoun is masculine here for the collective's grammatical form, unlike the feminine in al-Muminun.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:64-66/r5] نافع وابن عامر وأبو بكر ويعقوب | reports | These readers pronounce نسقيكم with an a-vowel here and in al-Muminun.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:64-66/r6] author | holds | Milk derives from selected fine portions of blood formed from digested food; Ibn Abbas's account of rumen layers, if sound, means precursors rather than completed milk and blood.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:64-66/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reports that ingested fodder becomes excrement below, milk in the middle, and blood above in the animal's rumen.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:64-66/r8] author | holds | The actual process involves the liver separating waste and humors, directing surplus fluids, then routing excess after pregnancy and birth to the udders to become milk.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:16:64-66/r9] author | holds | Reflection on the creation of humors and milk, their pathways, causes, and governing powers compels recognition of divine wisdom and mercy.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p261#4/r1] نافع والشامي وأبو بكر | reports | These readers vocalize the initial nūn with fatḥa in the verb 'We give you drink.'
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p261#4/r2] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj says saqaytu and asqaytu have the same meaning.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p261#4/r3] author | holds | Anʿām is a singular-form noun on the pattern afʿāl, so its pronoun is singular; in 23:21 the feminine plural reflects its collective meaning. | mentions 23:21
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p261#4/r4] author | holds | God creates pure milk between dung and blood, separated by a barrier so neither gives it color, taste, or smell; the organ distributes the digested feed into blood, milk, and residue.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p261#4/r5] author | reports | An account says feed is cooked in the animal's stomach: residue settles below, milk in the middle, blood above; the liver directs each to veins, udders, or stomach, where the residue descends.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p261#4/r6] author | holds | This process is evidence for reflection; Shuqayq defines sincerity as separating work from defects as milk is separated from dung and blood.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p261#4/r7] author | holds | The milk is easy to swallow, and it is said no one has ever choked on milk; the first min is partitive, while the second marks the starting point.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p553/r1] author | holds | After rain revives earth, the text recalls the life of familiar livestock sustained by rain-grown plants and highlights milk emerging between dung and blood as a great sign.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p553/r2] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd, along with al-Ḥasan, Zayd b. ʿAlī, Ibn ʿĀmir, Abū Bakr, Nāfiʿ, and Medinans, reads نَسْقِيكُم with fatḥa on nūn (from سقى); the other seven readers use ḍamma (from أسقى).
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p553/r3] أبو رجاء | reports | Abū Rajāʾ reads يُسْقِيكُم with passive-like ḍamma on yāʾ; its subject may be God or, according to the cited authority, the livestock as a benefaction that supplies drink.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p553/r4] أبو جعفر | reports | A group including Abū Jaʿfar reads تُسْقِيكُم with feminine tāʾ; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it weak, but the author says feminine agreement with the livestock and masculine agreement with its collective gender are both sound.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p554/r1] الكسائي | reports | For the masculine singular pronoun in بطونه, al-Kisāʾī supplies the understood phrase 'the bellies of what We mentioned'; others explain the singular by treating the non-rational broken plural as a collective or referring to a subset of milk-bearing livestock.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p554/r2] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says الأنعام is a singular noun on the أفعال pattern and explains the singular pronoun; feminine بطونها elsewhere reflects its collective sense.
