## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p188#2/r1] author | holds | The two young men were the king's enslaved baker and cupbearer, imprisoned on a poisoning accusation at the same time as Joseph; maʿa requires their entry to be in his company.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p188#2/r2] author | holds | The cupbearer sees himself pressing grapes for wine; grapes are named for their outcome, or khamr is the Omani word for grapes.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p188#2/r3] author | holds | The baker sees himself carrying bread over his head for birds to eat; they ask Joseph to interpret both dreams.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p188#2/r4] author | reports | Their praise means either that Joseph interprets dreams well or that he cares for prisoners by treating the ill, comforting the sad, and helping the poor.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p188#2/r5] author | reports | Another account says the pair conspired to test Joseph: the cupbearer dreamed of three grape clusters from a vine and serving the king; the baker dreamed of three food baskets eaten by birds.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:12:34-36/r3] author | holds | The cupbearer represents spiritual love, which discovers in a dream the wine of love pressed from the heart's knowledge while true witnessing is asleep.
[QASHANI:12:34-36/r4] author | holds | The baker represents soul-desire, which sustains the soul; his dream signifies total pursuit of pleasure and appetites, like birds for their swift movement toward gratification.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r1] author | holds | قال أحدهما is an independent clause, not a circumstantial one: they did not say this while entering, and entry cannot become a dream; إني is the object of قال.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r2] author | reports | Some treat dream أراني like a verb of knowledge taking two objects, with أعصر as the second; others take أعصر as a circumstantial clause.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r3] author | holds | Dream cognition allows subject and object pronouns to refer to the same person, as here; ordinary verbs instead require an explicit reflexive or separate object.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r4] author | holds | When the causative hamza is added to this dream verb, it takes a third object, as in the cited construction in al-Anfal 8:43. | mentions 8:43
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r5] author | reports | خمر means grapes metaphorically because grapes become wine; others say it literally meant grapes in Ghassan and Azd Oman speech.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r6] المعتمر | reports | Al-Muʿtamir recounts an Arab carrying grapes who called them wine.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r7] أبي وعبد الله | reports | Their explanatory reading أعصر عنبا does not establish synonymy or a recited variant; it is glossing, like Abdullah's explanatory ثريدا. | mentions 4:2
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r8] author | holds | تأكل الطير describes خبزا; فوق may modify carrying or be a circumstantial phrase from خبزا.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:36/r9] الشيخ | reports | Al-Shaykh and al-Zamakhshari take its pronoun in بتأويله as referring to what the two men recounted, like a demonstrative phrase; another view says each means his own dream.
