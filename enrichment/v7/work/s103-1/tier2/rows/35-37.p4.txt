## DURR-FULL: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[DURR-FULL:v7p30/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the people in Hell cry out for help.
[DURR-FULL:v7p30/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas identifies the period of life in which a person could reflect as sixty years; a Prophetic report asks where the sixty-year-olds are on Judgment Day.
[DURR-FULL:v7p30/r5] سهل بن سعد | reports | The Prophet says God has excused no one whose life was extended until sixty; a second report says reaching sixty means God has fully given a person an excuse.
[DURR-FULL:v7p31/r1] علي | reports | Ali says the life granted to them as a proof was sixty years; Abu Hurayra reports the Prophet said sixty years is a full excuse and most of the community live sixty to seventy.
[DURR-FULL:v7p31/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says the relevant lifetime is sixty years; Ibn Abbas gives forty-six and al-Hasan gives forty years.
[DURR-FULL:v7p31/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada says long life itself is evidence and asks God for refuge from being reproached for it; he notes an eighteen-year-old was among them and says lifespan and messengers were arguments against them.
[DURR-FULL:v7p31/r4] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi identifies the warner as Muhammad; Ibn Zayd agrees and cites another verse calling him a warner from earlier warners. | mentions 53:56
[DURR-FULL:v7p31/r5] عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima and Ibn Abbas identify the warner as gray hair.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r1] author | holds | The condemned cry for help loudly; the intensive form of the verb derives from shouting, with the inserted emphatic letter strengthening its force, and is used for seeking intercession.
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r2] author | holds | They ask to return to worldly life and do righteous deeds, meaning to believe instead of disbelieving and obey instead of sinning, since deeds are accepted only with faith.
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r3] author | holds | Their qualification that they will do deeds unlike their former ones reveals that they had mistaken whim, instinct, and opposition for righteousness.
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r4] author | holds | The question rebukes them: God gave them enough time and a span in which anyone willing could remember; the wording may mean a period or a time in general.
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r5] الكاشفي | reports | Kashifi takes the phrase as asking whether they were given a lifespan long enough for reflection by anyone willing to remember.
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r6] author | holds | Reflection means having the opportunity to consider one’s condition and amend it; after puberty, reason opens, requiring people to infer the Creator from creation, and the duty intensifies around eighteen or twenty.
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r7] النبي | reports | A report says God leaves no excuse to a person whose life He extends until sixty years.
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r8] النبي | reports | The Prophet said the community’s lifespans range from sixty to seventy, with few exceeding that; after sixty, old age makes seventy the final period for reflection.
[BURSEVI:v7p277#2/r9] النبي | reports | A daily and nightly caller warns people at forty that the harvest is near, at sixty to account for what they sent ahead, and at seventy to come to reckoning.
[BURSEVI:v7p278/r1] عبد القادر الكيلاني | reports | To a young penitent, Abd al-Qadir says God summoned him, preserved him despite his turning away, and moved him toward divine nearness and intimacy.
[BURSEVI:v7p278/r2] عبد القادر الكيلاني | reports | To an elderly penitent, he says youth and adulthood were met with pardon and delay, but estrangement in old age brought displeasure; return will still be accepted.
[BURSEVI:v7p278/r3] author | holds | A Persian couplet contrasts the youthful who hurry toward spiritual gain with old age, whose coldness prevents the vessel from advancing.
[BURSEVI:v7p278/r4] author | holds | Companions and their successors intensified worship and night prayer at forty or on seeing gray hair, reduced social contact, and left their beds; no age is safe because death’s time is hidden.
[BURSEVI:v7p278/r5] الجمهور | reports | The majority identify the warner as the Messenger; alternatives are the Qur’an, reason distinguishing good from evil, deaths among family and neighbors, or gray hair.
[BURSEVI:v7p278/r6] الكاشفي | reports | Kashifi says many scholars identify gray hair as the warner, since it signals life’s flame waning and the mirror of one’s being turning white.
[BURSEVI:v7p278/r7] author | reports | A report says Abraham was the first of Adam’s descendants to gray; God called gray hair dignity in this world and light in the next, and Abraham asked for more.
[BURSEVI:v7p278/r8] النبي | reports | A hadith says God dislikes an old man whose hair has not turned gray, as explained in al-Maqasid al-Hasana.
[BURSEVI:v7p279/r1] الكواشي | reports | Al-Kawashi allows every sign warning of departure from life to count as a warner and calls for heedfulness when it appears.
[BURSEVI:v7p279/r2] محمد بن الحسن | reports | The sounder view attributed to Muhammad al-Shaybani makes Hajj an expanded duty that may be delayed unless delay is likely to cause it to be missed; sudden death brings no sin, but clear warning makes delay impermissible.
[BURSEVI:v7p279/r3] author | holds | The condemned beg to return for the whole span of worldly existence; God answers that He gave them life and sent a warner, yet they admit they had both.
[BURSEVI:v7p279/r4] author | holds | The command to taste punishment follows from God’s granting life and sending a warner; the wrongdoers are those who wronged themselves through disbelief and idolatry, with no defender to repel punishment.
[BURSEVI:v7p279/r5] author | holds | Ishari reading: people were asleep in worldly heedlessness and only awaken fully after death and resurrection, when they taste and perceive punishment.
