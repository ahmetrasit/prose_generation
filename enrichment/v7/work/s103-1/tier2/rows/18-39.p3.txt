## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r1] author | holds | The opening لولا rebukes the garden-owner and urges him to say this at the very moment of entering.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r10] عمرو بن مرة | reports | Ibn Murra says that a person’s saying ما شاء الله is among the best supplications.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r11] أنس | reports | Anas reports a Prophetic promise that invoking the phrase over blessings in family, property, or children protects from every calamity until death.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r2] author | reports | ما شاء الله may mean “the matter is what God wills,” or “whatever God willed has come to be”; it can be a relative or a conditional clause.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r3] author | holds | Either construction urges recognition that the orchard and everything in it exist by God’s will and may be preserved or destroyed by Him.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r4] author | reports | If ما is conditional or functions like one, the wording also implies that what God does not will will not occur.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r5] القفال | reports | The Muʿtazilite al-Qaffal restricts “what God willed” to the fruit in the garden, denying that the clause is universal.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r6] الكعبي والجبائي | reports | Al-Kaʿbi and al-Jubbaʾi say the verse covers only what God directly does, not human acts; things He dislikes may occur in His dominion.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r7] author | rejects | The author insists that the natural reading is universal and says the Muʿtazilites depart from that sense to protect their doctrine.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r8] author | holds | “There is no power except through God” confesses the speaker’s inability and that his building and management depend on divine aid and empowerment.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p279#2/r9] أبو هريرة | reports | Abu Hurayra transmits the Prophet’s description of this invocation as a treasure of Paradise beneath the Throne; he distinguishes it from لا حول ولا قوة بالله.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p280/r1] أنس | reports | Anas says that invoking the phrase when something in one’s property pleases the viewer prevents calamity from striking it; another version is Prophetic.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p280/r2] مالك | reports | Malik said ما شاء الله when entering his home, citing the verse; Ibn al-ʿArabi inferred that it is recommended when entering one’s home.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p280/r3] عروة | reports | ʿUrwa said the phrase on seeing something pleasing in his property or entering a garden, applying the verse.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p280/r4] author | holds | Reports support saying the invocation on seeing anything pleasing, one’s own or another’s, as protection from the evil eye of admiration.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p280/r5] author | reports | In ترني أنا أقل, أنا emphasizes the object pronoun; تراني may denote knowledge, taking أقل as its second object, or sight, making it a circumstantial phrase.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p280/r6] author | reports | أنا can instead be a separator, in which case the seeing verb must be cognitive rather than visual.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p280/r7] عيسى بن عمر | reports | Isa ibn ʿUmar reads أقل in the nominative; أنا is then the subject and أقل its predicate.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p280/r8] author | holds | مالا وولدا are specification words on both readings.
