## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v7p48#2/r5] author | holds | The contrasting sincere donors spend for two ends: seeking God's pleasure and تثبيت of themselves.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p48/r4] القفال | reports | Al-Qaffāl offers a seed analogy: sincere deeds grow like seed in fertile garden soil, while the reproacher's deed is seed stored on sterile stone and yields nothing when needed.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49#2/r1] author | holds | Repeated giving for God's pleasure creates a lasting disposition: even an accidental lapse quickly returns the heart toward the sacred, spiritually aligning the person with angelic and holy realities. | mentions 14:27
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49#2/r2] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj says تثبيت is confident assurance that God will not waste or disappoint the donors' work, unlike the hypocrite who sees giving as wasted without belief in reward.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49#2/r3] الحسن ومجاهد وعطاء | reports | Al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, and ʿAṭāʾ interpret تثبيت as careful placement of alms among people of integrity; al-Ḥasan says a donor gives when the motive is truly for God.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49#2/r4] author | holds | The word ربوة means elevated, swelling ground; the author favors fertile soil that rises and grows with rain over interpreters' ordinary hill, citing Q 22:5 and the contrast with sterile stone. | mentions 22:5
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49#2/r5] عاصم وابن عامر | reports | ʿĀṣim and Ibn ʿĀmir read ربوة with fatḥa on rāʾ, a Tamīm dialect; the other readers use ḍamma, the more common Qurashī form, among seven variants.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49/r1] القاضي | reports | Al-Qāḍī says تثبيت means training oneself to preserve obedience and avoid what corrupts it, including subsequent reproach and injury.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid's reading, وتثبيتا من بعض أنفسهم, supports construing تثبيت as reassuring others that the donors' faith and sincerity are genuine.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49/r3] صاحب الكشاف | reports | The Kashshāf explains that struggle subdues part of the self attached to worldly life and wealth; spending money stabilizes part, while spending life stabilizes all, as in al-Ṣaff 61:11. | mentions 61:11
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49/r4] author | holds | Al-Rāzī proposes that the heart becomes firm through God's remembrance; spending brings calm only when done solely for worship, illustrated by ʿAlī's words in al-Insān 76:9. | mentions 13:28, 76:9
[RAZI-FULL:v7p49/r5] author | reports | He also cites the report about Abū Bakr's spending as seeking only God's face, and notes the rational principle that repeating acts produces stable dispositions. | mentions 92:19, 92:20, 92:21
[RAZI-FULL:v7p50/r1] المفسرون | rejects | Exegetes say a garden on a hill yields better, but al-Rāzī objects that elevation or a valley each has disadvantages and prefers fertile swelling soil, supported by Q 22:5 and the stone contrast. | mentions 22:5
[RAZI-FULL:v7p50/r2] ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو | reports | Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, and Abū ʿAmr lighten أكلها; the remaining readers intensify it. The word means edible fruit or produce, as in Ibrāhīm 14:25, with lexical poetry cited. | mentions 14:25
[RAZI-FULL:v7p50/r3] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj reads ضعفين as twice as much; ʿAṭāʾ says a single year's yield equals another garden's two-year yield, while al-Aṣamm and Abū Muslim give related doubled-yield readings.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p51/r1] author | holds | Ṭall is light rain. Either a fertile garden preserves its full yield despite receiving only light rain, or it still yields some fruit; sincere giving is never wasted, little or much.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p51/r2] author | holds | God sees the amount and manner of spending and its motives, then recompenses each person accordingly, for good with good and evil with evil.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r1] author | holds | A rabwa is a slightly elevated place, usually with deep soil and better growth, which explains why it is chosen for the parable.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r2] ابن عطية | rejects | Ibn Atiyya rejects al-Tabari's identification with the 'gardens of al-Ḥazn': those are the gardens of Najd, whose growth and air he says are superior to Tihama's.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r3] author | reports | An Arab woman compared her husband to a Tihama night, invoked as evidence that Tihama's air is unpleasant except at night.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r4] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi reads rabwa as rabāwa, a low-lying place; Ibn Atiyya calls the wording awkward, and the author says it is untenable because the root means increase and elevation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra explains 'a severe seizure' in 69:10 as increased and compares it with taking more than one gave, as evidence for the root's sense of increase. | mentions 69:10
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r6] الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil defines rabwa as elevated, good land; the author says the verse pictures a raised place without running water, familiar from Arab experience.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas defines rabwa as elevated land without rivers, inferred from the verse's rainfall; it cannot mean only river-fed land because 11:98 mentions a rabwa with stable water and a spring. | mentions 11:98
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r8] author | holds | In common Arabic, rabwa means any land raised above its surroundings, whether or not water flows there.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#2/r9] author | reports | The first four readings are ḍamma for Ibn Kathir, Hamza, al-Kisaʾi, Nafi, and Abu Amr; fatḥa for Asim, Ibn Amir, and al-Hasan; kasra for Ibn Abbas and Abu Ishaq; and rabāwa with fatḥa for Abu Jaʿfar and Abu Abd al-Rahman.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r1] author | holds | Aṣābah refers to the elevated ground; wābil is heavy rain, and the cited poem describes a luxuriant garden watered by a downpour.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r10] author | rejects | Some propose inverted word order, making the garden yield after either heavy rain or drizzle; the author prefers the ordinary order and sees no need for inversion.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r11] author | holds | The parable depicts sincere spending growing as God nurtures charity, like a foal or young camel, unlike dust washed off a bare rock.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r12] الفراء | reports | Al-Ashhab al-Uqayli reads rabāwa with kasra; al-Farra records rabāwa variants and derives them from rising, from rabā yarbū.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r2] author | holds | Ātāt means 'yielded'; ukul is edible fruit, with 14:25 as a parallel; ukla can also mean a bite or two, as in a Muslim report. | mentions 14:25
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r3] author | holds | The possessive link to the garden is one of association, like a horse's saddle or a house's door, since the garden itself does not eat fruit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r4] نافع | reports | Nafi, Ibn Kathir, and Abu Amr read ukul with a ḍamma on hamza and a still kāf in feminine possessives; Abu Amr lengthens other contexts where the others lighten, while Asim, Ibn Amir, Hamza, and al-Kisa'i lengthen all listed forms. | mentions 34:16
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r5] author | holds | Akl and ukl mean the same thing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r6] author | prefers | The garden yields twice the fruit of other land; the author prefers this over the report that it bears twice yearly, meaning one year's yield equals two years elsewhere.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r7] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad supplies an omitted phrase: if no downpour reaches it, a light rain is enough; al-Zajjaj instead glosses the phrase as the light rain that reaches it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Al-Tall is light, fine rain according to Ibn Abbas and others; Mujahid and some others identify it with dew, which Ibn Atiyya calls figurative comparison.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#3/r9] الماوردي | reports | Al-Mawardi notes that dew-fed crops yield less than rain-fed crops but retain some growth and benefit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#4/r1] رسول الله | reports | Abu Hurayra reports the Prophet saying that God takes a single date from lawful earnings and nurtures it like a colt or young camel until it becomes mountain-sized or greater; Muslim and the Muwatta transmit it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#4/r2] author | holds | God's seeing what people do conveys both promise and warning.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265#4/r3] الزهري | reports | Al-Zuhri reads 'they do' in the third person; the pronoun may refer to people generally or just donors, in which case the statement is pure promise.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265/r1] author | holds | Ibtighāʾ is parsed as a purpose complement and tathbītan coordinated with it; Makki treats both as purpose complements, but Ibn Atiyya rejects that analysis for tathbītan because spending is not done for the sake of stabilization.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265/r10] النحاس | reports | Al-Nahhas says Mujahid's interpretation would require tathabbatan; Qatada's 'expecting reward' is unfamiliar unless it means that their souls, expecting reward, strengthen them, which he finds far-fetched.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:265/r11] النحاس | prefers | Al-Nahhas approves al-Sha'bi's sense: one's own certainty strengthens resolve and judgment to spend in obedience to God.
