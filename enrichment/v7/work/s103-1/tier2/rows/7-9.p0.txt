## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v4p216/r1] author | holds | Weighing deeds tests faith in worldly life, marks the saved and doomed in the next, shows people their recompense, and establishes the proof against them.
[THALABI:v4p216/r2] author | holds | The weighing is comparable to the record that speaks with truth and sets out people’s deeds, despite God already knowing them. | mentions 45:29
[THALABI:v4p216/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid identifies the heavy balance-pan with a person’s good deeds.
[THALABI:v4p216/r4] author | holds | The phrase about wronging God’s signs means that they deny them.
[THALABI:v4p216/r5] حذيفة | reports | Gabriel weighs people’s deeds; wrongdoers’ good deeds transfer to victims, or victims’ sins are loaded onto them, leaving them burdened like mountains.
[THALABI:v4p216/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas describes a balance with tongue and two pans: a believer’s beautiful deeds outweigh sins and lead to Paradise, while a disbeliever’s ugly deeds are light and fall into Hell.
[THALABI:v4p216/r7] author | holds | Since deeds are accidents, the weighed objects are written records containing people’s deeds.
[THALABI:v4p216/r8] عبد الله بن عمر | reports | A man’s ninety-nine enormous sin-records are outweighed by a tiny card bearing the testimony that there is no deity but God and Muhammad is His messenger.
[THALABI:v4p216/r9] author | holds | The weighing is linked to the promise that just balances will be set up for the Day of Resurrection. | mentions 21:47
[THALABI:v4p217/r1] author | holds | The plural mawazin may be a plural form with singular meaning, like plural terms in the cited verse and al-Aʿsha’s poetry.
[THALABI:v4p217/r2] الأعشى | reports | Al-Aʿsha’s line uses plural ‘breasts’ and ‘forearms’ while intending one of each.
[THALABI:v4p217/r3] author | reports | Other interpretations take the plural as the deeds being weighed, or as one great balance with a balance for each servant.
[THALABI:v4p217/r4] author | holds | The plural can refer to the whole balance assembly: its two pans, beam, and tongue, which must come together for weighing.
[THALABI:v4p217/r5] author | reports | A moral interpretation calls reason the balance distinguishing truth from falsehood, knowledge the balance distinguishing lawful from unlawful, and sound will the balance distinguishing felicity from misery.

## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v4p2499/r3] author | holds | Weighing compares two things to reveal their relative measure, then extends metaphorically to prosody and measured speech; thaqal is opposite lightness.
[JISHUMI:v4p2499/r4] أبو هاشم | reports | Abū Hāshim treats heaviness as a distinct attribute in a heavy object: a necessary downward tendency.
[JISHUMI:v4p2499/r5] أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī says heaviness reduces to the parts composing the heavy object; they agree that lightness is no positive attribute but the absence of heaviness.
[JISHUMI:v4p2499/r6] author | holds | Loss means losing one's capital.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p215/r1] author | holds | Light scales describe those who deny God’s signs.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p215/r2] أبو بكر | reports | Abu Bakr says scales grow heavy through following truth in worldly life, even when truth weighs upon people; following falsehood makes them light.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p215/r3] author | reports | One explanation takes the plural form موازين as singular in meaning, like the plural address يا أيها الرسل.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p215/r4] author | reports | Another explanation assigns every servant an individual balance.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p215/r5] author | reports | A third view posits one great balance with a separate balance suspended for each servant.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v3p215/r6] author | reports | The plural may also refer to the balance’s two pans and indicator, whose combined parts are needed for weighing.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p375/r2] فرقة | reports | One group takes scales and weighing as a metaphor for the meticulous justice of Judgment, using the familiar image of a balance; Abu Talib's verse illustrates this sense.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p375/r3] author | rejects | Ibn Atiyya says the metaphorical view is weaker: Qur'anic wording and a Prophetic report direct a person to seek the Prophet at the visible balance, weighing poses no problem, and abandoning the literal sense without cause risks denying revealed realities.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p375/r4] مجاهد، الضحاك | reports | This metaphorical view is also reported from Mujahid, al-Dahhak, and others, who understand heaviness and lightness as the abundance or scarcity of good deeds.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p375/r5] جمهور الأمة | reports | The majority say the Judgment balance has a beam and two pans like worldly balances, displaying exact justice through a familiar object.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p375/r6] حذيفة بن اليمان | reports | Hudhayfa says Gabriel is the custodian of the balances on Judgment Day.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p376/r1] author | holds | The balance should be understood literally; reports say records of good and bad deeds go into its pans, where God creates heaviness or lightness.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p376/r2] author | holds | Weight must occur in a body, since an accident cannot inhere in another accident; it may occur in the records or nearby bodies, and Abu al-Maali favors the records.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p376/r3] author | holds | Unreliable reports about the balance's shape and length need not be repeated; Hasan's report that each person has a separate balance is rejected because the balance is one, though each person has an individual weighing.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p376/r6] author | holds | God's weighing, recording deeds, and making limbs testify despite His knowledge serve to establish proof; the testimony of divine oneness must tip a person's balance.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p376/r7] author | reports | The author asks how sinful believers can have a weighty balance and be judged successful yet still enter Hell.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p377/r1] author | holds | A light balance means the pan of good deeds rises; they lose themselves through destruction and eternal residence in Hell.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p377/r2] author | holds | Bi-mā expresses recompense, like thanking someone for what they did; mā is a verbal noun, and God's signs are proofs, commands, and prohibitions that they wrong by disbelief and denial.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p377/r3] طائفة | reports | One answer is that good deeds are weighed without tawhid first, then tawhid makes the balance heavy when sinful believers are released from Hell.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p377/r4] author | holds | Knowing that a sinner will not remain forever in Hell counts as success, even if disciplinary punishment comes first.
