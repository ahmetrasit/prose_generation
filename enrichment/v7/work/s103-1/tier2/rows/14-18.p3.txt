## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v4p270/r1] author | holds | The parable’s syntax may make the disbelievers’ strange condition the subject and their deeds like ashes its predicate, or leave the predicate implicit and explain it in a new sentence.
[BURSEVI:v4p270/r2] author | holds | The wind’s ‘intensifying’ means rushing away with the ashes; the day is called stormy by metonymy for its strong wind.
[BURSEVI:v4p270/r3] author | holds | At resurrection the disbelievers find no reward or mitigation in their good deeds, just as no trace remains of ash scattered by a gale.
[BURSEVI:v4p270/r4] author | holds | Their compounded error is disbelief, ostentation, and pride in deeds they imagine good; unlike sinful believers, they do not repent because they see no fault in themselves.
[BURSEVI:v4p270/r5] author | holds | Charity, kinship, freeing slaves or captives, helping the distressed, and hospitality are nullified when done without knowledge of God, faith, and seeking His face, like ashes blown away.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r1] سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh treats مثل الذين كفروا as a subject with an omitted predicate, meaning their remarkable description is what is recited here.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r10] author | holds | The deeds may be noble social acts such as kinship, manumission, freeing captives, hospitality, and aid to the distressed; they may also be sacrifices to idols, and the broader reading may include both.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r11] author | holds | The clause that their deeds are ashes answers why they ended in ruin by explaining the likeness under discussion.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r2] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya instead makes مثل الذين كفروا the subject and أعمالهم كرماد its predicate.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r3] الحوفي | reports | Al-Hawfi objects that the predicative clause lacks a link back to the subject and is not identical with it in meaning.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r4] السمين | reports | Al-Samin answers that the clause can itself name what is said of them, so no linking pronoun is needed; this makes مثل mean their description.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r5] الكسائي والفراء | reports | Al-Kisaʾi and al-Farraʾ treat مثل as an inserted element placed before or after the noun it accompanies.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r6] الحوفي | reports | Al-Hawfi also parses مثل as subject, كرماد as predicate, and أعمالهم as an appositive of inclusion.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r7] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari allows أعمالهم to replace مثل الذين كفروا if an omitted مثل أعمالهم is supplied, magnifying the comparison.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r8] الكشف | reports | Al-Kashf calls the proposed apposition total because the likeness of the disbelievers and likeness of their deeds coincide in essence; another analysis calls it inclusion because the relations differ.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p203#2/r9] ابن عيسى | reports | Ibn ʿIsa defines ashes as a body pulverized by burning into dust and notes its plural forms.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p204/r1] author | reports | The cited poetic line shows an adjective phrase describing a day darkened by the sun, supporting an analysis that supplies an omitted noun; al-Alusi calls that analysis weak.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p204/r2] author | rejects | Making عاصف an adjective of الريح by grammatical attraction fails because the words differ in definiteness; the plural reading الرياح makes that account even less plausible.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p204/r3] ابن أبي إسحاق وإبراهيم بن أبي بكر عن الحسن | reports | They read في يوم عاصف with genitive عاصف; Abu Hayyan explains it by omitting يوم's described noun, while another permits direct annexation of noun and adjective.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p204/r4] author | holds | The disbelievers cannot obtain any effect of reward or relief from their worldly deeds on Judgment Day; the alternative supplies an omitted word for reward, but the direct reading is stronger. | mentions 2:264
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p204/r5] عائشة | reports | Aisha asks whether Ibn Judʿan benefits from feeding the poor and keeping kinship ties in pagan times; the Prophet says it does not benefit him because he lacked faith and repentance for Judgment Day.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p204/r6] author | holds | The ashes image depicts deeds rendered futile and dispersed because they lacked belief, knowledge of God, and devotion to Him; it also mocks idolaters' belief that idols intercede.
[ALUSI-FULL:v13p204/r7] author | holds | ذلك refers to the evident misguidance of people who assumed they had achieved something; their error is far from truth and right guidance.

## IBNASHUR-FULL: Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393 AH
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v13p212#2/r1] author | holds | The parable explains why the charitable and hospitable good works of unbelievers bring them no benefit in the afterlife, answering the question raised by their severe punishment.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v13p212#2/r2] author | holds | المثل means an extraordinary condition; the intended comparison is the condition of their deeds, expressed through an elliptical nominal construction.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v13p212#2/r3] author | holds | Their many accumulated deeds resemble a heap of ash scattered by strong wind: a large collection vanishes so completely that it cannot be reassembled.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v13p212#2/r4] author | holds | Describing the day as stormy is a rational metaphor for its wind being stormy, like calling it a rainy day; compare Q 10:22.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v13p212#2/r5] author | holds | The ash image subtly evokes hospitality, one of the unbelievers' best-known virtues: abundant ash from cooking became an idiom for generosity.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v13p213/r1] نافع وأبو جعفر | reports | Nāfiʿ and Abū Jaʿfar read اشتدت به الرياح in the plural; the other readers have singular الريح, with no difference in meaning because the definite noun denotes a genus.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v13p213/r2] author | holds | The statement that they cannot benefit from what they earned explains the parable: their deeds are futile in the afterlife.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v13p213/r3] author | holds | “That is distant misguidance” summarizes their state; distant means the utmost degree of misguidance, as in Q 4:116.

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:14:15-18/r7] author | holds | The violent-wind image likens unbelievers’ deeds to ashes blown away, leaving them no result that can bring happiness; the verse concludes the preceding speech as its outcome.
[TABATABAI:14:6-18/r1] author | holds | The deeds of unbelievers are like ash scattered by a fierce wind, with no trace remaining, as in the image of deeds made scattered dust; no omitted word such as deeds needs to be supplied, and the verse is a conclusion rather than Moses’s continued speech. | mentions 25:23
