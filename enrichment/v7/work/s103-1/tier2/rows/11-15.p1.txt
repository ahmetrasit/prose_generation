[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r2] الزجاج | reports | Reporting al-Mubarrad, كان and تكون can describe states in both past and future; here the condition concerns the future, as in يكن.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r4] author | reports | Zuhayr's line illustrates the conditional use of a past-form verb with present or future meaning: whoever fears death's causes will meet them.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r5] ابن عباس | reports | In ʿAṭāʾ's report, those seeking immediate worldly life reject resurrection, reward, and punishment; this makes the passage about unbelievers.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r2] الفراء | prefers | Al-Farrāʾ adopts the view that a person of the qibla who intends worldly reward is paid fully in this life, without any shortfall.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r7] ابن الأنباري | reports | The verse's wording appears to say that ostentation and worldly intent without seeking afterlife reward invalidate faith at death, since “their deeds were null” includes faith and its branches.

## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:11:15-17/r1] author | holds | This concerns unbelievers who want worldly life but do not believe in resurrection, reward, or punishment; they receive full worldly recompense for good acts.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:11:15-17/r2] author | holds | Examples of such good deeds are feeding the hungry, clothing the unclothed, and supporting an oppressed Muslim; their reward comes as increased wealth in this life.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:11:15-17/r3] author | holds | They are not shortchanged in worldly recompense, but arrive in the afterlife with regret because they have no good deeds there.
