[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p414/r9] author | rejects | Some exegetes assign worldly ease to the first clause and afterlife ease to the second, but the Arabic structure makes the latter pure emphasis.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p415/r1] author | rejects | The rule that a repeated definite noun is identical and an indefinite one different does not establish “two eases”: it concerns an indefinite later made definite by the covenant article, in another sentence.

[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r1] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says ma'a makes relief follow hardship soon, so that the coming ease is treated as if accompanying it for comfort.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r2] النيسابوري | reports | Al-Nisaburi likewise says nearness makes the time of ease seem continuous with hardship and strengthens hope.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r3] الشيخ محمد عبده | reports | Abduh says ma'a secures hope that relief must come, as though already with hardship.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r4] author | prefers | The author agrees that ma'a signals accompaniment but rejects the simile and the generalization to believers: the context chiefly reassures the Prophet, and the ease is joined to hardship.
[BINTSHATI:v1p69/r5] الفراء والزجاج | reports | They argue that repeated definite al-usr refers to one known hardship, while the indefinite yusr presents two distinct eases; a report from the Prophet says one hardship cannot overcome two eases.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r1] أبو حيان | reports | Al-Bahr reports the view that one hardship comes with two eases because hardship is definite and ease indefinite.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r2] الجرجاني | rejects | Al-Jurjani warns that repeating a sentence about a horse with a sword does not prove the horse has two swords.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r3] النيسابوري | reports | Al-Nisaburi explores generic or definite hardship and repeated or new sentences, concluding that a sound hadith would require one hardship and two distinct eases; without it, several readings remain possible.
[BINTSHATI:v1p70/r4] author | rejects | The author finds the competing grammatical scenarios needlessly complex; some commentators also identify the two eases as worldly and afterlife relief or as conquests and afterlife reward.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r1] author | prefers | The second verse confirms the first to strengthen the Prophet's inward certainty in God's gifts, rather than introduce a separate promise.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r2] author | prefers | Al-usr is definite by reference to the Prophet's distress at facing entrenched idolatry, while indefinite yusr leaves the relief open rather than setting limits on it.
[BINTSHATI:v1p71/r3] author | holds | Usr denotes the most severe hardship and struggle, with Arabic usage for intense physical constriction and Qur'anic descriptions of the Day's hardship and other acute trials. | mentions 54:8, 74:9, 25:26, 92:10, 65:6, 65:7, 9:117, 18:73

## ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p363/r1] author | holds | Sūrah Alam Nashraḥ completes the preceding sūrah: 93:6’s sheltering and other favours concerned the period before and just after prophethood, while this sūrah points to blessings after Islam spread in Arabia. | mentions 93:6
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p363/r2] author | holds | The prior sūrah predicts a bright future; this one points to predictions already beginning to materialize and assures the Prophet that hardship yields to success if faced courageously.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p363/r3] author | holds | The discourse proceeds from inner expansion, through the recurring law of hardship with relief, to completion of the mission and disciplined use of its success.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p366/r1] author | holds | These verses teach the Prophet to endure affliction patiently: earlier hardship was followed by relief, and the same divine pattern will continue through the mission.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p366/r3] most commentators | reports | Most commentators take the repeated statement as mere emphasis.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p366/r4] author | rejects | Islahi rejects mere emphatic repetition: hardship and ease coexist and recur in cycles, so each success is followed by further trials requiring continued struggle on the path of truth.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p367/r1] author | holds | Those who remain steadfast through trials receive renewed ease after each difficulty, helping them continue toward their ultimate destination.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p367/r2] author | holds | Trials distinguish the righteous from hypocrites and believers from disbelievers, so reward and punishment follow deeds without grounds for a complaint of injustice.
