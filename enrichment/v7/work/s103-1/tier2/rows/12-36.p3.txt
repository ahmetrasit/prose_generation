## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107#2/r1] author | holds | Their visions matched their professions: one pressed grapes for wine, while the other carried bread for birds to eat.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107#2/r2] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud says the two men had seen nothing and invented dreams to test Joseph's interpretive skill.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107#2/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says they really dreamed upon entering prison: the cupbearer saw three vine branches, pressed their grapes into the king's cup, and served him.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107#2/r4] مجاهد | reports | In Mujahid's account, the baker saw three baskets of varied bread on his head while predatory birds tore from them.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107#2/r5] author | holds | The wording أرىني and their request for its interpretation show that they were describing dreams; otherwise saying I press wine would suffice.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107#2/r6] author | reports | He offers three explanations for pressing wine: omitted grapes, naming something by its eventual product, or calling grapes wine in Omani usage.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107#2/r7] أبو صالح | reports | Abu Salih says people in Oman call grapes khamr; al-Dahhak adds that the Quran came in the tongues of all Arab groups.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107/r5] author | holds | The text omits that Joseph was imprisoned; the next clause implies it. The two young men were the king's food and drink servants, jailed over a suspected poisoning plot.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107/r6] author | reports | He suggests the youths may have told Joseph of their sadness and dreams, or may have seen him demonstrate knowledge of dream interpretation before asking him.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p107/r7] author | holds | Joseph could infer that they served the king from their use of rabb for their master in the later statements about serving wine and asking to be remembered. | mentions 12:41, 12:42
[RAZI-FULL:v18p108/r1] author | holds | Taʾwīl is what a thing ultimately returns to, the endpoint toward which the matter leads.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r1] author | holds | Joseph explained that pressing wine meant pressing grapes; Ibn Masud's reading makes the word grapes explicit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r10] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas recounts that the men awoke troubled, told Joseph about their disturbing dreams, and asked him to interpret them; the account indicates actual dreams.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r11] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says Joseph's kindness meant tending sick prisoners, making room for cramped inmates, and gathering help for the needy.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r12] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra says محسنين can mean people who are accomplished in knowledge.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r13] ابن إسحق | reports | Ibn Ishaq takes من المحسنين to mean that Joseph had treated them well if he interpreted their dreams.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r14] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says خمر can mean grapes in the dialect of Oman; Ibn Masud reads عنبا, and al-Asma'i reports a Bedouin calling grapes khamr.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r15] author | reports | Another grammatical explanation supplies an omitted إضافة, meaning grapes for wine; the forms خمرة، خمر، خمور follow the pattern of date/date-palm plural.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r2] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud says the two men asked Joseph to test him despite having seen no dreams.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r3] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari says the men first asked Joseph about his knowledge; after he offered to interpret dreams, they asked about their visions.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r4] ابن عباس ومجاهد | reports | Ibn Abbas and Mujahid say the dreams were true visions, which is why their interpretations came true.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r5] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A Sahih report from Abu Hurayra says the most truthful of you in speech have the truest dreams.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r6] ابن مسعود والسدي | reports | Another view says they fabricated dreams to test Joseph; Ibn Masud and al-Suddi hold this.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r7] أبو مجلز | reports | Abu Mijlaz says the man who was crucified lied about his dream while the other told the truth.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r8] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | From Ibn Abbas, al-Tirmidhi transmits that a person who lies about a dream will be made to tie two barley grains together on Judgment Day, which he cannot do; al-Tirmidhi grades it hasan sahih.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36-37/r9] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report from Ali says a dream liar will be tasked on Judgment Day to tie a grain; al-Tirmidhi calls it hasan.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r1] author | holds | Fityan is the dual of fata, a weak-y verb; the form al-futu is irregular.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r10] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari says the man who dreamed of pressing grapes was Nabu; al-Suhayli mentions the other's name but says he did not retain it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r11] الماوردي | reports | Al-Mawardi says they are called fityan because they were slaves, since a slave is called fata whether young or old.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r12] القشيري | reports | Al-Qushayri says fata may be a customary word for slave, while another possibility is that it means a servant, whether or not enslaved.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r13] author | holds | The two men may have entered prison before, after, or at the same time as Joseph; the verse only requires that they entered the same room with him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r2] وهب وغيره | reports | Wahb and others say Joseph was paraded in chains on a donkey as punishment for disobeying his mistress; he said that was easier than Hell's punishment.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r3] author | reports | When Joseph reached the prison, its hopeless inmates heard him urge patience and promise of reward; they praised his speech and asked who he was.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says Joseph comforted the grieving, visited and treated the sick and wounded, prayed and wept until the prison seemed to weep, and became beloved to inmates and jailer.

[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r7] قيل | reports | An alternative says the baker put poison in food while the cupbearer warned the king not to eat; the baker warned him not to drink, but a test showed the food was poisoned. They spent a year in prison with Joseph.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r8] الثعلبي عن كعب | reports | Al-Tha'labi, citing Ka'b, names the cupbearer Manja and the baker Majlath.
[QURTUBI-FULL:12:36/r9] النقاش | reports | Al-Naqqash gives different names for the two men: Sharham and Sarham.
