## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p211#2/r1] author | holds | Although the Prophet is already upright, the command means to continue and remain steadfast, like telling someone already walking or eating to keep doing so; the address includes him and the Companions who repented from disbelief, and by implication the whole community.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p211#2/r2] author | reports | A scholar reportedly saw the Prophet in a dream and asked what in Hud made him say it and its companion suras had made him gray; the Prophet answered, 'Be steadfast as you were commanded.' Ibn Atiyya says the well-known explanation is his fear that earlier peoples' fates might befall this community.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p211#2/r3] author | holds | أمرت is an honorific plural form of address; ومن is coordinated with the pronoun in فاستقم, permitted without repeating it because كما أمرت makes the phrase long.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p211#2/r4] author | holds | ولا تطغوا means do not exceed God's limits; طغيان is crossing the limit, as with the flood and Pharaoh, or specifically letting blessings lead to transgression. | mentions 69:11, 20:24, 20:43, 79:17
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p211#2/r5] الجمهور | reports | The majority read تعملون with second-person tāʾ; Hasan and al-A'mash read يعملون with third-person yāʾ, and readers vary between open and rounded kaf in تركنوا, with the latter a dialect form meaning to settle or be content with something.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r1] أبو علي | reports | Abu Ali discusses a transmitted reading with tanwin on لما and compares it with the phrase أكلا لما, where the verbal noun describes the action.
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r10] علي ابن أبي الطيب | reports | Ali ibn Abi al-Tayyib supplies a fifth analysis with an omitted phrase, وإن كلا لما عملوا ليوفينهم ربك أعمالهم, as the poem illustrates ellipsis.
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r2] الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisa'i says he does not know why لما is doubled; Abu Ali accepts that this difficulty is real.
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r3] أبو علي | reports | Abu Ali says the doubled form could mean ما كل إلا ليوفينهم if إن were light and كل nominative, parallel to the cited expression; then لما could more readily be an adjective of indefinite كل. | mentions 43:35
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r4] author | holds | The syntactic analysis would require كل to be annexed to an indefinite noun so لما can modify it; a definite annexation would block that adjective reading, and no clause element governs it as a circumstantial phrase.
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra' takes لما as لمن ما, with one of the three mims deleted, and cites a poetic line.
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r6] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj takes لما as إلا, but al-Farra' says that use is restricted to oaths, as Abu Ali argues.
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r7] المازني | reports | Al-Mazini says لما is a lightened form that was doubled for emphasis; al-Zajjaj rejects this because Arabic permits weakening a doubled form only under poetic necessity, not doubling a light form.
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r8] author | reports | A fourth proposal derives لما from gathering, builds it on فعلى without declension like تترى, and reads it as all together; al-Zuhri's tanwin reading supports this.
[TABRISI:11:109-112/r9] ابن جني | reports | Ibn Jinni parses لما as a comprehensive fulfillment of all their deeds, like using قياما for emphatic emphasis.
[TABRISI:11:112#2/r1] author | holds | Muhammad is commanded to remain steadfast in preaching and warning, holding to obedience and calling people to practice it as the Qur'an commands; steadfastness means doing what is commanded and avoiding what is prohibited.
[TABRISI:11:112#2/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas takes those who repented with Muhammad as people who repented from associating partners with God and must also remain steadfast.
[TABRISI:11:112#2/r3] author | reports | Another view applies it to all believers who turn back to God and His Prophet; a further parsing has Muhammad maintain delivery while they maintain acceptance.
