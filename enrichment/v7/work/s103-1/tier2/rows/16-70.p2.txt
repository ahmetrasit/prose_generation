## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:16:70/r1] author | holds | In لكيلا, لام may mark purpose and كي is a source particle governing the subjunctive, with the phrase attached to يرد.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:70/r2] الحوفي | reports | Al-Hawfi calls it لام كي and كي emphatic; the author objects that كي is not causative here and its operation differs.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:70/r3] author | reports | Alternatively, لام marks an eventual result.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:70/r4] author | prefers | The Basran analysis, preferred by the author, assigns شيئا to the nearer علم in the competition between يعلم and علم.
[SAMIN-DURR:16:70/r5] author | rejects | The Kufan attachment to يعلم is rejected because علم would then need a missing object pronoun: علم إياه شيئا.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p289/r1] author | holds | Human life passes through infancy and growth, youth, maturity with declining strength, and old age with obvious weakness; some die in strength while others reach decrepitude.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p289/r2] author | holds | The frailest age returns a person to childlike weakness and can bring loss of knowledge, with no medicine or remedy able to avert it; people should reflect before it arrives.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p289/r3] author | holds | Removing the preposition before “anything” makes ignorance encompass the entire period after knowledge, continuing until death.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p289/r4] author | holds | God’s knowledge and power encompass all things: whatever He wills occurs, and His power can prevent what He knows will not occur.

[BURSEVI:v5p42#3/r7] author | holds | Though decrepit age is hard, it is also a time for forgiveness and higher rank; a report says good deeds are confirmed at eighty and sins erased.
[BURSEVI:v5p42/r4] author | holds | God takes people at different ages, and neither young nor old can advance or delay the appointed term.
[BURSEVI:v5p42/r5] author | holds | Some are returned to the most decrepit age, where old age and senility weaken body, reason, and understanding; there is no fixed threshold.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p187#2/r1] author | holds | After describing water, plants, livestock, and bees, the passage turns to the stages of human life from birth to death.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p187#2/r10] author | prefers | Other readings are that the elderly person learns nothing new, or loses the ability to reason; al-Alusi prefers extreme forgetfulness, with العلم meaning recognition in the main reading.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p187#2/r11] author | holds | شيئا may be a cognate accusative or direct object; the syntax may involve shared governance between يعلم and علم, with an omitted object understood generally.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p187#2/r12] author | holds | God knows everything, including the wisdom behind creation, death, and return to senility, and has power over all things.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p187#2/r2] author | holds | God creates people and takes them at different terms according to wise decree; the phrase about some being returned to senility is evidence for an omitted clause about those who die earlier.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p187#2/r3] author | holds | Arذل العمر is the lowest, most feeble period of senility, when strength and senses fail and the person resembles a child; the phrasing figuratively depicts reversal, as in 36:68. | mentions 36:68

[ALUSI-FULL:v14p187#2/r8] author | holds | The lām in لكي signals outcome, though originally it marks purpose; the clause means that a person becomes so forgetful that newly learned information soon vanishes.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p187#2/r9] الحوفي | reports | Al-Hawfi calls the lām a purpose marker reinforced by كي; al-Alusi rejects this analysis.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p188/r1] author | holds | God knows the appointed lifespans and can kill a vigorous young person while leaving a decrepit elder alive, showing that lifespan differences reflect wise divine determination rather than nature alone.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p188/r2] author | reports | Another explanation contrasts human decline in knowledge and power during old age with God's complete knowledge and power, which remain unchanged over time.
[ALUSI-FULL:v14p188/r3] author | holds | The nominal sentence indicates continued permanence; the attributes are ordered with knowledge first because loss of knowledge has just been mentioned, though their relation to things also explains the ordering.
