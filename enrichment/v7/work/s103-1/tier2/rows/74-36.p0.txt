## FARRA: al-Farrāʾ, d. 207 AH
[FARRA:v3p203/r1] أبي | reports | Ubayy's reading has the nominative singular nadhīrun lil-bashar, “a warner to humankind”; both readings are sound.
[FARRA:v3p204/r7] بعض النحويين | reports | Some grammarians connect accusative nadhīran to the opening command, “rise as a warner to humankind.”
[FARRA:v3p204/r8] author | rejects | Al-Farrāʾ rejects that distant connection because much speech intervenes and Ubayy's nominative reading rules it out.
[FARRA:v3p204/r9] author | prefers | He instead connects nadhīran with “one of the great calamities,” treating it as an indefinite accusative separated from a definite noun, or as a verbal noun of warning.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v23p445/r1] author | holds | Al-Ṭabarī first glosses “a warning to humankind” as Hell warning human beings; on this reading nadhīran is an accusative detached modifier of “one of the greatest.”
[TAB-FULL:v23p445/r2] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says people were warned of no greater or more dreadful calamity than this.
[TAB-FULL:v23p445/r3] أبو رزين | reports | Abū Razīn takes God as the subject: God says He warns people of Hell so they should fear it.
[TAB-FULL:v23p445/r4] author | reports | Another grammatical interpretation makes nadhīran an accusative verbal noun, “a warning,” outside the prior clause, or says the Fire was made one of the great matters as a warning.
[TAB-FULL:v23p445/r5] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says “humankind” means the children of Adam and identifies Muhammad as the warner, with nadhīran construed as a circumstantial accusative after “arise.”

## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH
[ZAJJAJ:v5p248#2/r5] author | reports | Nadhīran may instead be masculine by analogy with a woman called ṭāhir or ṭāliq, meaning one possessing purity or divorce, hence “possessing warning.”
[ZAJJAJ:v5p248#2/r6] author | reports | Alternatively, nadhīran can attach to the opening of the sūra: “arise as a warner to humankind.”

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v5p72/r1] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says no warning is more terrifying than Hell, or words to that effect.
[NAHHAS:v5p72/r2] أبو رزين | reports | Abū Razīn says God Himself is a warner to mankind.
[NAHHAS:v5p72/r3] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says Muhammad is the warner to mankind.
[NAHHAS:v5p72/r4] author | holds | Abu Jaʿfar records seven parses of nadhīran: circumstantial from anā or ihdā al-kubar, from a Fire-as-warning reading, implied clauses for God's warning, an imperative for Muhammad, verbal noun indhār, or 'I mean a warner.'
[NAHHAS:v5p72/r5] author | holds | If nadhīr describes the Fire, its yāʾ is omitted as a relational adjective.

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v10p76/r1] الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil says nadhir is a verbal noun like nakir, which allows it to describe a feminine subject; another construction makes it God's warning to humanity.
[THALABI:v10p76/r2] أبو رزين | reports | Abu Razin explains the warning as the speaker saying he warns people about the Fire and urges them to avoid it.
[THALABI:v10p76/r3] ابن زيد | reports | Another interpretation makes the warning a description of Muhammad, meaning he is commanded to rise and warn humanity.
[THALABI:v10p76/r4] إبراهيم عن أبي غيلة | reports | Ibrahim transmits a nominative reading nadhirun li-l-bashar, with an implicit huwa.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v22p448/r1] عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | Nadhiran means an imperative: rise as a warner to mankind; al-Kisaʾi and al-Zajjaj cite this as an accusative parsing.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v22p448/r2] الفراء | reports | He rejects the imperative explanation because much intervenes; nadhiran is an accusative detached from the definite phrase ihda al-kubar.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v22p448/r3] الفراء | reports | Alternatively, nadhir can mean warning, so the phrase means warn mankind with a warning; the description of Hell supports this. | mentions 74:28, 74:29
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v22p448/r4] الزجاج | reports | He treats nadhiran as a circumstantial expression, masculine in form because it describes punishment or means possessing a warning, like a woman described as pure or divorced.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v22p448/r5] أبو علي الفارسي | reports | He gives two possible circumstantial antecedents: the command to rise near the beginning of the surah, or the phrase one of the great calamities.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v22p448/r6] أبو علي الفارسي | reports | On either parse, he takes nadhiran as a verbal noun used circumstantially, which can modify a feminine singular or feminine plural, as well as a singular.
