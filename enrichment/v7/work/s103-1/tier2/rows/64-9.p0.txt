## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH

[ZAJJAJ:v5p180/r3] author | holds | The Day of Mutual Loss is when the people of Paradise gain over the people of the Fire and higher-ranked residents of Paradise gain over those below them.

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v4p443/r4] author | holds | Yawm is governed by tunabbaʾūn; the pronoun in yajmaʿukum refers to God, not the Day, since a day cannot be annexed to a verb whose pronoun points back to it.
[NAHHAS:v4p443/r5] author | holds | Dhālika yawmu al-tagābun is a subject-predicate clause; outside the Qur'anic reading, yawma may be an accusative temporal adverb.
[NAHHAS:v4p443/r6] author | holds | The clause 'whoever believes and does good' is coordinated; yaʿmal may instead be raised as a circumstantial clause.
[NAHHAS:v4p443/r7] author | holds | God erases sins and admits believers to gardens; khālidīna is circumstantial, abadan temporal, and al-fawz means salvation.

## FARISI-HUJJA: Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, d. 377 AH
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p295/r1] نافع وابن عامر | reports | Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir read both verbs with first-person plural nūn, speaking of God's forgiveness and admission into the gardens.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p295/r2] الباقون | reports | The other readers use third-person يَدْخُلْهُ, returning to the explicit preceding subject; the nūn shift is supported by a similar shift in the account of Moses. | mentions 4:13, 48:17, 17:1, 17:2

## SULAMI: al-Sulamī, d. 412 AH
[SULAMI:v2p330#3/r1] ابن عطاء | reports | People of obedience outstrip people of disobedience; among people of truth, degrees of loss reflect degrees of light at vision and manifestation, with heart's sight exceeding the eye's.
[SULAMI:v2p330#3/r2] ابن عطاء | reports | Heart's vision is greater than sight because the eye is limited in contemplation; when the Real appears, each person is silenced from speech and disputation.

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v9p328#2/r1] author | reports | Medinan and Syrian readers use first-person plural verbs for forgiving sins and admitting to Paradise; the other readers use third-person singular verbs.
[THALABI:v9p328#2/r2] author | holds | A calamity occurs by God's will and decree; faith guides the heart to certainty that what struck could not have missed and what missed could not have struck, as Ibn Abbas says.
[THALABI:v9p328#2/r3] علقمة | reports | Alqama says the guided believer suffers a calamity, recognizes it as from God, and accepts it with contentment and submission.
[THALABI:v9p328#2/r4] أبو بكر الوراق | reports | Abu Bakr al-Warraq says faith during ease guides to gratitude, while faith during hardship guides to acceptance and patience.
[THALABI:v9p328#2/r5] أبو عثمان الجيري | reports | Abu Uthman al-Jiri says sound faith guides the heart to following the Prophetic practice.
[THALABI:v9p328#2/r6] author | reports | The common reading has ya and fatḥa in 'yahdi qalbahu'; al-Sulami reads it passive with ḍamma, Talha reads first-person plural, and Ikrima reads 'yahda' as becoming still.
[THALABI:v9p328#2/r7] مالك بن دينار | reports | Malik ibn Dinar reads 'yahda qalbuhu' with a long alif replacing the hamza.
[THALABI:v9p328/r1] author | holds | The Day of Mutual Loss is named with the verbal form of loss: the day when people forfeit their share or advantage.
[THALABI:v9p328/r2] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Abu Hurayra reports that every believer shown the place in Hell he would have had by wrongdoing grows more grateful, while each person in Hell shown his possible place in Paradise grows more regretful.
[THALABI:v9p328/r3] المفسرون | reports | The commentators say unbelievers lose their homes and families in Paradise through unbelief, while believers regret their shortcomings and wasted days.

## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v10p6954/r1] يعقوب | reports | Yaqub reads najmaʿukum with first-person plural, aligning it with the first-person verb anzalnā.
[JISHUMI:v10p6954/r2] author | reports | The remaining readers use the third-person verb yajmaʿukum, referring to God's name.
[JISHUMI:v10p6954/r3] أبو جعفر ونافع وابن عامر | reports | They read nukaffir and nudkhil with first-person plural; the remaining readers use third-person forms.
[JISHUMI:v10p6954/r4] author | holds | Ghubn is loss or shortchanging; taghābun is mutual loss, originally used of buying and selling when something is taken below its value.
[JISHUMI:v10p6954/r5] author | holds | One who trades the afterlife for the world is cheated by taking something trivial in exchange for something precious; the people of Hell are cheated by Paradise's people.
[JISHUMI:v10p6954/r6] أبو مسلم | reports | An alternative etymology connects ghubn with concealing something, as in the hidden recesses called maghābin.
[JISHUMI:v10p6954/r7] author | reports | Yawm may be accusative as a time expression, or as an exhortation to remember the day and prepare for it.
[JISHUMI:v10p6955/r10] author | reports | A further view is that people in Paradise outdo one another in the increase of their ranks.
[JISHUMI:v10p6955/r11] author | holds | God erases the sins of believers who do good, admits them to gardens with rivers, and grants them eternal residence; this is great success, attaining what is sought and escaping harm.
[JISHUMI:v10p6955/r6] author | reports | God may gather people from their graves toward Judgment, or gather them on Judgment Day because all creatures assemble there.
[JISHUMI:v10p6955/r7] قتادة ومجاهد | reports | One interpretation says the people of Paradise cheat the people of Hell by winning the good outcome.
[JISHUMI:v10p6955/r8] author | reports | Another interpretation says the people of Hell lose by choosing transient worldly life and thereby forfeiting the far more valuable reward.
