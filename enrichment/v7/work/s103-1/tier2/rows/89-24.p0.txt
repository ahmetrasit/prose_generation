## TAB: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH

## RAZI: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI:89:24/r1] author | holds | One reading has the speaker wish that, during worldly life, he had prepared deeds for the everlasting afterlife; it is called “my life” because true life is there. | mentions 29:64
[RAZI:89:24/r2] author | holds | A second reading says Hell’s people are effectively lifeless despite not dying; the speaker wishes he had done deeds that would save him from Hell and make him among the living. | mentions 14:17, 20:74, 87:11, 87:13
[RAZI:89:24/r3] author | holds | A third reading takes the phrase as wishing he had acted during his lifetime in the world.
[RAZI:89:24/r4] المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazilites infer that people had genuine choice and will and were not prevented from obedience or driven into sin.
[RAZI:89:24/r5] author | rejects | The author replies that if a person’s intention depends on another intention, the chain regresses infinitely; if it depends on God’s intention, Muʿtazilism is refuted.

[ALUSI-FULL:v30p129/r2] author | reports | One reading is that he wished he had prepared good works for his afterlife; another takes the lām as “during my worldly life,” wishing he had acted then to benefit today. The view that he wished for life is dismissed.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p129/r3] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari treats the wish as evidence that a person acts independently; Alusi rejects that inference because the words show perceived capacity, not whether God creates the act or its acquired power.
