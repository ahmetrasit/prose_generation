## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r1] author | reports | Meccan leaders, or alternatively Uyayna b. Hisn and his party, asked Muhammad to dismiss poor believers such as Ammar, Suhayb, Salman, Ibn Masud, and Bilal; Ibn Atiyya prefers the Meccan leaders because the sura is Meccan.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r10] قتادة | reports | The morning-and-evening invocation specifically means the dawn and afternoon prayers.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r2] author | reports | The leaders complained that the poor companions' clothes smelled and demanded their removal before they would sit with the Prophet.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r3] author | reports | Reports say the Prophet sat with the poor believers and praised God for commanding him to be patient with them, welcoming those for whom his Lord reproached him.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r4] سلمان | reports | Salman reports that Uyayna, al-Aqra, and their companions, described as those whose hearts were reconciled, made the same demand, prompting the verse.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r5] author | reports | The report about the reconciled leaders could make the verse Medinan, but Ibn Atiyya thinks it was likely Meccan and a reply to Quraysh.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r6] author | holds | Here ṣbir means to hold or restrain oneself; the related term describes an animal held as a target, which the Prophet forbade.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r7] الجمهور | reports | The majority read al-ghadati; Ibn Amir and several early readers read al-ghudwati, despite the latter's grammatical weakness because ghudwa is a known proper noun.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r8] author | holds | The alif-lam in al-ghudwa can be explained as making the time word indefinite or generic, as in al-fina, so the reading has a possible defense.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#2/r9] ابن عمر ومجاهد وإبراهيم | reports | The morning and evening invocation refers to the five daily prayers.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r1] author | holds | The verse also includes people who invoke God outside prayer and those who gather to study religious knowledge.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r10] ابن جني | reports | Ibn Jinni explains the reading as meaning someone whom we supposed to be heedless of Us.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r11] author | reports | Farata may mean neglect and waste of duty, or excess and extravagance; exegetes used both senses, while Khabbab, Dawud, and Ibn Zayd gave related glosses.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r2] عبد الله بن عمر | reports | A report from the Prophet says remembering God morning and evening is better than breaking swords in God's path or freely giving wealth.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r3] أبو عبد الرحمن | reports | Abu Abd al-Rahman read al-ghudu without the final h; Ibn Abi Abla read the morning and evening terms as plurals.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r4] author | holds | Not turning one's eyes from the believers means not looking past them toward worldly people and the clothing of disbelievers.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r5] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan's first reading uses a form that makes the Prophet the one who must not turn his eyes away; another variant has a still ʿayn.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r6] خباب | reports | Khabbab identifies the heedless person as Uyayna b. Hisn and al-Aqra; another interpretation takes the description generally.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r7] author | prefers | Ibn Atiyya says the initial target is Quraysh disbelievers, since the verse is Meccan, while the wording can apply to anyone of this character.

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p512#3/r9] عمرو بن عبيد | reports | A variant attributed by al-Dani to Amr b. Ubayd understands the phrase as someone who neglected God's remembrance and left it.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:28-29#2/r2] author | reports | The verse was revealed about poor believers Salman, Abu Dharr, Suhayb, ʿAmmar, Khabbab, and others, after wealthy leaders led by ʿUyayna and al-Aqraʿ asked the Prophet to move them because of their smell and wool clothing.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#2/r3] author | reports | After the command, the Prophet searched for the poor companions, found them remembering God at the back of the mosque, and thanked God for commanding him to stay with people who would share his life and death.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#2/r4] author | holds | ‘Keep yourself with them’ means restrain yourself alongside believers who persist in prayer and supplication morning and evening, beginning and ending their days in worship.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#2/r5] author | reports | Seeking God's face means seeking His exaltation and nearness, without ostentation or desire for reputation.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#2/r6] author | holds | The Prophet was eager for leading polytheists to believe so their followers might follow; he did not desire worldly wealth, but was reproached for sometimes accommodating leaders and told to turn toward poor believers.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#2/r7] author | reports | One reading of God making a heart heedless means He exposed it to heedlessness; the author compares it with God turning aside those who first deviated. | mentions 61:5
[TABRISI:18:28-29#3/r1] author | reports | Other readings explain أغفلنا as describing a heart as heedless, finding it heedless, leaving it unmarked with the sign of believers, or abandoning it to its own course after withdrawing support; the last is attributed to al-Hasan.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#3/r2] author | reports | Al-Muqatil and al-Jubbaʾi gloss فرطا as excess or extravagance; al-Akhfash says transgression beyond the limit, while Mujahid and al-Suddi say loss and ruin.
[TABRISI:18:28-29#3/r3] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj explains the person who neglects the beginning of an affair as one who wastes and destroys it; here the person abandons faith and proof to follow desire.
[TABRISI:18:28-29/r1] author | reports | Ibn ʿĀmir reads the morning term as بالغدوة, while the other readers have بالغداة; al-Hasan has a noncanonical ولا تعد عينيك and ʿAmr ibn Faʾid reads من أغفلنا قلبه.
[TABRISI:18:28-29/r2] أبو علي | reports | Abu Ali says غدوة is inherently definite like a proper name and normally takes no article, although a dialect may use it as a generic term and admit the article.
[TABRISI:18:28-29/r3] author | reports | The anomalous reading ولا تعد عينيك is connected to turning the eyes away or passing beyond, as in a cited poetic use of تعدي.
[TABRISI:18:28-29/r4] author | reports | On the reading من أغفلنا قلبه, the phrase may mean one whom God found heedless, supported by the cited use of أغفلت for finding someone in a state.
