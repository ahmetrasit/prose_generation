## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p111/r1] author | holds | This is another parable showing the world's insignificance and short duration, aimed at wealthy polytheists who boast of riches and supporters over poor believers.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p111/r2] author | holds | Rain makes the earth's plants grow, sway, and look beautiful, as another verse says that the earth stirs and swells after rain. | mentions 22:5
[RAZI-FULL:v21p111/r3] author | holds | After a time the vegetation dries into hashim, broken and crumbled plant matter, then winds scatter it in every direction.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p111/r4] author | reports | An unnamed poem uses هشّم for crushing bread, illustrating the sense of breaking or crumbling.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p111/r5] author | holds | God's power appears in bringing the plant into being, nurturing it, and then abolishing it; worldly life likewise grows from beauty to decline and extinction.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p111/r6] author | holds | Because worldly beauty inevitably declines into destruction, a rational person should not take delight in it.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p111/r7] author | reports | One grammatical explanation of باء in فاختلط به نبات الأرض is that kinds of plants mix with one another because rain strengthens and entangles them.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p111/r8] author | reports | A second explanation is that water mixes with plants and plants with water until they are saturated and lush; either mingled thing may take the other's description.

[QURTUBI-FULL:18:45#2/r1] author | holds | Hashīm is dry, broken, crumbling vegetation after water stops; the root also describes dry wood, a generous person, a weak body, showing affection, milking a camel, and crumbling bread.

[QURTUBI-FULL:18:45#2/r6] سيبويه والفراء | reports | A line cited by Sibawayh and al-Farra uses the verb for wind blowing a bird off course, illustrating the sense of scattering.

[QURTUBI-FULL:18:45/r2] author | reports | The plant may be said to mix through the water until flourishing, or to mix with one another after rain, since rain makes vegetation abundant.

[QURTUBI-FULL:18:45/r5] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A man asking to be among the successful is told to leave worldly life and take from it like still water: little suffices and much intoxicates.
