## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p339/r1] author | holds | Spending for God's pleasure may mean supporting fighters who defend His religion or helping Muslims who obey Him.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p339/r2] الشعبي | reports | Al-Shabi, Ibn Zayd, and al-Suddi interpret inner steadfastness as strong conviction and support for the religion.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p339/r3] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan and Mujahid understand inner steadfastness as carefully choosing where to place one's alms.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p339/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas and Qatada understand it as seeking reward for themselves with God.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p339/r5] بعض المتكلمين | reports | Some theologians interpret it as preparing oneself to remain steadfast in obedience to God.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p339/r6] author | reports | A rabwa is an elevated place, perhaps level in its elevation, or any ground raised above a watercourse, according to al-Yazidi.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p339/r7] اليزيدي | reports | Al-Yazidi defines a rabwa as any land raised above the channel through which water runs.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p339/r8] author | reports | Wabil can mean heavy rain or abundant rain; Adi ibn Zayd's verse uses it for abundant downpour.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p340/r1] author | holds | A garden is placed on a rise because its growth is finer and its yield greater; al-Asha's verse illustrates a lush garden drenched by abundant rain.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p340/r2] author | holds | Akl, with a damma on the first letter, means food because it is meant to be eaten.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p340/r3] الجمهور | reports | Most interpreters take du'fayn as twofold: the thing plus an equal amount added; another view defines a double as two times the thing.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p340/r4] author | holds | Tall means dew, a lesser kind of rain; dew-grown crops are weaker and yield less than rain-grown crops, yet even small charity sustains and benefits.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p340/r5] author | holds | The parable compares abundant charity to a rain-fed crop and modest charity to a dew-fed crop: do not abandon small charity when unable to give much.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p415/r1] author | holds | This is a parable for those who spend seeking God's approval without reminding recipients of the gift or hurting them; the syntax omits “spending,” as in “God's horses, ride.”
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p415/r2] الزجاج | reports | He reads تثبيتا as spending with certainty that God will reward it; the sincere are sure of that recompense, unlike hypocrites who give for show without expecting reward.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p415/r3] السدي وأبو صالح وابن زيد | reports | They interpret تثبيتا as certainty.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p415/r4] الشعبي والكلبي | reports | They interpret it as affirmation or sincere belief that what they spend benefits them more than what they keep.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p415/r5] عطاء ومجاهد | reports | They take تثبيتا as deliberation: carefully selecting a pious, upright recipient for charity.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p415/r6] الحسن | reports | He says a person considering charity should test the motive: proceed if it is for God, but hold back if doubt is mixed into it.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p415/r7] author | holds | The deliberation sense works because they steady themselves in seeking recipients and directing funds properly; Arabic similarly says one who restrains himself is restrained.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p416/r1] author | holds | Rabwa means elevated ground and has several dialectal forms; al-Akhfash prefers the ḍamma form because its plural is usually الربى.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p416/r2] الأخفش | prefers | He prefers ربوة with ḍamma; it comes from rising or increasing, like الرابية, and is related to swelling breath and financial الربا.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p416/r3] author | holds | An orchard on high ground yields more and grows greener when it has enough water; the Aʿsha's poem names the fertile gardens of al-Ḥazn as an example.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p418/r1] author | holds | Wābil is the strongest rain; akl means edible food or fruit, as in 14:25, and lexically can mean a person's worldly share, with variant vowel and plural forms. | mentions 14:25
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p418/r2] المفسرون | reports | They take the crop in this verse to mean fruit.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p418/r3] ابن عباس في رواية عطاء | reports | The orchard produces in one year twice the crop another orchard produces in two years.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p418/r4] author | holds | Ṭall is a gentle rain with small drops; the source gives related verb and adjective forms and a customary prayer for abundant rain.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p418/r5] الفراء | reports | The conditional “if no heavy rain reaches it, then dew” has an omitted “if it receives dew,” as in the example about freeing two or one slave.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v4p418/r6] author | holds | Whether the orchard receives heavy rain or dew, its yield remains abundant; likewise God multiplies the sincere believer's reward whether the charity is small or great.

## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:265/r1] author | holds | The sincere spender seeks God's pleasure and has inward conviction and belief in the reward, unlike a hypocrite.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:265/r2] author | holds | A garden on elevated ground yields more than one on low ground, and heavy rain makes it produce double its usual crop.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:265/r3] author | holds | Even light rain leaves the elevated garden fruitful and blessed; likewise God multiplies a believer's reward whether the gift is small or large.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:265/r4] author | holds | A heavy downpour produces a crop twice that of other gardens' two-year yield.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:265/r5] author | holds | The garden simile leads into the example of the ostentatious spender and the person who neglects obedience until death.
