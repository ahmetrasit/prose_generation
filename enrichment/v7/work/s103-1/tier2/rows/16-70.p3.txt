## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:16:65-77#2/r1] author | holds | Differences among created things make power seem easier or harder according to causes and conditions, but God’s power is identical with His necessary essence and cannot depend on any external condition or absence of impediment.
[TABATABAI:16:65-77#2/r2] author | holds | God’s power is unlimited and relates equally to every thing; all existents depend on Him, and creating one thing with all its relations amounts to creating the whole world by one power, with no coequal cause outside it.
[TABATABAI:16:65-77#2/r3] author | holds | This account makes resurrection the reappearance of what was hidden, affirms equal divine power over all things, and shows that things are ultimately interconnected under one power; an ordinary causal view also allows God to act through intermediate causes.
[TABATABAI:16:65-77#3/r1] علي عليه السلام | reports | A report from Ali says the worst age is seventy-five; a report from Anas quoting the Prophet gives one hundred, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the reports do not conflict with his explanation.
[TABATABAI:16:69-70/r6] author | holds | The worst stage of life is senility, when perception and memory decline; its onset varies by temperament and usually begins around age seventy-five.
[TABATABAI:16:69-70/r7] author | holds | People are created, die at ordinary ages, and some return to senility and forget what they knew; this shows people do not control their life, death, or knowledge, which depend on God’s knowledge and power.
