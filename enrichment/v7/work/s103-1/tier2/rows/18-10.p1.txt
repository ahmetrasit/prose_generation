## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p537/r1] author | holds | The opening idh cannot attach to the preceding question because the youths' flight was long before the Prophet; it attaches to an omitted command, remember when they fled.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p537/r2] author | holds | The youths went to the cave and made it their shelter; Ibn Abbas says they fled there.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p537/r3] ابن عباس | reports | He glosses their going to the cave as fleeing to it.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p537/r4] author | holds | After fleeing those pursuing them, the youths prayed for mercy, understood here as forgiveness and provision.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p537/r5] ابن عباس | reports | They ask for mercy from God alone so that it will make them independent of everyone else.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p538/r1] author | holds | Hayyiʾ means set right or prepare; rushd and its related forms are the opposite of error.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p538/r2] أبو إسحاق | reports | He explains the prayer as asking for guidance to what brings one near to God and pleases Him; Ibn Abbas says to direct their deeds toward God's love.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p538/r3] أهل المعاني | reports | They parse the request as having an omitted noun, a thing of right guidance; further omission yields the sense, prepare what will enable us to attain guidance.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p145/r1] محمد بن إسحاق بن يسار | reports | Ibn Isḥāq recounts that Christian society fell into grave error and idolatry under King Daqyānūs, who persecuted believers; eight elite youths fled, prayed for rescue, were captured, and refused the king's demand to worship idols, with Miksalmina speaking for them.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p155/r1] author | holds | Awā means they went to the cave and made it their dwelling; the cave was in Mount Banjulus and was called Khayram.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p155/r2] author | holds | The requested mercy means guidance in religion, or, by another view, provision.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p155/r3] author | holds | Hayyiʾ means make easy; rushd is what seeks God's approval and what brings right guidance.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p155/r4] ابن عباس | reports | He interprets rushd as a safe way out of the cave.

## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p659/r7] author | holds | The requested mercy is from God's stores: forgiveness, provision, and safety from enemies; preparing their affair means guiding them as they leave unbelievers or setting their whole affair right.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p498#2/r1] author | reports | Reports describe the youths as sons of nobles in the city of the unbelieving king Daqiyus, also called Daqliyus or Daqinus; they were Romans and followers of Jesus, though another view places them before Jesus.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p498#2/r2] author | holds | The youths' foreign names have weak transmission; al-Tabari's list gives Makselminya, Majseliniya, Tamlikha, Martus, Kashutunus, Birunus, Dinamus, and Yatūnus.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p498#2/r5] author | reports | One account says they hid their escape by playing with a polo mallet and ball as they moved toward the cave; another says they escaped while being taken to a festival.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499#2/r1] author | reports | People recorded the youths' names, fathers, rank, and disappearance on lead or copper tablets; some say two secretly believing judges hid them, either at their homes or in the king's construction over the cave.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499#2/r3] author | reports | The persecuting king is also said to have been a Roman ruler who sought to abolish the religion of Jesus and killed believers publicly.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499#2/r4] author | holds | The youths sought God's mercy, interpreted by commentators as provision, and a good way out of their situation; the author says this was a worldly prayer, while they trusted their afterlife outcome.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499#2/r5] author | holds | The majority read rashadan with both vowels open; Abu Raja reads rushdan with a rounded first vowel and silent second, while the majority reading better matches surrounding verse endings.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499#2/r6] author | holds | The words may also include mercy in the afterlife, and the author recommends this verse as a sufficient prayer for a believer's worldly needs.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499/r1] author | reports | A separate account says the youths saw their city's festival idolatry, believed, and left the people to avoid sharing their punishment.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499/r2] وهب بن منبه | reports | Wahb's account has a disciple of Jesus work in a bathhouse, convert the city's noble youths, then flee with them after the prince and a prostitute died there and the disciple was blamed.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499/r3] عبيد بن عمير | reports | Ubayd ibn Umayr says the noble youths became believers one by one, met by chance beneath a tree, learned that their aims matched, and then fled together after confiding in pairs.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p499/r4] author | reports | Accounts say the dog belonged to a youth who hunted, or that a shepherd and his dog joined the group; its name is given as Himran or Qitmir.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:10-12/r1] author | holds | The youths took refuge in the cave to escape their people and protect their religion, asking God for mercy, rescue, and a way to right guidance.
[TABRISI:18:10-12/r2] ابن عباس | reports | The prayer may ask God to provide a safe way out of the cave.
[TABRISI:18:10-12/r3] قيل | reports | Alternatively, they ask to be guided to a course that will save them, or for an arrangement that earns God’s approval.
[TABRISI:18:10-12/r4] author | holds | The youths were believers who concealed Islam from a ruler named Daqiyānūs in Ephesus; he worshiped idols and killed opponents, though another account calls him a Magian who promoted that religion.
[TABRISI:18:10-12/r5] عبيد بن عمير | reports | ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr says the youths were close associates of the king who hid their faith from one another until they met, disclosed it, and fled to the cave.
[TABRISI:18:10-12/r6] قيل | reports | One account says they followed the religion of Jesus after the people of the Gospel had passed away; another places them before Jesus’s mission.
[TABRISI:18:9-12/r3] author | holds | Awā means to return or take refuge; futiya is a collective plural for young men, like ṣabī/ṣibya and ghulām/ghilma, though such formations are not fully productive.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p108/r1] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says āwaw ilayhi means they went to the cave and made it their refuge.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p108/r2] author | holds | Fitya is a plural of fatā, like ghulām/ghilma and ṣabī/ṣabiya; it is a collective form that does not generate parallel forms such as “ravens” or “wealthy men.”
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p108/r3] author | reports | Some commentators understand fitya to mean young men; Ibn Qutayba says fatā can mean a fully grown man, as in the verse about believing women.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p108/r4] author | holds | “From Your presence” means from You; raḥma means provision, and prepare our affair means set it right.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p108/r5] author | holds | Rushd means guidance toward what brings one near to God and is the opposite of error.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p70/r10] author | holds | The youths’ taking refuge means they went to the cave and made it their dwelling.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p70/r11] author | holds | Their prayer asks for mercy from God’s stores of grace, including guidance, knowledge, patience, provision, and safety from enemies.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p70/r12] author | holds | “From Your presence” signals the greatness of the requested mercy, fitting God’s generosity and ample bounty.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p70/r13] author | reports | Rashad can mean an affair endowed with right guidance so that they become guided, or that God make all their affair itself rightly guided.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p70/r9] author | holds | The temporal إذ cannot attach to the preceding question because the Prophet and the youths were separated by a long interval; an implicit اذكر, “recall,” is understood.
