[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p218/r8] إسحاق وأهل الظاهر | reports | Isḥāq and the literalists say there is no make-up in that case, analogizing it to forgetfulness; this is also reported from ʿUmar.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p218/r9] مالك | reports | Mālik requires both make-up and expiation when a person breaks the fast while uncertain about sunset; another reported position requires make-up alone by analogy with doubt at dawn.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r1] author | holds | The wording apparently commands completion only of a fast already begun; although the Sunna separately requires a healthy person who began the day without fasting to abstain.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r10] author | holds | If mubāsharah here included mere touching, intercourse would be prohibited a fortiori because it contains touching and more; the touching at issue is understood with desire.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r11] author | holds | In law, iʿtikāf means restraining oneself in a place for worship and nearness to God, and is an ancient religious practice.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r2] الحنفية | reports | The Ḥanafīs include voluntary fasting under the general command to complete the fast; the Shāfiʿīs restrict it to obligatory fasting because the passage explains its rules.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r3] بعض أرباب الحقائق | reports | A mystical allegory divides Ramadan between God’s due, completing the fast until night, and human needs, eating and drinking until dawn.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r4] author | holds | When intercourse was permitted at night, some retreatants left the mosque to have sex and returned after bathing; the verse forbids it during retreat both inside and outside the mosque.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r5] فرقة | reports | One group limits the prohibited mubāsharah in retreat to intercourse, following the occasion of revelation and immediate context; the majority include pleasurable contact.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r6] الجمهور | reports | There is consensus that the prohibition is categorical and intercourse invalidates retreat; schools differ on whether lustful looking, touch, or kissing also invalidates it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r7] مالك | reports | Mālik says lustful looking, touching, or kissing invalidates retreat; Abū Ḥanīfa conditions invalidation on ejaculation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r8] المزني عن الشافعي | reports | Al-Muzanī transmits from al-Shāfiʿī that such an act invalidates retreat if it would produce a comparable legal consequence with an unrelated woman.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p219/r9] عائشة | reports | ʿĀʾisha reports that she combed the Prophet’s head while he was in retreat in the mosque; scholars infer that nonlustful touch is allowed.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p220/r1] قتادة | reports | Qatāda reads ʿākifūn without the alif, and the clause is a circumstantial phrase meaning “do not approach them while in this state.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p220/r10] عبد الله وعائشة وإبراهيم وابن جبير وعروة وأبو جعفر | reports | They restrict retreat to a mosque where congregational prayer is held; this is another opinion of Mālik.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p220/r2] author | holds | Retreat is permissible and not obligatory by consensus; the Prophet practiced it, making it a Sunna, though this verse does not specify its recommendation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p220/r3] علي وابن عمر وابن عباس وعائشة وأبو حنيفة وأصحابه ومالك والثوري والحسن بن صالح | reports | These authorities require fasting as a condition of retreat; a report from ʿĀʾisha calls fasting a Sunna of the retreatant.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p220/r4] سعيد وإبراهيم وطاووس عن ابن عباس والشافعي | reports | These authorities say fasting is not a condition for retreat.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p220/r5] الشافعي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī says any duration that counts as remaining in place qualifies as retreat; Mālik’s well-known minimum is ten days, with a report of one day and night.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p220/r6] الشافعي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī allows retreat by night, day, or either alone, including a vow for only one night or one day.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p220/r7] سحنون وأبو حنيفة | reports | Sahnūn says a vow of one night is not binding; Abū Ḥanīfa says a vow of days includes their nights.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r1] عبد الله وحذيفة | reports | Some say retreat is valid only in one of the three holy mosques; another group limits it to a prophet’s mosque, a position Ibn al-Musayyab holds.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r10] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya understands the limits as the boundaries between permissibility and prohibition.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r11] author | holds | Ascribing the limits to God emphasizes that they must not be confused; the phrase is neither indefinite nor marked by the definite article.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r12] author | holds | Forbidding people to approach a limit is stronger than merely forbidding transgression, fitting a passage focused on prohibitions.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r2] الحارث عن علي | reports | Al-Ḥārith transmits from ʿAlī that retreat is only valid in the Sacred Mosque or the Prophet’s Mosque.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r3] author | holds | The verse expressly addresses men but is silent about women; Abū Ḥanīfa confines a woman to her home mosque, Mālik requires a congregational mosque, and al-Shāfiʿī permits any location.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r4] مجاهد والأعمش | reports | Mujāhid and al-Aʿmash read “in the mosque” in the singular; al-Aʿmash means the Sacred Mosque, while the author takes the word generically and favors the plural reading.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r5] بعض الصوفية | reports | Some Ṣūfīs say that the place of nearness is sacred and protected from the pursuit of bodily desires.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r8] شهر بن حوشب | reports | Shahr b. Ḥawshab glosses the limits as God’s obligations.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p221/r9] الضحاك والزمخشري | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Zamakhsharī take the limits as sins, sacred prohibitions, or bans.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p222/r2] author | holds | The choice of “do not approach” suits a prohibition: elsewhere “do not transgress” follows divorce rules, while “transgress His limits” follows inheritance and family-law allocations, so each wording fits its context. | mentions 2:229, 4:14
