## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:2:259#2/r1] أبو الحسن | prefers | The imperative reading اعلم addresses the speaker himself after he witnessed the resurrection, as poets sometimes address themselves in the second person; Abu al-Hasan says it fits best.

[TABRISI:2:259#2/r3] author | holds | خاوية means empty; a village is named for gathering residents, and خاوية على عروشها can mean its walls remain without roofs or that it lies fallen over its buildings.
[TABRISI:2:259#2/r4] أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu Ubayda interprets عروش as tents, the dwellings of nomads; another lexical view explains them as roofs or buildings.
[TABRISI:2:259#2/r5] author | holds | أنى asks from where or how; a year is a full cycle after winter and summer, while swimming and the verb لبث denote movement through water and remaining in place.
[TABRISI:2:259#2/r6] author | holds | The word حمار covers both domestic and wild asses; نشر is the opposite of folding and can also mean spreading news, scent, or a healing charm.
