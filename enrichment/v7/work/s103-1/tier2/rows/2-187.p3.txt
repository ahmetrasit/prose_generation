## IBNABIHATIM: Ibn Abī Ḥātim al-Rāzī, d. 327 AH
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p315/r1] معاذ بن جبل | reports | Mu'adh reports that fasting initially prohibited food, drink, and sex only after a person slept; after Umar had intercourse with his wife after sleeping and told the Prophet, the verse permitting night intercourse was revealed.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p315/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas glosses rafath as sexual intercourse; the source lists Ata, Mujahid, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Tawus, al-Hasan, al-Dahhak, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, and others in agreement.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p315/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says spouses are a source of tranquility for one another; Mujahid, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Qatada, al-Suddi, and Muqatil are reported to agree.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p315/r4] الربيع بن أنس | reports | Al-Rabi ibn Anas interprets the spouses' clothing metaphor as each being a covering or blanket for the other.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p315/r5] كعب بن مالك | reports | Ka'b ibn Malik recounts that people once had to abstain from food and drink after sleeping during a fast; Umar and Ka'b each had intercourse after their wives had slept, then Umar informed the Prophet and this verse was revealed.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p316/r1] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid explains “you were betraying yourselves” as “you were wronging yourselves.”
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p316/r2] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi explains the phrase as secretly having intercourse with their wives in violation of the former rule.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p316/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas connects God's pardon to what Umar had done, saying God revealed His forgiveness in these words.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p316/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says “be intimate with them” means intercourse, expressed by God euphemistically; Mujahid, Ata, al-Dahhak, Muqatil, al-Suddi, and others agree.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p316/r5] ابن عباس | reports | One interpretation of “seek what God has written for you” is to seek offspring; many early authorities are reported to share this view.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p316/r6] ابن عباس | reports | A second view from Ibn Abbas interprets what God has written as Laylat al-Qadr.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p316/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas explains the white and black threads as day and night: intimacy, eating, and drinking were permitted until dawn became clear, after which they were forbidden.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p318/r1] ابن جريج | reports | Ibn Jurayj says the command to eat and drink was revealed concerning Abu Qays ibn Sirma of the Khazraj.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p318/r2] النبي | reports | Adi ibn Hatim misunderstood the threads literally and watched two threads; the Prophet clarified that dawn means the whiteness of day and blackness of night.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p318/r3] سهل بن سعد | reports | Sahl says the words “of dawn” were added after men tied white and black threads to their legs and ate until they could distinguish them; the clarification means night and day.
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p318/r4] عطاء | reports | A report from Ata describes dawn as the white dawn, with the transmitted wording apparently incomplete after “the one beneath it.”
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p318/r5] النبي | reports | The Prophet forbade uninterrupted fasting for two days, likened it to Christian practice, and instructed Muslims to complete the fast until night and then break it.

[IBNABIHATIM:v1p319/r3] ابن عمر | reports | When asked whether men who sleep in the mosque and become ritually impure should be reported to the ruler, Ibn Umar says they are the people in retreat.

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
