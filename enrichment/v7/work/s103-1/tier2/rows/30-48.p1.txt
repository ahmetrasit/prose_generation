## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p398/r10] author | holds | When rain reaches the land God chooses, people become joyful at once; al-Akhfash treats “before it” as reinforcing “before it descends.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p398/r11] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the repeated “before it” shows how quickly people move from despair to delight, specifying that the joy follows close upon the rain.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p398/r12] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says the repetition indicates a long drought and settled despair, making their joy proportionate to their prior need.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p398/r5] author | holds | The verse about God sending winds resumes “Among His signs is that He sends winds”; the intervening sentence comforts the Prophet, promises victory, and warns unbelievers.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p398/r6] author | holds | The wind's force shows divine power: delicate air precedes lightning that uproots trees and demolishes buildings, though air itself is not the agent but a voluntary actor is.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p398/r7] author | holds | The same winds display wisdom by raising clouds, producing rain and crops, nourishing livestock, and distributing these benefits selectively according to God's will.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p398/r8] author | holds | “Stirring” means moving the clouds, spreading means dispersing them across the horizons, and “pieces” means fragments; the pronoun in “through it” most naturally refers to clouds.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p398/r9] وقيل | reports | The pronoun in “through it” may instead refer to the fragmented cloud in the reading that leaves the relevant consonant unvowelled; “sky” means the direction above.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p399/r1] author | rejects | The author finds the proposed implications of “before it” unobvious; the repetition serves emphasis and removes a figurative reading.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p399/r2] قطرب | reports | Quṭrub construes the words as “even before the sending,” meaning before the rain; the author rejects this as unidiomatic.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p399/r3] وقيل | reports | Another proposal supplies “before sowing,” inferred from rain and the later mention of crops turning yellow; the author says the syntax cannot support this attachment.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p399/r4] author | rejects | The proposed attachment to sowing fails because two prepositional phrases cannot depend on the same verb here without coordination or apposition, and rain is neither sowing nor part of it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p399/r5] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad takes the pronoun to refer to clouds, but the author notes that this analysis also needs a conjunction to link the two prepositional phrases.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p399/r6] علي بن عيسى | reports | ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā interprets “before it” as before the sending; al-Kirmānī interprets it as before the glad tidings because it is paired with despair.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p638#2/r1] author | holds | Rain reaching a land or person God chooses is a sheer favor, not something owed; the recipients owe worship, gratitude, and submission, yet they quickly rejoice in the hoped-for harvest despite possible setbacks.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p638#2/r2] author | holds | The people rejoice at once because they anticipate the beneficial effects of rainfall, such as fertility, moisture, and softness.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p638/r1] author | holds | The plural reading riyaḥ, used by most reciters rather than Ibn Kathir, Hamza, and al-Kisaʾi, highlights the varied winds God can use to raise clouds.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p638/r2] author | holds | God first makes the wind raise clouds that did not exist, then attributes spreading and dividing the clouds to himself to show that no natural force acts independently.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p638/r3] author | holds | The clouds' varied distances and forms show that God alone freely chooses how to spread them, without nature or another cause controlling him.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p638/r4] author | holds | God makes clouds into separate pieces, leaving openings through which rain can descend; al-wadq is abundant, continuous rain emerging through the clouds in either joined or divided states.
