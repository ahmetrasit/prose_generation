## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v17p129/r1] author | reports | Most Medinan, Basran, and some Kufan readers take “he said” as a report: God asks the people of Hell how long they stayed, and they say a day or part of one because punishment makes worldly time seem short.
[TAB-FULL:v17p129/r2] author | reports | Most Kufan readers instead read an imperative, “say,” reflecting their codex spelling without the alif; other codices have the alif.
[TAB-FULL:v17p129/r3] author | prefers | The declarative reading is preferable: the surrounding address is plural, so a command would naturally be plural “say”; singular “say” is possible, but declarative singular is more fluent Arabic.
[TAB-FULL:v17p129/r4] author | holds | On the preferred reading, the people answer that they stayed only a day or part of one and ask the counters, because they have forgotten the duration.
[TAB-FULL:v17p129/r5] author | reports | One explanation begins the counters' identity as the angels who record deeds and count people's hours.
[TAB-FULL:v17p131/r1] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid identifies the counters as angels.
[TAB-FULL:v17p131/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatada identifies the counters as accountants or people who calculate.
[TAB-FULL:v17p131/r3] author | prefers | The counters are whoever counts months, years, and other quantities; they may be angels, humans, or others, since no proof singles out one group.
