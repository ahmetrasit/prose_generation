## ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-7-english:p156/r4] author | holds | Verses 44–50 resume verse 36’s contrast between the opponents’ temporary worldly life and the believers’ lasting Hereafter reward; the believers’ traits make the glad tidings immediate for oppressed Muslims and foreshadow their success.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-7-english:p156/r5] author | holds | The discourse resumes by assuring the Prophet that opponents subject to God’s law cannot be guided by anyone else and will realize this too late, then gives them a final chance to reform and explains their arrogance as ingratitude for God’s favors.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-7-english:p159/r3] author | holds | In Islahi’s syntactic reading, “from God” connects to “arrives,” and “that cannot be put off” qualifies “day.”
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-7-english:p159/r4] author | holds | The verse warns people that there is still time to answer their Lord, but when the certain Day arrives there will be no refuge or ability to reject what comes; they must submit.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-7-english:p159/r5] author | prefers | The word nakīr is better understood as rejecting something repulsive from a sense of honour, rather than as simple denial.
