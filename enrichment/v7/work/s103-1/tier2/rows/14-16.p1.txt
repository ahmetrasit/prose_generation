## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p351#3/r1] ابن عباس وأبو عبيدة | reports | One interpretation of “behind him is Hell” is that Hell is ahead of him; Ibn Abbas and Abu Ubayda support this sense.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p351#3/r2] author | reports | A poem about the Marwanids uses warāʾ for what lies ahead, supporting the “in front” sense.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p351#3/r3] ابن الأنباري | reports | Another interpretation is “after,” specifically after he has despaired; the preceding “failed” implies despair.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p351#3/r4] النابغة | reports | Al-Nabigha's verse uses warāʾ to mean “after God,” supporting the second interpretation.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p351#3/r5] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says warāʾ means what is hidden from sight, whether in front or behind; he rejects treating it as a true antonym.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p351#3/r6] ثعلب | reports | Thaʿlab explains that warāʾ names what is hidden from one's sight, whether ahead or behind.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p351#3/r7] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra allows the forward sense for times and eras, but not to describe a person who is visibly in front or behind you.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p351#3/r8] author | reports | “After God there is no destination” is the sense of the cited line, linking the verse's “behind” with what comes after.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p352/r3] القرظي | reports | Al-Qurazi identifies it as the drainage of Hell's inhabitants, flowing from adulterers' genitals.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p352/r4] ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba says ṣadīd is served in place of water, or that the water is likened to pus.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH

[RAZI-FULL:v19p81/r2] أبو عبيدة وابن السكيت | reports | Abu Ubayda and Ibn al-Sikkit say warāʾ is an antonymic word for both behind and in front; what is behind can turn into what is in front. | mentions 18:79
[RAZI-FULL:v19p81/r3] ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbari says warāʾ means after, as in the cited line that a person has no destination after God.
[RAZI-FULL:v19p81/r4] author | holds | In context, the disbeliever's defeat comes first, and afterward he enters Hell.
[RAZI-FULL:v19p81/r5] author | holds | The drinking clause coordinates with an understood phrase: he is cast into Hell and given pus to drink.
[RAZI-FULL:v19p81/r6] author | holds | This drink is singled out because it seems among the severest forms of punishment, alongside near-death from every side without dying. | mentions 14:17
[RAZI-FULL:v19p81/r7] author | reports | Ṣadīd may explain the water as the discharge flowing from the skins of Hell's inhabitants, or as water like pus that God creates in its stench, thickness, and filth.
[RAZI-FULL:v19p81/r8] author | holds | Another explanation is that it is actual pus, so repulsive that its foulness prevents the drinker from taking it.
[RAZI-FULL:v19p81/r9] author | holds | The image is linked to boiling water that cuts the bowels in Q 47:15 and molten water that scalds faces in Q 18:29. | mentions 47:15, 18:29

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r1] author | holds | Waraʾahu Jahannam means Hell is after the unbeliever's destruction; waraʾ can mean after, as in al-Nābigha's line that no destination lies after God.
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r3] قيل | reports | Waraʾ may instead mean in front of, supported by several poems and by the description of the king ahead of the people in 18:79; Abū ʿUbayda and Quṭrub adopt this sense. | mentions 18:79
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r4] الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash explains the word through idioms: something behind you will come to you, and being behind someone means pursuing him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r5] النحاس | reports | Al-Naḥḥās says waraʾahu means in front of him, not as a true antonym, but because what is hidden is concealed from view.
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r6] الأزهري | reports | Al-Azharī and also Abū ʿUbayda treat waraʾ as an antonym meaning either behind or in front, both rooted in concealment; Ibn al-Anbārī's explanation that Hell is hidden is judged good.
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r7] author | holds | Ṣadīd is water like pus by simile, or the pus and blood flowing from the people of Hell; Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb and al-Rabīʿ say it is their wash water from adulterers' genitals, while another view derives it from repelling a loathed drink.
