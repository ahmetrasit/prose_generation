## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v17p129/r1] author | reports | Most Medinan, Basran, and some Kufan readers take “he said” as a report: God asks the people of Hell how long they stayed, and they say a day or part of one because punishment makes worldly time seem short.
[TAB-FULL:v17p129/r2] author | reports | Most Kufan readers instead read an imperative, “say,” reflecting their codex spelling without the alif; other codices have the alif.
[TAB-FULL:v17p129/r3] author | prefers | The declarative reading is preferable: the surrounding address is plural, so a command would naturally be plural “say”; singular “say” is possible, but declarative singular is more fluent Arabic.

## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH
[ZAJJAJ:v4p24#2/r2] author | holds | كم is accusative with لبثتم; عدد سنين is governed by كم, and doubled tāʾ is permitted in question and answer.

## IBNABIHATIM: Ibn Abī Ḥātim al-Rāzī, d. 327 AH
[IBNABIHATIM:v8p2511/r1] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says God asks the people of Paradise how long they lived on earth; they answer a day or part of one, and God calls it a good trade for his mercy, approval, and Paradise. ## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v3p123/r7] author | holds | The two questions ‘how long did you remain?’ have different senses and neither is better; ʿadada sinīn has a sound masculine plural accusative, though some Arabs use a genitive with tanwīn.

## FARISI-HUJJA: Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, d. 377 AH
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p306#3/r1] ابن كثير | reports | Al-Bazzī's transmission from Ibn Kathīr has an imperative at the question and a reported statement at the answer; Qunbul's cited transmission has imperatives at both places.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p306#3/r2] نافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر | reports | These readers use قال at both locations, presenting both clauses as reports rather than commands.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p306#3/r3] حمزة والكسائي | reports | Ḥamzah and al-Kisāʾī read قل at both locations, addressing the questioner with an imperative; the Kufan codex reading قل supports their choice.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p306#3/r4] أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي | reports | These readers assimilate thāʾ into tāʾ because their articulation points are close and both are voiceless; Ibn Kathīr favors clear separation because the articulation points differ.

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v7p59/r1] author | holds | They forget how long they lived in the world because of the great torment; the question rebukes deniers of resurrection and establishes the argument against them.
[THALABI:v7p59/r2] حمزة والكسائي | reports | They read قل كم as a command, matching Kufan codices without alif; it can address the group or each unbeliever individually.
[THALABI:v7p59/r3] باقي القراء | reports | They read قال in both places, matching their codices with alif and making God the speaker.
[THALABI:v7p59/r4] ابن كثير | reports | He reads قل كم first as a command, then قال as the reported answer, which the author calls an evident reading.

## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH

[MAWARDI-FULL:v4p68#2/r2] author | reports | Alternatively, they are asked about their graves; the brief answer reports what they knew, not a lie.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p82/r1] author | holds | God asks the disbelievers on the Day of Resurrection how many years they remained in the earth, meaning in their graves, as a rebuke for denying resurrection.

[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p82/r4] author | reports | Alternatively, قل is an instruction to the person who questions them on the Day of Resurrection about their stay.

## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v7p5098/r4] ابن كثير | reports | Ibn Kathir reads قل كم لبثتم as a command and قال إن لبثتم as a statement, which suits the second as an answer.
[JISHUMI:v7p5098/r5] حمزة، الكسائي | reports | Hamza and al-Kisa'i read both verbs as commands, matching Kufa codices; other codices and readers use indicative forms, with قل addressed to the singular Prophet as a collective command.
[JISHUMI:v7p5098/r6] author | holds | Sukhr is mockery; nisyān means the opposite of remembering and also leaving something, with the latter closer to its sense here; labth and mukth mean remaining over time.

## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v4p158#2/r1] نافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر | reports | These readers have qala kam labithtum and qul in labithtum; other readers vary between a command or report for the first verb.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v4p158#2/r2] حمزة والكسائي | reports | Hamza and al-Kisaʾi use qul for both questions and statements, while al-Bazzi from Ibn Kathir has a command for the question and a report for the answer.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v4p158#2/r3] author | holds | The readings mean either God asks and then tells them the stay was brief, an individual is ordered to answer and corrected, or they are questioned before being told the answer.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v4p158#2/r4] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari understands their stay in the earth as their living time in this world; they have forgotten through torment and estimate only a day or part of one.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v4p158#2/r5] author | prefers | Ibn Atiyya prefers the time they lay dead in the earth, since they denied resurrection and are now asked after rising from the soil.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v4p158#2/r6] author | holds | The point is that their lives were brief but disbelief led them to long punishment; ʿadad is a specification after kam, and al-Aʿmash reads it with tanwin.
