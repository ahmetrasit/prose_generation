## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r1] جمهور المتأولين من الصحابة والتابعين | reports | Most early interpreters identify the good deeds that erase sins as the five daily prayers.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r10] ابن عباس | reports | On the view that a zulfah is the first hour after sunset, the night portion means the nightfall prayer, according to Ibn Abbas.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r11] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan interprets the night portion as sunset and nightfall prayers.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r12] الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash says the phrase means night prayer without specifying which prayer.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says the good deeds include saying glory and praise to God and affirming His oneness and greatness; Ibn Atiyya treats this as an example.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r3] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya says Mujahid's list is illustrative; the wording is general about good deeds but specific to lesser sins, as long as major sins are avoided.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r4] author | holds | The occasion of revelation supports the majority: an Ansari, identified by some as Abu al-Yusr ibn Amr and by others as Abbad, had an intimate encounter short of intercourse.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r5] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In a Tirmidhi report from Abdullah, a man confesses to intimacy short of intercourse; after reciting the verse, the Prophet says it applies to everyone, not only that man. Tirmidhi grades it hasan sahih.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r6] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Another Tirmidhi report from Ibn Masud concerns a forbidden kiss; the Prophet says the verse applies to the questioner and whoever acts on it from his community, and Tirmidhi grades the report hasan sahih.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r7] مجاهد وابن محيصن | reports | Mujahid and Ibn Muhaysin also read زلفى, like قربى.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r8] author | reports | The remaining readers use زلف with an open lām, like غرفة and غرف.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#2/r9] ابن الأعرابي | reports | Ibn al-Arabi says zalaf means hours and zulfah is its singular.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r1] أبو اليسر | reports | Abu al-Yusr recounts kissing a woman who came to buy dates; after he confessed to Abu Bakr and Umar, he told the Prophet, feared Hell, and received the verse. The Prophet said it was for everyone; al-Tirmidhi calls this report hasan gharib and notes Qays ibn al-Rabi was weakened.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r2] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | An alternative report says the Prophet turned away from the man, then after the afternoon prayer Gabriel brought the verse; the Prophet asked whether he had prayed with them and called the prayer an expiation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r3] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Another report says the Prophet told the man to perform four cycles of prayer after reciting the verse.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r4] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Al-Tirmidhi al-Hakim transmits from Ibn Abbas that no good deed answers and reaches an old sin more quickly than a recent good deed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r5] author | holds | The verse and reports show that an unlawful kiss or touch does not incur the fixed penalty.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#3/r6] ابن المنذر | reports | Ibn al-Mundhir is said to choose that no fixed penalty or discretionary punishment applies when a man and woman are found in one garment, using this report as evidence.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#4/r1] author | holds | The Quran names prayer's postures and recitation, but gives its outline; the Prophet explained times, units, required conditions, sunnah, and virtues, as commanded in 16:44. | mentions 16:44, 17:78, 30:17, 30:18, 20:130, 22:77, 2:238, 7:204, 17:110
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#4/r2] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet said, Pray as you have seen me pray; the community transmitted his explanation broadly, and he completed what people need before his death.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#4/r3] author | holds | The completed religion and clarified path are affirmed by the verse declaring God's favor and religion complete. | mentions 5:3
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114#4/r4] author | holds | The Quran is an admonition and call to repentance for those who take heed; they are singled out because they benefit, and dhikra is a verbal noun with feminine ending.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r1] جمهور أهل التأويل | reports | Interpreters agree that prayer here means the obligatory prayers; prayer follows faith and is a refuge in hardship, as the Prophet turned to it when troubled.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r10] ابن عطية | rejects | Ibn Atiyya objects that sunset belongs to the night, so cannot be an end of daytime.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r11] ابن العربي | rejects | Ibn al-Arabi mocks al-Tabari's pairing as making the two ends of the day dawn and sunset, which he calls the ends of night.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r12] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari replies that since all agree one end is dawn and no other pairing is agreed, the other end must be sunset.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r13] author | rejects | The author calls Ibn al-Arabi's rebuttal excessive: Mujahid supports dawn, and the rule that deliberate eating or intercourse after dawn invalidates the fast confirms dawn is daytime, though the sunset objection remains.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r14] author | holds | Zulaf means successive near hours; al-Muzdalifa is named for being a stop near Mecca after Arafat.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r15] ابن قعقاع وابن أبي إسحاق وغيرهما | reports | These readers use زلف with a damma on the lām, either a plural of zalif or a dialectal singular zulfah.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r16] ابن محيصن | reports | Ibn Muhaysin reads زلف with a silent lām, treating it as the singular zulfah used collectively.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r2] شيوخ الصوفية | reports | Sufi authorities say the verse commands filling all time with worship, obligatory and voluntary.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r3] ابن العربي | rejects | Ibn al-Arabi rejects the all-time worship reading: the command covers obligations, while litanies and recommended prayers have bounded times and other voluntary acts rotate rather than fill every hour.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r4] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid identifies the two ends of the day as dawn at the first end and noon plus afternoon prayers at the second; Ibn Atiyya chooses this view.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r5] ابن عباس والحسن | reports | Ibn Abbas and al-Hasan identify the two ends as dawn and sunset; al-Hasan also reports a second end consisting of the afternoon prayer alone, as do Qatada and al-Dahhak.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r6] قيل | reports | Another view identifies the ends as noon and afternoon; its account of the night hours includes sunset, nightfall, and dawn, apparently tracking loud recitation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r7] الماوردي | reports | Al-Mawardi reports agreement that the first end of the day is the dawn prayer.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r8] author | rejects | The author says this reported agreement is qualified by the preceding view that does not place dawn at that end.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:114/r9] الطبري | prefers | Al-Tabari prefers dawn and sunset as the two ends, arguing that everyone agrees dawn is one end and sunset must be the other.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:114/r1] author | reports | The two ends of the day are morning and evening: dawn prayer is the first prayer near daytime, while the evening prayer may mean afternoon or noon and afternoon; the nearby night prayers are sunset and night prayer.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:114/r2] author | holds | Zulafan are hours near the day, a plural of zulfa; readings include zuluf, zuluf, and zulfa, with the latter meaning one near period.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:114/r3] author | holds | Good deeds erase sins, and prayer from one prayer to the next expiates what lies between them if major sins are avoided.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:114/r4] رجل | reports | A man told the Prophet that he had done something with a woman but had not had intercourse; this verse was revealed.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:114/r5] author | reports | The phrase 'that is a reminder' may refer to steadfastness and what follows or to the Qur'an; it is an admonition for those who take heed.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r1] author | holds | The two ends of day mean morning and evening: dawn prayer, and afternoon prayer after noon, which includes noon and afternoon; the night periods near day's end are sunset and night prayers.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r2] author | holds | The accusative dual 'ends' functions adverbially because the words are annexed to times, as in phrases giving a whole or half day.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r3] author | reports | The five prayers expiate sins committed between them; alternatively good deeds generally erase sins, as in the prophetic instruction to follow an evil deed with a good one.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r4] author | reports | The Prophet also recommends saying 'Glory be to God, praise belongs to God, there is no god but God, and God is greatest.'
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r5] author | holds | That may refer to the instruction beginning 'remain steadfast' and what follows, or to the Qur'an as admonition for those who take heed.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r6] author | reports | The verse was revealed about ʿAmr ibn Ghaziyya al-Anṣārī, a date seller who entered a woman's home and kissed her, then repented; the Prophet said his joining the afternoon prayer expiated it, and that the rule applied to everyone.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p175/r7] النبي | reports | The Prophet asks whether the man prayed the afternoon prayer with them, calls it an expiation, and says this applies to all people, not him alone.
