## FARRA: al-Farrāʾ, d. 207 AH
[FARRA:v3p264/r1] author | holds | Arabic usually repeats lā across coordinated statements; here one lā covers the three parts of the steep path: freeing a slave or feeding during famine, then believing. | mentions 75:31
[FARRA:v3p264/r2] العوام | reports | The common reading coordinates the verbal noun “freeing a slave” with “feeding.”
[FARRA:v3p264/r3] الحسن البصري وعلي بن أبي طالب | reports | Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib read the nouns as a coordinated pair, فك رقبة أو إطعام.
[FARRA:v3p265/r1] author | prefers | The reading فك رقبة أو إطعام best fits Arabic because إطعام is a noun and should coordinate with another noun; ثم كان also supports this parse.
[FARRA:v3p265/r2] author | holds | The alternative reading is also possible if an omitted أن is supplied; a poem with أن in the second clause shows that it can be understood in the first.
[FARRA:v3p265/r3] شاعر | reports | An unnamed poet uses overt أن in the second coordinated clause, illustrating the elided أن in the earlier clause.

## TAB: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB:90:13/r1] author | holds | Crossing the steep obstacle means freeing a person from slavery and the bonds of servitude.
[TAB:90:13/r10] author | prefers | He prefers the verbal reading as smoother Arabic because it pairs verbs with verbs and nouns with nouns, matching ثم كان.
[TAB:90:13/r11] author | holds | The noun reading remains possible by eliding أن before a verb, as in Ṭarafa's poetic construction.

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH

[NAHHAS:v5p229/r14] الأخفش | prefers | Al-Akhfash, Abū Ḥātim, and Abū ʿUbayd prefer the nominal reading.
[NAHHAS:v5p229/r15] author | holds | Both are sound readings and one may not attack the reading transmitted by the community; the past-verb coordination argument fails because it cannot coordinate by sense as proposed.
