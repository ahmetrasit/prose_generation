[TABRISI:11:114#2/r1] علي | reports | Ali reports that the Prophet called the prayer command in this verse the most hopeful verse in the Book.
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r2] علي | reports | The Prophet compares the five daily prayers to bathing five times in a river: prayer removes sins as washing removes bodily dirt.
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r3] author | reports | One interpretation is that persistence in good deeds leads a person to abandon evil deeds, as though the good deeds remove them.
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r4] author | reports | A second interpretation identifies the good deeds as repentance, which removes sins by cancelling their punishment; the author says there is no disagreement that repentance cancels punishment.
[TABRISI:11:114#2/r5] author | holds | The reminder is an admonition for whoever reflects on the teaching that good deeds remove evil deeds.
[TABRISI:11:114/r1] author | reports | Other glosses say to perform prayer evenly or to keep performing it continuously.
[TABRISI:11:114/r2] ابن عباس، ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zayd identify the two ends of day as dawn and sunset prayers and the night intervals as the first hours of night, including the final evening prayer.
[TABRISI:11:114/r3] ابن عباس، ابن زيد | reports | They say noon and afternoon prayers are omitted either because they are obviously daytime prayers or because they fall near the day's latter end after noon; 17:78 specifies the sun's decline, meaning noon, and this is the report from Abu Ja'far. | mentions 17:78
[TABRISI:11:114/r4] الزجاج، مجاهد، الضحاك، محمد بن كعب القرظي، الحسن | reports | Al-Zajjaj, Mujahid, al-Dahhak, Muhammad ibn Kaʿb al-Qurazi, and Hasan take the day prayers as dawn, noon, and afternoon and the night prayers as sunset and the final evening prayer, since sunset belongs to night.
[TABRISI:11:114/r5] الحسن | reports | Hasan reports the Prophet's wording that Maghrib and Isha are the two night intervals called zulf.
[TABRISI:11:114/r6] author | reports | A further view identifies the two ends as the dawn and afternoon prayers.
[TABRISI:11:114/r7] ابن عباس وأكثر المفسرين | reports | Ibn Abbas and most commentators say the five daily prayers expiate sins committed between them, taking the definite good deeds as referring back to prayer.
[TABRISI:11:114/r8] سلمان | reports | Salman demonstrates a dry branch shedding leaves, then reports that the Prophet taught that good ablution and the five prayers shed a Muslim's sins like those leaves and recited this verse.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r1] علقمة والأسود عن ابن مسعود | reports | A man confessed to the Prophet that he had kissed, embraced, and fondled a woman in a garden without intercourse; after the Prophet fell silent, this verse was revealed.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r10] الجمهور | reports | The first end of the day is the dawn prayer according to the majority; Ibn Jarir instead reports the noon prayer.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r2] عمر | reports | In one report Umar asks whether the verse applies only to the man in the incident; the Prophet answers that it applies to everyone.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r3] ابن مسعود | reports | Another Ibn Masud report says a man told the Prophet he had kissed a woman, and the verse descended; the man asks if it is for him, and the Prophet says it is for whoever acts by it.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r4] معاذ بن جبل | reports | Muadh reports a man described every unlawful intimacy short of intercourse; the Prophet told him to perform ablution well and pray, then the verse was revealed.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r5] أبو صالح عن ابن عباس | reports | Abu Salih identifies the man as Amr ibn Ghaziya al-Ansari, a date seller who lured a customer by claiming better dates were at home.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r6] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil identifies the man as Abu Miqbal Amir ibn Qays al-Ansari.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r7] أحمد بن علي بن ثابت الخطيب | reports | Al-Khatib identifies the man as Abu al-Yusr Ka'b ibn Amr al-Ansari.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r8] author | reports | The source gives three possible questioners asking whether the ruling was special: Abu al-Yusr, Muadh ibn Jabal, or Umar ibn al-Khattab.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p165#3/r9] author | holds | “Establish prayer” means perform its bowing and prostration completely.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r1] ابن عباس وابن زيد | reports | Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zayd identify the second end of the day with the sunset prayer.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r2] قتادة والحسن | reports | Qatada identifies the second end with the afternoon prayer; al-Hasan is reported to hold either this or the sunset view.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r3] مجاهد والقرظي | reports | Mujahid and al-Qurazi identify the second end of the day with both noon and afternoon prayers.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r4] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak is reported to accept all three interpretations of the second end of the day.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r5] أبو جعفر وشيبة | reports | Abu Ja'far and Shayba read zulafan with a ḍamma on the lām.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r6] أبو عبيدة والعجاج | reports | Abu Ubayda explains zulaf as hours or stages, singular zulfa; al-Ajjaj's poem uses it for successive nighttime intervals.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r7] ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba connects the term with drawing near: azlafani means brought me close, and mazalif are stages or steps.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r8] ابن عباس وابن زيد | reports | One interpretation identifies the nighttime intervals as the late evening prayer, reported from Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, and Mujahid and adopted by Ibn Zayd.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p167/r9] ابن عباس والحسن ومجاهد وقتادة ومقاتل والزجاج | reports | The other interpretation identifies them as sunset and night prayers; reports and named authorities support this view.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p168/r1] ابن مسعود وابن عباس وابن المسيب ومسروق ومجاهد والقرظي والضحاك ومقاتل | reports | These authorities identify the good deeds that erase sins as the five daily prayers.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p168/r2] منصور عن مجاهد | reports | Mansur transmits from Mujahid that the good deeds are saying “Glory be to God, praise be to God, no deity but God, and God is greatest.”
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p168/r3] author | prefers | The author prefers the five-prayer interpretation because most authorities hold it and a connected Prophetic report supports it.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p168/r4] عثمان عن رسول الله | reports | Uthman reports that ablution followed by each prayer expiates sins committed since the preceding prayer; these prayers are the good deeds that erase sins.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p168/r5] المفسرون | reports | The commentators identify the sins erased here as minor sins.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p168/r6] معاذ بن جبل عن النبي | reports | Muadh reports the Prophet's advice to fear God, follow a bad deed with a good one to erase it, and treat people well.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p169/r1] author | reports | “That” may refer to the Qur'an, the establishment of prayer, or all preceding counsel: steadfastness, avoiding transgression and unjust rulers, and prayer.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v4p169/r2] author | reports | The word “reminder” is interpreted either as repentance or as admonition.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v18p58#2/r1] author | holds | The command to pray follows the command to be steadfast, indicating that prayer is the greatest act of worship after faith.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p58#2/r2] الخوارج | reports | The Khārijites argue that only dawn and nightfall prayers are obligatory because they fall at the two day edges; they treat the night phrase as an adjective of the day edges.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p58#2/r3] الخوارج | reports | They also argue that the two prayers expiate every sin, even omission of the other prayers, by the statement that good deeds remove evil deeds.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p58#2/r4] author | rejects | Rāzī rejects the Khārijite claim that only two prayers are required as contrary to the community's consensus.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p58#2/r5] author | prefers | The closest reading of the day edges is dawn and afternoon prayer: sunrise marks one edge, sunset the other, while sunset prayer is included among the night intervals.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p58#2/r6] أبو حنيفة | reports | Rāzī says the verse supports Abū Ḥanīfa's preference to perform dawn prayer later in its time and afternoon prayer later, near sunset.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p59#2/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the five prayers expiate lesser sins, provided major sins are avoided.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p59#2/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid identifies good deeds as the devotional phrases “Glory be to God,” praise, the declaration of unity, and magnifying God.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p59#2/r3] author | reports | Those arguing sin cannot harm a believer infer that faith, the greatest good deed, removes unbelief and therefore lesser sins; at minimum it removes eternal punishment.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p59#2/r4] author | holds | The demonstrative “that” refers to the passage from “be steadfast” onward and serves as admonition for those who reflect and seek guidance.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p59/r1] author | holds | Because zulf is a plural with minimum three, Rāzī infers three night prayer intervals; since Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ account for two, he argues witr is obligatory for the Prophet and, by 34:20, everyone. | mentions 34:20
[RAZI-FULL:v18p59/r2] author | holds | The wording about glorification before sunrise and before sunset in 20:130 likewise refers to Fajr and ʿAṣr; the night portions parallel zulf. | mentions 20:130
[RAZI-FULL:v18p59/r3] author | reports | A report says a man who had intimate contact with a forbidden woman short of intercourse asked the Prophet what to do; he prescribed good ablution and prayer, then this verse was revealed and he said the ruling applied to everyone.
[RAZI-FULL:v18p59/r4] الليث | reports | Al-Layth defines a zulfah as a portion at the beginning of the night and zulf as its plural.
