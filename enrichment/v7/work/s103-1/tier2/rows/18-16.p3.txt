## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451/r1] author | holds | Since the youths lacked power against their numerous opponents, they migrated to preserve their faith after exposing their opponents' folly.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451/r2] author | holds | Their separation from their people and their worship indicates that their community practiced polytheism.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451/r3] author | reports | A possible interpretation is that they called the people's coerced submission to God's will and decrees a form of worship.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451/r4] author | holds | They take refuge in the cave because they have withdrawn from their people; God will extend enough mercy and prepare a useful comfort for them.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451/r5] author | holds | Their hope rests on God's power to protect whoever seeks refuge in Him and trusts Him; He fulfills it by letting them sleep and raising them centuries later.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451/r6] author | holds | The reading مرفقا has kasra on mim and fatha on fa for most readers, but fatha on mim and kasra on fa for Nafi and Ibn Amir.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v5p172#2/r1] author | holds | The continuation of the quoted verse praises retreat as a pleasant state for someone who inhabits the corner of seclusion.
[BURSEVI:v5p172#2/r2] author | holds | The seeker should cling to a perfected guide who trains him, increases his guidance, and strengthens his heart through spiritual care.
[BURSEVI:v5p172#2/r3] author | holds | A Mathnawī couplet warns that walking without a guide breeds self-conceit, error, and humiliation, and urges following the shaykh to receive his aid.
[BURSEVI:v5p172#2/r4] author | holds | The Cave youths were unusually drawn by God and raised without an intermediary; this is rare, while the normal way is guidance through prophets and then learned scholars and shaykhs.
[BURSEVI:v5p172#2/r5] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet said that God educated him and made his education excellent, illustrating direct divine instruction.
[BURSEVI:v5p172#2/r6] author | holds | Taking refuge in the cave inwardly means retreating into solitude and holding to guides who lead along the Sufi path.
[BURSEVI:v5p172#2/r7] التأويلات النجمية | reports | Special mercy means God's personal favor, drawing seekers into His attributes so they acquire their qualities; general mercy is shared by believers and unbelievers, jinn, humans, and animals.
[BURSEVI:v5p172#2/r8] التأويلات النجمية | reports | The prepared benefit is an opened path to arrival and union with God.

[BURSEVI:v5p172/r11] الحجندي | reports | His verse teaches that union is attained only by severance, beginning with cutting oneself off from everything else.
[BURSEVI:v5p172/r2] الكاشفي | reports | After Decianus challenged them and gave them respite, Yamlīkhā, their leader, spoke to them on the road as they fled.

[BURSEVI:v5p172/r5] author | holds | The cave command may indicate the unstated answer: inward separation from the people entails bodily withdrawal, or those wishing to leave should take refuge there.
[BURSEVI:v5p172/r6] author | holds | Ideological separation entails bodily withdrawal; Rasteghfanī consequently ruled out marriage between Sunnis and Muʿtazilites.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r1] author | holds | Their advice is to withdraw from the people and their worship, then worship God alone somewhere safe; its fulfillment confirms their sincerity and the preceding counsel.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r10] author | reports | Min amrikum may alternatively be an implied circumstantial phrase modifying marfiqan.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r11] author | holds | Putting lakum first signals early that the following provision benefits them and builds anticipation; their statement reflects trust, hope, and certainty in God.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r2] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra takes faʾwu as the answer to idha; another analysis makes it evidence of an implied answer: first separate inwardly, then physically if needed.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r3] author | rejects | Both conditional analyses are challenged because idha without ma is not conditional; the claim that it can be conditional is weak or merely semantic shorthand.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r4] author | reports | Accordingly, idha is causal or temporal, attached either to an implied command or to the stated command; Abu al-Baqa attaches it to an implied “they said to one another,” which the author calls astonishing.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r5] قتادة | reports | A report from Qatada gives Ibn Masud's codex the explanatory wording “what they worship besides God”; Harun reports “besides us” in another codex.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r6] في البحر | holds | The codex variants are explanatory glosses, not readings, because they conflict with the Uthmanic consonantal text.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p220#2/r7] author | holds | Yanshur means to spread and expand; rabb is the guardian who guided them to faith; mercy covers both worlds; yuhayyiʾ means to make easy, and the omitted object is good or similar benefit.
