## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v12p150/r1] author | holds | The worldly life and its wealth and adornment, dulled by loss and death, resemble rain that produces mixed vegetation for people and animals.
[TAB-FULL:v12p150/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas says the rain grows every kind of plant: grain, legumes, and fruit for people, and grasses and pasture for livestock.
[TAB-FULL:v12p150/r3] author | holds | The earth's taking its adornment means its beauty appears; its owners think they control what it has grown, but God's decree destroys it by night or day and leaves it cut down.
[TAB-FULL:v12p150/r4] author | holds | The crops seem to have never existed; the root of تغن means to dwell or remain, as al-Nābigha's verse illustrates, just as worldly splendor vanishes.
[TAB-FULL:v12p150/r5] author | holds | God explains signs for reflective people because they distinguish matters and investigate the realities behind doubts.
[TAB-FULL:v12p152/r1] قتادة | reports | Qatada warns that people who cling to worldly life will soon be cast off and undone by it.
[TAB-FULL:v12p152/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatada glosses وازينت as the earth having sprouted and become beautiful.
[TAB-FULL:v12p152/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas says Ubayy ibn Kaʿb taught an added reading that the earth would not be destroyed except for its people's sins; the phrase is absent from the codex.
[TAB-FULL:v12p152/r4] أبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن | reports | Abu Salama reports Ubayy's reading with the added explanation that God destroyed the land only because of its people's sins.
[TAB-FULL:v12p152/r5] author | prefers | Most readers pronounce وازينت as تزينت, assimilating tā into zā and adding a connecting alif; some read وأزينت, but al-Ṭabarī prefers the majority reading by reader consensus.

## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH
[ZAJJAJ:v3p14/r1] author | holds | Zakhraf is the full beauty of a thing; zayyanat is a geminated reading for ‘adorned,’ with tāʾ assimilated into zāy. 
[ZAJJAJ:v3p14/r2] author | reports | The lighter reading azyanat uses form IV, meaning the land produced its adornment. 
[ZAJJAJ:v3p14/r3] author | prefers | He prefers the geminated zayyanat as better Arabic; the lighter form would more naturally be azānat. 
[ZAJJAJ:v3p14/r4] author | holds | The landowners thought they could benefit from or make use of the harvest. 
[ZAJJAJ:v3p15/r1] author | holds | Taghna means ‘be inhabited’; maghānī are dwellings, and the phrase ‘we dwelt in a place’ uses the same root.

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v5p127/r10] author | holds | God’s command means His decree of destruction; ḥaṣīd describes crops cut down and uprooted.
[THALABI:v5p127/r11] author | holds | The phrase ka-an lam taghn means the land had not remained inhabited; its root refers to a place where someone stayed and built.
[THALABI:v5p127/r12] قتادة | reports | The common reading has feminine taghn referring to the earth, while Qatada reads yaghn with the masculine referent al-zukhruf.
[THALABI:v5p127/r5] author | holds | The worldly life is like rain that mingles with earth’s plants: people eat grains, vegetables, and fruits, while livestock graze on herbage.
[THALABI:v5p127/r6] author | holds | The common reading is wa-zzayyanat; Ibn Masʿud’s reading wa-tazayyanat confirms the sense that the earth becomes adorned.
[THALABI:v5p127/r7] أبو عثمان النهدي والضحاك | reports | Abu Uthman al-Nahdi and al-Dahhak read wāzānat on the pattern of ajāzat; ʿAwf says their elders read it likewise.
[THALABI:v5p127/r8] أبو رجاء وأبو العالية والشعبي والحسن والأعرج | reports | These readers pronounce wa-zzayyanat with a separated hamza and light form; Qutrub glosses it as bringing forth its adornment.
[THALABI:v5p127/r9] author | holds | Although the verse says the earth thinks it can control its produce, the pronoun is explained as the plants, the harvest, or the adornment.
