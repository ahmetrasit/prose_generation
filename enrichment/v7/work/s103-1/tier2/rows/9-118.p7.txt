## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v3p398#2/r1] author | holds | ‘The three left behind’ are those whose case was deferred without a ruling until revelation: Kaʿb ibn Mālik, Murāra ibn al-Rabīʿ, and Hilāl ibn Umayya.
[BURSEVI:v3p398#2/r2] author | holds | The earth became narrow when people avoided speaking or even greeting them; they feared dying without the Prophet or believers praying over them.
[BURSEVI:v3p398#2/r3] author | holds | Their souls were so filled with loneliness that no room remained for comfort or joy; saying these were constricted ‘upon them’ stresses their utter distress.
[BURSEVI:v3p398#3/r1] author | holds | They knew there was no refuge from God's anger except turning to Him for forgiveness; ‘they thought’ means ‘they knew,’ since this is praise and praise requires knowledge.
[BURSEVI:v3p398#3/r2] author | holds | Grammatically, an omitted pronoun of ‘the matter’ is the subject of softened anna; the clause fills both objects of ‘they thought,’ with exception from an omitted general term.
[BURSEVI:v3p398#3/r3] بعض المتقدمين | reports | An early saying advises frequent gratitude when blessings accumulate and frequent seeking forgiveness when worries multiply.
[BURSEVI:v3p398#3/r4] author | holds | One immersed in divine unity sees only God in existence and seeks refuge only in Him; visible forms and places are merely causes.
[BURSEVI:v3p398#3/r5] author | reports | Rūmī's Mathnawī compares a visible form to a shadow and urges seeking the reflected person, turning from secondary causes to the ultimate Causer.
[BURSEVI:v3p399/r1] author | holds | ‘Then He turned to them’ means He enabled them to repent; ‘so they might repent’ means they returned from sin.
[BURSEVI:v3p399/r2] author | holds | He distinguishes God's enabling, the person's repentance, and God's acceptance; enabling precedes acceptance by two ranks, so thumma signals rank rather than time.
[BURSEVI:v3p399/r3] author | holds | Alternatively, ‘He turned to them’ means He announced acceptance and ‘so they might repent’ means they became counted among the repentant; thumma then keeps its ordinary temporal sense.
[BURSEVI:v3p399/r4] author | holds | God is repeatedly accepting and merciful, accepting a repentant person even after a hundred returns while favoring people who deserve punishment.
[BURSEVI:v3p399/r5] author | reports | A Persian couplet says repentance needs hope of acceptance and a broken or uncertain vow does not complete sincere repentance.
[BURSEVI:v3p399/r6] author | reports | Some believers who had lagged behind regretted it and caught up with the Prophet.
[BURSEVI:v3p399/r7] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan recounts three cases: one leaves a valuable orchard that held him back, another his family, and a third his love of life; each crosses hardship to rejoin the Prophet.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r1] author | holds | The phrase 'and the three' is coordinated with the mention of the Prophet and shares the verb 'turned'; inserting an implicit 'turned' has been proposed because their repentance differs, but the author finds that unnecessary.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r2] author | holds | The three were left behind in judgment and their case postponed: no excuse was accepted or rejected until revelation came; the wording may be figurative or imply their case, or it may mean that they themselves stayed behind from the campaign.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r3] author | holds | They were Kaʿb b. Malik, Hilal b. Umayya, and Murara b. al-Rabiʿ; Muslim and others call Murara al-Amiri, while many hadith scholars correct this to al-Umari.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r4] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima, Ruzayn b. Hubaysh, and ʿAmr b. ʿUbayd read خلفوا with light lām and explain it as being left behind in Medina or becoming corrupt; ʿAli b. al-Husayn, al-Baqir, Jaʿfar al-Sadiq, and al-Sulami read خالفوا, while al-Aʿmash reads المخلفين.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r5] author | holds | The earth's constriction marks the end of their delayed case: people shunned them under the Prophet's order, and the image means they could not settle in the world despite its breadth; a cited poetic line evokes the fear of the pursued.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r6] author | holds | Their selves are their hearts; the heart's constriction means grief with no room for joy, and the move from constricted earth to constricted selves heightens the rhetoric.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r7] author | holds | They knew there was no refuge from God's displeasure except seeking His forgiveness and repenting to Him; ظنوا here means they knew.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r8] author | reports | God's turning to them may mean enabling them to repent, revealing their acceptance in the Qur'an, or repeatedly returning with mercy so they persist in repentance; another view says He accepted their lapse so that they could repent again without despair.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p41/r9] author | holds | God is repeatedly accepting repentance, even if a person returns to sin a hundred times in one day, and mercifully grants blessings despite their deserving punishment.
