## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p798#2/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reads the opening as a rhetorical question seeking affirmation.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p798/r1] author | holds | ألهاه and أقهاه mean that something occupied or distracted a person; التكاثر is rivalry and boasting in numbers, each group claiming superiority.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p798/r2] author | reports | Banu Abd Manaf and Banu Sahm competed in headcounts; after the former initially outnumbered them, Banu Sahm counted the living and dead and prevailed.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p798/r3] author | holds | The rivalry continued from counting the living to counting the dead in graves, a mocking description of their competition beyond death; some say they literally visited graves to count and name their dead.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p798/r4] author | holds | The rivalry may instead be in wealth and children, distracting people until they die and are buried, after spending life pursuing the world instead of religion and the afterlife; visiting graves is a euphemism for death.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p798/r5] author | holds | Two poetic examples use visiting graves as a figure for dying, including one in which Abu Malik visits graves and becomes the vilest of their visitors.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p518#3/r1] author | holds | Alha means to distract through pleasures, as in idle talk, sounds, and preoccupation with women; the verse rebukes and grieves.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p518#3/r2] ابن عباس وعمران الجوني وأبو صالح | reports | Ibn Abbas, Imran al-Jawni, and Abu Salih read the opening as a question.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p518#3/r3] author | holds | Takathur means boasting competitively about wealth, children, and numbers, a worldly habit escaped only by learned, pious people.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p518#3/r4] الأعشى | reports | Al-Aʿsha's poem contrasts not having the largest pebble count with the glory of being numerous.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p518#3/r5] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says a person's true possessions are only what he ate, wore, or gave away in charity.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:102:1-7/r1] قتادة | reports | The surah is said to address Jews who competed in numbers among their clans and died in error while distracted by rivalry.
[TABRISI:102:1-7/r2] أبو بريدة | reports | Another report says the surah was revealed about an Ansari clan competing in numbers.
[TABRISI:102:1-7/r4] author | holds | Rivalry in wealth and children distracted people from obedience to God and remembrance of the afterlife.
[TABRISI:102:1-8/r3] author | holds | Ilhāʾ means diverting someone toward pastime, while pastime is turning toward what desire invites.
[TABRISI:102:1-8/r4] author | holds | Takāthur is boasting about having more merits; ziyārah is visiting a place without settling there, also the root of deceptive imitation.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v32p72/r1] author | holds | Ilhāʾ is turning someone toward diversion; diversion follows desire and entails neglecting something else, so ألهاني means distracted or made me forget.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p72/r2] author | reports | Takāthur means boasting competitively about wealth, status, and distinctions; Abu Muslim says its morphology can express mutual competition, effort to increase, or simply becoming numerous. | mentions 18:34
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73#2/r4] author | reports | Another interpretation makes the rivalry about wealth; a report from ʿAbd Allah ibn al-Shikhkhir's father says a person's wealth is only what he consumed, wore, or gave away.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73/r2] author | holds | The verse condemns competition in every worldly pleasure, including numbers, wealth, status, relatives, supporters, and armies.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73/r3] author | reports | ألهاكم can be a statement about the people's distraction or a reproachful question asking whether competition distracted them.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73/r4] author | holds | Competition is not always blameworthy: the verse about pilgrimage water and the Kaaba key reports rivals boasting over religious services before ʿAli's claim of fighting unbelief. | mentions 9:19
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73/r5] author | holds | A person may display obedience and good character when it could encourage others; therefore competition in knowledge, devotion, and virtue is praiseworthy. | mentions 93:11
[RAZI-FULL:v32p73/r6] author | holds | The definite article in التكاثر refers to familiar worldly accumulation, not every kind of striving; that distraction obstructs worship, while competition in virtue is good.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r1] author | reports | Continuing the report introduced in the preceding segment, the Prophet says that a person would desire two valleys of gold and only dust fills his mouth, while God accepts repentance.

[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r3] ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-Arabi calls that an authentic, explicit report whose interpretive significance commentators overlooked.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2#2/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas reports that the Prophet defined wealth rivalry as gathering money unlawfully, withholding its due, and hoarding it in containers.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r1] author | holds | Alhaka means occupied or distracted; rivalry in wealth and numbers diverts people from obedience until death and burial.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r11] عمرو بن دينار | reports | Amr ibn Dinar swore that the surah descended about merchants.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r12] شبيان عن قتادة | reports | Shayban transmits from Qatada that it descended concerning the People of the Book.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r14] مطرف عن أبيه | reports | In Muslim, Mutarrif's father hears the Prophet explain that the only wealth a person truly keeps is what he consumes, wears out, or gives away; the rest is left behind.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r2] الشاعر | reports | A poem uses alhaytu to mean I distracted or occupied her.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r3] author | reports | Another gloss takes alhaka as made you forget; takathur can mean rivalry in possessions and children, as Ibn Abbas and al-Hasan say.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r4] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the rivalry is boasting about tribes and clans.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r5] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says the distraction is being preoccupied with livelihood and trade.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r6] author | holds | The root l-h-y means to forget, stop mentioning, or turn away from something; alha means occupied, and lahha means entertained or diverted.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r7] مقاتل وقتادة وغيرهما | reports | Some say it descended about Jews boasting that their lineages outnumbered one another until they died in error.
[QURTUBI-FULL:102:1-2/r8] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says the surah descended about a clan of the Ansar.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:102:1-2/r1] author | holds | ألهاكم means it distracted you into amusement, originally turning someone from attention; التكاثر is boasting about abundance.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:102:1-2/r3] author | holds | Alternatively, worldly competition in wealth and children distracts people until death and burial; visiting graves is a metaphor for dying.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p354#12/r1] author | holds | Rivalry and striving to outdo one another in accumulating wealth and children distracts people from obeying God.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:102:1-4/r1] author | holds | Rivalry in accumulation distracts people with fleeting sensory and imagined pleasures, wasting innate potential, purity, intellect, and lasting spiritual perfection for worldly boasting.
[QASHANI:102:1-4/r2] author | holds | Competition over wealth, children, and ancestry can extend even to boasting about long-dead ancestors’ bones, driven by imagination and concealing illusion.
