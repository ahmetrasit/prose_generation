## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p252#2/r1] author | holds | “The day” is governed by “you will be informed,” or invites the listener to remember the day everyone is gathered.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p252#2/r2] author | holds | Mutual loss is like losing a trade: the blessed occupy places the damned would have had, and the damned lose places they might have had; this is true loss, unlike worldly loss.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p252#2/r3] author | holds | “Whoever believes and does good” describes a source of action; the Medinan and Syrian readings use first-person plural for “We erase” and “We admit.”

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:64:9-11/r1] author | holds | Worldly possessions are inevitably transient, so their loss is no real fraud; true loss is wasting enduring capital, the light of readiness and perfection, and regret appears on Judgment Day.
[QASHANI:64:9-11/r2] author | holds | Wasting innate readiness and its light is absolute loss; keeping readiness but failing to reach its full potential is relative loss beside the perfected person, like a lost trade. | mentions 2:16
[QASHANI:64:9-11/r3] author | holds | Imitative faith, avoiding sins, and ordinary obedience expiate sins and lead to gardens of the self according to one’s deeds.
[QASHANI:64:9-11/r4] author | holds | Verified faith, renouncing one’s qualities, and following God’s qualities expiate psychic traits and lead to gardens of the heart; witnessed faith and vigilance about existence lead to the spirit’s gardens.
[QASHANI:64:9-11/r5] author | holds | True faith and vigilance about selfhood and witnessing its annihilation expiate residual ego and its shifts, leading to gardens of the divine Essence.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:64:9/r1] النحاس | reports | Al-Nahhas attaches يوم يجمعكم to لتنبؤن.
[SAMIN-DURR:64:9/r2] الحوفي | reports | Al-Hawfi attaches يوم يجمعكم to خبير.
[SAMIN-DURR:64:9/r3] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari supplies an implicit اذكر, making يوم يجمعكم a direct object.
[SAMIN-DURR:64:9/r4] أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa attaches the phrase to an implied clause: you will differ in standing on the day He gathers you.
[SAMIN-DURR:64:9/r5] العامة | reports | The common reading has fatḥa on yāʾ and ḍamma on ʿayn; Abu Amr is also reported with sukūn and an assimilated or colored ʿayn, as in 67:20. | mentions 67:20
[SAMIN-DURR:64:9/r6] يعقوب وسلام وزيد بن علي والشعبي | reports | These readers use the first-person plural نجمعكم, We gather you.
[SAMIN-DURR:64:9/r7] author | holds | التغابن is a reciprocal form from commercial loss, metaphorically taking something below its value; alternatively الغبن means concealment, as in a hidden sale, and the form can describe a one-sided action.
[SAMIN-DURR:64:9/r8] في التفسير | reports | The loss is explained by wrongful wealth passing to an heir who obeys God with it, so the first owner enters Hell and the heir Paradise.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p137/r1] author | holds | The Gathering is Resurrection, when the first and last generations assemble on one plain, hear the caller, and are all visible, as other verses say. | mentions 11:103, 56:49, 56:50

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p137/r3] مقاتل بن حيان | reports | There is no greater loss than some people entering Paradise while others are consigned to Hell. | mentions 64:10

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p12#2/r1] author | holds | The Day of Gathering brings every created community together for reckoning and recompense, from which some emerge as winners and others as losers.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p12#2/r2] author | holds | The Day of Mutual Loss is the only real loss because it is permanent and exposes each person’s hidden deficit before the whole assembly.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p12#2/r3] author | holds | The root gh-b-n concerns concealed loss, as in hidden bodily folds and a seller’s unnoticed loss in a transaction.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p12#2/r4] author | holds | Those entering Paradise are shown the place they would have occupied in Hell to increase gratitude, while those entering Hell see their lost place in Paradise and regret.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p12#2/r5] author | holds | Every unbeliever is cheated by rejecting faith, and believers lose through falling short in good works; worldly injustice cannot truly deprive the faithful.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p12#2/r6] author | holds | Remembering this day gives the strongest motive to obey commands and avoid prohibitions, lest one lose eternal reward.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p13/r1] author | holds | Faith must be renewed and confirmed by good deeds; God then conceals sins and admits believers to gardens with flowing rivers forever.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p13/r2] author | holds | The plural wording “khālidīn” emphasizes that all included believers share everlasting life, and “abadan” removes any doubt about its duration.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p13/r3] author | holds | Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir read the verbs with first-person plural nūn, stressing that only God has power to forgive and admit people.
[BIQAI-FULL:v8p13/r4] author | holds | “That is the great triumph” encompasses every benefit and removes every harm.

[BURSEVI:v10p31#2/r6] author | reports | Another report says a generous man was protected from Hell’s fire in an enclosure because of his generosity.
