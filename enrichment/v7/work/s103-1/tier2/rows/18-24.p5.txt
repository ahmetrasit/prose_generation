## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r1] أبو البقاء | reports | إلا may scope over the prohibition: do not say “I will do it tomorrow” unless God permits that utterance.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r2] أبو البقاء | reports | Or it may scope over فاعل: do not claim you will do it without adding إن شاء الله; a third analysis is a disconnected exception.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r3] أبو البقاء | reports | أن يشاء may be an exception with وقت omitted (“except when God wills”), or circumstantial with قول omitted (“while saying إن شاء الله”).
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r4] author | reports | A further estimate supplies بـ: speak while bound by God's will, i.e. while saying إن شاء الله.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r5] الزمخشري | rejects | Al-Zamakhshari rejects scope over فاعل because that expresses legitimate contingency, outside the prohibition's intended point.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r6] الزمخشري | reports | He attaches it to the prohibition as either permission to say the statement or saying it contingent on God's will; a third view makes it mean “never,” as at 7:89. | mentions 7:89
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r7] ابن عطية | rejects | Ibn Atiyya rejects the perpetuity interpretation after transmitting it from al-Tabari and others, without explaining the flaw.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r8] الشيخ | holds | The apparent exception cannot be part of the forbidden statement: “unless God wills” is true and cannot be forbidden.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:24/r9] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya supplies omitted words: unless you say “unless God wills” or “if God wills”; the command is to mention divine will.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p148/r1] author | holds | When resolving to do something later, the Messenger is taught to make it conditional on God's will: God knows the unseen, what occurred and will occur, and even what would have happened had it not happened.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p148/r2] النبي | reports | The Prophet reports that Solomon vowed to visit seventy women that night, with narrations giving ninety or one hundred; an angel told him to say God willing, but he did not, and only one woman bore half a person.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p148/r3] النبي | reports | The Prophet said that had Solomon said God willing, he would not have broken his oath and would have attained his wish, with all sons becoming horsemen for God's cause.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p148/r4] author | reports | The passage was also revealed after the Prophet promised to answer a question about the Sleepers the next day; revelation was delayed fifteen days, as recounted earlier in the sura.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p149/r1] أبو العالية والحسن البصري | reports | They interpret remembering the Lord after forgetting as remembering to add the exception, then saying it when one recalls.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p149/r2] ابن عباس | reports | A person who swears may add an exception even up to a year later; Ibn Abbas applied the verse to this, in a report transmitted through Mujahid and al-A'mash from Layth ibn Abi Sulaym.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p149/r3] ابن جرير | prefers | Ibn Abbas means that one who forgot to say God willing may say it when remembering, even a year later and after breaking the oath; this renews the recommended practice but does not remove the breach or its expiation. Ibn Kathir calls this correct.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p149/r4] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima reads remembering the Lord after forgetting as remembering when angry; Ibn Kathir calls this an interpretation by necessary implication.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p149/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Two al-Tabarani reports explain the instruction as remembering to say God willing: one comes through Jabir ibn Zayd and one through Mujahid.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p149/r6] ابن عباس | reports | A further report says the instruction to add the exception when remembered was specific to the Prophet; others may only qualify an oath in connection with making it. Al-Tabarani notes this route is unique through al-Walid ibn Muslim from Abd al-Aziz ibn al-Husayn.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p150/r1] author | holds | Another possible reading is to remember God whenever one forgets something in speech: forgetfulness begins with Satan, as in the attendant's explanation of forgetting the fish, while God's remembrance drives Satan away and brings recollection. | mentions 18:63
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p150/r2] author | holds | When asked about something unknown, ask God and turn to Him for help in reaching the soundest guidance; Ibn Kathir notes that other interpretations were also given without detailing them.
