## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v27p152#2/r1] قتادة، الحسن | reports | Qatāda and al-Ḥasan take the sites of the stars as their settings, since their disappearance points to an enduring, unchanging Agent and the time is associated with worship and mercy.
[ALUSI-FULL:v27p152#2/r10] ابن عباس، أهل المدينة، حمزة، الكسائي | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, the Medinans, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī read the singular mawqiʿ, intended collectively.
[ALUSI-FULL:v27p152#2/r2] أبو هريرة | reports | Al-Bukhārī and Muslim transmit a raised report from Abū Hurayra that God descends in the last third of the night and calls people to pray, ask, and seek forgiveness.
[ALUSI-FULL:v27p152#2/r3] الحسن | reports | A second report from al-Ḥasan takes the sites as the stars' scattering on the Resurrection; their fall need not occur all at once and displays divine majesty while confirming resurrection.
[ALUSI-FULL:v27p152#2/r4] أبو جعفر، أبو عبد الله | reports | Abū Jaʿfar and Abū ʿAbd Allāh, from their forefathers, identify the sites as where stars fall after jinn eavesdroppers are pursued.
[ALUSI-FULL:v27p152#2/r5] author | reports | Another view means the lunar mansions that pre-Islamic Arabs believed caused rain; the occasion report is not decisive, since ordinary settings could also be intended.
[ALUSI-FULL:v27p152#2/r6] قتادة | reports | Qatāda, in reports from ʿAbd al-Razzāq and Ibn Jarīr, says the sites are the stars' stations and paths, with “falling” meaning descent, a sign of God's immense power and wisdom.
[ALUSI-FULL:v27p152#2/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās and others say the sites are the occasions of Qurʾānic revelation; reports say it descended all at once to the lowest heaven, then in installments over years.
