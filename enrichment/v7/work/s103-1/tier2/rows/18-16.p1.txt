## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p661#2/r1] author | holds | The youths' words are one another's speech after their resolve to flee with their religion; wa-mā yaʿbudūn is coordinated with the attached pronoun, meaning they left their people and their objects of worship.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p661#2/r2] author | reports | The exception for God may be connected, since they acknowledged the Creator while associating others as Meccans did, or disconnected; alternatively, God interjects that the youths worshipped none but Him.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p661#2/r3] author | reports | Marfiqan is read with fatḥa or kasra on the mīm and means something beneficial to rely upon.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p661#2/r4] author | reports | Their confidence in finding relief may express trust and strong hope in God, or come from a prophet who told them, or from one of the youths being a prophet.

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p501/r4] author | prefers | The advice in verse 16 is best read as what they said to one another when they resolved to leave; if verse 14 means an audience with the king, an omitted 'some said to others' is needed here.

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p501/r8] author | holds | The verse pictures their mutual counsel: having separated from the unbelievers, they would take refuge in the cave and rely on God to spread mercy and provide ease; the prayer concerns worldly life.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:13-14/r1] author | reports | For marfiqan, people of Medina, Ibn ʿĀmir, al-Aʿshā, and al-Barjamī from Abū Bakr read fatḥa on mīm and kasra on fāʾ; the other readers reverse those vowels.
[TABRISI:18:13-14/r2] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj and Quṭrub acknowledge both vowel forms for ‘benefit/support’ and ‘joint’; kasra on the arm-joint form is more idiomatic.
[TABRISI:18:13-14/r3] أبو الحسن | reports | Abū al-Ḥasan says the fatḥa form means a thing used for benefit, while the kasra form is a noun; it may be a dialect or a verbal noun form.
[TABRISI:18:13-14/r5] author | holds | Iʿtizāl means withdrawing or distancing oneself; a line of poetry uses taʿazzul for withdrawal, and the name Muʿtazila was applied to ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd’s circle because they left al-Ḥasan’s gathering.
[TABRISI:18:15-16/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says Tamlikhā, the youths’ leader, spoke: if they left their people and idols, they would still not abandon worship of God.
[TABRISI:18:15-16/r4] author | reports | If some youths worshiped God alongside the idols, the exception ‘except God’ is connected; if all worshiped only idols, it is a disconnected exception.
[TABRISI:18:15-16/r5] author | holds | They should make the cave their refuge; God will spread mercy and arrange ease, gentleness, and what they need for their livelihood.
[TABRISI:18:15-16/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the arrangement as God making their fear of the ruler and his injustice easy to bear, with gentleness and relief.
[TABRISI:18:15-16/r7] author | holds | The passage shows the high status of migration for religion and the wrongness of staying in an unbelieving land when remaining requires professing disbelief.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p84#2/r1] author | holds | The youths tell one another that withdrawing from the people and their objects of worship leaves their worship of God intact; they should make the cave their refuge.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p84#2/r2] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ treats the cave command as the answer to idhā, like “when you do this, then do that”; it means go there and make it your abode.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p84#2/r3] author | holds | God will spread His mercy over them and prepare ease in their affairs.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p84#2/r4] نافع وابن عامر وعاصم | reports | Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, and ʿĀṣim in one transmission read marfaq with fatḥa on mīm and kasra on fāʾ; the others use kasra on mīm and fatḥa on fāʾ.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p84#2/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ regards both marfaq pronunciations as dialect forms derived from irtifāq.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p84#2/r6] الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisāʾī accepts only the kasra-on-mīm reading for the body's elbow, while al-Farrāʾ accepts it for both the elbow and an affair.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p84#2/r7] author | reports | Another explanation says both forms are dialects, with fatḥa more regular by analogy and kasra more common; marfaq can mean a means of support.
