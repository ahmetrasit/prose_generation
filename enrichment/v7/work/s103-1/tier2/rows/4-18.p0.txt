## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v6p515#2/r1] author | holds | Those who repent before death’s throes are accepted; someone who waits until death overwhelms understanding and says “I repent now” does so without genuine repentance, like a deceiver. | mentions 4:17
[TAB-FULL:v6p516/r1] author | holds | The verse denies repentance to persistent sinners who wait until death is present, see the angels coming for their souls, and speak while death’s throes have overcome them; this is not a genuine state of repentance.

[TAB-FULL:v6p516/r3] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says repentance is not accepted once death has become clear to the person.
[TAB-FULL:v6p516/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says that the person who says “I repent now” when death arrives has no repentance with God.
[TAB-FULL:v6p516/r5] عبد الله بن عمرو | reports | Abd Allah ibn Amr says repentance is accepted even shortly before death, down to an hour or a moment, and says he heard this from the Prophet.
[TAB-FULL:v6p516/r6] إبراهيم | reports | Ibrahim transmits the saying that repentance remains open until the dying person is seized by the throat.
[TAB-FULL:v6p518/r1] الربيع | reports | Al-Rabi says the passage’s first clause concerns believers, its middle clause about sinners at death concerns hypocrites, and its final clause concerns unbelievers.
[TAB-FULL:v6p518/r2] سفيان | reports | Sufyan says the clause about those who delay repentance until death concerns Muslims, as the verse separately names those who die unbelievers.
[TAB-FULL:v6p518/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the verse first addressed believers but was superseded by 4:48, which bars forgiveness for those dying as unbelievers while leaving monotheists to God’s will. | mentions 4:48
[TAB-FULL:v6p518/r4] author | prefers | Al-Tabari prefers Sufyan’s view that the first clause concerns Muslims: hypocrites are unbelievers, so identifying them here would make the separate clause about dying unbelievers redundant; the wording distinguishes sinners from those called unbelievers.
[TAB-FULL:v6p520/r1] author | holds | The phrase “those who die unbelievers” is coordinated in the genitive with “those who commit sins”; they are farther from repentance because they remain in unbelief, and God has prepared painful punishment for them.
[TAB-FULL:v6p520/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas explains that people who die as unbelievers are farther from repentance.
[TAB-FULL:v6p520/r3] author | reports | Basran grammarians derive أعتدنا from العتاد, meaning “prepared”; Kufan grammarians say أعتدنا and أعددنا are synonymous, while أليما means painful.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p390/r1] الوعيدية | reports | The Waʿidiyya use this verse to argue that sinful Muslims who postpone repentance until death join disbelievers in afterlife punishment, citing the final clause.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p390/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas in Ataa's report interprets the sinful acts here as shirk; Ikrimah likewise transmits from him that the people are polytheists.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p390/r3] الربيع بن أنس | reports | Al-Rabi ibn Anas says the one saying 'I repent now' is a hypocrite, as the verse then mentions disbelievers.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p390/r4] سعيد بن جبير | reports | Saʿid ibn Jubayr assigns 4:17 to believers, 4:18 to hypocrites, and the clause about dying as disbelievers to unbelievers. | mentions 4:17
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p390/r5] author | rejects | Because the Companion and Successor authorities who witnessed revelation apply the verse to disbelievers or hypocrites, the author rejects extending it to Muslims who pray.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p390/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says 4:48 later clarified that those dying in unbelief are denied forgiveness while monotheists remain subject to God's will and hope of pardon. | mentions 4:48
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p390/r7] author | rejects | The author denies that this is abrogation: abrogation is not attributed to Ibn Abbas here; 4:48 specifies that the first verse's apparent generality excludes monotheists. | mentions 4:48
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p392/r1] author | holds | The phrase 'nor those who die as disbelievers' is genitive by coordination with the earlier noun; it denies them repentance in the afterlife.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p392/r2] author | holds | After death, repentance is not accepted because responsibility ends and the promised afterlife reward and punishment are seen; this also explains rejection at death.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p392/r3] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says the dying person repents at a time when he can no longer act to fulfill repentance.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p392/r4] الليث | reports | Al-Layth explains aʿtadnā as prepared and readied; related forms describe what is present or reserved, a stored grooming case, equipment, and a horse made ready to ride.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p392/r5] الأزهري | reports | Al-Azhari presents two derivations: a root ʿ-t-d with a separate form, or doubled dāl as in aʿdadtu; he calls an independent ʿatad alongside doubled ʿadad the soundest account.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v6p392/r6] امرؤ القيس | reports | Imru al-Qays's wording 'I readied for war' with doubled dāl is cited as evidence for the aʿdadt derivation.
