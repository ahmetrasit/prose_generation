## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p153#3/r1] author | holds | The verse swears by the night as a genus, when its people are still; this may be a figurative attribution or an understood reference to its people.

## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:93:2-4#2/r3] author | holds | Abandonment can express either grace or chastisement; the contrast of forenoon and night hints that the Prophet remains under divine mercy, while the denial of hatred excludes wrath.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r1] author | holds | The phrase can describe the settled middle of night, after some time has elapsed, when darkness has reached its depth, covers what it will, and silence falls.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r10] author | holds | This stillness includes physical and spiritual relief, rest from distress, or the passing of grief as night ends.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r11] author | holds | Night can symbolize life's suffering, death's grief, cessation of revelation and knowledge, and spiritual constriction; here the focus is respite during that darkness or the joy that comes when revelation resumes.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r12] author | holds | Duha expresses the bright, youthful joy of life and revealed knowledge, while the settled night signals rest before a promised renewal; this relation fittingly introduces the assurance that the Lord has not abandoned Muhammad.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r2] author | reports | Other possibilities are the onset of darkness and covering, the stilling of the wind, or the night drawing to an end; each contrasts or corresponds with duha as grief, joy, or grief's passing.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r3] author | holds | Sajā derives from sujuww, meaning for something to settle and become still; bahr sājī is calm sea, tarf sājī a still eye, and layl sājī a quiet, windless night.
[ELMALILI:93:2-4/r4] author | holds | The same root yields sajiyya for a settled disposition and tasjiya for covering, so the night may be understood as quiet, fully dark and covering, or passing away.
