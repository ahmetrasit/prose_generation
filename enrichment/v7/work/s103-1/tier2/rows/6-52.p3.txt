## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p294#2/r1] author | holds | The people addressed were poor, socially weak believers, including Bilal, Ammar, Ibn Umm Abd, Murthid al-Ghanawi, Khabbab, Suhayb, Subayh, Dhul-Shimalayn, and al-Miqdad.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p294#2/r2] author | reports | Some unbelievers said their status and rank prevented them from mixing with the poor; if Muhammad expelled them, the elites would follow him. A report also attributes the advice to Abu Talib, and Quraysh leaders gathered to press it; the author thinks the proposal was likely a ruse.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p294#2/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says some unbelievers asked to move the poor believers out of the front prayer row so they could take their places and believe.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p294#2/r4] خباب بن الأرت عن الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari transmits that al-Aqraʿ ibn Habis and similar Arab notables asked for a separate meeting without slaves or allies and for a written agreement; Muhammad considered it before the verse came down.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p296/r1] المهدوي | reports | Al-Mahdawi says all the pronouns in the passage refer to the believers; Hasan says their accounting concerns their deeds, as most interpreters hold.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p296/r2] author | holds | The first min is partitive and the second is emphatic; the two fa-verbs follow the prior negation and prohibition, and being among wrongdoers means putting things in the wrong place.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:6:52-53#2/r1] author | reports | A report describes al-Aqraʿ b. Ḥābis, ʿUyayna b. Ḥiṣn, and other leaders disparaging the poor believers and asking the Prophet to dismiss them when Arab delegations came; he agrees, but Gabriel reveals the prohibition and the Prophet tears up their proposed written pledge.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#2/r2] النبي | reports | After the revelation, the Prophet tells the believers that their Lord has prescribed mercy, then quotes God's instruction to keep patient company with them and says he will live and die among them. | mentions 18:28
[TABRISI:6:52-53#2/r3] author | holds | God forbids His Messenger from acceding to the polytheists' demand to expel the believers who worship Him at dawn and evening.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r1] author | holds | The cited words from Noah say that their reckoning rests with the Lord alone, supporting the interpretation that God alone will judge the believers deeds.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r10] author | holds | The lām in the outcome clause marks consequence: God acted so they might be patient and grateful, and their question was the eventual outcome.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r11] author | holds | The question whether God knows the grateful is a confirming question, as illustrated by Jarīr poetry.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r2] author | holds | The polytheists despised the poor believers because of their poverty and devotion, wanting them set below themselves in the gathering; the verse rebuts this social contempt.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r3] author | holds | A second interpretation says the Prophet is not responsible for believers livelihood, so poverty is no reason to tire of them and expel them.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r4] author | holds | God provides both the poor believers and the Prophet, so neither party depends on the other for provision.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r5] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd interprets the warning as saying the Prophet would wrong the believers if he expelled them; Ibn ʿAbbās says such expulsion would harm the Prophet himself through disobedience.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r6] ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbārī says the warning is severe because the Prophet had considered preferring wealthy leaders to attract their followers, intending good rather than contempt for the poor, but God forbade it.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r7] author | holds | God tests wealthy leaders through poor believers: a proud notable may refuse faith when a poorer person has preceded him, lest he lose precedence.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r8] author | holds | God is described as testing though He needs no test because He treats people as a tester would; the outcome clause means the affair resulted in their asking whether God favored these believers.
[TABRISI:6:52-53#3/r9] الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī interprets the test as the difficult obligation imposed on Arab nobles to believe and give precedence to those who believed first; the clause lets believers accept the Prophet's honor toward them rather than denying God's favor.
[TABRISI:6:52-53/r1] ابن عامر | reports | Ibn ʿĀmir reads the temporal phrase with wāw as al-ghudwa throughout the Qurʾan; the other readers read al-ghadāh with alif.
[TABRISI:6:52-53/r2] أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī argues al-ghadāh is preferable because it is an indefinite noun that can take the article, whereas ghudwa is a proper name and does not become indefinite; the grammarians' reports also support ghudāh.
[TABRISI:6:52-53/r3] الخليل | reports | The reading ghudwa is also defended: al-Khalīl allows it as a temporal adverb like ḍuḥwa, and other time names can appear definite without the article.
[TABRISI:6:52-53/r4] author | holds | “Then you would expel them” answers the preceding negation, and “then you would be among the wrongdoers” is subjunctive as the result of the prohibition not to expel.
[TABRISI:6:52-53/r5] عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd reports Quraysh leaders passing the Prophet while he sat with poor Muslims—Ṣuhayb, Khabbāb, Bilāl, and ʿAmmār—and asking him to expel them so the leaders might follow him.
[TABRISI:6:52-53/r6] سلمان | reports | Salmān and Khabbāb say the verse was revealed concerning them.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p44/r1] سعد بن أبي وقاص | reports | The verse concerned six poor Companions, including Sa'd, Ibn Masud, Suhayb, Ammar, al-Miqdad, and Bilal; Quraysh demanded the Prophet expel them rather than be seen following them.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p44/r2] خباب بن الأرت | reports | Khabbab says poor believers learned from the Prophet morning and evening; al-Aqra and Uyayna demanded they be removed, then pressed for a written agreement, until Gabriel brought the warning against expelling them and the Prophet welcomed them. | mentions 6:53, 6:54
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p44/r3] ابن مسعود | reports | Quraysh leaders passing by the Prophet and poor Companions asked whether he expected them to become followers of those men.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p44/r4] عكرمة | reports | Nobles of Abd Manaf asked Abu Talib to make Muhammad expel clients and slaves; Umar suggested trying it, but the verse came and Umar apologized.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p44/r5] أبو صالح عن ابن عباس | reports | The verse concerns the clients Bilal, Suhayb, Khabbab, Ammar, Mihja, Salman, Amir ibn Fuhayra, and Salim, and another verse also addressed them.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p44/r6] العوفي عن ابن عباس | reports | Some nobles offered belief if the Prophet delayed the poor believers and let them pray behind the nobles; this report seeks moving them from the prayer row, unlike the others' request to remove them from his gathering.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r1] ابن عمر وابن عباس | reports | Calling upon their Lord means the obligatory prayers.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r10] author | holds | Morning stands for the whole day and evening for the night; if a person's daytime worship is sincere, nighttime worship is even purer.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid glosses the phrase as the five daily prayers.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r3] مجاهد وقتادة | reports | Another report from Mujahid and Qatada identifies the prayers as dawn and afternoon.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r4] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil says prayer at that time was two units at dawn and two at evening, before the five daily prayers were prescribed.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r5] إبراهيم النخعي | reports | Another interpretation of calling on God is remembering Him.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r6] الضحاك | reports | Another interpretation is worshipping God.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r7] أبو جعفر | reports | Another interpretation is learning the Qur'an morning and evening.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v3p46/r8] الزجاج | reports | Another interpretation is praying to God with monotheism, sincerity, and worship.

Farra sees ghudwah as inherently definite, Abu Ali says ghadah can take the article while ghudwah is a proper name, and al-Khalil permits it as a time word like duha. | mentions 18:28
