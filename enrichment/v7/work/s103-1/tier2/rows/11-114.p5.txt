## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p214/r1] الليث | reports | Al-Layth defines زلفة as a portion of the first part of night and gives زلف as its plural.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p214/r2] ثعلب | reports | Thaʿlab says زلف are the first hours of night and زلفة is singular.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p214/r3] أبو عبيدة، والأخفش، وابن قتيبة | reports | They gloss زلف as the hours and periods of night, each hour being a زلفة; al-Ajjaj's verse illustrates the word's repeated plural form.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p214/r4] author | holds | زلفة derives from nearness and approach, as shown by the related forms meaning to bring near and draw close.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r1] author | reports | A report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim says the verse was revealed after a man had relations with a non-maḥram woman short of intercourse.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r10] author | reports | A further view takes Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr as the boundaries and dawn, Maghrib, and ʿIshāʾ as night prayers, apparently grouping them by recitation aloud or quietly.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r2] author | reports | Another account says the verse predated the incident and the Prophet applied it to that man; when asked whether it was only for him, he said it was for everyone.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r3] author | holds | Addressing the Prophet directly in commands to do good, while directing prohibitions to the community, is a mark of eloquence.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r4] author | reports | The commanded prayer is the obligatory prayer; إقامة means maintaining it continuously, performing it fully, or performing it at its best times.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r5] author | holds | طرفي النهار is an adverbial accusative; because each طرف belongs to the thing it bounds, the apparent referents are dawn and afternoon.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r6] author | holds | The fasting rule that dawn belongs to daytime supports dawn as one boundary: deliberate eating or intercourse after dawn invalidates the fast and entails makeup and expiation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r7] الحسن وقتادة والضحاك | reports | They identify the two daytime prayers as dawn and ʿAṣr, and the night approaches as Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ, leaving Ẓuhr outside this verse.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r8] مجاهد ومحمد بن كعب | reports | They assign the first boundary to dawn and the second to Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr, with Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ at night; the passage says dawn is not included in this verse.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p221#2/r9] ابن عباس والحسن | reports | They identify the boundaries as dawn and Maghrib and the night prayer as ʿIshāʾ, excluding Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r1] الطبري | prefers | Al-Ṭabarī prefers Ibn ʿAbbās’s dawn-and-Maghrib interpretation; Abū Ḥayyān objects that Maghrib belongs to night, so calling it a daytime boundary requires metaphor.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r10] author | reports | Hasanāt can mean all good deeds, especially obligatory prayers and Ramadan fasting; sayyiʾāt in this reading means minor sins, as supported by the sound report about avoiding major sins.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r11] جمهور المتأولين من الصحابة والتابعين | reports | Most early interpreters identify the good deeds specifically as the five prayers; Mālik adopts this interpretation, and ʿUthmān illustrates it while performing ablution.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r12] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid interprets the good deeds as the formulas of remembrance: glorifying and praising God, proclaiming his oneness and greatness, and declaring reliance on him.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r13] author | holds | These interpretations should be understood as examples; the five prayers are the greatest deeds.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r14] author | holds | Minor sins are erased only when one repents and does not persist in them, a position stated explicitly by the most discerning legal theorists.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r15] author | holds | Erasing sins means expiating minor sins, not making past acts cease to have happened.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r16] author | reports | Another explanation is that good deeds graciously help people leave sins rather than erase sins already committed, as prayer restrains indecency and wrongdoing. | mentions 29:45
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r17] author | prefers | The demonstrative ذلك most apparently points to the nearest command: establishing prayer at these times as a reminder for those who take heed.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r18] author | reports | Other referents for ذلك are the news that good deeds erase sins, earlier commands and prohibitions, or the Qurʾān; al-Zamakhsharī gives the earlier counsel as his view and al-Ṭabarī points to this sūrah's commands.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r2] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī takes dawn as the morning prayer, Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr as afternoon prayers, and Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ as the night approaches.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r3] author | rejects | Calling the period after noon ʿashiyy does not make Ẓuhr a boundary, since the command names the day's two boundaries rather than morning and afternoon.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r4] الجمهور | reports | The majority read zulafan with an open lām.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r5] طلحة وعيسى البصرة وابن أبي إسحاق وأبو جعفر | reports | These readers use a damma on the lām, as though the word were a singular noun.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r6] ابن محيصن ومجاهد | reports | They read the lām quiescent; a further transmission from them has zulfa on the fuʿlā pattern, a feminine singular meaning a station or degree.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r7] author | holds | The plural readings treat the word as successive stations or as a collective noun; zulafā is equivalent to one zalfa.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r8] author | holds | The apparent syntax coordinates the night approaches with the two daytime boundaries as another bounded period.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p222/r9] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī explains zulaf as the part of the day near nightfall; another interpretation makes it night prayers of devotion and coordinates it with الصلاة instead.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114#2/r1] ثعلب | reports | Thaʿlab defines زلفة as the first hours of the night.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114#2/r2] الأخفش وابن قتيبة | reports | Al-Akhfash and Ibn Qutayba use زلف for the night hours generally, with every hour counting as a zulfa.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114#2/r3] العجاج | reports | Al-Ajjaj's verse uses زلفا زلفا for successive portions of night.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114#2/r4] author | holds | The root conveys nearness: أزلفه فازدلف means to bring near and draw close, also used in الشعراء 26:64 and the cited hadith. | mentions 26:64
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114#2/r5] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib says zulfa can mean rank or favor, can euphemistically denote punishment like بشارة, and gives the related meanings of steps and the name Muzdalifa.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114#2/r6] author | holds | من الليل is an adjective modifying زلفا.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114/r1] author | prefers | طرفي النهار is an adverb governed by أقم; attaching it to الصلاة as the times when prayer occurs is weaker.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114/r2] author | holds | طرف is not inherently a temporal adverb, but acquires that case after إضافة to a time expression, as in أول النهار and نصف الليل.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114/r3] العامة | reports | The common reading زلفا has zay with damma and lam with fatha, a plural of زلفة with a still lam.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114/r4] أبو جعفر وابن أبي إسحاق | reports | Their doubled-vowel reading can be a plural of زلفة with vowel harmony, a singular on the pattern of عنق, or a plural of زليف.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114/r5] مجاهد وابن محيصن | reports | They read زلفا with a still lam, either as a shortened vocalized form or as an original collective noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114/r6] مجاهد وابن محيصن | reports | They also read زلفى as a singular feminine adjective for a near rank or hour; al-Zamakhshari equates it with زلفة.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114/r7] author | prefers | زلفا most clearly coordinates with طرفي as a time adverb for the near hours of night; another parse coordinates it with الصلاة as an object.
[SAMIN-DURR:11:114/r8] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari allows coordination with الصلاة, meaning night prayers that draw worshippers near to God.
