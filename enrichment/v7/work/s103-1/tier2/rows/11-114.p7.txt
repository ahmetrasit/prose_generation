## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r1] author | holds | Sins are impurities and acts of obedience purify; ablution washes away sins with its drops, so some jurists considered used ablution cloth impure and disliked praying in it.
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r2] author | reports | A report has God tell Moses that each drop of ablution water used by Aḥmad and his community earns a garden as broad as the sky.
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r3] الحافظ | reports | Ḥāfiẓ's Persian couplet praises prayer and devoted appeal purified by tears and heart's blood.
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r4] author | holds | The highest good deed is knowledge of God, following monotheism, and opposing the lower self; remembrance purifies souls and hearts, strengthens obedience, and defeats Satan's schemes.
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r5] النبي | reports | When companions ask whether ‘There is no god but God’ is a good deed, the Prophet says it is the best of good deeds.
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r6] author | holds | The verse signals constant remembrance, worship, and obedience by day and night, with time for human needs, daytime work, and sleep to restore tired faculties for worship.
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r7] author | holds | In the inward reading, light from good deeds, remembrance, and watchfulness at day edges and night approaches erases the darkness of time spent on bodily needs.
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r8] author | holds | The luminous spirit would be lost through attachment to the dark body unless sacred good works nurture it, lift it toward spirituality and divine unity, and dispel bodily darkness.
[BURSEVI:v4p126#2/r9] author | holds | He compares this nurture to a seed that would be lost unless water made one seed into seven hundred, with God multiplying further as He wills. | mentions 2:261
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r1] author | holds | One reading assigns the afternoon edge of day to noon and afternoon prayers, and the night approaches to sunset and night prayers, covering all five daily prayers.
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r10] النبي | reports | The Prophet compares five daily prayers to bathing five times in a river at one's door, which leaves no dirt behind.
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r2] author | reports | Another reading takes the day's ends as morning and sunset and night approaches as night prayer plus tahajjud, obligatory for the Prophet; it cites the night vigil command. | mentions 17:79
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r3] author | reports | Other interpretations include witr according to Abū Ḥanīfa, or night prayer, witr, and tahajjud together, matching the plural ‘approaches.’
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r4] author | reports | A similar passage is read as morning before sunrise, noon and afternoon before sunset, and sunset and night prayers at night. | mentions 20:130, 50:39, 50:40
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r5] author | holds | Good deeds, especially the five prayers, erase consequences of minor sins rather than undoing sins already committed; prayers, Fridays, and Ramadans expiate between them when major sins are avoided.
[BURSEVI:v4p126/r6] author | holds | Prayer also restrains people from committing sins, as another verse says it keeps one from indecency and wrongdoing. | mentions 29:45

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r1] author | reports | Ṭalḥa, Ibn Abī Isḥāq, and Abū Jaʿfar read zuluf with a rounded lām; Mujāhid and Ibn Muḥayṣin read it with a silent lām, and a variant has zulfa, a synonymous form.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r2] author | holds | Good deeds expiate sins and remove liability, not sins as entities; another view says their records are erased. The view that they prevent sinning is remote and contrary to reports.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r3] author | holds | The apparent scope is obligatory and voluntary good deeds, though some restrict it to obligations. Reports about affirmation matching angels, fasting ʿArafa, and gratitude after eating or dressing support voluntary acts as expiators.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r4] أبو اليسر | reports | The occasion is reported as Abū al-Yusr regretting he kissed a woman; after waiting for revelation, the Prophet said prayer atoned for it. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, and Ibn al-Musayyib also transmit this view.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156#2/r5] author | prefers | Those who identify good deeds as obligatory prayers likely cite one important instance in the general category; the occasion does not restrict the verse.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156/r1] author | holds | Establish the prescribed prayers fully; other reported meanings are to maintain them regularly or perform them at the start of their times.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156/r2] author | holds | The two ends mean the beginning and end of the day. Zulf are hours near the day; al-Layth and Thaʿlab say the first part of night, while Abū ʿUbayda, al-Akhfash, and Ibn Qutayba say any night hour, supported by al-ʿAjjāj’s verse.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156/r3] author | holds | Zulf is coordinated with the two ends, and min al-layl qualifies it. End prayers are variously dawn and afternoon, dawn and sunset, or dawn then noon and afternoon; Abū Ḥayyān favors dawn and afternoon, while noon as an endpoint is questioned.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p156/r4] author | reports | Most identify the night prayers as sunset and evening; a report via al-Ḥasan from Ibn ʿAbbās specifies ʿatama. Another reading makes zulf night prayers drawing near to God, perhaps the Prophet’s night prayer or evening and witr.
