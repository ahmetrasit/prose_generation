 The codex of Abd Allah ibn Masud reads that God created humanity in loss.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p225/r1] الزجاج | reports | The statement that humankind is in loss is the answer to the oath.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p225/r2] author | holds | Al-insān means people collectively; khusr and khusrān have the same meaning.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p225/r3] أهل المعاني | reports | Loss means destruction or reduction of capital; a person who fails to use the self for lasting profit loses the self, their greatest capital.

## RAZI: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI:103:2/r1] author | reports | The definite article in الإنسان may refer to humanity as a class or to a previously known individual.
[RAZI:103:2/r10] author | holds | The indefinite خسر may magnify the loss: it is immense beyond human comprehension because sin is against God and against great blessings.
[RAZI:103:2/r11] author | reports | Alternatively, the indefinite may belittle the loss by implying human loss is less than Satan’s, a consoling reminder that someone is more disobedient.
[RAZI:103:2/r12] author | prefers | The author says the magnifying interpretation is the correct one.
[RAZI:103:2/r13] author | holds | Although الإنسان is involved in many losses, the singular wording points to the true loss: deprivation of serving God; loss of Paradise and punishment are negligible by comparison.
[RAZI:103:2/r14] author | holds | The purpose of creation is worship, so all other purposes are negligible by comparison. | mentions 51:56
[RAZI:103:2/r15] author | holds | The emphatic إن and lām, together with في, portray a person immersed and surrounded by loss.
[RAZI:103:2/r16] author | holds | The particle إن adds emphasis to the assertion of loss.
[RAZI:103:2/r17] author | reports | One construal takes في خسر as being on the path to loss, as with consuming an orphan’s wealth “in their bellies” when the outcome is Fire. | mentions 4:10
[RAZI:103:2/r18] author | reports | Another construal says no person escapes loss because life is capital that is continually spent.
[RAZI:103:2/r19] author | holds | Every hour spent in sin is loss; even permissible activity loses time without lasting effect, although it could have been used for enduring work.
[RAZI:103:2/r2] author | reports | On the generic reading, the verse means humanity generally is in loss, as the following exception for believers indicates. | mentions 103:3
[RAZI:103:2/r20] author | holds | Even worship can fall short: devotion has endlessly higher degrees as knowledge of God’s majesty grows, so settling for a lower degree is a kind of loss.
[RAZI:103:2/r21] author | holds | Humanity tends toward loss because afterlife-directed motives are hidden while worldly motives—senses, desire, and anger—are obvious, leaving most people absorbed in the world.
[RAZI:103:2/r22] author | holds | The apparent contrast with Surah al-Tin is resolved by distinguishing bodily conditions there from the soul’s conditions here. | mentions 95:4, 95:5
[RAZI:103:2/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas identifies a specific group of polytheists: al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, al-As ibn Waʾil, and al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Muttalib.
[RAZI:103:2/r4] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil says the verse was revealed about Abu Lahab.
[RAZI:103:2/r5] خبر مرفوع | reports | A report attributed to the Prophet identifies the person as Abu Jahl; no grading is stated.
[RAZI:103:2/r6] author | reports | A report says these people used to claim Muhammad was in loss, so God swore that the opposite was true.
[RAZI:103:2/r7] author | holds | خسر means loss or decrease, like خسران and كفران, and can mean losing one’s capital.
[RAZI:103:2/r8] author | reports | If الإنسان is generic, loss means wasting one’s self and lifetime, except for believers who gain everlasting bliss through them. | mentions 103:3
[RAZI:103:2/r9] author | reports | If الإنسان means unbelievers, loss is error and disbelief, from which those who believe escape into profit. | mentions 103:3

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r1] author | holds | The definite article in “al-insan” may refer either to humanity generically or to a previously understood particular person.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r10] author | holds | The indefinite “khusr” can magnify the loss or belittle it; Razi prefers magnification, a loss whose full extent only God knows because the sin is against God and His immense blessings.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r11] author | reports | On the belittling reading, human loss is less than Satan's, which offers reassurance that others are more disobedient.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r12] author | holds | Although “loss” is singular, the real loss is deprivation from serving the Lord; loss of Paradise and falling into Hell are negligible by comparison, since creation's highest purpose is worship. | mentions 51:56
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r2] author | reports | The generic interpretation compares the usage to saying that dirhams have become plentiful among people; the exception of believers supports this reading.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the verse refers to a group of polytheists, including al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, al-As ibn Wa'il, and al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Muttalib.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r4] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil says the verse was revealed about Abu Lahab.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r5] author | reports | An unspecified raised report identifies the person as Abu Jahl.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r6] author | reports | A report says these opponents used to claim that Muhammad was in loss, so God swears the truth is the opposite of their assumption.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r7] author | holds | “Khusr” means loss or diminution, the wasting of one's capital.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r8] author | reports | If “al-insan” is generic, loss means the destruction of one's self and lifetime, except for believers, who gain everlasting happiness through them.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p82#2/r9] author | reports | If “al-insan” means a particular unbeliever, loss means misguidance and disbelief; belief turns that loss into profit.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r1] author | holds | “Lafi khusr” depicts a person immersed in loss and surrounded by it on every side.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r10] author | holds | The apparent reversal with al-Tin is resolved because that sura describes bodily states, while this one describes the soul; thus they do not contradict each other. | mentions 95:4, 95:5
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r2] author | holds | The particle “inna” adds emphasis to the declaration of human loss.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r3] author | holds | Razi offers two readings of the lām in “lafi”: being on the road to loss, or being continually unable to escape loss.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r4] author | reports | On the first reading, humans are on the path to loss, as those who consume orphans' wealth are said to have fire in their bellies because that is their outcome. | mentions 4:10
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r5] author | reports | On the second reading, everyone incurs some loss because life's passing hours are capital that people rarely preserve.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r6] author | reports | Time spent in sin is plainly a loss.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r7] author | reports | Time spent in permissible activities is also lost because it leaves no lasting trace despite the chance to do enduring work.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r8] author | reports | Even time spent in worship is loss if one settles for a lower form, since the ranks of humility and knowledge of God's majesty have no end.
[RAZI-FULL:v32p83/r9] author | holds | Human happiness lies in loving the afterlife and turning from worldly life, but its causes are hidden while the senses, appetites, and anger draw people visibly toward this world, so most are absorbed in it and lost.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r1] ابن عباس عن أبي صالح | reports | Ibn Abbas, through Abu Salih, says the human in loss is a disbeliever.
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r10] علي | reports | Ali is said to have read an explanatory addition about the calamities of time and humanity remaining in loss to the end of time.
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r11] إبراهيم | reports | Ibrahim says humans decline and weaken as they age, except believers whose youthful deeds continue to earn reward, paralleling the account of human creation and decline. | mentions 95:4, 95:5
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r12] author | rejects | Qurtubi calls the received community and codex reading correct and says a variant attributed to Ali is not Qur'an for recitation, referring readers to his earlier rebuttal of departures from Uthman's codex.
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r2] الضحاك عن ابن عباس | reports | Al-Dahhak transmits Ibn Abbas's identification of the disbelievers as al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, al-As ibn Wa'il, and the two men named al-Aswad.
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r3] author | reports | Another interpretation reads al-insan generically as the human race.
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r4] الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash glosses loss as ruin.
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra glosses it as punishment and cites the outcome of a ruined affair. | mentions 65:9
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r6] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd glosses loss as evil; other views say deprivation, with the meanings close to one another.
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r7] سلام | reports | Salam transmits a reading of al-asr with a kasra on the sad.
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:2/r8] الأعرج وطلحة وعيسى الثقفي | reports | Al-A'raj, Talha, and Isa al-Thaqafi read khusr with a damma on the sin; Harun transmits it from Abu Bakr from Asim.
