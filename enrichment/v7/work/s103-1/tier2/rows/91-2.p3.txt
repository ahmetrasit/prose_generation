## ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p329/r2] author | holds | The world appears to contain opposing forces, yet God keeps them within limits and in harmony; their accord preserves the universe.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p331/r1] author | holds | The sun, moon, day, and night are complementary phenomena; their apparent oppositions in appearance, temperament, and effects serve in harmony.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p331/r2] author | holds | Earlier sūrahs used this complementarity for arguments about monotheism and the Day of Judgement; here the focus is how the apparent opposites work together.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p332/r1] author | holds | The cosmic order prevents the sun or moon from intruding on the other’s appointed time and keeps day and night within their roles, as 36:40 says. | mentions 36:40
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p332/r2] author | holds | Harmony among opposites sustains the universe; their obedience to limits teaches people to stay within God’s bounds, since exceeding them creates disorder God will not tolerate. | mentions 36:40
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p335/r6] author | prefers | Some take 91:9–10 as the oath’s complement; Islahi gives weight to al-Zamakhsharī’s rejection of that view.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p335/r7] author | holds | The sun, moon, day, and night show that a powerful, self-sustaining God keeps the world’s elements on their appointed paths, preventing their collision from destroying it.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p336/r3] author | holds | Suppressing the complement lets everything evident from the oaths count as its content; one prominent implication is that God destroys rebellious nations.
