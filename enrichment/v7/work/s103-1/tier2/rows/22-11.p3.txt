## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p400/r1] مجاهد وقتادة | reports | Mujahid, Qatada, and others interpret worshipping God on an edge as worship based on doubt.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p400/r2] آخرون | reports | Others take the phrase literally as being on an edge, like a mountain's rim: a person enters religion conditionally, staying if things go well and withdrawing otherwise.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p400/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas describes a man arriving in Medina who judges the religion good if his wife bears a son and his horses produce foals, but bad if neither occurs; Bukhari transmits it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p400/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Another report says Bedouins accepted Islam with the Prophet, then judged it good in years of rain, plenty, and healthy births, but worthless in drought and poor births; this was the occasion for the verse.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p400/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Al-Awfi's account says Medina's fever, a daughter rather than a son, or delayed charity could make a new convert think his religion brought only harm; this is the trial meant here. Qatada, al-Dahhak, Ibn Jurayj, and other early authorities give the same basic explanation.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p400/r6] عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم | reports | Ibn Zayd interprets the person as a hypocrite who worships only while worldly affairs prosper, then abandons worship and returns to disbelief when hardship or a test comes.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p402/r1] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid explains turning back on one's face as apostatizing into disbelief.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p402/r2] author | holds | The wavering worshipper gains nothing in this life and has rejected God for the next, suffering the clearest and greatest loss.

## DURR-FULL: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[DURR-FULL:v6p12/r7] ابن عباس | reports | A man judged the religion by whether his wife bore a son and his horses produced foals, praising it when these things went well.
[DURR-FULL:v6p12/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Bedouins praised Islam when rain, fertility, and births were good but called it worthless during drought and poor outcomes; this report gives the occasion of revelation.
[DURR-FULL:v6p12/r9] ابن عباس | reports | A migrant was content when his health, horse, and family prospered, but blamed Islam when illness, a daughter’s birth, and delayed charity came.
[DURR-FULL:v6p14/r1] ابن عباس | reports | The man’s worldly misfortunes, including his horse producing poorly, a daughter’s birth, and delayed charity, were the trial that made him reverse his commitment.
[DURR-FULL:v6p14/r2] أبو سعيد | reports | A Jewish convert who lost sight, wealth, and child asked the Prophet to release him from Islam; the Prophet said Islam purifies people as fire purifies metal, and the verse was revealed.
[DURR-FULL:v6p14/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid glosses worship on an edge as doubt, prosperity and wellness as good, settling as becoming established, trial as calamity, and turning back as apostasy.
[DURR-FULL:v6p14/r4] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan describes a migrant who praised Islam when health, alms, a son, and a horse’s foal came, but condemned it when sickness and losses followed.
[DURR-FULL:v6p14/r5] قتادة | reports | Qatada says worship on an edge is doubt: prosperity and comfort reassure the person, while affliction makes him abandon the truth.
[DURR-FULL:v6p15/r1] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the wavering person abandons the truth and loses the worldly life he cared for, then reaches the afterlife without a good deed to earn reward.

[BURSEVI:v6p7#2/r1] author | holds | Turning back on one's face means abandoning the direction and path of Islam and returning to disbelief.

[BURSEVI:v6p7#2/r6] بعضهم | reports | Another view defines worldly loss as abandoning obedience and persisting in disobedience, and afterlife loss as numerous adversaries and liabilities.
[BURSEVI:v6p7/r1] author | holds | Humility increases affection, forbearance brings supporters, gentleness wins hearts, fidelity sustains brotherhood, and truthfulness completes virtue; the prayer asks release from base traits and acquisition of good qualities.
[BURSEVI:v6p7/r2] الرواة | reports | The verse is reported to concern Bedouins who settled in Medina when healthy, prosperous, and blessed with children, but called the religion bad fortune when hardship came and turned away.
[BURSEVI:v6p7/r3] author | holds | Worshipping on an edge means standing at the religion's margin without inward roots, like a soldier who stays only while victory seems likely and otherwise flees.
[BURSEVI:v6p7/r4] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib says alphabet letters are the edges of a word joined to one another.
[BURSEVI:v6p7/r5] author | holds | Here good means worldly health and abundance; such a person settles outwardly in religion because of that good, without the inner assurance of steadfast believers.
[BURSEVI:v6p7/r6] الكاشفي | reports | Kashifi glosses the phrase as becoming settled and firm in religion because of that worldly favor.
[BURSEVI:v6p7/r7] author | holds | Trial means an unwelcome event affecting one's self, family, or wealth; good is also a trial, and hardship is not evil in itself because patient acceptance can bring nearness and rank.
