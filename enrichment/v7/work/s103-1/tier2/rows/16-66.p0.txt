## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v14p269#2/r1] author | holds | Livestock provide a lesson: God gives people pure, pleasant milk from within their bellies, from between digested matter and blood.
[TAB-FULL:v14p269#2/r2] author | holds | Most Meccan and Iraqi readers, along with Abu Ja‘far, read نسقيكم with a damma on the initial nūn, as a continuing provision; most other Medinese readers and Asim use fatḥa.
[TAB-FULL:v14p269#2/r3] الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisa’i distinguishes أسقينا for providing a lasting drink from سقينا for giving one drink; Arabs can also vary the hamza in usage.
[TAB-FULL:v14p269#2/r4] author | prefers | Both readings are valid, but al-Tabari prefers the damma reading because it is common for continuous watering and livestock milk is continually available.
[TAB-FULL:v14p271/r1] بعض نحويي الكوفة | reports | A Kufan grammarian says the masculine pronoun refers to النعم, a collective equivalent to الأنعام; Arabic poetry uses masculine agreement with a collective meaning.
[TAB-FULL:v14p271/r2] بعض نحويي الكوفيين | reports | Another explanation is “what is in the bellies of what We mentioned,” using masculine agreement for a generic thing or person; poetry and 6:78, 80:12, and 27:35 illustrate the construction. | mentions 6:78, 80:12, 27:35
[TAB-FULL:v14p271/r3] بعض البصريين | reports | A Basran grammarian says the phrase means the bellies of whichever livestock have milk, with “milk” understood; al-Tabari judges the first two explanations better Arabic.
