## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p56/r5] author | holds | Cloud and rain imagery follows the return to God as evidence of His control in changing states and anticipating resurrection; yuzjī means to drive gently and gradually, as with a heavy cloud or camel.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p56/r6] author | holds | Cloud is a collective noun; God moves one cloud toward another, joins their parts, and piles them densely so rain emerges through gaps formed by their compression.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p56/r7] ورش | reports | Warsh reads “yuʾallif” with wāw; the other seven readers use hamza.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p56/r8] author | reports | “Khilāl” is explained either as a singular or as a plural of khalal; Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and others read the singular “khalal.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p56/r9] مجاهد | reports | The apparent meaning, held by Mujāhid, al-Kalbī, and most commentators, is that there are mountains of hail in the sky like mountains of stone on earth; others take “mountains” as a metaphor for abundance or an ellipsis of comparison.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p57/r1] author | rejects | Al-Ḥūfī's appositional parse of the second “min” as referring to the sky fails: if it replaces the first, both must mark origin, as in “from Baghdad, from al-Karkh.”

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p57/r5] author | reports | Another parse makes the first two “min” particles indicate origin and the third redundant, yielding hail from the mountains of the sky.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p57/r6] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj compares the construction to an iron ring in one's hand: “of hail” qualifies the mountains and the object sent down is from those mountains, which must therefore be made of hail.
