## IBNABIHATIM: Ibn Abī Ḥātim al-Rāzī, d. 327 AH
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p500/r1] علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali identifies the passerby as the prophet Ezra, who left his city while young and came upon a ruined town; al-Hasan, al-Suddi, Ibn Burayda, and Qatada also identify him as Ezra.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p500/r2] رجل من أهل الشام | reports | A Syrian man gives a different identification: the person whom God caused to die for a hundred years was Hizqil ibn Buzā.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p500/r3] عبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير | reports | Ibn Ubayd ibn Umayr says the man was a prophet named Irmiya; Mujahid instead identifies him as an Israelite, and Wahb is reported to agree with Mujahid.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p500/r4] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the man was Ezra visiting Jerusalem after Nebuchadnezzar the Babylonian destroyed it; al-Dahhak is reported to agree.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p500/r5] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says “empty” means ruined.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p500/r6] قتادة | reports | Qatada explains that the ruined town had no one in it.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p500/r7] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak interprets its collapsed roofs as the roofs themselves; al-Suddi agrees.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p500/r8] قتادة | reports | Qatada paraphrases the passerby's question as asking how God will rebuild the town after its ruin; al-Rabi ibn Anas gives the same interpretation.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p501/r1] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi pictures Ezra arriving from Syria with a donkey, grapes, juice, and figs; on seeing the ruined town he gestured and asked how God would revive it, without denying or doubting resurrection.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p501/r2] اسباط | reports | The report says God caused the man and his donkey to die, and a hundred years passed over them.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p501/r3] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says God punished an Israelite for asking the question: the donkey stood beside him without food or drink for a hundred years while his food and drink remained nearby.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p501/r4] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the man died at the beginning of the day and was raised after a hundred years.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p501/r5] وهب بن منبه | reports | Wahb identifies the man as Jeremiah, who asked the question after Jerusalem and its books were destroyed; God restored his spirit after a century, when the city had been rebuilt, following an initial return of Israelites after seventy years and thirty years of rebuilding.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p501/r6] علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali says that when Ezra was raised after a hundred years, his eyes were the first part of him created.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p502/r1] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says God then brought Ezra back to life.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p502/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatada says Ezra was raised at the end of the day; al-Rabi says he was raised before sunset.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p502/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the man thought he had stayed a day, then saw the remaining sun and revised this to part of a day; al-Hasan and al-Rabi give a similar account.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p502/r4] علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali's account says Ezra's bones came together, were clothed with flesh, and received a spirit; returning to his city as a young man, he found his young shoemaker neighbor now old.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p502/r5] قتادة | reports | Qatada says Ezra's food was a basket of figs; Wahb ibn Munabbih and Mujahid are reported to give the same account.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p502/r6] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi identifies the food as figs and grapes.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p502/r7] وهب بن منبه | reports | Wahb identifies the drink as water kept in a small vessel.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p502/r8] قتادة | reports | Qatada identifies the drink as a skin filled with grape juice.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r1] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid identifies the food as a basket of figs and the drink as a skin of wine or juice.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says “it did not change” means the food and drink remained unchanged; al-Hasan, Mujahid, Wahb, Qatada, and others agree.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Al-Dahhak's report from Ibn Abbas stresses that food and drink remained unspoiled for a full hundred years, an extraordinary sign because they normally spoil sooner.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r4] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says the figs did not turn sour and the juice did not ferment over the hundred years; both remained lawful and unchanged.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r5] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid's second gloss of “لم يتسنه” is “it did not putrefy.”
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r6] أبو عمرو بن العلاء | reports | Abu Amr ibn al-Ala reads the word as “لم يتسن” and explains that the years had not passed over it.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r7] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says the donkey's separated joints came together one by one and were then clothed with flesh.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r8] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says the donkey had died and its bones had decayed.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p503/r9] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says Ezra watched God bring the donkey back to life.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p504/r1] الربيع بن أنس | reports | Al-Rabi ibn Anas says the donkey remained beside Ezra just as it was.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p504/r2] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says the man was raised young after 140 years, while his sons had become centenarian elders; al-A'mash and al-Minhal ibn Amr are reported to give the same contrast.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p504/r3] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says God made the man a sign by showing him this lesson in his own person.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p504/r4] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says Ezra returned to find his home rebuilt and his acquaintances dead; after proving his identity, his relatives vacated the house and returned it to him.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p504/r5] الربيع بن أنس | reports | Al-Rabi ibn Anas glosses “a sign” as “a lesson.”
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p504/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas explains “how We raise them” as making the bones stand up or rise, limb by limb.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p506/r1] وهب بن منبه | reports | Wahb says Ezra watched as the bones joined one another.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p506/r2] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi explains the bones being raised as being moved and then clothed; a wind gathered the donkey's bones from hills and plains, where animals had scattered them, and assembled a fleshless skeleton before Ezra.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p506/r3] أبو حفص مبشر بن عبيد | reports | Abu Hafs Mubashshir ibn Ubayd reports a reading, “كيف ننشزها,” with the meaning “we raise them upright.”
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p506/r4] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi says God clothed the bones with flesh and blood, then an angel blew into the donkey's nostril and it brayed.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p506/r5] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima describes the joined joints being clothed in flesh, then nerves and skin, before breath was blown into the donkey's nostril and it brayed.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p506/r6] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says the eyes were created first, and Ezra watched the rest of his body form and the bones receive flesh so he would learn that God revives the dead and can do all things.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p507/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the statement “when it became clear to him, he said, ‘I know that God is powerful over everything’” was said to the man.
[IBNABIHATIM:v2p507/r2] محمد بن إسحاق | reports | Ibn Ishaq explains that God can do whatever He wills with His servants, whether punishment or forgiveness.
