## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r1] author | holds | Striking a veil over their ears means they were put into deep sleep that sounds could not interrupt; hearing is one way sleep is broken, so blocking it signals its firmness.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r10] author | holds | An abundant count better displays divine power; a small count better suits the claim that the story is not uniquely astonishing, since even a long human span is like part of a day to God.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r2] author | holds | Blocking the eyes would not convey deep sleep because sights do not wake a sleeper; at most it could suggest the onset of sleep.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r3] القطب | reports | Al-Qutb objects to the metonymic explanation; al-Alusi says the objection is readily answered and offers an implicit-metaphor analysis of deep sleep as a veil struck over the ears.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r4] بعض المحققين | reports | Some investigators prefer reading the expression as a representational metaphor, comparing deep sleep to a veil laid over the ears.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r5] author | rejects | Some take striking the ears as preventing their function, like a ruler striking his subjects' hands; al-Alusi objects that this does not fit resurrection or establish sleep, though a proponent could intend sleep by it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r6] author | reports | The expression may derive from striking a lock onto a door or pitching a tent over its occupant.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r7] author | holds | The فاء parallels the response to Zachariah's prayer in 21:90; the sleep, turning, and awakening fulfill the youths' preceding supplication. | mentions 18:10, 21:90
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r8] author | holds | In the cave and for years are two adverbial phrases modifying the striking, one spatial and one temporal, so their combination is unobjectionable.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p211/r9] الراغب وغيره | reports | عدد may be a verbal noun used adjectivally to mean counted, or an adjective meaning numbered, or the verbal noun of an implied verb; number can indicate either abundance or scarcity.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p212/r1] الكشف | reports | Al-Kashf says abundance fits considering the plural addressees, while scarcity fits considering the singular addressee.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p212/r2] العز بن عبد السلام | reports | Al-ʿIzz ibn ʿAbd al-Salam failed to see how عدد could indicate abundance and thought scarcity impossible here; al-Alusi calls this strange for so eminent a scholar.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p212/r3] ابن حجر | reports | Ibn Hajar also discusses the issue in his Fatāwā Ḥadīthiyya, but al-Alusi says he does not find his remarks persuasive.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p258/r8] author | reports | Being made to sleep is allegorized as absorption in God and annihilation in everything besides Him.
