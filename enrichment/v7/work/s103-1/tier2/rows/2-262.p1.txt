## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:2:262#2/r1] محمد | reports | The Prophet says that the person who reminds others of a gift will not be addressed, regarded, or purified by God and will suffer painful punishment.
[TABRISI:2:262/r1] author | holds | Mann is reminding a recipient of a favor in a way that spoils it; its root means cutting off, as in an uncut reward or a frayed rope. | mentions 41:8
[TABRISI:2:262/r2] author | holds | Harm is injury that reaches its target; fear anticipates harm, and grief is distress that weighs heavily on the self.
[TABRISI:2:262/r3] author | holds | After commanding spending, God describes proper giving as one that is not followed by reminders or injury; reminders include saying I gave you this or rescued you.
[TABRISI:2:262/r4] author | holds | Harm may be insulting the recipient, frowning at him, burdening him with work, or diverting him to the donor's tasks; all spoil the gift and its reward.
[TABRISI:2:262/r5] author | holds | Their reward is recompense with God, secure from loss or diminution; the phrase at their Lord reassures the soul that what is with God cannot vanish.
[TABRISI:2:262/r6] author | holds | The verse makes the promise conditional: reward is deserved if spending is not followed by reminders or harm; without that condition, entitlement to reward does not follow.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p316#3/r1] ابن السائب ومقاتل | reports | The verse was revealed about Uthman's spending at Tabuk and gift of the well of Rumah to Muslims, and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf giving four thousand dirhams, half his wealth.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p316#3/r2] الجمهور | reports | Mann is reminding a poor recipient, “I helped and supported you,” in a boastful way.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p316#3/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Another view takes mann as claiming credit against God for giving charity.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p316#3/r4] author | holds | The praiseworthy mann means conferring a blessing, as with God’s favor; blameworthy mann means treating a gift as great and boasting about it.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p316#3/r5] أبو بكر الأنباري | reports | The accompanying poem about “giving little then quickly boasting” illustrates the blameworthy meaning of mann.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p316#3/r6] author | holds | Harm includes insulting the poor recipient or telling others about a gift when the recipient dislikes that disclosure; both hurt the recipient and contradict sincere charity.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v1p316#3/r7] حسان بن سنان | reports | Hasan ibn Sinan bought and freed a man's household without identifying himself or telling them who he was.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v7p40/r1] author | reports | The verse was revealed concerning Uthman and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf: Uthman equipped the Tabuk expedition with a thousand camels and a thousand dinars, while Abd al-Rahman gave half his wealth, four thousand dinars.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p40/r2] author | reports | The Prophet prayed that God be pleased with Uthman after his contribution.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p40/r3] بعض المفسرين | reports | Some commentators distinguish the previous verse as spending on oneself and this verse as spending on others, whose reward requires no taunting or harm. | mentions 2:261
[RAZI-FULL:v7p40/r4] القفال | reports | Al-Qaffal says the condition also covers someone spending on himself to join the Prophet and Muslims in jihad: he must not boast to them or insult fellow believers.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p40/r5] author | reports | Minn can mean benefaction; Ibn al-Anbari cites a poem asking someone to favor the speaker with a greeting, and the Prophet called Abu Bakr the most generous companion in wealth.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p40/r6] author | reports | Minn can also mean diminishing or withholding; the Quran describes a reward as غير ممنون, and death is called manun because it cuts lives short. | mentions 68:3
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41#2/r1] author | holds | The wording requires avoiding both taunt and harm in the sense that neither may occur; the reward is not preserved merely by avoiding one while committing the other.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41#2/r2] المعتزلة | reports | The Mu'tazila infer that major sins cancel reward, since the promised reward remains only when minn and adhā are absent.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41#2/r3] أصحابنا | rejects | Al-Razi's school says taunting and harm show the gift was given for self-exaltation, not God's pleasure, so it never earned reward in the first place.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41#2/r4] القاضي | reports | Al-Qadi objects that ثم makes taunting later than a valid act of giving, which should already have earned reward because a condition must exist when its cause occurs.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41#2/r5] أصحابنا | rejects | The response is that later taunting reveals the original gift was made for status, not God; alternatively, a later opposing event may prevent an earlier act from taking effect, as in the doctrine of ultimate outcome.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41#2/r6] author | holds | The verse indicates that both taunting and harm are major sins because either one strips this great act of its promised reward.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41#2/r7] المعتزلة | reports | The Mu'tazila say the servant's action makes God owe him reward; al-Razi's school says reward comes through God's promise, since performing an obligation does not itself earn payment.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41/r1] author | holds | The blameworthy minn is publicizing one's benefaction, while adhā is reproaching the recipient because of what was given.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41/r2] author | holds | Taunting deepens the needy recipient's humiliation and turns a prior benefit into harm after the giver has helped him.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41/r3] author | holds | Publicizing generosity discourages people in need from seeking the giver's charity.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41/r4] author | holds | A giver should recognize the gift as God's favor, be grateful for being enabled to give, and fear that some accompanying behavior may prevent acceptance.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41/r5] author | holds | The deeper reason is that God prepared the means of giving and removed its obstacles, so God is the true giver; fixating on visible causes instead of God leaves one at an animal level.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41/r6] author | prefers | Adhā here should mean the reproach attached to the gift, not every hurt to a believer; an example is telling the poor person that his visits distress the giver.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p41/r7] author | holds | One who gives without either taunt or harm receives great reward and abundant recompense.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p42/r1] أصحابنا | reports | Al-Razi's school argues this verse disproves cancellation of reward because it states without qualification that the spenders have their reward, even after grave sins.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p42/r2] الأمة | reports | The community agrees that having their reward with their Lord is conditioned on not committing unbelief; this shows a general expression may intend a restricted class.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p42/r3] author | holds | Since general wording may be restricted, general texts cannot provide categorical proof for the Mu'tazili doctrine of irrevocable threats.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p42/r4] author | reports | One reading says their spending is not wasted: its reward will be fully delivered on the Day of Resurrection, so they neither fear nor grieve over losing it. | mentions 20:112
[RAZI-FULL:v7p42/r5] author | reports | A second reading is that they will not fear punishment on the Day of Resurrection, as in the assurances in 27:89 and 21:103. | mentions 27:89, 21:103
