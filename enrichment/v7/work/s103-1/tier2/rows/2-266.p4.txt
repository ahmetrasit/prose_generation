## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#2/r1] author | holds | In له فيها من كل الثمرات, له is predicate while من كل الثمرات appears to be the subject, requiring interpretation because a prepositional phrase is not literally a subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#2/r2] author | holds | One solution supplies رزق or فاكهة, with the prepositional phrase standing as its adjective; al-Nābigha’s verse and 37:164 give parallels. | mentions 37:164
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#2/r3] الأخفش | reports | Another solution treats من as extra, yielding all fruits; since universal possession is impossible, كل means abundance rather than exhaustive totality. Al-Akhfash imposes no condition on extra من, unlike the Kūfan and Basran restrictions.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#2/r4] أبو البقاء | rejects | Abū al-Baqāʾ rejects extra من under both Sībawayh’s and al-Akhfash’s analysis because it would imply every fruit; he allows al-Akhfash’s analysis if abundance is meant.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#3/r1] author | holds | وأصابه الكبر may be circumstantial with قد understood, attached to أحدكم and governed by يود; compare 2:28 and 3:168. | mentions 2:28, 3:168
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#3/r2] الفراء | reports | The past may replace a present, ويصيبه, as in 11:98; al-Farrāʾ allows it after يود because that verb takes either أن or لو. | mentions 11:98
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#3/r3] author | holds | A third analysis coordinates by meaning: ‘would anyone wish that he had a garden and then old age struck him,’ converting present sense to past.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#3/r4] أبو البقاء | rejects | Abū al-Baqāʾ considers the semantic coordination weak because it changes the wording despite an adequate sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#3/r5] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī favors the semantic coordination as a question about having a garden and old age striking together.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#3/r6] الشيخ | rejects | Al-Shaykh objects that old age itself cannot be the object of someone’s wish, so it cannot coordinate with the wished-for garden.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#3/r7] author | holds | The objection is answered because the rhetorical question rejects the combined state of garden ownership and old age, not either element separately.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#4/r1] author | holds | وله ذرية is circumstantial from the pronoun in وأصابه; ضعاف, ضعفاء, and ضِعاف are regular forms of ضعيف.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#4/r2] أبو البقاء | reports | فأصابها إعصار coordinates with the earlier adjectival description of the garden, including من نخيل and what follows.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#4/r3] author | holds | Repeated أصاب highlights impact on each target more strongly than simply saying rain, old age, or a whirlwind occurred.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#4/r4] author | holds | إعصار is a strong rising wind, commonly called a whirlwind; al-Mahdawī reports ‘sirocco,’ explained by cloth-twisting or cloud-compressing.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#4/r5] author | holds | Its plural is أعاصير, witnessed by the cited line; the wind-name is masculine, explaining فيه.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#4/r6] author | prefers | In فيه نار, فيه may modify إعصار with نار as subject, or the clause may modify إعصار with نار as subject and فيه as predicate; the first is preferred because a simple adjective is nearer and preferable.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#5/r1] author | holds | فاحترقت is the inchoative counterpart of causative أحرق: the garden was burned and then burned up.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#5/r2] author | holds | حرق ناب الرجل means his anger intensified and can be transitive or intransitive; the cited line has نابه read nominative or accusative.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266#5/r3] author | holds | The conclusion كذلك يبين الله لكم الآيات was discussed earlier.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266/r1] author | holds | من نخيل is a nominative adjective of garden; نخيل is either a collective noun or a plural of collective نخل.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266/r2] الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib derives نخيل from selected or sifted trees and calls it the noblest growth; he also notes its uses and human analogies.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266/r3] author | holds | أعناب is plural of عنبة; عنباء also occurs with a long final vowel and no declension.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266/r4] author | holds | The Qur’an names the palm tree but grape fruit: the whole palm is useful, while the vine’s chief benefit is its fruit.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:266/r5] author | holds | تجري من تحتها may be a nominative adjective of garden, accusative circumstantial from its described noun, or a predicate of تكون according to Makkī.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p695#3/r1] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | Umar asks the Companions whom the garden parable concerns, then presses them to say whether they know.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p695#3/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas interprets the garden as a wealthy person’s good deeds later drowned by sins after Satan turns him toward disobedience.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p695#3/r3] author | holds | The parable warns of someone who began well, then replaced good deeds with sins and found no earlier good when most in need.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p695#3/r4] author | holds | An ʿiṣār is a fierce wind; its fire burns the garden’s fruit and destroys its trees, leaving the elderly owner helpless.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p695#3/r5] ابن عباس | reports | An alternate Ibn Abbas report depicts a man wasting his garden in youth, then losing it in old age when weak children cannot restore or support him.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p695#3/r6] author | holds | The ruined garden parallels the disbeliever at resurrection: he has no good to seek renewed standing and loses reward when most desperate for it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p695#3/r7] author | holds | Ibn Kathir notes that this interpretation is reported by al-Bukhari alone.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p696/r1] رسول الله | reports | Al-Hakim reports the Prophet praying that his broadest provision come in old age and near the end of life.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p696/r2] author | holds | The signs teach readers to consider and understand parables, then apply them to their intended meanings. | mentions 29:43

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p519/r3] author | holds | The question expresses intense desire for an orchard, its inner space screened by trees; dates and grapes specify what the earlier garden parable left general. | mentions 2:265
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p519/r4] الحرالي | reports | The date palm is useful in its entirety, from its lasting leaves and nourishing fruit to its wood, resembling a believer whose whole person benefits others.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p519/r5] الحرالي | reports | The grapevine branches upward, downward, and in every direction, resembling a pious believer honored through piety in every direction.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p519/r6] author | holds | Running rivers indicate fertile, generous land; the author contrasts this irrigated garden with the earlier rain-fed ridge orchard, since irrigation failure costs more than rain-fed cultivation. | mentions 2:265
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p519/r7] author | holds | The garden's abundance includes every kind of fruit in addition to dates and grapes, making its destruction especially devastating.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p519/r8] author | holds | The owners children are young and weak, compounding his own weakness from old age and making the garden essential to his dependents.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r1] الحرالي | reports | Iʿṣār is an intensified form linked with squeezing out hidden contents; al-Ḥarālī describes a fierce wind within cloud, with a cold edge likened to one kind of fire.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r10] الحكيم | reports | A sage's maxim reverses the sequence of thought and action: the end of work is the beginning of thought, and the end of thought is the beginning of work.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r2] الأصفهاني | reports | Al-Aṣfahānī defines an iʿṣār as a wind that swirls across the ground and then rises toward the sky like a column.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r3] author | holds | The fiery whirlwind burns the garden and leaves its aged owner helpless, burdened with dependents and stripped of wealth; the analogy depicts ruin at the moment of greatest need.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r4] الحرالي | reports | The first garden image is ruined by an affliction from beneath, at the root; this one is struck from above, at the branches, marking two directions from which harm can come.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r5] author | holds | Someone who spoils charitable deeds through abuse faces a loss like the orchard owner and his family when their hopes are destroyed. | mentions 2:264
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r6] عمر | reports | In a report recorded by al-Bukhārī in the tafsir, ʿUmar asks the Prophet's Companions what the garden verse means after they remain silent.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās explains the garden as a person's deeds: a wealthy man obeys God, then Satan leads him into sins until they drown his deeds.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r8] author | holds | God clarifies all signs so people may reflect and benefit; the passage distinguishes those whose reflection multiplies their yield from those whose unconsidered actions let desire bring disaster.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p520/r9] الحرالي | reports | Worship has no good without reflection; like a builder, a person must consider both the start and end of religious action, preparing its beginnings and later consequences.
