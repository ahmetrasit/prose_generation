## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v6p4386/r2] ابن عامر ويعقوب | reports | They read ولا تشرك with a second-person prohibition; the other readers use the indicative, saying that God has no partner in judgment.
[JISHUMI:v6p4392/r10] قتادة | reports | He says God knows how long they remained until the Qurʾān's revelation, or until they died, or simply knows without having told them.
[JISHUMI:v6p4392/r11] author | holds | God alone knows the unseen of the heavens and earth; ‘how seeing and hearing He is’ is an expression of wonder and exaltation, like the Arabic praise ‘how generous Zayd is.’
[JISHUMI:v6p4392/r12] أبو علي | reports | An alternative takes the phrase as a command to proclaim this to people, since God is hearing and seeing.
[JISHUMI:v6p4392/r13] author | prefers | The pronoun in ‘they have no protector’ may refer to the cave youths, but the better reading is all people, who have no protector besides God.
[JISHUMI:v6p4392/r14] قيل | reports | ‘No one shares His judgment’ may mean He needs no assistant, no one possesses judgment with Him, no one shares in His creation and beneficence, or idols have no divinity.
[JISHUMI:v6p4395/r4] author | holds | The description ‘how seeing and hearing He is’ affirms God's hearing and sight of secret and public, near and far, without imperfection.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p165#2/r1] علي | reports | Ali explains the extra nine as the difference between three hundred solar years known to the People of the Book and three hundred lunar years in the Qur'an: three years per century.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p165#2/r2] author | holds | The unseen is whatever escapes perception, but nothing escapes God's perception.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p165#2/r3] author | holds | The exclamation says how fully God sees every existent thing and hears every audible thing.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p165#2/r4] ابن عامر ويعقوب | reports | They read the closing verb as a direct prohibition: do not associate anyone in judgment.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p165#2/r5] author | holds | The other reading says God associates no one in His judgment; judgment here may mean knowledge of the unseen.

## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p669#2/r10] author | holds | The pronoun in “they have” refers to the inhabitants of the heavens and earth; a wali is one who manages their affairs.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p669#2/r11] author | holds | No one shares God’s judgment or decree.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p669#2/r12] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan reads the phrase as a prohibition addressed to the listener: “do not associate.”
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p669#2/r2] author | holds | “Say: God knows best” means God knows their duration better than those disputing it; the truth is what He revealed to the Prophet.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p669#2/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the statement quotes the People of the Book, and “God knows best” answers them.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p669#2/r9] author | holds | God alone knows what is hidden in the heavens and earth; the expressions of seeing and hearing marvelously signal perception beyond human limits, of the tiniest and largest, hidden and visible.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p510/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatada takes 'how seeing and hearing He is' as an exclamation: no one sees or hears more fully than God.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p510/r3] author | holds | Alternatively, God sees by His revelation and guidance and lets the Prophet see proofs and truth; the two verbs may express two distinct ideas rather than an exclamation.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p510/r4] author | holds | The pronoun in 'they have no protector' may refer to the sleepers, whom no one besides God guarded, or to the Prophet's unbelieving opponents, as a threat.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p510/r5] author | reports | Most readers use the indicative 'and He does not associate anyone in His judgment'; Ibn Amir, al-Hasan, Abu Raja, Qatada, and al-Jahdari read it as a prohibition addressed to the Prophet, while Mujahid has a jussive reading whose grammar Yaqub cannot explain.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p510/r7] ابن عباس | reports | On a Syrian campaign Ibn Abbas saw bones at a cave and said the sleepers had perished long ago; a monk was surprised that an Arab knew this.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p510/r8] author | reports | Another tradition says the Prophet foretold that Jesus would make the pilgrimage with the sleepers, who had not yet made it.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p511/r1] author | reports | Many people in Syria identify a cave with corpses, a mosque, a structure called al-Raqim, and a dog skeleton as the sleepers' site; Ibn Atiyya says he also visited the reported cave near Loja in Granada in 504 AH and saw corpses and a dog remains, nearby Roman ruins and a site called Daqiyus.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p511/r2] author | holds | He records these claimed sites because their enduring marvel is worth remembering, though earlier generations left no reliable indication of their identity.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:25-26/r5] author | reports | Ibn ʿĀmir, Rawḥ, Zayd from Yaʿqūb, and Sahl read ولا تشرك with second-person tāʾ in the jussive; the others read ولا يشرك with third-person yāʾ and indicative.
[TABRISI:18:25-26/r6] author | prefers | The third-person reading is more widespread and preferable because the preceding context uses third-person forms; its meaning is that no one shares God's judgment.
[TABRISI:18:25-27/r1] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the three-hundred-year statement is a quotation from the Jews, reasoning from the following instruction to say that God knows how long they stayed.
[TABRISI:18:25-27/r2] author | rejects | The author rejects treating God's statement as a quotation without decisive evidence; that reading leaves their duration unstated and defeats the passage's proof of God's power.
[TABRISI:18:25-27/r3] author | prefers | The duration has already been given; ‘God knows best’ refutes scripture scholars' disagreement, directing them to God's report rather than their own estimates.
[TABRISI:18:25-27/r4] author | holds | The unseen is what lies beyond perception; nothing in the heavens or earth is hidden from God's knowledge, including what creatures cannot perceive.
[TABRISI:18:25-27/r5] author | holds | ‘How perfectly He sees and hears’ is an exclamation of exaltation: God perceives every visible thing and hears every sound.
[TABRISI:18:25-27/r6] author | reports | A Jew asked Ali about the youths' duration; Ali answered that the Qur'anic nine years reconcile the difference between lunar years and the solar years in their scripture.
[TABRISI:18:25-27/r7] author | holds | No one in heaven or earth can protect or support creatures independently of God.
[TABRISI:18:25-27/r8] author | reports | On ولا يشرك, one reading means no judge may rule contrary to God's judgment; the alternative imperative means not to associate anyone with God in judgment, while another gloss limits the sense to God's unique knowledge of the unseen.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p131/r1] author | holds | God knows the duration better than the disputing People of the Book.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p131/r2] ابن السائب | reports | Christians of Najran said they understood the three hundred years but had no knowledge of the additional nine; the verse then affirmed that God knows best.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p131/r3] author | reports | Another account says the People of the Book counted 309 years from the youths' entry into the cave until their own time, whereas the verse refers to the time after their souls were taken, known only to God.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p131/r4] الماوردي | reports | The nine years account for the difference between solar and lunar years.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p131/r5] الزجاج | reports | أبصر به وأسمع is an exclamation: how perfectly God sees and hears; He knows the Cave story and all else, a reading he says scholars unanimously accept.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p131/r6] ابن الأنباري | reports | Alternatively, the phrase is an imperative to see and hear God's religion or guidance; the pronoun refers to God or guidance.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p131/r7] author | holds | No one can protect the inhabitants of heaven and earth besides God, or independently judge contrary to His ruling and thereby share His judgment.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p131/r8] ابن عامر | reports | He reads ولا تشرك with jussive tāʾ, an address commanding the human not to associate partners with God.
