## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH

[BAGHAWI-FULL:v4p341/r2] author | holds | Yakādu is an addition here, so the sense is that he cannot swallow it, as in the verse about the mirage. | mentions 24:40

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH

[RAZI-FULL:v19p82#2/r2] author | reports | Another explanation is that each new punishment the condemned faces is more severe than the one before.
[RAZI-FULL:v19p82#2/r3] المفضل | reports | Al-Mufaḍḍal explains the severe punishment as cutting off breaths and holding them in the bodies.

[RAZI-FULL:v19p82/r5] author | holds | The alternatives can be reconciled by saying he does not swallow all of it, or that some reaches his stomach without true swallowing because he takes it unwillingly and cannot tolerate its taste.
[RAZI-FULL:v19p82/r6] author | holds | Causes of death surround the condemned from every direction, or affect every part of his body, yet he does not die.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r2] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ takes waraʾ in 2:91 as meaning something other than what preceded; Abū ʿUbayda says it means what comes after. | mentions 2:91
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r3] قيل | reports | Waraʾ may instead mean in front of, supported by several poems and by the description of the king ahead of the people in 18:79; Abū ʿUbayda and Quṭrub adopt this sense. | mentions 18:79
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r4] الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash explains the word through idioms: something behind you will come to you, and being behind someone means pursuing him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r5] النحاس | reports | Al-Naḥḥās says waraʾahu means in front of him, not as a true antonym, but because what is hidden is concealed from view.
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r6] الأزهري | reports | Al-Azharī and also Abū ʿUbayda treat waraʾ as an antonym meaning either behind or in front, both rooted in concealment; Ibn al-Anbārī's explanation that Hell is hidden is judged good.
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:16-17/r8] أبو أمامة | reports | Al-Qurṭubī cites a report from Abū Umāma, from the Prophet, concerning the verse about being given pus to drink and gulping it; the report continues in the next discussion. | mentions 47:15, 18:29
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:17#2/r1] قيل | reports | Other explanations say God creates pains in the body each like the pain of death, or that the death pangs are prolonged to increase torment.
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:17#2/r2] author | rejects | The suggestion that this implies he dies is wrong: 35:36 says they are not made to die or relieved, and the unbelievers remain perpetually seized by deathlike agonies. | mentions 35:36
[QURTUBI-FULL:14:17#2/r3] author | holds | In the closing phrase, min warāʾihi means ahead of him, and ʿadhāb ghalīẓ is severe, continuous pain without respite, like the force meant in 9:123. | mentions 9:123
