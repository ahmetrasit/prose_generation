## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v3p205/r10] author | holds | Barzakh is the barrier ahead of the dead until resurrection; it does not mean they return at resurrection, which is only a return to the afterlife.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v3p205/r4] author | holds | “So that I may do good in what I left” means hoping to perform the faith omitted earlier, like building on a foundation means first laying that foundation.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v3p205/r5] قيل | reports | Another explanation is that the dying person asks to return to use the wealth he left behind.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v3p205/r6] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says that a believer offered return by the angels refuses the world of troubles and asks to proceed to God, while the unbeliever says, “Lord, return me.”
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v3p205/r7] author | holds | Kalla rebukes and rejects the request to return.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v3p205/r8] author | holds | “The word” means this connected portion of speech, the request to do good in what was left.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v3p205/r9] author | reports | “He will say it” means he cannot stop repeating it under grief and regret, or that only he says it and no answer is granted.

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v4p155#2/r7] author | holds | Barzakh literally means a barrier between two distances and here denotes the period between death and resurrection, as the exegetes agree.
