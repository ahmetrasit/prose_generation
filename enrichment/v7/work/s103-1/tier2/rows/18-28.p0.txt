## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:18:27-28/r3] author | holds | Stay with those who worship God by prayer at the two ends of the day and seek His face through prayer and fasting.
[MUQATIL:18:27-28/r4] author | reports | The verse was revealed about Uyaina ibn Hisn's request to separate poor clients from him; he complained of their smell and asked the Prophet to seat the groups apart.
[MUQATIL:18:27-28/r5] author | reports | Those present included Bilal, Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, Amir ibn Fuhayra, Muhja, Ayman, Abu Hurayra, and Ibn Masud; Muhja was the first martyr at Badr.
[MUQATIL:18:27-28/r6] author | holds | The Prophet is not to obey one whose heart has been made heedless of the Quran, who follows desire and lets his affair go to waste.
[MUQATIL:18:27-28/r7] author | holds | The heedless man's affair refers to his boasting about nobility and lineage; faraṭ means wasted in the Resurrection, as in 6:38. | mentions 6:38

## YAHYA-SALLAM: Yaḥyā b. Sallām, d. 200 AH
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p181/r1] قتادة | reports | Qatāda identifies the morning and evening remembrance with the dawn and afternoon prayers; the prescribed prayers began a year before the Prophet left Mecca for Medina.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p181/r2] author | holds | The verse was revealed when the polytheists demanded that the Prophet dismiss Salman, Bilal, Suhayb, Khabbab, and Salim before they would sit with him; it links to a similar command. | mentions 6:52
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p181/r3] معاوية بن قرة | reports | Muʿāwiya b. Qurra interprets the command to remain with those who call on God morning and evening as referring to prayer.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p181/r4] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet thanked God for placing in his community people with whom he was commanded to keep patient company.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p181/r5] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet says remembrance morning and evening is better than the finest swords or freely given wealth.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p181/r6] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet tells Suraqa that keeping company with people remembering God from dawn prayer to sunrise is dearer than freeing four slaves.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p181/r7] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet says sitting with people remembering God after the afternoon prayer until sunset is dearer than freeing eight descendants of Ishmael.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p182/r1] السدي | reports | Al-Suddī says the heedless person's desire is his appetite, and Mujāhid and al-Suddī gloss his affair as waste; the phrase is linked to two verses about neglect. | mentions 39:56, 6:31

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v15p236/r1] author | holds | The Prophet is to remain with companions who remember God through praise, glorification, prayer, and other good works, seeking God’s face rather than worldly gain.
[TAB-FULL:v15p236/r2] author | reports | The standard readers recite بالغداة; ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿĀmir and Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī are reported to read بالغدوة.
[TAB-FULL:v15p236/r3] author | rejects | Arabists disfavor بالغدوة because غدوة is already definite, takes no alif-lām or genitive construction; al-Ṭabarī follows the readers’ consensus on بالغداة.
[TAB-FULL:v15p237/r1] author | holds | ولا تعد عيناك means do not turn your gaze from the believers to others; the expression derives from surpassing or going beyond something.
[TAB-FULL:v15p237/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās explains the command as not passing over the believers for other people.
[TAB-FULL:v15p237/r3] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says some people asked the Prophet to leave the humble believers and sit with Arab nobles; the verse forbade neglecting or despising the believers.
[TAB-FULL:v15p237/r4] عبد الرحمن بن سهل بن حنيف | reports | ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Sahl reports that after the verse came, the Prophet found poorly dressed people remembering God, sat with them, and praised God for ordering his patience with them.
[TAB-FULL:v15p241#2/r1] author | holds | The people whose hearts God left heedless were the unbelieving leaders who asked that the worshippers be expelled; they chose desire over obedience.
[TAB-FULL:v15p241#2/r2] خباب | reports | Khabbāb’s report identifies the leaders who sought the separation as ʿUyayna and al-Aqraʿ.
[TAB-FULL:v15p241/r1] author | holds | The worldly adornment sought was status from keeping company with prominent polytheist leaders who objected to the Prophet’s poor companions.
[TAB-FULL:v15p241/r2] خباب | reports | A report from Khabbāb’s story says the Prophet was seen with Khabbāb, Ṣuhayb, and Bilāl; visitors asked him to make the poor men leave when they arrived. | mentions 6:52
[TAB-FULL:v15p241/r3] عيينة بن حصن | reports | Ibn Jurayj says ʿUyayna ibn Ḥiṣn, before becoming Muslim, complained of Salmān’s smell and requested a separate gathering.
[TAB-FULL:v15p241/r4] قتادة | reports | Qatāda says the Prophet praised God for placing in his community people with whom he was commanded to remain patient.
[TAB-FULL:v15p241/r5] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd explains زينة الحياة الدنيا here as the worldly elite.
[TAB-FULL:v15p241/r6] سلمان الفارسي | reports | Salmān reports that Uyayna, al-Aqraʿ, and their delegation wanted the Prophet to remove poor believers in wool garments so they could sit with him and learn; he found the believers remembering God and said their life and death were with him. | mentions 18:27, 18:29
[TAB-FULL:v15p242/r1] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid glosses فرطا as waste or loss.
[TAB-FULL:v15p242/r2] داود | reports | Dāwūd interprets فرطا as remorse.
[TAB-FULL:v15p242/r3] خباب | reports | Khabbāb’s report through al-Suddī interprets فرطا as destruction.
[TAB-FULL:v15p242/r4] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd interprets فرطا as being opposed to the truth.
[TAB-FULL:v15p242/r5] author | prefers | Al-Ṭabarī prefers waste and destruction: the careless person exceeds bounds through vanity, pride, and contempt for believers, thereby losing the truth and perishing.
[TAB-FULL:v15p242/r6] author | reports | Asked how ʿĀṣim read the phrase, Abū Bakr ibn ʿAyyāsh gives كان أمره فرطا; al-Ṭabarī adds that ʿUyayna used to boast, saying I, I.

[TAB-FULL:v9p265/r6] عبد الله بن عمر | reports | Ibn Umar says the callers in 18:28 are people who attend the prescribed prayers.
