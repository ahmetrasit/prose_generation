## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r1] أبو صالح عن ابن عباس | reports | Quraysh asked the Prophet about Dhul-Qarnayn, the Spirit, and the Companions of the Cave; he promised an answer tomorrow without saying God willing, so Gabriel's revelation was delayed fifteen days and he was distressed.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r10] author | holds | An exception is valid in an oath by God and in expiatory forms such as ẓihār and vows; these concern future acts, unlike the operative wording of divorce and manumission.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r3] ابن الأنباري | reports | Remember your Lord when you forget means remember Him after the lapse of forgetfulness; the example means to tell someone your need after prayer ends.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r4] سعيد بن جبير والجمهور | reports | If you forgot to make an exception, say God willing when you remember, even after a day, month, or year.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r5] عكرمة | reports | Another interpretation takes إذا نسيت to mean when you become angry; Ibn al-Anbari says this is plausible because anger produces forgetfulness.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r6] الماوردي | reports | A third interpretation is to remember God when you forget something so that He will remind you of it.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r7] author | holds | An exception keeps an oath-taker from lying if the promised act is not done; Moses' exception before his trial is an example. | mentions 18:70
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r8] أحمد ومالك | reports | Divorce or manumission conditional on God's will still takes effect; the author says this is the transmitted position from Ahmad and Malik's view.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p127#2/r9] أبو حنيفة والشافعي | reports | They hold that neither divorce nor manumission takes effect when made conditional on God's will.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r1] أحمد وأكثر الفقهاء | reports | The exception is valid only when joined directly to the oath; this is also reported from Ahmad.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r10] ابن الأنباري | reports | Alternatively, the Prophet asks God to reveal the answers to Quraysh's questions and guide him before the promised time.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r2] الحسن وطاووس | reports | Another view allows an exception as long as the speaker remains in the same gathering; something similar is reported from Ahmad.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r3] ابن عباس ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وأبو العالية | reports | A third view allows an exception even after a year.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r4] ابن جرير الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari says one may say God willing even after breaking the oath to remove the sin of omitting the exception; expiation remains unless the exception was attached to the oath.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r5] author | holds | Those who allow a year mean that the later exception removes blame for failing to say it, not the required expiation.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r6] نافع وأبو عمرو | reports | They pronounce the yāʾ of يهديني in connected recitation but omit it at a pause.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r7] ابن كثير | reports | He pronounces the yāʾ in both connected and paused recitation.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p128/r8] ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي | reports | They omit the yāʾ in both connected and paused recitation.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p92#2/r1] المفسرون | reports | Commentators say the Prophet promised to answer three questions the next day without saying “if God wills”; revelation was delayed fifteen days, or forty in another report, before this verse came.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p92#2/r2] القاضي | reports | Al-Qāḍī objects that the Prophet knew he could die or be impeded before tomorrow, so an unqualified promise risked a false statement and repelling people from him.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p92#2/r3] القاضي | reports | Al-Qāḍī also objects that the verse contains many rules and benefits, so it should not be restricted to one occasion; Rāzī replies that multiple benefits do not rule out a single occasion.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p92#2/r4] author | holds | Rāzī answers that saying “if God wills” is preferable, but the Prophet may have forgotten it, which would be omission of what is better rather than wrongdoing.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p92#2/r5] author | reports | One reading supplies “unless God permits you to make that statement,” so a person should not announce a future action without divine permission to say so.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p92#2/r6] author | reports | Another reading supplies the words “unless you say: if God wills”; this protects a promise from being a lie if death or another obstacle prevents its fulfillment.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93#2/r1] القائلون بأن المعدوم شيء | reports | Those who say nonexistent things are things argue that a future action, now nonexistent, is called “a thing” in the verse; Rāzī replies that what will become a thing may be named so in advance, as in 16:1. | mentions 16:1
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93#2/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says that if a person forgets to qualify a promise and remembers much later, saying “if God wills” then still suffices to prevent oath-breaking.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93#2/r3] سعيد بن جبير | reports | Saʿīd ibn Jubayr permits the delayed qualification after a year, month, week, or day.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93#2/r4] طاوس | reports | Ṭāwūs allows the qualification only while the person remains in the same sitting.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93#2/r5] عطاء | reports | ʿAṭāʾ allows the qualification for roughly the time it takes to milk a plentifully yielding camel.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93#2/r6] عامة الفقهاء | reports | The majority of jurists say the qualification has no legal effect unless it is joined to the original statement.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93#2/r7] ابن عباس | holds | Ibn ʿAbbās argues that the command to remember God when one forgets is not limited to a particular time, supporting a later qualification when one remembers.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93#2/r8] أبو حنيفة | reports | Abū Ḥanīfa warns al-Manṣūr that allowing delayed qualifications would let people leave a pledge and later undo their oaths; al-Manṣūr approved the argument.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93/r1] المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazila say God wills faith and obedience, but servants will unbelief and sin, so the servants' will overcomes God's.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93/r2] author | holds | Rāzī's school holds that everything God wills occurs, including faith or unbelief; divine will overcomes the servant's will.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93/r3] author | holds | The “if God wills” qualification removes a false promise only if God's will can override the servant's will; a defeated divine will cannot excuse failure.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93/r4] author | holds | The community agrees that adding “if God wills” to an oath prevents breach; Rāzī argues this proves God's will prevails and only what God wills comes to be.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93/r5] author | holds | If a debtor swears to repay tomorrow and adds “if God wills,” failure does not break the oath even though repayment is commanded; thus God may command without willing and prohibit while willing.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p93/r6] الفقهاء | reports | Some jurists say conditional divorce does not take effect if the husband says “if God wills”; Rāzī explains that proving God's will would otherwise depend circularly on proving the divorce, and vice versa.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p94/r1] author | rejects | Rāzī says the jurists' appeal to settled contracts is a weak restriction of the text by analogy; a whispered qualification also avoids the stated public-doubt objection while counting by agreement.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p94/r2] author | prefers | The stronger case for requiring a connected qualification comes from commands to fulfill contracts and covenants: joined exception and statement form one utterance, while an unqualified, complete promise creates an obligation. | mentions 5:1, 17:34
