[STUDYQURAN:93:1/r1] others | reports | Some identify morning brightness with the morning God spoke to Moses and night with the Night Journey and Ascension; others identify it as the moment Moses' opponents recognized truth and prostrated. | mentions 17:1, 20:59
[STUDYQURAN:93:1/r3] others | reports | Other symbolic readings make morning the Garden's light and night the Fire's darkness, or light in knowers' hearts and blackness in disbelievers' hearts.
[STUDYQURAN:93:1/r4] Sh and Ṭ | reports | “When still” may describe darkness spreading across the day; night may also mean as it approaches or as it leaves.
[STUDYQURAN:93:1/r5] Sh and Ṭ | reports | The night is also interpreted as the night approaching or departing.
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r1] author | reports | Al-Ḍuḥā is early Makkan and ranked fourth by many, seventh by others; it may be the first sūrah revealed whole, though others give that distinction to al-Fātiḥah.
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r3] several commentators | reports | One occasion account says revelation paused for twelve, forty, or fifty days, after which an idolater taunted Muhammad that his satan had forsaken him, prompting reassurance.
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r4] others | reports | Another account says idolaters declared that Muhammad's Lord had forsaken and despised him.
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r5] author | reports | A further account says illness kept the Prophet from night vigil for two or three nights; Umm Jamīl, Abū Lahab's wife, taunted him, and vv. 1–3 were revealed. | mentions 111:1, 93:1, 93:2, 93:3
[STUDYQURAN:93:intro/r6] author | holds | The sūrah consoles the Prophet: it assures him God will not abandon him, recalls God's favors, and ends by urging care for the downtrodden and preaching the message.
[STUDYQURAN:94:intro/r3] author | reports | Some say Sūrahs 93 and 94 were originally one, since Companions recited them together in one prayer cycle without a basmalah separating them. | mentions 1:1
[STUDYQURAN:94:intro/r5] author | holds | Both sūrahs present God's bounties as relief: Sūrah 93 addresses external needs, Sūrah 94 spiritual relief; 94 recalls blessings, reassures, then commands exertion and turning to God.
