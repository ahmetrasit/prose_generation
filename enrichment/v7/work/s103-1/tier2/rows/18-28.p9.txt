## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:18:28-29/r1] author | holds | Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects treating the verse as proof that God compels people to sin; punitive constraint follows their choices.
[TABATABAI:18:28-29/r2] author | holds | Punitive constraint does not negate free choice; only constraint imposed initially would do so.
[TABATABAI:18:28-29/r3] المجمع | reports | Al-Majmaʿ glosses faraṭ as exceeding the truth; following desire and excess explain the heedless heart and restate its condition.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r1] الراغب | reports | Rāghib defines ṣabr as restraint under constraint and, more generally, holding oneself to what reason and religious law require or away from what they forbid.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r2] author | holds | God’s “face” means His beautiful names and attributes through which people address and worship Him; the divine essence itself cannot be directly reached.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r3] author | holds | Seeking God’s active attributes, such as mercy and favor, means seeking to be covered by them and become recipients of mercy, approval, and grace.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r4] author | holds | Seeking non-active attributes such as knowledge, power, and majesty means drawing near by taking the humble, weak, and ignorant position appropriate before them.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r5] author | rejects | The author rejects interpretations that “His face” means approval, God’s essence through an omitted word, or simply turning toward God and seeking nearness.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r6] author | holds | Calling on God morning and evening is a metonym for continuing devotion, since mornings and evenings recur; interpreting it as the two daily prayers or obligatory prayers is unpersuasive.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r7] الراغب | reports | The root of “pass beyond” carries the sense of overstepping, so “let not your eyes pass beyond them” means do not leave or overlook the poor believers for worldly adornment.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r8] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says “pass” implicitly means that the eye shies away or rises above them; without that implication, the wording would need a direct object.
[TABATABAI:18:28/r9] author | holds | God’s making a person heedless is punitive forgetfulness after stubborn opposition to truth, parallel to the later image of coverings on hearts and deafness in ears. | mentions 18:57

[TABATABAI:18:29-31/r6] سلمان | reports | Salman reports that influential converts, including ʿUyayna ibn Badr and al-Aqraʿ ibn Ḥābis, asked the Prophet to remove shabby poor Muslims such as Salman and Abū Dharr; the passage then warned them of fire.
