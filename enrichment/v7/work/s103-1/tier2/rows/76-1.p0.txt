## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:76:1-3/r1] author | holds | The unmentioned period was 21,000 years, or three sevenths, after heaven and earth were made.
[MUQATIL:76:1/r1] author | holds | The unnamed human is Adam: for 21,000 years, or three seventh divisions, after heaven and earth were created, no one remembered him because he did not yet exist.
[MUQATIL:76:1/r2] author | holds | Jinn inhabited earth, then disobeyed God and harmed one another; God sent an angel tribe called Jinn, including Iblis, whose name was al-Harith, to expel them beyond earth.
[MUQATIL:76:1/r3] author | holds | The expelled jinn were driven into darkness behind a barrier mountain where the sun sets; between it and Mount Qaf lay a year-long distance of darkness and still water.
[MUQATIL:76:1/r4] author | holds | Iblis and his host purified earth and worshipped God; when God announced a human successor, the angels anticipated corruption and bloodshed from the jinn they had seen, not from knowledge of the unseen.
[MUQATIL:76:1/r5] author | holds | God sent Gabriel for soil from beneath the Kaaba, the earth's surface layer; mixed with water and left until it stank, it became sticky clay on top and dark mud below.
[MUQATIL:76:1/r6] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet said people made from the sticky clay were the people of the right, while those from dark mud were the people of the left.
[MUQATIL:76:1/r7] author | reports | Imru al-Qays ibn Abis al-Kindi and the Jewish Malik ibn al-Dayf disputed Adam's creation; Malik claimed the Torah placed it at the creation of heaven and earth, and revelation refuted him.

## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH

## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r1] ابن عباس ومقاتل والكلبي ويحيى بن سلام | reports | They classify Surat al-Insan as Meccan.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r10] يحيى بن سلام | reports | Not being mentioned means not being mentioned among created beings, although known to God.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r11] الفراء وقطرب وثعلب | reports | The human was an unrecognized shaped body of earth and clay, with no known name or purpose, then became mentioned when the spirit was breathed into him.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r12] مقاتل | reports | The word order is advanced and delayed: the sense is that a period came when the human was not mentioned, since humanity was created after all animals.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r2] author | reports | Others classify only verses 23 through the end as Meccan and the preceding part as Medinan.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r3] الفراء وأبو عبيدة | reports | هل here can mean قد, making the sentence a statement that a period did come upon humanity.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r4] ابن عيسى | reports | Alternatively, هل retains its interrogative force.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r5] قتادة والسدي وعكرمة | reports | The human here may be Adam, created after the heavens, earth, and everything between them, at the end of the sixth day, Friday.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r6] ابن عباس وابن جريج | reports | Alternatively, the human here means every human being.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r7] ابن عباس | reports | The period may be forty years before the spirit was breathed into Adam while he lay between Mecca and Taif; Abu Salih transmits this from Ibn Abbas.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Another report gives forty years as clay, forty as altered mud, and forty as dried clay, with Adam's creation complete after 120 years before the spirit was breathed in; al-Dahhak transmits it.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p161/r9] ابن عباس | reports | The period may instead be an unspecified time with no fixed limit.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p5#4/r1] ابن عباس ومقاتل والمفسرون وأهل المعاني | reports | They take hal ata as an affirmative statement meaning that a time did indeed come over the human.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p5#4/r2] الأخفش | reports | Hal can mean qad in this verse and in the question about the Overwhelming Event, just as a speaker may ask about something known to have happened. | mentions 88:1
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p5#4/r3] المبرد | reports | Here hal means qad, and he cites the question about the adversaries as another non-interrogative use. | mentions 38:21
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p5#4/r4] سيبويه | reports | Hal can be used for a purpose other than asking a question.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p5#4/r5] الكسائي | reports | Hal can be an interrogative, its normal function, a negation, or mean qad.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p5#4/r6] الفراء | reports | He reads the clause as a statement meaning qad ata; hal can introduce either a statement or a denial, and here it confirms what has happened.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p5#4/r7] أبو إسحاق | reports | He says hal is not interrogative and cites Abu Bakr's wish that Adam's unmentioned interval had continued without offspring or their trials; reports also attribute this wish to Umar and Ibn Masud. | mentions 76:1
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p5#4/r8] author | holds | The reported wish makes sense only if hal is declarative: a real question would call for yes or no, whereas this wish answers a statement.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p7/r1] جماعة من المفسرين | reports | They identify the human in this verse as Adam.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p7/r2] الكلبي | reports | The interval was forty years while Adam lay before the spirit was breathed into him.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p7/r3] عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | Adam remained forty years as clay and forty as shaped dark clay, making his formation complete after 120 years.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p7/r4] ابن مسعود | reports | He adds forty years as dust to Ibn Abbas's account, making Adam's formation complete after 160 years.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p7/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Adam was not mentioned in either heaven or earth.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p7/r6] الفراء | reports | Adam existed but was not mentioned from the time God created him until the spirit was breathed into him; al-Zajjaj gives a similar explanation.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p7/r7] الزجاج | reports | He also allows the phrase to mean humankind in general, who began as sperm, then clots, then tissue, until becoming something mentioned.
