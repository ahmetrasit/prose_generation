## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r4] author | prefers | The three hundred plus nine years are God’s report of the youths’ sleep in the cave until their discovery; Mujāhid sees this as specifying the ‘counted years’ of 18:11. | mentions 18:11
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r5] author | holds | After giving the exact duration, God commands the Prophet to say that God knows best: this is certain truth because God knows the unseen of heaven and earth.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r7] author | rejects | The suggestion that the additional nine means hours, days, months, or years, disputed by Israelites, is far-fetched: an unspecified addition after a specified number inherits the same unit.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r8] النقاش | reports | Al-Naqqāsh says the three hundred are solar years, with nine added because Arabs reckon lunar years, aligning the two calendars.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r9] قتادة ومطر الوراق | reports | Qatāda and Maṭar al-Warrāq say لبثوا is a report from the Israelites, citing ʿAbd Allāh’s codex; in the common reading it continues the people’s reported claims.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r1] author | reports | One view says the Prophet is told to return knowledge of the duration to God, refuting others; Abū Ḥayyān prefers that the phrase belongs to the disputants, as the context indicates and because the matter is among God’s hidden knowledge.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r2] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says مائة with tanwīn is apposition or بدل بيان; others call it explanatory or a specification, and al-Zamakhsharī calls it apposition to ثلاثمائة.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r3] author | rejects | Abū al-Baqāʾ reports allowing مائة as a substitute for مائة understood as ‘hundreds,’ but Abū Ḥayyān says Basrans reject apposition and that مائة normally takes a singular genitive, not plural سنين.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r4] حمزة والكسائي وطلحة ويحيى والأعمش والحسن وابن أبي ليلى وخلف وابن سعدان وابن عيسى الأصبهاني وابن جبير الأنطاكي | reports | These readers omit tanwīn from مائة and attach it to سنين, using a plural where singular is usual; Abū Ḥātim harshly criticized the reading.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r5] أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī says مائة is usually followed by a singular but may also be followed by a plural.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r6] أبي وعبد الله | reports | Ubayy and ʿAbd Allāh’s codex read سنة in the singular.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r7] الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk reads سنون with wāw, supplied as a predicate: ‘they are years.’
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r8] الحسن وأبو عمرو | reports | Al-Ḥasan and a narration from Abū ʿAmr pronounce تسعا with open tāʾ, like عشر.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r1] الأخوان | reports | The two readers attach مئة to سنين; al-Zamakhshari explains the plural as standing for a singular, like أعمالا for عملا. | mentions 18:103
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r10] الحسن وأبو عمرو | reports | Al-Hasan and Abu Amr in one transmission read تسعا with fatḥa on tā, like عشر.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r2] أبو حاتم | reports | Abu Hatim criticized the reading that annexes مئة to سنين.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r3] author | rejects | The author dismisses Abu Hatim's criticism of the annexed reading.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r4] عبد الله وأبي | reports | Ibn Masud's codex has singular سنة, and Ubayy read that form.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r5] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak read سنون as a predicate of an omitted subject, meaning they are years.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r6] author | holds | With the remaining reading, tanwin leaves سنين as بدل or عطف بيان for ثلثمئة; Abu al-Baqa allows بدل of مئة because it means a plural.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r7] author | rejects | سنين cannot be a classifier in this reading except by poetic necessity, and the cited verse uses a singular classifier.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r8] author | holds | تسعا means nine years; the classifier is omitted because the previous mention makes it clear, and تسعا is a direct object.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:25/r9] author | holds | ازداد is an iftiʿāl form with ta changed to dal after zay; the base verb takes two objects, while this derived form takes one fewer. | mentions 18:13

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p150#2/r1] author | holds | God reports that the Sleepers remained three hundred lunar years plus nine, which equals three hundred solar years because each century differs by three years in the two calendars.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p150#2/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada, followed by Mutarrif ibn Abd Allah, says the three hundred years plus nine was the People of the Book's statement, rejected by the following command to say God knows; he cites a variant reading attributed to Ibn Masud, وقالوا ولبثوا.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p150#2/r4] author | rejects | Ibn Kathir rejects Qatada's attribution: the People of the Book give three hundred solar years without nine; the verse is God's own report, as Ibn Jarir also chose. The Ibn Masud reading has a disconnected transmission and is anomalous against the majority reading, so it is not evidence.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p461/r1] author | holds | The three hundred years are solar years, using the reckoning of the Jewish questioners; the wording also alludes to the era's unbelieving rule and persecution.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p461/r2] author | holds | The added nine years are lunar, converting the three hundred solar years to the Arabs' lunar reckoning based on the difference between year lengths.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v5p182/r1] author | holds | The years clarify the youths' counted time in the cave while alive and asleep. The phrase is apposition, not a specification; the latter parse would imply 600 years under al-Khalīl or 900 under the usual plural minimum.
[BURSEVI:v5p182/r2] author | holds | On the tanwīn reading of مائة, the genitive plural follows; on the possessive reading, a plural stands for a singular, as in أعمالا.
[BURSEVI:v5p182/r3] author | holds | The extra nine years convert the People of the Book's solar reckoning to the Arabs' lunar reckoning, with three years' difference per century; the source gives the lengths of solar and lunar years.
[BURSEVI:v5p182/r4] الكاشفي | reports | He calculates 300 solar years as 309 lunar years plus two months and nine days.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#2/r1] مجاهد | reports | The statement that the youths stayed three hundred years and nine more is an independent sentence explaining the earlier summary that God kept them asleep for a number of years; Mujāhid is cited for this explanation, which several scholars choose.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r2] بعضهم | reports | The nine years may reconcile the solar-year count of the People of Scripture with the lunar-year count of Arabs, and this explanation is attributed by some to ʿAlī; al-Ālūsī details astronomical objections to the claimed nine-year difference.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r3] الخفاجي | reports | Al-Khafājī says the two counts are explicit in context: three hundred by the People of the Book's reckoning, then nine extra by the questioners' reckoning; the difference is approximate because the remaining fraction is below half a year.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r4] الطيبي | reports | Al-Ṭībī proposes that the sleepers nearly awoke after three hundred years but something kept them sleeping nine additional years; al-Ālūsī objects that the verse would then need to say their sleep increased.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r5] author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī rejects the nine-year sleep-extension proposal because it would require an unexpressed meaning, that their sleep itself grew stronger for nine years.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r1] بعضهم | reports | Some say the People of the Book disagreed over the sleepers' duration as they did their number; the verse resolves the disagreement, and the nine-year phrase confirms the answer like the exception in the account of Noah's thousand years. | mentions 29:14
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r2] قتادة | reports | Another view says the sleepers briefly awoke and then returned to sleep, explaining the increase; al-Ālūsī considers this closer than the solar-versus-lunar reckoning explanation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r3] ابن عباس | reports | A view attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās treats the duration statement as the People of the Book's quoted speculation, not God's assertion; he argues that a true duration would not be followed by saying God knows best, and cites the prior reports as conjecture. | mentions 18:22
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r4] author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī doubts this attribution: Ibn ʿAbbās accepted the seven-sleepers view even though it too is followed by God's knowing their number, so the same wording cannot distinguish rejection here. | mentions 18:22
