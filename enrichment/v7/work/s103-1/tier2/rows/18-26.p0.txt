## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:18:25-26/r3] author | holds | Muhammad is told to answer the Christians of Najran that God knows best how long they remained asleep.
[MUQATIL:18:25-26/r4] author | holds | God knows the unseen of the heavens and earth, including what will occur in them; none sees or hears better than He.
[MUQATIL:18:25-26/r5] author | holds | They have no protector besides God who can benefit them, and He shares His judgment with nobody.

## YAHYA-SALLAM: Yaḥyā b. Sallām, d. 200 AH
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p180#2/r1] قتادة | reports | Qatāda says God answers the claimed duration by declaring Himself the knower of how long they stayed and of the unseen in heaven and earth.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p180#2/r2] author | holds | The phrase 'How seeing and hearing He is!' is an Arabic expression like saying that someone is exceedingly discerning; Qatāda says no one sees or hears more than God.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p180#2/r3] author | holds | No protector besides God can shield people from His punishment.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p180#2/r4] author | holds | Two readings are reported: address the Prophet not to associate anyone in judgment, or state that God shares His judgment with no one.

## FARRA: al-Farrāʾ, d. 207 AH
[FARRA:v2p138/r6] author | holds | أبصر به وأسمع is an exclamation meaning how seeing and hearing He is; the same form expresses praise or blame, retaining doubled consonants and weak letters. | mentions 18:38
[FARRA:v2p138/r7] author | holds | The doubled form stays undivided because the exclamatory verb cannot be dualized or pluralized; مد is an exception because ordinary forms مدا and مدوا exist.
[FARRA:v2p139/r1] author | holds | ولا يشرك with ياء is indicative, meaning He does not share; لا تشرك with تاء is a jussive prohibition.

## AKHFASH: al-Akhfash al-Awsaṭ, d. 215 AH
[AKHFASH:v2p429#2/r3] author | holds | أبصر به وأسمع is an exclamation meaning How perceptive and hearing He is!, like أكرم به; a command would instead be أكرمي زيدا.

## IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB: Ibn Qutayba, d. 276 AH
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p266#3/r1] author | holds | Although the preceding verse gave their stay as three hundred years plus nine, this says God knows best because people disputed its duration.
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p266#3/r2] author | holds | Abṣir bihi wa-asmiʿ is an exclamation: how seeing and hearing He is!

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v15p231/r3] author | prefers | Al-Ṭabarī holds that God states their actual stay: three hundred years and nine from entering the cave until He awakened and exposed them; the verse gives this directly. | mentions 18:19, 18:21
[TAB-FULL:v15p231/r4] author | rejects | He rejects treating 18:25 as an unmarked quotation of the People of the Book: the text gives no signal of reported speech, and a claim about God’s words needs proof. | mentions 18:26
[TAB-FULL:v15p231/r5] author | holds | He reads God knows best how long they remained as referring to the time after their souls were taken following their awakening, up to the revelation; only God or one informed by Him knows it.
[TAB-FULL:v15p233/r1] author | holds | God knows the unseen of heaven and earth without anything escaping or being hidden from Him, so people should leave to Him the unknown duration after the youths’ awakening.
[TAB-FULL:v15p234#2/r1] author | holds | Creatures have no protector apart from their Lord to manage their affairs; God alone decides and governs them, without a partner in judgment.
[TAB-FULL:v15p234/r1] author | holds | أبصر به وأسمع is emphatic praise: God sees every existing thing and hears every sound, with nothing hidden from His sight or hearing.
[TAB-FULL:v15p234/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatāda says no one sees or hears better than God.
[TAB-FULL:v15p234/r3] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says God sees people’s deeds and hears them.

## ZAJJAJ: al-Zajjāj, d. 311 AH
[ZAJJAJ:v3p279/r1] author | holds | Scholars agree أبصر به وأسمع means how perfectly seeing and hearing He is; God knows the Cave companions' story.
[ZAJJAJ:v3p279/r2] author | holds | ولا يشرك may be read as prohibition; تشرك may address the listener and prohibit ascribing unseen knowledge to anyone.
[ZAJJAJ:v3p279/r3] author | holds | The third-person reading means God shares His unseen with no one, as stated in the verse about revealing the unseen. | mentions 72:26
[ZAJJAJ:v3p279/r4] author | prefers | A strong legal reading forbids judges from deciding except by God's rule or its indication, lest they share authority to prescribe law.

## IBNMUJAHID: Ibn Mujāhid, d. 324 AH
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p389/r2] ابن عامر | reports | Ibn ʿĀmir alone reads ولا تشرك with tāʾ and jazm; all others read ولا يشرك with yāʾ and رفع.

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v2p453/r9] author | holds | ‘How seeing and hearing He is’ has imperative form with inflection omitted, but functions as an exclamation: how well He hears and sees.

## IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA: Ibn Khālawayh (attr.), d. 370 AH
[IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA:v1p223/r4] author | reports | The yāʾ with indicative رفع reports God's action and makes لا mean ليس; tāʾ with jazm addresses the Messenger and makes لا prohibitive.

## FARISI-HUJJA: Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, d. 377 AH
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p141#2/r1] الباقون | reports | All readers except Ibn ʿĀmir read the third-person indicative يَشْرِكُ, continuing the preceding references to an absent subject; God does not share His judgment with anyone.
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p141#2/r2] ابن عامر | reports | Ibn ʿĀmir reads the second-person jussive prohibition, addressing a person: do not associate anyone in God's judgment. | mentions 7:191, 6:148
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p141#2/r3] author | prefers | The third-person reading is more widespread; the shift from divine absence to direct address is also idiomatic, as in the change to إياك نعبد. | mentions 1:5

## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p593/r1] الأخفش | reports | “How perfectly He sees and hears!” is an exclamation, like “How generous Zayd is!”; if it were a command to a woman, it would take a different form.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p593/r2] الفراء | reports | The referent is God: the construction means “How generous Abdullah is”; “how well they hear and see” in Maryam means the same kind of exclamation. | mentions 19:38
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p593/r3] أبو إسحاق | reports | Scholars agree that the phrase means “How well He hears and sees,” showing His knowledge of the Cave Companions and others.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p593/r4] أبو علي | reports | Arabic can use an imperative-shaped exclamation as a report and a report-shaped phrase as a prayer; the preposition and noun together occupy nominative position. | mentions 19:38, 19:75, 29:52
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p595/r1] author | holds | The imperative-shaped expression has become a conventional exclamation, with no difference in meaning from “How excellent Zayd is!”
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p595/r2] المفسرون | reports | The phrase means that God sees every existent and hears every audible thing; al-Wahidi says scholars agree on this exclamatory reading.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p595/r3] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd explains that God sees the deeds of those in the heavens and earth and hears them.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p595/r4] ابن عباس رواية عطاء | reports | A minority reading takes this as a command: make God's friends see the Quran's marvels and hear through it; the object is omitted, but al-Wahidi says the established view is the exclamation.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p596/r1] المفسرون | reports | “They have no guardian besides Him” means the inhabitants of the heavens and earth have no protector besides God; the pronoun returns to those inhabitants mentioned in the preceding phrase.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p596/r2] عطاء عن ابن عباس | reports | Alternatively, the pronoun refers specifically to believers: having recognized God's majesty and lordship, they take no guardian besides Him.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p596/r3] أبو إسحاق | reports | “No one shares His judgment” can mean no one shares God's knowledge and power concerning the unseen, as in the Jinn verse, or that no judge may rule except by God's rulings and evidence from them. | mentions 72:26
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p596/r4] ابن عامر | reports | Ibn Amir reads “do not associate” as a prohibition addressed to a human against making anyone share God's judgment; it shifts from third person to direct address and echoes the idols verse. | mentions 7:191
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p596/r5] author | prefers | The common “He does not associate” reading fits better because the preceding clause has God as the absent third-person referent.
