## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p358#2/r1] author | holds | The Qur’an sometimes mentions the opposite of what came before to make the contrast clear; after the empty gifts it presents accepted, fruitful charity, forbidding similar misconduct.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p358#2/r2] author | holds | The parable can be parsed as the spending of the spenders being like a planted garden, or as the spender being like a planter of a garden.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p358#2/r3] author | holds | Ibtighāʾ means seeking and is an accusative verbal noun functioning as a circumstantial expression; although it could be causal, its coordination with tathbītan makes the verbal-noun reading correct.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p358#2/r4] مكي | reports | Makkī calls both expressions causal accusatives, but Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects that for tathbītan because spending is not done for the sake of being strengthened.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p358#2/r5] الشعبي والسدي وقتادة وابن زيد وأبو صالح | reports | They interpret tathbītan as certainty: conviction in their own hearts reinforces them in spending in God’s obedience.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p358#2/r6] مجاهد والحسن | reports | Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan understand the phrase as checking where to place charity; al-Ḥasan says one gives only if certain it is for God.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p358#2/r7] author | prefers | Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers the certainty interpretation; the checking interpretation would be expressed by tathabbatan and requires an explicit prior verb to license that unusual verbal noun.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p358#2/r8] قتادة | reports | Qatāda also glosses tathbītan as generosity or goodness from their own selves; Ibn ʿAṭiyya regards this as close to the first view.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r1] author | holds | A garden is a plot whose trees conceal the ground.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r10] ابن كثير وحمزة والكسائي ونافع وأبو عمرو | reports | They read rabwa with ḍamma on rāʾ; ʿĀṣim and Ibn ʿĀmir use fatḥa, Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with kasra, and other readers have forms with a lengthened alif. | mentions 23:50
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r11] عاصم وابن عامر | reports | They read rabwa with fatḥa on the rāʾ. | mentions 23:50
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r12] ابن عباس | reports | A reading attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās has kasra on the rāʾ.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r13] أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الرحمن | reports | They read the variant rabāwa with a lengthened alif after the bāʾ and fatḥa on the initial consonants.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r14] الأشهب العقيلي | reports | Al-Ashhab al-ʿUqaylī reads rabāwa with kasra on the rāʾ.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r15] author | holds | Ātat means “yielded”; akl means edible fruit or food, and its possessive ending indicates association, as in a saddle belonging to a mount, not food consumed by the garden.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r16] ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو | reports | They lighten the hamza in akl when attached to a feminine noun; Abū ʿAmr alone differs in specified masculine or unpossessed forms, while ʿĀṣim and the other named readers use the heavy form throughout.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r2] author | holds | A rabwa is a slight rise with deep, fertile soil, which usually produces better growth.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r3] author | rejects | He rejects al-Ṭabarī’s identification of Riyāḍ al-Ḥuzn as this sort of garden, explaining that those Najd gardens differ from Tihāma’s and have more fragrant growth and cooler air.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r4] author | reports | An Arab woman’s description, “my husband is like Tihāma’s night,” illustrates the region’s oppressive daytime heat.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās defines a rabwa as elevated land without flowing rivers.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r6] author | holds | Ibn ʿAbbās’s description pertains to this particular garden, since it relies on rainfall, not to every hill; 23:50 describes a raised land with a spring. | mentions 23:50
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r7] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says the land is level and does not rise above the water, meaning it is not a mountain or a steep slope.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r8] الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl defines it as elevated, fertile land; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the verse selects a rain-fed location familiar in Arab lands.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p359/r9] السدي | reports | Al-Suddī reads it as rabāwa, a low place; Ibn ʿAṭiyya finds the expression awkward and derives rabwa from growth and increase.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r1] author | holds | Akl means food or something eaten, just as ṭaʿma derives from eating; twofold means twice the amount normally expected or gathered.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r2] author | holds | Even without heavy rain, light drizzle suffices for this fertile garden; ṭall usually means fine light rain, though some call it dew.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r3] ابن عباس وغيره | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās and others give the common-language meaning of ṭall as light rain; a report from Ibn ʿAbbās also supports dew as an alternative.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r4] قوم | reports | Some interpret ṭall as dew, a figurative comparison.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r5] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad supplies the sense “a light rain is enough for it.”
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r6] غيره | reports | Others construe the phrase as “the one that fell on it was light rain.”
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r7] author | holds | The flourishing garden parallels how God nurtures the sincere donors’ charity, like the growth of a young camel described in a report, unlike the bare stone in the preceding parable. | mentions 2:264
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r8] author | holds | God’s seeing all that people do carries both promise and warning.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p360/r9] الزهري | reports | Al-Zuhrī reads “they do” in the third person, perhaps referring to all people or to the donors specifically; in the latter case it is pure promise.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:2:265#2/r1] author | holds | Heavy rain gives the high garden twice the crop it would produce on ordinary lowland; the doubled yield may also mean two harvests yearly, six months apart. | mentions 14:25
[TABRISI:2:265#2/r2] أبو عبد الله | reports | Abu Abd Allah explains the doubled produce as the multiplication of reward for spending to seek God's approval.
[TABRISI:2:265#2/r3] author | holds | Even if heavy rain does not reach it, the garden receives light rain and remains fruitful; its benefit is reliable in either case.
[TABRISI:2:265#2/r4] author | holds | God sees what people do and rewards them accordingly; alternatively, he knows who acts ostentatiously and who sincerely, a warning and encouragement.
[TABRISI:2:265/r1] author | holds | Asim and Ibn Amir read ربوة with fatḥa, most others with ḍamma, and Ibn Abbas has a rare kasra; Ibn Kathir, Nafi, and Abu Amr lighten أكلها while others double it.
[TABRISI:2:265/r2] author | prefers | The author chooses the ḍamma form of ربوة, meaning a raised hill; أكل means produce or food rather than the act of eating, as 14:25 shows. | mentions 14:25
[TABRISI:2:265/r3] author | holds | A thing's double can mean itself plus an equal, while its two doubles mean two equal amounts added; some use double to mean two times the original.
[TABRISI:2:265/r4] author | holds | طل is light rain; the word also names blood left unavenged, a ruin's standing outline, or animal fat, all related to the image of dew or rain.
[TABRISI:2:265/r5] author | holds | In syntax, seeking God's approval is a purpose, تثبيتا coordinates with it, the hill phrase modifies the garden, and the twofold produce describes its state.
[TABRISI:2:265/r6] سعيد بن جبير والسدي والشعبي | reports | Sa'id ibn Jubayr, al-Suddi, and al-Sha'bi explain تثبيتا من أنفسهم as strong conviction and insight in religion.
[TABRISI:2:265/r7] الحسن ومجاهد | reports | Al-Hasan and Mujahid say spenders confirm for themselves where their charity should be placed; the author answers that confirming themselves is itself a valid sense of making firm.
[TABRISI:2:265/r8] أبو علي الجبائي | reports | Abu Ali al-Jubba'i reads تثبيتا as making oneself steadfast in obedience to God.
[TABRISI:2:265/r9] author | holds | A garden on high ground yields better and more than land receiving pooled runoff.
