AWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r1] author | holds | As with their earlier sleep, God raised them so they would ask and dispute about the duration, offering a lesson to observers.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r10] أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم | reports | They read بورقكم with a still rāʾ; Abu Amr's reading is assimilated with slight emphasis.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r11] author | reports | The city they left is called Daqsus; some say its present name is Tarsus.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r12] ابن عباس وعطاء | reports | Azka food means slaughtered meat lawful to eat; most people in the city were idolaters who sacrificed to idols, though some concealed their faith.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r13] سعيد بن جبير والضحاك | reports | A second view makes azka mean lawful food; al-Dahhak says much local property had been seized unjustly.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r14] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says they told their companion to avoid food tainted by injustice or usurpation.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r15] عكرمة | reports | A third interpretation is that azka means more plentiful.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r16] قتادة | reports | A fourth interpretation is better or higher quality.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r17] ابن السائب ومقاتل | reports | A fifth interpretation is tastier.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r18] يمان بن رياب | reports | A sixth interpretation is cheaper.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r19] ابن قتيبة | reports | The root zakā means growth and increase.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r2] author | holds | They entered in the morning and woke at day's end, so first guessed one day, then revised this to part of a day after seeing the sun.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r3] ابن عباس | reports | He identifies the speaker who returned the question to God's knowledge as Yamlīkha, their leader.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r4] ابن عباس | reports | In another report, Ibn Abbas names Miksalmina, the eldest, as the speaker.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r5] أبو سليمان | reports | This disagreement suggests they sensed they had stayed longer than their first estimate.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r6] author | reports | Another explanation for their uncertainty is that they saw how long their nails and hair had grown.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r7] ابن الأنباري | reports | أحدكم means one person from among them; واحدكم might imply a distinguished or honored one.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r8] author | holds | ورق means silver, whether coined into dirhams or not; the report that ʿUrfaja fashioned a silver nose supports this sense.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p120/r9] ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم | reports | They read بورقكم with a light rāʾ carrying kasra.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p122/r1] author | holds | They ask for food to eat, tell the messenger to inspect carefully and act discreetly, and forbid him from revealing their location.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r1] author | holds | Their awakening was like revival from sleep resembling death; they had remained alive without food or drink, and were turned over, so God revived them to question one another about their stay.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r10] المفسرون | reports | Commentators say the youths carried coins bearing the image of the king of their time and that their city was what is now called Tarsus.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r11] author | holds | The episode shows that taking steps to secure food is important and does not negate reliance on God.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r12] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the purest food as lawful slaughter, since most inhabitants were Magians, though some secretly believed.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r13] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid says they sought food farthest from the tyrant's injustice; other glosses understand the phrase as tastiest or cheapest.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r14] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj parses ayyuhā as subject, azkā as its predicate, and ṭaʿāman as a specification.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r15] author | holds | The emissary should act discreetly while entering the city and buying food, and tell nobody where the youths are.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r2] author | holds | Their questioning can itself be an intended purpose: it disclosed extraordinary aspects of God's power, and such disclosure is desirable for its own sake.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r3] المفسرون | reports | Commentators say they entered the cave in the morning and awoke near day's end, explaining why they guessed a day or part of one.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the speaker who returned the duration to God as their leader Yamlikhā, who inferred from their changed hair, nails, and faces that much time had passed.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r5] أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم | reports | Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim read wariqikum with sukūn on rāʾ and fatḥa on wāw; Ibn Kathīr reads kasra on rāʾ and assimilates qāf into kāf.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r6] ابن محيصن | reports | Ibn Muḥayṣin's reading combines kasra on wāw with sukūn on rāʾ and assimilation, which Rāzī rejects because it creates two consecutive sukūns.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r7] author | holds | Wariq means silver, coined or uncoined; a report about ʿArfaja using a silver nose supports the uncoined sense, and several vowel forms are attested.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r8] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ lists the forms waraqu, warqu, and wariqu, and calls kasra on wāw the least good form.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p87/r9] الأزهري | reports | Al-Azharī says riqqa, another word for silver, derives from wariq through the pattern of ṣila and ʿida.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#2/r1] عبد الرحمن بن عوف | reports | A Bukhari report says Abd al-Rahman contracted with Umayya ibn Khalaf to protect each other's dependents in Mecca and Medina; Umayya objected to the name al-Rahman.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#2/r2] الأصمعي | reports | Al-Asmaʿi explains saghiya as a man's adherents who incline toward him, from the verb meaning to incline.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#2/r3] author | holds | Agency is a delegated contract allowed because people need help managing affairs and not everyone can handle every task alone.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#2/r4] author | reports | Jurists support agency from this verse, 9:60, 12:93, and many hadiths, including the report of Urwa al-Bariqi. | mentions 9:60, 12:93
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#2/r5] جابر بن عبد الله | reports | Jabir says he was sent to Khaybar and told by the Prophet to collect fifteen wasqs from his agent, using a hand on the collarbone as proof if asked.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#2/r6] author | holds | Agency is valid in any right that can be delegated, but not for a usurper or an unlawful act.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#2/r7] author | holds | Agency in this story arose from fear of discovery; scholars agree that people with an excuse may appoint agents, while most permit healthy present people too.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#2/r8] أبو حنيفة وسحنون | reports | Abu Hanifa and Sahnun disallow agency for a person present without an excuse; Ibn al-Arabi speculates Sahnun used this against oppressors, while al-Qurtubi permits it for virtuous people.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#3/r1] النبي ﷺ | reports | In Abu Hurayra's report, the Prophet repaid a camel debt with a better camel, and the creditor praised him; the Prophet said the best people pay best.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#3/r2] author | holds | This sound report shows a healthy person present may appoint others: the Prophet authorized his Companions to repay for him, contrary to Abu Hanifa and Sahnun.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#3/r3] ابن خويز منداد | reports | Ibn Khuwayz says the verse supports partnership because the youths shared their money, agency because they sent a buyer, and joint eating despite unequal appetites. | mentions 2:220
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#3/r4] author | reports | The school allows a poor person to mix donated food with a rich person's meal and a business agent to mix provisions and eat together.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#3/r5] ابن العربي | rejects | Ibn al-Arabi says the verse does not prove shared partnership because each youth may have given money separately; he relies instead on two hadiths.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#3/r6] ابن عمر | reports | Ibn Umar cites the prohibition on taking two dates together without a companion's permission as evidence about shared food.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#3/r7] author | reports | The army-of-khabt report is less decisive because Abu Ubayda may simply have distributed equal portions rather than having everyone share one supply.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20#4/r1] author | holds | The permission to mix with orphans as siblings and the permission to eat together or separately support shared eating; the source cites 2:220 and labels the latter 24:16. | mentions 2:220, 24:16
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20/r1] author | holds | Baʿath means movement from stillness; here God awakened the youths in the same clothes and condition as before sleep.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20/r10] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says their silver coins carried the image of the king of their time.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20/r11] ابن عباس | reports | Azka food means meat slaughtered lawfully because townspeople sacrificed in the name of idols; Ibn Abbas also says most inhabitants were Magians.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20/r12] author | reports | Other readings of azka are more blessed, tastier, or cheaper; they may have asked for enough food for several people, with rice, raisins, or dates proposed.

[QURTUBI-FULL:18:19-20/r3] author | holds | The lām in li-yatasaʾalu is a consequence lām, not a purpose: their awakening resulted in their questioning, as in 28:8. | mentions 28:8
