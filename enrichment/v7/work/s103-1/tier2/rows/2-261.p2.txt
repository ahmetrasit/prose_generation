## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:261/r1] author | holds | The comparison means either that the donors' spending resembles a seed or that the donors resemble a person sowing one, with an omitted noun.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:261/r2] author | holds | The seed is credited with sprouting because it is a cause, as earth and water are; God is the true grower, who brings seven branches, each bearing a hundred-grain ear.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:261/r3] author | holds | This is an illustrative parable, not a claim that such growth must actually occur; it may occur in millet or, in fertile land, wheat.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:261/r4] author | holds | God multiplies reward according to His favor and the donor's sincerity and effort, explaining why deeds receive different measures of reward.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:261/r5] author | holds | God's bounty is ample and unconfined, and He knows the donor's intention and the amount given.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p128/r1] author | holds | After demonstrating God's power to revive, the passage urges spending in God's path and promises a great reward that He is able to grant.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p128/r2] author | holds | The comparison omits a possessive: it means the likeness of their expenditure, or the likeness of a sower who plants a seed.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p128/r3] author | holds | God is the true grower, but the seed is credited with growing because it is the cause; one stem branches into seven ears of a hundred grains each.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p128/r4] author | holds | The image makes multiplied rewards visible; millet and sorghum exemplify it, and wheat can sometimes branch to this yield in fertile soil; the parable also works hypothetically.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p128/r5] author | holds | Sanābil replaces the expected plural sunbulāt just as qurūʾ replaces aqrāʾ.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p128/r6] author | holds | God does not multiply seven hundredfold for every spender because conditions differ, or He may increase beyond seven hundred for whom He wills.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p128/r7] الشامي | reports | The Shami reading has yudaʿʿifu; the Makki reading has yuḍāʿifu.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p128/r8] author | holds | God is abundant in grace and generosity and knows spenders' intentions.

[QASHANI:2:261-262/r1] author | holds | The first kind of spending is in the realm of action, given in hope of God's reward; its return begins at seven hundredfold and may grow without limit by divine will.
