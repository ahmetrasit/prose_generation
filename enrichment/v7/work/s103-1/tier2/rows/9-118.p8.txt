## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r1] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | Ka‘b says he missed no expedition besides Tabūk except Badr, for which the Prophet did not reproach anyone; he valued his pledge at al-‘Aqaba above having fought at Badr.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r10] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | The Prophet affirmed that Ka‘b had spoken truthfully and ordered him to rise and wait until God judged his case.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r11] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | Some Banū Salima men urged Ka‘b to give an excuse like the others, saying the Prophet’s prayer would suffice; he nearly retracted, but learned Murāra and Hilāl had also told the truth and stayed firm.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r12] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | The Prophet forbade Muslims to speak with the three, and people shunned them for fifty nights; Ka‘b’s familiar land became strange, while his two companions stayed home weeping.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r13] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | Ka‘b continued attending communal prayers and greeting the Prophet, but the Prophet turned away when their eyes met; Ka‘b’s cousin Abū Qatāda also initially refused to answer whether he knew Ka‘b loved God and His Messenger.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r14] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | A man from Syria brought Ka‘b a letter from the king of Ghassān offering refuge and support; Ka‘b called it another trial and burned it.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r15] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | After forty nights, the Prophet ordered the three to separate from their wives, not divorce them. Hilāl’s wife received permission to serve her elderly, helpless husband without approaching him; Ka‘b declined to ask for similar permission because he was young.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r16] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | On the fiftieth morning, while deeply constricted as the verse describes, Ka‘b heard news on Mount Sal‘ that God had accepted their repentance; he prostrated, and messengers hurried to tell the companions.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r17] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | People congratulated Ka‘b and he borrowed clothes to go to the Prophet; among the Emigrants only Ṭalḥa stood, hurried to greet and congratulate him, a kindness Ka‘b remembered.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r18] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | The Prophet’s face shone with joy; he told Ka‘b that this was the best day since his birth and that the good news came from God.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r19] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | Ka‘b offered to give all his wealth in charity as repentance, but the Prophet advised him to keep part; he retained his share at Khaybar.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r2] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | He was unusually capable and well supplied for Tabūk, having two riding camels available, yet still failed to prepare in time.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r20] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | Ka‘b pledged to speak only truth for the rest of his life and said that, after Islam, no blessing surpassed being truthful rather than perishing through lies; he hoped God would protect him from lying. | mentions 9:117, 9:118, 9:119
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r21] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | Ka‘b says God’s revelation condemned the oath-taking liars and that he and his two companions were left waiting because their cases were deferred, not because they had simply failed to join the expedition. | mentions 9:95, 9:96
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r3] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | Unlike usual expeditions, the Prophet disclosed the destination because Tabūk was hot, distant, desert terrain, and faced a large enemy; the numerous Muslims were not all registered, so an absence might go unnoticed.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r4] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | The expedition began during the season of ripe fruit and pleasant shade; Ka‘b repeatedly delayed preparations, assuming he could make them later.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r5] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | After the army departed, Ka‘b kept trying to prepare but did nothing, then regretted not mounting a camel to catch up; in Medina he encountered only hypocrites or people with excuses.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r6] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | At Tabūk the Prophet asked what Ka‘b had done; a man of Banū Salima blamed his clothes and appearance, while Mu‘ādh b. Jabal defended him as known only for good, and the Prophet remained silent.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r7] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | On the return, Ka‘b considered lying and sought advice, but when the Prophet arrived he abandoned false excuses and resolved to tell the truth.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r8] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | More than eighty others offered excuses and swore oaths; the Prophet accepted their outward declarations, left their inward state to God, and prayed for their forgiveness. Ka‘b admitted he had no excuse.
[ELMALILI:9:118#2/r9] Ka‘b b. Mâlik | reports | Ka‘b reasoned that a lie might please the Prophet briefly but would anger God against him, whereas truthful admission could leave him hoping for God’s forgiveness.
[ELMALILI:9:118#3/r1] Ulemâ | reports | The scholars call repentance like that of Ka‘b and his companions sincere repentance: regret should make the world feel constricted and the self press painfully, leaving one to turn to God alone with sincerity.
[ELMALILI:9:118/r1] author | holds | The three left behind are Ka‘b b. Mālik, Hilāl b. Umayya, and Murāra b. al-Rabī‘, whose cases were deferred, unlike those whose confession and repentance were accepted immediately; Ka‘b had pledged at al-‘Aqaba and his companions fought at Badr. | mentions 9:106
[ELMALILI:9:118/r2] author | holds | The earth felt constricted because people would not look at or deal with them, and they could find nowhere to stay with reassurance.
[ELMALILI:9:118/r3] author | holds | Their own consciences also tormented them: they were deeply distressed and could find neither peace nor consolation; their hearts became a prison.
[ELMALILI:9:118/r4] author | holds | They understood through experience that only turning to God in repentance and seeking forgiveness could save them from His anger, so they abandoned other hopes and trusted wholly in Him.
[ELMALILI:9:118/r5] author | reports | The phrase that God turned to them so they might repent can mean that He revealed a verse confirming acceptance, or that He again regarded them with mercy, acceptance, and enabling so they would remain firm in repentance.
[ELMALILI:9:118/r6] author | holds | God alone is the abundant acceptor of repentance and the possessor of limitless mercy; no sin is too numerous or great for His gracious acceptance of repentance.
