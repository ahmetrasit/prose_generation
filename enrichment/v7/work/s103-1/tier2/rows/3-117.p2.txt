## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:117/r1] author | holds | The word mā can be a verbal noun or a relative pronoun with an omitted object pronoun; 'like a wind' means like a gust of wind.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:117/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says ṣirr is severe cold; one etymology connects it to the sound of a harsh wind.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:117/r3] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says ṣirr is the sound of flames in the wind.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:117/r4] author | holds | The verse compares disbelievers' spending, which is void and useless, to a crop destroyed by cold wind or fire after its owners expected a harvest; the meaning was also treated in al-Baqara.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH

[BAYDAWI-FULL:3:114-117/r7] author | holds | Sirr is intense cold, commonly used of a cold wind; the comparison is a composite image of a cold wind destroying a crop belonging to people who sinned.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:3:114-117/r8] author | reports | God did not wrong either the donors by wasting their gifts or the crop's owners by destroying it; they wronged themselves by unbelief, sin, or spending without a valid aim.
