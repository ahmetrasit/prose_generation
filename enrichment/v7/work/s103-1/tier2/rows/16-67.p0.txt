## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:16:64-67/r4] author | holds | Sakar means intoxicating drink made from dates or grapes, while good provision means wholesome, non-intoxicating drink and fruit; Muqatil says the intoxicant permission is abrogated by a verse in al-Ma'ida.
[MUQATIL:16:64-67/r5] author | holds | He glosses “goodly loan” in 2:245 as giving with a willing heart, illustrating the sense of good provision. | mentions 2:245

## YAHYA-SALLAM: Yaḥyā b. Sallām, d. 200 AH
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p72#2/r2] author | holds | God made the fruits of date palms and grapes available for intoxicants and wholesome provision.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p72#2/r3] ابن مجاهد | reports | Ibn Mujāhid's father identifies the intoxicant as wine made before its prohibition and wholesome provision as food.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p72#2/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās distinguishes prohibited fruit products as intoxicants and permitted products as wholesome provision.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p72#2/r5] قتادة | reports | Qatāda says the verse preceded wine's prohibition; wholesome provision includes eating, pressing, infusing, and pickling the permitted fruit, while sugar refers to foreign wines.

[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p72#2/r8] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | ʿUmar says fermented drinks derive from five ingredients—dates, raisins, honey, wheat, and barley—and whatever is fermented and aged is wine.

## TUSTARI: Sahl al-Tustarī, d. 283 AH

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v14p274/r1] author | holds | The verse omits a noun after “from the fruits,” meaning “some of what comes from the palms and grapes”; من already signals a partitive selection.
[TAB-FULL:v14p274/r2] بعض نحويي البصرة | reports | A Basran grammarian supplies شيء as the omitted noun; the pronoun in منه refers to that implied thing, and تتخذون describes it.
[TAB-FULL:v14p274/r3] بعضهم | reports | Some interpreters say sakar means wine and good provision means dates and raisins, and that the verse preceded the later prohibition of wine.
[TAB-FULL:v14p274/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas interprets sakar as the prohibited drink and good provision as what is permitted from the fruit.
[TAB-FULL:v14p277/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas repeatedly glosses sakar as what is forbidden from the two fruits and good provision as what is permitted.
[TAB-FULL:v14p277/r2] سعيد بن جبير | reports | Sa‘id ibn Jubayr says the forbidden part of the fruits is sakar and the permitted part is good provision.
[TAB-FULL:v14p279/r1] أبو رزين | reports | Abu Razin says the verse came while people still drank wine, before its prohibition.
[TAB-FULL:v14p279/r2] إبراهيم، الشعبي، أبو رزين | reports | Ibrahim, al-Sha‘bi, and Abu Razin regard the verse as abrogated; Ibrahim specifies that the wine prohibition abrogated it.
[TAB-FULL:v14p279/r3] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says God mentioned wine as a blessing before wine was prohibited; another report contrasts forbidden sakar with permitted provision.
[TAB-FULL:v14p281/r1] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid identifies sakar as wine before its prohibition and good provision as food, or as fresh dates and grapes.
[TAB-FULL:v14p281/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatada says sakar is foreign wine and good provision includes fermented drinks, vinegar, and food; the verse preceded wine’s later prohibition in al-Ma’ida.
[TAB-FULL:v14p281/r3] قتادة | reports | Another Qatada report says sakar is foreign wine, later abrogated in al-Ma’ida, while good provision includes fermented drinks, vinegar, and food.
[TAB-FULL:v14p281/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says people called wine sakar; he recounts the Wadi al-Sukran custom of accompanying travelers from Syria and says Abyssinians use sakar for vinegar.
[TAB-FULL:v14p281/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says good provision means lawful dates, raisins, and other non-intoxicating products.
[TAB-FULL:v14p281/r6] آخرون | reports | Another group says sakar is not wine itself but a fermented date or raisin infusion that becomes intoxicating and is prohibited like wine.
[TAB-FULL:v14p283/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the verse preceded wine’s prohibition and that intoxicating sakar is forbidden like wine; lawful provision includes dates, raisins, and vinegar.
[TAB-FULL:v14p283/r2] ابن عباس | reports | He says that after wine was prohibited, sakar was also prohibited as part of wine, while vinegar, lawful nabidh, and similar drinks remained permitted. | mentions 2:219
[TAB-FULL:v14p283/r3] عبد الله | reports | Abdullah, Ibn Abi Layla, Ibrahim, and Ibrahim with Abu Razin identify sakar as wine.
[TAB-FULL:v14p283/r4] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak defines sakar as whatever intoxicates from grapes or dates, and good provision as their fruit.
[TAB-FULL:v14p283/r5] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says the fruit was lawful until people altered it and made sakar from it.
[TAB-FULL:v14p283/r6] آخرون | reports | Another group takes sakar to mean any lawful drink such as non-intoxicating nabidh, vinegar, or fresh dates, and good provision as dates and raisins.
[TAB-FULL:v14p284/r1] الشعبي | reports | Al-Sha‘bi rejects the Nabataean intoxicating drink as sakar here; the verse means lawful nabidh or vinegar, with dates and raisins as good provision.
[TAB-FULL:v14p284/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says people made nabidh from dates and used dates and raisins as food or good provision.
[TAB-FULL:v14p284/r3] الشعبي | reports | Al-Sha‘bi again identifies sakar with nabidh and vinegar, and provision with dates and raisins.
[TAB-FULL:v14p284/r4] author | prefers | Al-Tabari prefers the lawful-drink interpretation, under which the verse remains in force rather than abrogated.
[TAB-FULL:v14p284/r5] author | holds | Sakar has four Arabic senses: intoxicating drink, food, stillness, and the verbal noun for becoming intoxicated; poetry illustrates food and stillness.
[TAB-FULL:v14p284/r6] author | holds | Because intoxicants are prohibited but the verse supplies no proof that non-intoxicating lawful fruit drinks are forbidden or abrogated, al-Tabari rules out wine, intoxicants, sakar itself, and stillness here.
[TAB-FULL:v14p286/r1] author | holds | The blessings of livestock, palms, and grapevines are clear signs for people who understand God’s proofs and heed His admonitions.
