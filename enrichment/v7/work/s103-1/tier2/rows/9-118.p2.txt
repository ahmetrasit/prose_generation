## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p412/r1] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak and Abu Malik say the three were left behind from repentance, with their acceptance delayed; they had not tied themselves to Abu Lubaba.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p412/r2] عكرمة | reports | Ikrima says they were left behind from the Prophet's expedition.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p412/r3] author | holds | The three were Hilal ibn Umayya, Murara ibn al-Rabi, and Ka'b ibn Malik.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p412/r4] author | holds | The earth felt constricted because Muslims stopped speaking to them, and their own selves because they endured this estrangement.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p412/r5] author | holds | They knew that only God could be a refuge who might pardon them and accept their repentance.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p412/r6] كعب بن مالك | reports | Ka'b ibn Malik says God accepted their repentance fifty nights after the Prophet returned from the Tabuk expedition.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p412/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas takes 'so that they might repent' to mean becoming steadfast; their repentance had preceded the test, which was meant to reform them and others.

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p84#2/r1] author | holds | The three left behind are those deferred in 9:106, and the phrase means that God accepted their repentance; Ibn Abbas and Mujahid say they were held back from its acceptance. | mentions 9:106
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p84#2/r2] كعب بن مالك | reports | Kaʿb ibn Malik, one of the three who stayed without excuse, says their absence was not the point: the Prophet had postponed deciding their case, as 9:106 says. | mentions 9:106
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p84#2/r3] المفسرون | reports | The earth felt constricted because believers would not speak or trade with the three, their wives were told to separate, and the Prophet turned away until their pardon after fifty days.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p84/r2] author | holds | The repeated mention of God's repentance is not redundant: pardon comes first as grace before the sin is mentioned, then is repeated; it may also mean mercy upon mercy.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p84/r3] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet says God forgives a Muslim's sin twenty times; Ibn Abbas glosses 'then He turned to them' as increased approval.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p85/r1] ابن عباس | reports | The constriction of their selves is distress in their hearts and loneliness when no believer spoke to them; they knew there was no refuge from God's punishment except with Him.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p85/r2] صاحب النظم | reports | The order of the verse implies that recognizing their sin included an inward request for repentance; God accepted and showed mercy to that sincere intention before the text resumes with 'then He turned to them'.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p85/r3] ابن عباس | reports | The second mention of God's turning to them emphasizes increased approval and protection.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p85/r4] author | holds | God's turning to them 'so that they might repent' means He gently enabled and guided them; without His will and enabling, a servant cannot repent.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p85/r5] ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbari also reads the phrase as God's turning so they persist in repentance or benefit from its reward; both depend on God's prior turning to them.

## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v5p3256/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the three delayed their decision and did not confess their sins; they were not hardened hypocrites but deferred, to be forgiven if they repented and punished if they persisted.
[JISHUMI:v5p3256/r2] author | reports | Another account says they had repented, but God then revealed the verse naming the three and accepted their repentance.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r1] author | holds | The common passive reading خلفوا means they were left behind by others; ʿIkrima's active form means they stayed back after the Messenger and failed in their duty.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r10] author | reports | Exegetes agree the verse concerns Kaʿb ibn Mālik, Murāra ibn al-Rabīʿ, and Hilāl ibn Umayya, believers who stayed behind with hypocrites and offered excuses; they numbered among more than eighty who stayed.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r11] الأصم | reports | Al-Aṣamm says the three were Muslims without hypocrisy: one stayed to tend his land, one was newly married, and the third had neither family nor property and was negligent.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r2] عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima reads خلفوا with both consonants open and no doubling, deriving it from staying behind after the Messenger.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r3] جعفر بن محمد الصادق | reports | Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq reads خالفوا, meaning they opposed or acted differently.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r4] الأعمش | reports | Al-Aʿmash reads المخلفين.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r5] author | holds | Khalf can mean the opposite of front, a successor, or poor speech; the lexicon also explains خلفه and related forms for substitution and succession.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r6] author | holds | Raḥb means breadth or spaciousness, as in welcoming someone with room, describing a wide place, or urging horses to spread out.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r7] author | holds | A maljaʾ is a place of refuge; to take refuge is to seek protection there, while iljāʾ means compelling someone into a place by preventing alternatives.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r8] author | holds | The conjunction in وعلى الثلاثة coordinates them with God's repentance toward the Prophet and Emigrants; الثلاثة identifies a known trio, not a generic three.
[JISHUMI:v5p3284#2/r9] author | holds | لا ملجأ is accusative under negation, as in لا رفث; it may also be written with nunation to mean that there is no refuge.
