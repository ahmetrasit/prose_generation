## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:2:187/r1] author | holds | The permission for marital intercourse on Ramadan nights was a concession for believers after Umar's conduct.
[MUQATIL:2:187/r2] author | holds | Rafath means intercourse, and spouses are garments for one another in the sense that each is the other's repose.
[MUQATIL:2:187/r3] author | holds | The self-betrayal refers to Umar's intercourse with his wife; khiyāna here means sin or disobedience.
[MUQATIL:2:187/r4] author | holds | Muqatil glosses the parallel uses of khiyāna in 66:10 and 5:13 as disobedience. | mentions 66:10
[MUQATIL:2:187/r5] author | holds | God forgave them by leaving them unpunished, and intercourse is permitted throughout the whole night.
[MUQATIL:2:187/r6] author | holds | The command to seek what God prescribed means seeking offspring, and it was revealed concerning Ṣirma b. Anas.
[MUQATIL:2:187/r7] author | holds | The white thread is the first crosswise light of dawn, distinguishing daylight from the darkness of night.
[MUQATIL:2:187/r8] author | reports | The ban on intercourse during retreat was revealed about Ali, Ammar b. Yasir, and Abu Ubayda, who left the mosque at night and returned after sex and washing.
[MUQATIL:2:187/r9] author | holds | Intercourse with women is forbidden both night and day while one is in mosque retreat; these are God's limits, meant to protect against sins during retreat.

## FARRA: al-Farrāʾ, d. 207 AH
[FARRA:v1p114/r4] author | reports | Abdullah's reading has فلا رفوث ولا فسوق; رفوث means intercourse, and it is nominative because أحل is passive.
[FARRA:v1p114/r5] author | reports | الآن باشروهن refers to the newly revealed permission, previously unavailable; ما كتب الله لكم is explained as offspring or read واتبعوا, and Ibn Abbas says the two readings mean the same.

## AKHFASH: al-Akhfash al-Awsaṭ, d. 215 AH
[AKHFASH:v1p139/r1] author | holds | A masculine demonstrative after feminine فتنة is explained by an understood masculine noun: this matter you were hastening. | mentions 51:14, 6:78
[AKHFASH:v1p139/r2] author | holds | In الرفث إلى نسائكم, إلى is used because rafath here has the meaning of ifḍāʾ; normally one says rafatha bi-imraʾatihi.
[AKHFASH:v1p139/r3] author | holds | Arabic usage can put bi in place of إلى or على, as in أحسن بي for أحسن إلي, غما بغم for غما على غم, and تأمنه بقنطار for على قنطار. | mentions 12:100, 3:153, 3:75
[AKHFASH:v1p139/r4] الشاعر | reports | The poet’s علي is used for عني, providing evidence that Arabic prepositions can substitute for one another.
[AKHFASH:v1p139/r5] author | holds | خلوا إلى شياطينهم can be compared with both خلوت إليه and خلوت به; إلى may also mean مع, as in من أنصاري إلى الله, while من can mean على in ونصرناه من القوم. | mentions 2:14, 3:52, 21:74
