## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#2/r1] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet says a miser who refuses a relative's request for surplus wealth will be brought on Judgment Day as a panting fire-serpent that collars him; no grading is stated.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#2/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and other scholars say the verse instead concerns the People of the Book concealing what they knew about Muhammad; on this reading, 'will be collared' means they will bear punishment, from the sense of capacity in 2:184. | mentions 2:184
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#2/r3] إبراهيم النخعي | reports | Ibrahim al-Nakha'i says they will be given a fiery collar on Judgment Day, an interpretation that fits the financial reading.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#2/r4] author | reports | Another explanation is that people will be bound to their deeds as a neck is bound by a collar, paralleling 17:13; a poem by Abdullah ibn Jahsh to Abu Sufyan uses the image of burdening someone like a dove's collar. | mentions 17:13
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#2/r5] author | holds | Bukh is withholding a right that is due; refusing something not owed is not blameworthy. Hijaz and other Arabs differ in the verb's morphology.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#2/r6] النحاس | reports | Al-Nahhas notes that Hijaz speakers say yabkhalūna/bakhilū, while other Arabs use the other conjugation; Ibn Faris gives additional noun and verb forms.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#2/r7] رسول الله | reports | In a report cited by al-Mawardi, the Prophet asks the Ansar to name their leader; when they name the stingy Jad ibn Qays, he says no disease is worse than miserliness and tells a story of hosts avoiding guests until men and women become sexually occupied with their own sex.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#3/r1] author | reports | Views differ on whether bukh and shuḥḥ are synonymous: bukh is withholding what one has, while shuḥḥ is eagerness to acquire what one lacks; another says shuḥḥ is greed, which the author prefers.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#3/r2] رسول الله | reports | Jabir reports the Prophet warning against injustice and greed, which destroyed earlier peoples, led them to bloodshed, and made them violate sacred things; Muslim transmits it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#3/r3] author | rejects | This report refutes the claim that shuḥḥ is only refusal of recommended charity: that would not merit such severe warnings and ruin in both worlds.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#3/r4] رسول الله | reports | Abu Hurayra reports a Prophetic statement that fighting dust and Hell's smoke never meet in a Muslim's nostrils, nor do faith and greed coexist; al-Nasa'i transmits it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#3/r5] author | holds | The warning shows shuḥḥ is more blameworthy than bukh, although a report that a believer cannot be miserly suggests a degree of equivalence.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#3/r6] author | holds | God says He will inherit the heavens and earth: after creation and ownership disappear, He remains; human inheritance is only an analogy, since God already owned everything and human wealth was a loan returned to its owner.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180#4/r1] author | holds | The parallel in 19:40 teaches people to spend before death rather than leave their wealth as God's inheritance; only what they spent benefits them. | mentions 19:40
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180/r1] الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil, Sibawayh, and al-Farra supply an omitted object: people should not suppose stinginess is good for them; the omission is signaled by the verb 'are stingy.'
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180/r2] author | holds | The omitted noun is illustrated by 'whoever is truthful, it is better for him,' where 'truth' is understood from 'truthful'; a poem similarly uses 'fool' to imply folly.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180/r3] حمزة | reports | Al-Nahhas calls Hamza's second-person reading very remote; it can be parsed as forbidding one to consider the stinginess of the stingy good, like 'ask the village.'
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180/r4] author | reports | The pronoun huwa is an emphatic separator for Basran grammarians and a supporting particle for Kufans; it can also be the subject of a nominal sentence meaning 'it is better for them.'
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180/r5] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad says the sīn in 'they will be collared' signals a threat: they will be made to wear it later.
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180/r6] ابن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masud, Ibn Abbas, Abu Wa'il, Abu Malik, al-Suddi, al-Sha'bi, and others say the verse concerns withholding wealth, spending in God's path, and obligatory zakat; Abu Hurayra reports the zakat-evader transformed into a bald serpent that collars him and says, 'I am your wealth, your treasure.'
[QURTUBI-FULL:3:180/r7] رسول الله | reports | A report from Ibn Masud says anyone who withholds zakat will be made into a bald snake that collars him; the Prophet recites this verse as proof. Ibn Majah transmits it.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:3:180/r1] author | holds | The readings vary in person and syntax; with a second-person reading, an omitted noun is supplied to make the two objects agree, while third-person readings can take either the Prophet or the stingy as subject.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:3:180/r2] author | holds | Stinginess is evil because it brings punishment; on the day of resurrection its consequences will cling to the miser like a collar.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:3:180/r3] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A prophetic report says that one who fails to pay zakat will have his wealth made into a serpent around his neck on the Day of Resurrection.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:3:180/r4] author | reports | God's inheritance of heaven and earth means either that all their contents belong to Him, so misers withhold His property, or that He inherits what they hoard after they die, leaving them regret and punishment.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:3:180/r5] author | holds | God knows both withholding and giving and will recompense them; Nafi, Ibn Amir, Asim, Hamza, and al-Kisa'i read the address in the second person, intensifying the threat.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p194#2/r1] author | holds | The t- and y-readings allow different implied subjects: on one parse bukhul is supplied, هو is a separator, and خير is the second object; with the y-reading the subject may be the Prophet or another person.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p194#2/r2] author | holds | If the hoarders themselves are subject, the understood phrase is 'their miserliness'; هو remains a separator and خير the second object.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p194#2/r3] author | holds | Miserliness is evil because wealth will leave them while its burden remains with them.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p194#2/r4] author | reports | A hadith says a person withholding zakat will have the wealth made into a male bald snake with two fangs, placed around his neck to bite and drive him toward the Fire.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p194#2/r5] author | holds | God inherits everything in the heavens and earth; the hoarders withhold what belongs to Him. Mawārith derives from mawārith with wāw changed to yāʾ after kasra.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p194#2/r6] المكي وأبو عمرو | reports | They read the closing verb with yāʾ; the t-reading is a rhetorical shift that intensifies the threat, while yāʾ follows the apparent subject.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:3:179-180/r4] author | holds | Those who hoard God's gifts of wealth, knowledge, power, or self instead of giving them to the deserving, prophets, and saints will bear them as a collar causing deprivation, disgrace, and a veil from divine beauty.
[QASHANI:3:179-180/r5] author | holds | Souls, powers, abilities, knowledge, wealth, and all that exists already belong to God as the inheritance of heaven and earth, so miserliness with them is senseless.
