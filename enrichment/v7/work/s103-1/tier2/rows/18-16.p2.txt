## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:14-18/r5] author | holds | The youths address one another about abandoning the people and their idols; the exception may exclude worship of God, be a verbal noun meaning worship, or be a divine parenthesis affirming their monotheism.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:14-18/r6] author | holds | God's mercy includes expanded provision and benefit in both worlds; the youths speak confidently because of their firm trust in His favor.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6/r1] author | holds | The believers address one another after firmly resolving to flee with their religion; what they worship is grammatically coordinated to the pronoun, meaning abandon the people and their objects of worship.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6/r2] author | reports | The exception may be connected because the people acknowledged the Creator but associated partners; or disconnected, referring to unbelievers and their idols; alternatively, it is God's parenthetical report that the youths worshipped none but Him.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6/r3] author | holds | They tell each other to make the cave their refuge; God will spread mercy, meaning provision, and prepare a useful means of ease.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6/r4] المدني والشامي | reports | The Madinan and Shami readings have marfiqan.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6/r5] author | holds | Their confidence and strong hope rest on trust in God and clear certainty; alternatively, a prophet of their time told them this.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:18:16/r1] author | holds | Separation means disengaging from selves and faculties; retreat to the body permits disciplined use of bodily tools for knowledge and action, with voluntary death from lower impulses.
[QASHANI:18:16/r2] author | holds | God's mercy gives true life through knowledge, while its utility is delight in witnessing and perfection; a cited verse describes one formerly dead who receives light. | mentions 6:122
[QASHANI:18:16/r3] عليه السلام | reports | A saying about Abū Bakr calls him a dead man walking on earth, meaning dead to himself and walking through God.
[QASHANI:18:16/r4] author | holds | Alternatively, leaving the community's idols and desires means retreating into bodily caves, limiting excess and practicing discipline; God gives celestial aid and a useful path through which others may be guided.
[QASHANI:18:16/r5] author | holds | The cave refuge hints at the Mahdi entering a cave before emergence and Jesus's descent; latent mercy unfolds through bodily attachment and its preparations for perfection.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r1] أبو جعفر والأعرج وشيبة وحميد وابن سعدان ونافع وابن عامر وأبو بكر وأبو عمرو | reports | These readers pronounce مرفقا with an open mīm and kasra on fāʾ; other readers use a kasra mīm and open fāʾ, and both forms are reported for a facility and a bodily joint.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r2] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ says he knows only kasra on mīm for the practical facility, the hand joint, and every sense.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r3] الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisāʾī denies that the bodily joint is مرفق except with open mīm and kasra fāʾ; Abū Ḥātim disagrees, treating that form as a place noun.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r4] أبو زيد | reports | Abū Zayd analyzes مرفق as a verbal noun like رفق on the pattern مَفْعَل.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ says Hijaz speakers use open mīm and kasra fāʾ for a useful facility and reverse the vowels for the human joint; other Arabs may use kasra mīm for both.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r6] معاذ | reports | Muʿādh permits opening both mīm and fāʾ.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150/r1] author | holds | The youths address one another; withdrawing from their people means both leaving their homeland and rejecting their beliefs, a bodily and inward separation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150/r10] author | holds | The cited Zamzam line uses that water in place of another, illustrating Ibn al-Anbārī’s explanation of مرفقا as a substitute.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150/r4] author | holds | If the townspeople did not know or worship God, إلا الله is a disconnected exception because God was not among the objects they worshipped.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150/r5] author | holds | The wording وما يعبدون من دوننا in ʿAbd Allāh’s codex is explanatory gloss, not Qurʾān; it conflicts with the universally transmitted consonantal text وما يعبدون إلا الله.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150/r6] author | reports | An alternative makes وما يعبدون إلا الله a parenthetical divine statement that the youths worshipped no one but God, with إلا governed by an unrestricted exception.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16#2/r1] author | holds | من أمركم attaches to the preceding verb; من may mark origin or part.

[SAMIN-DURR:18:16#2/r4] الفَرّاء | reports | Al-Farra's poem uses both forms for body joints; al-Zajjaj says both forms may denote a joint or aid.

[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r1] author | holds | إذ اعتزلتموهم is governed by omitted speech, meaning someone told the others what to do when they separated.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r10] مكي | reports | Maki quotes al-Farra as knowing only kasra; the author says the mutawatir Nafi and Syrian readings disprove that.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r11] الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisa'i rejects kasra for the joint, recognizing only fatḥa, opposite al-Farra's report.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r12] author | reports | Al-Farra reports Hijaz usage with fatḥa-mim for aid and kasra for the elbow; other Arabs use kasra for both; Muadh allows both open as a مصدر.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r2] بعضهم | reports | Another analysis makes إذ causal (“because you withdrew”); the author rejects it.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r3] author | reports | If ما is relative, it means those worshipped, with an omitted object; إلا الله can be connected if they worshipped God and associates, or disconnected if only idols.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r4] author | reports | Under the relative parse, the report says they either worshipped God while associating others or worshipped only idols; the exception's antecedent may be ما or its object pronoun.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r5] author | reports | If ما is a مصدر particle, it means abandoning worship; إلا الله supplies omitted عبادة and has the same two exception options.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r6] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari takes ما as negative and the clause as God's parenthetical comment that they worship none but Him; إلا is free-standing.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r7] أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa offers connected or disconnected exceptions under the negative reading; the author says his presentation wrongly ties both to that one reading.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r8] author | holds | Most read مرفقا with kasra ميم and fatḥa فاء; Nafi and Ibn Amir reverse the vowels.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:16/r9] ثعلب | reports | One view says the two forms are synonymous for something leaned on, not a مصدر; another distinguishes a joint from a task.
