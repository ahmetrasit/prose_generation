## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p693/r1] author | holds | God praises those who give in His cause and then neither boast to recipients nor remind them of their gift in word or deed.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p693/r2] author | holds | They do not mistreat beneficiaries in a way that cancels their earlier kindness; their reward is with God, they need not fear future terrors, and they will not grieve over lost family or worldly pleasures.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p693/r6] أبو ذر | reports | The Prophet warned of painful punishment for three people: one who flaunts what he gave, one who lets his garment trail, and a merchant who uses false oaths.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p693/r7] أبو الدرداء | reports | A report says that a person disobedient to parents, one who flaunts charity, a habitual wine drinker, and a denier of divine decree do not enter Paradise; other transmitters report a similar version.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p694/r1] ابن عمر | reports | Another prophetic report lists the disobedient child, habitual wine drinker, and person who flaunts gifts among three whom God will not look upon at the Resurrection.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p694/r2] ابن عباس | reports | A report through Mujāhid says habitual wine drinkers, those disobedient to parents, and those who flaunt gifts do not enter Paradise; other transmissions attribute related reports to Mujāhid from Abū Saʿīd and Abū Hurayra.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p516/r1] author | holds | The text attributes the wealth to the spenders and stresses spending in God's way to encourage sincere care in preserving its reward.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p516/r2] الحرالي | reports | Mann is reminding the recipient of one's gift, which cuts the relationship by looking down on the recipient; adhā is telling others in a way that humiliates the recipient.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p516/r3] author | holds | Both conditions must be met: repeating no before injury as well as mann emphasizes that the absence of either fault is required for reward.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p516/r4] author | holds | The reward is introduced without a causal conjunction to show that it is a generous, initial gift from God rather than a payment caused by their conduct; forbidden conduct forfeits it.
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p516/r5] author | holds | God as their Lord nurtures and preserves donations until they grow beyond description; those rewarded need fear no loss or regret no omission because He supplies every fitting good.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v1p342/r1] السعدي | reports | Saʿdī's poem recounts ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz selling his precious gem-set ring during a famine and giving the proceeds to the needy rather than remain comfortable while citizens suffered.
[BURSEVI:v1p342/r2] السعدي | reports | The poem praises preferring people's comfort over one's own and refusing to keep luxury while the poor are distressed.
[BURSEVI:v1p342/r6] author | holds | The spenders put wealth in its proper place; ثم signals the high rank of adding restraint from boasting and harm after the gift.
[BURSEVI:v1p342/r7] author | holds | Mann is reminding recipients of one's favor as if they now owe a debt; harm includes reproaching them for ingratitude, repeated requests, or annoyance.
[BURSEVI:v1p342/r8] author | holds | Those who give without boasting or harm receive their reward from God, face no fear of future punishment, and do not grieve over worldly things left behind.
[BURSEVI:v1p343/r1] author | reports | A story says Hasan ibn ʿAli sells Fatima's shirt for six dirhams, gives the money to a beggar, then arranges a deferred camel purchase and resale; the Prophet identifies the beggar, seller, and buyer as angels.
[BURSEVI:v1p343/r2] author | reports | Some exegetes say the two verses concern ʿUthmān, who equipped the Tabuk army with a thousand camels and a thousand dinars, and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, who gave half his wealth.
[BURSEVI:v1p343/r3] author | reports | The Prophet prayed that God be pleased with ʿUthmān; he blessed ʿAbd al-Raḥmān's retained and donated money, and both gave without boasting or hurting recipients.
[BURSEVI:v1p343/r4] author | reports | One opinion compares boasting to hypocrisy and harm to ostentation.
[BURSEVI:v1p343/r5] author | reports | Scholars differ on consequences: one view denies reward and assigns sin, Wahb denies both reward and sin, and another preserves the basic reward but removes multiplication and adds sin for boasting.
[BURSEVI:v1p343/r6] author | holds | Although God may bestow favor on His servants, humans cannot boast: God's ownership and power are complete, while any virtue in a servant comes from God.
[BURSEVI:v1p343/r7] author | holds | The recipient already feels humbled by need; displaying the gift further crushes the recipient and turns a benefit into harm and mistreatment.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r1] author | holds | The passage begins by explaining the proper manner of the previously praised spending: donors must not follow gifts with reminders of favor or injury to recipients.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r2] author | holds | Man originally means cutting, and injury means patronizing or boasting over recipients; man precedes injury because it occurs more often.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r3] author | holds | The word thumma signals the greater rank and distance between giving and avoiding reproach or harm; al-Intiṣāf instead reads it as prolonged persistence in avoiding them, which the author considers preferable.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r4] author | prefers | Al-Intiṣāf’s interpretation makes thumma signify enduring continued conduct, as in steadfastness after initial commitment and lasting guidance, and preserves its core sense of temporal extension. | mentions 41:30, 28:22
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r5] author | reports | Al-Wāḥidī, transmitting from al-Kalbī with an explicit reservation, says the verse was revealed about ʿUthmān and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf’s large charitable contributions.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r6] author | reports | ʿAbd al-Raḥmān gave four thousand dirhams, half of his eight thousand savings, as a loan to God; the Prophet blessed both the gift and what he kept for his family.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r7] author | reports | ʿUthmān pledged to equip anyone lacking equipment on the Tabūk expedition, supplied a thousand camels with gear, and gave the Muslims his well of Rumah.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r8] أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | Abū Saʿīd says he saw the Prophet praying for ʿUthmān with raised hands until dawn, after which the verse about those who spend was revealed.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p33/r9] author | holds | The promise that donors have their reward with their Lord is restated for emphasis and honor; omitting an expected conditional fa suggests their inner good intention merits reward in itself.
[ALUSI-FULL:v3p34/r1] author | holds | The promise means fear and grief will be absent continuously, rather than merely denying that their absence itself will ever end.
