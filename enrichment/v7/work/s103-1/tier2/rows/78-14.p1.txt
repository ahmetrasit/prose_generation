## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:78:2-16/r15] مجاهد وقتادة والكلبي | reports | They interpret the “mu'sirat” as winds carrying cyclones; al-Azhari says min can mean bi because wind draws out rain.
[TABRISI:78:2-16/r16] الربيع وأبي العالية | reports | Another reading takes the mu'sirat as rain-bearing clouds; this is also transmitted by al-Walibi from Ibn Abbas.
[TABRISI:78:2-16/r17] مجاهد | reports | Thajjaj means rain pouring forcefully, or abundant rain.
[TABRISI:78:2-16/r18] قتادة | reports | Qatada explains thajjaj as rain falling in successive, continuous waves.

## IBNJAWZI-ZAD: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p6#2/r1] أبي بن كعب والحسن وابن جبير | reports | One interpretation of al-muʿṣirāt is the heavens.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p6#2/r2] ابن عباس، مجاهد، عكرمة، قتادة، مقاتل | reports | Another interpretation is the winds; Zayd ibn Aslam specifies the south wind, and min is understood as bi because the winds draw out rain.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p6#2/r3] زيد بن أسلم | reports | He identifies the winds as the south wind.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p6#2/r4] الفراء | reports | Alternatively, the term means rain-bearing clouds, likened to a young woman nearing menstruation.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p6#2/r5] ابن قتيبة | reports | He likewise compares the clouds to maidens who have nearly, but not yet, menstruated.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p6#2/r6] الزجاج | reports | Clouds are called muʿṣirāt when they are ready to rain, by analogy with crops described as ready to be harvested.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p6#3/r1] مقاتل | reports | Thajjāj means abundant rain pouring down continuously.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p6#3/r2] author | holds | Thajja means to pour or stream.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v31p9/r1] author | holds | Thajj can be intransitive, meaning to flow, or transitive, meaning to pour; the hadith pairs loud talbiya with pouring sacrificial blood.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p9/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas was called thajjaj because he poured forth abundant speech in his sermons.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p9/r3] الكلبي ومقاتل وقتادة | reports | Al-Kalbi, Muqatil and Qatada explain thajjaj as water flowing in a continuous, abundant stream.
[RAZI-FULL:v31p9/r4] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj glosses thajjaj as pouring, as though the water pours itself; the point is repeated rain and abundant benefit.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:11-14/r10] author | prefers | Qurtubi prefers clouds, since rain is known to come from them; if rain came from winds, wind would be the more natural source.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:11-14/r7] مجاهد وقتادة وابن عباس | reports | They explain mu‘sirat as winds that compress the clouds, while a second report from Ibn Abbas says it means clouds themselves.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:11-14/r8] سفيان والربيع وأبو العالية والضحاك | reports | They say mu‘sirat are clouds ready to pour rain but not yet raining, like a young woman close to menstruation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:11-14/r9] قتادة | reports | Qatada also identifies mu‘sirat as the sky; al-Nahhas says these explanations can converge because winds pollinate clouds and bring rain.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:14-15/r1] author | holds | The term mu‘sir can also mean a girl nearing puberty or a cloud nearly ready to rain; linguistic explanations connect both to approaching maturity or holding water.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:14-15/r2] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad says a mu‘sir cloud holds water and releases it little by little; related words can mean a refuge.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:14-15/r3] ابن عباس وعكرمة | reports | Ibn Abbas and Ikrima read “bi-l-mu‘sirat,” whereas the written codices have “min al-mu‘sirat.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:14-15/r4] أبي بن كعب والحسن وابن جبير وزيد بن أسلم ومقاتل بن حيان | reports | These authorities take “from the mu‘sirat” to mean from the heavens.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:14-15/r5] ابن عباس ومجاهد | reports | They explain thajjaj as pouring continuously and heavily; the word can be transitive or intransitive.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:14-15/r6] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj glosses thajjaj as pouring, with the water understood as pouring itself.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:14-15/r7] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Asked about accepted pilgrimage, the Prophet answered: raising the voice in talbiya and spilling blood through sacrifice.
[QURTUBI-FULL:78:14-15/r8] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says thajjaj means abundant; Qurtubi says this is the same meaning.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:78:14-16/r1] author | holds | المعصرات may be clouds nearly pressed by winds, winds ready to squeeze clouds, or whirlwind-bearing winds; they initiate rain by forming or driving clouds, supported by reading بالمعصرات.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:78:14-16/r2] author | holds | ثجاجا means pouring abundantly; a Prophetic saying glosses الحج العج والثج as loud talbiya and flowing sacrificial blood, and the reading ثجاجا and مثاجج denote outflows.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p310#13/r1] author | reports | Al-muʿṣirāt may be clouds close to being squeezed by winds into rain, or the winds that form clouds and draw their moisture; a report says winds carry water from the sky to clouds.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p310#13/r2] author | holds | Thajjājan means abundant, pouring water.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14#2/r1] author | holds | Thajj means pouring in great quantity and force; the verb may be intransitive (‘the water poured’) or transitive (‘I poured it’).
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14#2/r2] author | reports | A hadith phrase pairs ‘ajj’ and ‘thajj’: ‘ajj’ is raising one’s voice in the talbiya and ‘thajj’ is spilling sacrificial blood.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14#2/r3] الشاعر | reports | The cited poem uses ‘thajjajan’ for a copious, forceful flow, witnessing the sense of abundant pouring.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14#2/r4] الأعرج | reports | Al-A‘raj reads ‘thajjahan’ with a final unpointed ḥā’ instead of jīm.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14#2/r5] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari calls the spillways of water ‘mathajih’ and uses ‘yantajihu’ for water flowing into the valley.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r1] author | holds | The preposition min may mark the starting point, while the reading with ba by Abd Allah ibn Yazid, ‘Ikrima, and Qatada introduces a causal sense; the readings reflect different meanings of ‘mu‘sirat’. 
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r10] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari resolves the form issue by taking the hamza as entry into a state: clouds have entered the point at which they are about to bring relief through rain.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r2] الزمخشري | reports | One interpretation is clouds nearing the point when winds press them to rain, like crops nearing harvest or a girl nearing puberty; Abu al-Najm’s line witnesses the usage ‘a‘sarat’. 
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r3] ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba cites Abu al-Najm’s verse ‘qad a‘sarat’ as evidence for the usage meaning that the time for a girl’s puberty is near.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r4] author | holds | Without the ‘nearing’ interpretation of ‘a‘sarat’, the form ‘mu‘sirat’ would be expected with a fatḥa on ṣād as a passive participle, since winds press clouds.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r5] الزمخشري | reports | For ‘bil-mu‘sirat’, al-Zamakhshari allows winds ready to press clouds or clouds themselves, since water descends through them, analogous to giving from or by one’s hand.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r6] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid interprets ‘mu‘sirat’ as winds carrying whirlwinds.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r7] الحسن، قتادة | reports | Al-Hasan and Qatada interpret the word as the heavens: water passes from heaven to clouds, as though the heavens are pressing or enabling the pressing.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r8] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari explains the min-plus-winds interpretation by saying winds form clouds and release their moisture, and reports that God sends winds carrying water from heaven if that report is sound.
[SAMIN-DURR:78:14/r9] ابن كيسان | reports | Ibn Kaysan proposes ‘mu‘sirat’ as ‘those bringing relief’, but the author questions how a form-IV participle can carry the meaning of the triliteral ‘aṣara’ (to relieve).
