## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p254#2/r1] author | holds | حفص reads دأبا with fatḥa and most with sukūn; a light alif form is also transmitted. It is a verbal noun of toil, extended to habitual persistence, and can be a circumstantial description of the cultivators or an absolute object of an omitted verb.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p254#2/r2] المبرد | rejects | Al-Mubarrad parses دأبا as an absolute object of تزرعون, like قعد القرفصاء; Al-Alusi rejects that analysis.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p254#2/r3] author | holds | Joseph interprets the seven fat cows and green ears as seven fertile years and the lean cows and dry ears as seven famine years, during which they should cultivate diligently.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p254#2/r4] بعضهم | reports | Some read تزرعون as an imperative to cultivate, cast as a report to make fulfillment sound already accomplished; the harvest instruction would then also be imperative.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p254#2/r5] author | reports | The command to leave grain in its ears prevents weevils from consuming it; Egyptian grain stores can be ruined after roughly two years, and the green ears may suggest this storage counsel is another imperative.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p254#2/r6] author | reports | Grammarians object that an imperative harvest instruction cannot coordinate with a declarative forecast; replies treat it as independent advice, an answer to an implied condition, or understand the imperative as a report because it is Joseph's guidance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p254#2/r7] الكشف | prefers | Al-Kashf finds the strongest reading predictive: the cultivation forecast interprets the dream, while the storage instruction interrupts it as urgent advice, making what will happen seem already present.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p254#2/r8] بعضهم | reports | If تزرعون is instead an imperative, some treat the storage instruction as part of the dream's interpretation because the lean cows and dry ears predict eating what the fertile years produced; others regard it as advice outside the interpretation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255/r1] author | holds | They should leave all but the small amount needed to eat during the fertile years; the advice encourages moderation in food.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255/r2] السلمي | reports | Al-Sulami reads مما يأكلون with yā, meaning what people eat, rather than addressing the audience as 'you'.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p255/r3] author | holds | The seed grain is not separately excepted because the command to cultivate seven years already makes retaining seed understood.

## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:12:43-57#2/r2] الرضا عليه السلام | reports | A report says Joseph stockpiled the harvests, sold food during famine first for money, then valuables, livestock, servants, property, and land, and finally for people’s freedom; he then freed the Egyptians and restored their possessions and kingship.
[TABATABAI:12:43-57#2/r3] author | holds | The author omits many similar reports about the famine economy because most do not directly serve the interpretation of these verses.
[TABATABAI:12:46-49/r3] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib defines daʾb as continued travel and, by extension, a lasting habitual practice; another reported sense is strenuous effort. | mentions 14:33, 3:11
[TABATABAI:12:46-49/r4] author | holds | Accordingly, Joseph’s instruction is to farm continuously for seven years, either as a sustained practice or with persistent effort.
[TABATABAI:12:46-49/r5] author | holds | Tazraʿun is a report in the form of a command, emphasizing that they must comply; the following imperative to leave the harvest in its ears confirms this. | mentions 61:11
[TABATABAI:12:46-49/r6] author | reports | Some explain storing grain in its ears as protection from insects and spoilage, since husked and cleaned grain deteriorates faster.
[TABATABAI:12:49#2/r1] author | prefers | The dream depicts the practical measures the king must take for a coming famine: repeated planting, preserving grain in ears, and rationing reserves, rather than simply forecasting years.
[TABATABAI:12:49#2/r2] author | holds | Cows represent yearly provisions: fat cows picture plentiful harvests from sustained planting, lean cows picture shortages, and their eating the fat ones pictures famine consuming saved food.
[TABATABAI:12:49#2/r3] author | holds | The green ears represent stores to be saved and the dry ears indicate preserving grain unthreshed; the dream shows the work required to protect food supplies.
[TABATABAI:12:49#2/r4] author | holds | The exception allowing a little consumption is not dream interpretation but permission to use the amount needed for food during the seven productive years.
[TABATABAI:12:49/r8] author | rejects | The author says interpreters wrongly treat the dream as a forecast of seven fertile then seven barren years; Joseph instead states the required policy for surviving the anticipated famine.
