## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p550/r1] author | holds | The address to the viewer is hypothetical: it means that if you saw the sun at sunrise, you would see it turn away from their cave.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p550/r2] ابن عباس | reports | He glosses tazāwar as moving away; in another report he says it tilts away from the youths.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p550/r3] author | holds | Tazāwar means to lean or turn, paralleling tamāyal; a poem describing a horse uses tamāyal as evidence.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p550/r4] أهل الكوفة وابن عامر | reports | The Kufans read the verb without the extra ta of the reciprocal form, while Ibn Amir reads tazawwar.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p550/r5] أبو الحسن | reports | He objects that azwar is not used for leaning away, only for being withdrawn or contracted; Antara line about recoiling from a spear supports that sense.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p550/r6] author | holds | Jarir line about the palanquins turning away from a tree supports using the verb for the sun apparent turning aside.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r1] author | holds | Dhāt al-yamīn means toward the right; dhāt stands in for a directional noun, and al-Akhfash parses it as an adverb.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r10] أبو إسحاق | rejects | He rejects the explanation that their physical location alone blocked the sun; God withheld its approach, light, and heat by power as a sign.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r11] author | holds | The verse sign is God power to keep the sun from reaching them, rather than their lying in a naturally shaded place.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r2] الوالبي عن ابن عباس | reports | He glosses taqriḍuhum as leaving them.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r3] قتادة ومقاتل | reports | Qatada says the sun leaves them; Muqatil says it passes them by.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r4] الأخفش والزجاج وأبو عبيدة | reports | They explain the sun as turning away from the youths and leaving them; al-Kisaʾi says the verb means to pass a place by.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r5] ذو الرمة | reports | His line about riders passing mountain passes on their left illustrates taqriḍ as going past or bypassing.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r6] أبو عبيد | reports | He says qarḍ can mean cutting or traversing a land; a traveler who passed a place on the left has bypassed it.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r7] الكلبي | reports | He describes a cave in Roman territory oriented toward Banat Naʿsh: the sun passes the cave right at sunrise and its left at sunset without striking it.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r8] المفسرون | reports | They say the cave was in a sunny, open place facing Banat Naʿsh, yet the sun path spared the youths from heat, exposure, and worn clothing.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p552/r9] أبو علي الفارسي | reports | He infers that the sun never, or almost never, reached them, preserving their bodies.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p555/r1] author | holds | They lay in a broad part of the cave where cool air and a breeze could reach them; faja means an open, spacious area.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p555/r2] أبو عبيدة | reports | He gives the plural forms of faja as fajawat and faja, and cites a report about moving quickly when one finds an open space.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p555/r3] مجاهد | reports | He glosses faja as a place extending broadly in width; Ibn Abbas calls it spaciousness.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p555/r4] author | holds | The sun turning and passing away from the cave are signs of God power and kindness to the youths.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v13p555/r5] author | holds | God guided the youths, and a person is guided only by God guidance; the example of misguidance is Daqyanus and his followers, who have no guiding patron.
