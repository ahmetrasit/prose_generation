## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p132/r10] عنترة | reports | ʿAntara’s line uses azwar for turning aside from a spear’s impact.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p132/r11] بشر بن أبي حازم | reports | Bishr ibn Abī Ḥāzim’s verse witnesses the noun azwirār for a directional deviation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p132/r12] author | holds | Qaraḍa means cut off; Kufans instead say it can mean to approach or move alongside a place, citing Bedouin usage.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p132/r13] الكوفيون | reports | The Kufans report Arabs saying qaraḍtahu qablan wa-dabran for approaching something from its front and back.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p132/r14] author | holds | Fajwa is an open space, from a root describing separation between the thighs; fajāʾ is its plural.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p132/r8] author | holds | Tazāwar means turn away or incline; al-Akhfash glosses it as withdrawing, and azwar can describe an eye or anything that leans.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p132/r9] ابن أبي ربيعة | reports | Ibn Abī Rabīʿa’s verse witnesses the use of azwar for one who leans or turns aside.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r10] ابن أبي إسحاق وطلحة والأعمش وباقي السبعة | reports | These readers use تزور, on the pattern of تحمر.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r11] الجحدري وأبو رجاء وأيوب السختياني وابن أبي عبلة وجابر | reports | These readers use تزوار, on the pattern of تحمار; this reading is also reported from Ayyūb.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r7] author | holds | The account omits connecting events: the youths entered the cave, God caused sleep, answered their prayer, and provided comforts there.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r8] الحرميان وأبو عمرو | reports | The Ḥaramayn readers and Abū ʿAmr assimilate the tāʾ of تتزاور into zāy.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p150#2/r9] الكوفيون والأعمش وطلحة وابن أبي ليلى وابن مناذر وخلف وأبو عبيد وابن سعدان ومحمد بن عيسى الأصبهاني وأحمد بن جبير الأنطاكي | reports | This group reads the verb with a light zāy after deleting the tāʾ.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r1] ابن مسعود وأبو المتوكل | reports | Ibn Masʿūd and Abū al-Mutawakkil read تزوئر with hamza before rāʾ, as in other hamzated forms to avoid two adjacent sukūns; it means turn or incline away.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r10] أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī reads تقرضهم as giving them some sunlight briefly, like a loan that is taken back; he says the sun touched them lightly morning and evening.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r11] author | rejects | He rejects the loan derivation: that would require a different four-letter verb and vowel pattern; تقرضهم instead means cuts off some of its light for them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r12] author | holds | God placed them where sun was limited enough to avoid heat but not wholly absent, preventing stale air and decay; this special solar path is a sign of divine care.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r13] author | holds | If the cave faced Banāt Naʿsh and naturally blocked the sun, the signs include their monotheistic guidance, shelter, protection, awe, preserved bodies and clothes, and sleep through the long period.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r2] author | holds | ذات اليمين means the cave’s right-hand side, defined from the entrant’s or youths’ perspective; تقرضهم means cuts them off rather than approaching them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r3] الجمهور | reports | Most readers pronounce تقرضهم with tāʾ; another group reads يقرضهم, making the cave the subject that keeps the sun off them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the sun did not touch the youths at all.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r5] فرقة | reports | Another group says afternoon sun reached them in a way beneficial to their bodies; this implies barriers to south and west and a corner position.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r6] عبد الله بن مسلم | reports | ʿAbd Allāh b. Muslim says the cave entrance faced the Banāt Naʿsh stars, which would leave its upper part sheltered from rain.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r7] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the cave faced Banāt Naʿsh, so neither sunrise nor sunset entered; God chose a broad, airy shaded resting place that avoided the cave’s distress.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r8] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says they stayed in shade all day despite an open, broad site exposed to the sun, which God screened from them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p151/r9] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj says the sun’s behavior itself is a sign; the cave need not face a direction that naturally causes it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p152/r1] author | holds | The guidance statement is general: it includes the cave youths as those guided and Daqyānūs and his followers as those led astray.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p152/r2] author | holds | The address in وترى الشمس and وتحسبهم is to anyone who might see the cave.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH

[SAMIN-DURR:18:17#2/r2] author | holds | ذات اليمين and ذات الشمال are spatial adverbials meaning the two directions.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:17#2/r3] author | holds | وهم في فجوة منه is circumstantial; the sun's avoidance despite open space makes their protection more striking; فجوة means open space.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:17#2/r4] author | reports | ذلك refers to their whole story as subject of من آيات الله, or is a predicate of omitted الأمر with من آيات الله circumstantial.

[SAMIN-DURR:18:17/r10] الفارسي | reports | Al-Farisi instead says the sun lends them light and quickly withdraws it; the author says that sense should use passive تُقرضهم.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p142/r1] author | holds | The cave entrance faced north: sunrise falls away to the right as the shadow recedes, then sunlight passes them to the left at sunset; Ibn Abbas, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, and Qatada gloss tazawwar as inclining.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p142/r2] author | holds | The sun's path rules out an eastern entrance, which would receive no sunset rays; a southern entrance, which would receive neither sunrise nor sunset rays; and a western entrance, which would get no light until after noon.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p142/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas and others explain taqriduhum as passing them by or leaving them; Malik from Zayd ibn Aslam glosses tazawwar as inclining.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p142/r4] author | rejects | The cave's location has no religious benefit; proposed sites include near Aila according to Ibn Abbas, near Nineveh according to Ibn Ishaq, and Roman or Balqa lands, but scripture and the Prophet's teaching give no reason to identify it.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p142/r5] ابن عباس | reports | The youths lay in a roomy interior space where sunlight did not touch them, since direct exposure would burn their bodies and clothes.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143/r1] author | holds | The cave was a sign because God guided the youths to shelter where they remained alive and sunlight and wind could reach them to preserve their bodies.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p143/r2] author | holds | God alone guided the youths; whoever He guides is rightly guided, while a person He misleads has no guide or protecting ally.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451#2/r1] نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو | reports | These readers assimilate the ta of the tafaʿul form in تزاور.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451#2/r2] عاصم وحمزة والكسائي | reports | These readers omit the ta in the recitation of تزاور.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451#2/r3] ابن عامر ويعقوب | reports | They read تزور on the pattern of تحمر, which the author relates to the sun's state at the end of its inclination.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451#2/r4] author | holds | The sun inclines away at dawn and veers aside at sunset so its warmth benefits the sleepers without overheating or harming them.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451#2/r5] author | holds | The author infers a cave entrance facing Banat Naash and a mountain north of Mecca, but says the directions may instead be relative to someone leaving the cave.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451#2/r6] الأصبهاني | reports | Al-Asbahani reports that the cave entrance faced north, with sunrise to its right and sunset to its left.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p451#2/r7] author | holds | The root of قرض centers on cutting, from which the senses of veering aside, passing, and giving a loan cut from one's wealth are derived.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p452/r1] author | holds | The sleepers lie in the cave's broad central space, where fresh air helps preserve them from decay and other harm.
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p452/r2] author | holds | Their guidance, planning, and the cave's protective ventilation are signs of God's knowledge and power, even if small beside the Qur'an.
