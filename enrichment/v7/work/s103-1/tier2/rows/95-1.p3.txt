## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v10p359/r1] author | holds | The oaths name the edible fig and the olive from which oil is pressed, singled out from other fruits for their notable qualities.
[BURSEVI:v10p359/r2] author | holds | The fig is sweet, light, and quickly digested; the source lists many medicinal benefits, including loosening the bowels, dissolving phlegm, cleansing the kidneys, removing bladder gravel, and aiding the body.
[BURSEVI:v10p359/r3] أبو ذر | reports | Abu Dharr reports that the Prophet ate a gift of figs and urged the companions to eat them, saying that if a fruit descended from Paradise it would be this seedless fruit; he also attributed relief for hemorrhoids and gout to it.

[BURSEVI:v10p359/r5] الإمام | reports | A report says Adam covered himself with fig leaves after sin; when lonely after descending, he fed leaves to visiting gazelles, and God made the first group beautiful and gave them musk because they came for Adam rather than for food; later, greedy gazelles gained beauty without musk.
[BURSEVI:v10p359/r6] أسئلة الحكم | reports | A cited wisdom text says the fig first appears without a covering because it alone covered Adam: after it covered him, God made it bring forth meaning before claim, while other trees show claim before meaning.
