## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH

[QURTUBI-FULL:90:4/r10] الحسن | reports | He says people struggle with worldly calamities and the hardships of the Hereafter; they also struggle to give thanks in ease and patience in adversity, a view narrated from Ibn Umar.
[QURTUBI-FULL:90:4/r11] يمان | reports | No creature endures what Adam's descendants endure, even though humans are the weakest creatures.
[QURTUBI-FULL:90:4/r12] علماؤنا | holds | They catalogue hardship from cutting the umbilical cord through infancy, work, aging, death, questioning, and the grave, concluding that a creator has governed these stages and should be obeyed.

[QURTUBI-FULL:90:4/r16] بعض المفسرين | reports | Others interpret it as bold-hearted and thick-livered despite weak creation and humble substance.
[QURTUBI-FULL:90:4/r17] ابن عطاء | reports | He glosses the phrase as being in darkness and ignorance.

[QURTUBI-FULL:90:4/r2] author | holds | The human meant is Adam; k abad means hardship and strain in worldly life.
[QURTUBI-FULL:90:4/r3] author | holds | The root conveys severity, as milk thickens and becomes firm, and a person says he endured a difficult matter.
