## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v4p269#2/r1] author | holds | ‘He gulps it’ answers an implied question; the verb conveys forced effort and repeated sips driven by thirst and heat because the drink is bitter and foul.
[BURSEVI:v4p269#2/r2] author | holds | He can scarcely swallow it and chokes, enduring prolonged torment sip after sip; smooth passage down the throat is what ‘easy to swallow’ means.
[BURSEVI:v4p269#2/r3] author | reports | A report describes the drink scorching the face and making the scalp fall as it nears; when swallowed, it cuts the intestines until they emerge from the anus.
[BURSEVI:v4p269#2/r4] author | holds | Death comes from every direction as the causes of suffering, from all six sides or every part of the body; any one pain would be fatal if death were possible, but the person remains alive.
[BURSEVI:v4p269#2/r5] author | holds | ‘Behind him’ means ahead of him, after the pus; the next punishment is an ever thicker torment beyond what he has experienced.
