## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v7p268#2/r1] author | holds | The deniers of resurrection say only worldly life exists and people alternate between dying and living. This includes Arab polytheists, philosophers who deny creation and return, and cyclical materialists who deny a Creator and posit endless 36,000-year repetitions.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v7p268#2/r2] author | holds | Their claim that time alone destroys them is unsupported conjecture; a sound divine report says God is the one who turns night and day, and people should not curse time.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v7p268#2/r3] أبو هريرة | reports | A Prophetic report says God is harmed by the human who curses time, since God controls the affair and turns night and day; another wording prohibits cursing time.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v7p269/r1] أبو هريرة | reports | Further reports from Abū Hurayra describe pre-Islamic people blaming night and day for death and life; a divine report says a human curses time while God controls night and day. Another report says God lent to a servant who refused and was insulted as 'time'.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v7p269/r2] الشافعي وأبو عبيدة | prefers | Al-Shāfiʿī, Abū ʿUbayda, and other imams explain that Arabs blamed and cursed time for hardship, though God actually causes those events; cursing time therefore indirectly curses God. Ibn Kathīr calls this the best explanation.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v7p269/r3] ابن حزم | rejects | Ibn Ḥazm and like-minded literalists wrongly count al-dahr as one of God's beautiful names based on this hadith.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v7p105/r1] author | holds | The deniers claim worldly life is the only life, though “worldly” is a relative description that itself suggests another, later life.
[BIQAI-FULL:v7p105/r2] author | holds | Their saying “we die and live” means some people die and others are born; they deny that the same person lives after death and reduce themselves to animal-like attention to particulars.
[BIQAI-FULL:v7p105/r3] author | holds | They attribute their eventual destruction to time, which overcomes people through successive changes and unpleasant events; the author connects dahr to the verb meaning to overcome.
[BIQAI-FULL:v7p105/r4] author | holds | They have no knowledge, only conjecture based on observing human decline with age and the fact that no dead person has returned.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v8p347#3/r1] author | reports | The speakers are deniers of resurrection, identified as Quraysh and Arab polytheists; Kashf al-Asrar attributes the claim to heretics who called people like grass.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#3/r2] author | holds | They say that life is only the present worldly life and that death and life recur here, with no existence beyond it; the word order may also serve the rhyme or simple coordination.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#3/r3] author | reports | Some allow a transmigration reading: idol worshipers believed souls repeatedly transfer to new bodies after death; they attributed to a supposed prophet the claim of having seen 1,700 incarnations.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#3/r4] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib defines transmigration’s proponents as rejecting the scriptural resurrection and believing souls pass eternally from one body to another.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#3/r5] التعريفات | reports | Al-Ta‘rifat defines transmigration as a soul attaching to another body immediately after leaving its former body, due to an innate affinity between soul and body.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r1] author | holds | Dahr means the passage of time or the world’s duration from its beginning to end, then any long period; zaman can denote either a short or long span.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r10] النبي | reports | A divine report says that when Adam’s child curses time, God is time: He sends night and day and can seize them whenever He wills.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r2] القاموس | reports | Al-Qamus defines dahr as long time, extended eternity, or a thousand years.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r3] الصوفية | reports | Sufis define dahr as the enduring present, the extension of divine presence and hidden side of time, through which past and future are renewed.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r4] author | holds | The deniers blamed the passage of days and nights for death, rejecting the Angel of Death and his taking souls by God’s command.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r5] النبي | reports | The Prophet forbade cursing time: God, rather than time, brings events and is the true agent behind them.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r6] الكاشفي | reports | Kashifi says the Exalted God turns the ages; time has no independent choice or agency in anything.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r7] author | reports | A poem warns against blaming time for betrayal: events obey a commanding Lord, while worldly abundance can increase a disbeliever’s unbelief and a believer’s faith despite poverty.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r8] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib explains the hadith to mean that God causes the good, evil, joy, and sorrow people attribute to time, so cursing time curses Him.
[BURSEVI:v8p347#4/r9] بعضهم | reports | Another interpretation takes the hadith’s second ‘dahr’ as an active participle: God is the manager and disposer of every occurrence; the author says the first reading is more apparent.
[BURSEVI:v8p348/r1] author | holds | The first hadith’s meaning is readily understood in Sufi interpretation: God, not time, acts through the events people blame on time.
[BURSEVI:v8p348/r2] author | holds | Their claim means that life is confined to this world and that life and death are attributed to time; they have no rational or transmitted knowledge for it.
[BURSEVI:v8p348/r3] author | holds | They rely only on conjecture and imitation, whereas believers follow revealed texts with certainty and affirm both sensible and spiritual resurrection, the bridge, Paradise, and Hell.
[BURSEVI:v8p348/r4] author | holds | Believers also affirm the gathering of individual souls with the universal soul and the union of intelligible and sensible realities, displaying divine power and perfection across unseen and visible realms.
[BURSEVI:v8p348/r5] author | holds | This is the belief of prophets, messengers, and their believers; those who share it are saved, while others perish.
[BURSEVI:v8p348/r6] author | holds | Tawhid requires attributing every event to God; the Prophet forbade cursing the wind because it is controlled by an angel under God, and all acts return to Him.
