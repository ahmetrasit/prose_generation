[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r1] مجاهد | reports | Ḍuḥāhā means the sun's light and radiance; this is the second oath, and the term is attached to the sun because it comes when the sun rises.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r10] author | holds | Those who define it as all daylight do so because the sun's light persists; those who define it as light or heat treat them as inseparable. | mentions 20:119
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r11] المبرد وأبو الهيثم | reports | They give competing etymologies: ḍuḥā derives from ḍaḥ, sunlight opposite shade, with the final letter shifted in pronunciation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r2] قتادة | reports | He interprets the term as the sun's splendor.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r3] السدي | reports | He interprets it as the sun's heat.

[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r5] اليزيدي | reports | He glosses ḍuḥāhā as the sun's spreading or extension.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r6] الماوردي | reports | Another view takes it as all creatures on earth made visible by the sun, so the oath includes them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r7] author | holds | Ḍuḥā is usually feminine, though it can be masculine; grammarians connect it either to the plural of ḍaḥwah or to a noun-pattern like ṣurad.

[QURTUBI-FULL:91:1/r9] الفراء وقتادة | reports | They use ḍuḥā for the whole day, while common Arabic usage means sunrise and the period shortly afterward; extended brightness is ḍuḥāʾ.
