## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:28/r1] author | holds | The command is to hold oneself firmly with those who invoke God at the gathering times or at the day's two ends; Ibn Amir reads the form as al-ghudwa.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:28/r2] author | holds | Seeking God's face means seeking His approval and obedience.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:28/r3] author | holds | Do not let your gaze pass over the poor believers, turning away from their shabby clothes in desire for wealthy people's finery; the construction embeds the sense of turning away.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:28/r4] author | reports | The phrase is also read with the dual eyes or with a form derived from causing the eye to pass beyond them.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:28/r5] author | holds | The person whose heart was made heedless is identified as Umayya ibn Khalaf, who urged Muhammad to expel poor believers for Quraysh's leaders.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:28/r6] author | reports | Mu'tazilites reject the apparent divine attribution of heedlessness, suggesting God found the heart so, attributed it to the person, or left it unmarked; the author answers with his previous arguments.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:28/r7] author | holds | Following desire means the person's affair is excessive, advancing past truth and casting it behind his back; the root also describes a horse that runs ahead.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
