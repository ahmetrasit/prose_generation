## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r1] قتادة | reports | Qatada identifies the passerby as Ezra; Wahb identifies him as Jeremiah; Ibn Ishaq identifies him as al-Khidr.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r10] author | holds | He learned that a century had passed from signs and reports and seeing his grandchildren as old people, though their fathers had been young men when he left.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r11] علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali reports that Ezra left his pregnant wife at age fifty, returned from the hundred-year death at age fifty, and found his son a hundred years old, fifty years older than he was.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r12] ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو | reports | Ibn Kathir, Nafi, and Abu Amr read nunshiruha with ra, meaning revive the bones; the dead are like something folded and revival unfolds them into action.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r13] الباقون | reports | The other readers pronounce nunshizuha with zay, meaning to raise the bones together; nushuz denotes elevation, as with raised land or a wife's rising from obedience.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r14] author | reports | It is said that God restored the man's eyes and sight before his body, so he watched the bones assemble, flesh cover them, and his donkey revive.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r15] author | reports | The one asking how long he had remained may have been an angel, a prophet, or an aged believer who witnessed his death and revival.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r2] وهب وقتادة والربيع بن أنس | reports | Wahb, Qatada, and al-Rabi ibn Anas identify the village as Jerusalem after Nebuchadnezzar destroyed it; Ibn Zayd says it was the village from which thousands fled fearing death.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Khawiya means ruined according to Ibn Abbas, al-Rabi, and al-Dahhak, or empty; its root denotes emptiness, also used for a house without inhabitants or a hungry belly.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r4] author | holds | Aruš means buildings or construction, so the village lay collapsed over its structures.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r5] author | reports | The question may ask how God will rebuild the ruined village or how He will restore its dead inhabitants.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r6] author | holds | He guessed he had been dead a day because he died early and awoke near the end of the day a century later, then revised his estimate on seeing the remaining sun.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v1p331/r7] ابن زيد | reports | The food and drink's failure to decay means they remained unchanged, from asin, water that has not changed; Ibn Zayd distinguishes it from ajin, changed but drinkable water.
