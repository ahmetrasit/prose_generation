## DURR-FULL: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[DURR-FULL:v8p534/r5] ابن مسعود | reports | He reports that Abu Bakr bought and freed Bilal for God; the verses contrast their efforts with those of Umayya and Ubayy, identify the good as the testimony of faith, and the hard path as Hell.
[DURR-FULL:v8p534/r6] ابن عباس عن عكرمة | reports | Giving means giving from one's bounty and fearing God; the good promise is God's compensation, ease is good, and the opposite is withholding wealth, self-sufficiency, denial, and ease toward evil.
[DURR-FULL:v8p534/r7] قتادة | reports | He said the giver pays God's due, avoids prohibitions, and believes God's promise; the miser withholds that due, feels self-sufficient, and denies the promise.
[DURR-FULL:v8p534/r9] ابن عباس | reports | He also glossed the good as affirming that there is no god but God; the miser is one who withholds zakat despite having been enriched.
[DURR-FULL:v8p535/r4] ابن عباس عن أبي صالح | reports | In a report through al-Kalbi and Abu Salih, the giver and God-fearing person is Abu Bakr, while the miser who feels self-sufficient and denies the good is Abu Sufyan ibn Harb.
[DURR-FULL:v8p535/r5] علي بن أبي طالب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet said each person's place in Paradise or Fire is written, but commanded people to act: each is made ready for the deeds of the saved or the wretched, then recited this passage.
[DURR-FULL:v8p537/r2] جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Suraqa asked whether deeds were predetermined; the Prophet affirmed the decree but urged action, since each person is eased toward what he was created for, then recited the passage.
