## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:23:91-100/r8] author | holds | At death the unbeliever sees his sins as the Angel of Death takes him; struck by disgrace, he asks to return to worldly life and do the good he left behind, meaning faith.

## YAHYA-SALLAM: Yaḥyā b. Sallām, d. 200 AH
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p415/r1] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says anyone without God's friendship asks to return at death once the Fire becomes clear, even someone mute who never spoke; those nearby do not hear the request.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p415/r3] رسول الله | reports | A report from an unnamed man of Banū Ḥāritha says a dying person sees what had kept him from his obligations placed before him, prompting the plea to return; versions place these things before him or between his eyes.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p415/r4] السدي | reports | Al-Suddī says a dying unbeliever sees few good deeds and many bad ones, sees the Angel of Death, and wishes to return, then God says he will not return; descendants do not hear the utterance.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v17p107/r1] author | holds | At death the polytheist regrets neglected obedience and asks to return to worldly life so he can do the righteous deeds he abandoned.
[TAB-FULL:v17p107/r3] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd says the plea occurs as worldly life ends and the person sees the afterlife, before tasting death.
[TAB-FULL:v17p107/r5] الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says those asking “My Lord, return me” are the people of shirk.
[TAB-FULL:v17p107/r6] author | holds | The plea begins by addressing God, then shifts to the plural “return me,” because the request is directed to the angels who take the soul.
[TAB-FULL:v17p107/r7] بعض نحويي الكوفة | reports | A Kufan grammarian explains the plural form as following the Qur'anic pattern in which God describes himself with plural wording, as in “I created you.” | mentions 19:9

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p60/r1] author | holds | Those who denied resurrection ask to return to worldly life when they see death approaching.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p60/r2] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil says the person who asks to return is a disbeliever.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p60/r3] الكلبي ومقاتل | reports | They explain “death came” as meaning the Angel of Death came.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p60/r4] غيرهما | reports | Others take “death” to mean its causes and early signs, as in the verse about death coming from every direction. | mentions 14:17
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p60/r5] ابن عباس والمفسرون | reports | “My Lord, send me back” means return me to worldly life.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p60/r6] الفراء والزجاج وجميع أصحاب العربية | reports | The plural imperative “send me back” addresses God alone; Arabic uses a plural form for a majestic speaker, as in God's “We give life and cause death.” | mentions 50:43
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p60/r7] ابن جريج | reports | Ibn Jurayj says the dying person first calls on God and then turns to the angels to ask for return.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v16p60/r8] المبرد | prefers | Al-Mubarrad chooses the angels as the addressees of “send me back,” after the person first appeals to God; Arabic can shift addressees mid-speech, as in lines by al-Ahwas and al-Nabigha addressing a house before its owner.
