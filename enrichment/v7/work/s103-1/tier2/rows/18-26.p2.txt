## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26#2/r1] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya cites a report that Jesus will perform pilgrimage with the Cave Companions, who have not yet made pilgrimage.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26#2/r2] author | holds | On this account, the youths remain asleep rather than dead until shortly before the Hour; the author says scripture identifies Jesus as God’s servant and messenger and says he will pass al-Rawha as pilgrim or visitor.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r1] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid takes the stated duration as the time from the youths’ death until the Qur’an revealed their story.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r10] author | reports | The ya reading with a nominative kaf reports that God shares His judgment with none.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r11] ابن عامر والحسن وأبو رجاء وقتادة والجحدري | reports | These readers use second-person ta with a still kaf, addressing the Prophet and coordinating the clause with the command to see and hear.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r12] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid reads the ya with a jussive; Yaqub says he does not know its grammatical basis.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r13] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas said the youths had perished: near their cave he found bones and told his party they had vanished long ago.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r2] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says the duration means until the youths died.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r3] author | reports | Another reported interpretation measures their time in the cave, the duration Jews had given with additions and omissions.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r4] author | holds | Only God knows the duration, unless He has disclosed it to someone.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r5] قتادة | reports | Qatadah reads the phrase as an exclamation: none sees or hears better than God; the wording describes perception.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r6] author | reports | An alternative takes the verbs as commands: by His revelation and guidance God showed the Prophet the truth and made the world hear it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r7] author | reports | Another reading means that God showed the youths and made them hear what He said about them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r8] author | reports | The lack of any guardian may refer to the Cave Companions, whom only God protected.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:26/r9] author | reports | Alternatively it refers to disbelievers disputing the youths’ duration: they have no manager besides God, so cannot know without His disclosure.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:26/r1] author | holds | God knows all hidden conditions of the heavens and earth; nothing among their inhabitants escapes His knowledge.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:26/r2] author | holds | The exclamation about God's seeing and hearing stresses perception beyond created senses: nothing veils Him and subtle, dense, small, great, hidden, and clear are alike to Him.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:26/r3] author | reports | Sibawayh parses the pronoun as nominative and the preposition as extra; al-Akhfash instead parses the pronoun as object and the doer as an implied command addressed to everyone.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:26/r4] author | holds | God alone governs all beings, with no protector for them and no one sharing His judgment.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:26/r5] ابن عامر وقالون عن يعقوب | reports | Ibn Amir and Qalun from Yaqub read the final verb as a jussive prohibition in the second person against anyone associating in judgment.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:26/r6] author | holds | The unseen story of the sleepers proves revelation to the Prophet; he is then commanded to continue reciting the Book and keep company with its people.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p11#2/r1] author | reports | “God knows best how long” may be God's correction of those disputing about the sleepers, or the People of the Book's statement repeated as a rebuttal.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p11#2/r2] author | prefers | The majority holds that God states they remained in the cave for the duration He has reported.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p11#2/r3] author | holds | God alone knows hidden conditions of the inhabitants of heaven and earth; no one besides Him governs them, and none shares in His judgment.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p11#2/r4] author | holds | The emphatic expressions mean how perfectly God sees every existent and hears every audible thing.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p11#2/r5] author | holds | The Syrian reading makes “do not associate anyone” a prohibition; the context answers demands to bring a different Qur'an or alter it. | mentions 10:15

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:18:25-26/r3] author | holds | The years remain indefinite in the wording; this supports the statement that God knows best how long they stayed.
[QASHANI:18:25-26/r4] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the duration is a continuation of the People of the Book’s reported claim, which the command that God knows best rejects.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r5] author | holds | After giving the exact duration, God commands the Prophet to say that God knows best: this is certain truth because God knows the unseen of heaven and earth.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p163/r6] author | reports | ما لبثوا may refer to the preceding sleep, or to the time from their discovery until the Prophet’s era.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r1] author | reports | One view says the Prophet is told to return knowledge of the duration to God, refuting others; Abū Ḥayyān prefers that the phrase belongs to the disputants, as the context indicates and because the matter is among God’s hidden knowledge.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r10] author | holds | The pronoun in به refers to God; grammarians disagree whether the expression is nominative or accusative and whether the verbs are literal commands or formulas of amazement.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r11] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya, citing Ibn al-Anbārī, allows ‘see by God’s religion and hear by His guidance,’ with the pronoun referring to guidance or God.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r12] عيسى | reports | ʿĪsā reads أسمع به وأبصر as past indicatives, meaning God made His servants perceive Him through knowledge and made them hear; the pronoun refers to God.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p164/r9] author | holds | God knows all unseen affairs in the heavens and earth; أبصر به وأسمع expresses the extraordinary reach of His perception, from the smallest and hidden things to the largest and most visible.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p165/r1] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī takes ما لهم to refer to the inhabitants of heaven and earth, who have no independent guardian over their affairs.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p165/r2] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya offers several possible referents for ما لهم: the cave youths, the Prophet’s hostile contemporaries as a threat, believers in heaven and earth, or those disputing the youths’ duration, who cannot know without God’s disclosure.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p165/r3] الجمهور | reports | Most readers have ولا يشرك in the indicative with yāʾ, stating that no one shares God’s judgment.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p165/r4] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid reads ولا يشرك with yāʾ in the jussive; Yaʿqūb says he does not know the rationale.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p165/r5] ابن عامر والحسن وأبو رجاء وقتادة والجحدري وأبو حيوة وزيد وحميد بن الوزير والجعفي واللؤلؤي | reports | This group reads ولا تشرك with second-person tāʾ and jussive as a prohibition.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r1] author | holds | أبصر به is a metaphorical exclamation meaning ما أبصره, with the pronoun referring to God.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r10] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid also reads the verb with second-person ta and jussive mood.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r11] يعقوب | reports | Yaqub says he cannot identify the grammatical basis for Mujahid's reading.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r12] author | holds | The author supplies the basis: the subject is an understood human pronoun, omitted because it is known.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r13] author | holds | The pronoun in مالهم refers to the contemporaries of the Messenger.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r14] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya calls the verse an interrupting threat, meaning it concerns people beyond those previously addressed, not a technical parenthesis.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r2] author | prefers | The strongest analysis treats the form as an imperative in wording but declarative in meaning, with an extra ب before the grammatical subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r3] قيل | reports | A second analysis makes the grammatical subject a pronoun referring to the verbal noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r4] قيل | reports | A third analysis takes the subject as the addressee: the command means that the addressee sees.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r5] قيل | reports | Another view takes it as a literal command rather than an exclamation and refers the pronoun to guidance understood from context.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r6] عيسى | reports | Isa read أبصر and أسمع as past verbs with God as subject; به likewise refers to God, who made His servants see and hear.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r7] author | holds | من ولي may be parsed either as a grammatical subject or as a topic.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r8] ابن عامر | reports | Ibn Amir reads ولا تشرك in second person with a jussive verb, addressing the human listener.
[SAMIN-DURR:18:26/r9] الباقون | reports | The other readers use third-person ي and an indicative verb, a straightforward denial that anyone shares God's judgment.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p150#2/r2] author | holds | Without revealed knowledge or instruction about their duration, one must not speculate but say God knows; only God, or someone He informs, knows the unseen. Ibn Kathir says this is also the view of many early and later exegetes, including Mujahid.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p150#2/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada, followed by Mutarrif ibn Abd Allah, says the three hundred years plus nine was the People of the Book's statement, rejected by the following command to say God knows; he cites a variant reading attributed to Ibn Masud, وقالوا ولبثوا.
