## QUTB-ZILAL: Sayyid Quṭb, d. 1386 AH
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:7/r1] author | holds | Al-ma'un means aid, kindness, and goodness for fellow human beings and God's servants; withholding it shows prayer's intended effects are absent.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:7/r2] author | holds | If people truly established prayer for God, they would not withhold help from His servants; their treatment of others tests whether worship is sincere and accepted.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:7/r3] author | holds | The warned worshippers perform empty motions for show without devotion or effect on their hearts and actions, so their prayer becomes a sin awaiting punishment.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:7/r4] author | holds | God's call to faith and worship serves human flourishing, not any need of God: it seeks pure hearts, happiness, mutual care, generosity, love, and brotherhood.
[QUTB-ZILAL:107:7/r5] author | holds | Qutb asks why humanity wanders into dark confusion when divine goodness and mercy offer a clear route to an elevated, generous life.

[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:4-7:kommentar/r5] author | holds | Māʿūn and manaʿa are near-anagrams built from permutations of the same consonants, m-n-ʿ and m-ʿ-n.

[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r3] Nöldeke | rejects | Nöldeke accepts Geiger's derivation but rejects the assumption that Arabic must preserve the Hebrew word's original meaning.

[CORPUSCORANICUM:107:7:anmerkung/r7] author | holds | A deliberate Qur'anic echo of Hebrew māʿôn remains possible despite the Arabic word's changed meaning.
