[SAMIN-DURR:3:180#2/r1] author | reports | The cited poem continues Ibn ʿAṭiyya’s evidence that a participle can imply its verbal noun: السفيه signals السفه.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:180#2/r2] الشيخ | rejects | Al-Shaykh says the analogy fails: verbs signal their verbal nouns more strongly than participles, and the poem uses implication rather than the verse’s deletion.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:180#2/r3] أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ alternatively makes هو the first object, referring to the hoarded wealth signaled by يبخلون. | mentions 5:8
[SAMIN-DURR:3:180#2/r4] author | rejects | This fails because an object of يحسبن should be accusative إياه, with no need to posit a nominative pronoun replacing an accusative.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:180#2/r5] الشيخ | reports | Al-Shaykh proposes a competing-governance analysis: يحسبن seeks two objects and يبخلون a prepositional object, so the latter governs the shared phrase and the former’s first object is omitted.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:180#3/r1] author | holds | The author notes a grammatical dispute about applying a later verb to a shared object: the Basran view deletes the earlier non-subject object, except an object of ظن, while another view challenges this restriction; the Kūfans allow the deletion.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:180#3/r2] author | holds | The author questions the restriction: if one object can be deleted concisely outside verb competition, it may also be omitted when verbs compete; this strengthens al-Shaykh’s parse.
[SAMIN-DURR:3:180#3/r3] مكي | reports | Makkī discusses the two readings; ميراث is a verbal noun like ميعاد, with yāʾ replacing wāw after kasra.

[SAMIN-DURR:3:180/r3] author | holds | In Ḥamza’s reading هو separates the two objects, not emphasis; other parses fail because خيرا is accusative and the preceding noun is explicit though omitted analytically.
