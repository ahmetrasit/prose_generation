## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r1] author | reports | Tathbit min anfusihim may mean strengthening part of themselves in faith, since wealth is kin to the soul and giving wealth steadies part of oneself while giving wealth and life steadies all of it.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r2] author | reports | Alternatively, it means confirming Islam and confidence in recompense from the core of themselves; spending purifies the donor from miserliness and love of wealth.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r3] author | holds | The elevated garden represents charity, especially zakat; its height improves its appearance and fruit.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r4] author | holds | Ibn Amir and Asim read rabwah with fatḥa; a kasra reading is also reported, and all three vowel forms are dialect variants.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r5] author | holds | Ibn Kathir, Nafi, and Abu Amr read akl with sukūn as a lightened form; the crop yields twice its usual amount after heavy rain.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r6] author | reports | Du'fayn means twice as much, with du'f meaning a like amount; another view takes it as four times the yield. | mentions 11:40
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r7] author | holds | The accusative is circumstantial, meaning multiplied; if no heavy rain falls, light rain suffices because of the garden's rich soil and cool elevated air.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r8] author | holds | The parable says sincere donations are accepted and never lost, though their value varies with accompanying circumstances; the garden can represent their standing with God and heavy or light rain their greater or lesser donations.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:265/r9] author | holds | God sees people's deeds, warning against show and encouraging sincerity.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p130/r1] author | holds | Seeking God's approval and تثبيت mean affirming Islam and truly believing in recompense from within oneself; spending evidences sincere faith in reward.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p130/r2] author | holds | Min marks the origin of تثبيت, coordinated with the purpose of spending; the likeness concerns the standing of their purified expenditure with God.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p130/r3] author | holds | A garden on high ground is especially apt because its trees produce purer and better fruit.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p130/r4] عاصم والشامي | reports | They read the word for high ground as rabwa.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p130/r5] نافع ومكي وأبو عمرو | reports | Their reading gives the word for its produce the vocalization recorded in the source.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p130/r6] author | holds | A downpour makes the garden yield twice its previous crop; even a light drizzle suffices because of the excellence of its soil.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p130/r7] author | holds | The garden and its rains portray the spenders' large or small expenditures: either is pure and increases their nearness and good standing with God when done for His approval.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p130/r8] author | holds | God sees both the amount spent and the intentions behind it, including any ostentation.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:2:263-265/r3] author | holds | The second kind of spending is superior to the first: a garden continues bearing fruit unlike a grain, signifying enduring possession as an acquired quality.
[QASHANI:2:263-265/r4] author | holds | Giving stabilizes the soul in generosity, a divine attribute; the hill signals higher rank, and abundant or light rain signifies abundant or smaller shares of mercy.
[QASHANI:2:263-265/r5] author | holds | God sees the acts and knows which category of giving they belong to.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p650/r7] الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl defines ربوة as elevated, fertile land; the word also has variant forms and a cited poem describes rain over its slopes.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p651/r1] الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash prefers ḍamma in ربوة because the plural الربا is commonly heard; the source rejects al-Suddī’s interpretation as low-lying land and connects the root with increase and height.
