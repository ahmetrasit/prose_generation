## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#10/r1] author | holds | Laḥman is the second object of naksūhā, as with a verb of giving; clothing the bones in flesh is metaphorical, like Labīd's verse about wearing Islam as a cloak.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#10/r2] author | prefers | For falammā tabayyana, one view supplies an implicit subject such as the manner in which revival became clear; the author prefers it over al-Zamakhsharī's 'what had confused him' because the context supports the former.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#10/r3] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī analyzes tabayyana and aʿlama by syntactic attraction: anna Allāha can be the subject of the first and object of the second, with the second verb governing and the first subject omitted.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#10/r4] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says this is the preferred Basran form of tanāzuʿ, illustrated by Q 18:96 and 69:19; he says first-verb governance would require a different object pronoun construction. | mentions 18:96, 69:19
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#10/r5] الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh objects that tanāzuʿ requires the two governors to share a syntactic link; here the second verb is governed by qāla (the answer to lammā), while tabayyana is governed within the lammā clause, so the verbs do not compete for one constituent.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#11/r1] الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh notes that grammarians do not analyze unlinked sequences such as 'when he came, I struck Zayd' as tanāzuʿ, and rejects al-Zamakhsharī's alleged competition between tabayyana and aʿlama. | mentions 4:176, 63:5, 69:19
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#11/r2] author | rejects | The Shaykh also sees inconsistency in calling tabayyana's subject 'omitted' after calling it 'implicit'; if deletion is intended, that is al-Kisāʾī's account, since he disallows a hidden subject before its later expression.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#11/r3] الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh cites poetry to debate whether an unexpressed subject can be anticipated before its later mention, adducing taʿfaqu bi-l-arṭā and the line huwīnani wa-hawaytu.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#11/r4] author | holds | Ibn ʿAbbās reads tabayyana passively, with the following prepositional phrase as deputy subject; Ibn al-Sumayfiʿ reads ybayyanu without tāʾ, also passive, with an understood 'manner of revival' or the prepositional phrase as deputy subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#12/r1] author | holds | The majority read qāla actively. With Ḥamzah and al-Kisāʾī's imperative aʿlam ('know'), the speaker may be God or an angel addressing the passer, or the passer may address himself by treating himself as another person (tajrīd).
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#12/r2] أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ calls the passer's self-address tajrīd, as if he detached a speaker from himself; several cited poems use such self-address.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#12/r3] author | holds | On the other readers' present-tense aʿlam, the passer is the subject and says, 'I know.'
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#12/r4] الأعمش | reports | Al-Aʿmash reads qīla passively; its deputy subject may be the verbal noun or the following clause, as in an earlier discussion in the sūrah.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#12/r5] author | holds | Ḥamzah and al-Kisāʾī have the imperative aʿlam; most others have first-person aʿlam, while al-Jaʿfī from Abū Bakr has the imperative of aʿlama. The anna clause fills both objects, or one with the other omitted, under the prior disagreement.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#2/r1] author | holds | The clause wa-hiya khāwiyah has five proposed functions: circumstantial of the passer; circumstantial of the town (if 'on its roofs' modifies it or a bare indefinite can take a circumstantial); circumstantial of its roofs, fronted; or of the possessive pronoun in 'its roofs'.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#2/r2] أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ allows the circumstantial to come from the pronoun attached to ʿurūsh, with the relation of possession as governor; the author calls this weak but eased by the roofs being part of the town.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#2/r3] الزمخشري | reports | A fifth option makes wa-hiya khāwiyah an adjective of qaryah, although the author notes the usual objection that wāw does not separate adjective and noun; al-Zamakhsharī explains it as emphasizing attachment, following Ibn Jinnī. | mentions 15:4
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#2/r4] author | holds | Abū al-Baqāʾ's detailed dependencies tie the descriptive/circumstantial options to whether ʿalā ʿurūshihā is a repeated-preposition substitute for qaryah or an adjective; the author says this argument has an evident problem.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#3/r1] author | holds | ʿAlā ʿurūshihā can be a badal of qaryah with the preposition repeated, an adjective ('a town fallen upon its roofs'), a complement of khāwiyah meaning collapsed, or attached to an omitted thābit ('standing') if khāwiyah means empty of inhabitants; the author says the last is unsupported.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#3/r2] author | reports | Some take ʿalā to mean maʿa ('with'), and ʿurūsh then means the buildings themselves.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#3/r3] author | holds | Khāwī means empty or vacant and has several related forms; khawāʾ may describe an empty house, hunger from an empty belly, level terrain, or a camel lifting its side from the ground.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#3/r4] author | reports | A poetic line uses khawāʾ for a camel holding its side above the ground.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#3/r5] author | holds | ʿUrūsh is the plural of ʿarsh, a house roof or anything built for shade; another view uses it for the building itself, supported by a line about toppling a people's structures.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#4/r1] author | prefers | In annā yuhyī, annā can mean 'when' (then temporal) or 'how' (then circumstantial); the apparent sense is 'how', governed by yuhyī, as is baʿda.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#4/r2] أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ assigns annā a circumstantial position when it means 'how'.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#4/r3] author | holds | The revival and death may be figurative for rebuilding and ruin, or literal with 'the inhabitants' supplied; hadhihi may instead point to the decayed bones and scattered bodies of the townspeople.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#4/r4] أبو البقاء | rejects | Abū al-Baqāʾ says miʾata ʿāmin is a time adverb by sense ('kept him for a hundred years'), or supplies an omitted fa-labitha; the author says both repairs are unnecessary because God made him dead for that duration.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#4/r5] author | holds | Miʾah is a known unit of one hundred with deleted final yāʾ, inferred from amʾaytu ('made into a hundred'); its plural is miʾāt, with the poetic form miʾūn rare.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#4/r6] النقاش | reports | Al-Naqqāsh derives ʿām from ʿāmah/ʿāwmah, a year-long solar circuit, linking it to swimming and Q 36:40; on this account ʿām and ʿawm parallel qawl and qāl. | mentions 36:40
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#4/r7] author | holds | The wāw in ʿām is shown by the diminutive ʿuwaym and plural aʿwām.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#5/r1] author | holds | Kam is an accusative time adverb governed by labithta, with an omitted specification such as 'days' or 'time'.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#5/r2] author | prefers | Aw in yawman aw baʿḍa yawm most likely means bal ('rather'), not doubt; bal labithta coordinates with an omitted 'I did not stay a day or part of one'.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#5/r3] author | holds | Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, and Ibn Kathīr pronounce the thāʾ distinctly throughout the Qurʾān; the other readers assimilate it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#5/r4] author | holds | Lam yat as a circumstantial clause is debated: some prefer wāw before imperfect negation with lam, others prefer mā or lammā; both rules are false because Qurʾān has examples with and without wāw, including Q 3:174 and 6:93. | mentions 3:174, 6:93
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#5/r5] author | holds | The singular pronoun in lam yatasannah after food and drink is explained because they form one nourishment, or because it refers to the nearer drink and the food clause is elided (or its nonchange follows a fortiori); a third account uses singular for dual, which the author rejects.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:259#5/r6] author | holds | Ḥamzah and al-Kisāʾī retain hāʾ at pause but drop it in connected speech; the other readers retain it in both positions. For their reading it marks pause, while the common reading may retain a pause marker in connection or have original hāʾ.
