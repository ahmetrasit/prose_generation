## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p84#2/r10] author | holds | The doubled form comes from tat-zāwar with assimilation, the light form is a reciprocal form from zawr, and tazawwar derives from izwirār.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r1] author | holds | The sun turns away from the cave at sunrise; the address is hypothetical, meaning that a viewer would see this, not that the Prophet actually saw them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r10] مجاهد | reports | The majority reading has the sun leave them at sunset; Mujahid glosses taqriḍuhum as 'leave them.'
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r11] قتادة | reports | Qatada glosses the verb as 'abandon them'; Basran authorities confirm that qarada can mean leave.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r12] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says the sun never touched them, as an honor: it passed to the cave's right at sunrise and left by its left at sunset.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r13] author | reports | The cave faced Banat Naʿsh in Roman territory; its orientation or southern and western barriers kept sun from burning them, changing their color, or wearing their clothes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r14] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says the sun's course was a divine sign, not an effect of the cave opening's orientation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r15] فرقة | reports | A group reads the verb with yāʾ from qard, meaning the cave's shade cuts them off from sunlight.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r16] فرقة | reports | Another interpretation says the sun gives them only a little of its rays, perhaps beneficial in the evening.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r17] author | holds | The point is that God sheltered them in a cave that protected their bodies and appearance from injury, heat, and cold; cloud or another cause could have diverted the sun.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r18] author | holds | Fajwa means a spacious part of the cave; the word has plural forms and denotes enough room for moving air to reach them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r19] شاعر | reports | The author introduces a poetic example after listing the plural forms of fajwa.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r2] author | holds | Tazawaru means to withdraw or incline, from azwar, a slant; azwar can describe a gaze turned aside and is extended beyond eyes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r3] ابن أبي ربيعة | reports | Ibn Abi Rabiʿa's line uses azwar for a person turning aside in fear of others.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r4] عنترة | reports | Antara's line uses azwara for turning aside from the impact of a spear.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r5] author | reports | The Mu'ta report describes Abd Allah ibn Rawaha's bier as slanted away from the biers of Jaʿfar and Zayd.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r6] أهل الحرمين وأبو عمرو | reports | They assimilate the tāʾ into zāy in tazāwaru, whose underlying form is tatazāwaru.

[QURTUBI-FULL:18:17-18/r9] الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ also records tazwāru, like taḥmāru; all these forms mean the same thing.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:17-18/r1] author | holds | The sun's rays miss the youths because the cave faces south, or because God diverted the sun; the forms تزاور, تزور, and تزوار all mean to incline.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:17-18/r2] author | reports | The Kufans drop the initial tā; Ibn Amir and Yaqub read تزور, and another reading is تزوار.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:17-18/r3] author | holds | At sunset the light cuts past the cave's sides; the youths lie in its broad middle, where air reaches without narrow-cave stuffiness or damaging solar heat.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:17-18/r4] author | holds | The cave's entrance faces the stars of the Great Bear; the sun's seasonal paths illuminate its sides to ventilate and purify it without harming the sleepers or their clothes.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:17-18/r5] author | holds | The sign may be the sleepers and their cave, the revealed story, or the sun's turning away and grazing past them; guidance benefits only those enabled to reflect.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:17-18/r6] author | holds | One who is left astray will find no protector to guide him.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#2/r1] author | holds | Readings include tazāwar, tazūr, and tazāwar; the first is from tatāzāwar, reduced by assimilation or deletion, and the root denotes turning aside.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#2/r2] author | holds | At sunrise the sun inclines away from the cave so its rays do not fall on the sleepers; at sunset it leaves them on the other side.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#2/r3] author | holds | Taqriḍuhum means that the sun cuts past them, leaves them, and turns away; they remain shaded through the day despite the cave's openness.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#2/r4] author | reports | One explanation says the cave's spacious opening let them receive fresh air and cool breezes without suffering the cave's confinement.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#2/r5] author | holds | The sun's unusual avoidance of them, though it ordinarily reaches that exposed place, is a divine sign and a special honor for them.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#2/r6] author | reports | Another explanation makes the cave's north-facing entrance keep the sleepers perpetually in shade; their story itself is also called a sign.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p6#2/r7] author | holds | Their guidance is praise for striving in God and submitting to Him, through which He led them to this noble honor; one whom God misleads has no guide.
