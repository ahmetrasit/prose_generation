## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v6p120#2/r2] author | holds | Night and day succeed one another continuously: when one goes, the other arrives, as also described in 14:33, 7:54, and 36:40. | mentions 14:33, 7:54, 36:40
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v6p121/r1] author | holds | Their alternation times worship: a person who misses a devotional portion at night can make it up by day, or vice versa; a sound hadith also describes God's nightly and daytime acceptance of repentance.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v6p121/r2] الحسن | reports | ʿUmar lengthened his Duḥā prayer to complete or make up his regular portion, citing this verse.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v6p121/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says missed worship at night may be performed in the day, and missed daytime worship at night; ʿIkrima, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, and al-Ḥasan agree.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v6p121/r4] مجاهد وقتادة | reports | Mujāhid and Qatāda explain khilfatan as differing in character: one is dark and the other bright.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p333/r1] author | holds | Night, whose sign is the moon, and day, whose sign is the sun, succeed one another with opposite qualities and replace one another in many functions, allowing what is missed in one to be made up in the other.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p333/r2] ابن جرير | reports | Ibn Jarir says the Arabs use khalaf and khilfa for alternating opposites, such as light and darkness or heat and cold.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p333/r3] الرازي | reports | Al-Razi in al-Lawamiʿ explains khilfa as things taking turns, each succeeding its companion, or as people who differ.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p333/r4] author | reports | The majority's doubled reading yadhdhakkara points to reflection through reason: change on this scale requires a powerful, choosing changer and can bring even a disbeliever to faith.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p333/r5] حمزة | reports | Hamza's light reading yadhkura means remembrance; the signs of divine power and knowledge are so clear that a slight attention suffices without extended reflection.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p333/r6] author | holds | The alternating cycles invite believers to give profound thanks: each night or day arrives before the previous one's benefits are lost, while perpetual light or darkness would produce fatigue and disrupt timed duties.

## DURR-FULL: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[DURR-FULL:v6p269/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas glosses night and day as black and white; he also says one can make up an act missed at night during the day, or vice versa.
[DURR-FULL:v6p269/r5] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says night and day succeed each other so people can remember God’s favors and give thanks; believers may also make up missed remembrance during the other period.
[DURR-FULL:v6p269/r6] الحسن | reports | Umar lengthened his forenoon prayer to complete a missed regular portion, citing the alternation of night and day; Saʿid ibn Jubayr also says either period substitutes for the other to make up worship.
[DURR-FULL:v6p271/r1] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says worship that cannot be done at night can be done by day, and each period lets a person make amends for what he could not do in the other.
[DURR-FULL:v6p271/r2] قتادة | reports | Salman advises a man unable to pray at night to keep up worship by day; Qatada reports a nightly hour when a Muslim’s prayer for good is granted and urges good works because day and night carry people toward their lifespans and Resurrection.
[DURR-FULL:v6p271/r3] عاصم | reports | ʿĀṣim recited yadhakkara with a doubled consonant; Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi is also reported to recite this form.

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v6p173#2/r1] author | holds | Because خلفة is a verbal noun, the construction needs an omitted noun; night and day succeed one another, allowing missed optional worship to be made up in the other period.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#2/r2] النبي عليه السلام | reports | The Prophet told Umar that a missed night recitation can be made up in daytime and a missed daytime recitation at night.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#2/r3] author | holds | Alternatively, night and day alternate so people can count years and time, and have set periods for earning a livelihood and for rest.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#2/r4] author | holds | The alternation recalls God's blessings and alerts people to His perfect wisdom and power.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#3/r1] author | holds | Remembering means reflecting on God's favors and creation to recognize the wise, necessary, merciful Creator; this is directed to unbelievers, while gratitude is directed to believers.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#3/r2] author | holds | شكورا with damma is a verbal noun for thanking God through obedience; أو may also mean and, making night and day occasions for remembering and thanking.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#3/r3] author | holds | The use of أو emphasizes that either remembrance or gratitude independently fulfills the purpose; coordination with و might imply both are required together.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#3/r4] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib defines gratitude as recognizing and showing a favor, contrasts it with concealing blessings, and describes gratitude of heart, tongue, and limbs.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#3/r5] author | holds | An optional litany is not legally obligatory to make up, but doing so is recommended: regular litany brings regular spiritual influx and connection, like a river supplied by mountain rain and snow.
[BURSEVI:v6p173#3/r6] الصائب | reports | Sa'ib's couplet says a weak stream cannot reach the sea, illustrating why worshippers replace missed litanies to keep their spiritual supply flowing; the author urges morning and evening practice and warns against neglect.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r1] author | reports | Khilfah may mean night and day alternate, each replacing the other in appropriate activities; this is transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r10] author | holds | Remembering may figuratively mean making up missed worship; Majmaʿ al-Bayān also says voluntary prayer after obligatory prayer.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r11] author | holds | Another good reading is reflection on God’s creative signs and gratitude for blessings in night and day; the author says it nearly eclipses alternatives absent transmitted views.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r12] author | reports | The lām may attach to ‘made’ or express purpose; aw distributes the senses or permits either independently, while the longer wording emphasizes remembrance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r13] أبي بن كعب | reports | Ubayy reads an yatadhakkara, the original form before tāʾ changes to dhāl and assimilates.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r14] النخعي وابن وثاب وزيد بن علي وطلحة وحمزة | reports | These readers use yadhkura, the form-II imperfect meaning ‘remember.’
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r2] author | reports | Alternatively, one succeeds and follows the other; khilfah names the resulting state.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r3] مجاهد | reports | Some read khilfah as difference in length or darkness and brightness, the latter attributed to Mujāhid; it may encompass both and other contrasts.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r4] البحر وزهير | reports | Al-Baḥr cites an idiom for frequent trips to relieve oneself; Zuhayr’s verse about animals following behind illustrates repeated succession.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r5] author | reports | A second poet depicts a woman moving between winter and summer homes, illustrating repeated alternation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r6] القاموس | reports | Al-Qāmūs defines khalf and khilfah as mutually different; this needs no omitted ‘possessing alternation,’ since singular names the difference.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r7] author | reports | Another reading is that each of night and day frequently comes and goes.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r8] author | reports | Alternation lets one make up a devotional portion missed in one period during the other; early authorities transmit this sense.
[ALUSI-FULL:v19p42#2/r9] عمر | reports | Al-Ṭayālisī and Ibn Abī Ḥātim report that ʿUmar prolonged forenoon prayer to complete a missed litany and recited the verse.
