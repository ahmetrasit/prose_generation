## ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p353/r2] author | holds | The discourse moves from day and night as models of developmental trials, to reassurance, promised better times, examples of past divine help, and duties arising from those favours.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p356/r2] author | holds | The oaths make the complementary pattern a proof: just as day and night are both needed, alternating ease, hardship, joy, and sorrow train the soul.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p356/r3] author | holds | God tests whether people respond to good times with gratitude and hard times with patience; the Prophet’s opposition and scant resources are training, not abandonment.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p356/r4] author | holds | For brevity, the oath’s answer states only the reassurance the Prophet most needed: his present hardship does not mean God is indifferent or displeased.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p357/r1] author | holds | The Prophet feared that his own mistakes had intensified Quraysh hostility and angered God; waiting for revelation could deepen the worry, though revelation comes according to divine wisdom. | mentions 94:2, 94:3
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p357/r2] author | holds | This assurance addresses the Prophet’s private anxiety about his mission and revelation, a concern also discussed in Sūrah Ṭāhā and Sūrah al-Qiyāmah.

## SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT: فاضل صالح السامرائي
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p411/r1] author | holds | The object of “forsake” is explicit, but the object of “despise” is omitted as a courtesy: speakers avoid even negating hurtful conduct toward someone they honor.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p411/r2] author | holds | Naming the object with the favorable act of farewell and omitting it with the negative act teaches Muslims respectful speech; the verse honors the Prophet twice.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p411/r3] author | holds | Calling God “your Lord” reassures the Prophet: a Lord nurtures and cares for his servant, and the term conveys the Prophet's particular relationship and protection.
