## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p59/r1] author | holds | Noah's son plans to climb a mountain, mistakenly treating the divinely sent flood as ordinary waters from which elevation could protect him.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p59/r2] author | holds | Noah's لا عاصم denies every possible protector, emphasizing that the mountain cannot save anyone from God's punishment; اليوم distinguishes this event from ordinary calamities where natural means can help.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p59/r3] author | holds | Calling the flood أمر الله instead of repeating ماء magnifies the punishment, corrects the son's mistaken description, and establishes that only God's mercy can grant protection. | mentions 11:40
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p59/r4] author | prefers | The strongest interpretation makes rescue by divine mercy the exception, redirecting Noah's concern for his son toward God as the true refuge.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p59/r5] author | reports | A second view treats عاصم as a rare relational form, so that the exception means no possessor of protection or protected person exists except one shown mercy; a passive reading supports this.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r1] author | reports | A third reading takes ʿāṣim literally and man raḥima as the one shown mercy: none protects from God’s decree, but one whom He pities is protected; the exception is disconnected.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r10] أبو حيان | reports | He makes al-yawm accusative through an implied verb indicated by ʿāṣim, with min amr Allāh attached to that verb.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r11] الحوفي | reports | Al-Ḥūfī allows al-yawm and the prepositional phrase to attach to an omitted predicate, or al-yawm to modify ʿāṣim with that phrase as predicate.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r12] أبو البقاء | reports | Abū al-Baqāʾ rejects a time adverb as predicate of a concrete noun; he makes min amr Allāh govern the noun and the prepositional phrase the predicate. Abū Ḥayyān also rejects the adverb as modifier.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r13] author | holds | The wave cut off Noah’s exchange with his son; a report says a huge wave arrived before their conversation ended and swallowed the son and his horse, while he rode proudly.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r14] author | holds | The verse itself only states that the wave intervened; Noah learned of his son’s drowning afterward.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r15] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ reads ‘between them’ as between Noah’s son and the mountain; Ibn Abī Ḥātim and Abū al-Shaykh transmit it from al-Qāsim ibn Abī Bazza.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r2] author | reports | This disconnected exception is challenged as uncommon because its clause differs from the first in both affirmation and nominal versus verbal form.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r3] author | reports | A fourth reading makes ʿāṣim mean maʿṣūm and man raḥima mean the merciful; the disconnected exception means only the merciful can rescue whomever he wills.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r4] author | reports | The cited line uses fātin for one overcome, supporting ʿāṣim as the one protected.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r5] author | reports | A fifth reading supplies an omitted place and connects the exception: only the ark, place of the people shown mercy, protects; it fits the mountain refuge and ranks after the first reading.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r6] صاحب الكشف | reports | The sixth reading means no protected one exists except the place of those shown mercy; by metonymy, protecting the ship protects its occupants.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r7] author | reports | A seventh reading treats the exception as free: no person or place is protected except one whom God shows mercy.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r8] author | prefers | Although some call the free-exception reading closest, al-Ālūsī chooses none over the first interpretation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p60/r9] أبو حيان | reports | Abū Ḥayyān says lā’s predicate is omitted as understood, ‘existent’; Hijazi speakers commonly omit it, while Tamim speakers require it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p61/r1] أبو السعود | rejects | Abū al-Suʿūd objects that the son’s drowning follows the wave separating him from Noah, since the mountain could not protect him even without a wave between him and the refuge.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p61/r2] author | reports | A reply says the consequence need not be immediate, or that the son assumed the water would not reach him.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p61/r3] author | holds | The verse depicts unbelievers’ drowning as emphatically certain; kāna intensifies their being among the drowned rather than merely becoming so.

## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:11:43/r1] author | holds | This is not an ordinary flood from which customary means might rescue someone: it is a special divine command and tremendous punishment, from which neither a mountain nor anything else can save.
[ELMALILI:11:43/r2] author | holds | Those excepted by God’s mercy are the people aboard the Ark.
[ELMALILI:11:43/r3] author | holds | The well-known view is that the Flood was universal, though its universality does not extend to realms beyond the earth. | mentions 71:26
[ELMALILI:11:43/r4] author | holds | On earth, universality must be measured over the inhabited regions; whether Noah was sent to all humanity or only a particular people is disputed, so a universal flood within his people’s region is certain and beyond it conjectural.
