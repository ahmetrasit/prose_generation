## MUJAHID: Mujāhid b. Jabr, d. 104 AH

## MAJAZ: Abū ʿUbayda Maʿmar b. al-Muthannā, d. 209 AH
[MAJAZ:v2p270/r2] author | holds | Halāʿ is the verbal noun for the worst kind of impatience shown by one who despairs at harm and withholds good.

## TUSTARI: Sahl al-Tustarī, d. 283 AH
[TUSTARI:70:20-21/r1] author | holds | When a person becomes poor he grieves, and when he becomes wealthy he withholds.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH

## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v22p226/r1] author | holds | Harm means hardship and poverty, and one who panics does not endure patiently or seek reward.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v30p113#3/r1] بعضهم | reports | Some take 'the human being' as the unbeliever; others take it generally, since the verse then exempts those who pray.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p113#3/r2] author | holds | Haluʿ is intense acquisitiveness and little patience; al-Farrāʾ and al-Mubarrad gloss it as fretful, while Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā says the verse itself explains it: extreme distress under harm and withholding under benefit.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p113#3/r3] القاضي | reports | The Qāḍī argues that 'the human was created anxious' does not mean God made him inevitably blameworthy: God does not blame His own act, and believers can struggle against this trait.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p113#3/r4] author | holds | Haluʿ has an inward psychological state and outward acts showing that state; the involuntary inner state is created by God, while speech and actions expressing it remain choices people can refrain from.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p114/r1] author | reports | The harm and good may mean poverty and wealth, or illness and health: in hardship a person complains, and in ease withholds what is good.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p114/r2] author | holds | The fault is not seeking relief itself but restricting concern to immediate bodily conditions; one should accept poverty or illness as God's decree and use health or wealth to seek the afterlife.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:70:18-21/r3] author | holds | When harmed, a person is greatly distressed; when prosperous, he withholds intensely. These three qualities are innate dispositions, actual or expected; each إذا governs its following description.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p279#10/r1] author | holds | “The human” denotes the species, allowing worshippers to be excepted; Ibn ʿAbbās explains halūʿ by the next two verses as quickly distressed at harm and withholding at good.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p279#10/r2] author | holds | Halūʿ is quick distress when touched by harm and quick withholding when touched by good; one who receives good hoards it and denies it to others.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p279#10/r3] محمد بن عبد الله بن طاهر | reports | When asked by Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ṭāhir, Thaʿlab replied that God's own explanation is clearest.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p279#10/r4] author | holds | This is human temperament, but people are commanded to oppose their temperament and follow revealed law; harm may be poverty or illness and good may be wealth or health.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p274/r9] ثعلب | reports | Asked by Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Tahir what hala' means, he says the following verses define it: distress makes a person intensely anxious and prosperity makes him withhold.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p275/r1] author | holds | Human anxiety and withholding are so entrenched that humanity is described as created bearing them, as in 21:37; al-khayr here means wealth. | mentions 21:37

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r1] author | prefers | ‘Jazu‘an’ and ‘manu‘an’ can be overlapping circumstantial states of the pronoun in ‘halu‘an’; the two time phrases modify those states.
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r2] author | holds | Abu al-Baqa’s wording calls ‘jazu‘an’ another circumstantial but can mislead readers into treating it as separate; his stated governor shows the intended overlapping relation.
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r3] مكي | reports | Makki analyzes both adjectives as predicates of an omitted ‘kana’ or ‘sara’: when harm touches him he becomes distressed, and when good touches him he becomes withholding.
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r4] مكي | reports | Makki also allows the two words to modify ‘halu‘an’, but finds this distant because the time phrase must be understood before its adjective; the author rejects that objection as misplaced.
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r5] author | rejects | The objection to the adjectival analysis is mistaken: its issue is an adverbial phrase preceding its governor, whereas the real prohibited order would put an adjective’s governed element before the adjective.
