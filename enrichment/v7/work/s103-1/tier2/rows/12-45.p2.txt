## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p283/r1] author | holds | After the dream stumped the king's court, the cupbearer remembered Joseph and his own and companion's interpretations after a long interval; أمّة means a long period and the clause is circumstantial.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p283/r2] author | holds | The common reading ادكر derives from اذْتكر by changing and assimilating consonants; al-Ḥasan reads واذكر with a different assimilation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p283/r3] الأشهب العقيلي | reports | Al-Ashhab al-ʿUqaylī reads after immah with kasra on the hamza, meaning after the blessing of surviving execution.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p283/r4] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains the blessing as God drawing Joseph near to release and cites a poem using immah for blessing or good fortune.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p283/r5] author | reports | Yughāth may derive from rescue and relief or from rain; an Arab woman says the latter form when asking for rain.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r1] author | holds | Immah with fatḥa on hamza means blessing or a good condition; readers transmit forms with an open hamza, a light mīm, or a still mīm.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r10] author | holds | Ṣiddīq is an intensive form; the cupbearer calls Joseph that after knowing him for a long time and testing his truthfulness in both dream interpretations.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r2] ابن عباس وزيد بن علي والضحاك وقتادة وأبو رجاء وشبيل بن عزرة وربيعة بن عمرو | reports | These readers use after ummah with fatḥa on hamza, a light mīm, and hāʾ; similar readings are variously reported from Ibn ʿUmar, Mujāhid, and ʿIkrima.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r3] عكرمة ومجاهد وشبيل بن عزرة | reports | They also read ummah with a still mīm as an irregular verbal noun.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r4] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī calls the still-mīm reading an error; Abū Ḥayyān objects that he habitually labels readings erroneous.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r5] author | holds | The cupbearer says he will report the interpretation from someone who possesses its knowledge, not from himself.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r6] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan reads أنا أتيكم, from the verb meaning to come, as also found in the codex.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r7] أبي | reports | Ubayy's codex adds فأرسلون, meaning send me to Joseph and allow me to ask for an interpretation.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says Joseph's prison was outside the king's city; another report places it inside the city, with a present-day site identified by the Nile eight miles from Fusṭāṭ.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v6p284/r9] author | holds | The narrative elides the dispatch and arrival: the king sends the cupbearer to Joseph, who then addresses him.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45#2/r1] author | holds | أنا أنبئكم is the clause quoted after the statement verb.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45#2/r2] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan reads أنا آتيكم from أتى, a meaning close to أنا أنبئكم.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45/r1] author | prefers | وادكر is most likely circumstantial from the relative pronoun or its referent, the subject of نجا; it may alternatively coordinate with نجا.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45/r2] العامة | reports | The common ادكر is from اذتكر: tāʾ becomes dāl after ذال and the neighboring consonants assimilate; al-Hasan reads with ذال instead, as in مدكر at 54:15. | mentions 54:15
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45/r3] العامة | reports | The common reading أمة has a damma, doubled mīm and tanwin, meaning a long period.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45/r4] الأشهب العقيلي | reports | Al-Ashhab al-Uqayli reads إمة with kasra, interpreted as a blessing; al-Zamakhshari cites Adi's line as evidence.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45/r5] ابن عباس وزيد بن علي وقتادة والضحاك وأبو رجاء | reports | They read أمه with an open hamza, light mīm, and hāʾ tanwin, deriving it from أمه, forgetfulness.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45/r6] مجاهد وعكرمة وشبيل بن عزرة | reports | They read بعد أمه with a still mīm as a nonstandard verbal noun of أمه, forgetfulness.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45/r7] الزمخشري | rejects | Al-Zamakhshari reports that some grammarians call the still-mīm reading erroneous; the author says Zamakhshari only attributes the criticism to others and an authentic reading cannot be wrong.
[SAMIN-DURR:12:45/r8] author | holds | بعد is governed by ادكر.

[BURSEVI:v4p173/r1] author | holds | The survivor was the cupbearer; ‘remembered’ derives from idh-tadhakkara, with tāʾ changed to dāl and assimilated with dhāl, meaning he recalled Joseph.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r1] author | holds | The survivor is Joseph's cupbearer. Most read وادكر with dāl, from a form where t assimilated to d; al-Hasan reads اذكر with ذ assimilated to ذ, while the first reading is more eloquent. Both mean recalling Joseph.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r10] author | reports | The plural imperative send me can address only the king as an honorific plural; a report says the cupbearer knelt and asked that the king send him to a knowledgeable man in prison. Ibn Abbas says the prison was outside the king's city, while another view puts it inside; Al-Alusi leaves its actual location to God.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r2] author | holds | After a long interval, after umma means after a span or community of time.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r3] الأشهب العقيلي | reports | Al-Ashhab al-Uqayli reads إمة as a favor: release from the threat of execution and prison, and the king's beneficence toward the cupbearer; a verse illustrates this usage.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r4] ابن عطية | rejects | Ibn Atiyya takes the favor as God's favor on Joseph by bringing his release near, but Al-Alusi considers this interpretation far-fetched.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r5] ابن عباس وزيد بن علي | reports | Ibn Abbas, Zayd ibn Ali, Mujahid, Ikrima, and Shubayl ibn Azra are reported to read with a light, open form from a verb meaning to forget; Al-Alusi dismisses those who rejected this reading.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r6] author | holds | The phrase after a long time is parenthetical between the speech verb and its content; it could be circumstantial with an understood قد, but joining it to the relative clause is rejected because the later remembering is new information, not a known description.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r7] author | holds | The cupbearer offers to inform them through access to someone who knows the interpretation, not from his own knowledge; he says send me so they will dispatch him to Joseph.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r8] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan reads أنا آتيكم, 'I will bring you,' instead of 'I will inform you'; when corrected, he asks whether Joseph himself had been informing them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v12p253/r9] أبي | reports | Ubayy is also reported to read أنا آتيكم; al-Bahr says this is likewise the reading in the codex.
