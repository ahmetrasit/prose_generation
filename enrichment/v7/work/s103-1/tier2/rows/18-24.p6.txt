## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v5p181#2/r1] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Abū al-Layth transmits via Abū Hurayra that Solomon vowed to visit one hundred wives to have warriors, forgot to say إن شاء الله, and all but one-half of a childless birth failed; the Prophet said the vow would have succeeded had he said it.
[BURSEVI:v5p181#2/r2] author | holds | When people act as if they have independent will, God customarily brings about the opposite to show that only He has true will.
[BURSEVI:v5p181#2/r3] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A hadith says complete faith includes making an exception in all speech, inwardly as well as outwardly; a merely verbal exception is ineffective.
[BURSEVI:v5p181#2/r4] مولانا | reports | His couplet says that omitting an exception hardens intention, and the soul—not just passing words—must join the exception.
[BURSEVI:v5p181#2/r5] روضة الخطيب | reports | A humorous story tells of a man who refused to say إن شاء الله while going to buy a donkey, lost his money, and later described the loss with repeated exceptions.
[BURSEVI:v5p181#2/r6] ابن عباس | reports | He permitted a delayed exception based on this verse, while most jurists rejected that rule because it would unsettle confessions, divorce, emancipation, and reports about the future.
[BURSEVI:v5p181#2/r7] القرطبي | reports | The verse allows a delayed exception to retract and escape sin, but an exception changing a legal ruling must be connected to the original statement.
[BURSEVI:v5p181#2/r8] أبو حنيفة | reports | At al-Manṣūr's court, Abū Ḥanīfa ruled an exception after a pause to an oath ineffective; Ibn Isḥāq appealed to Ibn ʿAbbās's view, but Abū Ḥanīfa explained its valid interpretation and warned that broad use could let people evade allegiance. Al-Manṣūr imprisoned Ibn Isḥāq.
[BURSEVI:v5p181/r1] author | holds | A future commitment is permissible only while conditioned on God's will; inwardly, choices and deeds all depend on God's will. When the qualification is remembered, one should say إن شاء الله.
[BURSEVI:v5p181/r2] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says the Prophet said إن شاء الله when the revelation arrived after his lapse.
[BURSEVI:v5p181/r3] author | holds | He asks God for a clearer sign than the Cave youths' story as proof of prophethood; God gave him even more evident signs in distant prophetic histories and future events to the end of time.
[BURSEVI:v5p181/r4] سعدي المفتي | reports | Since the Jews made the Cave story evidence for Muhammad's prophethood, God diminished its importance with this wording, as at the chapter's opening.
[BURSEVI:v5p181/r5] السمرقندي | reports | Another reading says that when someone forgets a thing, he should remember God and ask to be guided to something else that is closer, better, and more beneficial.
[BURSEVI:v5p181/r6] الإمام | reports | One may die before tomorrow or be obstructed while alive; without conditioning a promise on God's will, failure would be a lie, repugnant and unfitting for prophets.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p247#3/r1] ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAtiyya, following al-Tabari's rebuttal, calls the exception for God's will corrupt; Alusi says this is unfair and the exception properly attaches to the prohibition. | mentions 18:23
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p247#3/r2] الكشف | reports | The exception can mean do not say it in any state except while mentioning God's will, since a spoken promise cannot literally be joined to the divine will; another analysis makes it a state exception with an elided bāʾ.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p247#3/r3] author | reports | The exception may instead refer to times, with God's will interpreted as permission: do not speak until God authorizes it; it may also be a disconnected exception signaling a permanent prohibition because the time of God's will is unknown.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p247#3/r4] author | rejects | The permanent-prohibition reading is not that God never wills the Prophet to speak because such speech is intrinsically bad; limiting the rule to revelation still does not follow from the text's indefinite noun under negation. | mentions 7:89
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p247#3/r5] author | prefers | The apparent reading attaches the exception to the prohibition, and the Prophet's community is included in the address; it cannot sensibly attach to the declaration that he will act, because either proposed sense conflicts with the prohibition. | mentions 18:23
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r1] الخفاجي | rejects | Al-Khafaji rejects attaching the exception to nonperformance: Sunnis and Muʿtazilites alike cannot make that a coherent prohibition, since God can prevent an intended act through death or another impediment.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r10] الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib defines exception as removing what a preceding general statement entails, as in the verse about what may be eaten, or what a particular utterance entails, as in a conditional divorce. | mentions 6:145
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r11] author | holds | The Prophetic report says that whoever swears to something and says if God wills has made an exception; al-Ālūsī therefore rejects explanations that call the phrase an exception only because of its Quranic wording or resemblance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r12] author | holds | Remembering the Lord means remembering His will, with an omitted genitive; the preceding command is to say if God wills, as the Prophet did when this verse came down.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r13] author | holds | Forgetting means an inadvertent omission; the command applies upon remembering, not during forgetfulness, and requires recovery whether the interval is short or long.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r14] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās held that a person may add the exception even after a year; this is also reported from the Imams of the Prophet's family and as one narration from Aḥmad.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r15] ابن جبير | reports | Ibn Jubayr says a person who swore and forgot to except retains the right of exception for a month.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r16] عطاء | reports | ʿAṭāʾ, reporting from ʿAmr ibn Dīnār, allows the exception for as long as it takes to milk a camel.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r17] طاوس | reports | Ṭāwūs limits the exception to the time a person remains in the same sitting.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r18] إبراهيم | reports | Ibrāhīm says the exception is available only while the person is still speaking.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r19] عامة الفقهاء | holds | Most jurists require the exception to be connected to the oath; allowing it after any delay would undermine the finality of acknowledgments, divorce, emancipation, and truthful or false statements.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r2] الخفاجي | rejects | He also rejects reading the verse as forbidding a Muʿtazilite to claim independent authorship whenever God's will does not accompany the act.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r20] أبو حنيفة | reports | In a story, Abū Ḥanīfa answers al-Manṣūr's objection to his disagreement with Ibn ʿAbbās by warning that delayed exceptions would let people leave a pledge of allegiance and then break it; al-Manṣūr approves the answer.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r3] الاعتراض على المعتزلة | reports | A familiar objection says that if God wills only obedience, a debtor who vows to pay tomorrow if God wills must be perjured when he fails to pay, making the exception ineffective; it is like a condition whose condition occurs.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r4] المرتضى | reports | Al-Murtaḍā distinguishes exceptions that suspend the legal force of oaths, contracts, and reports; this use may be retrospective but is unsuitable for sins because it displays reliance on God.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r5] المرتضى | reports | A second use in permitted matters asks God to enable the act and preserve the existing state; al-Murtaḍā considers this possible in the verse.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r6] المرتضى | reports | A third use merely expresses reliance on God without deciding whether the statement is true or false, and could also fit the verse.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r7] المرتضى | reports | A fourth use seeks divine grace and facilitation, which applies only to obedience and cannot explain the verse's broader scope; if the debtor intended another sense, he need not be perjured.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r8] author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī says the objection and this answer are unpersuasive, and the Muʿtazilite position can be refuted without them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p248/r9] السيرافي | reports | Using exception for qualification by saying if God wills, and for qualification by a condition generally, is established in Arabic usage.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p250#2/r1] بعضهم | reports | One interpretation makes remembering the Lord mean glorifying and seeking forgiveness after forgetting the exception, urging it by implying that its omission merits repentance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p250#2/r2] بعضهم | reports | Another reads the command as remembering God and His punishment after leaving an instruction, to prompt correction; forgetfulness here is figurative for leaving something.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p250#2/r3] بعضهم | reports | A further reading says to remember God when forgetfulness comes so He may remind the person of what was forgotten; it treats forgetfulness as intransitive, but connects poorly to the context.
