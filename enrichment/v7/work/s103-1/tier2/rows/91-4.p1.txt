## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p141#2/r1] author | holds | The night covers the sun and hides its light; other views take the pronoun to mean Earth or the world. | mentions 91:3
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p141#2/r2] أبو حيان | reports | Abu Hayyan says the present tense maintains the verse-ending rhyme; using ghāshāh would require omitting one of the verb's two objects.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p141#2/r3] بعض الأجلة | reports | Some scholars say the present tense indicates that all times are alike to God.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p141#2/r4] الخفاجي | reports | Al-Khafaji says night here is the darkness that arises after light, rather than original nonbeing; this more clearly manifests power and calls for the changed tense.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p141#2/r5] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari sees a problem in governing each idha: coordination seems to join constituents governed by different operators, while treating each as an oath risks several oaths sharing one answer, which al-Khalil and Sibawayh disliked.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p141#2/r6] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari answers that the oath wāw stands for the oath verb and the later coordinating wāws inherit its genitive and accusative work, as in his example of striking several objects.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p141#2/r7] author | rejects | Alusi objects that the first conjunction has no accusative object and that conjunctions are not generally grammatical operators; other analyses also remove the difficulty by allowing coordination across operators or separate oath answers.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p141#2/r8] author | holds | The objection also assumes idha must be temporal; it may instead be a substitute for its following noun, or be attached to omitted nouns such as the moon's following, the day's display, and night's covering.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p142/r1] author | reports | The line about companions departing uses idha as a substitute for “tomorrow”; if idha remains temporal, omitted nouns for the moon's following, the day's display, and the night's covering can govern it. | mentions 91:2, 91:3
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p142/r2] author | prefers | The deeper analysis is that idha attaches to an omitted noun such as “greatness,” since oaths honor their objects and are not temporally restricted; objections to this analysis are answered by the figurative force of the oath. | mentions 81:17, 81:18
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p142/r3] author | reports | Alusi rejects the claim that this omitted-noun analysis is redundant even if the oath itself is figurative, because there would otherwise be no syntactic element to which the intended emphasis attaches.
