## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:4-5/r3] author | holds | God rolls night over day and day over night, concealing one with the other like clothing wrapped around its wearer or a wrapped object, or making them follow in recurrent courses.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r1] author | holds | The sun and moon each run to a fixed endpoint, either the end of its cycle or the cessation of its motion.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r2] author | holds | God is mighty over every possible thing and all things; delaying punishment preserves the mercy and general benefit in these creations.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH

[NASAFI-FULL:v4p47#3/r2] author | holds | The sun and moon are appointed to run until Judgment Day.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p47#3/r3] author | holds | God's might punishes those who fail to infer the Creator from the sun and moon, while He forgives those who reflect and believe in their governor.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:39:5-6/r1] author | holds | God creates by manifesting in forms and veiling through them, while governing all; the sun and moon’s subjection proves that no other has independent essence, quality, or act.
[QASHANI:39:5-6/r2] author | holds | Al-ʿAzīz is the one who subdues all by power; al-Ghaffār either veils creation in His own light or covers the existence and qualities of whoever He wills.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p184/r2] author | holds | The ordered creation of the upper and lower worlds, alternation of night and day, and regulated sun and moon demonstrate God's unity and dominion.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p184/r3] author | reports | “A fixed term” may be the Resurrection, when the world's structure collapses, or their daily setting and rising, or the moon's phases each month.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p184/r4] author | holds | Takwir means extending one over the other, as though the one partly covers its counterpart.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p184/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas glosses the alternation as night being carried over day.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p184/r6] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says the increase of one is introduced through the decrease of the other.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p184/r7] أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu ʿUbayda simply says one enters into the other.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p184/r8] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari offers images of one replacing the other like clothing, hiding it from sight, or rolling on in successive coils like a turban.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p184/r9] author | holds | Al-ʿAziz cannot be overcome; al-Ghaffar forgives penitents, or the name metaphorically describes God's patient forbearance.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:39:5/r1] author | prefers | The تكور clause is most clearly an independent statement; Abu al-Baqa's circumstantial parse is weak because the event follows creation unless treated as a prospective state.
[SAMIN-DURR:39:5/r2] author | holds | التكوير means wrapping or rolling; night and day replace or cover one another like clothing, conceal each other, or roll over each other repeatedly, the last best matching the root according to al-Zamakhshari.
[SAMIN-DURR:39:5/r3] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib defines تكوير as rotation and gathering, and reads the verse as the sun's course and the lengthening and shortening of night and day; he also lists related senses for horses, bees, a travel saddle, and a district.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v7p86/r1] author | holds | God created and owns everything in heaven and earth, and makes night and day follow each other rapidly, as Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, and al-Suddī explain. | mentions 7:54
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v7p86/r2] author | holds | The sun and moon run until a term known to God, ending at the Resurrection; He is mighty yet forgiving to those who repent and return to Him.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p419/r4] author | holds | The heavens and earth were brought from nonexistence in truth, perfectly and without flaw, variation, or disorder.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p419/r5] author | holds | The repeated covering of night and day demonstrates divine power and subjugation even more strongly than the image of one entering the other.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p419/r6] author | holds | Takwīr depicts night rapidly wrapping over and covering day, then day covering night; each replaces and conceals the other in a repeated cycle.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p419/r7] author | holds | The alternation also includes each light period lengthening as it takes time from the other, like clothing wrapped over what it covers.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p420/r1] author | holds | The ordering of night before day follows the order of creation, since darkness precedes the light of sunrise.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p420/r2] author | holds | God subdued the sun and moon, compelling each to follow its known course and fixed limit without benefit to the objects themselves.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p420/r3] author | holds | Both bodies run by God's irrevocable decree, and their courses illustrate alternating human states of contraction and expansion, cognition and ignorance.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p420/r4] القشيري | reports | Al-Qushayrī maps the sky's cycles onto spiritual states: reason, knowledge, and recognition wax like stars, moons, and suns, while unity and connection alternate with doubt and separation.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p420/r5] author | holds | The appointed term is the endpoint of each orbit and motion; specifying the governing decree emphasizes God's domination more than naming an endpoint alone.
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p420/r6] author | holds | God alone is mighty; His repeated forgiveness explains why He delays seizing sinners, while He may erase their sins inwardly and outwardly or punish them by His might.
