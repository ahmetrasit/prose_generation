## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:9/r1] author | holds | Believers are warned against the hypocrites' habits: those hypocrites withheld their wealth and urged others not to spend on the Messenger's companions.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:9/r2] author | reports | The remembrance from which wealth distracts may mean Hajj and zakat, Quran recitation, or continual remembrance.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:9/r3] الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says the remembrance means the five prescribed prayers.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:9/r4] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says it means all obligations, equivalent to saying obedience to God.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:9/r5] author | reports | Another view takes the address as directed to hypocrites: they professed faith, so they should believe inwardly as well.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:9/r6] author | holds | Anyone distracted by property and children from obedience to the Lord is among the losers.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:63:8-10/r2] author | holds | Wealth and children distract through their management and attention from prayer and other worship that recalls God; addressing the possessions directly heightens the prohibition.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:63:8-10/r3] author | holds | Anyone diverted by possessions is a loser, having traded the lasting great good for a paltry, transient thing.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p249#4/r1] author | holds | Wealth and children should not distract from the five prayers or Qur'an; their management, increase, affection, and care can occupy people.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p249#4/r2] author | reports | Another interpretation is to become busy investing wealth instead of managing one's afterlife, or pleasing children instead of preparing for return to God.
[NASAFI-FULL:v4p249#4/r3] author | holds | Such people lose their trade by selling the enduring for the transient.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:63:9-11/r1] author | holds | Sincere faith makes love of God outweigh love of family and wealth; otherwise attachments veil believers, waste their innate light, and lead to loss.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p184/r2] author | holds | Money distracts through pursuit of increase and enjoyment of accumulation; children distract through delight and concern for their interests. Remembrance broadly includes prayer, praise, glorification, and supplication.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p184/r3] الضحاك وعطاء | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and ʿAṭāʾ restrict remembrance to obligatory prayer; al-Ḥasan also says all duties, al-Kalbī says jihad with the Messenger, and another view says Qurʾān.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p184/r4] author | holds | Those distracted by wealth and children lose by choosing the immediate over the later and the perishable over the lasting.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p133/r1] author | holds | Believers are urged to remember God abundantly and not let wealth or children distract them; whoever is absorbed by worldly adornment instead of obedience loses himself and his family at Resurrection.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p133/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says someone with means for Hajj or liable zakat who neglects it will ask to return at death; he gives two hundred as the zakat threshold and provisions plus a mount for Hajj.

[BIQAI-FULL:v7p614/r5] author | holds | Whoever gives religious duties priority can entrust worldly needs to God, who provides for and protects the servant devoted to Him.
