[TABATABAI:70:19-21/r2] author | prefers | Haluʿ means intense eagerness; the following verses aptly explain it as panic at harm and withholding when good comes.
[TABATABAI:70:19-21/r3] author | holds | Human eagerness targets what a person sees as good or useful for himself, producing distress at harm and selfish withholding of benefits.
[TABATABAI:70:19-21/r4] author | holds | Innate eagerness rooted in self-love is not itself a vice; it enables pursuit of happiness and becomes blameworthy only through excess, deficiency, or poor judgment.
[TABATABAI:70:19-21/r5] author | holds | Children initially pursue whatever their instincts present as good and resist sharing; reason later revises their judgments, while persistent appetite can lead to rejecting truth and violating others' rights.
[TABATABAI:70:19-35/r1] author | holds | As reason develops, eagerness can turn toward real good: fear of sin and haste toward forgiveness, while worldly hardship and duty call for the patience God prescribes.
[TABATABAI:70:19-35/r2] author | holds | When people ignore reason and follow appetite without limits, they turn a God-given means of happiness into a cause of misery, withdrawal, and hoarding.
