## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:93:3/r1] author | holds | ‘Ma wadda‘aka’ is the oath’s answer; the common reading doubles dāl from tawdi‘, while ‘Urwa, his son Hisham, Abu Haywa, and Ibn Abi ‘Abla read a light dāl from wada‘ (‘leave’).
[SAMIN-DURR:93:3/r2] author | reports | Although standard Arabic generally uses taraka in place of wada‘ and its derivatives, the verb occurs in poetry; two anonymous poems demonstrate it.
[SAMIN-DURR:93:3/r3] author | reports | One explanation says tawdi‘ intensifies wada‘, since someone who bids farewell while leaving has emphatically abandoned the person.
[SAMIN-DURR:93:3/r4] author | holds | ‘Ma qala’ means ‘he did not hate you’; the usual imperfect qalahu has kasra, while Tayyi’ uses fatḥa, and a poem witnesses the latter.
[SAMIN-DURR:93:3/r5] author | holds | The object of ‘qala’ is omitted because it is understood, and for the verse cadence; the same ellipsis follows ‘fa-awa’ and what comes after it.
