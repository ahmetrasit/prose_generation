## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v10p233/r10] النبي | reports | After the verse came down, the Prophet told his companions to rejoice: ease had arrived and one hardship would not overcome two eases.
[THALABI:v10p233/r11] ابن مسعود | reports | He swears that even if hardship were hidden in a burrow, ease would seek it out; one hardship cannot overcome two eases.
[THALABI:v10p233/r12] العلماء | reports | They explain the two-eases saying from Arabic definiteness: repeated definite hardship denotes one, while repeated indefinite ease denotes two.
[THALABI:v10p233/r7] author | holds | The hardship is the Prophet’s struggle against polytheists and his difficult mission; its ease is relief and victory that brings them to accept the truth willingly or unwillingly.
[THALABI:v10p233/r9] قيل | reports | Another interpretation assigns the first ease to this world and the repeated ease to the hereafter.
[THALABI:v10p234#2/r1] النبي | reports | The Prophet tells Ibn Abbas to rely on God, seek Him in ease and hardship, remain patient, and know that patience brings victory, distress brings relief, and hardship comes with ease.
[THALABI:v10p234#2/r2] العتبي | reports | Al-Utbi says an unseen caller answered his grief with a poem urging him to recall the opening of al-Sharh, because hardship is between two eases; God then removed his distress.
[THALABI:v10p234#2/r3] إسحاق بن بهلول | reports | His poem counsels against despair during a difficult day by remembering that easier times have come during a long life.
[THALABI:v10p234/r1] الحسن بن يحيى بن نصر الجرجاني | rejects | He rejects the grammar offered for one hardship and two eases: repeating a definite noun and an indefinite noun does not force one sword but two in the example sentence.
[THALABI:v10p234/r10] أبو بكر الوراق | reports | He says that with hardship in this world comes the reward of Paradise.
[THALABI:v10p234/r11] القاسم | reports | He describes the gladness of this happiness as extending from the dawn hours of worldly life to the pleasure of the afterlife.
[THALABI:v10p234/r12] قراءة العامة | reports | The common reading pronounces the sīn lightly in both repetitions of yusr.
[THALABI:v10p234/r13] أبو جعفر وعيسى | reports | They read both instances with a damma on the sīn.
[THALABI:v10p234/r14] قراءة عبد الله | reports | The codex attributed to Abdullah has the ease clause only once, without repetition.
[THALABI:v10p234/r2] author | holds | The saying means one hardship cannot overcome both promised eases; it can overcome worldly ease, but the hereafter’s lasting ease cannot be defeated.
[THALABI:v10p234/r3] author | holds | The Prophet was poor and mocked by the polytheists, who offered to gather wealth for him; he grieved, thinking they rejected him because of poverty.
[THALABI:v10p234/r4] author | holds | The first verses recount God’s favors, while the prefixed fa in the first ease clause links it as a promise of immediate worldly relief and wealth.
[THALABI:v10p234/r5] author | holds | The prefixed fa in fa-inna signals coordination or response; it reassures the Prophet not to grieve over their words, promising ease soon in this world.
[THALABI:v10p234/r6] author | holds | The worldly promise came true when God opened the Arab towns to the Prophet and expanded his means until he could give two hundred camels.
[THALABI:v10p234/r8] author | holds | The hadith means the single worldly hardship cannot overcome both the worldly ease promised to the Prophet and the enduring ease promised to believers in the hereafter.
[THALABI:v10p234/r9] author | holds | He compares this wording to the two feast months not both being diminished: two things are not jointly overcome by one event.
