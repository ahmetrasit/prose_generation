[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r1] author | holds | The opponents claimed to dispute with Muhammad to establish truth, but Rāzī says envy and refusal to follow him were their real motives.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r2] author | holds | The verse resembles 17:18 and 42:20, which distinguish those seeking worldly life from those seeking the harvest of the hereafter. | mentions 17:18, 42:20
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r3] author | reports | One view restricts the verse to disbelievers because its ending about hell and nullified deeds cannot describe believers generally; the general opening is narrowed to those seeking only worldly adornment.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r4] الأصم | reports | Al-Aṣamm says the disbelievers who deny resurrection are intended, since they desire only worldly happiness.
[RAZI-FULL:v17p158#2/r5] author | reports | Another view identifies hypocrites who joined the Prophet's campaigns to seek spoils without belief in afterlife reward.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15#2/r1] author | holds | The verse’s rule that reward follows intention means a Ramadan fast not intended as the Ramadan fast does not count, nor does washing for cooling count as prayer ablution; the principle extends to similar acts.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15#2/r2] author | holds | Most scholars take this verse and similar statements in al-Shura and Ali Imran as unrestricted, then qualified by the verse on the immediate life: God grants what He wills to whomever He wills. | mentions 42:20, 3:145, 17:18
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15#2/r3] الضحاك عن ابن عباس | reports | Al-Dahhak transmits from Ibn Abbas that this verse was abrogated by the verse about whoever desires the immediate life. | mentions 17:18
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15#2/r4] author | prefers | Qurtubi rejects abrogation here and prefers an unrestricted-versus-qualified reading; an apparently universal promise of answering prayer is similarly qualified by God’s will. | mentions 2:186, 6:41
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15#2/r5] author | holds | Abrogating statements of fact is impossible because truths cannot change and God cannot lie; reports about legal rulings may be abrogated, subject to scholarly debate.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15/r1] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra’ treats kāna as extra, explaining the jussive response in the verse.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15/r2] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj reads man kāna as a jussive condition with nuwaf as its answer, meaning whoever wants; the shift from past to future is supported by Zuhayr’s poetry.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15/r3] الضحاك والنحاس | reports | Al-Dahhak says the verse concerns disbelievers, and al-Nahhas prefers this because the next verse denies them any afterlife share beyond the Fire; worldly charity earns health and wealth only.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15/r4] author | reports | Another view applies it to believers who intend only worldly reward: they receive full worldly recompense but punishment in the afterlife because their purpose was confined to this world.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15/r5] أبو هريرة | reports | A report about show-offs says they fasted, prayed, gave charity, fought, and recited to gain praise, and will be among the first fuel of Hell; Abu Hurayra wept, affirmed it, and recited these verses.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15/r6] مجاهد وميمون بن مهران | reports | Mujahid and Maymun ibn Mihran apply the verse generally to anyone who intends something other than God, whether or not the person has basic faith.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15/r7] ميمون بن مهران | reports | Maymun says every good deed is repaid: a sincere Muslim receives reward in this world and the next, while a disbeliever receives it in this world alone.
[QURTUBI-FULL:11:15/r8] author | reports | A narrower view concerns people who joined the Prophet’s campaign for worldly spoils; Qurtubi rejects that limitation and prefers the general reading.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:15/r1] author | holds | People seeking worldly life through benevolence receive its worldly recompense, such as health, leadership, abundant provision, and many children, without any reduction.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:15/r2] author | holds | The verb has readings with a third-person subject, passive voice, lightening, or indicative mood; a poem illustrates the indicative form after a past conditional.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:11:15/r3] author | reports | The verse may concern show-offs, hypocrites, or disbelievers whose stated purpose is worldly recompense and benevolence.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:11:15/r1] author | holds | Anyone whose work, even apparently religious, intends worldly advantage receives its full recompense here and none in the afterlife.
[QASHANI:11:15/r2] author | holds | Each person has a worldly share from constitution and an afterlife share from innate nature; turning wholly to the lower world makes it a veil, reverses fitra, and recruits the heart to serve the ego.
[QASHANI:11:15/r3] author | holds | They are not shortchanged in worldly reward because the heart, molded into the ego's form, receives its share in the ego's image.
