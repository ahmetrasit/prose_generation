## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r10] الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbaʾi explains the rule by the risk that a person may be prevented by illness, incapacity, or a change of intention; saying ‘if God wills’ avoids a false report because the act did not occur only if God did not will it.
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r6] الأخفش | reports | One reading of the exception supplies an implied verb: do not promise tomorrow unless you say ‘if God wills’; this teaches people to qualify future promises and avoid a lie or broken oath if prevented.
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas associates the exception with teaching people to qualify what they report they will do, so an obstacle does not make the report false or an oath broken.
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r8] الفراء | prefers | Al-Farraʾ reads the exception literally as ‘only by God's will’; this fits the surface wording without supplying omitted speech, and can mean that one should promise only acts God wills, namely obedience.
[TABRISI:18:22-24/r9] author | holds | On al-Farraʾ's reading, the exception does not bar reports about permissible acts, since the prohibition is discouragement rather than a ban: omitting it is not sinful by consensus.
