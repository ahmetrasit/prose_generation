## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r1] author | reports | Abraham answered “Submit” by submitting to the Lord of the worlds and remained truthful through trials of wealth, child, homeland, and self. | mentions 2:131
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r10] يحيى | reports | The promise they see is punishment; they seem to have spent only an hour in worldly life before it arrived.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r11] النقاش | reports | Al-Naqqāsh identifies the promise as the afterlife and says their apparent hour is the period spent in their graves before resurrection.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r12] author | reports | Another explanation is that the horror of what they see makes their long worldly stay seem short.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r13] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says balāgh means this Quran is a message or conveyance; its nominative case assumes an omitted subject. | mentions 14:52, 21:106
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r14] ابن عيسى | reports | Ibn ʿĪsā takes balāgh to mean that the brief stay itself is sufficient as a warning; pause placement then may fall after balāgh or nahār.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r15] أبو حاتم | reports | Abū Ḥātim reports a reader pausing after “do not hasten” and starting “for them” as a phrase meaning they have a message.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r16] ابن الأنباري | rejects | Ibn al-Anbārī rejects that pause because it separates the lām from balāgh and attaches it to an unrelated governor.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r17] author | holds | Balāghan may be accusative as a verbal noun or modifier of hour, or genitive as a modifier of day; ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and al-Ḥasan read the accusative.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r18] قراءة بعض القراء | reports | Some recite baligh as an imperative, pausing after “day” and beginning with the command to convey.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r19] ابن عباس وغيره | reports | The wrongdoers who perish are those who depart from God's command.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r2] author | reports | Moses' resolve is illustrated by his declaration that God is the Lord of his people and their forefathers. | mentions 26:26
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r20] ابن محيصن | reports | Ibn Muḥayṣin reads the verb with the people as its grammatical subject, rather than leaving the verb passive.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r21] ابن عباس | reports | He recommends writing the verse and a supplication on a page, washing it, and giving it to a woman struggling in childbirth to drink.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r3] author | reports | David erred, was admonished, and wept in repentance for forty years beneath a tree grown from his tears.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r4] author | reports | Jesus' resolve is shown by refusing to build worldly structures, saying the world is a crossing to pass through rather than inhabit.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r5] author | holds | The Prophet is urged to match Abraham's truthfulness, Moses' trust in divine help, David's concern over past lapses, and Jesus' renunciation of worldly life.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r6] author | reports | One view says this patience command was abrogated by the sword verse; Qurtubi says that is more apparent because the surah is Meccan.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r7] مقاتل | reports | Muqātil says this verse came to the Prophet on the day of Uḥud to comfort and steady him after his injury.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r8] مقاتل | reports | Do not hasten for them means do not pray against them; another view says do not hasten their punishment, whose furthest delay is Resurrection.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#2/r9] author | holds | The object of hastening is omitted and understood to be the punishment.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#3/r1] author | holds | The quotation from al-Nāziʿāt likewise says people will feel they had stayed only an evening or its morning when they see Resurrection. | mentions 79:46
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#3/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatāda says only someone doomed, an unbeliever, perishes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35#3/r3] author | reports | An unnamed view calls this the strongest verse concerning hope.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r1] ابن عباس | reports | He defines the resolute messengers as people of resolve and patience.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r10] الحسن بن الفضل | prefers | He chooses that list because the following verse commands the Prophet to follow their guidance. | mentions 6:90
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r11] ابن عباس | reports | A further view says all messengers are resolute; ʿAlī ibn Mahdī al-Ṭabarī prefers it and takes min as generic, not partitive.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r12] author | reports | An alternative excludes Jonah from all prophets of resolve because he left his people in anger and the Prophet was warned not to imitate his haste; Abū al-Qāsim al-Ḥakīm relates trials involving plunder, a wolf, and the whale.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r13] author | reports | Another account says twelve Israelite prophets in Syria chose to bear God's punishment themselves rather than let it fall on the disobedient Israelites; rulers tortured and killed them in several ways.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r14] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan identifies four resolute messengers: Abraham, Moses, David, and Jesus.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid lists five lawgiving messengers: Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r3] أبو العالية | reports | Abū al-ʿĀliya lists Noah, Hūd, and Abraham, and says God asked the Prophet to be the fourth.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r4] السدي | reports | Al-Suddī names six: Abraham, Moses, David, Solomon, Jesus, and Muhammad.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r5] author | reports | Another list is Noah, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Shuʿayb, Lot, and Moses, the sequence named in al-Aʿrāf and al-Shuʿarāʾ.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r6] مقاتل | reports | Muqātil lists six examples of endurance: Noah endured his people, Abraham the fire, Isaac sacrifice, Jacob loss of son and sight, Joseph the well and prison, and Job affliction.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r7] ابن جريج | reports | Ibn Jurayj includes Ishmael, Jacob, and Job, while excluding Jonah, Solomon, and Adam.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r8] الشعبي والكلبي ومجاهد | reports | They say the resolute are messengers ordered to fight who openly confronted and fought the disbelievers.
[QURTUBI-FULL:46:35/r9] author | reports | One interpretation identifies eighteen notable messengers from al-Anʿām as the people of resolve.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:46:35/r1] author | holds | Those of resolve are steadfast and serious; من may explain the group or indicate some of them, and the Prophet belongs to their company.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:46:35/r2] قيل | reports | One view identifies the resolute as law-bearing prophets who established and defended their laws despite hardship: Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:46:35/r3] قيل | reports | Another view includes those patient under trials: Noah’s abuse, Abraham’s fire and attempted sacrifice, Jacob’s loss and blindness, Joseph’s well and prison, Job’s affliction, David’s forty years of weeping, and Jesus’ simple life; Moses quotes 26:61–62. | mentions 26:61, 26:62
[BAYDAWI-FULL:46:35/r4] author | holds | Do not hurry Quraysh’s punishment; it will certainly arrive at its appointed time.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:46:35/r5] author | holds | When they see the promised punishment, its terror will make their worldly stay seem no longer than an hour.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:46:35/r6] author | holds | بلاغ may mean the admonition or sura is sufficient, the Messenger’s delivery (supported by بلغ), or a promised term they reach; the reading with accusative means they delivered a message.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:46:35/r7] author | holds | The doomed are those who depart from taking heed or obedience; variant readings include passive/active يَهْلِك and first-person plural with القوم accusative.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:46:35/r8] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report promises ten good deeds for each grain of sand in the world to whoever recites Sūrat al-Aḥqāf; no grading is stated.
