## TAB: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH

## MAWARDI-FULL: al-Māwardī, d. 450 AH
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p327#2/r12] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says the scales are a real balance with two pans for weighing good and bad deeds; Abu Bakr says a scale holding only truth deserves to be heavy.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p327#2/r13] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says the scales mean the account, as illustrated by calling the human tongue their measure and by a poem about the measure.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p328/r1] عبد العزيز بن يحيى | reports | Abd al-Aziz ibn Yahya says the scales are proofs and arguments, citing the same poem as evidence.
[MAWARDI-FULL:v6p328/r2] author | holds | Mawardi says موازين may be a plural of ميزان, or a plural meaning weighed things.

## QUSHAYRI: al-Qushayrī, d. 465 AH

[RAZI-FULL:v32p70/r2] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas takes موازين as scales with a tongue and two pans; good deeds appear beautiful and outweigh, while the unbeliever's ugly deeds are light.
