## MUQATIL: Muqātil b. Sulaymān, d. 150 AH
[MUQATIL:22:11-12/r1] author | holds | Worshipping God on an edge means worshipping in doubt; the passage was revealed about Bedouins of Asad ibn Khuzayma and Ghatafan.
[MUQATIL:22:11-12/r2] author | holds | In response to someone asking on which letter one worships, Muqatil says to answer that one worships God alone, who has no partner.
[MUQATIL:22:11-12/r3] author | reports | A migrant whose land yielded, horse bred, son was born, health held, and alms arrived praised Islam as a good religion.
[MUQATIL:22:11-12/r4] author | reports | If drought, illness, a daughter, or lack of alms came, the migrant called Islam bad and returned to his former unbelief, losing both worlds.

## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v16p472#2/r1] author | holds | The verse describes Bedouins who migrated to the Prophet and stayed Muslim when life prospered, but apostatized when worldly conditions worsened; “on an edge” means doubt.
[TAB-FULL:v16p472#2/r2] author | holds | “Good” means worldly ease and its like, which makes a person settle in Islam; “trial” means hardship, which turns him back to disbelief.
[TAB-FULL:v16p472#2/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas describes migrants who trusted Islam after bodily health, a fine foal, and a son, but called it bad after illness, a daughter, or delayed alms, under Satan's prompting.

[TAB-FULL:v16p472#2/r5] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid explains good as ease and well-being, trial as punishment and calamity, and turning back as apostasy into disbelief.
[TAB-FULL:v16p472#2/r6] ابن جريج عن مجاهد | reports | Ibn Jurayj reports Mujahid's account of people from Arab tribes and nearby villages who tested the Prophet's community by whether their livelihood improved.
[TAB-FULL:v16p475#2/r1] author | holds | The doubter loses his worldly aims through worship performed in uncertainty and loses the afterlife through punishment in the blazing Fire; this is manifest ruin.
[TAB-FULL:v16p475#2/r2] author | reports | All regional readers except Ḥumayd al-Aʿraj read “he lost” as a past-tense verb; Ḥumayd reads “a loser” in the accusative as a circumstantial participle.
[TAB-FULL:v16p475/r1] قتادة | reports | Qatada says the doubter grows confident when wealth and livestock increase, but turns back if they are lost.
[TAB-FULL:v16p475/r2] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak describes migrants who judged Islam by livelihood, horse offspring, and whether their wives bore sons or daughters, then returned to their families when fortune disappointed them.
[TAB-FULL:v16p475/r3] ابن زيد | reports | Ibn Zayd calls this person a hypocrite who worships only while worldly affairs go well and returns to disbelief when tested or afflicted.
