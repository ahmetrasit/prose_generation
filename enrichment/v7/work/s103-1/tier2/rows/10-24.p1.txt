## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:10:24/r1] author | holds | The parable depicts the transient life of this world: rain produces plants eaten by people and livestock until the earth is lush and adorned.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:10:24/r2] author | holds | When the landowners expect to harvest and benefit, God's punishment comes by night or day and leaves the land cut down as if it never flourished.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:10:24/r3] author | holds | Worldly wealth likewise accumulates and flourishes, then is taken from its owner through death or a disaster that destroys it.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r1] author | holds | The adorned, boasting worldly life of wealth and children, which ends in extinction, is likened to rain that produces mixed growth.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r10] author | holds | The arriving أمر is a destructive force such as wind, cold, or simoom; specifying night or day warns that danger and loss of security can come at any time.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r11] author | holds | حصيد is an active-form word with passive meaning, describing ruined vegetation whether harvested or destroyed by blight, as though calamity cut it down early.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r2] فرقة من القراء | reports | Some readers stop at فاختلط, taking it to mean that water mixed with earth, then resume with نبات الأرض as a new clause with a fronted predicate; به can refer to water or to the implied mixing.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r3] فرقة | reports | Another group connects نبات as the subject of اختلط: plants mixed with one another because of the water.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r4] author | holds | What people eat means crops, trees, and similar growth; what livestock eat means all grazing herbage.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r5] author | holds | أخذت الأرض is a common idiom, as in 7:31; زخرف means colorful adornment and can also mean gold, from which it is made. | mentions 7:31

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r8] author | holds | وظن أهلها is read in its ordinary sense; عليها refers to the earth and its blessings and plants. The whole worldly-life scene is compared to the described field.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p114/r9] author | holds | حتى marks the endpoint and begins a clause with إذا; the description extends through the people's belief that they control the land, after which the outcome begins.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p115/r1] author | holds | تغن means to flourish or thrive; Qatada reads يغن with yāʾ referring to the ruined crop, and Marwan reads كأن لم تتغن. المغاني means inhabited dwellings, supported by a poetic example.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p115/r2] أبي بن كعب | reports | Ubay's codex adds that the land would not have been destroyed except for its people's sins; this is transmitted by Ibn Abbas, with another version naming God as the subject.

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p115/r4] author | holds | The parable warns against being deluded by worldly life, which is exposed to destruction through death or other calamities; mentioning reflective people honors their rank and invites others to compete for it.
