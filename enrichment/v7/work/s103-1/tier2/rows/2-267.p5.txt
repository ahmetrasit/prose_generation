## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v7p53#2/r1] author | holds | The preceding passage distinguished spending followed by reproach from sincere spending and supplied each type a clarifying parable; this verse specifies what kind of wealth to give.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p53#2/r2] الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan takes the command to spend good things as obligatory alms; he argues that the imperative's apparent force is obligation.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p53#2/r3] قوم | reports | Some authorities take the command as voluntary charity, citing reports of people donating their poorest produce and the revelation that followed.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p53#2/r4] علي بن أبي طالب والحسن ومجاهد | reports | Reports say ʿAlī, al-Ḥasan, and Mujāhid observed people giving inferior fruit and poor property as charity, prompting the verse.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p53#2/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās reports that a man brought a bunch of spoiled dates for charity and the Prophet condemned the act before this verse was revealed.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p53#2/r6] author | prefers | Al-Rāzī says the command can cover both obligatory and voluntary giving because its wording urges action without specifying whether omission is permissible.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p53#2/r7] أبو حنيفة | reports | If the command is obligatory, its general wording supports zakāt on all acquired assets and all crops, even small amounts; opponents restrict crops by reports excluding vegetables and yields below five wasqs.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p54#2/r1] author | holds | Q 3:92 supports reading ṭayyib as valuable goods one likes to own, rather than worthless items one is eager to discard. | mentions 3:92
[RAZI-FULL:v7p54#2/r2] القاضي | reports | Al-Qāḍī argues quality alone cannot be intended, since that would require giving good property even if unlawful; he infers that ṭayyib means lawful.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p54#2/r3] author | prefers | Al-Rāzī proposes ṭayyib can convey both lawful and high quality through the shared idea of being agreeable to reason and religion or to inclination and desire.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p54#2/r4] author | holds | For zakāt, all-good or all-poor holdings yield the corresponding share, while mixed holdings require a middle quality; the Prophet told Muʿādh not to take the best assets. Voluntary giving should be from one's best.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p54#2/r5] author | holds | Al-Rāzī asks what benefit the preposition min adds before the phrase about produce from the earth.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p54/r1] author | reports | One view takes ṭayyib as good-quality property and khabīth as inferior property; another, held by Ibn Masʿūd and Mujāhid, takes them as lawful and unlawful.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p54/r2] author | holds | The revelation context of people giving inferior goods supports the quality reading; moreover, unlawful property cannot normally be accepted even with indulgence, which challenges the lawful/unlawful reading.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p54/r3] القفال | reports | Al-Qaffāl answers that إغماض can mean leniency: the verse describes people willing to accept knowingly unlawful property by overlooking its source.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p55/r1] author | holds | Ammamtuhu, yammamtuhu, and taʾammamtuhu all mean to aim at something; al-Aʿshā's verse illustrates the verb تيمم.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p55/r2] ابن كثير | reports | Ibn Kathīr doubles the tāʾ in تيمموا by assimilating the two original tāʾs; the other readers pronounce it lightly, with analogous variation elsewhere.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p55/r3] أبو علي | reports | Abū ʿAlī rejects this assimilation because a doubled initial consonant would require a wasl hamza, which is not used with the present tense.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p55/r4] سيبويه والفراء | reports | Authorities disagree about which tāʾ is deleted in the common light reading: Sībawayh says the second; al-Farrāʾ says either may be dropped because the remaining one represents it.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p55/r5] author | reports | One syntax ends the clause after ولا تيمموا الخبيث and makes منه تنفقون a rhetorical question; another leaves the clause open and supplies an omitted relative pronoun, as in al-Baqara 2:256. | mentions 2:256
[RAZI-FULL:v7p56/r1] author | holds | إغماض literally means lowering or closing the eyes and is related to something hidden or subtle, such as obscure speech or a low-lying place.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p56/r2] author | reports | One interpretation is accepting inferior goods only by lenience or embarrassment, as when a person closes his eyes to what he dislikes; the verse asks why donors offer God what they would not accept.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p56/r3] author | reports | Another interpretation makes the donor compel the seller to close his eyes, meaning lower the price before the donor would accept the goods.
[RAZI-FULL:v7p56/r4] author | reports | God needs no charity and is praised for explaining; alternatively, غني warns against giving inferior goods and حميد means He praises good deeds, as in Q 21:94. | mentions 21:94

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#2/r1] رسول الله | reports | The cited Prophetic report says children are among one's good earnings and permits eating from their property; no grading is stated.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#2/r2] author | holds | What God brings from the earth includes three categories: plants, minerals, and buried treasure.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#2/r3] عائشة | reports | Aisha reports the Prophetic rule that crops below five wasqs owe no zakat; a wasq is sixty sāʿs, so the threshold is 300 sāʿs of wheat, barley, dates, or raisins; vegetables owe none.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#2/r4] author | reports | Some invoke the verse's general wording to support Abu Hanifa's view that zakat applies to everything the earth produces, in any quantity and category; details are deferred to al-An'am.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#2/r5] رسول الله | reports | Abu Hurayra reports that injury caused by an animal, a well, or a mine is uncompensated, while buried treasure owes one fifth.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#2/r6] author | holds | The scholars infer that mines and buried treasure have different rules because the hadith separates them with a conjunction; otherwise it would have stated that the mine owes one fifth.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#2/r7] author | holds | Rikaz linguistically and in the majority's legal usage is gold, silver, or gems embedded in the ground; an unworked nugget found inside a mine owes one fifth, while Malik's first and relied-upon view treats it like mined material requiring labor.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#2/r8] أبو هريرة | reports | A report asks the Prophet to define rikaz, and he calls it gold God placed in the earth when He created the heavens and earth; its transmitter Abdullah ibn Said is abandoned, and another route is unsound.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#3/r1] author | holds | The transmitter Abdullah ibn Said is abandoned, and another route for the Abu Hurayra report is unsound, as al-Daraqutni notes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#3/r2] author | holds | Scholars agree that pre-Islamic buried wealth is rikaz; money buried after Islam is treated as lost property.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#3/r3] مالك | reports | Malik assigns pre-Islamic treasure in certain Arab or peaceful Muslim lands to its finder with a fifth due; treasure in Islamic territory is lost property, conquest land belongs to its conquerors, and treaty land belongs to its residents, with another view giving it to all treaty residents.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#3/r4] إسماعيل | reports | Ismail says treasure follows the spoils rule because a Muslim finds pagan property as if he had fought and seized it, receiving four fifths.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#3/r5] ابن القاسم | reports | Ibn al-Qasim recounts Malik changing his position on found objects such as jewelry, iron, and lead: one fifth, then none, and finally one fifth again.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#4/r1] author | prefers | The author says Malik’s final view that one fifth is due on found valuables is correct, supported by the general hadith and followed by most jurists.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#4/r2] أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa and Muhammad assign treasure found in a house to its owner, with a fifth due; Abu Yusuf and al-Thawri assign it to the finder. In open land all give it to the finder with a fifth, without distinguishing lands.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#4/r3] author | reports | Some Medinese Malikites, al-Layth, Ibn Nafi, al-Shafi'i, and most scholars make no land-based distinction; the finder may keep the fifth if needy or give it to the poor.

[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#4/r5] سحنون | reports | Sahnun says a person's separate mines are not combined; each mine is assessed only when it independently reaches 200 dirhams or twenty dinars.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:267#4/r6] محمد بن مسلمة | reports | Ibn Maslama says separate mines are combined and assessed together, like crops.
