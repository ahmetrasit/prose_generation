[RAZI-FULL:v30p113#3/r3] القاضي | reports | The Qāḍī argues that 'the human was created anxious' does not mean God made him inevitably blameworthy: God does not blame His own act, and believers can struggle against this trait.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r1] author | prefers | ‘Jazu‘an’ and ‘manu‘an’ can be overlapping circumstantial states of the pronoun in ‘halu‘an’; the two time phrases modify those states.
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r2] author | holds | Abu al-Baqa’s wording calls ‘jazu‘an’ another circumstantial but can mislead readers into treating it as separate; his stated governor shows the intended overlapping relation.
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r3] مكي | reports | Makki analyzes both adjectives as predicates of an omitted ‘kana’ or ‘sara’: when harm touches him he becomes distressed, and when good touches him he becomes withholding.
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r4] مكي | reports | Makki also allows the two words to modify ‘halu‘an’, but finds this distant because the time phrase must be understood before its adjective; the author rejects that objection as misplaced.
[SAMIN-DURR:70:20-21/r5] author | rejects | The objection to the adjectival analysis is mistaken: its issue is an adverbial phrase preceding its governor, whereas the real prohibited order would put an adjective’s governed element before the adjective.
