## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v30p17#2/r10] author | reports | The appeal for more time comes when death's signs appear; the person asks to be returned to life long enough to give charity and purify himself.

[RAZI-FULL:v30p17#2/r13] صاحب الكشاف | reports | Al-Kashshāf places the appeal before the dying person sees the signs that end hope of delay, constrict the throat, and make spending impossible.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p17#2/r14] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās paraphrases the command as giving charity before death arrives, when repentance is no longer accepted and deeds no longer benefit.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p17#2/r15] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās explains the promised righteous conduct as performing pilgrimage.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p17#2/r16] author | reports | The readings and parsing differ: fa-akūna follows fa-aṣṣaddaq; al-Mubarrad makes akuna coordinate with the subjunctive answer to the wished-for question, while Ubayy reads fa-ataṣaddaq in the original form and coordinates akuna by position.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p17#2/r9] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās takes the commanded spending as zakat; another view says it is obligatory expenditure generally.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p18/r1] سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh cites poetry for coordinating a word by its syntactic position; the line addressed to Muʿāwiya uses the accusative al-ḥadīda after a preposition whose meaning is only confirmatory.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p18/r2] ابن أبي سلمى | reports | Ibn Abī Salmā's poem illustrates coordination by understood meaning: sābiqan follows the meaning of mudrikin, as if the phrase were bi-mudrikin.
[RAZI-FULL:v30p18/r4] author | holds | The warning about wealth and children calls for remembrance before death; the order to spend calls for gratitude for what God provided.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11#2/r1] ابن عباس | reports | He says this verse is especially severe for monotheists, since no one with good in the afterlife wishes to return or have life extended; Qurtubi excepts a martyr who longs to return after seeing the honor.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r1] author | holds | The command to spend before death shows that zakat must be paid promptly and may not be delayed; the same applies to other duties once their time is fixed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r10] author | holds | The other readers put wa-akun in the jussive, coordinating with the underlying grammatical position because aṣṣaddaqa would be jussive without the fāʾ.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r2] author | holds | The dying person asks to return to worldly life to do righteous deeds.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's view that someone able to afford Hajj or owing zakat who fails to perform it will request return at death.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r4] ابن عباس | reports | When challenged that only disbelievers request return, Ibn ʿAbbās cites this passage; he says zakat becomes due at 200 and Hajj requires provisions and a mount. | mentions 63:9, 63:10, 63:11
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r5] الحليمي | reports | Al-Ḥalīmī presents the saying about a person with means for Hajj asking to return as a Prophetic report, which Qurtubi says was previously given in Āl ʿImrān.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r6] ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabī says Ibn ʿAbbās applies the verse to obligatory, not voluntary, spending; the zakat interpretation and amount are sound, but Hajj application depends on whether it is immediately due or may be delayed, and the means test is disputed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r7] ابن العربي | holds | Ibn al-ʿArabī concludes that return and threatened punishment concern agreed obligations, so the verse covers obligatory spending through consensus or Quranic text, not voluntary giving.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r8] author | reports | Lawlā means “why not?” as a question, or lā is extra and the phrase expresses a wish; fa-aṣṣaddaqa is subjunctive after the optative particle.
[QURTUBI-FULL:63:10-11/r9] أبو عمرو وابن محيصن ومجاهد | reports | They read wa-akun in the accusative, coordinated with fa-aṣṣaddaqa.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH

[SAMIN-DURR:63:10/r2] الباقون | reports | The other readers use jussive وأكن, deleting wāw where two consonants meet.
[SAMIN-DURR:63:10/r3] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari coordinates the jussive with the syntactic position of فأصدق, as if the delayed wish yielded two promises.

[SAMIN-DURR:63:10/r5] سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh's well-known analysis makes وأكن jussive by presuming the condition implied by the wish; Ibn Atiyya says positional coordination requires an overt condition, as at 7:186. | mentions 7:186

[SAMIN-DURR:63:10/r7] الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh distinguishes position-based coordination, where a governing factor exists without its case effect, from assumed coordination, where the factor is absent but its effect remains.
[SAMIN-DURR:63:10/r8] author | holds | The author illustrates the distinction with ضارب زيد وعمرا and says the present verse has no jussive governor but retains its effect; Zuhayr's verse is clearer because the absent bāʾ leaves a genitive trace.
