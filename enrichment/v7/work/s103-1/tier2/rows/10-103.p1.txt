## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v4p48#2/r1] author | holds | The verse connects to an implied preceding clause: God destroys past peoples, then saves messengers and believers as if narrating those past events in the present.
[BURSEVI:v4p48#2/r2] author | holds | ‘Just so’ describes deliverance; ‘truly upon Us’ interrupts the verb and object, with an understood verb meaning the promise is truly established.
[BURSEVI:v4p48#2/r3] author | holds | God's established practice is to save believers from every hardship and punishment, as He saved earlier messengers and followers and fulfilled His promise; this continues while the law and its practice remain.

[BURSEVI:v4p48#2/r6] author | holds | The best worship is waiting for relief because it brings ease of heart and patience's reward; trusting that God alone removes affliction reduces pain and panic.

[ALUSI-FULL:v11p196/r2] author | holds | The rescue clause follows an implied statement that earlier nations were destroyed; the intervening text hastens the threat, and this passage narrates destruction before rescue.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p196/r3] author | holds | The present-tense rescue vividly recounts a past event; believers are saved as in prior instances, and the concluding sentence reaffirms this outcome.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p196/r4] author | holds | That rescue is a sure divine promise, termed obligatory only figuratively, since nothing truly imposes an obligation on God.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p196/r5] author | reports | Believers may mean all believers, including messengers and followers, or only followers; messengers are omitted in the latter reading because they were already named.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p196/r6] author | holds | The concluding clause teaches that faith is the basis of rescue and seals the preceding statement by reaffirming its meaning.
