## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p25#2/r1] author | holds | If 'those' refers only to dying unbelievers, their punishment is eternal; if it includes sinners who delay repentance until death, their punishment is not eternal.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p25#2/r2] author | holds | Aʿtadnā means prepared and made present; the verse's wording indicates that Hell already exists.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p25/r10] ابن عباس، ابن زيد وجماعة المفسرين | reports | They say the excluded person is one whose death has arrived and who has entered despair, like Pharaoh during drowning.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p25/r11] الربيع | reports | Al-Rabi says the first verse addresses believers and the second originally addressed Muslims, then was abrogated by Qur'an 4:48 and 4:116, which denies forgiveness for associating partners and leaves other believers to God's will. | mentions 4:48, 4:116
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p25/r12] author | rejects | The abrogation claim is weak: although phrased as a report, the verse establishes a legal ruling, and no abrogation is needed because it excludes only repentance at death, not forgiveness for an unrepentant sinner under Qur'an 4:48 and 4:116. | mentions 4:48, 4:116
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p25/r13] أبو المعالي وآخرون | reports | A repentant person who turns back before death has the status of the repentant; al-Maali says the scriptural promise makes salvation likely, while others say forgiveness is certain.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v2p25/r14] author | holds | One who waits until death is not treated as repentant: an unbeliever remains eternally in punishment, while a sinful believer is under God's will, with fear of punishment prevailing though some are certainly forgiven by grace.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:4:17-18#3/r2] author | holds | Accepted repentance does not extend to persistent sinners who delay until death's signs and hope of life have ended; at that point they say they repent now.
[TABRISI:4:17-18#3/r3] author | holds | Exegetes agree the verse concerns sinful Muslims, apart from a report from al-Rabīʿ that it concerns hypocrites; the author rejects that because hypocrites are unbelievers, separately named in the next clause.
[TABRISI:4:17-18#3/r4] author | holds | The unbelievers named afterward are those who die in unbelief and regret only after death; God has prepared for them painful punishment.
[TABRISI:4:17-18#3/r5] author | holds | Repentance at death is invalid because the dying person is compelled to choose good and abandon evil, outside moral responsibility and thus outside the conditions of repentance; the afterlife is similarly beyond obligation.
[TABRISI:4:17-18#3/r6] author | reports | The wording “prepared a painful punishment” does not prove that sinful believers who die unrepentant must be punished: it can mean they merit Hell, may refer to the nearer unbelievers, or describe what justice would impose absent divine pardon.
[TABRISI:4:17-18#3/r7] author | holds | The promise to forgive lesser sins whoever God wills applies only to monotheist believers who die with an unrepented major sin; the righteous, repentant, and unbelievers are not in that class. | mentions 4:48, 4:116
[TABRISI:4:17-18#4/r1] الربيع | reports | Al-Rabīʿ claims this verse was abrogated by the promise that God forgives lesser sins for whom He wills in 4:48 and 4:116. | mentions 4:48, 4:116
[TABRISI:4:17-18#4/r2] author | rejects | The author rejects abrogation because this verse states a report about what God has prepared, and reports cannot be abrogated as legal commands can.
[TABRISI:4:17-18/r2] author | reports | Aʿtadnā may derive from aʿdadnā with tāʾ substituting for dāl, or may be a verb formed from ʿatād, equipment; the latter is illustrated by ʿAdī b. al-Riqāʿ's poetry.
[TABRISI:4:17-18/r3] author | holds | The phrase about those who die is grammatically genitive by coordination with the earlier phrase, with an implied repetition of “nor for.”

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v10p6#2/r1] author | holds | Repentance is not accepted once death's signs and terrors arrive, as shown also by the rejected belief of Pharaoh at drowning and by the deathbed pleas in other passages. | mentions 40:85, 10:90, 10:91, 23:99, 23:100, 63:10, 63:11
[RAZI-FULL:v10p6#2/r2] أبو أيوب | reports | Abu Ayyub transmits from the Prophet that God accepts a servant's repentance until the soul reaches the throat and rattles there.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p6#2/r3] عطاء | reports | Ata says repentance is accepted even until a moment before death, as short as the time it takes a camel to be milked.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p6#2/r4] الحسن | reports | Hasan relates that Satan vowed to mislead Adam's children while their souls remained in their bodies, and God vowed to keep repentance open until the soul rattles in the throat.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p6#2/r5] author | holds | When death comes means when its signs and approach are present, as in the command about an inheritance when death comes in 2:180. | mentions 2:180
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7#2/r1] author | rejects | Razi challenges the Mu'tazilite claim that resurrection makes knowledge of God necessary: observing revival and fire does not prove God caused them, since those conclusions remain inferential.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7#2/r2] author | rejects | Even if resurrection knowledge were necessary, the Mu'tazilites have not shown why that ends duty: a servant might still sin while believing God is merciful and unaffected by human obedience.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7#2/r3] author | rejects | Their distinction between theoretical and necessary knowledge is weak: if the possibility of the opposite remains, there is only opinion; if it does not, theoretical knowledge cannot be weaker than necessary knowledge.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7#2/r4] author | holds | God acts as He wills: by grace He promised acceptance at some times and by justice announced rejection at another, and He can reverse either judgment, as 21:23 says. | mentions 21:23
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7#2/r5] author | holds | A three-part division follows: repentance from ignorant sin is promised acceptance in 4:17; repentance only at death is rejected; deliberate sinners who repent earlier are left to God's will, as in 4:48. | mentions 4:17, 4:48
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7/r1] المحققون | reports | The investigators say nearness to death and its pain do not itself bar repentance; the true barrier is witnessing events that make knowledge of God unavoidable and remove moral responsibility.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7/r2] author | holds | People revived after death, including Israelites and Job's children, were again charged with duties, so experiencing death does not itself end accountability.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7/r3] author | holds | Death's pains resemble colic or labor, which do not end responsibility; rising distress should make repentance more likely to be accepted, since God responds to the distressed in 27:62. | mentions 27:62
[RAZI-FULL:v10p7/r4] author | holds | At death, witnessed terrors make knowledge of God necessary and end obligation; people of the afterlife likewise cannot repent even before entering Hell because their knowledge is necessary.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p8/r1] author | reports | The phrase about those who die as disbelievers can mean people near death, whose faith too is rejected, or unbelievers who die in disbelief and cannot repent in the afterlife.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p8/r2] الوعيدية | reports | Threat proponents argue that the conjunction separates sinners who repent at death from disbelievers, yet the same painful threat covers both; they also say forgiveness without repentance would make the warning pointless.
[RAZI-FULL:v10p8/r3] author | rejects | Razi answers that the nearest referent of the prepared painful punishment can be disbelievers alone; because their unbelief is worse, they may receive added humiliation beyond sinful believers. | mentions 2:81
[RAZI-FULL:v10p9/r1] author | rejects | The absence of repentance at death still leaves punishment or forgiveness possible and thus warns the sinner, like 4:48; moreover, the Mu'tazilites themselves reject arguments from what a text merely implies. | mentions 4:48
[RAZI-FULL:v10p9/r2] author | holds | Because disbelievers are conjoined separately from those who sin, the verse refutes the Kharijite claim that a Muslim sinner is an unbeliever; the hypocrite reading also fails because hypocrites are unbelievers, as 63:1 says. | mentions 63:1
[RAZI-FULL:v10p9/r3] أصحابنا | reports | Aʿtadna means prepared or made ready; the past tense in 4:18 and the parallel descriptions of Hell in 2:24 and 3:131 are used to argue that Hell is already created. | mentions 2:24, 3:131
