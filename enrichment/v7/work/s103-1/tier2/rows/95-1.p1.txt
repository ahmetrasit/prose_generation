## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p778/r1] author | holds | Figs and olives are wondrous among fruit-bearing trees; reports mention their virtues and use as food and tooth-stick.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p778/r10] author | holds | The oath honors blessed places through the good associated with prophets and righteous people: Abraham migrated to the fig-and-olive region, Jesus was born and raised there, Moses was called at the Mount, and Muhammad was born and sent in Mecca.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p778/r2] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report says the Prophet ate a plate of figs, called them a fruit of Paradise if any could be so called, and described them as seedless and beneficial for hemorrhoids and gout.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p778/r3] معاذ بن جبل | reports | Muadh passed an olive tree, took a branch for brushing his teeth, and said he heard the Prophet praise olive wood as a blessed tooth-stick that freshens the mouth and helps the teeth.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p778/r4] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet is also reported to say that olive wood was his tooth-stick and that of earlier prophets.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p778/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas interprets the words as referring to your Prophet and your olives.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p778/r6] author | reports | Alternatively, the words name two mountains in the Holy Land, called in Syriac Tur Tina and Tur Zayta because figs and olives grow there.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p778/r7] author | reports | Another view places the fig mountains between Hulwan and Hamadan, and the olive mountains in Syria, identifying their growing regions.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p780/r2] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report promises health and certainty in this life to whoever recites Surat al-Tin, and after death a reward equal to the number of its reciters.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:95:1-3/r2] ابن عباس | reports | The oath refers literally to the edible fig and the olive from which oil is pressed; al-Hasan, Mujahid, ʿIkrima, and Qatada agree.
[TABRISI:95:1-3/r3] author | prefers | Al-Tabrisi prefers the literal fruit reading as the apparent meaning; the fig's size, smoothness, and design show divine favor.
[TABRISI:95:1-3/r4] أبو ذر | reports | A report from Abu Dharr quotes the Prophet praising the fig as a fruit from Paradise, without pits, and as beneficial for hemorrhoids and breath.
[TABRISI:95:1-3/r5] author | holds | Olive oil is used as a condiment in many foods, and the fig is a nourishing food with many benefits.
[TABRISI:95:1-3/r6] قتادة | reports | One geographical interpretation takes the fig and olive as mountains associated with Damascus and Jerusalem.
[TABRISI:95:1-3/r7] كعب الأحبار | reports | The fig may mean the mosque of Damascus and the olive the Temple in Jerusalem; ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Ghanm and Ibn Zayd share this view.
[TABRISI:95:1-3/r8] ابن عباس | reports | Another interpretation identifies the fig with Noah's mosque on Mount Judi and the olive with Jerusalem.
[TABRISI:95:1-3/r9] الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak identifies the fig with the Sacred Mosque in Mecca and the olive with al-Aqsa.
