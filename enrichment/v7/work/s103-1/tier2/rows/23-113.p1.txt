## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:23:112-114/r3] author | reports | They may forget their time in the graves from the terror of punishment, or because torment was suspended between the two trumpet blasts; a comparison says those killed by or dying in a prophet’s presence are punished until the first blast, then held as still as water until the second.
[QURTUBI-FULL:23:112-114/r4] author | reports | Another explanation is that they deem both worldly life and the grave interval brief beside what now faces them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:23:112-114/r5] قتادة | reports | Qatadah says al-ʿāddīn are the people who count and know the duration; Mujahid says they are angels who were with people in the world.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:23:113/r1] author | holds | They call their stay a day or part of one because it seems short beside eternal punishment, because happy days feel short, or because elapsed time is as nothing.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:23:113/r2] author | holds | They tell the questioner to ask those able to count or angels who count lifespans and deeds; another light reading gives the sense of wrongdoers, and another means the ancients.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:23:113/r3] author | reports | The phrase is also read al-ʿādīn for wrongdoers or al-ʿādiyīn for people of earlier generations.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH

[NASAFI-FULL:v3p132#5/r2] author | reports | The counters may be arithmetic or angels who counted people's lifespans and deeds; the unhamzated “fasal” reading is Meccan and ʿAlī.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:23:113-114/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the people forget the interval of torment between the two trumpet blasts, during their concealment in the barzakh.
[QASHANI:23:113-114/r2] author | holds | They call the stay brief because it has ended; God affirms it was brief, and knowing this would have led them to prepare and detach from its pleasures.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p588/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid identifies the counters as angels who record people's deeds and count their hours; Qatāda says they are accountants, while the author allows anyone who can count.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p588/r4] author | holds | The apparent sense includes angels or others who can count, since sleepers and the dead cannot track time. Al-Zamakhsharī says torment makes the interval feel slight and leaves them unable to calculate or explain it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p588/r5] الحسن | reports | Ḥasan and al-Kisāʾī transmit a light-dāl reading meaning wrongdoers; another form with long yāʾ means ancient people or long-lived elders, who would consider the period short too.

[SAMIN-DURR:23:113/r3] أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa takes the light-dāl reading as predecessors, like an old well; the author clarifies that the second, moving yāʾ is deleted first.

[SAMIN-DURR:23:113/r5] ابن خالويه | reports | Ibn Khalawayh notes a further form العاديين with a doubled yāʾ, the plural of عادية meaning the ancients.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p500/r1] author | holds | The question exposes how little of their short worldly lives they spent worshipping God; patience in that brief life would have brought the pious believers' success.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p500/r2] author | holds | They say they stayed a day or part of a day and are told to ask those who count the time.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p500/r4] أيفع بن عبد الكلاعي | reports | A report attributed to the Prophet says that, after the two groups enter their abodes, the people of Paradise call one brief day a good bargain for mercy and bliss, while the people of Hell made a bad bargain for fire and wrath.
