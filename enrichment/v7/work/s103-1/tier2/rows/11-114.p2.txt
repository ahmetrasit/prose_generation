## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:11:114-116/r1] author | holds | “The two ends of day” are the morning and sunset prayers, while the early portions of night mean the ʿishāʾ prayer near nightfall.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:11:114-116/r2] author | reports | Another interpretation counts the ends of day as dawn, noon, and afternoon prayers, with sunset and night prayers among the early night prayers.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:11:114-116/r3] author | holds | The five prayers expiate sins committed between them when major sins are avoided.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:11:114-116/r4] author | holds | This is an admonition for people who remember and take heed.

## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v5p3576/r6] أبو مسلم | reports | Zulfā means closeness in rank; its plural appears in the name Muzdalifa and in the phrase that Paradise is brought near, while Abū Muslim also glosses it as successive time periods.
[JISHUMI:v5p3576/r9] author | reports | One occasion report concerns an Anṣārī date seller who lured a woman into his home and embraced her; she reminded him to fear God, he stopped and regretted it, and after he confessed, the Prophet said his prayer expiated it. ʿUmar asked whether this applied to him alone or everyone, and the Prophet answered everyone.
[JISHUMI:v5p3579/r1] أبو علي | prefers | Abū ʿAlī's second interpretation is that repentance expiates major sins; the author says God intended both interpretations under discussion.
[JISHUMI:v5p3579/r12] author | holds | Establishing prayer entails prayer in the specified times and establishes the times in general; the Prophet explained their details.
[JISHUMI:v5p3579/r13] author | prefers | The soundest account is Abū ʿAlī's: obedience removes sin through repentance; the claim cannot apply to every good deed, since unbelievers perform many good deeds without their sins being erased.
[JISHUMI:v5p3579/r2] author | reports | Another view says good deeds mean all good actions by the Prophet and believers, while sins mean all evil deeds by the polytheists; the verse promises victory for the Prophet and dominance of his religion.
[JISHUMI:v5p3579/r3] أبو مسلم | reports | Abū Muslim links the promise of good overcoming evil to the statement that truth is hurled against falsehood and crushes it. | mentions 21:18
[JISHUMI:v5p3579/r4] أبو مسلم | reports | Abū Muslim says the reminder makes believers firm in their religion.
[JISHUMI:v5p3579/r5] الأصم | reports | Al-Aṣamm says prayer reminds people of their sins, leading them to repent and avoid evil, as in the statement that prayer restrains indecency and wrongdoing.
[JISHUMI:v5p3579/r6] author | reports | Other explanations take ذلك as the Qur'an or as the promise itself being a reminder.

## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p410/r1] author | holds | The two ends of the day are morning and evening: dawn is the morning prayer, noon and afternoon are the evening prayers, and the night intervals are sunset and night prayers.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p410/r2] author | holds | The accusative of the two ends is adverbial because the words are annexed to times; the annexed term takes the syntactic status of its complement.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p410/r3] author | reports | He records forms with damma, quiescent lām, and the form zu lfā; the last means a near interval of night, as does qurbā.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p410/r4] author | reports | On another parsing, the night intervals are prayers by which one draws near to God, so the phrase coordinates with prayer.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p410/r5] author | holds | One explanation is that acts of obedience expiate minor sins.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p410/r6] author | reports | A prophetic report says one prayer expiates what lies between it and the next, provided major sins are avoided.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p410/r7] author | reports | A second explanation is that good deeds act as a moral prompting that helps people leave sins; he links this to reciting scripture. | mentions 29:45
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p410/r8] author | reports | The verse is reported to have followed the repentance of Abu al-Yusr ʿAmr ibn Ghaziyya after he embraced and kissed a woman, then confessed to the Prophet.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p411/r1] author | reports | A report says Abu Bakr and ʿUmar each advised the penitent to conceal the matter and repent; when ʿUmar asked whether the verse applied only to him, the Prophet said it was for everyone.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p411/r2] author | reports | Another prophetic report instructs him to perform ablution well and pray two rakʿas, then cites the verse about good deeds removing sins.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p411/r3] author | holds | The demonstrative refers to the command to remain upright and what follows it; this is an admonition for those who take admonition.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p212#2/r1] جمهور المتأولين | reports | Most early interpreters take the good deeds that erase sins as the five prayers; Uthman invoked this verse while performing ablution, and this is Malik's interpretation.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p212#2/r2] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says the good deeds are a person's repeated glorification, praise, profession of divine unity, and magnification of God.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p212#2/r3] author | prefers | These are examples of good deeds; the verse's wording is general for good deeds but specific to lesser sins in light of the Prophetic phrase 'if major sins are avoided.'
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p212#2/r4] author | reports | A report says the verse came after an Ansari man—identified as Abu al-Yusr ibn Amr or as Abbad—met a woman alone and kissed and fondled her without intercourse; after he told Umar and Abu Bakr, he confessed to the Prophet, who recited the verse. Muadh asked if it was only for him; the Prophet said it was for everyone. Another report says the verse predated the incident and was applied to him.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p212#2/r5] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet said Friday to Friday, the five prayers, and Ramadan to Ramadan expiate what lies between them if major sins are avoided.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p212#2/r6] جمهور أهل السنة | reports | Most Sunni scholars make avoiding major sins a condition of the entire promise: without it, the named acts do not expiate lesser sins.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p212#2/r7] فرقة | reports | Another group says major sins are the sins the acts do not erase; the condition preserves the hadith's broad wording while the acts still erase lesser sins.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p213/r1] author | prefers | Ibn Atiyya adopts the second interpretation: the hadith about sins falling away with drops of water supports it, provided minor sins are repented of and not persisted in; he calls this the precise view of expert legal theorists. On the first interpretation, the verse applies only to those who avoid major sins.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p213/r2] author | reports | ذلك may refer to the prayers as a cause and setting for remembrance, to the claim that good deeds erase sins as encouragement toward good deeds, or to all the preceding commands and prohibitions, as al-Tabari explains.
