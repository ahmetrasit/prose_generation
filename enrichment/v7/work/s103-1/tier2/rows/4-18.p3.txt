## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r1] author | holds | Repentance is not accepted from people persisting in sins when death arrives and they see the irreversibility of return and the Angel of Death; saying 'I repent now' is not treated as genuine repentance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r10] بعضهم | reports | Some distinguish repentance from faith: repentance renews an existing covenant with God and may be accepted at despair, while faith initiates a new covenant; the Bazzaziyya calls acceptance of repentance, unlike faith, the sound position.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r11] القاضي عبدالصمد الحنفي | reports | Al-Qadi ʿAbd al-Samad al-Hanafi says Sufis hold that faith still benefits at the sight of punishment; Ibn al-ʿArabi likewise validates faith under compulsion, and Ibn ʿUmar hoped well for a polytheist who gurgled Islam.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r2] author | holds | The plural 'sins' reflects repeated occurrence over an extended time rather than all categories of sin; the previously mentioned evil is one type of sin.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r3] author | holds | The verse says 'he said' rather than 'he repented' to avoid counting this last-moment utterance as repentance; the conditional clause after حتى marks the limit of the preceding denial.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r4] author | holds | The dying state resembles the afterlife, which begins with death: this world is a place of action without requital, while the next is requital without action.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r5] author | holds | The second group is also denied acceptance; mentioning them alongside last-moment repenters warns that delayed repentance is nearly worthless, and repeating the negative may suggest its futility is even more emphatic.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r6] أكثر أهل العلم | reports | Most scholars understand the first group to include sinful believers and disbelievers, and the second group to mean disbelievers only.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r7] author | reports | Other interpretations take both groups as disbelievers, both as sinners with 'disbelievers' used for severity, or both as including the two groups with the label based on predominance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r8] الربيع | reports | Al-Rabiʿ and Abu al-Aliya report that the preceding repentance verse concerned believers, this verse's first group hypocrites, and its final group polytheists; the author calls this weak because persistent hypocrites cannot repent, while repentant ones are no different from others. | mentions 4:17
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p239#2/r9] author | holds | The verse is evidence that repentance at the point of despair is not accepted, but scholars dispute whether such repentance remains valid while hope, regret, and resolve to stop are possible.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p240/r1] أبو ذر | reports | A report from Abu Dharr says God accepts repentance or forgives a servant until the veil falls; the Prophet explains that the veil falls when the soul departs while the person is still an idolater, supporting repentance before that point.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p240/r2] author | holds | The verse appears to support rejection of last-moment repentance; some answer that it denies guaranteed acceptance rather than guaranteeing rejection, leaving the matter under God's will, while 4:48 excludes the persistent disbeliever from forgiveness. | mentions 4:48
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p240/r3] بعضهم | reports | Some say the dying sinner only says 'I repent now' without true repentance; even if قال has another rhetorical purpose, the word 'now' may show that resolve not to return, a condition of valid repentance, is absent.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p240/r4] author | holds | The referent of 'those' in the punishment clause is parsed as the two groups mentioned, and the preparation of painful punishment is rendered with emphasis on its being readied for them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p240/r5] قيل | reports | A lexical explanation says أعتدنا means أعددنا, with the dāl changed to tāʾ; the adjective painful intensifies the punishment, and the indefinite noun magnifies it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p240/r6] المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazilites use the verse to argue that believers who die with major sins unrepented must be punished; the response is that preparing Hell does not state that God will certainly admit them, and may mean punishment if He does not pardon them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p240/r7] الربيع | reports | Al-Rabiʿ says the verse was abrogated by 4:48, which says God forgives what is below associating partners to whom He wills. | mentions 4:48
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p241/r1] author | rejects | The abrogation claim is rejected because أعتدنا is a report, and reports are not abrogated.
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p241/r2] بعضهم | reports | An alternative reading makes 'those' refer only to those who die as disbelievers, or to both groups understood as hypocrites first and polytheists second.

## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:4:17-18/r2] author | holds | Repentance is not promised for those who repeatedly commit wrong and wait until death arrives before saying they repent, nor for those who die as disbelievers.
[ELMALILI:4:17-18/r3] author | holds | For deliberate sinners who do not repent soon and make sin habitual, but repent before the death throes and despair of life, acceptance is possible by God's will but is not certain.
[ELMALILI:4:17-18/r4] author | holds | Faith adopted before the death throes, while a person still hopes to live, is accepted; faith adopted in despair during the death throes is not.
[ELMALILI:4:17-18/r5] author | holds | A new believer needs time after faith to acquire good deeds.
[ELMALILI:4:17-18/r6] author | holds | A sinful believer's repentance at the last breath may be accepted, as in the call not to despair of God's mercy (39:53), but acceptance is still not guaranteed. | mentions 39:53
