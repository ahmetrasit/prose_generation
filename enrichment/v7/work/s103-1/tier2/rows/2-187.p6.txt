## WAHIDI-WAJIZ: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:186-187/r10] author | holds | The stated limits are God's prohibitions, which people must not commit; this clarification aims to help them avoid forbidden acts.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:186-187/r4] author | holds | At first, intercourse during fasting nights and eating or drinking after the last evening prayer were forbidden; God later permitted all of them until dawn.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:186-187/r5] author | holds | Rafath means intercourse; spouses are garments for one another, meaning bedding or covering during intercourse.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:186-187/r6] author | reports | Umar ibn al-Khattab and others had intercourse during Ramadan nights, then asked the Messenger, and the permission was revealed.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:186-187/r7] author | holds | God's pardon forgave conduct before the permission, while what God prescribed to seek means the child destined for the couple.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:186-187/r8] author | holds | Eating and drinking remain allowed throughout the night until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct from night's blackness; the phrase specifies that the thread is dawn.
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:2:186-187/r9] author | holds | Fasting then means abstaining from these acts until night, and intercourse is forbidden during mosque retreat because it invalidates it.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p206/r1] author | holds | Rafath is a euphemism for sexual intercourse; Ibn Abbas says the Qur'anic terms for intimate contact likewise refer to intercourse.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p206/r2] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says rafath broadly covers everything men desire from women.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p206/r3] author | reports | At first, after breaking the fast one could eat, drink, and have intercourse only until the night prayer or sleep; then these acts were forbidden until the next night.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p206/r4] author | reports | After Umar had intercourse after the night prayer, he regretted it and asked the Prophet for relief; the Prophet replied that Umar was not worthy of that conduct, and others confessed similar acts before the verse was revealed.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207#2/r1] author | reports | The revelation followed an exhausting day of work by an unnamed account's Abu Sirma ibn Qays, or Abu Qays ibn Sirma according to Ikrima, or Abu Qays Sirma ibn Anas according to al-Kalbi; he fell asleep before eating and became dangerously weak.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207#2/r2] author | holds | The white and black threads mean daylight and nighttime darkness; they were likened to threads because each first appears as an extended line.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207#2/r3] سهل بن سعد | reports | Sahl says the phrase was first revealed without 'of dawn'; some tied white and black threads to their legs, so the clarification 'of dawn' specified natural light and dark.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207#2/r4] عدي بن حاتم | reports | Adi put a black and white cord beneath his pillow and watched them unsuccessfully; the Prophet explained that the words mean night's blackness and day's whiteness.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r1] author | holds | Spouses are garments for one another, meaning sources of comfort; the author supports this with the verse that God made a mate so one could find repose in her. | mentions 7:189
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r2] author | reports | Other explanations compare spouses to a shared garment during sleep, or say each veils the other from what is forbidden; Rabi ibn Anas instead calls them bedding and coverlet.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r3] أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu Ubayda and others say a woman may be called one's garment, bedding, or waist-wrapper; a hadith says marriage protects two thirds of one's religion.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r4] البراء | reports | Al-Bara says that when Ramadan fasting was first prescribed, men abstained from wives throughout the month, and some wronged themselves by breaking that restriction.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r5] author | holds | The former restriction was a betrayal and injustice to oneself; God relented, erased the sin, and then permitted intercourse.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r6] author | holds | Bashiruhunna means have lawful intercourse; it is called direct contact because the partners' skin touches.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r7] author | reports | Most exegetes understand 'seek what God wrote for you' as seeking a child; Mujahid says seek offspring, even with another wife if one does not conceive.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r8] قتادة | reports | Qatada interprets the phrase as seeking the dispensation God ordained: eating, drinking, and intercourse during the fast's nights.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p207/r9] معاذ بن جبل | reports | Muadh interprets what God wrote as Laylat al-Qadr.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p209/r1] author | holds | Fasting begins at true dawn, when eating and drinking cease, and ends at sunset, when the fast is broken.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p209/r2] عمر بن الخطاب | reports | Umar reports the Prophet's rule that the fast is broken once night comes from the east, day recedes in the west, and the sun sets.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p209/r3] author | holds | I'tikaf is remaining in a mosque in worship; it is a recommended practice, valid in any mosque, and not outside one.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p209/r4] author | holds | The ban addresses intercourse during i'tikaf, which is prohibited and invalidates the retreat; intercourse was forbidden day and night for the companions who left the mosque for it.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p209/r5] عائشة | reports | Aisha reports that the Prophet observed the final ten days of Ramadan in i'tikaf until his death, and his wives continued the practice after him.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p209/r6] author | reports | Most scholars, and al-Shafii's stronger view, consider kissing or touching with desire disliked but not invalidating i'tikaf; Malik says it invalidates it.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p209/r7] author | reports | Another view says emission invalidates the retreat, while touch without emission does not, by analogy with fasting.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p209/r8] عائشة | reports | Aisha reports that the Prophet would bring his head near her while in i'tikaf so she could groom him, and he entered the house only for a human need; the author uses this for non-desirous touch.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p210/r1] author | holds | The stated limits are the fasting and i'tikaf rulings just described; 'limits' means what God has forbidden people to violate.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p210/r2] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi glosses God's limits as His conditions; Shahr ibn Hawshab calls them His obligations.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p210/r3] author | holds | The Arabic root of hadd is prevention, as a gatekeeper is called a haddad because he prevents entry.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p210/r4] author | holds | Do not approach the limits means do not commit the prohibited acts; God clarifies His signs so people may avoid them and escape punishment.
