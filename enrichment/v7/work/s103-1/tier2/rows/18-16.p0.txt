## TAB-FULL: al-Ṭabarī, d. 310 AH
[TAB-FULL:v15p182#2/r1] author | holds | Marfiqan means something by which the youths can find ease or support in their distress and fear for faith and life.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182#2/r2] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra reports both vowel patterns for marfiq in the hand and in the sense of a useful thing.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182#2/r3] الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisa'i accepts only the pattern with fatha on fa and kasra on mim for the elbow.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182#2/r4] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra reports both forms for the arm joint and for a beneficial thing, and cites a rajaz line using marfiq and mirfaq.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182#2/r5] الشاعر | reports | The rajaz poet's pairing of marfiq and mirfaq illustrates the two forms.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182#2/r6] نحويي أهل البصرة | reports | A Basran grammarian treats marfiqan as a noun meaning a useful thing, patterned like masjid, or as a form meaning ease.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182#2/r7] author | prefers | Both regional readings are valid and equivalent, but he prefers the Iraqi kasra-mim, fatha-fa reading as the more eloquent and common Arabic form.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182/r1] author | holds | The youths tell one another to leave their people, who worship gods besides God, and take refuge in the cave so that God may spread mercy and ease their situation.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182/r2] author | holds | In the phrase about what their people worship, ma is accusative and coordinated with the pronoun suffix in iʿtazaltumuhum.
[TAB-FULL:v15p182/r3] قتادة | reports | Qatada notes that Abdullah's codex reads wa-ma yaʿbuduna min dun Allah, clarifying that the people worshipped other than God.

## NAHHAS: Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338 AH
[NAHHAS:v2p450/r3] author | holds | The opening ‘when you withdrew’ means ‘remember when you withdrew’; it is one youth speaking to the others.
[NAHHAS:v2p450/r4] author | holds | The object of ‘you withdrew from’ is what they worshipped; the exception excludes God, whom they did not abandon in worship.
[NAHHAS:v2p450/r5] author | holds | ‘Then take refuge in the cave’ is answered by ‘your Lord will spread for you’.
[NAHHAS:v2p450/r6] الأصمعي | reports | He knows only the kasra form marfiq for a matter, a hand, or anything else.
[NAHHAS:v2p450/r7] الكسائي والفراء | reports | They say the kasra is the fluent form, while the fatha is also allowed; the fatha distinguishes a facility from a human elbow.
[NAHHAS:v2p450/r8] الأخفش سعيد | reports | He recognizes three good marfiq forms: one for a tool or facility, one patterned like masjid, and one meaning gentleness.
