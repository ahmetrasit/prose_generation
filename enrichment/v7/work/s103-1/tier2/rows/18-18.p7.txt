[BURSEVI:v5p174/r8] التأويلات النجمية | reports | The cited inward reading begins an analogy about the seeker trained directly by God, who must surrender like a corpse before a washer.
[BURSEVI:v5p175/r1] الإمام اليافعي | reports | One of the dog's ten traits is that when it leaves a place it does not look back, a sign of the sorrowful.
[BURSEVI:v5p175/r2] author | holds | If one saw the sleepers, one would flee; الاطلاع means looking over something by direct sight. Fleeing is a verbal noun for turning away, and رعب is fear filling the chest.

[BURSEVI:v5p175/r4] ابن عباس | reports | When Muʿāwiya wanted to inspect the cave, Ibn ʿAbbās said even someone better than him had been barred by the verse; Muʿāwiya nevertheless sent men, and a wind burned them or drove them out.
[BURSEVI:v5p175/r5] author | holds | The verse's bar means no one can scrutinize them; Muʿāwiya thought the bar applied only before awakening and discovery, while Ibn ʿAbbās knew it applied in every age.
[BURSEVI:v5p175/r6] author | holds | The address speaks to the Messenger but signifies anyone in his community, including Muʿāwiya; inspecting extraordinary matters is not accessible to every observer.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r1] author | reports | Some see the contrast between deep sleep and repeated turning as a display of divine power, but the author considers this unlikely because deep sleep ordinarily has little movement.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r10] author | holds | Kalbahum most naturally means their actual dog, which has many names and inspired a separate treatise by al-Suyuti.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r11] كعب الأحبار | reports | Kaʿb says a dog followed them after they repeatedly drove it away; it reassured them that it loved God's beloved and would guard them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r12] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says it was a shepherd's dog that followed its owner, who had joined the youths' faith.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r13] عبيد بن عمير | reports | Ubayd ibn Umayr says it was either one youth's hunting dog or a sheep dog; keeping a dog for hunting or livestock is permitted, unlike other purposes.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r14] ابن عمر | reports | A hadith in al-Bukhari from Ibn Umar says keeping a dog outside hunting or livestock reduces one's deeds daily by two qirats, or one in another narration.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r15] عبيد | reports | A report from Ubayd says the dog was red like an embroidered cloak; another from Kathir al-Nawwa says yellow, while other accounts say it was spotted.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r16] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan calls the dog Qitmir; Mujahid calls it Qitmur, while other reports name it Rayyan or Thawr.

[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r5] قتادة | reports | Qatada says they were turned annually during the first three hundred years, but not in the second sleep of nine years.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p224#2/r6] الإمام | rejects | The Imam says such timetables cannot be inferred by reason or the Quran and lack an authentic report; the imperfect, intensified verb instead suggests repeated turning.
