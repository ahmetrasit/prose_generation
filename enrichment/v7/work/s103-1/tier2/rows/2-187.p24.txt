## SUYUTI-LUBAB: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p34/r1] معاذ بن جبل | reports | Muadh reports that before this ruling people could eat, drink, and have intercourse until they slept; Qays ibn Sirma slept after the evening prayer without food and woke exhausted, while Umar had intercourse after sleeping and told the Prophet; the verse then came.
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p34/r2] author | holds | The report through Ibn Abi Layla from Muadh is well known, though Ibn Abi Layla did not hear directly from Muadh; it has corroborating reports.
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p34/r3] البراء | reports | Al-Bara reports that anyone who slept before breaking the fast could not eat that night or the next day; Qays's wife sought food while he worked, he slept, and he fainted the following day before the verse permitted night intimacy and food until dawn.
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p34/r4] البراء | reports | Al-Bara also reports that when Ramadan fasting was first prescribed, men abstained from women for the entire month and some betrayed themselves, prompting the clause about God forgiving them.
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p34/r5] كعب بن مالك | reports | Ka'b reports that after sleeping, Ramadan fasting forbade food, drink, and sex until the next sunset; Umar and Ka'b each had intercourse after sleep, and Umar informed the Prophet, after which the verse was revealed.
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p35/r1] سهل بن سعد | reports | Sahl reports that the initial wording lacked “at dawn”; some tied white and black threads to their legs and kept eating until they could distinguish them, then the clarification showed that night and day were meant.
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p35/r2] قتادة | reports | Qatada says a person in retreat could leave the mosque and have intercourse if he wished, prompting the prohibition on intimacy during retreat in mosques.
