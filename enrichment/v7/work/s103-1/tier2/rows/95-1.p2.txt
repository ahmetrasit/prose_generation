## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r1] author | holds | The fig recalls the leaves Adam used to cover himself in Paradise. | mentions 7:22
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r10] author | prefers | Qurtubi's preferred ruling requires zakat on both figs and olives, while urging people to give God His due.
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r2] author | reports | Another explanation says the fig is sworn by to display its great blessing: attractive, fragrant, delicious, easy to pick, and small enough for a bite.
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r3] الشاعر | reports | One poem likens the fig's split skin and bowed neck to a once-rich person stripped of new clothing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r4] الشاعر | reports | A second poem praises the sweet fig hanging from its branch, with a face like one bowing in awe of God.
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r5] author | holds | The olive recalls Abraham's image of a blessed olive tree. | mentions 24:35
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r6] author | holds | Olive is a staple in Syria and the Maghreb, used as relish, in cooking, as lamp oil, and for internal ailments, sores, and wounds.
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r7] النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A prophetic report recommends eating and applying olive oil because it comes from a blessed tree.
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r8] ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-Arabi argues that because figs are a divine bounty and a stored staple, their zakat should be paid; rulers' unjust collection made many scholars avoid stating this.
[QURTUBI-FULL:95:1#2/r9] الشافعي | reports | Al-Shafi'i says olives are not subject to zakat, partly for the same concern about rulers' exactions.
