[BURSEVI:v5p178/r1] author | holds | As with their sleep and awakening, God exposed the youths to display His power and wisdom and strengthen their certainty.
[BURSEVI:v5p178/r2] author | holds | Aʿtharna means making people discover them, from the image of stumbling upon something hidden; the cause is named for the knowledge it produces.
[BURSEVI:v5p178/r3] تهذيب المصادر | reports | The lexicon glosses إعثار as bringing someone upon a thing and اطلاع as bringing someone to hidden knowledge.
[BURSEVI:v5p178/r4] author | holds | Discovery convinced people who denied bodily resurrection that God's promise is true: He held the youths' souls while preserving their bodies for centuries, then returned the souls, as He will gather all bodies for judgment.
[BURSEVI:v5p178/r5] author | holds | God graciously guided the people through the sign of sleep, proving the truth in a way that served as a messenger's proof for receptive hearts.
[BURSEVI:v5p178/r6] الإمام الثعلبي | reports | A report says the Prophet wished to see the Cave youths; Gabriel said he would not see them in this world, so he sent Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿAlī, and Abū al-Dardāʾ to invite them to Islam. Solomon's swift mount carried them; the dog first barked then recognized them. The youths awoke, exchanged greetings, accepted Islam, conveyed greetings to Muhammad, and slept again; they will awaken to greet the Mahdī and die before resurrection.

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232#2/r1] بعض المحققين | reports | A scholar argues that seeing God preserve the youths' souls and bodies for over three hundred years and return their souls proves He can resurrect, judge, and reward the dead.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232#2/r2] author | rejects | Alusi questions whether the episode establishes judgment and recompense; an objection says preserving bodies is unlike reassembling scattered parts, and he finds the reply from intuitive inference worth considering but not decisive.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232/r1] author | holds | The youths sent one of their number, identified by many as Yamlikha; God caused them to be discovered just as He had put them to sleep and awakened them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232/r2] العز بن عبد السلام | reports | Ibn ʿAbd al-Salam takes the demonstrative to refer specifically to their awakening after the long sleep.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232/r3] الراغب | reports | Raghib gives the original meaning of ʿathara as falling face-first and cites proverbs about a generous horse rarely stumbling and the safe well-trodden road.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232/r4] المطرزي | reports | Al-Mutarrizi explains discovery as a metaphor from stumbling: a fallen person looks at where he fell, so the word came to mean finding out, by a familiar causal relation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232/r5] author | holds | The first object of aʿtharnā is omitted to make it general: God let people discover the youths.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232/r6] أبو حيان | reports | Abu Hayyan identifies the people who learned as the townspeople who saw the youths' extraordinary condition; God's promise means the resurrection promise or what He promised, namely resurrection, is true and certain.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p232/r7] author | holds | The Hour is the time when all creatures are raised for judgment and recompense; witnessing the youths' extraordinary death-like sleep and awakening defeats reliance on improbability as a reason to doubt it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p233/r1] author | prefers | Alusi prefers explaining the inference from the youths' preserved bodies, suspended senses, and restoration after centuries: an event as extraordinary as resurrection removes the doubt that arises from finding resurrection improbable.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p233/r2] author | holds | The event directly establishes resurrection's possibility, not certainty that it will happen; certainty of fulfillment comes from the truthful report, while the event supplies a final part of the proof for those who heard it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p233/r3] author | reports | Another interpretation makes God's promise cover all He promised and the Hour a specification after that general statement; Alusi accepts it as a possible reading but disputes calling it the obvious one.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p233/r4] author | holds | The temporal phrase idh yatanaazaʿun most naturally modifies aʿtharnā, placed first to emphasize the purpose; Abu Hayyan and Abu al-Baqa also attach it to liyaʿlamu, though Alusi raises objections.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p233/r5] author | holds | Tanaazuʿ originally means pulling against one another and figuratively means disputing; it takes a direct object in its literal sense but fi with the sense of quarrel. | mentions 4:59
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p233/r6] author | reports | The dispute is about resurrection: people differed between affirming it, denying it, raising souls alone, or raising both souls and bodies, the last being the Muslim doctrine.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p233/r7] author | reports | A narrative says a later believing king, faced with disagreement over resurrection, prayed for a sign; a shepherd opened the cave, the youths awoke, their old coins exposed their story, and after meeting the king they died, were placed in wooden coffins, and a mosque was built.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p234/r1] author | reports | An alternative report says the king matched the youth's names to an old tablet, then the youth entered first to spare his companions fear; God took their souls and obscured their traces, and the townspeople built a mosque after trusting the youth's account.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p234/r2] author | reports | Another interpretation makes their affair the story of their former ordeal under the tyrant, which people had heard as legend without knowing whether the youths survived.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p234/r3] author | holds | On either interpretation, the fa before build is an abbreviated transition: the purpose of discovery was achieved, the youths died, and the people then proposed a structure at the cave entrance.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p234/r4] الراغب، أبو البقاء | reports | Bunyanan is parsed either as a singular noun with no plural, a plural of bunyana, or a cognate accusative; the builders sought to protect the youths' resting place from intrusion.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p235/r1] author | reports | Rabbuhum aʿlam bihim may be the finders submitting knowledge of lineage, condition, and duration to God, or God's rebuke to those speculating, including the finders or the People of the Book in the Prophet's time.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p235/r2] author | reports | Some assign amrahum to the youths and read the dispute as what to do after their death, how to honor them, or uncertainty whether they had died or merely slept again.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p235/r3] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari offers further options: amrahum may concern the finders' religion and resurrection, the people's discussion of the cave youths' story, or plans to conceal the youths' location after death.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p235/r4] صاحب الكشف | reports | The author of al-Kashf distinguishes the conjunction: the fa is explanatory in one account and consecutive in the others, including when people stop discussing the story and turn to the practical matter of building.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p235/r5] أبو حيان | reports | Abu Hayyan allows the pronoun in liyaʿlamu to refer to the youths, with their discovery increasing their knowledge; the dispute then begins a new account about the townspeople and their building plans.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p235/r6] author | holds | Alusi says this reading is not far-fetched if one sets aside external reports, though he knows of no commentator who takes all the pronouns as referring to the youths and idh as modifying discovery.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236#2/r1] author | reports | A report says the first group were unbelievers seeking a church or pagan sanctuary, while believers opposed them and built a mosque; another report says the king built a church with an inscription, suggesting mosque here names the Christian community's place of worship.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236#2/r2] السدي | reports | Al-Suddi reports that the king planned a mosque beside the righteous youths where he could worship God until death.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236#2/r3] الحسن | reports | Hasan says the mosque was for the youths to pray in upon waking; this assumes they remained asleep, a view extended by some to say they await the Mahdi, which Alusi dismisses as fables.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236#2/r4] author | reports | If the first group were unbelievers, not every finder shared the resurrection lesson; they may have wished to seal the cave because they did not know the youths had slept and awakened.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236#2/r5] الحسن، عيسى الثقفي | reports | They read ghulibū in the passive: believing finders who opposed construction were overpowered and yielded that, if anything were built, it should be a mosque.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236#2/r6] author | rejects | Alusi rejects using the verse to permit building mosques over righteous graves or praying there, despite al-Khafaji's claim; he cites multiple prophetic reports cursing grave-visitors who make mosques and lamps at graves and those who turn prophets' graves into mosques.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236#2/r7] رسول الله | reports | Reports from Ibn Abbas, Aisha, Abu Hurayra, and others condemn turning prophets' graves into mosques; another says people who build over a righteous person's grave and place images there are among the worst at the Resurrection.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236#2/r8] رسول الله | reports | Other reports call grave-mosque makers among the worst people and say God cursed earlier Israelites for making graves into mosques.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p236/r1] author | prefers | Alusi considers it remote that rabbuhum aʿlam is God's speech rebuking the disputants, and very remote to assign all pronouns to the youths; its apparent sense is the finders' response to uncertainty over life and death.
