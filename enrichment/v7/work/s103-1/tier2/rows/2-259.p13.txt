[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r10] القتبي | reports | Al-Qutbī is reported to identify the passerby as Isaiah in one of his positions; another report says he was Lot’s servant.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r11] وهب بن منبه وقتادة والربيع بن أنس | reports | Wahb ibn Munabbih, Qatāda, and al-Rabīʿ identify the town as Jerusalem, which Bukhtanaṣṣar had destroyed; the food was said to be green figs and grapes and the drink wine, juice, or water.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r12] ابن عباس | reports | In a report from Ibn ʿAbbās via Abū Ṣāliḥ, Bukhtanaṣṣar deported Israelites, including the learned ʿUzayr, to Babylon; ʿUzayr later stopped at a ruined town and wondered how God would revive it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r2] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad supplies an ellipsis: “Have you seen the one who disputed with Abraham—have you seen who he is?—like the one who passed a town.”
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r3] أبو سفيان بن حسين | reports | Abū Sufyān ibn Ḥusayn reads the waw in “or the one who passed” with fatḥa, treating it as coordination preceded by the interrogative alif of emphasis.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r4] author | holds | The word for town, qarya, is linked to people gathering there, as in the expression for collecting water.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r5] سليمان بن بريدة وناجية بن كعب وقتادة وابن عباس والربيع وعكرمة والضحاك | reports | Sulaymān ibn Burayda, Nājiya ibn Kaʿb, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Rabīʿ, ʿIkrima, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk identify the passerby as ʿUzayr.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r6] وهب بن منبه وعبد الله بن عبيد بن عمير وعبد الله بن بكر بن مضر | reports | Wahb ibn Munabbih and others identify the passerby as the prophet Irmiyāʾ (Jeremiah).
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r7] ابن إسحاق | reports | Ibn Isḥāq identifies Irmiyāʾ as al-Khiḍr, a claim also attributed by al-Naqqāsh to Wahb; Ibn ʿAṭiyya questions it because al-Khiḍr was contemporary with Moses whereas this event was later.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r8] author | reports | The author says Irmiyāʾ could be al-Khiḍr if the sound view that al-Khiḍr remained alive from Moses onward is correct; if he died before the event, Ibn ʿAṭiyya’s objection stands. | mentions 18:60
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:259/r9] مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid is reported to say the passerby was an unnamed man from the Children of Israel.

## BAYDAWI-FULL: al-Bayḍāwī, d. 685 AH
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259#2/r1] author | reports | The bones may be those of the donkey or of the dead people whose revival astonished him.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259#2/r2] author | reports | Nanshizu means either revive the bones or raise and assemble them; kayfa is adverbial and the clause depicts the bones revived.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259#2/r3] author | holds | Ibn Kathir, Nafi, Abu Amr, and Yaqub read nunshiruha, from God raising the dead; another reading derives it from nashara with the same sense.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259#2/r4] author | holds | The subject of 'when it became clear to him' is omitted and supplied by the following clause, or the preceding event explains what became clear.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259#2/r5] author | holds | Hamza and al-Kisa'i read 'know' as an imperative, addressed either to the listener or reflexively to the speaker as a rebuke.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r1] author | holds | The construction is estimated as 'or have you seen the like of the one who passed'; the omitted phrase is understood from the preceding 'have you not seen.'
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r10] author | reports | The question 'How long did you remain?' is spoken by God; this is possible because the man believed after revival or was close to believing. Another view assigns it to an angel or prophet.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r11] author | reports | The man guessed 'a day or part of a day'; one account says he died at forenoon and awoke before sunset, first saying a day, then correcting himself after seeing the sun.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r12] author | holds | Lam yatasannah means the food and drink had not changed; the word may derive from sanah with h as root or pause marker, or from a form related to al-hama al-masnun.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r13] author | holds | Hamza and al-Kisa'i read lam yatasan without final h in connected recitation; the dual food and drink take a singular pronoun because they form one kind.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r14] author | reports | The food may have been figs and grapes and the drink juice or milk, all preserved unchanged.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r15] author | reports | The donkey may be shown in scattered bones, or still safe where it was tied, preserved without water or fodder; the scattered bones better fit the scene and following verse.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r16] author | reports | The event made him a sign: he returned as a young man while his children were elderly, or read the Torah from memory and was recognized, after which people called him God's son.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r2] قيل | reports | The comparative particle is extra, yielding 'Have you not seen the one who disputed, or the one who passed?'
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r3] قيل | reports | The phrase is conjoined according to sense, as though it said, 'Have you not seen like the one who disputed, or like the one who passed?'
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r4] قيل | reports | Alternatively, these words are Abraham's reply to an opponent: if you claim to revive, revive as God revived the passerby.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r5] author | reports | The passerby is identified as Uzayr son of Sharahya, al-Khidr, or a disbeliever in resurrection; the last view is supported by the passage's sequence with Nimrod.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r6] author | reports | The village may be Jerusalem when Nebuchadnezzar destroyed it, the village from which thousands departed, or another place; qaryah derives from gathering.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r7] author | holds | The village is empty, its walls fallen onto its roofs; the speaker's question expresses either confessed inability and awe at revival or skeptical disbelief.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r8] author | holds | Anna is parsed as an adverb meaning 'when' or as a circumstantial expression meaning 'how.'
[BAYDAWI-FULL:2:259/r9] author | reports | God either kept him dead for a hundred years or caused him to die and leave him dead for a hundred years before reviving him.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r1] author | holds | The opening 'or like the one' has an omitted 'Have you seen?' supplied by the earlier question; both expressions signal wonder, or the syntax follows meaning rather than wording.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r10] author | holds | لم يتسنه means it did not change; its final h may be radical or a pause h, deriving from سنة as سنهة or سنوة; Hamza and Kisāʾī omit h in connected reading but retain it at pause.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r11] author | reports | His donkey's bones had separated and decayed; alternatively, the donkey remained alive where tied for a hundred years without food or water, a great sign.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r12] author | reports | Ezra's return was a sign: one account says he arrived mounted and recited the Torah from memory after no one had recited it visibly before him; another says his children were old while he was young.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r13] author | holds | The bones are either the donkey's or the bones of the dead whose revival he questioned; nunsizuhā means move and raise them together, while Hijazi and Basran read nunshiruhā, 'revive them.'
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r14] author | holds | Clothing bones with flesh is a metaphor; in 'when it became clear to him' the understood complement is that God can do all things, or what had puzzled him: resurrection.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r15] حمزة وعلى | reports | They read aʿlam as an imperative: God tells Ezra to know, or Ezra addresses himself; the other reading is his statement that he knows.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r2] صاحب الكشف | reports | Al-Kashshāf's author says the kāf is redundant and alladhī coordinates with the person who disputed Abraham.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r3] الحسن | reports | Hasan identifies the passerby as an unbeliever in resurrection, linked with Nimrod by context and by the expression of incredulity.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r4] الأكثر | reports | Most identify the passerby as Ezra, who sought to witness revival and increase certainty, as Abraham had; his question admits ignorance of how and magnifies God's power.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r5] author | holds | The ruined town is Jerusalem, destroyed by Nebuchadnezzar; it is also the town from which thousands had departed.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r6] author | reports | The roofs may have fallen together with their supports, or roofs fell first and walls then collapsed on them.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r7] author | holds | The question asks how God will revive the town's inhabitants after their death; God then caused the passerby to die for a hundred years and raised him.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r8] author | holds | His estimate of a day or part of one was conjecture; he died in the morning and awoke before sunset a century later, so he first saw a day then the remaining sun.
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p126/r9] author | reports | His food was figs and grapes and his drink juice and milk; the fruit seemed freshly picked and the drink unchanged.

## QASHANI: ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736 AH
[QASHANI:2:259#2/r1] author | holds | Feeling a long interval as a day resembles resurrection passages: the unaware do not sense motion or time, and separation seems never to have occurred once reunion arrives. | mentions 10:45, 79:46, 30:55
[QASHANI:2:259#2/r2] قيل | reports | An earlier interpretation identifies the food as figs and grapes and the drink as wine and milk.
[QASHANI:2:259#2/r3] author | holds | Figs represent universal intelligibles with particulars latent in them, while grapes represent particulars retaining material adjuncts in cognition.
[QASHANI:2:259#2/r4] author | holds | Milk signifies useful knowledge such as revealed laws; wine signifies love, will, and knowledge of mystical realities.
[QASHANI:2:259#2/r5] author | holds | Unchanged food signifies knowledge deposited eternally in each soul
