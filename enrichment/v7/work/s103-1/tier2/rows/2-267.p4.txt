## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r1] author | reports | The preceding phrase is illustrated by the command to spend what one loves in order to attain righteousness. | mentions 3:92
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r10] author | holds | The produce from the earth means crops and fruits subject to zakat, which should also be given.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r11] author | holds | Do not deliberately select inferior wealth, whether earned or grown; some interpret khabīth as unlawful wealth, but the first interpretation fits the comparison to something accepted only reluctantly.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r12] البراء بن عازب | reports | One explanation of accepting it only with igmāḍ is that a creditor would take such an item from a debtor only by lenient accommodation.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r13] الحسن | reports | Another explanation is that one would accept it only by discounting its price; al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, and al-Zajjāj are cited for this sense.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r14] author | holds | God is independent of charity yet worthy of praise; hamīd may mean deserving praise, eliciting praise through gifts, or praising and rewarding donors, with the last fitting this context best.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r15] author | holds | Hamīd suits this verse better than ḥalīm: after commanding the best earnings, God says He needs none of them yet praises those who obey and rewards their giving.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r2] عبيد بن رفاعة | reports | A report from ʿUbayd b. Rifāʿa quotes the Prophet warning that merchants are sinners except those who are God-fearing, dutiful, truthful, and charitable.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r3] النبي | reports | The Prophet is also quoted as saying that nine tenths of provision comes from trade and the remainder from seafaring.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r4] عائشة | reports | ʿĀʾisha reports the Prophet saying that the best food a person eats is from his own earning, and that his child is part of his earning.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r5] النبي | reports | A report through Saʿīd b. ʿUmayr quotes the Prophet saying that a person best earns through work by hand and every sound sale.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r6] علي | reports | ʿAlī warns that anyone trading without knowledge becomes entangled in usury again and again.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r7] عبيدة السلماني | reports | One view makes the command refer specifically to obligatory alms; this is attributed to ʿUbayda al-Salmānī and al-Ḥasan.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r8] الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī limits it to voluntary charity, since obligatory alms have a set value owed as a debt if underpaid, and even entirely poor-quality wealth may be used for zakat.
[TABRISI:2:267#2/r9] author | prefers | The author prefers a general command covering obligatory and voluntary spending in every good cause; it also indicates that charity from earned lawful wealth brings greater reward because it costs more.
[TABRISI:2:267/r1] ابن كثير | reports | Ibn Kathir reads the verb with a doubled tāʾ here and in thirty-one Qurʾanic places; the other readers use the lighter form.
[TABRISI:2:267/r10] author | holds | The passage completes the prior discussion of spending by describing the gift and its recipient: believers are told to give charity while preserving it from invalidation by reproach or harm.
[TABRISI:2:267/r11] ابن مسعود | reports | One explanation of good earnings is lawful income acquired through trade, reported from Ibn Masʿūd and Mujāhid.
[TABRISI:2:267/r12] author | reports | Another explanation takes the phrase as the best and finest of what one has earned.
[TABRISI:2:267/r2] author | holds | The two readings mean the same; the doubled form restores and assimilates the dropped tāʾ, while common recitation omits one of the two tāʾs for ease.
[TABRISI:2:267/r3] author | holds | Tayammum means deliberately intending or aiming at something; Khifaf and al-Aʿshā use it this way, as do related forms for intending or being a target.
[TABRISI:2:267/r4] author | holds | Khabīth means the defective or inferior thing; the dross expelled from silver and iron is called khabath, whose root sense is badness.
[TABRISI:2:267/r5] author | holds | In trade, igmāḍ means accepting a reduced price for a defect; its root concerns concealment, with related senses of closing the eyes and sleep.
[TABRISI:2:267/r6] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ construes an in illā an tughmiḍū as conditional: if you show some forbearance, you will take it, so the hamza is kasra.
[TABRISI:2:267/r7] المحققون | rejects | The leading grammarians reject the conditional analysis: an here is a verbal noun, meaning you would take it only through your forbearance.
[TABRISI:2:267/r8] أبو عبد الله | reports | One reported occasion is people donating assets from pre-Islamic usury; they were forbidden to give these and commanded to give lawful, wholesome wealth.
[TABRISI:2:267/r9] علي | reports | Another occasion concerns people putting poor-quality dried dates into the charity dates; this is reported from ʿAlī, al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib, al-Ḥasan, and Qatāda.
