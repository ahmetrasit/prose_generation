## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p132#4/r1] author | reports | The speaker may be God or an angel ordered to ask; the imperative reading “say” is attributed to Meccan, Ḥamza, and ʿAlī readers and orders Mālik to question them.
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p132#4/r2] author | holds | “In the earth” refers to worldly life; “number of years” specifies the duration and is governed by “how long.”

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p587/r8] حمزة | reports | Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, and Ibn Kathīr read “qul kam” as an imperative addressed to an angel or another inhabitant of Hell; the remaining readers have “qāla,” spoken by God or an assigned angel.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p587/r9] author | holds | The question about years is a demand that fixes them to an answer; most read “ʿadada sinīn” as a construct, with “how many” measuring time and “number” specifying it. A reading with indefinite “ʿadadan” allows other parses, but “labithtum” as “you counted” is remote.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:23:112/r1] الأخوان | reports | The two readers use the imperative قل in both instances; Ibn Kathir matches them only in the first, while the remaining readers use قال as a report by God or an angel.
[SAMIN-DURR:23:112/r2] author | holds | The two verbs lack alif in Kufan codices and include it in other regional codices; Hamza and Kisai follow Kufa, Asim differs or assumes a written omission, Ibn Kathir follows Mecca only in the second, and the others follow their codices.
[SAMIN-DURR:23:112/r3] author | holds | كم is an accusative time adverb meaning how many years; عدد may be بدل of كم, as Abu al-Baqa says, but the author prefers عدد سنين as its specification.
[SAMIN-DURR:23:112/r4] الأعمش والمفضل عن عاصم | reports | They read عددا with tanwin; al-Lawamiʿ takes it as an advanced verbal-noun modifier, though the author says the Basran parse should be a circumstantial accusative.
[SAMIN-DURR:23:112/r5] صاحب اللوامح | reports | A second analysis treats لبثتم as meaning counted, making عددا a verbal noun and سنين a بدل; al-Lawamiʿ calls this unlikely because staying does not imply counting.
[SAMIN-DURR:23:112/r6] author | holds | A third analysis makes عددا the specification of كم and سنين its بدل.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p500/r1] author | holds | The question exposes how little of their short worldly lives they spent worshipping God; patience in that brief life would have brought the pious believers' success.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p500/r2] author | holds | They say they stayed a day or part of a day and are told to ask those who count the time.

## BIQAI-FULL: al-Biqāʿī, d. 885 AH

[BIQAI-FULL:v5p225#2/r2] author | reports | On the reading of the majority, God directly asks the question; Ibn Kathir, Hamza, and al-Kisaʾi read qul, making an appointed angel the speaker as God turns away from and punishes them.
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p225#2/r3] author | holds | They are asked about worldly years in which they thought themselves victorious and dominant over their enemies.
