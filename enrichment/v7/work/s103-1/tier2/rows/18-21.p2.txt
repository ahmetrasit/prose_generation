## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r1] author | holds | 'Likewise' links discovering the youths to the prior awakening; athara is the causative of stumbling on something, a metaphor for discovering concealed knowledge.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r2] الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari says 'so that they might know' refers to the Muslim community of the youths' era, which had fallen into dispute over bodily resurrection; God revealed the sleepers, convincing doubters and consoling a distressed king.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r3] author | reports | On al-Tabari's reading, the king was so troubled by people's doubt that he wore rough garments, sat in ashes, and prayed for proof until the youths were discovered.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r4] author | reports | Alternatively, 'so that they might know' refers to the youths themselves, whose story became a sign of bodily resurrection.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r5] author | holds | The phrase 'the Hour has no doubt in it' asserts that the Hour is objectively certain even though people may doubt it.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r6] author | reports | A separate disagreement concerns whether to cover the cave or build over it; some said the youths were dead and others alive, and a dominant party proposed a mosque. Qatada identifies that party as the rulers.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r7] author | reports | One account says believers who wanted the cave obscured prevailed over those who insisted on construction, and made the required building a mosque.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r8] author | reports | Another account says unbelievers wanted a church or pagan shrine, but believers resisted and insisted on a mosque.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p506#2/r9] عبيد بن عمير | reports | Ubayd ibn Umayr says God concealed the youths' trace from people, prompting construction of a building to mark their location.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:21-22/r1] ابن عباس | reports | An alternative account places the dispute after the ruler and people entered the cave and the youths fell dead; some proposed a tomb-like building, others a mosque at the entrance.
[TABRISI:18:21-24/r1] author | holds | ʿAthara means to come upon something; aʿthara means to reveal it, and an ʿāthūr is a pit dug to catch a lion, hence a person caught in trouble is said to have fallen into one.
[TABRISI:18:21-24/r2] author | holds | Marāʾ means disputation or argument.
[TABRISI:18:21-24/r3] author | reports | The time phrase idh yatanaazaʿūn may attach to ‘We revealed them’ or to ‘so they would know.’
[TABRISI:18:21/r1] author | rejects | The proposed ‘waaw of eight’ in the opening of 18:21 is an unfamiliar grammatical category unknown to grammarians; the argument from Paradise's eight gates is unsound.
[TABRISI:18:21/r2] author | reports | The exegetes recount that the ruler who sealed the youths in the cave expected them to die there, while two believers recorded their names, lineage, and story on lead and hid the record in a copper chest in the wall.
[TABRISI:18:21/r3] ابن إسحاق | reports | The later righteous ruler who governed the region is called Nadlis; another transmitted form of his name is Bandusis.
[TABRISI:18:21/r4] author | reports | After generations passed, people divided over resurrection; the righteous king prayed for a sign, and God caused the cave entrance structure to be dismantled, awakened the youths, and exposed their story when one went to seek food.
[TABRISI:18:21/r5] author | holds | The youths' long sleep while alive and their awakening demonstrate that the one who can preserve and awaken them can also cause death and resurrection.
[TABRISI:18:21/r6] author | reports | The dispute about resurrection included those who denied it, those who accepted resurrection of souls alone, and those who affirmed resurrection of both souls and bodies.
[TABRISI:18:21/r7] عكرمة | reports | The phrase about disputing their affair is an idiom for what people did in their matter, as in the cited Arabic expression.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89#2/r1] author | reports | After the townspeople saw them, God may have returned the youths to the cave and caused their death; people then disagreed whether they slept again or had now died.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89#2/r2] author | reports | Some proposed sealing the cave so no one could enter, while others proposed building a mosque; this second proposal suggests they knew God, worship, and prayer.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89#2/r3] author | reports | Another account says unbelievers claimed the sleepers followed their religion and wanted a monument, while Muslims said they followed theirs and proposed a mosque.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89#2/r4] author | reports | Other reported disputes concerned how long the youths stayed and their number and names.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89#2/r5] author | reports | “Their Lord knows best” may be the disputants' admission after failing to establish their names, circumstances, or duration, or it may be God's own correction of their speculation.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89#2/r6] author | reports | Those who prevailed over the matter are variously identified as the Muslim king, the sleepers' relatives, or the town's leaders.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89#2/r7] author | holds | They proposed a mosque where people could worship God and preserve the youths' traces.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89/r1] author | holds | As with their guidance and awakening, God made others discover their state because it carried evident lessons; athartu means to discover or learn something, from stumbling upon it and then recognizing it.

[RAZI-FULL:v21p89/r3] author | reports | Another account says the emissary's old coins were recognized by a seller as long obsolete; the king learned the man had fled Daqyānūs the day before and concluded God had revived him.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89/r4] author | holds | God revealed the youths so people would know His promise of resurrection is true; their awakening after a long sleep demonstrates bodily resurrection.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89/r5] author | reports | One account says the king denied resurrection but was fair-minded; another says people disputed whether body and soul or only the soul would be raised, and the king prayed for a sign that settled the issue.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89/r6] author | reports | The phrase “while they were disputing among themselves” attaches to God making the people discover the youths, at the time of their dispute.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p89/r7] author | reports | Their dispute may have concerned resurrection: believers used God's preservation of the bodies for 309 years as proof that He can raise bodies after death.
