## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r1] قطرب | reports | Qutrub derives ‘ma‘un’ as fa‘ul from ma‘n, ‘a small thing’, citing ‘ma-lahu ma‘na’ for someone with little property.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r2] author | reports | A second derivation makes it a passive participle of ‘a‘ana’; its original form would be ma‘wun, with metathesis and vowel change producing the attested form and pattern maf‘ul.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r3] author | holds | That derivation has three irregularities: a passive from form IV normally has the maf‘al pattern, it uses metathesis, and it changes an unvocalized weak letter to alif; the author calls comparison with ‘taba’ and ‘ṣama’ weak.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r4] author | reports | A possible answer to the final irregularity is that the wāw was vocalized before metathesis, as the reconstructed ‘ma‘wun’ suggests.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r5] author | prefers | The preferred derivation is ma‘una, with alif replacing the feminine tā’; on this account ma‘un means anything people use and benefit from, such as an axe, bucket, or fire striker.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r6] الأعشى | reports | Al-A‘sha’s poem uses ‘ma‘unihi’ for useful provisions or resources; it is offered as poetic evidence for the preferred sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:107:7/r7] author | holds | The object of ‘yamna‘un’ is omitted because people or petitioners are understood, or because the point is to condemn withholding trifling things that everyone recognizes should not be denied.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p495#2/r1] author | holds | The verse condemns refusing to lend useful objects whose substance remains and is returned; those who refuse these favors are even more likely to withhold zakāt and other devotions.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p495#2/r2] علي | reports | ʿAlī and many early authorities identify al-māʿūn as zakāt.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p495#2/r3] الحسن البصري | reports | Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī says they pray to show off, do not regret missing prayer, and withhold the zakāt or charity of their wealth.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p495#2/r4] زيد بن أسلم | reports | Zayd ibn Aslam says they are hypocrites who perform the visible prayer but withhold the zakāt that was enjoined.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p495#2/r5] عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd explains al-māʿūn as ordinary items people lend one another, such as an axe, cooking pot, and bucket.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p495#2/r6] أصحاب رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Ibn Masʿūd reports that the Prophet's Companions would say that al-māʿūn meant a bucket, axe, and cooking pot, necessities that people lend.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r1] عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | A report says every good deed is charity, and that in the Prophet's time they counted lending a bucket or pot as al-māʿūn.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r10] الزهري | reports | Al-Zuhrī says that in Quraysh usage al-māʿūn means wealth.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r11] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A strange report from Qurra ibn Damʿūs says the Prophet instructed delegates not to withhold al-māʿūn, explaining it as stone, iron, and water, including cooking pots and axes.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r2] عبد الله بن مسعود | reports | Ibn Masʿūd identifies al-māʿūn as loaned household goods: pots, scales, and buckets.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and other early authorities explain al-māʿūn as household belongings lent to others.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r4] ابن عباس | reports | A separate report from Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the phrase as meaning that the owners have not yet come for their belongings.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r5] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās reports disagreement: some say al-māʿūn is zakāt, some obedience, and others lending items.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r6] علي | reports | ʿAlī is also reported to explain al-māʿūn as refusing people an axe, cooking pot, or bucket.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r7] عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima says the head of al-māʿūn is zakāt, while its least forms include a sieve, bucket, and needle.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r8] author | prefers | Ibn Kathīr approves ʿIkrima's synthesis because it includes all reported views: the shared idea is refusing help through wealth or useful service.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p496/r9] محمد بن كعب | reports | Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb explains al-māʿūn as ordinary kindness, supported by the report that every good deed is charity.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p497/r1] author | rejects | Ibn Kathīr calls the preceding report extremely strange and its Prophetic attribution rejected, noting an unknown narrator.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p497/r2] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | A report cited from Ibn al-Athīr's Companion biography of ʿAlī al-Numayrī says a Muslim should greet another, return a better greeting, and not withhold al-māʿūn.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p497/r3] رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | In the same report, the Prophet explains al-māʿūn as stone, iron, and similar useful things.
