[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#2/r1] author | holds | The interrogative آلآن rebukes Pharaoh for professing faith only after despairing of life; an omitted verb is estimated after the adverb to direct the reproach at the delay.

[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#2/r3] أبو أمامة | reports | A report from Abū Umāma via Abū al-Shaykh says Gabriel hated Pharaoh, feared he might utter the confession and escape, put mud into his mouth, and that God became angrier than Gabriel.

[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#2/r5] ابن عباس | reports | A report transmitted by several collectors and authenticated by al-Tirmidhī and al-Ḥākim has Gabriel say that he put sea mud into Pharaoh's mouth lest mercy reach him.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#3/r1] إرشاد العقل السليم | reports | Irshād construes mercy as worldly rescue, which need not entail valid faith, as in the case of Jonah's people; Gabriel merely closed off a remote possibility. Al-Ālūsī finds this reading unlikely. | mentions 10:98
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#3/r2] أبو هريرة | reports | A report from Abū Hurayra says Gabriel covered Pharaoh with one hand and put mud in his mouth fearing God's mercy might reach him and God forgive him; al-Ālūsī says this denotes forgiveness, not earthly rescue.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#3/r3] بعض المحققين | reports | Another verifier explains Gabriel's act as anger and fear that repeated confession might, by extraordinary mercy, be accepted; he says approving one's own unbelief is unbelief, while disapproving another's is not necessarily so.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#3/r4] الطيبي | reports | Al-Ṭībī says sound transmitted reports should be accepted even when reason cannot explain them, with the limitation assigned to the reader.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#3/r5] author | holds | Al-Ālūsī counters that if a report contradicts clear reason or attributes an impossible defect to an infallible angel and admits no sound interpretation, doubt should fall on its transmitter rather than reason.
[ALUSI-FULL:v11p182#3/r6] author | holds | The phrase وقد عصيت قبل is a circumstantial clause intensifying the rebuke: Pharaoh delayed faith without excuse, while his corruption also harmed others through oppression and blocking Israel's way.
