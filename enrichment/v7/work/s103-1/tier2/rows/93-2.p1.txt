## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:93:2/r1] الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib says saja means to become still, as with settling sea waves, or slack as with an unfocused eye; the dead are figuratively ‘covered’ by a shroud.
[SAMIN-DURR:93:2/r2] الأعشى | reports | Al-A‘sha’s poem contrasts a heaving sea with a still one, witnessing saja as calm or stillness.
[SAMIN-DURR:93:2/r3] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra’ says saja means ‘grew dark’; Ibn al-A‘rabi intensifies this to ‘became very dark’, with a poem describing the moonlit, dark night.
[SAMIN-DURR:93:2/r4] author | reports | Other views take saja as receding, or as its opposite; the author notes the ambiguity without choosing between them.
[SAMIN-DURR:93:2/r5] author | holds | The root is one of the verbs with wāw; its final vowel is inclined for the rhyme, as with duha, which also comes from a wāw verb.
