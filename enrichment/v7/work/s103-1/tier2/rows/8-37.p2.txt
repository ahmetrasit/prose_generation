[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p317/r1] ابن القشيري | reports | Ibn al-Qushayr says God distinguishes the groups by delaying punishment of this community until the Resurrection, allowing believers to emerge from unbelievers' descendants; on this view the distinction occurs in worldly history.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p317/r3] ابن عطية | reports | On the wealth interpretation, Ibn ʿAṭiyya attaches the purpose lām to يغلبون; Zamakhshari instead attaches it to the later promise that spending will become regret.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:8:37#2/r1] author | holds | فيركمه coordinates with the preceding subjunctive; ركم means piling something over something until it forms a heap, like sand or clouds.

[SAMIN-DURR:8:37#2/r3] author | holds | المرتكم can mean the main road, named for the crowding of passersby and their traces.
[SAMIN-DURR:8:37#2/r4] author | reports | جميعا is either a circumstantial accusative or, according to some grammarians, an emphatic.
[SAMIN-DURR:8:37/r1] author | holds | The purpose lam in ليميز attaches to يحشرون; the earlier dispute about the verb's reading was treated under آل عمران.
[SAMIN-DURR:8:37/r2] author | reports | يجعل may be causative and take two objects, or mean casting and take one; in either parse بعضه is partitive apposition.
[SAMIN-DURR:8:37/r3] author | reports | With causative يجعل, على بعض is the second object; with the casting sense it completes the verb, like casting belongings one over another.
[SAMIN-DURR:8:37/r4] أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa instead allows على بعض to be a circumstantial phrase meaning the vile things are piled with some above others.

## IBNKATHIR-FULL: Ibn Kathīr, d. 774 AH
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p53/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Ishaq’s account, citing Ibn Abbas, says Quraysh traders funded war after Badr to avenge relatives killed there, and the spending passage was revealed about them.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p53/r2] مجاهد وسعيد بن جبير والحكم بن عتيبة وقتادة والسدي وابن أبزى | reports | Mujahid and others say the passage concerned Abu Sufyan’s spending to fight the Prophet at Uhud; al-Dahhak says it concerned the people of Badr.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p53/r3] author | holds | Although the occasion may be specific, the warning applies generally to disbelievers spending to block truth; their money becomes regret, they are defeated and humiliated in this world, and face lasting punishment in the Fire.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p54/r1] ابن عباس | reports | The wicked-versus-good distinction separates the people of felicity from the people of misery.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p54/r2] السدي | reports | The distinction is between believer and unbeliever.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p54/r3] author | holds | One possible distinction occurs in the Hereafter, separating associators from their partners and separating groups at resurrection. | mentions 10:28, 30:14, 30:43, 36:59
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p54/r4] author | holds | Alternatively, this distinction appears in worldly conduct, visible to believers, as obedience in fighting enemies versus disobediently hanging back. | mentions 3:166, 3:167, 3:179, 3:142
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p54/r5] author | holds | On the worldly reading, the causal lām explains why God enables unbelievers to spend wealth obstructing the path: the ordeal distinguishes people.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v4p54/r6] author | holds | God gathers the wicked together, piling them up as rakkama means collecting things upon one another, as with piled clouds; they are the losers in both worlds. | mentions 24:43
