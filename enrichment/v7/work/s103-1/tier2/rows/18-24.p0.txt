## YAHYA-SALLAM: Yaḥyā b. Sallām, d. 200 AH
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p178#2/r2] author | holds | A future intention must be qualified by God's will; when the Prophet promised to answer the Jews the next day without qualifying it, the verse was revealed, and remembering later requires saying 'if God wills'.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p178#2/r3] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says a person who remembers to qualify an oath but does not do so has no valid exception; someone who forgot has one while still in that gathering, even after speaking.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p178#2/r4] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says that adding 'if God wills' after an oath means there is no expiation due.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p178#2/r5] رسول الله | reports | The Prophet says someone who swears and then says 'if God wills' may either carry out the oath or leave it.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p178#2/r6] إبراهيم | reports | Ibrāhīm says an inward exception does not count until spoken aloud; Yaḥyā endorses this gloss.
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p180/r3] الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan says one who swears but later sees a better course should take it and expiate the oath, whether by release or manumission.

[TAB-FULL:v15p226/r2] أبو العالية | reports | Abū al-ʿĀliya says that when one remembers the omitted exception, one should then say it.
[TAB-FULL:v15p226/r3] الحسن | reports | A report attributed to al-Ḥasan says that when one remembers not saying God willing, one should say it then.
[TAB-FULL:v15p226/r4] عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima offers another interpretation: remember your Lord when you disobey.
[TAB-FULL:v15p226/r5] author | prefers | Al-Ṭabarī prefers remember your Lord when you have left off remembering Him, since nisyān in Arabic can mean leaving something.
[TAB-FULL:v15p226/r6] author | holds | One may add the exception even after breaking the oath to remove the sin of omission, but the expiation remains due unless the exception was joined to the original oath.
[TAB-FULL:v15p226/r7] author | holds | Reports allowing an exception after a year, a month, or the same sitting all address relief from blame after delay; none says that delayed exception erases an expiation for breaking the oath.

## SULAMI: al-Sulamī, d. 412 AH
