## THALABI: al-Thaʿlabī, d. 427 AH
[THALABI:v5p160#2/r1] النبي | reports | Abu Hurayra transmits that on Judgment Day the first people summoned include a Quran reciter, a wealthy man, and one killed in battle, each questioned about what he did with his gifts.
[THALABI:v5p160#2/r2] النبي | reports | The reciter, donor, and fighter are each condemned as seeking fame rather than God; the Prophet says these three are the first fuel for Hell.
[THALABI:v5p160#2/r3] معاوية | reports | When Shuqayy related Abu Hurayra’s report, Muʿawiya feared what this meant for others, wept, and affirmed the verse about worldly seekers’ ruined deeds. | mentions 11:15, 11:16

## WAHIDI-BASIT: al-Wāḥidī, d. 468 AH
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r10] ابن عباس | reports | In al-Kalbī's report through Abū Ṣāliḥ, the passage was revealed about people of the qibla.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r11] مجاهد | reports | The people described are those who act for show.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r5] ابن عباس | reports | In ʿAṭāʾ's report, those seeking immediate worldly life reject resurrection, reward, and punishment; this makes the passage about unbelievers.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r6] قتادة | reports | One whose sole concern is worldly life receives recompense for good deeds here, then reaches the afterlife without a good deed to reward; believers are rewarded in both.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r7] author | reports | Some exegetes apply the verses to unbelievers because of the following verse: believers seek both worlds, with the afterlife their greater aim. | mentions 11:16
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r8] author | reports | On this reading, an unbeliever's good acts such as feeding, clothing, and defending oppressed Muslims are repaid through worldly prosperity and comforts.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p366/r9] سعيد بن جبير | reports | The unbelievers receive their good deeds' reward in this world and have only the Fire in the afterlife, where they regret having no good deed.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r1] الضحاك | reports | A believer who performs a righteous act without God-consciousness is given its reward in this world.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r2] الفراء | prefers | Al-Farrāʾ adopts the view that a person of the qibla who intends worldly reward is paid fully in this life, without any shortfall.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r3] أبو هريرة | reports | The Prophet describes ostentatious reciters, spenders, and fighters, who acted to earn reputations, as the first people whose Fire is kindled on Judgment Day.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r4] معاوية | reports | After hearing the report, Muʿāwiya wept, affirmed that God and His Messenger spoke truth, and recited these two verses.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r5] ابن الأنباري | reports | The warning concerns Muslims whose good acts serve worldly aims without thought of the afterlife; they receive worldly recompense but face punishment if they did not seek God's approval and reward.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r6] ابن الأنباري | reports | Although the wording appears to entail eternal punishment for believers, a believer who dies in faith is not eternally in Hell.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r7] ابن الأنباري | reports | The verse's wording appears to say that ostentation and worldly intent without seeking afterlife reward invalidate faith at death, since “their deeds were null” includes faith and its branches.
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p368/r8] ابن الأنباري | reports | People of monotheism do not remain in Hell forever; they are burned for prior sins and then enter Paradise.

## JISHUMI: al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494 AH
[JISHUMI:v5p3468#2/r8] author | holds | Because pursuit of worldly adornment can deter people from religion, those who seek only this life receive the full recompense of their deeds in it, without shortchanging; in the hereafter they have only the Fire and their deeds are null.
[JISHUMI:v5p3468#2/r9] الأصم | reports | Al-Aṣamm says the verse concerns polytheists who do good deeds such as maintaining kinship or avoiding injustice without believing in resurrection; their deeds are rejected because they were not done in worship of God, yet they receive their full worldly provision and lifespan.

## BAGHAWI-FULL: al-Baghawī, d. 516 AH

[BAGHAWI-FULL:v4p166/r2] author | reports | The Prophet calls showiness the minor shirk he most fears for his community.
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v4p166/r3] author | reports | Another view applies the passage to disbelievers; a believer’s dominant aim is the Hereafter, so good deeds can earn reward in both lives.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p155#2/r4] قتادة والضحاك | reports | They take the universal wording about seeking worldly life as specifically referring to disbelievers.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p155#2/r5] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid includes disbelievers and believers who act for show; Muawiya adopted this after Shafi ibn Mati al-Asbahi relayed the Prophetic report about a charity-giver, slain fighter, and Qur'an-reciter whose ostentation makes them first fuel for Hell, then wept and cited the passage.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p155#2/r6] author | holds | For the disbeliever reading, يريد means to intend and aim: worldly life is the person's sole goal, without belief in an afterlife; God repays good deeds with worldly favors, though provision may be narrow or ample, and their afterlife is only Fire.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p156/r1] author | prefers | Ibn Atiyya prefers the disbeliever interpretation because the preceding context concerns those opposing the Qur'an.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p156/r2] ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير | reports | On the view that the passage includes sinful believers, يريد means love, prefer, and choose; God rewards good deeds not done for Him with worldly favors, while 'only Fire' means it is what they deserve, though God may forgive them.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p156/r3] أنس بن مالك | reports | Anas says the verses concern the People of the Book; Ibn Atiyya says they are included as disbelievers, not the exclusive referent.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p156/r6] author | holds | حبط means to become void or fall away, used of corrupted deeds; the Prophet's phrase يقتل حبطا أو يلم illustrates the usage.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p156/r7] author | holds | The pronoun in فيها first refers to worldly life and in its third occurrence to the afterlife; alternatives make all three refer to this world or the second to deeds.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p156/r8] الجمهور | reports | The majority read وباطل in the nominative; Ubay and Ibn Masud read وباطلا in the accusative, attested in four codices, with يعملون governing it and ما treated as extra.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p156/r9] author | holds | باطل is anything whose nature prevents it from attaining a final end such as reward.
