## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:18:10-12/r7] author | holds | God made them sleep for many counted years, answering their prayer and blocking sound from reaching them; this shows they remained alive and slept safely and peacefully.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p111/r2] وهب بن منبه | reports | Wahb says a shepherd opened the cave to shelter his flock from rain, and God restored the youths' souls the next morning.

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v21p70/r14] author | holds | “We struck upon their ears” means God put them to sleep by placing a barrier that kept awakening sounds from reaching their hearing; “barrier” is omitted from the wording.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p70/r15] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj says describing countable things with “numbered” implies many, because small numbers need no enumeration.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p71/r1] الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj treat adadan as an adjective of years: years having a count, with either an omitted noun or the object named by its verbal noun.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p71/r2] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjāj also allows adadan to be an accusative cognate object, meaning they were counted with counting.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p73/r2] author | holds | The youths remained alive and unharmed through 309 years of sleep, shielded from the sun; Rāzī treats this as a karāma. | mentions 18:17, 18:18
[RAZI-FULL:v21p73/r4] القاضي | reports | Al-Qāḍī objects that a prophet must have been present in that era for the extraordinary sleep to count as a prophetic miracle.
[RAZI-FULL:v21p73/r5] author | rejects | Rāzī replies that the sleep itself was ordinary, and its truth could not be verified until the exact sleepers were recognized after 309 years; therefore it cannot be a prophet’s miracle but is a gift to saints.

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r1] author | holds | The expression describes God putting the youths to sleep and is a notably eloquent Qur'anic phrase that challenged Arab eloquence.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r10] author | reports | Some take the following statement of 300 years plus nine as specifying the duration mentioned here. | mentions 18:25
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r2] الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj explains the phrase as preventing them from hearing, since a sleeping person wakes when he hears.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r3] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas says their ears were blocked against incoming sounds by sleep.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r4] author | reports | A further interpretation says God answered their prayer, diverted their people's harm, and made them sleep; the readings converge in meaning.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r5] قطرب | reports | Qutrub compares the idiom to striking a subject's hand to stop wrongdoing or a slave's hand to stop his trading activity.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r6] الأسود بن يعفر | reports | Al-Aswad's verse uses the phrase 'struck the earth over me' to witness this idiom; he was blind.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r7] author | holds | Ears are singled out because sound most often disrupts sleep, and sleep deepens when hearing is inactive; a hadith describes Satan urinating in the ear of a long sleeper.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r8] author | holds | Adadan modifies years as counted; counting conveys a long duration since a small number needs no enumeration. It can also be an accusative of verbal noun, per Abu Ubayda.
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:11/r9] أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu Ubayda parses adadan as an accusative cognate object.

## NASAFI-FULL: al-Nasafī, d. 710 AH
