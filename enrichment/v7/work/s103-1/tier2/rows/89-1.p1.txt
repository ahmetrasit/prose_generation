## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:89:1/r1] ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbari identifies ‘inna rabbaka la-bil-mirsad’ as the stated answer to the oath by dawn. | mentions 89:14
[SAMIN-DURR:89:1/r2] author | reports | Alternatively, the oath’s answer is omitted but understood as a promise to requite each person, indicated by the enumeration of what happened to earlier peoples.
[SAMIN-DURR:89:1/r3] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari supplies ‘He will surely punish’, supported by the account of former peoples being struck down. | mentions 89:6, 89:13
[SAMIN-DURR:89:1/r4] الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh infers the omitted answer from the preceding sura’s ending: people return to God, who holds them to account. | mentions 88:25, 88:26
[SAMIN-DURR:89:4/r2] author | reports | An omitted noun may be supplied, making the oath by the dawn prayer or by the Lord of dawn.

[SAMIN-DURR:89:4/r4] ابن خالويه | reports | Ibn Khalawayh calls the poetic nunation ‘tanwin al-tarannum’, including nunation at verse endings even for verbs and definite nouns; his quoted couplet illustrates it.
[SAMIN-DURR:89:4/r5] author | holds | Some grammarians object to the label ‘tanwin al-tarannum’ and prefer ‘tanwin tark al-tarannum’; another type, ‘tanwin al-ghali’, appears on a closed rhyme and is disputed, with different rules from ordinary nunation.
[SAMIN-DURR:89:4/r6] author | holds | The reader has treated the Qur’anic endings like rhyme endings; the author cites similar forms in al-Ahzab and al-Ra‘d as parallels. | mentions 33:66, 33:67, 33:10, 13:9
