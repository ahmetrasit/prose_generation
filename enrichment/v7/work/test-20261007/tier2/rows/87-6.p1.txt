## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r1] author | holds | The phrase means God will teach Muhammad the Qur’an so he memorizes it; Ibn Wahb transmits this interpretation from Malik.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r10] author | reports | Another reading makes forgetting mean abandoning practice: God protects him from leaving the teaching except where He abrogates its application.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r11] الجنيد | reports | Al-Junayd answers that you will not forget means you will not neglect acting on the teaching; Ibn Kaysan praises the answer.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r12] author | reports | The particle fa may be negation rather than prohibition; another view takes it as prohibition, with yāʾ retained for the verse ending.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r2] author | holds | This is glad tidings of a clear sign: an unlettered Prophet hears Gabriel recite revelation and retains it without forgetting.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r3] مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says Muhammad repeated revelation to himself for fear of forgetting, then was told he was spared that worry.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r4] مجاهد والكلبي | reports | They say Muhammad would begin an ayah before Gabriel finished it, fearing he would forget; thereafter he forgot nothing because God took care of it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r5] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra says the exception is a formal qualification with no intended forgotten item, as in the promise that the righteous will remain in Heaven unless God wills otherwise.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Abu Salih transmits Ibn Abbas’s report that Muhammad forgot nothing after the verse came down until he died, apart from what God willed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r7] قتادة | reports | Saʿid transmits Qatadah’s report that the Messenger forgot nothing except what God willed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r8] author | reports | One explanation allows a temporary lapse followed by recollection; a report says Muhammad omitted an ayah in prayer, Ubay thought it abrogated, and Muhammad said he had forgotten it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r9] author | reports | Forget may mean that God causes Muhammad to forget what He abrogates; exception is then a kind of abrogation of recitation.

[QASHANI:87:6-10/r1] author | holds | The Prophet reads the universal Quran of realities written in his book of preparedness, the Quranic intellect, and recalls it without forgetting.
[QASHANI:87:6-10/r2] author | reports | The exception allows God to make the Prophet forget or lose awareness of something, reserving it for his praiseworthy station at resurrection.

## ABUHAYYAN-FULL: Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745 AH
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p454/r7] author | holds | The preceding sūra called revelation decisive speech, and this sūra identifies it with God's promise to recite it to the Prophet. | mentions 86:13
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r10] الفراء وجماعة | reports | Al-Farrāʾ and others treat the exception as a conventional formula, with nothing actually exempted.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r11] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī adopts that view: the exception denies forgetting altogether, using rare phrasing without intending a real exception.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r12] author | rejects | He rejects the empty-exception account as making God's exception no exception at all, unsuitable even in eloquent speech.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r13] author | rejects | He also rejects the weak proposal that lā is prohibitive and the alif remains only for rhyme.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r14] author | prefers | The clear sense is that the Prophet will not forget except through abrogation, forgetting to establish a practice, or temporary forgetting followed by recall; he is protected from forgetting what he must convey.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r2] الحسن وقتادة ومالك | reports | Al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, and Mālik take the promise as meaning the Prophet should not move his tongue hastily while revelation is coming. | mentions 75:16
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r3] author | holds | God promises to recite revelation and preserve it for the unlettered Prophet, making this a sign of prophethood.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r4] author | reports | An alternative takes the promise as recitation of the sūras and the negative as emphatic reassurance; it then warns against neglecting regular review.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r5] author | holds | The alif remains in falā tansā despite the jussive prohibition particle, to regularize the verse endings.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r6] الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما | reports | They take the exception as what God decreed to abrogate, removing both its recitation and ruling.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r7] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says God may make the Prophet forget something so that he can establish it as a practice.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r8] author | reports | Another view allows temporary forgetting followed by a reminder, as when the Prophet said ʿAbbād's recitation reminded him of verses in a sūra.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p456/r9] author | reports | A further view interprets not forgetting as not abandoning practice, except where God abrogates the ruling.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p457/r1] author | holds | The promise follows the command to glorify because glorification requires reciting revelation; it allays the Prophet's fear of forgetting while reserving forgetfulness for a divine purpose.

## SAMIN-DURR: al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756 AH
[SAMIN-DURR:87:6/r1] author | reports | One view takes 'do not forget' as a negative statement that the Prophet will not forget; another takes it as a prohibition with a lengthened alif.
