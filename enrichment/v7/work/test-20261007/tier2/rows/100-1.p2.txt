[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p526/r1] author | holds | Al-ʿādiyāt describes swift runners, while exegetes dispute what is running; ḍabḥ is a loud sound during strenuous running, distinct from ordinary animal calls.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p526/r3] أبو حنيفة | reports | Abū Ḥanīfa's verse about a bow uses ḍabḥ for the bow's sound, likened to a fox.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p526/r4] author | holds | The word may originally describe a fox and be transferred to horses; it is linked to fire changing a color without fully burning it, or a slight darkening.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p526/r5] أبو عبيدة | reports | Abū ʿUbayda equates ḍabḥ and ḍabʿ with running very fast.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p526/r6] المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad derives ḍabḥ from the horse's quick gait.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p527/r10] author | holds | Ḍabḥan is parsed as an omitted cognate verb, a circumstantial expression, or—following Abū ʿUbayda's gloss of running—as a cognate accusative.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p527/r11] الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī allows ḍabḥan to modify al-ʿādiyāt as if the phrase meant panting runners; Abū Ḥayyān objects that panting and running are not identical.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p527/r6] الجمهور من أهل التفسير واللغة | reports | The majority of exegetes and linguists identify the runners as horses racing in God's way and panting as they run; ʿAntara's verse illustrates their panting.
