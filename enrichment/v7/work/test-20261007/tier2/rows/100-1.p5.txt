:100:1/r1] author | holds | The oath depicts horses charging the enemy in battle, then swears by them because after humans they were the most important element of old warfare. It points to God as the giver of these useful, much-loved possessions and uses what people valued to stress the truth and importance of the following message.

## STUDYQURAN: Seyyed Hossein Nasr (ed.-in-chief), Caner K. Dagli, Maria Massi Dakake, Joseph E. B. Lumbard, Mohammed Rustom
[STUDYQURAN:100:1/r1] al-Bayḍāwī | reports | Al-Bayḍāwī allegorizes the chargers as souls transmitting holiness, kindling spiritual knowledge, attacking caprice, raising desire for God, and entering the highest Garden. | mentions 83:18, 83:19
[STUDYQURAN:100:1/r2] author | holds | The oaths can refer to battle horses or pilgrimage camels, and to both since each performs a function worthy of God's oath.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r1] most commentators | reports | Most commentators date the surah early Makkan, likely after al-Qāriʿah; others place it early Madinan.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r2] author | holds | The Makkan attribution draws on stylistic similarity; the Madinan view draws on some interpretations of the subject.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r3] author | reports | One occasion report describes al-Mundhir ibn ʿAmr's expedition to Banū Kinānah: after news was delayed and hypocrites said the men had been killed, the verse announced its horses.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r4] others | reports | Others connect the verses to Badr; a report attributed to ʿAlī says only two horses were there and the other mounts were camels.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r5] ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib | reports | Another account attributed to ʿAlī sees the oaths as pilgrims rushing from ʿArafāt to Muzdalifah and Minā.
[STUDYQURAN:100:intro/r6] author | holds | The first five verses swear by chargers; 6–8 discuss human ingratitude, while 9–11 warn of judgment and disclosure of hearts.
