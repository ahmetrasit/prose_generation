## KASHSHAF-FULL: al-Zamakhsharī, d. 538 AH
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p793/r1] author | holds | The oath is by war horses running and panting; ḍabḥ is the sound of their breathing, which Ibn Abbas imitated as “aḥ aḥ.”
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p793/r11] ابن عباس | reports | A questioner repeated Ibn Abbas’s horse interpretation to Ali, who rebuked him: at Badr the Muslims had only two horses.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p793/r12] علي | reports | Ali argues that Badr had only two horses, one belonging to al-Zubayr and one to al-Miqdad, and interprets the oath as pilgrimage camels traveling between the rites.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p793/r13] author | reports | If the report is sound, applying ḍabḥ to camels is a metaphor, like borrowing animal terms for human or other bodies; another view restricts ḍabḥ to horses, dogs, and foxes.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p793/r14] author | reports | A further, expressly insecure explanation equates ḍabḥ with ḍabʿ, the camel’s stretching of its forelegs while moving.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p793/r2] عنترة | reports | Antarah’s line depicts horses panting as they struggle in the pools of death, serving as poetic evidence for ḍabḥ.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p793/r3] author | reports | Ḍabḥan can be a cognate accusative of an implied verb, modify the horses as “panting,” or function as a circumstantial accusative.
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p795/r8] النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | A report promises ten good deeds for reciting al-ʿĀdiyāt for every person who spent the night at Muzdalifah and witnessed Jamʿ.

## IBNATIYYA-FULL: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542 AH
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p513#2/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas, Qatada, Mujahid, and Ikrima identify the oath animals as horses running with their riders and making a sound; an expedition to Banu Kinana may have occasioned the surah.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p513#2/r2] author | reports | Another view takes the oath as horses generally, whose speed and strength have long made them conquer lands and kingdoms; good remains in their forelocks.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p513#2/r3] علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali, Ibn Masʿud, Ibrahim, and Ubayd ibn Umayr say the animals are camels, which make a ḍabḥ while running; Ali includes pilgrims' and Badr's camels.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p513#2/r4] علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali says the oath refers to pilgrims' camels running between Arafat and Muzdalifa, as well as Badr's camels, since that battle had very few horses.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p513#2/r5] author | holds | Ḍabḥ is a loud sound during intense running, distinct from neighing, roaring, and barking; it is an unusual animal sound.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p513#2/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas is reported to say that only horses and dogs make a ḍabḥ; the author rejects this as unsound because Arabs also apply the word to camels, snakes, owls, rabbits, foxes, echoes, and bows.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p513#2/r7] أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa's verse about a bow making a fox-like ḍabḥ in the hand supports the word's broader use beyond horses and dogs.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p513#2/r8] author | holds | The oath may concern warhorses, pilgrimage camels, or both.

## TABRISI: al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548 AH
[TABRISI:100:1-4/r1] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās and several Successors identify the chargers as horses running against unbelievers.
[TABRISI:100:1-4/r2] author | holds | For the horse view, dabḥ is their breath while running, not neighing or whinnying.
[TABRISI:100:1-4/r3] علي | reports | ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd, and al-Suddī identify the chargers as camels, including pilgrims' camels going from ʿArafāt to Muzdalifah and Minā.
[TABRISI:100:1-4/r4] صفية بنت عبد المطلب | reports | Ṣafiyyah's poem pictures chargers at Jamʿ with dust rising, supporting the camel view.
[TABRISI:100:1-4/r5] أبو صالح | reports | Abū Ṣāliḥ says ʿAlī's view is more authoritative than Ibn ʿAbbās's because his master knows better.
[TABRISI:100:1-4/r6] ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās argues horses raise dust with their hooves and pant; ʿAlī replies Badr had only two horses.
[TABRISI:100:1-4/r7] علي | reports | In the report from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿAlī rebukes Ibn ʿAbbās, says the chargers are pilgrimage camels, and Ibn ʿAbbās retracts.
[TABRISI:100:1/r12] مقاتل | reports | Muqātil says the delayed Kinanah expedition under al-Mundhir ibn ʿAmr prompted the revelation announcing its horses after hypocrites said the men were killed.
[TABRISI:100:1/r13] أبو عبد الله | reports | A report from Abū ʿAbd Allāh connects the surah with ʿAlī's campaign at Dhāt al-Salāsil after prior commanders had returned.
[TABRISI:100:1/r5] author | holds | Dabḥ is horses' panting or strained breath; another view equates it to dabʿ, extending the foreleg in fast travel.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p206#2/r3] ابن عباس | reports | The other view identifies the oath as horses campaigning for God; it is attributed to Ibn Abbas, Hasan, Ata, Mujahid, and other early authorities and linguists.

[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p206#2/r5] ابن عباس | reports | In the expedition account, the horses snort through their nostrils, spark with their hooves on stones, attack at dawn, raise dust, and reach the enemy together.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p206#2/r6] مقاتل | reports | Muqatil says a force sent to two Banu Kinana clans under al-Mundhir ibn Amr was delayed; rumors by Jews and hypocrites made Muslims fear their relatives had died.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p206#2/r7] مقاتل | reports | The surah was revealed to report how the expedition had fared and to allay the fear stirred by the rumors.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p206#2/r8] الفراء | reports | Al-Farra defines ḍabḥ as the sound of horses' breaths when they run.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p206#2/r9] ابن قتيبة | reports | Ibn Qutayba says ḍabḥ is the sound in horses' throats when they run.
