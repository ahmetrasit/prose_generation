## meaning and promise
- 87:6/v001 God will teach the Prophet the Qur’an and preserve his recitation so he does not forget it. (Some specify Gabriel as the intermediary; al-Ṭabarī links preservation with the warning against haste in 75:16–17.) [BAGHAWI-FULL,ELMALILI,MAWARDI-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,QUSHAYRI,STUDYQURAN,TAB,WAHIDI-BASIT,WAHIDI-QT,WAHIDI-WAJIZ; مالك بن أنس: NAHHAS; ابن جرير: IBNKATHIR-FULL+; Asad: ASAD-NOTES]
- 87:6/v002 God gathers the Qur’an in the Prophet’s heart, preserving it there and enabling its recitation on his tongue. (Muqātil also qualifies this preservation by what God wills to abrogate and replace.) [مقاتل: IBNJAWZI-ZAD,MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,QUSHAYRI,QUTB-ZILAL; كشف الأسرار: BURSEVI]
- 87:6/v003 God makes the Prophet a reader by inspiring recitation, so he retains what he reads. (Al-Bayḍāwī offers this alongside recitation through Gabriel.) [BAYDAWI-FULL,ELMALILI,WAHIDI-BASIT; الواحدي: RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/v004 Gabriel delivers and teaches revelation to the previously unlettered Prophet, who recites it from memory without needing a written text. (Al-Zajjāj says he teaches his Companions without repetition; Elmalılı identifies Gabriel as the Trustworthy and Holy Spirit and cites 42:52.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,ELMALILI,KASHSHAF,QURTUBI-FULL,ZAJJAJ; أبو إسحاق: WAHIDI-BASIT; قتادة: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 87:6/v005 God enables the Prophet to recite revelation exactly as given, without addition, omission, or distortion through forgetfulness. (Ibn ʿĀshūr emphasizes full delivery of every recited revelation.) [IBNASHUR-FULL,TABATABAI]
- 87:6/v006 The promise rests on the Prophet’s unusually strong, exact memory despite his being unlettered. (The sources also take this retention as a sign of prophethood.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,ELMALILI]
- 87:6/v007 The Prophet will not forget the truth that he hears. (Al-Rāghib’s formulation.) [الراغب: BURSEVI]
- 87:6/v008 The promise concerns retention of revealed law and sūras and reinforces the duty to keep them in mind. (This account distinguishes the verse from the haste passage in al-Qiyāmah.) [IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 87:6/v009 God’s guarantee of preservation continues as the growing Qur’an is revealed, with recitation preparing for the central promise not to forget. (Islāḥī assigns collection, recital, following, and explanation to God.) [IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:6/v010 The promise may concern recitation of the sūras, with the negative giving emphatic reassurance and a warning against neglecting regular review. (An alternative reported by Abū Ḥayyān.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## how recitation occurs
- 87:6/v011 The verb for making someone recite means teaching and making that person a reciter; it is also glossed as keeping the Qur’an in the ear. (The second gloss is attributed to Tāj al-maṣādir.) [BURSEVI]
- 87:6/v012 Iqraʾ can mean learning a reading by listening so errors can be corrected; qirāʾah is recitation related to gathering letters. (Al-Majmaʿ likewise defines making someone recite through listening and correction.) [JISHUMI; المجمع: TABATABAI]
- 87:6/v013 Reading is the joining of letters and words in measured recitation, rather than every gathering or the utterance of a single letter. (Definition attributed to al-Mufradāt.) [المفردات: TABATABAI]
- 87:6/v014 God’s teaching the Prophet to recite does not imply correcting a misreading: no such correction or forgetting of revelation is known. (This rejects the corrective-listening sense as an explanation here.) [TABATABAI-]
- 87:6/v015 Gabriel repeats the revelation until the Prophet memorizes it. (One explanation of how the promise is fulfilled.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/v016 God strengthens the Prophet so that he memorizes revelation after hearing it once. (An alternative to repeated recitation by Gabriel.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/v017 The Prophet could read writing but could not write, becoming a reader through inspiration without instruction. (Attributed to Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq.) [جعفر الصادق: ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:6/v018 The inspired-reader interpretation conflicts with transmitted early explanations and with the causal fāʾ. (Al-Ālūsī’s rejection of that interpretation.) [ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:6/v019 The verse connects the Qur’an’s comprehensive knowledge with God making it easy to act on it. (A third explanation of the promise.) [RAZI-FULL]

## occasion and reports
- 87:6/v020 The Prophet rehearsed revelation inwardly or privately because he feared forgetting it, and the promise relieved that fear. (Reported from Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās; Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ transmits a similar account.) [مجاهد: IBNABIHATIM,MUJAHID,NAHHAS,QURTUBI-FULL,TAB; ابن عباس: DURR-FULL,MAWARDI-FULL; سعد بن أبي وقاص: DURR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:6/v021 Fearing forgetfulness, the Prophet began repeating or moving his tongue to recite revelation before Gabriel finished; God reassured him, and reports say he thereafter forgot nothing. (Sources attribute this to Mujāhid, al-Kalbī, Muqātil, or Ibn ʿAbbās; Ibn ʿĀshūr says he subsequently recited exactly as Gabriel did.) [مجاهد: THALABI,WAHIDI-BASIT; قيل: JISHUMI; مجاهد والكلبي: BAGHAWI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL; مجاهد ومقاتل والكلبي: RAZI-FULL; ابن عباس: IBNASHUR-FULL,SUYUTI-LUBAB; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,TABATABAI; the Prophet: STUDYQURAN]
- 87:6/v022 The chain of the report from Ibn ʿAbbās about reciting before Gabriel finished includes Juwaybir, judged very weak. (Al-Suyūṭī’s assessment of that report.) [SUYUTI-LUBAB]
- 87:6/v023 The Prophet struggled to retain revelation and moved his lips while Gabriel recited, but later recited it exactly as Gabriel had. (Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the linked report as authentic and cites 75:16–18.) [ابن عباس: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v024 The Prophet’s eagerness arose from love and responsibility for each letter of revelation. (Quṭb explains the emotional burden behind his repetition.) [QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:6/v025 The Prophet eagerly repeated each word and wanted the whole revelation at once; God told him not to hurry because revelation follows a wise timetable. (The study Qur’an also stresses that revelation is given in due time.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR,STUDYQURAN]

## links to other verses
- 87:6/v026 The promise recalls the commands in 20:114 and 75:16–19 not to hasten recitation while revelation arrives. (Al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, and Mālik are cited for the connection; the verse stops haste while assuring preservation.) [KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,RAZI-FULL-,STUDYQURAN,TAB,WAHIDI-BASIT; الحسن: IBNATIYYA-FULL; الحسن وقتادة ومالك: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 87:6/v027 The sequence may be 87:6 first, then 75:16–19, then 20:114, all addressing haste in receiving revelation. (Ibn ʿĀshūr argues al-Qiyāmah was revealed later; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the full order plausible.) [IBNASHUR-FULL,TABATABAI+]
- 87:6/v028 God’s promise to preserve the reminder in 15:9 supports this assurance. (Al-Zajjāj’s supporting cross-reference.) [ZAJJAJ]
- 87:6/v029 For qaraʾa, compare 96:1; an imperative followed by a future first-person plural revelation promise also appears in 73:2–5. (A literary cross-reference.) [CORPUSCORANICUM]

## readings and grammar
- 87:6/v030 Falā tansā is a declarative promise that the Prophet will not forget, rather than a prohibition. (Al-Naḥḥās prefers this on exegetical grounds; al-Rāzī likens it to promising clothing so someone will not be naked; Ibn ʿĀshūr says declarative form functions as a guarantee.) [IBNASHUR-FULL,JISHUMI,NAHHAS,NAHHAS+,QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,RAZI-FULL+,SAMIN-DURR]
- 87:6/v031 Falā tansā can be a jussive prohibition against forgetting, with the final alif retained or lengthened for the verse ending. (Al-Bayḍāwī compares the form al-sabīlā in 33:67; Ibn Kathīr’s version excepts abrogated recitation.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,JISHUMI,QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; قيل: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 87:6/v032 The final yāʾ or alif in tansā is written to match the verse endings. (Al-Marzūqī says an alif deleted in inflection is restored when lengthened at a pause.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL; الإمام المرزوقي: ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:6/v033 A prohibition on forgetting is strained because forgetting lies outside a person’s control. (Al-Rāzī additionally says it weakens divine glad tidings, requires extra constructions, and conflicts with 75:16; al-Ālūsī rejects figurative neglect and orthographic defenses.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL-,ALUSI-FULL-,IBNASHUR-FULL,NAHHAS,RAZI-FULL-; مكي: SAMIN-DURR]
- 87:6/v034 A prohibition can reasonably address avoiding the causes of forgetting, so involuntariness does not settle the grammar. (Al-Samīn’s reply to Makkī.) [SAMIN-DURR-]
- 87:6/v035 On a prohibitive reading, the Prophet is told not to neglect recitation and repetition. (This reading adds an alif to fit the verse ending.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/v036 The future marker sīn either strengthens the promise or marks recitation now and continuing through future revelation. (Ibn ʿĀshūr says it confirms a coming act and signals renewal alongside an act already underway.) [ALUSI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v037 The switch to God’s first-person plural marks a direct, majestic promise to the Prophet. (Corpus Coranicum calls the shift iltifāt; Abdel Haleem says it heightens and dramatizes authority without requiring redaction; this is the sūra’s first explicit divine speaker and promises further revelation.) [CORPUSCORANICUM,IBNASHUR-FULL; Abdel Haleem: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:6/v038 Recitation is attributed to God because He creates and commands the words, with Gabriel acting as His intermediary. (Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this a rational attribution; al-Ālūsī a metonymy.) [ALUSI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v039 The object of ‘God wills’ is omitted by ordinary Arabic usage; the exception covers whatever He chooses the Prophet to forget. (Bursevī describes the exception as taken from the broadest class of things recited.) [BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL; Asad: ASAD-NOTES]

## exception: abrogation
- 87:6/v040 The exception covers revelation God abrogates and causes to leave memory or recitation. (Some specify both recitation and ruling, others only recitation; Muqātil mentions replacement with better, Bursevī erasure from pages and hearts, and Islāḥī temporary directives whose ending God announces.) [مقاتل: MUQATIL,RAZI-FULL; قائلو هذه المقالة: TAB; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,QURTUBI-FULL,TAB+; الحسن وقتادة: MAWARDI-FULL; الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 87:6/v041 An abrogated verse ceases to be recited or used in prayer and eventually leaves memory. (Al-Rāzī attributes the first formulation to Muqātil.) [مقاتل: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v042 The exception covers what God abrogates, removing both the recitation and its ruling. (An explicit twofold account of abrogation.) [قائلو هذه المقالة: TAB; الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 87:6/v043 Abrogated recitation is forgotten because God has the Prophet and community stop reciting it. (Bursevī adds erasure from pages and hearts.) [BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v044 A revealed stoning verse and a warning against disowning one’s fathers are reported as examples of material once revealed. (Report attributed to ʿUmar in the discussion of abrogated recitation.) [عمر: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## exception: actual forgetting
- 87:6/v045 God may make the Prophet forget what He wills; reports say he forgot nothing beyond that exception. (Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās are among the reported speakers; Ibn ʿAbbās’s report extends the claim through the Prophet’s death.) [قتادة: ABDURRAZZAQ,IBNKATHIR-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,TAB; ابن عباس: QURTUBI-FULL; Asad: ASAD-NOTES]
- 87:6/v046 The exception may permit a temporary ordinary lapse followed by recollection through a reminder. (Some call such lapses rare; this differs from permanent loss through abrogation.) [الزجاج: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ABUHAYYAN-FULL+,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:6/v047 The Prophet omitted a verse during prayer; Ubayy asked if it had been abrogated, and the Prophet answered that he had forgotten it. (Bursevī adds that the answer followed Ubayy’s question.) [الزجاج: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,QURTUBI-FULL; عائشة: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v048 A man’s nighttime recitation reminded the Prophet of verses he had omitted or forgotten. (Abū Ḥayyān names the reciter ʿAbbād; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿĀʾishah’s report.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL; عائشة: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v049 God may make the Prophet forget something in order to establish it as a practice. (Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās.) [ابن عباس: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 87:6/v050 Forgetting may be limited to a rare lapse of etiquette or sunnah, not an obligation, because losing a required ruling would damage the law. (A boundary on permissible lapses.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/v051 The Prophet is protected from forgetting what he must convey, although other temporary or purposeful forgetting is possible. (Al-Qushayrī allows forgetting material outside the duty to convey before it is conveyed.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL+,QUSHAYRI]
- 87:6/v052 The exception refers to a revelation God delays, so the Prophet has not yet recited it. (Attributed to Ibn ʿĪsā.) [ابن عيسى: MAWARDI-FULL]

## exception: rhetorical
- 87:6/v053 The exception is conventional or rhetorical, with no particular forgotten revelation intended; the promise denies forgetting in practice. (Al-Farrāʾ compares conventional exceptions in speech and oaths and 11:107; al-Kalbī says nothing was forgotten after the verse; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also sees preserved power to cause forgetting.) [FARRA,RAZI-FULL,TABATABAI; بعض أهل العربية: TAB; الفراء: QURTUBI-FULL,THALABI; الكلبي: RAZI-FULL; الفراء وجماعة: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 87:6/v054 An empty exception makes God’s exception no exception at all and is unsuitable even in eloquent speech. (Abū Ḥayyān rejects the conventional-formula account.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 87:6/v055 The non-actual exception teaches saying ‘if God wills’ about a future event, even when its outcome is known. (Connected to 18:23–24.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/v056 The exception keeps the Prophet vigilant about whether any revealed portion could be excluded. (A proposed purpose for a non-actual exception.) [RAZI-FULL]

## exception: divine will
- 87:6/v057 The exception expresses God’s continuing power and free will to cause forgetting even after promising preservation, though He does not exercise it. (Al-Farrāʾ says preservation is God’s favor rather than the Prophet’s own power; Quṭb extends the principle to God’s lasting laws and promises.) [الفراء: RAZI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL,TABATABAI]

## exception: inward reading
- 87:6/v058 The Prophet reads the universal Qur’an of realities inscribed in his preparedness and Qur’anic intellect, remembering it without forgetting. (Al-Qāshānī’s inward reading.) [QASHANI]
- 87:6/v059 God may remove the Prophet’s awareness of something now and reserve it for his praiseworthy station at resurrection. (Al-Qāshānī’s reading of the exception.) [QASHANI]

## forgetting as practice
- 87:6/v060 Not forgetting means not neglecting or abandoning action on revelation’s commands and prohibitions. (Al-Junayd is repeatedly credited; al-Ālūsī reports this as a promise of compliance but rejects it as far-fetched.) [آخرون: TAB; الجنيد: IBNATIYYA-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,SULAMI,THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL-; Bazıları: ELMALILI]
- 87:6/v061 On the practice reading, God protects the Prophet from abandoning a teaching except where God abrogates its application. (Al-Māwardī’s variant allows what God permits as a concession and reads the statement as prohibiting shirk.) [آخرون: TAB; ABUHAYYAN-FULL,MAWARDI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:6/v062 Ibn Kaysān praised Junayd’s explanation that the Prophet would not forget to act on revelation and said Junayd deserved to preside. (Muḥammad al-Baghdādī reports Ibn Kaysān’s attendance at Junayd’s gathering and his question.) [محمد بن الحسن البغدادي: SULAMI; الجنيد: QURTUBI-FULL,SULAMI,THALABI; ابن كيسان النحوي: SULAMI]
- 87:6/v063 The practice reading is far-fetched as an interpretation of the promise. (Al-Ālūsī’s rejection.) [ALUSI-FULL-]

## forgetfulness and theology
- 87:6/v064 Forgetfulness is an act created by God. (Abū ʿAlī’s account.) [أبو علي: JISHUMI]
- 87:6/v065 Forgetfulness is a loss of ordinarily necessary knowledge, rather than a distinct entity or act. (Abū Hāshim’s alternative to Abū ʿAlī.) [أبو هاشم: JISHUMI]

## signs of prophethood
- 87:6/v066 An unlettered man retained and taught an extensive book without study, writing, or repeated rehearsal; this is a sign of prophethood. (Al-Rāzī stresses the book’s extent; al-Ālūsī adds the Qur’an’s inimitability and reports of unseen events; Kuran Yolu sees the sign continuing in communal memorization.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,ELMALILI,IBNATIYYA-FULL,KASHSHAF,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,ZAJJAJ; أبو إسحاق: WAHIDI-BASIT; قتادة: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 87:6/v067 Because an early Meccan promise of extraordinary recitation and retention was later fulfilled, the accurate prediction is itself a further miracle. (Al-Rāzī emphasizes foreknowledge; al-Biqāʿī distinguishes the recitation and fulfilled prediction.) [BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,ELMALILI,RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/v068 The Prophet’s protected memory is a proof of God’s power, also visible in the community’s ability to read and memorize the Qur’an. (Kuran Yolu’s extension to the community.) [KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## coherence
- 87:6/v069 The promise follows the command to glorify because complete glorification includes reciting God’s revelation, relieving the Prophet’s fear of forgetting. (Abū Ḥayyān says any forgetfulness reserved by the exception serves a divine purpose.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,RAZI-FULL]
- 87:6/v070 The previous sūra calls revelation decisive speech, and this sūra identifies that speech with God’s promise to recite it to the Prophet. (Linked to 86:13.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 87:6/v071 The sūra moves from general guidance to creatures to special guidance of the Messenger: receiving and preserving the Qur’an and guiding humanity. (Al-Biqāʿī lists God’s existence, attributes, creative power, guidance, and governance as the preceding themes.) [ALUSI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,BURSEVI]
- 87:6/v072 The sūra first establishes God’s essence, attributes, self-subsistence, and acts, leading from recognition of prophethood to obedience and countering ignorance and arrogant denial. (Al-Biqāʿī’s ordering of the argument.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:6/v073 The sūra affirms God’s freedom from deficiency, including haste, inability to resurrect, purposeless creation, false speech, and speech that humans can imitate. (Al-Biqāʿī’s account of the sūra’s purpose.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:6/v074 Sūrat al-Ṭāriq alludes generally to denials that this sūra explains in detail. (Al-Biqāʿī’s connection between the sūras.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:6/v075 Human flourishing requires an infallible exemplar, while love for God’s sake supports religion. (Al-Biqāʿī’s rationale for turning to the Messenger.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:6/v076 God repeatedly makes the unlettered Prophet a reciter, fully knowledgeable in life-giving revelation and able to spread and clarify it. (The Messenger’s role in the sūra’s movement.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:6/v077 Worldly ornaments and pleasures perish like withered debris; attachment to them distracts from special glorification and veils a person’s possible perfection. (Bursevī includes food and drink and describes glorification as stripping the divine essence of attachments.) [BURSEVI]
- 87:6/v078 The sūra steadies the Prophet with promised revelation, a gracious law, and a book that reminds the purified and God-fearing. (Ibn ʿĀshūr’s account of the sūra’s support for the mission.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v079 Revelation confirms earlier messengers’ scriptures and so comforts the Prophet amid pagan rejection. (Ibn ʿĀshūr’s connection to earlier scriptures.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v080 God’s fashioning and guidance equip the Prophet for his mission, including retention of revelation and an easy law. (Ibn ʿĀshūr connects 87:6 and 87:8 with the sūra’s earlier themes.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v081 The opening command to glorify gives general glad tidings; here God explicitly promises preservation of every recited revelation for full delivery. (Ibn ʿĀshūr’s account of the passage’s placement.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:6/v082 The Prophet’s preservation and obedience are assured because God knows both what is manifest and his hidden concern for revelation. (Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the guarantee to God’s knowledge.) [TABATABAI]
- 87:6/v083 The shift to third-person ‘He knows’ points to divine power as the basis for the exception. (Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī’s explanation of the shift.) [TABATABAI]
- 87:6/v084 God’s assurance comforts the Prophet and community because the faith originates with God, who guards it in the Prophet’s heart. (Quṭb’s account of the reassurance.) [QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:6/v085 The preceding sūra tells the Prophet that persistent deniers have only temporary respite and cannot escape God; this sūra turns primarily to him to ease his preaching fears. (Islāḥī sees the reassurance continuing across at least ten subsequent sūras.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:6/v086 Verses 6–8 promise revelation’s maturation and preservation, with only what God sets aside excepted, and ease after known hardships. (Islāḥī reads the three promises together.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:6/v087 The gradual order seen in human development and vegetation also governs prophethood: revelation will mature and the Prophet will overcome obstacles. (Islāḥī’s analogy with earlier natural images.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:6/v088 The Prophet’s eagerness for revelation came from needing support against opposition, guidance for the future, and answers to objections. (Islāḥī connects this need with 20:114 and 75:16.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
