## surah context
- 100:1/v001 The surah is titled al-ʿĀdiyāt, sometimes with its opening wāw in the title and sometimes without it. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 100:1/v002 The surah has eleven verses. [DURR-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,QUTB-ZILAL]
- 100:1/v003 The surah is Meccan; one early-revelation ordering places it fourteenth, after al-ʿAṣr and before al-Kawthar. (The notes attribute the Meccan view to several early authorities; Study Quran calls it the majority's early Meccan dating, probably after al-Qāriʿah.) [الحسن وعكرمة وقتادة: JISHUMI; DURR-FULL,IBNKATHIR,QUTB-ZILAL; ابن عباس: DURR-FULL; ابن مسعود: IBNASHUR-FULL; جابر بن زيد: IBNASHUR-FULL; most commentators: STUDYQURAN]
- 100:1/v004 The surah is Medinan, a dating associated with interpretations involving raids or jihad after the Hijra. (Al-Rāzī reports al-Kalbī's conditional argument; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the Medinan dating, while Study Quran ties dating partly to the chosen referent.) [الحسن وعكرمة وقتادة: JISHUMI; الكلبي: RAZI-FULL; أنس بن مالك: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+,STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI; most commentators: STUDYQURAN]

- 100:1/v007 The following Sūrat al-Qāriʿah is Meccan according to all authorities cited. [MAWARDI-FULL]

## occasion and reports
- 100:1/v008 A delayed expedition against Banū Kinānah under al-Mundhir ibn ʿAmr occasioned the surah: rumors, including claims by hypocrites that the force had perished, caused anxiety, and revelation announced its horses or successful raid. (Some say the force went against two Kinānah clans; al-Mundhir is identified as a leader at al-ʿAqabah. The reports differ in how much detail they give.) [مقاتل: IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,MUQATIL,TABRISI,THALABI,WAHIDI-ASBAB,WAHIDI-BASIT; الكلبي: RAZI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL,STUDYQURAN]
- 100:1/v009 An alternative delayed expedition went to Tihāmah; Jewish and hypocritical onlookers alarmed relatives, and Gabriel arrived Friday morning to report horses charging until they panted like foxes. [مقاتل: MUQATIL]
- 100:1/v010 The Prophet sent cavalry far away and received no news for a month; the surah then described their progress and the sound, sparks, dawn attack, dust, and arrival at the enemy. - 100:1/v012 The delayed-expedition report may be accepted despite its strangeness, but one version was called very strange and its chain contains the weak Ḥafṣ ibn Jamīʿ. (Ibn ʿĀshūr still prefers Medinan dating.) [ابن كثير: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## grammar and syntax
- 100:1/v055 The opening wāw is an oath particle that governs the genitive. [ELMALILI,NAHHAS]
- 100:1/v056 The opening wāw can instead be rendered as an address, 'Oh,' because the following scene is a parable. (Asad prefers this over 'Consider' or the common oath rendering 'By.') [Asad: ASAD-NOTES+]
- 100:1/v057 The oath wāw need not literally magnify its object; it may serve a further rhetorical purpose. (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says magnification has encouraged far-fetched interpretations and refers to her treatment of 93:1.) [BINTSHATI]
- 100:1/v058 Ḍabḥan is a cognate accusative or verbal noun governed by an implied verb, 'they pant a panting.' (Al-Samīn says the omitted verb is circumstantial to the runners; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers the verbal noun for an absolute, unrestricted sense.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BINTSHATI+,ELMALILI,KASHSHAF-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR,THALABI; الزجاج: RAZI-FULL]
- 100:1/v059 Ḍabḥan can be a circumstantial accusative, circumstantial verbal noun, or adjective describing the runners as panting. (One analysis paraphrases 'possessing panting'; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows manner of running when derived from ḍabʿ.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BINTSHATI+,IBNASHUR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; البصريون: RAZI-FULL]
- 100:1/v060 If ḍabḥ means running quickly like ḍabʿ, ḍabḥan can be a cognate accusative for the act of running. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; أبو عبيدة: ALUSI-FULL]
- 100:1/v061 Al-ʿādiyāt may govern ḍabḥan as though it meant 'the panting ones,' because panting accompanies running. [ELMALILI,KASHSHAF-FULL; الفراء: RAZI-FULL; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]
- 100:1/v062 The proposed equation of running with panting is challenged because the two are not identical; a reply says 'as though' asserts inseparable association and grammatical governance, not synonymy. (Al-Samīn rejects the objection to al-Zamakhsharī's formulation.) [الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; الشيخ: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 100:1/v063 Ḍabḥan can function as an adverb of time. [NAHHAS]
- 100:1/v064 The sequence of faʾs links successive actions; the sparks follow as a result of hard running and hooves striking stones. [BINTSHATI,ELMALILI,TABATABAI+]

## pilgrimage reading
- 100:1/v065 On the camel interpretation, the following fire-makers can be pilgrims who travel at night from ʿArafāt to Muzdalifah and light a fire at the Sacred Monument. [ELMALILI; علي بن أبي طالب: DURR-FULL]
- 100:1/v066 The oath by pilgrimage camels can recall their benefits in 88:17, contrast people's rebellion with God's service to them, or encourage Hajj; neglect of required Hajj is characterized as disbelief. (The note cites 3:97 for the Hajj point.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 100:1/v067 Pilgrimage camels are honored as animals whose service both pagans and Muslims esteem, making the oath persuasive to both. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## oath and purpose
- 100:1/v068 The oath by obedient horses supports the claim that humans are ungrateful to their Lord: exhausted horses spend their last energy fulfilling their master's task. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 100:1/v069 God's oath by horses honors their strength, speed, benefits in pursuit, escape, attack, retreat, and defense, while warning against keeping them merely for display. (Muḥammad ʿAbduh urges acquiring, training, and riding horses for defense and horsemanship; the Turkish commentary stresses horses' old military importance and God's gift.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,RAZI-FULL; ابن القيم: BINTSHATI; محمد عبده: BINTSHATI]
- 100:1/v070 A horse's back can bring honor and its belly wealth; used for pride or harm it earns sin, while used for God's cause with proper care it brings protection and reward. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 100:1/v071 A cited Ḥamāsī verse praises spending on a thoroughbred horse as a house sentinel and keeping a servant for it, showing the esteem accorded horses. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 100:1/v072 Horses, mules, and donkeys in 16:8 are for riding and adornment, but the verse's purpose marker applies specifically to riding. [RAZI-FULL]
- 100:1/v073 A saying calls failure to recognize the sanctity of a warrior's horse a branch of hypocrisy. [QURTUBI-FULL]
- 100:1/v074 The oath by horses' special features ultimately points to God as their sole giver and sovereign; it uses valued possessions to stress the truth of the following message. [BIQAI-FULL,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 100:1/v075 God swears elsewhere by Muhammad or his life, and here by his cavalry, its neighing, dust, and sparks. (The note cites 36:1–2 and 15:72.) [ابن العربي: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 100:1/v076 The first five verses swear by chargers; verses 6–8 expose human ingratitude or love of wealth, and verses 9–11 warn of judgment and disclosed hearts. (Ibn ʿĀshūr says this reassures believers, threatens deniers, and warns Muslims against the traits prevalent among pagans and hypocrites.) [IBNASHUR-FULL,STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI]
- 100:1/v077 Following al-Zalzalah's judgment scene, the surah traces evil deeds to preference for fleeting worldly gain and ingratitude for divine blessings, warning people to prepare for judgment. (Al-Biqāʿī says most people risk ruin by preferring fleeting status and wealth to what endures with God.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 100:1/v078 The surah's title fits its purpose because its oath and the answer to that oath point toward the warning that follows. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 100:1/v079 The basmala presents God as sovereign and universally generous in creation and explanation, and specially merciful by guiding devoted servants. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 100:1/v080 The basmala is a jealous word fit only for a tongue guarded from idle talk and backbiting, a guarded heart, and a spirit freed from attachments and veils. [QUSHAYRI]

## literary reading
- 100:1/v081 The charging horses' panting, sparks, dawn attack, dust, and breakthrough form a rapid scene echoed by the surah's forceful, noisy rhythm and its disclosure of graves and hearts. (Quṭb compares the surah's movement and ending to a runner finishing; its cavalry frame mirrors the moral and resurrection scenes.) [QUTB-ZILAL]

## inward reading
- 100:1/v082 The chargers symbolize striving souls traveling God's path through ascetic discipline, exertion, and longing; their panting is the exhalation of longing's ardor. [QASHANI]
- 100:1/v083 The chargers can symbolize souls transmitting holiness, kindling spiritual knowledge, attacking caprice, raising desire for God, and entering the highest Garden. (Attributed to al-Bayḍāwī; the note mentions 83:18–19.) [al-Bayḍāwī: STUDYQURAN]

## recitation merits
- 100:1/v084 A report promises ten good deeds for reciting al-ʿĀdiyāt for every person who spent the night at Muzdalifah and witnessed Jamʿ. (Al-Rāzī describes a report attributed to Ubay and raised to the Prophet, measuring reward by those at Muzdalifah and Jamʿ.) [النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: JISHUMI; النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم: KASHSHAF-FULL; أبي عن النبي: RAZI-FULL]
- 100:1/v085 Reports assign al-ʿĀdiyāt and al-Zalzalah each half the Qur'an in merit; one also assigns a third to al-Ikhlāṣ and a quarter to al-Kāfirūn. [الحسن: DURR-FULL; ابن عباس: DURR-FULL]
