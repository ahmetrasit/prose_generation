## grammar and syntax
- The future marker sīn can intensify the promise or indicate that recitation will continue with present and future revelation. (Ibn ʿĀshūr says the future marker, together with recitation already underway, signals renewal and continuation.) [ALUSI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL]
- Attributing recitation to God is understood as direct glad tidings to the Prophet; God is its rational source because He creates the words and commands their recitation. (The first-person plural also marks a shift in speaker.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- The shift from third-person singular to first-person plural is a rhetorical change of perspective, giving the promise directness and authority. (Abdel Haleem says the shift need not imply redaction and heightens the surah's authority and drama.) [CORPUSCORANICUM; Abdel Haleem: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## links to other verses

## grammar and syntax
- The phrase is declarative reassurance: God guarantees that Muhammad will retain the Qur'an, rather than issuing an obligation not to forget. (Arguments include that forgetting is involuntary, the prohibition needs strained grammatical additions, and the declarative reading better conveys glad tidings and fits 75:16.) - An alternative parses falā tansā as a prohibition against forgetting or neglecting recitation, sometimes retaining a final alif for rhyme or verse endings. (Proposed meanings include not neglecting review and avoiding causes of forgetting. Al-Ālūsī rejects the rhyme and neglect readings as strained.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,JISHUMI,QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; قيل: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## theology
- Forgetfulness is analyzed either as an act created by God or as loss of ordinarily necessary knowledge rather than an entity or act. (The two accounts are attributed respectively to Abū ʿAlī and Abū Hāshim.) [أبو علي: JISHUMI; أبو هاشم: JISHUMI]

## occasion and reports
- Muhammad privately rehearsed revelation because he feared forgetting; the promise relieved that concern and assured him that God would preserve it. (Some reports describe inward repetition; others describe moving the tongue. The fear arose from care for the revelation.) [مجاهد: IBNABIHATIM,MUJAHID,NAHHAS,QURTUBI-FULL,TAB; ابن عباس: DURR-FULL,MAWARDI-FULL; سعد بن أبي وقاص: DURR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL,RAZI-FULL; the Prophet: STUDYQURAN]
- Muhammad began reciting or repeating revelation before Gabriel had finished, fearing he would forget; after the assurance he retained it. (Ibn ʿĀshūr calls his report authentic; al-Suyūṭī separately notes that the chain in the al-Ṭabarānī report contains Juwaybir, whom he rates very weak.) [مجاهد: THALABI,WAHIDI-BASIT; قيل: JISHUMI; مجاهد والكلبي: BAGHAWI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL; مجاهد ومقاتل والكلبي: RAZI-FULL; ابن عباس: IBNASHUR-FULL,SUYUTI-LUBAB; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,TABATABAI]

## meaning of the word
- One interpretation makes the promise concern comprehensive knowledge of the Qur'an and the ease of acting on it. [RAZI-FULL]
- A proposed mechanism is that Gabriel repeats the recitation until Muhammad memorizes it. [RAZI-FULL]

## theology
- Preserving revelation in the Prophet's heart removes the burden of retention and comforts both him and his community, since the faith comes from God and He guards it. [BIQAI-FULL,QUTB-ZILAL]
- The Prophet's special guidance follows general guidance to creatures: he receives and preserves the Qur'an and is enabled to guide humanity. [ALUSI-FULL,BURSEVI]
- The exception allows God to cause forgetting or loss of awareness, reserving something for the Prophet's praiseworthy station at resurrection. [QASHANI]
- The exception leaves God's will unrestricted even when He makes a firm promise or establishes a lasting law. (Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God has power to cause forgetting after protected recitation, though He will not do so.) [QUTB-ZILAL,TABATABAI]
- The promise is a sign of prophethood: an unlettered man receives, memorizes, and teaches an extensive revelation without ordinary study or writing. (Some sources make strong memory itself a sign; others stress revelation without study, repetition, or writing.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,ELMALILI,IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,KASHSHAF,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,RAZI-FULL,ZAJJAJ; أبو إسحاق: WAHIDI-BASIT; قتادة: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- Because the verse came early in the Meccan period, its prediction and subsequent fulfillment are also presented as a miracle of foreknowledge. [BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,ELMALILI,RAZI-FULL]

## exception
- The exception covers revelation God abrogates: its recitation, ruling, or both may be withdrawn, after which the Prophet may forget it. (Some specify both recitation and ruling; others mention only abrogated recitation or application. Al-Ṭabarī prefers abrogation and withdrawal of revelation.) [مقاتل: MUQATIL,RAZI-FULL; قائلو هذه المقالة: TAB; BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,QURTUBI-FULL,TAB+; الحسن وقتادة: MAWARDI-FULL; الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ابن جرير: IBNKATHIR-FULL+; قيل: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- The exception can mean forgetting whatever God wills, without specifying the item; several reports state that the Prophet forgot nothing except what God willed. (This broad formulation is explained by some through abrogation and by others without specifying the cause.) [قتادة: ABDURRAZZAQ,IBNKATHIR-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,TAB; مالك بن أنس: NAHHAS; Asad: ASAD-NOTES; الحسن وقتادة: MAWARDI-FULL]
- The exception can preserve divine power to cause forgetting while God in fact did not will Muhammad to forget; preservation is God's favor, not the Prophet's own power. (Al-Farrāʾ is cited for the distinction between what God could will and what He did will.) [الكلبي: RAZI-FULL; الفراء: RAZI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL,TABATABAI]
- The exception may be a conventional or rhetorical qualification that excludes no actual forgotten material. (Al-Farrāʾ compares it with ordinary speech and oaths that state an exception while intending to withhold nothing. Abū Ḥayyān rejects this as making the exception no exception at all.) [FARRA,RAZI-FULL; بعض أهل العربية: TAB; الفراء: QURTUBI-FULL,THALABI; الفراء وجماعة: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- Saying if God wills can teach a formula for speaking about future acts even when God already knows what will happen. (The cited parallel is 18:23–24.) [RAZI-FULL]
- Mentioning the exception may keep Muhammad vigilant and attentive to whether any portion is excluded. [RAZI-FULL]
- The exception can allow a temporary lapse followed by recollection or a reminder; reports describe an omitted verse in prayer and verses recalled after another recited them. (The prayer report says Ubayy thought the verse was abrogated, and Muhammad replied that he had forgotten it; another account describes a night recitation prompting recollection.) [الزجاج: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL; عائشة: IBNASHUR-FULL]

- The Prophet may forget material outside the duty to convey before conveying it; God knows both public and secret matters. (The sources distinguish this from forgetting revelation he is required to deliver.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL+,QUSHAYRI,TABATABAI]
- God may delay a revelation so that Muhammad has not yet recited it, rather than making him forget it. [ابن عيسى: MAWARDI-FULL]
