## occasion and reports

(The accounts differ in details of the force, the delay, and how the report reached the Prophet; Ibn Kathīr calls one version very strange, and Ibn ʿĀshūr notes its weak chain.) ## grammar and syntax
- 100:1/v026 Al-ʿādiyāt is parsed as the feminine plural active participle of running quickly; the feminine form agrees with a non-rational referent, and the initial wāw is commonly taken as an oath particle governing the genitive. (Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr also discuss the root and form of al-ʿādiyāt.) [ELMALILI,IBNASHUR-FULL,JISHUMI,NAHHAS,SAMIN-DURR; الجمهور: ALUSI-FULL]
- 100:1/v027 The root ʿ-d-w is explained through crossing or exceeding a limit; its derivatives can express running, enmity, injustice, or spatial contagion, and the battle term ʿādiya can name troops that rush to attack. (Some restrict ʿadī to attacking infantry and ʿādiya to attacking cavalry.) [BINTSHATI,ELMALILI,QURTUBI-FULL; Bazıları: ELMALILI]
- 100:1/v028 Ḍabḥan is commonly analyzed as a cognate accusative, often with an implied verb meaning “they pant”; it can also be treated as a verbal noun or an adjective-like description of the runners. (Other parses treat it as a circumstantial accusative, or as governed by an implied verb; the notes preserve those alternatives below.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BINTSHATI+,ELMALILI,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR,THALABI; الزجاج: RAZI-FULL; الزمخشري: SAMIN-DURR]

## readings and rhetoric
- 100:1/v034 The common reading treats the opening wāw as an oath; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the oath convention magnifies its object and encourages far-fetched restrictions to warhorses or pilgrimage camels. (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ leaves the dispute unresolved and suggests the wāw may have moved beyond its literal oath function for a rhetorical purpose.) [BINTSHATI,ELMALILI]
- 100:1/v035 Because the following clauses depict an imaginary parable, Asad prefers translating the opening wāw as “Oh” instead of “By” or his usual “Consider.” (This is a distinct translation choice for the opening particle.) [Asad: ASAD-NOTES+]
- 100:1/v036 Al-ʿĀdiyāt names the surah in some early codices and commentaries without an opening wāw; other tafsirs retain the wāw in the title. (This note concerns the surah's title form.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## surah context and dating
- 100:1/v037 The surah is classified as Meccan in several reports and as Medinan in others; the horse interpretation and delayed-expedition reports are sometimes used to support a Medinan date, while stylistic similarity supports a Meccan date. (The reports disagree on dating; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the Medinan view stronger despite noting a weak chain in one expedition report.) [الحسن وعكرمة وقتادة: JISHUMI; DURR-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL+,IBNKATHIR,MAWARDI-FULL,STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI; ابن عباس: DURR-FULL; ابن مسعود: IBNASHUR-FULL; أنس بن مالك: IBNASHUR-FULL; جابر بن زيد: IBNASHUR-FULL; most commentators: STUDYQURAN; الكلبي: RAZI-FULL]
- 100:1/v038 The surah has eleven verses. (Qutb also describes its rapid, forceful progression.) [DURR-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,QUTB-ZILAL]
- 100:1/v039 Jābir ibn Zayd places the surah fourteenth in revelation order, after al-ʿAṣr and before al-Kawthar. (This order accompanies the Meccan classification.) [جابر بن زيد: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## coherence and structure
- 100:1/v040 The surah's charging cavalry opening moves through a rapid sequence of attack images; its rough, rumbling rhythm mirrors the dust and the later disclosure of graves and hearts, framing the moral and resurrection scenes. (The image and frame are said to mirror each other; verses 6–8 discuss ingratitude and verses 9–11 judgment and disclosure.) [QUTB-ZILAL,STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI]
- 100:1/v041 Al-Biqāʿī reads the surah after al-Zalzalah as tracing the disposition behind evil deeds and imagined worldly gain, rebuking people who neglect Judgment, prefer worldly life, and fail to thank God. (He says the title fits because the oath and its answer point to the surah's purpose.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 100:1/v042 The oath by horses serves as evidence for the later claim that people are ungrateful to their Lord; their exhausted obedience contrasts with human ingratitude. (Islāḥī links the horses' exertion to dutiful service of their master.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 100:1/v043 Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the surah recalls human ingratitude and love of wealth, which people know and which will be evidence against them at Judgment. (This is the surah's moral and eschatological message.) [TABATABAI]

## theology and ethics
- 100:1/v044 The oath recognizes horses' worldly and religious benefits: they can bring honor, wealth, protection, pursuit, escape, attack, and retreat, and can earn reward when used for God or sin when used for pride and harm. (Ibn al-Qayyim says the oath need not be limited to raiders; Muḥammad ʿAbduh reads it as encouragement to value, acquire, train, ride, and prepare horses for defense.) [BIQAI-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,RAZI-FULL; ابن القيم: BINTSHATI; محمد عبده: BINTSHATI]
- 100:1/v045 Al-Rāzī relates the oath to the benefits of camels in 88:17, contrasts human rebellion with God's service to people, and links it to pilgrimage; neglecting required Hajj is characterized as disbelief. (The cited verses are 88:17 and 3:97.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 100:1/v046 One transmitted saying calls failure to recognize the sanctity of a warrior's horse a branch of hypocrisy. (This is a separate ethical report about the horse.) [QURTUBI-FULL]
- 100:1/v047 Al-Biqāʿī says reflection on horses' distinctive features points to their being bestowed by one sovereign agent, so the oath is ultimately by God. (This is his theological explanation of the oath.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 100:1/v048 Ibn ʿĀshūr says that if the oath concerns pilgrimage camels, it honors mounts whose service is esteemed by both pagans and Muslims. (This explains why the camel reading could address both communities.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 100:1/v049 Ibn al-ʿArabī describes God as swearing by Muḥammad in Yā Sīn, by his life in al-Ḥijr, and here by his cavalry, including its neighing, dust, and sparks. (The cited passages are 36:1–2 and 15:72.) [ابن العربي: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 100:1/v050 The basmala presents God as sovereign over all affairs, generous in creating and explaining, and specially merciful through guidance to devoted servants. (This is a theological reflection accompanying the surah commentary.) [BIQAI-FULL]

## links to other verses
- 100:1/v051 The verse about horses, mules, and donkeys says they are for riding and adornment, but al-Rāzī argues its purpose marker applies to riding rather than ornament. (The cited verse is 16:8.) [RAZI-FULL]

## inward reading
- 100:1/v052 Al-Qushayrī describes the basmala as a jealous word fit for a tongue protected from idle talk and backbiting and a guarded heart; only a spirit protected from attachments and veils is fit to love it. (These are devotional reflections accompanying the commentary.) [QUSHAYRI]

## reports about recitation
- 100:1/v053 A report promises ten good deeds for reciting al-ʿĀdiyāt for every person who spent the night at Muzdalifah and witnessed Jamʿ. (The report appears in more than one source.) [النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: JISHUMI; النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 100:1/v054 A report attributed to Ubayy and raised to the Prophet measures a recitation reward by the number of people spending the night at Muzdalifah and witnessing Jamʿ. (This wording does not specify ten deeds.) [أبي عن النبي: RAZI-FULL]
- 100:1/v055 Reports say al-Zalzalah and al-ʿĀdiyāt each equal half the Qur'an; another report assigns half to the two surahs together, a third to al-Ikhlāṣ, and a quarter to al-Kāfirūn. (The transmitted wordings differ on how the half-Qur'an reward is distributed.) [الحسن: DURR-FULL; ابن عباس: DURR-FULL]

## modern readings
- 100:1/v056 Elmalılı extends the image to fire-sparking weapons and, by extension, fast motorized assault vehicles; he says a Meccan date would make this future reference especially clear and sees the plural forms and successive fāʾs as an ordered development of attack vehicles. (This modern application is Elmalılı's own extension.) [ELMALILI,ELMALILI+]

- 100:1/v060 Linguists describe the word's use for horses at the charge and compare it with poetic usage of horses in Iraq; another line describes blood-soaked chargers with necks like sacrificial stones. (The notes provide poetic support for the horse sense.) [أهل اللغة: QURTUBI-FULL,THALABI; شاعر: THALABI]

## grammar and syntax
- 100:1/v061 Al-ʿādiyāt derives from running or racing quickly; in battle usage ʿadī and ʿādiya can name troops that habitually rush to attack. (The root is also discussed through meanings of exceeding, hostility, and transgression above.) [ELMALILI,IBNASHUR-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL]

## occasion and reports
- 100:1/v062 In one expedition account, the horses snort through their nostrils, spark with their hooves on stones, attack at dawn, raise dust, and reach the enemy together. (These actions describe the reported cavalry expedition.) [ابن عباس: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## meaning of the word
- 100:1/v063 Some say either a horse or a dog makes ḍabḥ while running. (This is one account of which animals make the sound.) [ELMALILI]

## theology and ethics
- 100:1/v064 Ibn ʿĀshūr says the surah condemns traits leading to loss in the afterlife, especially common among pagans and hypocrites, and warns Muslims against them. (This is his statement of the surah's admonition.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
