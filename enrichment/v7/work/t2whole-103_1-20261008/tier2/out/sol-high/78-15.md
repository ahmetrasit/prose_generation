# 78:15: 29 views from 36 rows (sol-high)

## whole verse · rhetoric
- 78:15/v001 The paired sūras share an affirmative-question style directed at deniers: each verse reproaches them, while believers' glad tidings are understood against the warning. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 78:15/v002 The signs of providence, mercy, wisdom, and power throughout earth and heavens show that humanity is accountable and that a fixed day of reckoning must arrive. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## whole verse · links
- 78:15/v003 The sky, earth, rain, and plants resemble a reproductive pair: sky is male, earth female, water semen, and vegetation their offspring; springs and pools are implied. (The image connects this verse's rain and plants with the preceding sky and earth signs.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 78:15/v004 Muqātil says each of the signs just mentioned is more astonishing than resurrection. [مقاتل: BIQAI-FULL]
- 78:15/v005 Paired with Sūrat al-Mursalāt, this sūra uses providence in heaven and earth to establish Judgment as the necessary end of a purposeful world, rewarding the God-fearing and punishing rebels. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 78:15/v006 The seven firm heavens, sun, rain-bearing clouds, and life-sustaining growth show God's power to create the new realm of the afterlife. (The note mentions 2:29 and 67:3.) [KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 78:15/v007 Abundant, beneficial rain also produces luxuriant gardens and orchards alongside grain and vegetation, with fruits of varied colors, flavors, and scents. (The gardens are named in the following verse; Ibn Kathīr supplies the sensory details.) [IBNKATHIR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## لِّنُخْرِجَ (word 1) · grammar
- 78:15/v008 The lām marks the purpose or consequence of sending rain: bringing forth crops. (The note allows either purpose or consequence.) [ELMALILI]

## لِّنُخْرِجَ (word 1) · rhetoric
- 78:15/v009 ‘Bring forth’ instead of ‘grow’ portrays resurrection from the earth as the passage's main point. In Sūrat Qāf, favor is foregrounded, so growth comes first and resurrection follows as an inference. (The note cites 50:9, 50:11, and 20:55.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## بِهِۦ (word 2) · grammar
- 78:15/v010 The bi marks rain as the causal means by which the crops are brought forth. [ELMALILI]

## بِهِۦ (word 2) · theology
- 78:15/v011 According to early authorities, the water is the direct means of bringing forth crops. (Al-Ālūsī contrasts this with the Ashʿarī account that crops come into being at the water's presence.) [ALUSI-FULL]
- 78:15/v012 According to Ashʿarīs, crops come into being at the presence of water. (Al-Ālūsī contrasts this with the early authorities' account of water as the direct means.) [ALUSI-FULL]
- 78:15/v013 God remains the primary cause of grain, plants, and gardens; rain is His means, since He made every living thing from water. (The note cites 21:30, 24:25, and 25:54.) [STUDYQURAN]

## حَبًّۭا (word 3) · meaning
- 78:15/v014 Ḥabb denotes grain or seed enclosed in a husk; the husked crop is harvested. (The husk and harvest descriptions are supplied by different notes; an alternative identifies the grain as pearls.) [NAHHAS,RAZI-FULL,TABRISI; الضحاك: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 78:15/v015 Ḥabb is a collective for edible seeds, including wheat, barley, sesame, millet, corn, rice, and pulses. (The examples vary by source; Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the collective-noun description.) [BIQAI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,MUQATIL,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL]
- 78:15/v016 Everything harvested counts as grain, while vegetation is pasture eaten by livestock. (Attributed to al-Zajjāj by Ibn al-Jawzī; al-Ḍaḥḥāk's account adds a husk to the harvested seed.) [الزجاج: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; الضحاك: MAWARDI-FULL]

## حَبًّۭا (word 3) · rhetoric
- 78:15/v017 Grain comes first as the basic, nobler staple food, even though it sprouts later; plants follow because animals need fodder, and orchards come last because fruit is less essential. (The later sprouting and nobility points come from al-Ālūsī; the reason for placing orchards last comes from al-Rāzī.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,RAZI-FULL]

## حَبًّۭا (word 3) · referent
- 78:15/v018 On an alternative explanation, grain is pearls and vegetation is grass: every drop of heavenly rain produces a pearl in the sea or grass on land. (Attributed to ʿIkrima; it differs from the crop-grain explanations.) [عكرمة: IBNJAWZI-ZAD,MAWARDI-FULL]
- 78:15/v019 Grain is what people sow, whereas vegetation is what grows without their sowing. (This distinguishes the two by cultivation rather than by who eats them.) [MAWARDI-FULL]

## حَبًّۭا (word 3) · interpretation
- 78:15/v020 Rain produces grain as food for people and vegetation as fodder for livestock; the latter includes grass, straw, and pasture. (The human and livestock uses are assigned to different products here; other notes allow both people and animals to eat the plants.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,QURTUBI-FULL,TABRISI,WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 78:15/v021 Grain also benefits animals: it may serve as their food, and stored grain feeds both people and livestock. (Ibn Kathīr specifies storage and use by both people and livestock.) [IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 78:15/v022 Terrestrial growth falls into three classes: husked grain, grasses without husks or stems, and trees forming gardens. This verse gives the first two, which are also named in 20:54; the following verse gives gardens. [RAZI-FULL]
- 78:15/v023 Together grain and vegetation supply food for people and other animals and encompass the earth's produce. (These notes describe the pair's combined scope without assigning one product exclusively to people or animals.) [TABATABAI,TABRISI]

## وَنَبَاتًۭا (word 4) · meaning
- 78:15/v024 Nabāt denotes grass, herbage, pasture, fodder, and similar plant growth, including straw. (The notes give different examples of the same pasture and plant sense.) [BAYDAWI-FULL,NAHHAS,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,TABRISI; الضحاك: MAWARDI-FULL; الزجاج: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 78:15/v025 Nabāt has a broad sense encompassing all growth, including grain. (This differs from Ibn ʿĀshūr's narrower conventional use for plants whose seeds are not eaten.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 78:15/v026 Nabāt was originally a verbal noun and became a conventional noun for useful vegetation whose seeds are not eaten, such as straw, clover, and grass. (Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions 71:17; this conventional use is narrower than Abū Ḥayyān's broad sense.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## وَنَبَاتًۭا (word 4) · referent
- 78:15/v027 The plants include wild trees and grass growing in open country that renew each year. [MUQATIL]

## وَنَبَاتًۭا (word 4) · interpretation
- 78:15/v028 Vegetation can be eaten by people as well as livestock; grass may be used fresh by either. (Unlike the human-grain and livestock-vegetation allocation, these notes include people among those who eat vegetation.) [IBNATIYYA-FULL; الجمهور: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 78:15/v029 Rain produces edible fresh plants. (Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies fresh grass used by people or animals.) [IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNKATHIR-FULL]
