- 79:46/v021 The brief sojourn is the time spent in the graves before resurrection; seeing the Resurrection's terror makes that interval seem only an afternoon or morning. (The sources differ on whether the short interval is worldly life or the grave stay; Study Quran attributes this view to most commentators.) [ALUSI-FULL+,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,KASHSHAF-FULL,TABATABAI+,THALABI,WAHIDI-WAJIZ; الكلبي: WAHIDI-BASIT; بعض المفسرين: QURTUBI-FULL; most commentators: STUDYQURAN]
- 79:46/v022 The stay can encompass both life in the world and the ensuing time in the graves, together appearing no longer than part of a day when the Hour comes. (Tabataba'i describes worldly life until resurrection; Biqa'i expressly names the world and graves.) [BIQAI-FULL,TABATABAI]
- 79:46/v023 The grave period is preferable as the intended sojourn because people's entire worldly life is counted for judgment; the worldly-life reading is rejected on this ground. - 79:46/v024 An alternative takes the sojourn as the interval from asking when the Hour will come until resurrection, but Tabataba'i rejects it: the interval is witnessed only at resurrection, and death rather than the question precedes it. - 79:46/v025 Ibn Abbas, through al-Dahhak, says people will think they remained in the world for only one day. (This estimate is a full day, distinct from the part-day estimates.) [الضحاك عن ابن عباس: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 79:46/v026 The short apparent stay warns people that worldly pleasures will soon end and they will leave them as though they had remained only an evening or morning. [ELMALILI,NAHHAS]
- 79:46/v027 Against the endless afterlife, worldly time is negligible; one comparison likens this world's share to a finger dipped in the sea and withdrawn. (Biqa'i attributes the finger-and-sea comparison to the Prophet.) [BIQAI-FULL,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:46/v028 Disbelievers give differing estimates of how long they stayed, yet their past appears only a brief hour beside the endless future they face. [BIQAI-FULL]

- 79:46/v030 Psychologically, finished past time seems short, whereas the open, uncertain future feels more important and can provoke anxiety. [KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## عَشِيَّةً (word 7) · meaning
- 79:46/v033 Evening denotes the period from noon or afternoon until sunset, while forenoon or morning denotes the period from sunrise until noon; Muqatil describes the morning as lasting until the sun rises higher. (The precise morning endpoint differs slightly across the notes.) [BIQAI-FULL,BURSEVI,MUQATIL; الكلبي: WAHIDI-BASIT; ابن عباس: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 79:46/v034 Arabic can pair an evening with the following morning, or a morning with its preceding evening; on this account 'its forenoon' can be the morning after the named evening. (These notes differ from those that locate the morning earlier on the same day; Wahidi's wording also associates it with the evening's day.) [FARRA,QURTUBI-FULL,ZAJJAJ; الفراء والزجاج: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 79:46/v035 Evening has no literal forenoon; the Arabic expression pairs the end and beginning of a day to indicate one of its two ends, rather than a forenoon belonging literally to an evening. (The notes use beginning/end and morning/evening terminology for the idiom.) [FARRA,IBNATIYYA-FULL,TAB-FULL; الفراء: BAGHAWI-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,QURTUBI-FULL,THALABI]
- 79:46/v036 A verse by a poet of Banu Uqayl uses 'the evening of the new moon' for the evening of its setting or hidden phase; the citation supports the Arabic time-pairing idiom. (The reports vary in how they describe the moon's hidden phase.) [بعض بني عقيل: FARRA,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI]

## عَشِيَّةً (word 7) · rhetoric
- 79:46/v037 The compact phrase expresses 'only an hour of a day, its evening or forenoon'; attaching 'its' makes the measure a part of one day, excluding a span from one day's evening to another day's forenoon. (The wording's brevity and the possessive carry the point.) [IBNATIYYA-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,NASAFI-FULL; الزمخشري: ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:46/v038 Al-Tibi allows evening or forenoon to represent a whole day metaphorically, with the pronoun reinforcing that sense as in saying one saw something 'with one's own eyes.' (Other notes take the phrase as less than a whole day.) [الطيبي: ALUSI-FULL]

## ضُحَىٰهَا (word 9) · rhetoric
