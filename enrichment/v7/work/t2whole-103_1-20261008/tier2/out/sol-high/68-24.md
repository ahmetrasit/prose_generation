# 68:24: 31 views from 45 rows (sol-high)

## أَن (word 1) · meaning
- 68:24/v001 Takhāfut means whispering to one another so that nobody else hears the plan. [BINTSHATI]

## أَن (word 1) · grammar
- 68:24/v002 The particle أَن explains the content of the preceding whispering, which implies speech without quoting it directly. (The verbal-noun analysis is a separate alternative.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,NAHHAS,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]
- 68:24/v003 The clause introduced by أَن can function as a verbal noun expressing what they whispered; one analysis supplies an omitted ب before it. (This competes with taking أَن as explanatory.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]
- 68:24/v004 With أَن omitted, an unspoken verb of saying or the noun قول supplies the speech governing the prohibition; this is identified as the Basran analysis. (The competing Kufan analysis treats whispering itself as a speech verb.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL; عبد الله وابن أبي عبلة: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]
- 68:24/v005 The preceding verb for whispering can govern the prohibition directly because whispering conveys speech; this is identified as the Kufan analysis. (This competes with supplying an omitted verb of saying.) [ALUSI-FULL,NAHHAS; عبد الله وابن أبي عبلة: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]

## أَن (word 1) · readings

## أَن (word 1) · interpretation
- 68:24/v007 The owners privately agreed that no poor person should enter their garden that day. (Several specify that they whispered so no one, especially the poor, would hear; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that the poor might notice and claim a share.) [مجاهد: MUJAHID; ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,ELMALILI,IBNKATHIR-FULL,IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB,MUQATIL,TABATABAI,TABRISI,WAHIDI-WAJIZ; يحيى بن سلام: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 68:24/v008 They meant to finish harvesting completely and leave nothing for anyone, acting with extreme greed. [ELMALILI]
- 68:24/v009 They forgot that they had needed oaths to reassure one another and establish their cooperation the day before; the orchard's yield likewise depended on trust. [ELMALILI]
- 68:24/v010 They believed themselves able to prevent the poor from receiving their share, yet ended up deprived themselves. (Al-Kashshāf, as cited by Bursawī, adds that they meant to distress the poor despite being able to help them, then lost their wealth.) [BURSEVI; الكشاف: BURSEVI]
- 68:24/v011 Another explanation of their ḥard is that they thought themselves capable of carrying out the plan to exclude the poor. (This competes with reading ḥard as anger toward the poor.) [BURSEVI,QUSHAYRI]

## أَن (word 1) · links
- 68:24/v012 The justest brother's call to glorify God means that they should have made an exception by saying إن شاء الله, as in the instruction to remember God after forgetting in 18:24. [FARRA]

## لَّا (word 2) · rhetoric
- 68:24/v013 Although the words bar a poor person from entering, their intended command to one another is not to admit or enable any poor person to enter; phrasing it as an order to the poor makes the ban more emphatic. (Al-Ālūsī compares the phrasing to لا أرينك ههنا.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL]

## يَدْخُلَنَّهَا (word 3) · grammar
- 68:24/v014 The emphatic nūn in يَدْخُلَنَّهَا reinforces the owners' oath. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## يَدْخُلَنَّهَا (word 3) · reports
- 68:24/v015 Exegetical reports place the garden in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. (These are reported alternatives.) [BINTSHATI]
- 68:24/v016 Exegetes elaborate that the garden belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town. [BINTSHATI]

## يَدْخُلَنَّهَا (word 3) · interpretation
- 68:24/v017 They intended to search throughout the day so no poor person could reach the fruit or find an opening into the garden. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 68:24/v018 They should have remembered that God gave them the orchard and watched over it while they slept. [ELMALILI]
- 68:24/v019 They rushed out without first considering the orchard's condition. [ELMALILI]

## مِّسْكِينٌۭ (word 6) · meaning
- 68:24/v020 A miskīn has nothing and is more destitute than a faqīr. [BURSEVI]

## مِّسْكِينٌۭ (word 6) · reports
- 68:24/v021 A custom allowing the poor to glean or share in the harvest is described as ancient, dating from Biblical times or the ancient Near East. [ASAD-NOTES,STUDYQURAN]
- 68:24/v022 ʿIkrima reports that the owners were Ethiopians whose father used to feed the poor from his garden; after his death, his sons called him foolish and vowed to exclude the poor. [عكرمة: TAB-FULL]
- 68:24/v023 Qatāda reports that a charitable old man owned the garden, but his sons opposed his charity and, after his death, set out to keep poor people away. [قتادة: TAB-FULL]
- 68:24/v024 Ibn ʿAbbās reports a father who gave every rightful person a share and five sons who planned to withhold an unusually abundant harvest from Muslim poor; four agreed while the moderate brother objected. [ابن عباس: TABATABAI]

## مِّسْكِينٌۭ (word 6) · interpretation
- 68:24/v025 The poor had a rightful share of the garden's harvest, which the owners intended to deny them. (The amount or form of the due, its history, and its connection to 6:141 receive separate views.) [ASAD-NOTES,ELMALILI,JISHUMI,STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI; ابن عباس: TABATABAI]
- 68:24/v026 Gratitude required sharing the crop with the poor and cultivating beneficiaries and well-wishers, whereas greed and despotism made enemies. [ELMALILI]
- 68:24/v027 One explanation of their ḥard is anger toward the poor, expressed as forceful prevention. [BURSEVI,QUSHAYRI]

## مِّسْكِينٌۭ (word 6) · law
- 68:24/v028 The poor had a due from the harvest, variously identified as one tenth or the fruit that fell during picking. (The notes report these as alternative identifications of the due.) [JISHUMI]

## مِّسْكِينٌۭ (word 6) · links
- 68:24/v029 The poor's right to a harvest share is connected to the Quranic command in 6:141 to give the crop's due on harvest day. [ASAD-NOTES,STUDYQURAN]
- 68:24/v030 Denying the poor their right is the parable's second, specifically social, sin and connects with the people described in verses 10–13. [ASAD-NOTES]
- 68:24/v031 The whispered ban repeats the owners' stinginess already signaled in verse 18. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
