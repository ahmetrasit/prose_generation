## وَٱلضُّحَىٰ (word 1) · meaning
- 93:1/v001 Ḍuḥā can mean the whole daylight or day, set against night. Support includes Arabic usage for appearing in sunlight and Qurʾānic contrasts of night with punishment arriving at ḍuḥā. ## وَٱلضُّحَىٰ (word 1) · grammar
- 93:1/v012 The morning time is named by metonymy, or an omitted noun such as 'time' is understood to make its reference parallel to night. [BURSEVI]
- 93:1/v013 The definite article may mark ḍuḥā generically, but Elmalılı prefers a specific dawn of truth: the Muhammadan mission beginning to radiate mercy and light. [ELMALILI+]
- 93:1/v014 The final alif is written with an alif shape because the form comes from ḍaḥā yaḍḥū. (Competing orthographic accounts appear in the following views.) [محمد بن يزيد: NAHHAS]
- 93:1/v015 Spelling should record pronunciation, not the supposed yāʾ or wāw origin of a root; root-based spellings conflict with spoken forms, conventional and Qurʾānic spellings, and examples such as rāmā, ghazā, mā zakkā, rabayān, and yarbū. (Al-Naḥḥās rejects the Kufan convention; the examples and the objection about the dual are transmitted from other grammarians.) [NAHHAS-; محمد بن يزيد: NAHHAS; أبو إسحاق: NAHHAS]
- 93:1/v016 The muṣḥaf writes the final alif of al-ḍuḥā in a yāʾ shape despite its wāw root, matching other endings, most of them yāʾ-rooted, and supporting imāla in relevant dialects. (Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Ibn Mālik on the spelling pattern.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## وَٱلضُّحَىٰ (word 1) · rhetoric
- 93:1/v017 The wording is an oath by ḍuḥā or its light. [IBNKATHIR-FULL,IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA,TAB-FULL]
- 93:1/v018 An implied 'Lord' is the oath's object: the sense is 'by the Lord of morning' and likewise of night. (This differs from taking the created time itself as the oath object.) [أهل المعاني: QURTUBI-FULL,THALABI; TABRISI]
- 93:1/v019 The forenoon is singled out as the day's youthful, vigorous, bright, and honored phase, fitting the Prophet's exceptional rank. [ALUSI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,ELMALILI]
- 93:1/v020 The oath by morning light draws attention to a great sign and blessing among created things, and created things in oaths can be glorified as signs. (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ treats glorification as a possible impression rather than the device's central function.) [ابن قيم الجوزية: BINTSHATI; الشيخ محمد عبده: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/v021 The oath's wāw strongly directs attention to familiar sensory realities that prepare the reader for unseen meanings; the quality chosen for the oath suits its context. (This is Bint al-Shāṭiʾ's preferred account of its rhetorical function.) [BINTSHATI,BINTSHATI+]
- 93:1/v022 Although ḍuḥā admits several lexical senses, the setting's rhetoric selects a single fitting sense here. [BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/v023 The order of this morning oath before night gives daylight priority through its intrinsic and incidental honor; night leads in the preceding sūrah because darkness is the original state to which air returns after sunset. [BURSEVI]
- 93:1/v024 Some explain the order of morning then night as preferring life until believers mature, avoiding despair first and fear of divine plotting later. [بعض المفسرين: BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/v025 Bint al-Shāṭiʾ finds no clear link between the oath and its answer in al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, and Abū Ḥayyān, and says they neglect comparison with explicit Qurʾānic denials of oaths. [BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/v026 The sūrah's graceful diction and musical cadence embody its gentleness and point to God's unmatched creative work. [QUTB-ZILAL]
- 93:1/v027 Al-Ālūsī rejects the Imam's flower metaphor for the relation of this sūrah to the previous one as untenable. [ALUSI-FULL-]

## وَٱلضُّحَىٰ (word 1) · readings
- 93:1/v028 At this word the reading difference concerns imāla versus open or emphatic pronunciation, while the phrase remains an oath. [IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 93:1/v029 Readers differ in imāla of these endings: Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, and Ibn ʿĀmir use open forms; al-Kisāʾī inclines all; Ḥamza distinguishes yāʾ and wāw origins; Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr have transmitted intermediate, open, or inclined forms with differing scope. (Abū ʿAmr is also described as following the sūrah's ending pattern; Medinans use a middle degree in one account.) [NAHHAS; ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر: FARISI-HUJJA; الكسائي: FARISI-HUJJA; نافع: FARISI-HUJJA; أبو عمرو: FARISI-HUJJA; أبو عمرو ونافع: FARISI-HUJJA]
- 93:1/v030 Imāla is ordinarily licensed when an alif behaves like one originating in yāʾ, including derived verbs and nouns; wāw-origin alifs usually resist it, and rare exceptions do not overturn that rule. [FARISI-HUJJA]
- 93:1/v031 Verbs of wāw origin can nevertheless be inclined when their inflected forms shift to yāʾ, as with ḍuḥā and sajā. [NAHHAS]
- 93:1/v032 A transmitted practice begins takbīr at al-Ḍuḥā and continues through the Qurʾān's end, saying 'Allāhu akbar' and, in one account, the basmala at each sūrah's ending. (One transmission is attributed to Ibn Kathīr; another gives a Prophetic instruction.) [ابن كثير: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; ابن عباس: DURR-FULL]

## وَٱلضُّحَىٰ (word 1) · interpretation
- 93:1/v059 Midmorning brings people together and ease after night's isolation, so its oath promises renewed revelation and comfort after the Prophet's distress during the pause. [RAZI-FULL]
- 93:1/v060 Morning brightness represents revelation's light spreading through the world like sunrise. [İbn Âşûr: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 93:1/v061 A short hour of ḍuḥā balances a whole night as Muhammad balances all prophets and their communities; brief joy similarly balances longer worldly sorrow and anxiety. [BURSEVI; النيسابوري والرازي: BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/v062 The daily sequence of bright morning and quiet night pictures revelation's pause and return as part of wise change, without implying that heaven abandoned earth; light and darkness can also picture guidance and truth against misguidance and falsehood. [BINTSHATI,BINTSHATI+; فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري: BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/v063 Morning as first revelation gives life and activity; night as quiet rest restores strength for later work, and renewed revelation relieves the Prophet's distress. (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects that this explanation nearly loses the sensory analogy.) [الشيخ محمد عبده: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-]
- 93:1/v064 The oath answers the unbelievers' taunt because they could not prove that God had abandoned Muhammad. [فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري: BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/v065 Night and day alternately prevail; created people likewise undergo change and are not exempt from it. [فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري: BINTSHATI]
- 93:1/v066 Light and dark alternation displays the Creator's power. [IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 93:1/v067 The oath's paired morning and night create a tender, reflective setting, making the spirit sense creation worshipping its Maker and the Prophet feel accompanied in a living universe. [QUTB-ZILAL]
- 93:1/v068 Morning is bright youthful joy and revealed knowledge, while settled night provides rest before renewal; the pairing introduces reassurance that the Prophet has not been abandoned. [ELMALILI]
- 93:1/v069 Day and night together are divine mercy: night permits rest, and day renewed work and pursuit of bounty; both sustain the world. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:1/v070 Morning brightness evokes revelation and guidance; settled night evokes the Prophet's audible Qurʾān recitation and perhaps his night prayer. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:1/v071 The opening oath rebuts idolaters who inferred a halt in revelation from several nights when the Prophet did not recite; the warning addresses their insinuation, while the Prophet himself had no doubt. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:1/v072 Revelation and Gabriel's visits sustained the Prophet amid rejection and persecution; their interruption left him isolated and distressed. [QUTB-ZILAL]
- 93:1/v073 The sūrah comforts the Prophet with assurance that God has not abandoned him, promises future satisfaction, recalls earlier care, and calls for care of the vulnerable and preaching. (Quṭb emphasizes hope, closeness, calm, and the afterlife's superiority.) [QUTB-ZILAL,STUDYQURAN]

## وَٱلضُّحَىٰ (word 1) · law
- 93:1/v075 The ḍuḥā prayer is unanimously a sunnah; its time runs from when the sun is high until shortly before noon. [BURSEVI]
- 93:1/v076 Abū Ḥanīfa sets the ḍuḥā prayer at two or four units with one salutation; Mālik sets no fixed upper limit; al-Shāfiʿī and Aḥmad require at least two, with maxima of twelve and eight respectively. (Most Shāfiʿī authorities adopt eight as the maximum, which al-Nawawī validates.) [أبو حنيفة: BURSEVI; مالك: BURSEVI; الشافعي: BURSEVI]
