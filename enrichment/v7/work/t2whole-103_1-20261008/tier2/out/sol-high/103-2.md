## whole verse · meaning
- 103:2/v001 Īmān derives from amn, with senses of granting peace, attesting, trusting and certain faith; God is called muʾmin because He grants safety to those seeking refuge. (Attributed to Farāhī; the note cites 106:4.) [Im ām Far āhī: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 103:2/v002 Ṣāliḥāt denotes actions enabling outward and inward, worldly and religious, individual and collective development toward human potential. (Attributed to Farāhī.) [Im ām Far āhī: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 103:2/v003 An elegy depicts life deceiving people while death feeds on it and every breath weaves a shroud. (The elegy is for the author's teacher and Balkan War dead.) [ELMALILI]

## whole verse · grammar
- 103:2/v004 The generic expression for righteous works includes every commanded good deed and abstention from sin; the relative clause explains why the believers escape loss. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## whole verse · rhetoric
- 103:2/v005 The late-afternoon oath followed by rebuke and believers' exception forms a three-rhyme sūra; a hymn-like analysis of it is disputed. (Corpus Coranicum rejects Neuwirth's proposed hymn-like resonance.) [CORPUSCORANICUM,CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 103:2/v006 The oath by time points to the changes, gains, harms and wonders it contains and draws attention to human gain or loss within it. (One explanation treats these events as signs of the Creator's power.) [ELMALILI]
- 103:2/v007 The oath honors time as a valuable blessing and warns that it consumes life unless people work toward reform through faith and good deeds. [ELMALILI,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 103:2/v008 Time's senses of restraint and bestowal support both the warning of loss and the exception of those who gain through faith and works. [ELMALILI]
- 103:2/v009 The oath can encompass every sense of ʿaṣr, particularly the final era as time's afternoon and Muhammad's age. [ELMALILI]
- 103:2/v010 Afternoon evokes humanity's late creation, the closing stage of life and sorrow at returning without gain. (This also relates the sūra to al-Takāthur.) [ELMALILI]
- 103:2/v011 The oath by the Prophetic age before the warning of loss carries special force because Muhammad's mission arose amid widespread ignorance, unbelief, injustice and religious disorder, calling humanity toward reform and salvation. (The notes mention the first revelation's command iqra on a Night of Power and a transformation from the middle ages to the last era.) [ELMALILI]
- 103:2/v012 The sūra's title and images present human salvation as extracting a lasting essence from transient life, just as pressing extracts an essence and ʿaṣr is the essence of time. (Biqaʿī also calls humankind the noblest, best-proportioned species.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 103:2/v013 The sūra begins with the ruined and then the saved because preventing harm precedes securing benefit. [BIQAI-FULL]

## خُسْرٍ (word 4) · theology
- 103:2/v119 The gravest loss is deprivation of serving the Lord; loss of Paradise and entry into Hell are slight by comparison because worship is creation's highest purpose. (Rāzī cites 51:56.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 103:2/v120 Faith alone does not prevent every kind of loss: failure to act righteously and counsel truth and patience leaves sinful believers exposed to loss, contrary to the Murjiʾa claim. (Elmalılı attributes the contrary claim to the Murjiʾa and supports the Ahl al-Sunna position.) [محمد بن كعب القرظي: IBNABIHATIM; ELMALILI; Mürcienin: ELMALILI-]
- 103:2/v121 Faith ultimately saves sinful believers, but those whose sins outweigh their good deeds experience Hell's loss until cleansed. [ELMALILI]
