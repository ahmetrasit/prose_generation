## whole verse · reports
- 18:11/v002 The cave story circulated in detailed reports from al-Qummi, Ibn Abbas, Ikrima, Mujahid, Ibn Ishaq, Wahb ibn Munabbih, al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, and others. [TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v003 Transmitted accounts diverge on major features, especially dates, rulers, ancestry, appearance, names, and the title 'Companions of al-Raqim.' [TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v004 The story's spread among People of the Book, its transmission to Quraysh, and the wide circulation of Christian images and monuments encouraged communities to reshape its details around their beliefs. [TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v005 Muslims collected outside accounts broadly, including material from converts such as Wahb ibn Munabbih and Ka'b al-Ahbar; later transmitters treated some of it as prophetic reports. [TABATABAI]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 18:11/v006 The best account of a beloved is heard from the beloved himself; a poem asks Sa'd to repeat the beloved's story because hearing it deepens longing. [QUSHAYRI]
- 18:11/v007 The Qur'an selects lessons needed for guidance rather than giving a complete history; it omits names, ancestry, personal traits, dates, rulers, cities, persecutors, and the dog's identity, and later accounts filled those gaps divergently. [TABATABAI]

## whole verse · links
- 18:11/v008 The opening verses summarize the youths' flight and prayer, counted years of sleep, and awakening to determine which group counted the duration; later verses expand the story and what followed their discovery. (Islahi says this summary-first narrative pattern keeps the story's purpose in view and prevents secondary details from obscuring it.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR,TABATABAI]

## فَضَرَبْنَا (word 1) · meaning
- 18:11/v009 Here 'struck' can mean placed or imposed, as when a barrier is set over someone. (Al-Alusi offers a lock struck onto a door or a tent pitched over its occupant as possible idiomatic origins.) [ALUSI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:11/v010 The expression can describe afflicting someone with a condition, like the Arabic idiom of striking someone with paralysis. (This analogy supports the sense of imposing sleep.) [TAB-FULL,THALABI]
- 18:11/v011 Quṭrub compares striking the ears with an emir or master striking a person's hand to prevent action, wrongdoing, or authorized trading. (Al-Alusi rejects a mere loss of ear function as insufficient to establish sleep or resurrection, though he concedes its proponents may intend sleep.) [قطرب: JISHUMI,QURTUBI-FULL,TABATABAI,TABRISI,THALABI; ALUSI-FULL-,JISHUMI,TABRISI]
- 18:11/v012 Al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur's verse about earth being struck over him with barriers illustrates an idiom of blocking or confinement; some notes say he was blind. (The poetry is cited as linguistic evidence for the verse's expression.) [الأسود بن يعفر: JISHUMI,QURTUBI-FULL,TABRISI,THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; الأسود: SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:11/v013 A line attributed to al-Farazdaq, including its spider image, illustrates striking over someone as firm imposition or covering. (The line is linguistic evidence for the verse's image.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL; الفرزدق: SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:11/v014 Abū Dharr uses the same expression, saying God struck upon their hearing. [أبو ذر: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## فَضَرَبْنَا (word 1) · grammar
- 18:11/v015 The object of 'struck' is omitted: a barrier, covering, curtain, or veil over the ears that prevents hearing is understood. (The accounts vary between literal ellipsis and figurative analysis of the image.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; الزمخشري: TABATABAI]

## فَضَرَبْنَا (word 1) · rhetoric
- 18:11/v016 Striking upon the ears pictures sleep as forcefully imposed, firmly attached, or persisting over time, like other Arabic expressions for imposed humiliation, taxation, levies, or fixed qualities. (Ibn Ashur also relates the usage of 'struck' to Qur'anic phrasing about imposed humiliation.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:11/v017 The expression for putting them to sleep is an exceptionally eloquent Qur'anic idiom. (Thalabi calls it unmatched; Qurtubi says it challenged Arab eloquence; Tabatabai prefers al-Majma's direct account of imposed sleep to al-Zamakhshari's barrier paraphrase.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR,JISHUMI,QURTUBI-FULL,TABATABAI,TABRISI,THALABI; المجمع: TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v018 The full force of this Qur'anic wording cannot be preserved in translation. [TABATABAI,TABRISI]
- 18:11/v019 The barrier over the ears may be analyzed as an implicit metaphor or as a representational metaphor for deep sleep. (Al-Qutb objects to a metonymic explanation; al-Alusi considers the objection answerable and offers implicit metaphor, while other investigators prefer representational metaphor.) [القطب: ALUSI-FULL; بعض المحققين: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:11/v020 Covering the ears stands by metonymy for the youths' deep sleep, and Ibn Ashur regards this usage as a Qur'anic feature unknown before this verse and an aspect of its inimitability. (Al-Qutb's reported objection and al-Alusi's answer concern this metonymic analysis.) [IBNASHUR-FULL; القطب: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:11/v021 The phrase may evoke a mother gently tapping near her child's ear to lull the child, presenting the youths' sleep as tender or loving care. (Islahi also says the patting or sealing idiom developed a sense of stopping someone from hearing.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR,TABATABAI]

## فَضَرَبْنَا (word 1) · interpretation
- 18:11/v022 God put the youths to sleep in the cave by striking upon their ears. (Several notes also explain this through blocked hearing or describe the idiom's eloquence; those details have separate views.) - 18:11/v024 The sleep was deep and firm enough that ordinary sounds could not interrupt it. (Several of these notes explain the depth of sleep through a figurative barrier over the ears.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL; الزمخشري: TABATABAI; IK, Th, Ṭs: STUDYQURAN]

- 18:11/v027 The centuries-long sleep represents an intermediate realm between life and death, analogous to the state between individual death and universal resurrection. [STUDYQURAN]

## فَضَرَبْنَا (word 1) · theology
- 18:11/v028 The youths' surviving unharmed through 309 years of sleep, shielded from the sun, is treated by Razi as a saintly gift or karama. (Razi argues the sleep itself was ordinary and its truth verifiable only once the same sleepers were identified centuries later.) [RAZI-FULL,RAZI-FULL-]
- 18:11/v029 Al-Qadi argues a prophet must have been present in that era for the extraordinary sleep to count as a prophetic miracle. (Razi rejects this classification and treats the event as a gift to saints.) [القاضي: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## فَضَرَبْنَا (word 1) · links
- 18:11/v030 The initial 'fa' connects the sleep with the youths' preceding prayer: God answered it by protecting them, granting sleep, later turning and awakening them, and eventually showing people the sign. (Al-Alusi parallels the response to Zachariah's prayer in 21:90; Bursevi calls the sleep and turning hidden grace beyond ordinary causes.) [ALUSI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,TABRISI]
- 18:11/v031 Alternatively, the 'fa' connects the sleep with their taking refuge in the cave, showing that God quickly granted what they sought beyond expectation. (This differs from connecting it directly to their prayer.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## ءَاذَانِهِمْ (word 3) · grammar
- 18:11/v039 'Ear' is grammatically feminine, and a ḍamma is omitted because its pronunciation would be heavy. [NAHHAS]

## ءَاذَانِهِمْ (word 3) · rhetoric
- 18:11/v040 The ears are singled out because hearing commonly wakes a sleeper and sleep deepens when hearing is inactive; a report about Satan urinating in the ear of a person who sleeps through the night illustrates unusually heavy sleep. (The hadith detail appears in Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, and Abu Hayyan.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNATIYYA-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,ZAJJAJ]
- 18:11/v041 Covering the eyes would not convey established deep sleep: sights do not wake a sleeper, and shutting the eyes could show only sleep's onset. [ALUSI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL]

## ءَاذَانِهِمْ (word 3) · interpretation
- 18:11/v042 Blocking hearing itself did not cause their sleep; it kept waking sounds from reaching them after they slept. (This distinguishes the prevention of waking from the cause of sleep.) [MAWARDI-FULL]

## ءَاذَانِهِمْ (word 3) · inward
- 18:11/v043 For retreat seekers, shutting the ears blocks human speech and other sensory impressions from engraving the heart. [التأويلات النجمية: BURSEVI]

## فِى (word 4) · grammar
- 18:11/v044 'In the cave' marks the place and 'for years' the duration of the striking or sleep; both are adverbial complements of the action. (Al-Alusi says the spatial and temporal complements can occur together without difficulty.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,WAHIDI-BASIT]

## فِى (word 4) · referent
- 18:11/v045 The excavated cave at Rujayb in Jordan, forgotten for centuries, is considered the strongest fit for the Qur'anic cave based on archaeological signs and some Muslim reports. (The author also says no proposed cave is conclusively identified.) [TABATABAI+]
- 18:11/v046 Other proposed caves lie on Mount Qasiyun near Damascus, at Petra, in Scandinavia with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and near Nakhchivan. [TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v047 No proposed cave can be securely identified as the Qur'anic one; the Scandinavian and Caucasus claims conflict with the story's Roman setting and the limits of Roman power. (This qualifies the author's relative preference for Rujayb.) [TABATABAI+]

## فِى (word 4) · reports

- 18:11/v049 At sunset, after eating and talking, the youths and their dog slept in the cave, with their provisions nearby or beside their heads. (Tabari places the dog at the entrance.) [TAB-FULL,THALABI]
- 18:11/v050 When Daqyanus returned and threatened the believers, Tamlikha warned the youths, who prostrated and prayed for protection from trial. [THALABI]

- 18:11/v052 God inspired Daqyanus to seal the cave so the youths' bodies would be preserved for a later community as a sign of resurrection. (These reports describe a divine purpose for sealing the cave.) [TAB-FULL,THALABI]
- 18:11/v053 Daqyanus sealed the cave to let the youths die from hunger and thirst, believing them awake, while they had already been placed in a sleep-like death or protective sleep. (The sources describe the condition as sleep-like death or protective sleep.) [IBNATIYYA-FULL,THALABI]
- 18:11/v054 Wahb reports that the king's men found the cave but were terrified when trying to enter; they blocked it and left the youths to die. [وهب بن منبه: IBNJAWZI-ZAD,THALABI]

- 18:11/v056 Their people lost the youths' trail and kept a record of their names and disappearance in the king's treasury. [عبيد بن عمير: THALABI]
- 18:11/v057 A landowner named Olyas in one account removed the cave's covering stones or barrier to build a sheep pen. [THALABI; ابن السائب: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 18:11/v058 Wahb says a shepherd opened the cave to shelter his flock from rain, and God restored the youths' souls the following morning. (This is a different opening account from the sheep-pen report.) [وهب بن منبه: IBNJAWZI-ZAD,THALABI]
- 18:11/v059 A report depicts the youths as believers under an idolatrous tyrant who killed dissenters; they feigned a hunt to flee, and a shepherd and his dog joined after the shepherd accepted their faith. [أبو عبد الله عليه السلام: TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v060 A report says the youths entered the cave with the dog, slept until the old king and city had passed away, and awoke thinking only a day or part of one had elapsed. [أبو عبد الله عليه السلام: TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v061 Most historians place the story between Jesus and Muhammad; it is absent from the two Testaments, although some Muslim reports say Quraysh learned it from Jews and Christians preserved it extensively. [أهل الرواية والتاريخ: TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v062 Christian accounts resemble Ibn Ishaq's report from Ibn Abbas in some respects but differ from Muslim accounts about the number of sleepers, the dog, and the sleep's duration. [TABATABAI]
- 18:11/v063 Amman occupies the site of Philadelphia, a major city before Islam that remained under Roman control from the early second century until the Muslim conquest. [TABATABAI]

## سِنِينَ (word 6) · grammar
- 18:11/v065 'Years' is an accusative adverb of time governed by 'struck'; it can also appear with declension on the nūn. (The alternative declension is noted by al-Nahhas.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,NAHHAS,SAMIN-DURR,TABATABAI,TABRISI]

## سِنِينَ (word 6) · rhetoric
- 18:11/v066 An abundant count better displays divine power, whereas a small count better suits the claim that the story is not uniquely astonishing; the long human span is like part of a day to God. (Bursevi likewise offers magnifying divine power or making the duration small beside God's timeless measure.) [ALUSI-FULL,BURSEVI]
- 18:11/v067 The indefinite wording for the years reflects uncertainty about the duration amid the polytheism of that era. [BIQAI-FULL]

## سِنِينَ (word 6) · interpretation
- 18:11/v068 Specifying that the years were counted emphasizes a long or abundant span, since a small quantity needs no enumeration. (Al-Zajjaj is repeatedly cited for the small-versus-large counting argument; Ibn Ashur compares Aisha's report of many nights of retreat at Hira.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,KASHSHAF-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,TABRISI,WAHIDI-BASIT,ZAJJAJ; الزجاج: BIQAI-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,TABATABAI; عائشة: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:11/v069 A numbered span can suggest either many years or few, depending on the point of comparison; even a long span is small in God's reckoning. (Abu Hayyan rejects al-Zamakhshari's comparison with 23:113: that passage calls worldly life short when people see the promised punishment.) [BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,KASHSHAF-FULL; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,TABATABAI; الراغب وغيره: ALUSI-FULL; Z: STUDYQURAN]
- 18:11/v070 'Counted' can suggest a small, readily numbered span, fitting the passage's point that this story is not uniquely astonishing and that the sleepers were alive rather than dead. (Tabatabai prefers this reading; al-Alusi presents it as one side of an abundance-versus-scarcity comparison.) [ALUSI-FULL,TABATABAI+]
- 18:11/v071 The youths slept in the cave for three hundred and nine years. (Mujahid places the period under the tyrant Deqianus; other notes connect the number to later verses.) [BURSEVI,MUQATIL,RAZI-FULL,STUDYQURAN; مجاهد: TAB-FULL]
