# 74:34: 39 views from 50 rows (sol-high)

## whole verse · rhetoric
- 74:34/v001 The preceding 'nay' rebukes deniers and rebels, specifically rejecting claims that people can know the number of God's hosts or overcome them. [IBNATIYYA-FULL; Āl, Ṭ: STUDYQURAN]
- 74:34/v002 The emphatic negation precedes the oath because the audience's denial is objectionable enough to be rejected before proof is offered. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:34/v003 The oaths substantiate the following claim by answering disbelievers who ask why the promised Judgment has not yet come. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 74:34/v004 The oaths honor divine signs and direct reflection to God, who sustains all existence; they also emphasize the importance of the warning to humanity. [IBNATIYYA-FULL,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:34/v005 Nature's manifestations point to the Hereafter and expose heedlessness; the Qur'an warns people to prepare, while evaders retain stubbornness as their excuse. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:34/v006 The moon's gradual phases answer why Judgment is delayed: humanity likewise advances through stages, and the Day arrives at God's appointed time when the universe's purpose is fulfilled. (The note cites 36:39 for the moon's phases.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:34/v007 God's signs follow a prescribed plan whose benefits only He knows, so justice will come at its appointed time despite demands for haste. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## whole verse · links
- 74:34/v008 The preceding moon oath is explained as referring to the crescent after its third night. [أبو الليث: BURSEVI]
- 74:34/v009 The oath recalls other Qur'anic oaths by the moon and by the alternation of night and day. (The note cites 84:16–18, 89:1–4, 91:1–4, 92:1–2, and 81:17–18.) [Āl, Ṭ: STUDYQURAN]

## وَٱلصُّبْحِ (word 1) · rhetoric
- 74:34/v010 The brightening morning is a third object of the oath, coordinated with the moon and night clauses. [BURSEVI,MAWARDI-FULL; قتادة: TABRISI]
- 74:34/v011 Some take the oaths as sworn by the Lord of the moon, night, and morning, because an oath may be made only by God. [TABRISI]
- 74:34/v012 The answer to the oath follows the morning clause. [NASAFI-FULL]

## وَٱلصُّبْحِ (word 1) · interpretation
- 74:34/v013 Specifying dawn invites contemplation of its marvels and the ordered regularity of its many motions. [فتح الرحمن: BURSEVI]
- 74:34/v014 The second dawn is the sun's white glow beneath the red twilight; sunset proceeds from red to white to darkness, and sunrise reverses that order before the sun's disk appears. [قوت القلوب: BURSEVI]
- 74:34/v015 Sunrise illuminating darkness depicts knowledge and guidance arriving and dispelling confusion. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 74:34/v016 Night yielding to morning illustrates this world's darkness giving way to Judgment at its appointed time, as certainly as daily dawn arrives. (The note cites 81:17–18.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:34/v017 The daily alternation of night and dawn rehearses Judgment and continually offers sincere truth-seekers evidence of that Day. (The note cites 81:17–18.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## وَٱلصُّبْحِ (word 1) · links
- 74:34/v018 The morning's unveiling is set against the night's retreat; idbār reverses night's arrival while isfār describes dawn clearing. [TABATABAI]

## وَٱلصُّبْحِ (word 1) · inward
- 74:34/v019 Dawn's brightening signifies lights of spiritual illumination appearing in hearts. [القاسم: SULAMI]
- 74:34/v020 Dawn is spirit-light fully illuminating a receptive, clear heart and removing its darkness, after the night of the lower self or ego recedes. (Both accounts also take the moon as the receptive, clear heart.) [QASHANI; الكاشفي: BURSEVI]

## إِذَآ (word 2) · grammar
- 74:34/v021 The 'when' of dawn is future-looking because dawn follows the night. [BURSEVI]

## إِذَآ (word 2) · readings
- 74:34/v022 A reading attributed to ʿĪsā ibn al-Faḍl and Ibn al-Sumayfi is written 'idhā asfara' in this note and likened to morning casting darkness off its face; Abū Ḥātim considers it weak. [عيسى بن الفضيل وابن السميفع: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 74:34/v023 A reading of the preceding night clause with idhā and hamzated adbara parallels idhā asfara; the codices of ʿAbd Allāh and Ubayy reflect that parallel. [الحسن وأبو رزين وأبو رجاء وابن يعمر والسلمي وطلحة والأعمش ويونس بن عبيد ومطر: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 74:34/v024 Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī regards both readings of the preceding adbara as sound. [أبو علي الفارسي: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## أَسْفَرَ (word 3) · meaning
- 74:34/v025 Asfara means that the morning or dawn becomes bright, clear, and luminous. (Qatāda also describes the dawn as coming on.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,QUSHAYRI,RAZI-FULL,TAB-FULL,TABRISI,WAHIDI-BASIT,WAHIDI-WAJIZ; قتادة: TAB-FULL,TABRISI]
- 74:34/v026 Dawn spreads its light before sunrise. [IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 74:34/v027 The morning's brightening clears darkness and makes visible objects appear. [TABRISI]
- 74:34/v028 Isfār denotes appearance or becoming visible; the cited verse uses sufūr for a woman appearing without her veil. [JISHUMI]
- 74:34/v029 A shining face can be described with this word, including the radiant faces of 80:38. [QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 74:34/v030 Uncovering a woman's face offers a possible sense or derivation for safara or asfara. [ابن السميقع: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 74:34/v031 The word may derive from sweeping darkness away, as a broom clears a house. [QURTUBI-FULL]
- 74:34/v032 Al-Rāghib distinguishes safara, uncovering a physical covering, from asfara, a color becoming clear and bright. [الراغب: BURSEVI]
- 74:34/v033 The prophetic instruction to pray fajr at isfār uses the word in the sense of evident light, after doubt about dawn has gone. [RAZI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 74:34/v034 The expression 'pray at isfār' can mean entering the dawn time. [BURSEVI]

## أَسْفَرَ (word 3) · readings
- 74:34/v036 The common or majority reading uses the quadriliteral asfara with alif. [ALUSI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL; الجمهور: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; عيسى بن الفضل وابن السميفع: SAMIN-DURR]
- 74:34/v037 Ibn al-Sumayqa or al-Sumayfi and ʿĪsā ibn al-Faḍl read the triliteral safara without alif; it is also a valid or dialectal form for dawn's brightening. (The reading is also explained as dawn removing darkness from its face.) [ابن السميقع: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL; ابن السميفع وعيسى بن الفضل: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; عيسى بن الفضل وابن السميفع: SAMIN-DURR]

## أَسْفَرَ (word 3) · reports
- 74:34/v038 The Prophet's instruction to delay dawn prayer until it brightens is explained as praying after the light appears. [النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 74:34/v039 The Prophet's instruction about dawn prayer is alternatively explained as lengthening the prayer until sunrise approaches. (The two explanations differ over whether the delay precedes or occurs within the prayer.) [النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: QURTUBI-FULL]
