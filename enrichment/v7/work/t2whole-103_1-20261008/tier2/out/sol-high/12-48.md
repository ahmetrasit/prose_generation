# 12:48: 45 views from 109 rows (sol-high)

## whole verse · reports
- 12:48/v001 Exegetes differ whether Joseph's interpretation came from knowledge God taught him or from direct revelation; the unasked-for prediction of a further abundant year supports revelation. (Qatada says the first fertile and next drought years follow the dream, while God gave Joseph knowledge of the later abundant year.) [WAHIDI-BASIT; قتادة: RAZI-FULL]
- 12:48/v002 A report says Joseph stockpiled harvests, then sold food during famine for money, valuables, livestock, servants, property, land, and finally freedom; he later freed the Egyptians and restored their possessions and kingship. (The report is attributed to al-Rida.) [الرضا عليه السلام: TABATABAI]
- 12:48/v003 The author omits many similar reports about the famine economy because most do not directly aid interpretation of these verses. [TABATABAI]
- 12:48/v004 Al-Qummi reports the king's dream of seven fat cows eaten by seven lean cows and green ears with dry ones; after a delay, the cupbearer recalled it, and Joseph interpreted plentiful years, famine years, then rain. [تفسير القمي: TABATABAI]
- 12:48/v005 Al-Sadiq interprets the following year as a year of rain; a report gives the passive reading 'they are rained upon,' deriving the verb from rain rather than rescue, and al-Ayyashi transmits the same reading. (These notes concern the year after the seven severe years.) [أبو عبد الله عليه السلام: TABATABAI]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 12:48/v006 Joseph's reply combines interpretation of the dream by meaning rather than wording, practical advice, and a report about the unseen year after the famine. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 12:48/v007 The dream presents the practical measures needed for famine: repeated planting, preserving grain in its ears, and rationing reserves, rather than merely forecasting fertile and barren years. (Tabatabai rejects interpreters who read Joseph's words simply as a forecast of seven fertile and seven barren years.) [TABATABAI+,TABATABAI-]
- 12:48/v008 The Qur'anic Joseph story illustrates God's hidden direction of human affairs: people may dislike what benefits them or love what harms them, unlike Genesis' romantic account of Joseph's fortunes. (The note cites 2:216.) [ASAD-NOTES]
- 12:48/v009 Joseph's interpretation covers the next fifteen years of Egypt's agricultural cycle. [STUDYQURAN]

## whole verse · theology
- 12:48/v010 An unbeliever's dream can be true and interpreted as seen, especially when it concerns a believer; here it also serves as a sign and miracle for a messenger. [QURTUBI-FULL]

## سَبْعٌۭ (word 6) · meaning
- 12:48/v012 The years are called severe because the people living through them suffer hardship. (Tabatabai prefers the competing sense of years that attack, because it fits the following image of devouring.) [BAGHAWI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,MAWARDI-FULL,TABATABAI+]
- 12:48/v013 Shidda denotes severity, firmness, or difficulty; an account lists seven uses of the term, including time, anger, pain, drink, and the body. [TABRISI]
- 12:48/v014 Arabic descriptions of famine say a year strips everything bare and dries stars, meat, and bone. [IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## سَبْعٌۭ (word 6) · grammar
- 12:48/v015 The counted noun 'years' is omitted after 'seven severe ones' and understood from the preceding mention of seven years. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]

## سَبْعٌۭ (word 6) · rhetoric
- 12:48/v016 Calling the years severe is a rational metaphor: hardship occurs during them. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:48/v017 The severe years are pictured as attackers or ravenous predators that pounce and devour the stored food; this reading of 'severe' fits the following image of eating. (Tabatabai prefers this sense over severity as hardship for the people.) [TABATABAI,TABATABAI+]

## سَبْعٌۭ (word 6) · referent
- 12:48/v018 The lean cows and dry ears of the king's dream represent the seven barren or famine years; the fat cows represent fertile years, and ears of grain signify provisions. (Ibn Ashur explains agricultural years through cows' fatness and leanness.) [BIQAI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNKATHIR-FULL,TABATABAI,WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## سَبْعٌۭ (word 6) · reports
- 12:48/v019 Joseph recognized the first severe year when a man who normally left half of his food ate his whole portion; one report says Joseph prepared food for two. (The reports name Ibn Zayd or Zayd ibn Aslam and differ in how they describe the portion.) [ابن زيد: IBNABIHATIM; زيد بن أسلم عن أبيه: MAWARDI-FULL; زيد بن أسلم: QURTUBI-FULL,TABRISI]
- 12:48/v020 The Prophet prayed for Quraysh to experience seven years like Joseph's famine; the hardship began but ended before seven years. [IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## سَبْعٌۭ (word 6) · links

## يَأْكُلْنَ (word 8) · meaning
- 12:48/v024 Eating can mean using up, wearing away, or destroying; Arabs say that a journey 'ate' a camel's flesh, and a verse by Dhu al-Rumma illustrates this use. (Ibn Ashur also cites Qur'an 4:2 for the sense of consumption.) [ابن الأنباري: WAHIDI-BASIT; ذي الرمة: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,TABRISI; بعضهم: ALUSI-FULL]

## يَأْكُلْنَ (word 8) · rhetoric
- 12:48/v025 Eating is attributed figuratively to the years, although their inhabitants actually eat the stored food during them. Poetry similarly attributes heedlessness to day and sleep to night. (The notes illustrate temporal attribution with day or night, including comparisons to 10:67, 27:86, and 40:61.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,KASHSHAF-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR,TAB-FULL,TABRISI,THALABI,WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 12:48/v026 Describing the years as eating matches the dream's image of lean cows consuming fat cows. (Bursevi says the dream cows did not literally eat one another: the image means famine consumes stored grain.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI]

## يَأْكُلْنَ (word 8) · readings
- 12:48/v027 Al-Sulami reads the verb as 'they eat' in the third person, referring to the people, instead of addressing the listeners. [السلمي: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## يَأْكُلْنَ (word 8) · referent
- 12:48/v028 The lean cows eating the fat cows picture famine using up the provisions produced during prosperous years; the seven dry ears signify provisions stored across seven years. (Tabatabai treats the cows as yearly provisions from sustained planting.) [IBNASHUR-FULL,TABATABAI]

## يَأْكُلْنَ (word 8) · interpretation
