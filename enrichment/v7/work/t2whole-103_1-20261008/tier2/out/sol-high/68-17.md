## whole verse · meaning
- 68:17/v001 Al-ṣarīm can describe the garden as cut down, black as night, dried like daytime vegetation, empty, or sandy; ṣārimīn means harvesters. (These are alternative senses of a later word in the orchard episode.) [KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 68:17/v002 Yatakhāfatūn means whispering; khafiya, khafata, and khafada all denote concealment, and al-khafdūd names a bat. [KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 68:17/v003 Ḥard can describe a year withholding its good, camels withholding milk, or the sons' resolve and speed to deprive the poor. (A poem and the name of swift birds support the speed sense; their attempt to deprive the poor left them deprived instead.) [KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 68:17/v004 An alternative takes ḥard as the anger the owners could muster toward one another, in line with their subsequent mutual blame. (This differs from senses of resolve, speed, or withholding.) [KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 68:17/v005 Awsaṭuhum means the most just and best among them, from language for a person in the middle of their people; compare the 'middle community.' [KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 68:17/v006 Their declaration that they were rāghibūn to their Lord means seeking good from Him and hoping for pardon. [KASHSHAF-FULL]

- 68:17/v024 Their worldly punishment resembles that of Mecca's people, while punishment in the afterlife is greater. [KASHSHAF-FULL]

- 68:17/v027 The story warns deniers that blessings and power can vanish suddenly, leaving loss, useless grief, and repentance too late. - 68:17/v028 The story illustrates a recurring divine law across generations, rather than an isolated event in Muhammad's time. [ELMALILI]
- 68:17/v029 If these verses were revealed with the preceding passage, the warning applies to deniers generally and recalls their surprising defeat at Badr as worldly proof that apparent causes can fail. (This is a conditional allusion to Badr, distinct from making Badr the occasion of revelation.) [TABATABAI]

## إِذْ (word 7) · law
- 68:17/v073 Their resolve to deny the poor was blameworthy before they acted; actually taking property and denying rights would be worse. [JISHUMI]
- 68:17/v074 Because no one can be sure a future statement will come true, the owners' blame for omitting an exception supports requiring one when speaking about the future. (The omission is debated as failing to say 'if God wills' or failing to reserve the poor's share.) [JISHUMI]
