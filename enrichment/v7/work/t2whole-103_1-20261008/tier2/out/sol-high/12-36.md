## whole verse · interpretation
- 12:36/v010 Symbolic dreams range from manageable transformations to tangled sequences; the latter are confused dreams that resist interpretation, unlike clear or interpretable symbolic dreams. [TABATABAI]

## whole verse · theology
- 12:36/v011 True dreams exist and may disclose future events; denying every true dream conflicts with scripture and prophetic reports. Clear dreams can correspond to later events without needing interpretation. (Natural explanations of some dreams do not establish that all dreams are unreal.) [TABATABAI,TABATABAI-,ZAJJAJ; الزجاج: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 12:36/v012 Dream interpretation can disclose reality because the sleeping rational soul is less occupied with the body and encounters higher realities, which appear as images requiring an interpreter. [RAZI-FULL]
- 12:36/v013 Ordinary dreams can arise from imagination recombining stored images under bodily conditions, temperament, emotions, chance, or preoccupations; these dreams need not foretell external events, though they still have causes. [TABATABAI]
- 12:36/v014 True dreams have a real relation to external or hidden events beyond ordinary bodily and emotional causes; the sleeping soul may connect to causes beyond the physical world. [TABATABAI,TABATABAI-]
- 12:36/v015 The proposed account distinguishes changing physical matter, an imaginal world of immaterial forms that causes physical events, and an intellectual realm of universal truths that causes the imaginal realm. [TABATABAI]
- 12:36/v016 A soul may grasp universal causes directly or picture them through familiar images; sound clear dreams perceive causes without distortion, while symbolic dreams use images or opposites and require interpretation. (Examples are a crown for honor and darkness for ignorance.) [TABATABAI]

## بِتَأْوِيلِهِۦٓ (word 23) · grammar
- 12:36/v079 The singular pronoun refers to all that the men recounted, like 'that,' or to each man's own dream or to the class of dreams. (The notes offer these as alternative antecedents.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,TABATABAI; الشيخ: SAMIN-DURR]

## إِنَّا (word 24) · rhetoric
- 12:36/v080 Calling Joseph a benefactor is a polite appeal: they promise to count interpretation as a kindness to them, or approach him through his established generosity. (Unlike the prior-kindness and skill readings, this praise is conditional on Joseph's response.) [QUSHAYRI,TAB-FULL; ابن إسحاق: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,MAWARDI-FULL,TAB-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; مجاهد: IBNABIHATIM; الفراء وابن إسحاق: THALABI; ابن إسحق: QURTUBI-FULL]

## إِنَّا (word 24) · interpretation
- 12:36/v085 They praised Joseph for treating prisoners well through care, courtesy, comfort, and practical help; his conduct made him trustworthy to interpret for them. (Al-Tabari prefers practical kindness; other notes prefer knowledge or a conditional request.) [BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,TAB-FULL,TAB-FULL+; قالوا: ZAJJAJ; مجاهد: IBNABIHATIM; الضحاك: MAWARDI-FULL; الضحاك وقتادة: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL; أبو عبد الله عليه السلام: TABATABAI; Al-Rāzī: STUDYQURAN]
- 12:36/v086 They meant Joseph was accomplished in knowledge or especially skilled at interpreting dreams; ihsān here can mean doing something precisely and well. (Ibn al-Anbari supplies an omitted object, 'in knowledge'; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they inferred skill from intelligence without direct proof.) [ALUSI-FULL,ASAD-NOTES,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,FARRA,IBNASHUR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL; قالوا: ZAJJAJ; الفراء: IBNJAWZI-ZAD,QURTUBI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; الجمهور: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL; الزجاج: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; Baghawi, Zamakhshari, Baydawi: ASAD-NOTES]
- 12:36/v087 Joseph is good to himself by obeying God, or he accepts excuses and puts others' rights first; these are offered as additional senses of his excellence. [MAWARDI-FULL; ابن الأنباري: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 12:36/v088 The men saw signs of Joseph's goodness and assumed that a pure heart perceives events soundly; another account says they had seen him interpret other prisoners' dreams. [TABATABAI; JJ: STUDYQURAN]
- 12:36/v089 The dreamers asked Joseph for interpretation because they regarded him as a benefactor. [WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## إِنَّا (word 24) · links
- 12:36/v090 God made Joseph's skill and kindness in prison a path to release: interpreting the two inmates' dreams later led to the king's dream and Joseph's rise. (Al-Qushayri emphasizes that companionship can bear fruit after delay, despite the cellmate forgetting Joseph.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR,QUSHAYRI,WAHIDI-BASIT]
