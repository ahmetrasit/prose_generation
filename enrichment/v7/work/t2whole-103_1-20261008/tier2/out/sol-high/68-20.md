## whole verse · reports
- 68:20/v001 A heavenly visitor or divine command struck the garden at night while its owners slept; Arabic ṭāʾif ordinarily denotes a nighttime visitor, although daytime can be specified. [IBNKATHIR-FULL,QUSHAYRI,TAB-FULL,TABATABAI; قتادة: DURR-FULL]
- 68:20/v002 Fire destroyed the orchard overnight and consumed its fruit, leaving it scorched or blackened. [BURSEVI,ELMALILI,FARRA,QUSHAYRI; مقاتل: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 68:20/v003 The sons at first thought they had lost their way, then recognized that their refusal to help the poor had brought the loss of the garden. [FARRA]
- 68:20/v004 A Prophetic warning says disobedience can withhold provision already prepared for a person; the garden owners lost their crop because of sin. One version also names lost knowledge and missed night prayer. [عبد الله بن مسعود: IBNKATHIR-FULL; النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: DURR-FULL; the Prophet: STUDYQURAN]
- 68:20/v005 Expanded accounts say the orchard belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; they place it near Ṣanʿāʾ in Yemen, in Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. [BINTSHATI]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 68:20/v006 The loss of the garden's fruit may have been punishment or a trial that served as grace, since it brought the owners to repentance. [JISHUMI]
- 68:20/v007 The disaster came wholly unexpectedly and devastated the orchard in an instant; attributing it to God highlights that sudden ruin, and ‘from your Lord’ reassures the Prophet amid Quraysh's apparent ease. (One description calls its reduction to nothing in a blink miraculous.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 68:20/v008 The heirs' sworn plan came true only to their harm and in an unforeseen way, without their carrying it out; their wish to take everything and deny charity ended in the orchard's complete ruin. [BIQAI-FULL,ELMALILI,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## كَٱلصَّرِيمِ (word 2) · meaning
- 68:20/v013 Ṣarīm is glossed as black ash in a reported dialectal usage, variously named Khudhaym, Khuzaima, Khuzaymī, Judhayma, or Khuzaʿa in the notes. (The dialect names vary across sources; the gloss is the shared point.) [ابن عباس: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,MAWARDI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,THALABI; BINTSHATI,BIQAI-FULL,ELMALILI,TABRISI; جذيمة أو خزيمة: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:20/v014 Both night and day are called ṣarīm because each is separated or cut off from the other; the word expresses severance. (This derivation supports either the night or the day reading.) [BAYDAWI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB,JISHUMI,MAJAZ,RAZI-FULL,THALABI; شمر: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; الأخفش: BAGHAWI-FULL; الفراء، ابن قتيبة: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; الثوري: ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:20/v015 Ṣarīm can specifically mean dawn or morning severed from night. (Al-Akhfash is repeatedly named for this explanation.) [الأخفش: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,THALABI,WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL]
- 68:20/v016 One derivation calls night ṣarīm because its darkness cuts off people's movement or sight; an objection says this cannot explain why day bears the same name. (Al-Qushayrī rejects the movement based derivation on the day usage.) [غيره: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; شمر: QURTUBI-FULL; القشيري: QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 68:20/v017 Ṣarīm fundamentally denotes something wholly cut off or severed; its applications include cut fruit, a harvested field, a portion of night, and separated barren sand. [BINTSHATI,ELMALILI,MAJAZ,NAHHAS,THALABI]
- 68:20/v018 In a dialogue with Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq, Ibn ʿAbbās says ṣarīm means gold and cites an anonymous poem using the word. [ابن عباس: DURR-FULL]

## كَٱلصَّرِيمِ (word 2) · grammar
- 68:20/v019 Ṣarīm can be a passive-form adjective meaning maṣrūm, something cut off, like qatīl meaning maqtūl. [ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,NAHHAS,RAZI-FULL; الحسن: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 68:20/v020 A derivation of ṣarīm for night takes it as an active form: darkness cuts off people's ability to move. (The source says this derivation does not explain the word's use for day.) [غيره: WAHIDI-BASIT]
