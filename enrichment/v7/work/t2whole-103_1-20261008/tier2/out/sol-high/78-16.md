## وَجَنَّٰتٍ (word 1) · meaning
- 78:16/v001 Jannah is a tree-covered garden that conceals the earth; it can also name the covering trees themselves. Al-Farrāʾ distinguishes a palm grove from a vine-bearing firdaws, and a line by Zuhayr uses jannah for sheltering trees. [ALUSI-FULL; زهير: ALUSI-FULL]

## وَجَنَّٰتٍ (word 1) · grammar
- 78:16/v002 The syntax may omit a repeated 'We bring forth' before 'gardens'. [QURTUBI-FULL]

## وَجَنَّٰتٍ (word 1) · rhetoric
- 78:16/v003 'Gardens' stands for their trees; 'trees of' is understood from context. [IBNASHUR-FULL,TABRISI]
- 78:16/v004 The gardens evoke shade, fruit, water, and beauty; their dense growth adds beauty even though farmers may space trees for a better yield. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:16/v005 The sky, earth, rain, and plants resemble a reproductive pair: sky as male, earth as female, water as semen, and vegetation as offspring; springs and pools are implied. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 78:16/v006 Both paired sūras use affirmative questions to reprove deniers; each verse reproaches them, while glad tidings for believers are understood against the warning. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## وَجَنَّٰتٍ (word 1) · interpretation
- 78:16/v009 Rain-grown gardens provide varied fruits with diverse colors, flavors, and scents. [IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 78:16/v010 God is the primary cause of the gardens and other living growth; rain is the means by which He produces them, consistent with life being made from water. (The note mentions 21:30, 24:25, and 25:54.) [STUDYQURAN]
- 78:16/v011 The created gardens and other signs demonstrate the Creator's power to resurrect the dead and create the afterlife. (Muqātil says each sign is more astonishing than resurrection; Kur'an Yolu includes the heavens, sun, clouds, and growth among the signs.) [أبو إسحاق: WAHIDI-BASIT; المفسرون: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; مقاتل: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 78:16/v012 The acts of creation show God's power, wisdom, and purpose and so support resurrection; waking from sleep and plants emerging from earth serve as analogies, rebuking deniers' mockery. [ALUSI-FULL]
- 78:16/v013 Nature's purposeful order points to a conscious Creator and anticipates judgment over whether people follow moral standards known through instinct and revelation. (The note mentions 3:191.) [ASAD-NOTES]
- 78:16/v014 Signs of providence, mercy, wisdom, and power across earth and heaven show humanity is accountable and a fixed day of reckoning must come. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## وَجَنَّٰتٍ (word 1) · links
- 78:16/v015 The surrounding growth sequence covers three classes: husked grain harvested for food, grasses and fodder without husks or stems, and the trees that form gardens; 20:54 names the first two. (The grain includes barley and dates; the fodder includes grasses and crops.) [RAZI-FULL,TAB-FULL]
- 78:16/v016 The order of grain, plants, then orchards follows human basic food, animal fodder, and the less essential fruit of gardens. [RAZI-FULL]
- 78:16/v017 Rain brings forth grain for people, edible vegetation or fodder for livestock, and luxuriant gardens. (Ibn Kathīr adds that grain can be stored and plants eaten fresh.) [IBNKATHIR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 78:16/v018 The interwoven gardens grow together despite sharing water; their diversity is compared with the other verse mentioned as 13:4. [ابن عباس: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 78:16/v019 Paired with al-Mursalāt, the sūra uses providence in heaven and earth to establish judgment as the necessary end of a purposeful world, with reward for the God-fearing and punishment for rebels. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 78:16/v020 The sequence from 'Did We not make' through 'thick gardens' has no intervening stop; a necessary pause occurs at either 'pegs' or 'livelihood.' (The note mentions 78:6, 78:10, and 78:11.) [NASAFI-FULL]

## أَلْفَافًا (word 2) · meaning
- 78:16/v021 Alfāf describes gardens growing densely together, their trees, branches, leaves, or plants interwoven and clustered, sometimes one beside or over another with little open space. ## أَلْفَافًا (word 2) · grammar
- 78:16/v023 Alfāf can have laf or liff as its singular, a descriptive term for a wrapped or dense garden; most lexicographers accept liff, and a poetic line with 'jannah liff' attests it. (The notes variously write the singular as laf and as liff with kasra. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās prefer the direct singular because the shared interpretation is interwoven, gathered gardens, whereas lafāʾ chiefly means thick.)
