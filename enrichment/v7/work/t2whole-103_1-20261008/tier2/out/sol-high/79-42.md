## whole verse · interpretation
- 79:42/v002 The answer addresses both the request for timing and the use of delay to deny the Hour: it will occur, and those who see it will feel their wait lasted only part of a day. [IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## whole verse · links
- 79:42/v003 After establishing Resurrection and describing its general, moral, and punitive states, including the Great Calamity, the passage turns to the question of when it will happen. [BINTSHATI,IBNASHUR-FULL,RAZI-FULL]
- 79:42/v004 The sūrah establishes resurrection, gathering, and humanity's final fate after the preceding sūrahs' exposition; its oaths mark the subject's decisive climax. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:42/v005 The title al-Nāziʿāt points to angels extracting souls and then to Pharaoh and Moses; the names al-Sāhirah and al-Ṭāmmah also suit the oaths and the sūrah's progression. [BIQAI-FULL]

## يَسْـَٔلُونَكَ (word 1) · referent

(Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies them with the earlier questioners who asked whether they would be restored to their former state (79:10).) ## يَسْـَٔلُونَكَ (word 1) · reports
- 79:42/v008 A report attributes the verse's revelation to Meccan polytheists mockingly asking the Prophet when the Hour would come. (Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the Ibn ʿAbbās report's chain as weak.) [ابن عباس: QURTUBI-FULL,TABATABAI; some: STUDYQURAN]
- 79:42/v009 Reports from Ṭāriq ibn Shihāb and ʿĀʾishah say the Prophet frequently mentioned the Hour and was asked about it until this passage was revealed; ʿĀʾishah says he then stopped. [طارق بن شهاب: TAB-FULL; عائشة: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 79:42/v010 A report says the Prophet answered Bedouins' question by pointing to the youngest among them and saying their Hour would come if he lived a century. (Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the report as unsuitable date-setting because revelation repeatedly says only God knows the Hour.) [عائشة: TABATABAI-]

## يَسْـَٔلُونَكَ (word 1) · interpretation
- 79:42/v011 They ask mockingly or sarcastically about the Hour's time, rather than seeking information; the frightening descriptions themselves become material for their taunt. (The attribution to Makkan idolaters is reported in some sources; the occasion reports are recorded separately.) [BIQAI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,RAZI-FULL,STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI; ابن عباس: QURTUBI-FULL; some: STUDYQURAN]
- 79:42/v012 They impatiently demand that the Hour come soon, deeming it impossible because it has not yet occurred. (Rāzī cites 42:18 and Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 10:48 for demands that it be hastened.) [IBNASHUR-FULL,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,RAZI-FULL]
- 79:42/v013 The sarcastic question is meant to make the questioners' followers think Resurrection has no basis. [RAZI-FULL]
- 79:42/v014 The questioners aim to put the Prophet in difficulty by demanding a date he cannot give. [KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:42/v015 They take the Hour's delay as disproof, assuming an angry God would retaliate immediately as an impetuous human would; the Qur'an rejects that analogy. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:42/v016 The Prophet wished he could answer their timing question, hoping an answer would help them believe. [KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:42/v017 One reading asks why the Prophet is occupied with mentioning and disclosing the Hour's appointed time. (Rāzī pairs this reading with ʿĀʾishah's report that the Prophet kept mentioning and being asked about the Hour until this verse came down.) [RAZI-FULL]
- 79:42/v018 Another reading rebukes the timing question itself: the signs of the Hour's nearness are enough to prepare people, while its time belongs to God alone. (This differs from the reading that questions the Prophet's occupation with its appointed time.) [RAZI-FULL]

## ٱلسَّاعَةِ (word 3) · meaning
- 79:42/v019 In ordinary Arabic sāʿah can mean a portion of time, and the definite form can mean the present moment; later it came to mean a conventional sixty minutes and to name a timekeeping instrument. [BINTSHATI]
- 79:42/v020 An indefinite sāʿah in the Qur'an denotes a short, unspecified span, while the definite Hour consistently denotes the decisive event of the Afterlife. [BINTSHATI]

## ٱلسَّاعَةِ (word 3) · rhetoric
- 79:42/v021 The Qur'an's verbs of coming and rising present the Hour as active and momentous, marking a change in cosmic order. [BINTSHATI]
- 79:42/v022 Naming the event ‘the Hour’ instead of repeating ‘the Great Calamity’ gives the sentence independent force and varies the naming. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## ٱلسَّاعَةِ (word 3) · reports

## ٱلسَّاعَةِ (word 3) · interpretation

## أَيَّانَ (word 4) · meaning
- 79:42/v025 Ayyān means ‘when’ and asks about the time of a future event, as distinct from ayn, which asks about place. [BURSEVI,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,JISHUMI,ZAJJAJ]

## أَيَّانَ (word 4) · grammar
- 79:42/v026 Because a time cannot directly predicate an event, the clause is understood as ‘at what time is its appointed establishment?’ [BURSEVI]

## أَيَّانَ (word 4) · rhetoric
