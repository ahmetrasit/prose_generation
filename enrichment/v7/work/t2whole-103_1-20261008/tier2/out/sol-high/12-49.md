## whole verse · reports
- 12:49/v001 A report says Joseph tested the approaching famine by giving each man two portions; when one ate both, Joseph announced that the hard years had begun. [زيد بن أسلم: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/v002 A report recounts Joseph's famine economy: he stockpiled harvests, exchanged food successively for money, valuables, livestock, servants, property, land and people's freedom, then freed the Egyptians and restored their possessions and kingship. [الرضا عليه السلام: TABATABAI]
- 12:49/v003 Whether dreams take effect according to their first interpretation is debated: counselors dismissed the king's dream as confused; al-Alusi responds that such dismissal may not count as interpretation, and Ibn al-ʿArabī restricts the rule to dreams allowing several meanings. (Al-Alusi cites the hadith of a dream suspended on a bird's wing until interpreted.) [البلخي: ALUSI-FULL; ابن العربي: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/v004 Al-Ṣādiq reports a revealed wording 'what you bring near for them' in the preceding passage and explains the following year as one of rain. [أبو عبد الله عليه السلام: TABATABAI]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 12:49/v005 The commentator omits other famine-economy reports because most do not directly clarify these verses. [TABATABAI]
- 12:49/v006 The passage is cited to show that an unbeliever may have a true dream; advice on when to ask for interpretation and a claim that a sleeping dream interprets itself are also transmitted, though al-Alusi finds many such claims opaque. [ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:49/v007 Joseph guided Egypt through the drought and gained admiration from the king and ordinary people. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:49/v008 Joseph's revealed forecast and the king's dream illustrate that dreams should be interpreted by qualified people. [KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## whole verse · links
- 12:49/v009 Unlike a romantic account of Joseph's fortunes, the Qur'anic story illustrates God's hidden direction of human affairs: people may dislike what benefits them or love what harms them. [ASAD-NOTES]

## فِيهِ (word 7) · rhetoric
- 12:49/v036 Repeating fīhi separates rain or relief as God's act from pressing as people's act; it can also enumerate benefits, distinguish times or kinds of benefit, emphasize how much greater the rain is than during the drought, or serve the rhyme. (Al-Alusi also notes that fronting the phrase marks its focus.) [ALUSI-FULL,BURSEVI]
- 12:49/v037 Mentioning pressed crops explicitly is special good news because rain does not ensure them as directly as grain; it may also suit the king's circumstances and the second-person reading. (Islahi links grape pressing specifically to the king's wine-server.) [ALUSI-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:49/v038 Joseph shifts from direct address to 'people' and third-person pressing, suggesting they will no longer need special management of their livelihoods. [TABATABAI]

## فِيهِ (word 7) · interpretation
- 12:49/v046 The people cry to God in the extreme drought, and their pleas are answered with rain and aid. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:49/v047 Egyptian agriculture can still be described as receiving rain because rain upstream in Sudan feeds the Nile floods on which Egyptian crops depend. (Al-Tabataba'i rejects the objection that Egypt relies on Nile flooding rather than local rain.) [ASAD-NOTES,TABATABAI-]
- 12:49/v048 The rain sense fits the next clause about pressing produce better than the general sense of aid. (Al-Tabataba'i allows aid to fit better if the following verb is read passively as being rained upon.) [TABATABAI,TABATABAI+]
- 12:49/v049 Pressing produce visibly marks the transition to prosperity: during the famine people pressed nothing, according to one report, or had gone fourteen years without pressing oil or grapes. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,FARISI-HUJJA,IBNATIYYA-FULL,TABRISI; أبو علي الفارسي: WAHIDI-BASIT+; R: STUDYQURAN]
- 12:49/v050 Al-Tabari rejects deriving the active verb from rescue because the Companions and Successors unanimously interpreted it as pressing produce; he also rejects a report through al-Faraj ibn Faḍāla from ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭalḥa as contrary to known Arabic and Ibn ʿAbbās's usage. (Ibn ʿAtiyya says al-Tabari supplies no proof against the rescue derivation.) [TAB-FULL-; الطبري: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 12:49/v051 The passive rain reading is preferred in reports from ʿAlī and al-Ṣādiq: it describes rain falling on people, as with the rain clouds of 78:14, rather than pressing wine.
