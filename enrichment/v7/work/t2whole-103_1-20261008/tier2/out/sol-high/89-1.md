## whole verse · interpretation

- 89:1/v021 The tranquil soul submits to God, remains steady through gain and loss, and receives the promised peaceful outcome. [STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI]
- 89:1/v022 The basmala depicts God gathering scattered people, granting clarifying guidance and faith, and reserving contentment and gardens for His chosen servants. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 89:1/v023 A cultic understanding of the ten nights remains persuasive despite objections to Neuwirth's liturgical reading of cosmic oath sequences. [CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:1/v024 The harmony of opposed natural forces points to one wise and powerful sovereign; rival deities would destroy that harmony. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/v025 Without a Day of Judgment, good and evil would be treated alike and creation would lack purpose. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/v026 Night and daylight sustain material life; ease and hardship train gratitude and patience, preparing the person for the tranquil state described later. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

- 89:1/v028 Reports about Dhu al-Hijjah's nights, Arafah, and the Sacrifice Day best fit the oath sequence and sura's purpose, gathering its possible senses and urging worship within life's limited time for afterlife joy. [ELMALILI+]
- 89:1/v029 The Prophet warns through signs in heaven and earth even when people fail to see them; he cannot make the blind accept guidance. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/v030 Al-fajr denotes dawn alongside even and odd, the withdrawing night, and intellect in the subsequent oaths. [TABATABAI]

## whole verse · links
- 89:1/v032 Al-Ghashiyah's ending announces return and reckoning; this sura renews that warning through God's watchfulness, while the dawn and pilgrims' rites evoke rising again to reward or punishment. (Al-Biqai specifies sleepers rising from death-like sleep, pilgrim clothing, talbiya, and travel; Islahi reasons that wisdom and mercy require accountability.) [ALUSI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,JISHUMI]
- 89:1/v033 The sura follows al-Ghashiyah's two kinds of faces with arrogant deniers and the blessed tranquil soul; its opening oaths reinforce the prior promise and threat and echo its opening questions. [ALUSI-FULL,STUDYQURAN]
- 89:1/v034 Dawn's opposition to departing night ties the first and last oaths together; the ten nights and middle oaths stand between and elaborate the night side. (The ten nights uniquely quantify night among parallel Quranic dawn and night oaths.) [CORPUSCORANICUM,IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/v035 The succession of dawn, crescent, and night turns physical light emerging from darkness into an image of truth after tyranny and prepares the sura's tests and opposed outcomes. [BINTSHATI]
- 89:1/v036 The oaths' cosmic contrasts of dawn/night and even/odd resemble other Quranic oath sequences, where natural duality anticipates good and evil and their different afterlife recompense. (Islahi adds that this world's incomplete moral pairs call for an afterlife to complete judgment.) [CORPUSCORANICUM,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/v037 The opening oath resembles oath openings in earlier suras 93, 95, 100, and 103 and roughly contemporary suras 91 and 92. (The commentary refers readers to its discussion of the oath type at 93:1–2.) [CORPUSCORANICUM]

## وَٱلْفَجْرِ (word 1) · meaning
- 89:1/v039 Fajr is the first light or time of daybreak: darkness splits at the horizon and daylight begins to appear. (The word is also used by extension for the time of the light's appearance.) [ALUSI-FULL,BINTSHATI,ELMALILI,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,JISHUMI,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,MAWARDI-FULL,QUSHAYRI,TABRISI,ZAJJAJ; ابن عباس، علي بن عبيد الله: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; الراغب: BINTSHATI]
- 89:1/v040 The root evokes splitting or bursting forth, as with water emerging from earth; related words describe a person breaking from obedience. (Bint al-Shati prefers the broader idea of emergence or outburst over deriving all uses narrowly from splitting.) [ابن عباس، علي بن عبيد الله: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL,BINTSHATI,BINTSHATI+; الراغب: BINTSHATI]
- 89:1/v041 Arabic applies the root to emergence in light, goodness, generosity, and virtue, and to emergence in evil, sin, or transgression; the Fijar days were four pre-Islamic battle days. [BINTSHATI]
- 89:1/v042 The root occurs twenty-four times in the Quran; its verbs mostly concern water gushing, its noun can name rivers or a spring, and it also denotes wickedness opposite piety. Dawn occurs in six verses, whose imagery locates its white light after night darkness. (One non-water verbal use refers to transgression; 2:187 uses the white thread as a dawn sign.) [BINTSHATI]
- 89:1/v043 Unlike fajr, which marks the moment darkness gives way at the horizon, subh extends from dawn through the time after sunrise and is therefore qualified in other oaths. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/v044 Kufan grammarians describe dawn's light as white; Medinan grammarians call it red, citing Arabic use of the word for a red-dyed garment. (The disagreement concerns the color associated with dawn's light.) [أهل الكوفة: NAHHAS; أهل المدينة: NAHHAS]
- 89:1/v045 Dawn has a narrow upright first streak, sometimes called false dawn because it can mislead, followed by true dawn, whose light spreads horizontally. (The false dawn is likened to a wolf's tail; al-Tabrisi calls its upright light an announcement that night is ending.) [ALUSI-FULL,BURSEVI,ELMALILI,JISHUMI,MAWARDI-FULL,TABRISI; ابن عباس: RAZI-FULL]
- 89:1/v046 A cited poem calls true dawn al-musaddiq, the truthful one, because it reliably signals morning. [الشاعر: MAWARDI-FULL]

## وَٱلْفَجْرِ (word 1) · interpretation
- 89:1/v073 The opening oaths draw attention to valuable signs and blessings that point to the Creator and divine lordship. (Elmalili stresses worldly change from darkness to light and grief to joy; Islahi stresses God's sole control of bringing forth, removing, and granting respite.) [ELMALILI,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,JISHUMI]
- 89:1/v074 Dawn's light, awakening life, and creatures setting out for provision offer an image of resurrection: as morning follows night and waking follows sleep, people will rise for judgment. (Islahi mentions a waking supplication; al-Razi also notes both true and false dawn.) [ابن عباس: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL,BURSEVI,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:1/v075 The appearance of dawn after receding night serves as evidence that Judgment is an appointed, momentous event, as in al-Muddaththir. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:1/v076 Dawn is a gentle, joyful return of life in a waking world; creation seems to pray as it opens. [ELMALILI,QUTB-ZILAL]
- 89:1/v077 Dawn's image urges stained sinners to wash themselves with tears of repentance. [BURSEVI]
- 89:1/v078 A likely authentic poem by Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt greets the Lord at every dawn, supporting a cultic or liturgical association for dawn. [CORPUSCORANICUM]
