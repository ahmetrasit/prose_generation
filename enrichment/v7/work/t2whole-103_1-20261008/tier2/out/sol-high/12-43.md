## لِلرُّءْيَا (word 23) · rhetoric
- 12:43/v109 Fronting 'for the dream' before its verb foregrounds the dream and preserves the cadence; its definite article is generic. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## لِلرُّءْيَا (word 23) · readings
- 12:43/v110 The hamzless reading of al-ruʾyā elides the hamza without changing meaning. [QIRAAT-ER]
- 12:43/v111 Assimilating wāw to yāʾ in al-ruʾyā produces ruyyā, a phonological variant only. [QIRAAT-ER]
- 12:43/v112 Abū Jaʿfar reads al-ruʾyā as al-riyyā after changing hamza to wāw and assimilating to yāʾ; grammarians call it irregular because wāw substitution is optional. (The reports describe the intermediate sound changes somewhat differently.) [أبو جعفر: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]

## تَعْبُرُونَ (word 24) · meaning
- 12:43/v113 The light verb ʿabartu is the established form for interpreting a dream; some authorities reject the doubled verb, taʿbīr, and muʿabbir. (Al-Alusi calls the light form more established and forceful but acknowledges evidence for the doubled form.) [ALUSI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL+,NASAFI-FULL+; الزمخشري: SAMIN-DURR+]
- 12:43/v114 A Bedouin poem cited by al-Mubarrad attests the doubled form and a noun for dream interpretation, contrary to claims that such usage is rejected. (One note gives the poetic form ʿibārā.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL; الزمخشري: SAMIN-DURR; المبرد: ALUSI-FULL]

## تَعْبُرُونَ (word 24) · grammar
- 12:43/v115 The verb can function as an adjective meaning 'interpreters'; examples elsewhere show an adjective replacing a verb. (The note cites 4:90, 3:7, and 7:193.) [صاحب النظم: WAHIDI-BASIT]
