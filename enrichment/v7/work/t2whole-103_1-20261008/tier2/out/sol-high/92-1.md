# 92:1: 89 views from 113 rows (sol-high)

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · meaning
- 92:1/v001 A poem by al-Nābigha compares night to a pursuer who catches the speaker despite the apparent distance of escape. [النابغة الذبياني: NAHHAS]
- 92:1/v002 Tajallī means appearing or becoming uncovered, with physical senses such as a receding hairline, sight clearing by polish, displaying a bride, or polishing a blade or mirror. (Jishumī also gives illumination and clarity.) [BINTSHATI,JISHUMI]

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · grammar
- 92:1/v003 The definite article in al-layl can denote night as a whole kind or one particular terrifying night, literally or figuratively. [ELMALILI]
- 92:1/v004 The case of idhā under coordinated oaths is problematic: making the wāws govern both accusative and genitive, or repeating oath wāws, creates disputed constructions. (This is al-Zamakhsharī's reported analysis.) [الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 92:1/v005 Coordinating wāws do not themselves govern case; the first conjunct's governing element governs the later conjuncts too. (Abū Ḥayyān rejects the contrary analysis.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 92:1/v006 The construction coordinates genitive and accusative pairs rather than coordinating under two separate governors, as in 'I passed Zayd yesterday and ʿAmr today.' (Sībawayh's poetic parallel coordinates a genitive and nominative in the same case pattern.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; سيبويه: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 92:1/v007 Al-Khalīl treats the first wāw like an oath preposition, but the last two wāws as ordinary noun coordinators. [الخليل: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 92:1/v008 Al-Khalīl's statement makes the disputed construction prohibited, whereas al-Zamakhsharī describes it as merely disliked. (Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī's characterization.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 92:1/v009 Ibn Jinnī takes the reported reading without mā as evidence for an analysis making the male and female pair genitive in apposition to mā. [النبي وعلي بن أبي طالب وابن مسعود وأبو الدرداء وابن عباس: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB]

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · rhetoric
- 92:1/v010 Night's darkness conceals what daylight revealed, like a garment, while day clears and exposes it. (Quṭb adds that night and day oppose each other in cycle, appearance, qualities, and effects.) [IBNKATHIR-FULL,NAHHAS,QUTB-ZILAL,TAB-FULL]
- 92:1/v011 Using a past verb for a present event can make it vivid and immediate, transporting the listener to the event or bringing the event before the listener. (The notes illustrate this with 2:144 and 3:21 and say Arabic past forms have several temporal uses.) [SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:1/v012 Contrast clarifies meaning: night covers while day becomes manifest. [SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:1/v013 This verse opens an oath. [MAWARDI; القمي: TABATABAI]
- 92:1/v014 The sworn object is the whole night, whichever object of its covering is supplied. [ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:1/v015 Oaths by natural phenomena establish a premise. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:1/v016 The oaths display the worth of created things and the greatness of their Creator, and direct attention to the coming subject. [KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:1/v017 Quranic oaths probably draw on a characteristic expression of pre-Islamic soothsayers, though this connection has not been sufficiently studied. [CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:1/v018 The name al-Layl signals darkness and sleep, which frustrate ordinary desire; sleep is death's sibling. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:1/v019 Sajā, suggesting both stillness and darkness, suits the pause of revelation and loss of light; yaghshā and yasri suggest movement and fit that setting less well. (The note compares 92:1 with 89:4.) [SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · readings
- 92:1/v020 Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, and Ibn ʿĀmir pronounce the relevant verse endings openly in this sūra, al-Layl, and al-Ḍuḥā. [ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر: FARISI-HUJJA]
- 92:1/v021 Al-Kisāʾī uses imālah throughout the relevant endings here and in al-Layl and al-Ḍuḥā. [الكسائي: FARISI-HUJJA]
- 92:1/v022 Nāfiʿ's transmitted pronunciations differ: intermediate endings, fully open endings through Warsh and Qālūn, or an open talāhā with the rest inclined. [نافع: FARISI-HUJJA]
- 92:1/v023 Abū ʿAmr is reported with intermediate imālah; ʿAbbās transmits full inclination for selected endings, and other transmissions vary across the verse endings. [أبو عمرو: FARISI-HUJJA]
- 92:1/v024 The Prophet, ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd, Abū al-Dardāʾ, and Ibn ʿAbbās are reported reading 'the male and the female' without mā. (A report in both Ṣaḥīḥs says Abū al-Dardāʾ heard the Prophet read it so and ʿAlqama reported Ibn Masʿūd's same wording.) [النبي وعلي بن أبي طالب وابن مسعود وأبو الدرداء وابن عباس: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; علقمة: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:1/v025 Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the prophetic reading without mā as an early permitted variant later replaced by the final reading agreed upon by the reciters and written in Abū Bakr's muṣḥaf. (He says al-Zamakhsharī called it the Prophet's reading.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · reports
- 92:1/v026 A stingy palm owner denied fallen fruit to poor children; a believer exchanged forty palms for that tree and gave it to the family after the Prophet promised its owner a palm in Paradise. (Ibn Kathīr's account says the original owner refused the Prophet's offer.) [ابن عباس: IBNABIHATIM,IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 92:1/v027 The palm transaction and charitable buyer occasioned the revelation of this sūra's opening and its contrast between giving and withholding. [ابن عباس: IBNABIHATIM,IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 92:1/v028 Ibn Kathīr calls Ibn Abī Ḥātim's palm story very unusual. [IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 92:1/v029 Most reports connect the sūra's revelation with Abū Bakr; sound chains are cited from Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, and others. [ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:1/v030 Ibn Masʿūd reports that Abū Bakr bought Bilāl from Umayya ibn Khalaf for a cloak and ten uqiyyas, freed him, and the opening of this sūra was revealed. (Ālūsī gives the broader Abū Bakr attribution; the Bilāl transaction details are from Ibn Masʿūd.) [İbn Mes‘ûd: ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:1/v031 Al-Suddī links revelation to Abū al-Daḥdāḥ's charitable purchase of a palm for orphans. (Ālūsī says al-Suyūṭī judged the associated Ibn ʿAbbās report weak.) [ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:1/v032 God first created light and darkness, then distinguished them as dark night and bright, seeing day. [قتادة عن سعيد: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:1/v033 The Prophet rebuked Muʿādh for a long prayer and instructed him to recite shorter sūras, including ones beginning with glorification, the sun, dawn, and night. [النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: DURR-FULL]
- 92:1/v034 The Prophet instructed recitation of both al-Layl and al-Shams in the dawn prayer. [ابن عباس: DURR-FULL]

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · interpretation
- 92:1/v042 Night shelters living creatures, stills them, and brings restorative sleep; day sends people, birds, and other creatures out for livelihood. (Rāzī says their alternation serves both work and rest, citing 25:62 and 14:33.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,RAZI-FULL]
- 92:1/v043 Night and day are two great signs God alternates over creation. [قتادة: TAB-FULL]
- 92:1/v044 Night evokes confusion and obscurity, gradually hiding what daylight made visible, and is suited to secrecy. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:1/v045 People seeking secrecy choose night, while those seeking publicity choose day, whether righteous or wicked. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:1/v046 The image can warn of a town or people engulfed by punishment, the era of ignorance around the Prophet's mission, death agonies, or resurrection events. [ELMALILI]
- 92:1/v047 Night and day, like male and female, are complementary pairs: each fills the other's void and together they fulfill their created purpose. (The note says the capacity and use of either member make little sense without its counterpart.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:1/v048 The outer and inner signs in verses 1–4 establish that Judgment must come: the Hereafter is this world's counterpart and gives it meaning. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:1/v049 Night and day's alternation directly affects the soul, evoking the mysterious universe, change, human inability to control it, and a power directing it. [QUTB-ZILAL]
- 92:1/v050 Night's darkness alludes to the solar system's original state before daylight followed the sun's creation; reckoning time began with nights before days. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · theology
- 92:1/v051 The harmony of complementary opposites argues for monotheism and, here, for the Hereafter; this world's counterpart completes its purpose. (The note cites 51:49.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:1/v052 The precise order of night and day shows that a director governs the heavens and human lives and did not create people without purpose. [QUTB-ZILAL]
- 92:1/v053 Even when deniers divert attention, the heart stays connected to the universe and recognizes its director instinctively and through reflection. [QUTB-ZILAL]

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · law
- 92:1/v054 God may swear by any creature, while people may swear only by God. (Both are transmitted through Imams called al-Bāqir; the second comes through ʿAlī ibn Mahziyār.) [محمد بن مسلم: TABATABAI; علي بن مهزيار: TABATABAI]

## وَٱلَّيْلِ (word 1) · links
- 92:1/v055 The structure and function of this oath type are linked to 93:1–2 and its parallels. [CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:1/v056 The night and day oath parallels other early Meccan oaths, especially the wording of 91:3–4; the brightening day corresponds to ḍuḥā in 91:1 and 93:1. (Ibn ʿĀshūr specifically connects the time restrictions on night and day with the preceding surah's oaths.) [CORPUSCORANICUM,IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:1/v057 Oaths by night and day, then male and female, set cosmic opposites alongside diverse human efforts and their contrasting outcomes. (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ includes the denier's fire and the pious giver's satisfaction; Neuwirth calls the pairs a structural matrix; Ibn Kathīr connects the male and female with other created pairs.) [BINTSHATI,IBNKATHIR-FULL,QUTB-ZILAL,STUDYQURAN; Neuwirth: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:1/v058 The night and day pair and the male and female pair form two cosmic oppositions; the second oath enigmatically points to God, who speaks explicitly in verses 7–10. [CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:1/v059 The sūra pairs closely with al-Shams in central themes and inner and outer features. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:1/v060 After the preceding sūra's believer and disbeliever and their outcomes, this sūra presents the contrast again. [JISHUMI]
- 92:1/v061 This sūra explains which traits produce the success and failure named in the preceding sūra, then warns of the Fire and distinguishes those who enter it from those spared. (Iṣlāḥī stresses identifying corruption and protection or purification of the soul.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:1/v062 After al-Shams depicts purified and corrupted souls and Thamud's fate, this sūra treats people's divergent good and evil efforts under God's governance through piety and wickedness. (Biqaʿī calls God's governance complete and freely chosen despite people's shared aims.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:1/v063 The opening oaths summarize al-Shams: that sūra moves from light to darkness, knowledge of God and self, and Thamud's ruin; this one moves from annihilation toward witnessed light, purification, growth, and abiding in God. [ELMALILI]
- 92:1/v064 The previous sūra ends with warning; this one starts with warning and proceeds to glad tidings. A people's destruction does not diminish the Creator, who can renew creation through day, male, and female. [ELMALILI]

## إِذَا (word 2) · meaning
- 92:1/v070 The phrase can mean that night darkens. (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ specifies a covering of deep darkness.) [مجاهد: MAWARDI; BINTSHATI]
- 92:1/v071 Ghishāʾ or yaghshā means covering or veiling. (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces the root gh-sh-y.) [BINTSHATI,JISHUMI,QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:1/v072 The root gh-sh-y also denotes veiling or dulling perception in sleep or fainting, including sight or insight. (The cited Quranic usages include rejection, sleep, fainting, and veils over heart and senses.) [BINTSHATI]
- 92:1/v073 The root gh-sh-y can describe the overwhelming manifestation at the lote tree. (The note cites 53:16.) [BINTSHATI]
- 92:1/v074 The root gh-sh-y often describes Judgment and its punishments covering people, faces, or places. [BINTSHATI]
- 92:1/v075 The root gh-sh-y can name terrors or darkness. [BINTSHATI]

## إِذَا (word 2) · grammar
- 92:1/v076 The object of yaghshā is left unexpressed because the listener can infer it from context. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,NAHHAS,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:1/v077 Yaghshā functions intransitively here, so no object needs to be supplied. (The effect conveyed is night covering everything in darkness.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:1/v078 The conditional idhā asserts realization; with the imperfect verb, the event may be present or future. [ELMALILI]

## إِذَا (word 2) · rhetoric
- 92:1/v079 Leaving the object of yaghshā unspecified lets the covering include every possible object. (Naḥḥās calls this deliberate vagueness; Elmalılı says it heightens terror.) [ELMALILI,NAHHAS,SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:1/v080 Yaghshā uses the present tense because night covers gradually, while the past tense for daylight's appearing fits its sudden arrival at sunrise. (The note compares the verbs in al-Shams.) [SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
