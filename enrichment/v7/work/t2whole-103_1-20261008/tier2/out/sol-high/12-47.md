# 12:47: 52 views from 171 rows (sol-high)

## whole verse · readings
- 12:47/v001 Jaʿfar ibn Muhammad is reported to read mā qarabtum, 'what you brought near,' and a related report gives 'what you bring near for them' in place of 'what you laid up'; the latter is glossed as placing the harvest near in the revealed sequence. (The reported variants concern wording outside the quoted verse.) [جعفر بن محمد: TABRISI; أبو عبد الله عليه السلام: TABATABAI]

## whole verse · referent
- 12:47/v002 The messenger's 'the people' can mean the king and his people, or respectfully refer to the king alone. (This concerns the preceding request to return to the people.) [MAWARDI-FULL]

## whole verse · reports
- 12:47/v003 Qatada reports that Joseph left home at seventeen, spent seven years in the well and seven in prison, stored food for seven years, and, according to the report's audience, then met his father. [قتادة: DURR-FULL]
- 12:47/v004 A Prophetic report marvels at Joseph's patience and generosity: though asked to interpret the cows, he did not hurry out when summoned and wanted his innocence established. [عكرمة: DURR-FULL]
- 12:47/v005 Joseph served one man food for two, leaving half uneaten each day; when the man consumed the whole serving, Joseph recognized that the first hard year had begun. [زيد بن أسلم: DURR-FULL]
- 12:47/v006 A report from al-Riḍā says Joseph stockpiled harvests and sold famine food successively for money, valuables, livestock, servants, property, land, and freedom, then freed Egyptians and restored their possessions and kingship. [الرضا عليه السلام: TABATABAI]
- 12:47/v007 The commentator omits many similar accounts of the famine economy because most do not directly aid interpretation of these verses. [TABATABAI]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 12:47/v008 The dream calls for a practical famine policy of repeated planting, preserving grain in its ears, and rationing reserves; it is not merely a forecast of seven fertile years followed by seven barren ones. (This disputes the reading that takes Joseph's words chiefly as a forecast of the sequence of years.) [TABATABAI+,TABATABAI-]
- 12:47/v009 Joseph added practical advice on cultivation, storage, and provisioning to his interpretation of the dream; his answer also conveys a later unseen year. (Ibn Ashur calls the king's dream divine kindness to Egypt and perhaps a sign of the king's readiness for faith and reform; Abu Hayyan distinguishes interpretation, advice, and the unseen report.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,TAB-FULL; عبد الرحمن بن زيد: IBNABIHATIM]
- 12:47/v010 One account says Joseph knew the additional abundant year through direct revelation, beyond dream interpretation; another says God had taught him the interpretation, so his forecast was certain. (The positions disagree over direct revelation versus knowledge taught for dream interpretation; Qatada says God granted Joseph knowledge of the later year.) [IBNJAWZI-ZAD,KASHSHAF-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; قتادة: KASHSHAF-FULL,RAZI-FULL]
- 12:47/v011 Joseph may have omitted an audible 'if God wills' while intending the exception inwardly, just as his brothers did when promising to bring their brother. (Offered as an answer to why the prediction lacks an explicit exception.) [ابن الأنباري: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 12:47/v012 The messenger's hope that 'they may know' expresses concern that a false dream or rejection might prevent acceptance until events confirm the interpretation; it does not express doubt in Joseph. (This concerns the preceding request and its outcome.) [MAWARDI-FULL]

## whole verse · theology
- 12:47/v013 Revealed laws guide worldly interests so people may know and worship God and attain happiness after death; according to Sunni authorities, this care is divine grace, not an obligation God owes. [QURTUBI-FULL]

## whole verse · law
- 12:47/v014 Preserving public interests serves religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property; safeguarding these is benefit, their loss is harm, and averting harm is itself benefit. [QURTUBI-FULL]

## whole verse · links
- 12:47/v015 The Qur'anic account of Joseph illustrates God's hidden direction of human affairs, in which people may dislike what benefits them or love what harms them, as in 2:216; this differs from Genesis' romantic account of his fortunes. [ASAD-NOTES]

## تَزْرَعُونَ (word 2) · meaning

## تَزْرَعُونَ (word 2) · grammar
- 12:47/v017 The imperative to leave the grain can follow a declarative prediction: it may be independent advice, an answer to an implied condition, or guidance expressed in the form of a report. (These are replies to the objection that an imperative cannot be coordinated with a declarative forecast.) [ALUSI-FULL]

## تَزْرَعُونَ (word 2) · rhetoric
- 12:47/v018 The declarative 'you cultivate' functions as a command to sow; expressing the command as a report emphasizes compliance or presents the action as certain. The following explicit command to leave the harvest in its ears supports this reading. (Mawardi adds that Joseph as a prophet may advise people on their interests; Ibn al-Jawzi offers this command reading as one answer to why Joseph did not say 'if God wills'.) [BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,KASHSHAF-FULL,MAWARDI-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,TABATABAI; المفسرون: WAHIDI-BASIT; صاحب النظم: WAHIDI-BASIT; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; بعضهم: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:47/v019 A present verb can also express a prayer; because prayer asks for an act, this use resembles an imperative, as illustrated by the plea to forgive and show mercy in 23:118. [صاحب النظم: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## تَزْرَعُونَ (word 2) · interpretation
- 12:47/v020 'You cultivate' predicts seven successive years of ordinary farming; the later command to store grain supplies new advice and does not turn the prediction into an imperative. (Abu Hayyan adds irrigation. Al-Samin says routine farming needed no instruction, whereas preservation did; al-Kashf says the storage advice interrupts the forecast with urgency. This disputes the command reading.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL+,ABUHAYYAN-FULL-,BIQAI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,SAMIN-DURR+; الشيخ: SAMIN-DURR-; الكشف: ALUSI-FULL+]

## سَبْعَ (word 3) · interpretation
- 12:47/v022 The seven fat cows and green ears signify seven fertile years, and the lean cows and dry ears seven years of famine or drought; the cultivation years supply the food later needed. (Ibn Kathir adds that cows symbolize years because they plow fields, and describes the fertile years as rainy; Ibn Ashur takes ears as provisions. Al-Qummi's reported account adds rain after the famine.) [ALUSI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNKATHIR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,QURTUBI-FULL,THALABI; المفسرون: WAHIDI-BASIT; قتادة: RAZI-FULL; تفسير القمي: TABATABAI; R: STUDYQURAN]
- 12:47/v023 In the dream's imagery, cows represent yearly provisions: fat cows show plentiful harvests from sustained planting, lean cows shortages, and the lean cows eating the fat ones famine consuming saved food. (Part of the practical-policy reading of the dream.) [TABATABAI]
- 12:47/v024 Each of the seven green ears stands for one year's food, signifying seven years whose harvest is eaten fresh. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:47/v025 Joseph's answer concerns a fifteen-year agricultural cycle: seven productive years, seven years of scarcity, and a further abundant year. (The further year is connected with revelation in the cited notes.) [KASHSHAF-FULL,STUDYQURAN; قتادة: RAZI-FULL]
- 12:47/v026 The phrase can mean seven consecutive years of planting, year after year, following the people's established agricultural practice. (Ibn Abbas especially glosses it as consecutive years.) [BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,MAWARDI-FULL,MUQATIL,QURTUBI-FULL,TAB-FULL,TABATABAI,WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ابن عباس: TABRISI,WAHIDI-BASIT; أهل المعاني واللغة: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## دَأَبًۭا (word 5) · grammar
- 12:47/v029 One distinction treats the form with sukūn as a verbal noun and the form with fatḥa as a noun; others treat both pronunciations as verbal nouns of persistent work. ## فَذَرُوهُ (word 8) · grammar
- 12:47/v042 'Leave it' is explicitly imperative, although the preceding cultivation clause has declarative form; the verbs dhar and daʿ lack past-tense forms because taraka supplies that function. (The unusual verb paradigm is reported separately from the contrast in clause forms.) [MAWARDI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,TABRISI]

## فَذَرُوهُ (word 8) · interpretation

## فِى (word 9) · interpretation
- 12:47/v046 The dream's green ears depict stores that must be saved and its dry ears point to keeping grain unthreshed; the imagery indicates the work needed to protect supplies. (Part of the practical-policy reading of the dream.) [TABATABAI]
- 12:47/v047 Egyptian grain may fail to keep for even two years outside its ear, so storage in the ear is especially useful there; consume the oldest stored grain first while retaining the rest for famine. (Al-Alusi describes such stores as liable to ruin after roughly two years.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL; أبو حيان: BIQAI-FULL]
- 12:47/v048 Grain left in its ear is not readily eaten, which helps preserve the stockpile. [عبد الرحمن بن زيد: IBNABIHATIM; MAWARDI-FULL; ابن زيد: DURR-FULL; قتادة وابن جريج: DURR-FULL]
