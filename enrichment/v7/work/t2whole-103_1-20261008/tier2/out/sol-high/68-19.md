# 68:19: 44 views from 81 rows (sol-high)

## whole verse · meaning
- 68:19/v001 الصرام means date harvesting, from a root meaning cutting; صرم and صريمة refer to separation and to a broken-off sand tract, as illustrated by poems including one of Imru al-Qays. (This lexical explanation concerns the following verse's wording.) [SAMIN-DURR]
- 68:19/v002 Other forms of the same cutting root describe a decisive cutter, a she-camel whose milk has stopped, a month or year nearing its end, and a person whose fortune has turned bad. (This lexical explanation concerns the following verse's wording.) [SAMIN-DURR]
- 68:19/v003 الصريم can describe trees stripped of fruit. (Tabatabai describes cut, fruitless trees; the word appears in the following verse.) [قيل: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 68:19/v004 الصريم can mean night, day, or dawn; al-Shammir explains night and day as opposites named because each parts from the other. (Ibn Abbas compares the ruined garden to black night; the word appears in the following verse.) [قيل: SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL,TABATABAI; قتادة: DURR-FULL]
- 68:19/v005 Ibn Abbas says الصريم means ash in the language of Khuzaima. (This explanation concerns the following verse's wording.) [ابن عباس: SAMIN-DURR]
- 68:19/v006 الصريم can describe a known barren sand tract in Yemen. (This explanation concerns the following verse's wording.) [ابن عباس: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]

## whole verse · reports
- 68:19/v007 A Prophetic report applies the warning that sin can withhold prepared provision to the owners' loss of their crop. (One version also says sin can cause lost knowledge and missed night prayer.) [عبد الله بن مسعود: IBNKATHIR-FULL; النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: DURR-FULL]
- 68:19/v008 Accounts describe the garden as belonging to a righteous man who left it to his family and town, and variously place it near Sana'a in Yemen, in Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. (These are reported elaborations of the story.) [BINTSHATI]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 68:19/v009 The garden's loss may be punishment or a trial serving as grace, because it brought its owners to repentance. (The two accounts differ in how they characterize the disaster.) [JISHUMI]
- 68:19/v010 Their refusal to help the poor caused the garden's loss; at first the sons thought they had lost their way, then recognized the consequence of their refusal. [FARRA]
- 68:19/v011 The owners' firm resolve and oath to withhold charity made them culpable before they carried out the refusal. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:19/v012 The garden became like dry harvested stubble after the visitation. (Ibn Kathir attributes the stubble explanation to al-Thawri and al-Suddi.) [IBNKATHIR-FULL,QUSHAYRI]

## whole verse · law
- 68:19/v013 The passage admonishes people who do not share their wealth, but its worldly punishment does not establish that the owners withheld obligatory zakat. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## whole verse · links
- 68:19/v014 The parable addresses people who call the Prophet possessed and feel secure: divine punishment can ruin plans fixed by pledges just as people wake to carry them out. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## طَآئِفٌۭ (word 3) · meaning
- 68:19/v020 A طائف is an event or visitation that comes at night; on this reading the word itself marks the calamity as nocturnal. (Al-Farra is repeatedly named for this restriction. Other notes challenge it by citing 7:201.) [BIQAI-FULL-,FARRA,MAWARDI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,TAB-FULL; الكلبي: WAHIDI-BASIT; قتادة: ALUSI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; الفراء: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,QURTUBI-FULL; قيل: SAMIN-DURR]
- 68:19/v021 Arab usage can explicitly speak of visiting by day, despite the ordinary night sense; Abu al-Jarrah's poem gives an example, and al-Farra cites a daytime expression for something unexpected or impossible. [FARRA,TAB-FULL]
- 68:19/v022 The word need not be restricted to night, since 7:201 uses طائف for Satan's touch without specifying nighttime. (These authors object to or reject al-Farra's nighttime restriction.) [الفراء: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL; قيل: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL-]
- 68:19/v023 طائف can mean a calamity or destructive occurrence and generally suggests harm. (Alusi reports Ibn Abbas calling it an event and Qatada calling it punishment.) [قيل: SAMIN-DURR; قتادة: ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:19/v024 Al-Farra associates طائف with a dream specter, but Ibn Ashur questions deriving it from a portion of the night. [الفراء: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## طَآئِفٌۭ (word 3) · rhetoric
- 68:19/v025 The kind of calamity is left unnamed because its effect matters more than its type; assigning the action to an unspecified visitor functions like a passive construction. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:19/v026 The indefinite طائف magnifies the event, which the next verse reveals as a ruinous calamity. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## مِّن (word 4) · interpretation

- 68:19/v039 The destroying visitor was a slight act for God though devastating to the orchard; calling it from your Lord frames the punishment as merciful correction intended to reform the Prophet's people. [BIQAI-FULL]
