- 6:52/v094 The clause is circumstantial, attached to those who invoke; one analysis considers subject or object attachment but prefers the subject: they seek His face through invocation at those times. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,QUSHAYRI,SAMIN-DURR+]

## يُرِيدُونَ (word 8) · rhetoric
- 6:52/v095 Mentioning sincerity as their qualification highlights its centrality to worship and establishes their claim to honor. [BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## يُرِيدُونَ (word 8) · reports
- 6:52/v096 Quraysh disparaged the poor believers as insincere followers seeking food, clothes or shelter rather than faith. (Ibn Ashur disputes a related accusation of hypocrisy.) [BURSEVI,RAZI-FULL; müşrikler: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; Some commentators: STUDYQURAN]

## يُرِيدُونَ (word 8) · interpretation
- 6:52/v097 Seeking God's face means sincerely seeking His favor or approval through worship and obedience, without worldly aims or ostentation. (Some phrase it as seeking reward; others stress no worldly benefit.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BURSEVI,ELMALILI,IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNKATHIR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,TUSTARI,WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ابن عباس: WAHIDI-BASIT; IK, R: STUDYQURAN]
- 6:52/v098 The worshippers seek God's reward, pardon, and nearness through righteous deeds, not partnership with Him. [BAGHAWI-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,TAB-FULL; ابن عباس: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 6:52/v099 Their public invocation professed faith in God alone and implies conviction; the claim that Quraysh accused them of hypocrisy lacks sound report. [IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## يُرِيدُونَ (word 8) · inward
- 6:52/v100 For spiritual seekers, outward morning and evening acts coexist with uninterrupted inward desire and presence of heart before God. [BURSEVI,QASHANI,QUSHAYRI]
- 6:52/v101 The seeker exerts intention and effort, while the one sought receives divine enabling and rises through stations; both states are God's favor. [TUSTARI]
- 6:52/v102 Spiritual desire is a heart's need that gives no rest until reaching God; poetic longing can carry the traveler fearlessly through wilderness. [QUSHAYRI]
- 6:52/v103 The fully unified seek God's essence without reward, fear, attributes, intermediaries or awareness of themselves; their worship is heart-presence, spiritual witnessing and love. [QASHANI]
- 6:52/v104 God's testimony publicly confirms their inward sincerity; without it, no observer could confidently say a creature truly seeks God. [QUSHAYRI]
- 6:52/v105 Their ragged appearance conceals ardent longing; God adorns chosen lovers with righteous acts and pure states although ordinary people do not see their rank. [BURSEVI,QUSHAYRI]
- 6:52/v106 They have no worldly or afterlife demand beyond their Master; devotion to Him draws His special care. [QUSHAYRI]

## وَجْهَهُۥ (word 9) · meaning
- 6:52/v107 Face can denote the essence or reality of a thing as an honorific expression; thus seeking His face can mean seeking God Himself. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,JISHUMI,KASHSHAF-FULL,MAWARDI-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,RAZI-FULL; أبو إسحاق: WAHIDI-BASIT; الزجاج: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; المشهور: ALUSI-FULL; بعضهم: ALUSI-FULL]

## وَجْهَهُۥ (word 9) · interpretation
- 6:52/v108 Another interpretation takes God's face as the direction, path, or obedience He commands people to pursue. [BAGHAWI-FULL,MAWARDI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL; أهل المعاني: WAHIDI-BASIT; الزجاج: ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:52/v109 The beloved's face evokes love and longing to see God; seeking His face may thus signify seeking His approval or literally hoping for that vision. (Al-Alusi questions the metaphorical love account.) [RAZI-FULL; الإمام: ALUSI-FULL; R: STUDYQURAN]

## وَجْهَهُۥ (word 9) · theology
- 6:52/v110 Anthropomorphists cite God's face as a bodily part; Razi and Abu Hayyan reject that, while Qurtubi also reports the description of God as possessing the Face. (The disagreement turns on whether 'face' denotes a bodily member.) [المجسمة: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL]
- 6:52/v111 Will ordinarily concerns originated possible things; describing seekers as willing God's eternal essence raises a theological difficulty requiring explanation. [QUSHAYRI; المشهور: ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:52/v112 God has no bodily face or material direction; face can name the aspect through which a thing is encountered, and divine names, attributes, religion, good deeds, and near-to-God beings are faces of God that endure by His permanence. (The grammatical argument at 55:27 is that 'possessor of majesty' describes the face.) [TABATABAI]

## مَا (word 10) · grammar
- 6:52/v113 The first min can be partitive while the second is added for emphatic, comprehensive negation. [IBNASHUR-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL; عطاء: ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:52/v114 In the first clause, ma may govern an accusative predicate or be inactive; alayka is a fronted predicate, min shay'in the subject, and min hisabihim may be a preceding circumstantial partitive rather than a delayed adjective. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:52/v115 The genitive 'their accounting' may denote the Prophet accounting for the believers or the polytheists' reckoning of their poverty and weakness. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:52/v116 Abu Hayyan calls al-Zamakhshari's paraphrase ungrammatical because 'his companion' lacks a singular antecedent; al-Samin suggests repairing it by supplying 'each one.' [ABUHAYYAN-FULL-,SAMIN-DURR; الشيخ: SAMIN-DURR-]

## مَا (word 10) · rhetoric
- 6:52/v117 Both reciprocal clauses state the independence of each account; some say both are required, while others say the second reinforces the first though it could be omitted. (The disagreement concerns necessity versus emphasis of the repeated clause.) [الزجاج: WAHIDI-BASIT; BURSEVI,IBNASHUR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,RAZI-FULL; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; أبو السعود: BURSEVI]
- 6:52/v118 Putting the Prophet first in both reciprocal clauses honors him; the reversal also forms a rhetorical return from ending to beginning. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,ALUSI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:52/v119 Fronting alayka conveys burden or obligation and may emphasize or restrict the negation; a restrictive reading confines the accounting to God. [ALUSI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:52/v120 Two added min particles, fronting, reciprocal negation and possible restriction pile up emphasis against yielding to the chiefs' request. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:52/v121 The reciprocal wording exposes the chiefs' pride disguised as advice: they pretended excluding the poor would win their belief and benefit the Prophet. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:52/v122 Al-Alusi objects to al-Zamakhshari's treatment of the reciprocal denials as one thought like 'no bearer bears another's burden,' finding it an unfitting account of the wording. [الزمخشري: ALUSI-FULL]

## مَا (word 10) · interpretation
- 6:52/v123 The Prophet and the poor worshippers each answer for their own deeds; God alone judges and assigns reward or punishment, so neither bears the other's account. (The no-bearer-bears-another's-burden principle is cited by several sources.) [الحسن: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,MAWARDI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; ASAD-NOTES,BAGHAWI-FULL,ELMALILI,IBNKATHIR-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR,STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; one view: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:52/v124 The Prophet judges outward signs of piety; alleged hidden insincerity in the poor believers is their own account before God and cannot justify dismissing them. (Abu Hayyan objects that God already attests their sincerity.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,BURSEVI,KASHSHAF-FULL,RAZI-FULL; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; Z: STUDYQURAN+; R: STUDYQURAN]
- 6:52/v125 God's testimony of their sincerity makes a hypothetical blameworthy inner state inappropriate, according to Abu Hayyan. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:52/v126 A competing explanation makes hisab provision or sufficiency: neither the Prophet nor the poor believers must maintain the other, because God provides for both. (Poverty therefore gives no reason to expel them.) [BAGHAWI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,IBNJAWZI-ZAD,QURTUBI-FULL,RAZI-FULL,TAB-FULL,TABRISI,WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ابن بحر: MAWARDI-FULL; أهل المعاني: WAHIDI-BASIT; الطبري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; ابن زيد: ALUSI-FULL; another interpretation: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:52/v127 Ibn Ashur rejects making both parties' accounts God's alone as out of context and rendering the second clause redundant. (This conflicts with the widespread mutual-deeds interpretation.) [IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## مَا (word 10) · inward
- 6:52/v128 The poor are light in burden; God alone accounts for every person and gives the outcome of deeds. [QUSHAYRI]

## حِسَابِهِم (word 13) · meaning
- 6:52/v129 Hisab originates in numerical counting and extends to measuring deeds for recompense; it can also mean sufficient provision, as illustrated by 78:36, 2:212 and Arabic usage of ahsabtuhu. [ابن بحر: MAWARDI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL,TABATABAI; أبو عبيد الهروي: BIQAI-FULL]

## حِسَابِهِم (word 13) · rhetoric
- 6:52/v130 The accounting sentence may explain the prohibition or invalidate the chiefs' rationale for expulsion, with its separation marking a reason for the ban. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## حِسَابِهِم (word 13) · referent
- 6:52/v131 The pronouns in the reciprocal accounting clauses most apparently refer to the poor worshippers, as does the object of 'expel them.' (This is preferred against the polytheist-pronoun reading.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL+,RAZI-FULL+,SAMIN-DURR+,WAHIDI-BASIT+; المهدوي: IBNATIYYA-FULL; عطاء: ALUSI-FULL; ağırlıklı görüş: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 6:52/v132 An alternative sends the account pronouns to the polytheists, while 'expel them' refers to the poor believers: the Prophet need not court the chiefs' conversion at his followers' expense. (The referent differs from the nearer poor-believer reading.) [ابن عباس: ALUSI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,RAZI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; قيل: KASHSHAF-FULL; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ابن عطية: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; الزمخشري وابن عطية: SAMIN-DURR; الشيخ: SAMIN-DURR; some interpreters: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; Z: STUDYQURAN+]

## حِسَابِهِم (word 13) · interpretation
- 6:52/v133 Ibn Ashur takes the clause as denying the Prophet responsibility for unbelievers' conversion, or their social reckoning of believers' poverty, so believers must retain their rights. [IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:52/v134 Al-Tabataba'i locates reward and therefore the reckoning of deeds with God alone. [TABATABAI]

## وَمَا (word 16) · grammar
- 6:52/v135 The parallel min hisabika is difficult to parse as a preceding circumstance because it would precede both its semantic governor and its possessor; an explanatory or predicate analysis is proposed. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,SAMIN-DURR]

## فَتَكُونَ (word 23) · grammar
- 6:52/v136 One parse makes 'then you would be among wrongdoers' the subjunctive result of the initial prohibition, with the accounting and expulsion clauses intervening. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,AKHFASH,BAGHAWI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,TAB-FULL,TABRISI,ZAJJAJ; مكي والواحدي وأبو البقاء: SAMIN-DURR; أبو حيان: SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:52/v137 A competing parse coordinates 'be among wrongdoers' with 'expel them' as the consequence of the negation; wronging follows from expulsion. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL+,ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,KASHSHAF-FULL,NASAFI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR+; الزمخشري: SAMIN-DURR; الإمام والزمخشري: ALUSI-FULL]

## فَتَكُونَ (word 23) · interpretation
- 6:52/v138 Expelling innocent, sincere worshippers would wrong them by withholding the welcome and honor they deserve. [ابن زيد: TABRISI,WAHIDI-BASIT+; ALUSI-FULL,ASAD-NOTES,BIQAI-FULL,ELMALILI,QURTUBI-FULL,WAHIDI-WAJIZ; عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم: IBNABIHATIM]
- 6:52/v139 Another account says expulsion would wrong the Prophet himself through disobedience, loss or diminution of reward. [ابن عباس: WAHIDI-BASIT; ابن زيد: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:52/v140 The conditional warning also depicts the chiefs' request itself as proud and unjust. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:52/v141 The Prophet would be wrong to disregard sincere believers for chiefs who might never believe; guidance belongs to God. [BIQAI-FULL]
- 6:52/v142 The warning is severe because the Prophet had considered giving wealthy leaders precedence over the weak. [ابن الأنباري: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## فَتَكُونَ (word 23) · theology
- 6:52/v143 The warning is conditional instruction and does not prove the Prophet actually expelled anyone, like the hypothetical warning against his associating partners with God. [ALUSI-FULL,IBNASHUR-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL]
- 6:52/v144 Critics of prophetic impeccability say the episode shows the Prophet sinned by dismissing the poor; Razi replies he sought conversion, intended no contempt, and at most left a preferable course. (Razi also answers appeals to Noah's refusal and warnings against worldly adornment.) [الطاعنون في عصمة الأنبياء: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL,STUDYQURAN; al-Rāzī: STUDYQURAN]

## ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ (word 25) · meaning
- 6:52/v145 The wrongdoers are those who put a thing in the wrong place; the word can also express diminution. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,IBNATIYYA-FULL; ابن عباس: WAHIDI-BASIT]
