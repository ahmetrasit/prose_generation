- 2:265/v058 Zayd ibn Aslam identifies the land as Egypt, describing its productive dew and the effect of heavy rain; one account says rain weakens it. (The accounts differ over whether heavy rain doubles or weakens Egyptian land.) [زيد بن أسلم: ABUHAYYAN-FULL,DURR-FULL,IBNABIHATIM,THALABI]

## بِرَبْوَةٍ (word 13) · interpretation
- 2:265/v059 The raised garden yields more or finer fruit through fertile deep soil, adequate water, fresh air, sun, and pollinating winds. (Some contrast stagnant lowland air, hail, and runoff; poetry about upland gardens is cited.) ## أَصَابَهَا (word 14) · meaning
- 2:265/v060 Wābil is an intense or copious downpour; Arabic also has paired verb forms for raining and dew, and poetry uses wābil for abundant rain. (Akhfash notes related forms for severe and for land struck by rain.) ## أَصَابَهَا (word 14) · grammar
- 2:265/v061 The rain-striking clause can modify the garden as a second adjective, modify the raised tract, or be circumstantial; its pronoun can refer to the elevated ground. (Adjectival order puts the permanent location before the temporary rain event.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,QURTUBI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR,SAMIN-DURR+; أبو البقاء: SAMIN-DURR]

## أَصَابَهَا (word 14) · rhetoric

## فَـَٔاتَتْ (word 16) · grammar
- 2:265/v069 Ātat normally takes two objects, with its recipient, the garden owner or people, omitted; ukul is the given fruit and ḍiʿfayn a circumstantial accusative. ## فَـَٔاتَتْ (word 16) · rhetoric
