## فَلَمَّا (word 1) · grammar
- 68:26/v001 The construction with 'when' makes the owners' answer follow their seeing the garden immediately and instinctively. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## فَلَمَّا (word 1) · interpretation
- 68:26/v002 The owners' awakening from heedlessness and self-blame for pride and ingratitude illustrate how a trial can turn people back. (Ibn ʿĀshūr connects this lesson with 7:168.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:26/v003 Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh's maxim says that ingratitude exposes blessings to loss, while gratitude secures them. [ابن عطاء الله الإسكندري: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## فَلَمَّا (word 1) · links
- 68:26/v004 Their quick regret and hope for a better replacement urge the idolaters to repent just as promptly. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## رَأَوْهَا (word 2) · reports
- 68:26/v005 Reports about the garden story say it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town. [BINTSHATI]
- 68:26/v006 Reports variously place the garden in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, in Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. [BINTSHATI]

## رَأَوْهَا (word 2) · interpretation
- 68:26/v007 The garden was burned, blackened, bare, or stripped of crop and fruit, so it no longer resembled the orchard the owners knew and they failed to recognize it. (Al-Biqāʿī specifies that after a short walk they found no trace of crop or fruit; Ibn ʿAṭiyya places their arrival at dawn.) ## قَالُوٓا۟ (word 3) · rhetoric
- 68:26/v008 Attributing the speech to the owners in the plural indicates that they all agreed about the cause of what happened to them. (Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the group of owners with 68:17.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## إِنَّا (word 4) · rhetoric
- 68:26/v009 The emphatic wording expresses grief and regret at their newly recognized earlier heedlessness. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:26/v010 On the lost-road reading, the following 'rather' corrects their first impression: this was indeed their own garden, destroyed and deprived of produce. (The correction is in the next verse.) [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## إِنَّا (word 4) · links
- 68:26/v019 Their initial mistaken belief that they had strayed, followed by recognition of earned punishment, alludes to Muhammad's opponents thinking themselves guided before realizing their error. (The note connects this with 68:5–7.) [STUDYQURAN]
