# 68:18: 28 views from 66 rows (sol-high)

## whole verse · reports
- 68:18/v001 After a father who shared his garden's produce with the poor died, his sons or heirs resolved to stop that giving. (Al-Farra and one report in Abu Hayyan say the sons cited growing dependents; al-Farra also says their wealth had shrunk. Ikrima says they called their father foolish; Qatada says they opposed his charity. Another account calls what the owner paid God's due.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,FARRA; عكرمة: TAB-FULL; قتادة: TAB-FULL; ابن عباس: TABATABAI]
- 68:18/v002 Reports place the garden in Yemen near San'a, at Surawan near San'a after Jesus, or among Ethiopians or other people of Abyssinia or Scripture. (Bint al-Shati reports the story of a righteous man who left the garden to his people and town; these locations are alternative reports.) [عكرمة: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL,BINTSHATI]
- 68:18/v003 In one account the owner left poor neighbors the dates and grapes that harvesters missed, and the sons later decided to stop sharing them. [ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 68:18/v004 Ibn Abbas reports five sons planning to keep an unusually abundant harvest from Muslim poor; four agreed and the moderate brother objected. (Their father had given each rightful person a share.) [ابن عباس: TABATABAI]
- 68:18/v005 God heard their failure to say 'if God wills' and sent fire from heaven that night, burning the garden black. [MUQATIL]

## whole verse · links
- 68:18/v006 The preceding oath was to cut or harvest the fruit at dawn, before poor people could learn of the harvest or ask for charity; the oath's response predicts that cutting. (Al-Tha'labi glosses aqsumu as 'swore,' yasrim as 'cut or harvest,' and musbihin as 'at dawn'; al-Farra and Abu Hayyan describe harvesting secretly in darkness.) [ABUHAYYAN-FULL,FARRA,IBNKATHIR-FULL,THALABI]
- 68:18/v007 The test of the garden owners parallels God's test of the Quraysh disbelievers. [TAB-FULL]
- 68:18/v008 The failure to say 'if God wills' is the parable's first lesson and connects it to the question about their wealth in verses 14-15. [ASAD-NOTES]
- 68:18/v009 The oath to leave nothing for the poor stresses the same stinginess as Quraysh leaders' forbidding good, especially Abu Lahab and his associates. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## وَلَا (word 1) · meaning
- 68:18/v010 Saying 'if God wills' can be called an exception because, though grammatically a condition, it makes the act depend on God's will and limits or suspends the oath's absolute force. (Al-Jishumi distinguishes excluding a member of a general class from lifting a statement's ruling by invoking God's will; al-Baydawi compares intending harvest with not harvesting until God wills.) [ALUSI-FULL,BAYDAWI-FULL,BIQAI-FULL,JISHUMI,NASAFI-FULL,SAMIN-DURR; الزمخشري: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 68:18/v011 Istithna' derives from th-n-y, restraining or turning back; a qualification turns an oath back to God's will or prevents it from taking unconditional effect. (Al-Wahidi notes several forms for an oath without exception.) [RAZI-FULL,WAHIDI-BASIT; الإمام: ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:18/v012 An exception removes part of a preceding general statement or removes a ruling from the form in which it was stated. (Al-Bursevi reports al-Raghib's definition and cites 6:145.) [الرغب: BURSEVI; JISHUMI]

## وَلَا (word 1) · grammar
- 68:18/v013 The clause begins a new sentence; treating it as circumstantial is weak because a negated present verb does not take an introductory waw, and no hidden topic is needed. (This disagrees with the circumstantial parse reported from al-Kashf.) [SAMIN-DURR+]
- 68:18/v014 The clause is circumstantial: they swore while in a state of making no exception. (Al-Alusi reports this from al-Kashf; al-Samin considers the parse weak.) [في الكشف: ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:18/v015 On the reading that they reserved no share for the poor, the clause may attach either to their harvesting oath or to its timing. [ALUSI-FULL]

## وَلَا (word 1) · rhetoric
- 68:18/v016 The present tense, where a past tense might be expected, evokes their ongoing failure to make an exception and signals their error. [ALUSI-FULL]

- 68:18/v022 The justest brother's later call to glorify God means making the omitted exception by saying 'if God wills.' (Al-Farra connects this with the instruction to remember God after forgetting in 18:24.) [FARRA]
- 68:18/v023 A person should qualify plans for future action with God's will, since an intended act may be prevented and human will alone cannot ensure it happens. (Al-Nahhas adds that without qualification a prevented action makes the speaker a liar. Elmalili names reaching morning safely and escaping accidents as possible qualifications; Kuranyolu cites 18:23-24 and 31:34.) [ELMALILI,JISHUMI,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR,NAHHAS]
- 68:18/v024 Their omission shows overconfidence and reliance on themselves or outward causes instead of acknowledging God's will and trusting Him. (Elmalili says they did not consider failing their vow or a reversal of fortune.) [مجاهد: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ELMALILI,RAZI-FULL,STUDYQURAN,TABATABAI; Bg, IK, Z: STUDYQURAN]
- 68:18/v025 The owners are blamed both for omitting the exception and for swearing to deny the poor their due. [JISHUMI]
- 68:18/v026 Resolving to commit the evil act was itself blameworthy and brought punishment before the wrong was done; actually taking property and denying rights would be worse. [JISHUMI]
- 68:18/v027 They intended to give nothing to anyone else and did not consider that someone besides them might harvest the garden. (This is offered as another interpretation of their failure to make an exception.) [ELMALILI]
