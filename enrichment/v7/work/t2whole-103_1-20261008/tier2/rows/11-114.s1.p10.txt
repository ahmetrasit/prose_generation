[ASAD-NOTES:11:114#n144/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The literal phrase is at the two ends of the day.
[ASAD-NOTES:11:114#n145/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The command covers the obligatory prayers whose forms and exact times are set by the Prophet's authenticated sayings and practice: dawn, after midday, afternoon, sunset, and early night.

[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:11:114/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Day runs from dawn to sunset and night from sunset to dawn; the day’s two edges are dawn and sunset. The prayer at the second edge is variously identified as noon and afternoon before sunset, or sunset and night prayers after it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:11:114/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Zulef, plural of zulfa, means the night’s early hours near day and refers here to the night prayer; because the verse gives no form or exact times, it stresses prayer at the indicated periods without detailing them, as Shawkani notes.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:11:114/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · some interpreters | reports | Some hold that this verse sets the times for all obligatory prayers; the mutawatir Sunna supplies their specific times and manner.
