[TABATABAI:2:265/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | One reported interpretation takes 'stabilizing themselves' to mean assent and certainty.
[TABATABAI:2:265/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Another reported interpretation says they carefully decide where to place their money.
[TABATABAI:2:265/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | A further interpretation says they verify their spending, proceeding if it is for God and withholding if ostentation intrudes.
[TABATABAI:2:265/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Another interpretation makes it a resolve of the self to obey God.
[TABATABAI:2:265/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قيل | reports | Another interpretation is to establish oneself in faith by habituating oneself to give for God's sake.
[TABATABAI:2:265/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The author says none of these reported explanations fits the wording without strained interpretation.
[TABATABAI:2:265/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | The passage first excludes ostentation, which invalidates a deed from the outset, and spending later spoiled by mann or harm; it then describes sincere givers who hold fast to their pure intention.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:265/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Giving to those in need from faith and sincere inner conviction for God's pleasure yields in proportion to intention, like an orchard protected from harmful winds that bears much in heavy rain and some in drizzle.
[QIRAAT-ER:2:265:17#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | Changing fatḥa to ḍamma on rāʾ gives rubwa instead of rabwa; both dialectal forms mean elevated ground.
[QIRAAT-ER:2:265:22#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | holds | Sukūn on kāf lightens pronunciation while preserving meaning; both forms are canonical.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r10] STUDYQURAN · IK, Ṭ | reports | IK and Ṭ understand giving out of inner confirmation as expressing certainty of faith in the act of giving.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r11] STUDYQURAN · Z | reports | Z reads inner confirmation as confirmation of God’s Truth or certainty.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r12] STUDYQURAN · Q, R | reports | Q and R understand the phrase as giving carefully and confirming that charity is placed where it will be used properly.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Rain imagery contrasts charity spoiled by bad intention with giving done for God’s pleasure and secure faith.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Rain on a fertile garden doubles its yield, symbolizing charity given with the right intention.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Even without a downpour, a softer rain lets the garden thrive and bear fruit.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r6] STUDYQURAN · Some commentators | reports | Some commentators say elevated oases gather rich earth and flourish; because streams do not reach them, rain alone nourishes them.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r7] STUDYQURAN · Others | reports | Others read bi-rabwah as something that rises, meaning fertile soil from which crops grow upward.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r8] STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī | reports | Al-Rāzī argues hills lack streams and face too much wind, while hollows flood; he says level ground is best and prefers a meaning tied to growth.
[STUDYQURAN:2:264/r9] STUDYQURAN · author | reports | A hadith says God grows a lawfully earned date given in charity until it becomes mountain-sized or larger.
