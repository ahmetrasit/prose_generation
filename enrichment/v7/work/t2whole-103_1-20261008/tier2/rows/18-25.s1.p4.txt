[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | The nine years may reconcile the solar-year count of the People of Scripture with the lunar-year count of Arabs, and this explanation is attributed by some to ʿAlī; al-Ālūsī details astronomical objections to the claimed nine-year difference.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الخفاجي | reports | Al-Khafājī says the two counts are explicit in context: three hundred by the People of the Book's reckoning, then nine extra by the questioners' reckoning; the difference is approximate because the remaining fraction is below half a year.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الطيبي | reports | Al-Ṭībī proposes that the sleepers nearly awoke after three hundred years but something kept them sleeping nine additional years; al-Ālūsī objects that the verse would then need to say their sleep increased.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p251#3/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Al-Ālūsī rejects the nine-year sleep-extension proposal because it would require an unexpressed meaning, that their sleep itself grew stronger for nine years.
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p252/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · بعضهم | reports | Some say the People of the Book disagreed over the sleepers' duration as they did their number; the verse resolves the disagreement, and the nine-year phrase confirms the answer like the exception in the account of Noah's thousand years. | mentions 29:14
