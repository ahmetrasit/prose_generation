# 78:14: 48 views from 179 rows (sol-high)

## whole verse · rhetoric
- 78:14/v001 The sūra and al-Mursalāt share an affirmative-question style addressing deniers; each question reproaches them while good news for believers is understood against the warning. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## whole verse · sciences
- 78:14/v002 The sūra is Meccan and, in Jābir ibn Zayd's revelation order, follows al-Maʿārij and precedes al-Nāziʿāt. [IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 78:14/v003 Medinan, Syrian, and Basran verse counters count forty verses in the sūra; Meccan and Kufan counters count forty-one. [IBNASHUR-FULL]

## whole verse · interpretation
- 78:14/v004 Sky, earth, rain, and plants are pictured as a reproductive pair: sky as male, earth as female, water as semen, and vegetation as offspring; springs and pools are implied. [BIQAI-FULL]

## whole verse · links
- 78:14/v005 The signs of creation, including rain, display power and purposeful providence and support the certainty of resurrection, judgment, reward, and punishment. (Muqātil calls each sign more astonishing than resurrection; Iṣlāḥī says their mercy, wisdom, and power show humans are accountable.) [مقاتل: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL,ISLAHI-TADABBUR,KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 78:14/v006 Paired with al-Mursalāt, this sūra uses signs in heaven and earth to establish Judgment as the necessary end of a purposeful world. (The account includes reward for the God-fearing and punishment for rebels.) [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ (word 3) · interpretation
- 78:14/v027 Al-Azharī favors rain clouds over whirlwind winds because whirlwinds do not carry the abundant rain described here. (The disagreement turns on whether the proposed winds bring rain.) [الأزهري: WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 78:14/v028 The sky interpretation can describe water passing from heaven into clouds, with heaven conceived as pressing or enabling the pressing. (Al-Naḥḥās allows winds, clouds, and sky to converge in a rain process; al-Ālūsī considers the heaven reconciliation strained.) [الحسن، قتادة: SAMIN-DURR; قتادة: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 78:14/v029 Al-Ālūsī objects to taking muʿṣirāt as the heavens because heaven is not squeezed to produce rain. (He calls a proposed reconciliation far-fetched.) [أبو الحسن وابن جبير وزيد بن أسلم ومقاتل وقتادة: ALUSI-FULL]

## مَآءًۭ (word 4) · reports
- 78:14/v030 A report says winds collect water and drive it into clouds until they fill like a milch camel; the water then falls in heavy showers distributed by winds. (The notes vary between wind carrying water, clouds filling, and drops resembling water drawn from a pregnant she-camel.) [ابن مسعود: NAHHAS; عبد الله بن مسعود: DURR-FULL; THALABI]

## مَآءًۭ (word 4) · interpretation
- 78:14/v031 Winds are said to carry water from heaven or its lowest sea of provisions into clouds, where it gathers and becomes rain. (The wind-water report in al-Samīn is qualified 'if sound'; Muqātil links the heavenly provision to 51:22.) [MUQATIL,NASAFI-FULL; الزمخشري: SAMIN-DURR]
- 78:14/v032 Rain may descend through clouds or arise through celestial causes, including the sun lifting water from earth and seas into clouds. (This reconciles rain's relation to the sky with its passage through clouds.) [BURSEVI]
- 78:14/v033 Rain from the clouds cools the sun's heat and enables food and drink. (This is the stated benefit of the water.) [BIQAI-FULL]
- 78:14/v034 Pounding, widespread, continuous rain displays harmony between sky and earth and supports monotheism, providence, and accountability. [ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## مَآءًۭ (word 4) · links
- 78:14/v035 Muqātil says the Hour cannot come while any water remains, connecting the rain provision to the end of the world. (The same note describes clouds and successive rain.) [MUQATIL]
