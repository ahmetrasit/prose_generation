# Notes

## 18:82
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p181/r8] verses 18:82 | Muqatil says their father was a trustworthy man. «وقال مقاتل كان أبوهما ذا أمانة»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p181/r9] verses 18:82 | The shift from “I intended” to “we intended” to “your Lord intended” restricts the final will to God and clarifies the earlier wording; varied phrasing is also more pleasing in Arabic. «أتبعهما بما يحصر الإرادة عليه ويزيلها عن غيره ويكشف البغية من اللفظتين الأولين»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p182/r1] verses 18:82 | The wall’s restoration was a mercy from God to the boys; Qatada says Khidr was a servant acting under command, not on his own initiative. «رحمة من ربك أي رحمهما الله بذلك وما فعلته عن أمري قال قتادة كان عبدا مأمورا»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p182/r2] verses 18:82 | In “you could not endure,” tastatiʿ and tastaṭiʿ have the same meaning. «فأما قوله تسطع فان استطاع و اسطاع بمعنى واحد»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p185/r1] verses 18:77, 18:82 | Qarya can designate a city: the story first calls the settlement a village and then a city, as in other Qur'anic usage. «في هذه الآية دليل على إطلاق القرية على المدينة»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p185/r2] verses 18:82 | Ikrima, Qatada, and others understand the treasure as buried wealth; Ibn Kathir calls this the apparent sense and reports Ibn Jarir's preference. «قال عكرمة، وقتادة، وغير واحد: كان تحته مال مدفون لهما.»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p185/r3] verses 18:82 | Ibn Abbas and Sa'id ibn Jubayr say the treasure was knowledge, while Mujahid calls it knowledge-bearing pages. «وقال العوفي عن ابن عباس: كان تحته كنز علم.»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p185/r4] verses 18:82 | A raised report describes a gold tablet of maxims about divine decree, remembering Hell, and remembering death; Ibn Kathir says it supports the knowledge view if sound. «عن أبي ذر رضي الله عنه، [رفعه] (1) قال: "إن الكنز الذي ذكر (2) الله في كتابه:»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p185/r5] verses 18:82 | Hasan says the treasure was a gold tablet with maxims against grief despite belief in decree, joy despite certainty of death, and trust in worldly life; al-Uqayli notes a transmitter's error in the raised report. «قال: سمعت الحسن -يعني البصري-يقول في قوله: {وكان تحته كنز لهما} قال: لوح من ذهب»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186#2/r1] verses 18:82 | A righteous man's worship can protect descendants in this life and the next through intercession and raised rank; Ibn Abbas says the boys were protected by their father's piety although their own is unstated. «فيه دليل على أن الرجل الصالح يحفظ في ذريته»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186#2/r2] verses 18:82 | The boys' reaching maturity is assigned to God's will, while the ship's damage is assigned to Khidr and the boy's replacement to a shared we. «هاهنا أسند الإرادة إلى الله تعالى؛ لأن بلوغهما الحلم (3) لا يقدر عليه إلا الله؛»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186#2/r4] verses 18:82 | Some say Khidr was a messenger; al-Mawardi transmits that he was an angel, while many hold he was a saint rather than a prophet. «وقال آخرون: كان رسولا. وقيل بل كان ملكا. نقله الماوردي في تفسيره.»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186#2/r5] verses 18:82 | Al-Nawawi and Ibn al-Salah leaned toward Khidr remaining alive, but Ibn Kathir says the reports for this view are unsound, including the famous weak condolence report. «وحكى هو وغيره في كونه باقيا إلى الآن ثم إلى يوم القيامة قولين»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186#2/r6] verses 18:82 | Ibn Kathir favors the view Khidr is not alive: no human before Muhammad was immortal, he never joined the Prophet despite the universal mission, and a report says none alive that night remained after a century. «ورجح آخرون من المحدثين وغيرهم خلاف ذلك، واحتجوا بقوله تعالى:»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186#2/r7] verses 18:82 | The Prophet said that if Moses and Jesus were alive they would have to follow him. «وقد قال: "لو كان موسى وعيسى حيين ما (10) وسعهما إلا اتباعي"»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186/r1] verses 18:82 | Umar, freedman of Ghufra, reports a gold tablet questioning anxiety after certainty of decree and security after certainty of death, and warning against laughter after knowing Hell. «عن عمر (11) مولى غفرة (12) قال: إن الكنز الذي قال الله في السورة التي يذكر فيها الكهف:»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186/r2] verses 18:82 | Ja'far ibn Muhammad says the tablet's two and a half lines warn against tiring over provision, heedlessness about judgment, and joy despite certainty of death. «سمعت جعفر بن محمد يقول في قول الله تعالى (15) {وكان تحته كنز لهما} قال: سطران ونصف»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186/r3] verses 18:82 | A report says the boys were preserved because of their father's righteousness though their own is unstated; seven generations separated them, and their ancestor was a weaver. «قالت: وذكر أنهما حفظا بصلاح أبيهما، ولم يذكر منهما صلاح، وكان بينهما وبين الأب»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p186/r4] verses 18:82 | The gold-tablet reports do not conflict with the view that the treasure was wealth: the tablet was gold and could hold valuable wisdom and admonitions. «وهذا الذي ذكره هؤلاء الأئمة، وورد به الحديث المتقدم وإن صح، لا ينافي قول عكرمة:»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p188#2/r1] verses 18:82 | The attendant's alleged immortality story has a weak chain: al-Hasan is abandoned and his father is unknown. «إسناد ضعيف، والحسن متروك، وأبوه غير معروف.»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p188/r1] verses 18:82 | The Prophet says Khidr was named because he sat on dry vegetation and it became green beneath him; Ibn Kathir explains furwa as dry, brittle plants, though another view takes it as the earth's surface. «عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم [في الخضر قال] (1) إنما سمي "خضرا"؛ لأنه جلس على فروة بيضاء،»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p188/r2] verses 18:82 | After explanation, the Qur'an shortens tastaṭiʿ to tasṭaʿ; the heavier form earlier matched the harder burden of an unresolved puzzle. «فقابل الأثقل بالأثقل، والأخف بالأخف، كما قال تعالى:»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p188/r3] verses 18:82 | The attendant is secondary to the story's focus on Moses and Khidr; sound reports identify him as Joshua son of Nun, who later led Israel. «وقد صرح في الأحاديث المتقدمة في الصحاح وغيرها أنه يوشع بن نون، وهو الذي كان يلي بني إسرائيل بعد موسى،»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p188/r4] verses 18:82 | Ibn Kathir rejects Ibn Jarir's weak report that the attendant drank life-water, became immortal, was trapped in a boat, and released into the sea. «وهذا يدل على ضعف ما أورده ابن جرير في تفسيره حيث قال»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p473/r7] verses 18:60, 18:82 | The account completes the preceding instruction for the Prophet to be patient with his arrogant enemies. «In the succeeding verses, this topic is brought to completion.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p474/r1] verses 18:60, 18:82 | At the surah’s climax, patience is an active virtue on which rights and duties toward God and people depend. «patience is not a negative attitude. It is a positive reality. It is on it that all rights thrive and sustain.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p474/r2] verses 18:60, 18:82 | Patience rests on belief that everything occurs by God’s permission and will, down to the movement of a particle. «whatever happens in this world happens because of God’s permission and under His will and intention.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p474/r3] verses 18:58, 18:60, 18:82 | God is good and wise: respite for wrongdoers serves a good purpose, while hardship for the righteous can open a beneficial way. «If He gives respite to evildoers, it is not because He loves them or is helpless before them. In this respite too, He wants to nourish a great good.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p474/r4] verses 18:60, 18:82 | Human knowledge is limited, so people should patiently and gratefully perform their duties; the wisdom of God’s decisions will be fully disclosed in the Hereafter. «All the secrets of these intentions will only stand revealed in the hereafter. The right attitude for human beings in this world is that they should be patient and grateful»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p482/r1] verses 18:82 | Moses’ companion clarifies that he did none of the acts from his own opinion; God’s concessions to wrongdoers serve a hidden expediency and complete the case against them while advancing truth for its adherents. «these miscreants only complete God’s conclusive argument against them. However, through them God furthers the cause of the adherents of truth»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p483/r3] verses 18:79, 18:82 | When a person’s will accords with God’s, an act may be attributed to either; rhetorical etiquette assigns the apparently bad act of damaging a ship to Khidr, while the good act of preserving orphans’ treasure is attributed to God. «when a person’s will matches the will of God, he can ascribe it to God as well as to himself. The difference has much to do with the requisites of eloquence.»
