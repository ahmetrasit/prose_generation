[SAMIN-DURR:17:110/r9] verses 17:110 | A conditional parse that stops on ayyan is rejected: mā does not refer to individual rational beings, conditionals require generality, and both parts would be omitted. «وهذا مردود بأن " ما " لا تطلق على آحاد أولي العلم، وبأن الشرط يقتضي عموما، ولا يصح هنا»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r1] verses 17:110 | God and al-Raḥmān are both divine names; al-Raḥmān is a name of the Essence and also functions as a proper name. «the latter, one of the most important Names of God, is considered a Name pertaining to the Divine Essence»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r10] verses 17:110 | He says one should avoid loud prayer as ostentation and quiet prayer out of fear of others. «According to Ibn ʿAbbās, one should not pray loudly, so as to make an ostentatious display for other people, or too quietly, out of fear of them (IK).»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r2] verses 17:110 | One report says the verse followed idolaters hearing the Prophet call on Allah and al-Raḥmān, then accuse him of invoking two gods. «invoking two separate Divine beings (Bḍ, IK)»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r3] verses 17:110 | Another account has the Prophet call al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm, prompting the same accusation; the verse clarifies the names refer to one God. «invoking two separate Divine beings (Bḍ, IK)»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r4] verses 17:110 | The Most Beautiful Names are mentioned elsewhere, and their significance spans Muslim belief, practice, and piety. «The mention of God’s Most Beautiful Names is also found in 7:180; 20:8; 59:24.»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r5] verses 17:110 | Prayer should avoid loudness and show reverence and humility; some connect this with commands not to raise voices before the Prophet during prayer. «One should be not loud in . . . prayer (see also 7:205), but rather demonstrate reverence and humility when praying»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r6] verses 17:110 | Some say quiet recitation protects the sacred prayer from idolaters overhearing and cursing or disparaging it. «the idolaters overhear the prayer and respond by cursing or disparaging the sacred act (Bḍ, IK).»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r7] verses 17:110 | Prayer should not be too quiet, so that beneficiaries can hear; an imam must be audible to those behind him. «the imām, or prayer leader, should not recite so quietly that it is inaudible to those praying behind him (Bḍ).»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r8] verses 17:110 | One report applies the instruction to personal supplication, although ṣalāh usually means canonical prayer. «this injunction pertains to personal supplicatory prayer (duʿāʾ), although the verse uses ṣalāh, which usually designates the canonical prayer (IK).»
[STUDYQURAN:17:110/r9] verses 17:110 | The middle way in prayer reflects Quranic moderation, the middle community, and the straight path. «One should seek a way between is consistent with the Quran’s exhortation toward moderation in other matters»
[SULAMI:v1p399#2/r1] verses 17:110 | No one invokes God except through faith; a true invocation is not attained in that way. «ما دعا الله أحد قط إلا إيمانا وإما دعوة حقيقة فلا»
[SULAMI:v1p399#2/r2] verses 17:110 | God's names cannot be counted, and His essence is not truly pointed to or described except by praise; the Real exceeds concepts and understanding, so predicates cannot contain Him. «قال الواسطي رحمة الله عليه : أسماؤه لا تدخل تحت الحصر ، وذاته ليس بمشار إليه ولا بموصوف حقيقة»
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p141/r2] verses 17:110 | Ibn Abbas reports that when the Prophet prayed in Mecca and invoked Allah and al-Rahman, polytheists mocked him for supposedly calling two gods, prompting the verse permitting either name. «عن ابن عباس قال صلى الله عليه وسلم بمكة ذات يوم فدعا فقال في دعائه يا الله يا رحمن فقال المشركون انظروا إلى هذا الصابىء»
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p141/r3] verses 17:110 | Ibn Abbas reports that while secretly in Mecca the Prophet recited aloud in prayer; polytheists insulted the Qur'an, its revealer, and its bearer, prompting the instruction not to recite too loudly or too quietly. «عن ابن عباس في قوله ﴿ ولا تجهر بصلاتك ولا تخافت بها ﴾ قال نزلت ورسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم مختف بمكة»
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p141/r4] verses 17:110 | Aisha reports that the instruction not to recite too loudly or quietly concerned supplication; Ibn Jarir also transmits a similar report from Ibn Abbas. «وأخرج البخاري أيضا عن عائشة أنها نزلت في الدعاء»
[SUYUTI-LUBAB:v1p141/r5] verses 17:110 | Suyuti and Nawawi prefer the first, stronger-chain explanation; Ibn Hajar says both can be reconciled if the supplication occurred inside prayer, supported by a report from Abu Hurayra about prayer at the Kaaba. «ثم رجح الأولى لكونها أصح سندا وكذا رجحها النووي وغيره وقال الحافظ ابن حجر لكن يحتمل الجمع بينهما بأنها نزلت في الدعاء داخل الصلاة»
