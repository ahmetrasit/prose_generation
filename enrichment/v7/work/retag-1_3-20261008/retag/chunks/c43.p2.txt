[BURSEVI:v3p11#2/r3] verses 6:12 | Following senses and conjecture, blind imitation, and neglecting reflection disables reason, entrenches disbelief, and removes people from special mercy. «فإن إبطال العقل باتباع الحواس والوهم والانهماك في التقليد وإغفال النظر أدى بهم إلى الإصرار على الكفر»
[BURSEVI:v3p11#2/r4] verses 6:12 | Al-Qadi says mercy encompasses both worlds, including guidance to know God and His oneness through proofs, revealed books, and respite for unbelievers. «القاضي : والمراد بالرحمة ما يعم الدارين ومن ذلك الهداية إلى معرفته»
[BURSEVI:v3p11#2/r5] verses 6:12 | The Kashifi commentary identifies this as God's essential, all-encompassing mercy, whose first gift is bestowed without prior request, need, or deserving claim. «وفي "تفسير الكاشفي" : (مراد رحمت»
[BURSEVI:v3p11#2/r6] verses 6:12 | A Mathnawi couplet supports the claim that people had no prior claim to existence, life, or knowledge; God's grace hears even an unspoken need. «ما نبوديم وتقاضا مان نبود لطف تونا كفته ما مى شنود»
[BURSEVI:v3p11#2/r7] verses 6:12 | Abu Hurayra reports the Prophet saying God kept ninety-nine of one hundred parts of mercy and sent one to earth, from which creatures show mercy, even a mare lifting its hoof for its foal. «عن أبي هريرة قال سمعت رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[BURSEVI:v3p11#2/r8] verses 6:12 | This report gives Muslims great hope: one part already accounts for the world's outward and inward blessings, so the hundred parts in the next world promise far more. «فهذا مما يدل على كمال الرجاء والبشارة للمسلمين لأنه حصل في هذه الدار من رحمة واحدة ما حصل من النعم»
[BURSEVI:v3p11#2/r9] verses 6:12 | Umar reports that the Prophet compared God's mercy for His servants to a mother who would never cast her nursing child into fire. «وعن عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه قال : قدم على النبي عليه السلام»
[BURSEVI:v3p11/r1] verses 6:12 | A cited Hafez couplet longs for the return of people to their former ordered condition; the author glosses it as a return to the state they had been in. «أي : كالوضع الذي كانوا عليه من الانتظام مطلقا»
[BURSEVI:v3p11/r2] verses 6:12 | The question presses Meccans to admit that all rational and non-rational things in heaven and earth belong to God by creation, ownership, and control. «إلجاء لأهل مكة إلى الإقرار بأن الكل من العقلاء وغيرهمخلقا وملكا وتصرفا»
[BURSEVI:v3p11/r3] verses 6:12 | Because the answer is self-evident, the questioner answers before others; the passage hints that no answer should be awaited before confessing it. «وفي تصدي السائل للجواب قبل أن يجيب غيره إيماء إلى أن مثل هذا السؤال لكون جوابه متعينا»
[BURSEVI:v3p11/r4] verses 6:12 | God's self-imposed mercy means He graciously undertook it, delays punishment, and accepts repentance; nothing is truly obligatory upon Him. «ومعنى كتب الرحمة على نفسه التزمها وأوجبها تفضلا وإحسانا لأنه تعالى منزه عن أن يجب عليه شيء حقيقة»
[BURSEVI:v3p11/r5] verses 6:12 | Calling God “Himself” is evidence against those who deny that the word nafs may be used of God. «وفي التعبير عن الذات بالنفس حجة على من ادعى أن لفظ النفس لا يطلق على الله تعالى»
