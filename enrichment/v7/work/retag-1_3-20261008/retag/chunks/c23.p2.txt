[THALABI:v1p92/r7] verses 1:1 | Abu Khalid ibn Yazid al-Muradi says the alif of ism was dropped for frequent use and its length was transferred to the bā; where ism retains alif, the bā keeps its ordinary form. «العلة فيها إسقاط الألف من الاسم، فلما أسقطوا الألف ردوا طول الألف الى الباء»
[THALABI:v1p92/r8] verses 1:1 | The phrase has an omitted command, understood as “say” or “begin” in God’s name. «وفي الكلام إضمار واختصار تقديره: قل، أو ابدأ بسم الله»
[THALABI:v1p92/r9] verses 1:1 | Adam treats ism as an added word, glossing the phrase as “By God, the Merciful, the Compassionate, He is”; Labid’s verse uses ism for “peace.” «وقال آدم: الاسم فيه صلة، مجازه: بالله الرحمن الرحيم هو»
[THALABI:v1p93/r1] verses 1:1 | The Qurʾānic phrase about people worshiping only names they assigned means the objects themselves, since they worshiped the named beings. «وأراد الأشخاص المعبودة لأنهم كانوا يعبدون المسميات»
[THALABI:v1p93/r2] verses 1:1 | The commands to glorify or bless God’s name use “name” to mean God Himself; the report about Mudar likewise says no one would worship Him. «أي حتى لا يعبد هو»
[THALABI:v1p93/r3] verses 1:1 | Although ism is more commonly used for naming than for the named, its plural is asmāʾ; its derivation from samā means to rise, since existence rises above nonexistence, while naming is an expression or description. «واشتقاقه من (سما) (يسمو) ، فكأن المخبر عنه بأنه معدوم»
[THALABI:v1p93/r4] verses 1:1 | In a report from Abu Saʿid al-Khudri, Jesus asks his teacher about bismillah; Jesus explains bā as God’s splendor, sīn as His radiance, and mīm as His kingdom. «قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: «إن عيسى بن مريم»
[THALABI:v1p93/r5] verses 1:1 | Abu Bakr al-Warraq describes bismillah as a garden of Paradise whose every letter has its own interpretation, expanded in the following segment. «سمعت أبا بكر محمد بن عمر الوراق يقول في بسم الله: إنها روضة من رياض الجنة»
[THALABI:v1p94/r1] verses 1:1 | Al-Warraq’s letter gloss gives six b-attributes: creator, seer, provider, everlasting, raiser of the dead, and benefactor; the commentary supplies Qurʾānic proofs for them. «فالباء على ستة أوجه: بارئ خلقه من العرش الى الثرى»
[THALABI:v1p94/r2] verses 1:1 | The sīn gloss gives five attributes: hearing, lordship, swift reckoning, safeguarding creatures from injustice, and forgiving sins, with Qurʾānic proofs. «والسين على خمسة أوجه: سميع لأصوات خلقه من العرش إلى الثرى»
[THALABI:v1p94/r3] verses 1:1 | The mīm gloss gives twelve attributes, including ruler, owner, benefactor, glorious, giver of security, overseer, powerful, sustainer, ennobler, bestower, and creator; each receives a Qurʾānic proof. «والميم على اثني عشر وجها: ملك الخلق من العرش إلى الثرى»
[THALABI:v1p95/r1] verses 1:1 | The final mīm glosses are that God grants favor to all creation and forms His creation, supported by verses on divine favor and creation. «متفضل على خلقه من العرش إلى الثرى، بيانه: ولكن الله ذو فضل على العالمين»
[THALABI:v1p95/r2] verses 1:1 | The people of spiritual realities say the basmala is for blessing and encourages beginning words and deeds with it, following the Qurʾān’s own opening. «في بسم الله التيمن والتبرك وحث الناس على الابتداء في أقوالهم وأفعالهم»
[THALABI:v1p95/r3] verses 1:1 | The Kufans derive Allah from ilāh: the definite article magnified the name, then the hamza dropped through frequent use and the two lām letters assimilated. «أصل هذه الكلمة (إله) في قول أهل الكوفة، فأدخلت الألف واللام فيها»
[THALABI:v1p95/r4] verses 1:1 | The Basrans derive Allah from lāh with the definite article, citing a verse that preserves the alleged original form. «وقال أهل البصرة: أصلها (لاه) ، فألحقت بها الألف واللام»
[THALABI:v1p95/r5] verses 1:1 | Another account says the definite article replaced the deleted hamza and therefore remains inseparable; this explains why one cannot say “yā ayyuhā Allāh.” «وقال بعضهم: أدخلت الألف واللام بدلا من الهمزة المحذوفة في (إله)»
[THALABI:v1p95/r6] verses 1:1 | Al-Khalil and others say Allah is a proper name, not derived; the verse “Do you know any namesake for Him?” supports its unique reference. «فقال الخليل بن أحمد وجماعة: (الله) اسم علم موضوع غير مشتق بوجه»
[THALABI:v1p95/r7] verses 1:1 | They say Allah is exclusive to God because it contains lordship and encompasses all meanings; its successive deletions yield li-llāh, lah, and huwa. «ألا ترى أنك إذا أسقطت منه الألف بقي لله، وإذا أسقطت من لله اللام الأولى بقي (له)»
