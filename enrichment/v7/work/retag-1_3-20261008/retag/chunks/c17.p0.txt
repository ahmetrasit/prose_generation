# Notes

## 1:1
[RAZI-FULL:v1p143/r4] verses 1:1 | In a story, Umar sends Caesar a cap with Bismillah inside; wearing it relieves Caesar’s headache, which returns when he removes it. «كتب قيصر إلى عمر رضي الله عنه أن بي صداعا لا يسكن فابعث لي دواء»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p143/r5] verses 1:1 | A report says invoking God’s name in ablution purifies the whole body, while invoking Him from the heart should purify the heart of unbelief and innovation. «السابعة عشرة : قال صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p143/r6] verses 1:1 | A story says Khalid ibn al-Walīd ate deadly poison after invoking Bismillah and survived by God’s permission, prompting the Magians to accept Islam. «إنك تدعي الإسلام فأرنا آية لنسلم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146#2/r1] verses 1:1 | It is called the Foundation because it is the first sura, contains the noblest aims, and is indispensable to prayer, the greatest worship after faith. «أنها أول سورة من القرآن فهي كالأساس»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146#2/r2] verses 1:1 | The Prophet calls the Fātiḥa healing from every poison; a companion’s recitation cured an epileptic, and the Prophet also calls it healing from every disease. «أبي سعيد الخدري قال : قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Rāzī explains the healing spiritually and physically: since unbelief is called a disease, the sura’s theology, duties, and spiritual unveilings heal all three domains. «والدليل عليه أنه تعالى سمى الكفر مرضا»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146#2/r4] verses 1:1 | It is called prayer because God says He divided the prayer between Himself and His servant in two halves, meaning this sura. «الاسم التاسع : الصلاة قال عليه الصلاة والسلام»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146#2/r5] verses 1:1 | A report says whoever is occupied with remembering God rather than asking receives more than those who ask; Abraham models praise followed by requests. «روي أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم حكى عن رب العزة سبحانه وتعالى»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146#2/r6] verses 1:1 | Because the sura moves from praise to worship to a request for guidance, it teaches that religious guidance is the highest aim and the garden of knowledge surpasses the garden of pleasure. «وهذا يدل على أن أكمل المطالب هو الهداية في الدين وهو أيضا يدل على أن جنة المعرفة خير من جنة النعيم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146#2/r7] verses 1:1 | It is called the Sura of Gratitude because it praises God for His favor, generosity, and beneficence. «سورة الشكر وذلك لأنها ثناء على الله بالفضل والكرم والإحسان»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146#2/r8] verses 1:1 | It is called the Sura of Supplication because it includes the request for the straight path. «سورة الدعاء لاشتمالها على قوله»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r1] verses 1:1 | Ibn Durayd says umm can mean a banner; the Fātiḥa is the believers’ rallying point as a banner is an army’s, while earth is called mother because all return to it and umm can mean a destination. «الأم في كلام العرب الراية التي ينصبها العسكر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r2] verses 1:1 | One explanation of al-sabʿ al-mathānī is that half the sura is the servant’s praise and half God’s gift to the servant. «نصفها ثناء العبد للرب ونصفها عطاء الرب للعبد»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r3] verses 1:1 | Another explanation is that it is repeated in every prayer cycle. «سميت مثاني لأنها تثنى في كل ركعة من الصلاة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r4] verses 1:1 | A Prophetic report says no comparable sura occurs in the Torah, Gospel, Psalms, or Qur’an, and identifies it as the seven oft-repeated verses and the great Qur’an. «قال عليه الصلاة والسلام صلى الله عليه وسلم : والذي نفسي بيده ما أنزل في التوراة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r5] verses 1:1 | A further explanation says each of its seven verses equals the recitation of seven portions of the Qur’an, so reciting it earns the reward of reciting all of it. «سميت مثاني لأنها سبع آيات كل آية تعدل قراءتها قراءة سبع من القرآن»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r6] verses 1:1 | Gabriel says the seven verses overlay Hell’s seven gates, so reciters pass them safely; he had feared punishment before the Fātiḥa was revealed. «روي أن جبريل عليه السلام قال للنبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r7] verses 1:1 | It is called the oft-repeated because it is recited in prayer and then followed by another sura. «لأنها تقرأ في الصلاة ثم إنها تثنى بسورة أخرى»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r8] verses 1:1 | Another explanation takes mathānī as praises and commendations of God. «سميت مثاني لأنها أثنية على الله تعالى ومدائح له»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p146/r9] verses 1:1 | A final explanation says God revealed the sura twice, which is why it is called oft-repeated. «سميت مثاني لأن الله أنزلها مرتين»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p147/r1] verses 1:1 | ʿAlī reports that the Fātiḥa descended in Mecca from a treasure beneath the Throne; al-Thaʿlabī says most scholars agree. «روى الثعلبي بإسناده عن علي بن أبي طالب رضي الله عنه»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p147/r2] verses 1:1 | ʿAmr ibn Shurayḥil says the first revelation was al-Ḥamd lillāh; after Muhammad confided his fear to Khadīja and consulted Waraqa, Gabriel instructed him to recite Bismillah and the Fātiḥa. «وروى أيضا بإسناده عن عمرو بن شرحبيل»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p147/r3] verses 1:1 | A report from Ibn ʿAbbās says the Prophet recited Bismillah and Quraysh cursed him. «وبإسناده عن أبي صالح عن ابن عباس»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p147/r4] verses 1:1 | Mujāhid says it descended in Medina, but Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl rejects this as a lapse against scholarly consensus. «روى الثعلبي بإسناده عن مجاهد أنه قال»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p147/r5] verses 1:1 | Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl argues that the Meccan Sūrat al-Ḥijr already refers to the seven oft-repeated verses and that it is implausible for the Prophet to live in Mecca over a decade without the Fātiḥa. «أن سورة الحجر مكية بالاتفاق»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p147/r6] verses 1:1 | A third view holds that the sura descended once in Mecca and again in Medina, its double revelation explaining its honorific name al-mathānī. «هذه السورة نزلت بمكة مرة وبالمدينة مرة أخرى»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p148/r1] verses 1:1 | Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī reports from the Prophet that the Fātiḥa heals poison. «عن أبي سعيد الخدري عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه قال»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p148/r2] verses 1:1 | Ḥudhayfa ibn al-Yamān reports that a child’s recitation of al-Ḥamd lillāh during a destined calamity leads God to lift it for forty years. «وعن حذيفة بن اليمان قال»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p148/r3] verses 1:1 | Al-Ḥusayn says knowledge flowed from 104 heavenly books through the four major books and the Qur’an until it was concentrated in the Fātiḥa, whose interpretation equals knowledge of all revelation. «أنزل الله تعالى مائة وأربعة كتب من السماء فأودع علوم المائة في الأربعة وهي التوراة والإنجيل والزبور والفرقان»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p148/r4] verses 1:1 | Rāzī explains that the Fātiḥa contains all divine aims because it fully treats theology, religious practice, and spiritual unveiling. «والسبب فيه أن المقصود من جميع الكتب الإلهية علم الأصول والفروع والمكاشفات»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p148/r5] verses 1:1 | The sura omits seven letters associated with torment: thāʾ with ruin, jīm with Hell, khāʾ with disgrace, zāy and shīn with breath, the tree or misery, ẓāʾ with shade and blazing fire, and fāʾ with separation or falsehood. «أن هذه الحروف السبعة مشعرة بالعذاب فالثاء تدل على الويل والثبور»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p148/r6] verses 1:1 | Rāzī answers that, although all letters can occur in words about punishment, these seven omitted initials correspond to Hell’s seven gates; understanding the sura protects readers from them. «والله تعالى أسقط سبعة من الحروف من هذه السورة وهي أوائل ألفاظ دالة على العذاب»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p160#2/r1] verses 1:1 | Al-Qadi Abu Bakr calls the basmala's Quranic status categorical: error is at least sinful, since its inclusion must be proved by mass transmission, not a solitary report. «وزعم القاضي أبو بكر أنها من المسائل القطعية قال : والخطأ فيها إن لم يبلغ إلى حد التكفير»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p160#2/r2] verses 1:1 | Al-Ghazali counters that denying the basmala's Quranic status faces the same proof dilemma; although absence is the default, its Quranic-script inscription requires separate evidence. «واعلم أن الشيخ الغزالي عارض القاضي فقال : نفي كون التسمية من القرآن إن ثبت بالتواتر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p160#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Razi says transmission establishes that the basmala was revealed to Muhammad and written in Quranic script; inclusion matters only for legal rulings such as prayer, recitation in impurity, and touching the codex, which are ijtihadi, so the issue is not categorical. «والذي عندي فيه أن النقل المتواتر ثابت بأن بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم كلام أنزله الله على محمد»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p160/r5] verses 1:1 | Al-Shafi'i says the basmala is a verse of al-Fatiha and must be read; Malik and al-Awza'i exclude it except in the Quran's Naml passage, with a Ramadan-night exception, while Abu Hanifa orders quiet recitation without stating its status. «قال الشافعي رضي الله عنه : بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم آية من أول سورة الفاتحة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p160/r6] verses 1:1 | The narrator reports asking Muhammad b. al-Hasan whether the basmala is Quran, introducing a discussion of its legal status. «قال يعلى سألت محمد بن الحسن عن بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم فقال»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p161/r1] verses 1:1 | Razi calls the report from Umm Salama an explicit text supporting the basmala inclusion in al-Fatiha. «وهذا نص صريح . الحجة الثانية :»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p161/r2] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | Umm Salama reports that the Prophet recited al-Fatiha while counting the basmala as its first verse, followed by the rest of its verses. «روى الشافعي رضي الله عنه عن مسلم عن ابن جريج عن ابن أبي مليكة عن أم سلمة أنها قالت»
