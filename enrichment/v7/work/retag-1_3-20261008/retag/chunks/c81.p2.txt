[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p159/r2] verses 18:74 | They use the doubled form throughout; Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim reads this word lightly. «وقرأ ابن عامر وعاصم في رواية أبي بكر في كل القرآن: (نكرا) و (نكر) مثقل»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p159/r3] verses 18:74 | Nāfiʿ's transmitters differ: Ismāʿīl lightens throughout except at al-Qamar, while Ibn Jamāz, Qālūn, al-Musayyibī, Warsh, and other named transmitters use the doubled form throughout. «واختلف عن نافع فروى إسماعيل بن جعفر نكرا خفيفا في كل القرآن»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v5p159/r4] verses 18:74 | Nukr is an adjective pattern that can be lightened like ʿunuq, ṭunub, and shughul; either lightening or doubling is correct, and readers may use both forms in different places. «فمن خفف ذلك، فكما يخفف العنق والعنق، والطنب والطنب»
[FARRA:v2p155/r2] verses 18:74 | The boy was innocent of any offense when Moses saw and killed him. «أقتلت نفسا (زكية) [74] مر بغلام لم تجن جناية رآها موسى فقتله.»
[FARRA:v2p155/r3] verses 18:74 | These readers have zakiyya; the Hijaz readers and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī have zākiya with alif, paralleling qāsiyya/qasiyya. «وقوله (زكية) قرأها عاصم ويحيى بن وثاب والحسن (زكية) وقرأها أهل الحجاز وأبو الرحمن السلمي (زاكية) بألف .»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p244/r2] verses 18:19, 18:74, 18:75 | The surah's placement near the Qur'an's midpoint is a divinely inspired arrangement: one proposed midpoint letter is ت in وليتلطف (Q 18:19), another is ن in نكرا (Q 18:74), and the midpoint of its thirty parts is at Q 18:75. «وهو ( التاء ) من قوله تعالى : وليتلطف ( الكهف : 19 ) وقيل نصف حروف القرآن هو ( النون )»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p377/r1] verses 18:74 | The transition after Moses' apology shows Khidr accepted it and they continued together; the sentence structure follows the ship episode. «يدل تفريع قوله : { فانطلقا حتى إذا لقيا غلاما } عن اعتذار موسى ، على أن الخضر قبل عذره»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p377/r2] verses 18:74 | The fronted timing shows Khidr killed the boy immediately upon meeting him, even more swiftly than he punctured the ship after boarding. «فكانت المبادرة بقتل الغلام عند لقائه أسرع من المبادرة بخرق السفينة»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p377/r3] verses 18:74 | Moses objects in the same pattern but calls this act نكر, an intellectually repugnant actual harm stronger than إمرا, which was only a route to possible harm. «وصف هذا الفعل بأنه نكر ، وهو بضمتين»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p377/r4] verses 18:74 | The soul is pure because the boy had not reached puberty and committed no sin; زكاء means increase in goodness. «ووصف النفس بالزاكية لأنها نفس غلام لم يبلغ الحلم فلم يقترف ذنبا»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p377/r5] verses 18:74 | Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Abū Jaʿfar, and Ruways read زاكية with an alif; the rest read زكية, with both forms meaning the same. «وقرأ نافع ، وابن كثير ، وأبو عمرو ، وأبو جعفر ، ورويس عن يعقوب { زاكية } بألف»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p377/r6] verses 18:74 | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the nūn of نكرا is the midpoint of the Quran's letters; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the majority instead place its midpoint at the tāʾ in 18:19. «قال ابن عطية : النون من قوله : { نكرا } هي نصف القرآن»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r1] verses 18:74 | After leaving the boat, they passed boys at play; Khidr chose a handsome boy and killed him by pulling his head off, crushing him with a rock, or cutting his throat, according to reports. «فمرا بغلمان يلعبون، فعمد الخضر إلى غلام حسن الهيئة وضيء، فاقتلع رأسه،»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r13] verses 18:74 | The verse notes that the Qurʾan’s division reaches the letter nun in nukran at its midpoint after the disjointed letters. «ونصف القرآن بعد الحروف انتهى إلى النون من قوله نكرا»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r2] verses 18:74 | One group says the boy was prepubescent, so Moses called him pure because he had not sinned; another says he was an adult youth, whom Arabs still call a boy, as in Layla al-Akhyaliyya’s line on al-Hajjaj. «وقال بعض الناس كان الغلام لم يبلغ الحلم، ولذلك قال موسى زكية أي لم تذنب،»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r3] verses 18:74 | A report says the boy did wrong, swore he had not, and his parents backed his oath and protected him from pursuers. «وفي الخبر أن هذا الغلام، كان يفسد في الأرض ويقسم لأبويه أنه ما فعل»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r4] verses 18:74 | Most read zākiya; al-Hasan, ʿAsim, and al-Jahdari read zakiyya. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the readings mean the same, despite attempts to distinguish them. «وقرأ ابن عباس والأعرج وأبو جعفر ونافع والجمهور «زاكية» ، وقرأ الحسن وعاصم»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r5] verses 18:74 | “Without a soul” implies the boy was grown: killing would be allowed if he killed another, while a child could not be killed either way. «وقوله بغير نفس يقتضي أنه لو كان عن قتل نفس لم يكن به بأس،»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r6] verses 18:74 | Most read nukran with silent kaf; Ibn ʿAmir, Abu Bakr from ʿAsim, Abu Jaʿfar, and Shayba round it; it means objectionable. «وقرأ الجمهور «نكرا» وقرأ ابن عامر وأبو بكر عن عاصم وأبو جعفر وشيبة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r7] verses 18:71, 18:74 | One group says killing is more objectionable because the wrong is visible and complete, unlike the boat’s impending danger; another says the boat threatened many and was worse. «فقالت فرقة هذا قتل بين، وهناك مترقب ف نكرا أبلغ وقالت فرقة هذا قتل واحد،»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p532/r8] verses 18:71, 18:74 | Both descriptions fit: the boat’s imr is more terrifying because disaster was expected, while nukr marks wrongdoing more plainly because harm occurred. «وعندي أنهما المعنيين، قوله إمرا [الكهف: 71] أفظع وأهول من حيث هو متوقع عظيم،»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p533/r4] verses 18:74, 18:71, 18:77 | A report says God pointed out parallels in Moses’s past: a box cast into water, killing an Egyptian, and lifting a well-stone for Shuʿayb’s daughters without wages, yet he objected to Khidr. «وذلك أنه لما أنكر أمر خرق السفينة، نودي يا موسى أين كان تدبيرك هذا»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p161/r6] verses 18:74, 18:77, 18:78 | The report continues with Khidr killing a boy and restoring a leaning wall after a village refused them food; Moses proposes taking wages, and Khidr declares their separation. «فقال موسى قوم أتيناهم فلم يطعمونا ولم يضيفونا لو شئت لاتخذت عليه اجرا»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p161/r7] verses 18:60, 18:61, 18:71, 18:74, 18:77 | Ibn al-Jawzi calls this report authentic and says it was recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim; he omits the chain here because he gave it in al-Ḥadāʾiq. «هذا حديث صحيح أخرجه البخاري ومسلم في الصحيحين وقد ذكرنا إسناده في كتاب الحدائق»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r1] verses 18:74 | Al-Mawardi says Joshua may have stayed behind, since only two travelers are reported, or he may have accompanied them but been omitted as subordinate to Moses. «يحتمل أن يوشع تأخر عنهما لأن الإخبار عن اثنين ويحتمل أن يكون معهما ولم يذكر»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r10] verses 18:74 | Abu Ubayda glosses zakiyya as purified. «والخامس أن الزكية المطهرة قاله أبو عبيدة»
