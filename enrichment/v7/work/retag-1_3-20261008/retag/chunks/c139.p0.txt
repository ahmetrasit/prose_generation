[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p474#3/r1] verses 67:3 | He glosses تفاوت as disturbance and difference. «{ما ترى في خلق الرحمن من تفاوت} أي اضطراب واختلاف.»
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p474#3/r2] verses 67:3 | He derives تفاوت from فوت: one thing escapes another, causing a flaw while the parts remain connected. «وهو: أن يفوت شيء شيئا، فيقع الخلل ولكنه متصل بعضه ببعض.»
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p474#3/r3] verses 67:3 | He glosses فطور as fissures, illustrated by a camel’s tooth splitting the flesh and appearing. «{هل ترى من فطور} ؟ أي من صدوع. ومنه يقال: فطر ناب البعير؛ إذا شق اللحم وظهر.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r1] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | Islahi adopts the muṣḥaf reading, finding no need to force an interpretation because context and Qur’anic parallels yield a clear reading; variant readings would revive doubts removed by Abū Bakr and ʿUmar. «At no place did I need to artificially adjust an interpretation; at all places a very clear and lucid interpretation in the light of the context and parallels of the Qur’ ān came to surface»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r2] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | He rejects identifying the seven aḥruf with seven readings; taking ḥarf as writing, discourse, or style makes the report refer to the Qur’an’s seven discernible groups. «if the word ḥarf (singular of aḥruf) is interpreted as “a writing, a discourse or a style,” which has full sanction from lexicons as well as usage»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r3] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | Each of the seven groups gathers the Qur’anic message under a central theme, varying style and argument so repetition stays fresh and new in flavor. «under a comprehensive central theme, all the basic meanings of the Qur’ ānic preaching are mentioned in various styles in such a manner that, in spite of repetition, a reader does not feel tired of the discourse.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r4] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | The groups together constitute the great Qur’an referenced in 15:87; the particle wāw there is explanatory. «the great Qur’ān, (15:87)) mentioned in Sūrah al-Ḥijr. The particle وas I have explained in the exegesis of the sūrah, is for explication.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r5] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | The sūrah’s central theme is warning about both worldly punishment for rejecting a messenger and punishment in the Hereafter. «The central theme of this sūrah is indhār (warning) and this includes indhār regarding both types of punishment: the punishment which the rejecters of a messenger of God face in this world, and the punishment they will have to face in the Hereafter.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r6] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | The warning is argued from signs in the world: observation reveals divine attributes that require the world to reach its culmination. «The arguments are drawn from the signs of God in the world around man. It has been told in this sūrah that, an observation of this world higlights those attributes of God which necessitate the fact that this world shall one day reach its culmination.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r7] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | Those enslaved to desire who fail to use reason go to Hell, while those who reason and fear God unseen merit great reward. «People who led their lives while being slaves to their desires and did not use their intellect will be cast into Hell, and those who used their intellect, and feared the Almighty even though they had not seen Him, will become worthy of great reward.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r8] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | Scrutiny of the universe shows that its sovereign is blessed and powerful; life and death have purpose, and the world is a test rather than an endless or abrupt process. «A close scrutiny of the universe reveals that its sovereign is a Blessed and Powerful Being. He has created life and death with a purpose and it is not that this world is to continue like this forever or suddenly end»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p401/r9] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | The world is a trial to distinguish those who take the right path from those who take the wrong one, requiring a Day when the righteous are rewarded and rebels punished. «adopts the right path and who the wrong one.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p402/r1] verses 67:1, 67:2, 67:3, 67:4, 67:5 | Repeated observation of the universe’s power and blessings reveals no flaw in its vast expanse. «not even a minor flaw can be pointed out in its great expanse. Repeated observations will only second this inference.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p402/r2] verses 67:3 | The nearest heaven is adorned with stars that guide travelers and repel Satan and his army when they try to overhear revelation. «The nearest of the seven skies has been adorned with stars by the Almighty; they serve the dual purpose of guiding people in their journeys and pelting Satan and his army if they try to overhear something revealed by God.»

[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p580/r4] verses 67:3 | Ṭibāqan means heaven upon heaven, fitted layer upon layer; grammatically it can be a verbal noun, an ellipsis for “having layers,” or a passive construction. «طباقا } مطابقة بعضها فوق بعض ، من طابق النعل»

[KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p580/r6] verses 67:3 | The no-discord clause qualifies the layered heavens; replacing “in them” with “in the creation of the All-Merciful” magnifies their creation and signals that their harmony comes from His power. «فوضع مكان الضمير قوله : { خلق الرحمان } تعظيما لخلقهن»
