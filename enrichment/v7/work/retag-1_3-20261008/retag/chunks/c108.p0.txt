- 25:71: وَمَن تَابَ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحًۭا فَإِنَّهُۥ يَتُوبُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ مَتَابًۭا
- 25:72: وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَشْهَدُونَ ٱلزُّورَ وَإِذَا مَرُّوا۟ بِٱللَّغْوِ مَرُّوا۟ كِرَامًۭا
- 25:73: وَٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِّرُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّهِمْ لَمْ يَخِرُّوا۟ عَلَيْهَا صُمًّۭا وَعُمْيَانًۭا
- 25:74: وَٱلَّذِينَ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا هَبْ لَنَا مِنْ أَزْوَٰجِنَا وَذُرِّيَّٰتِنَا قُرَّةَ أَعْيُنٍۢ وَٱجْعَلْنَا لِلْمُتَّقِينَ إِمَامًا

- 25:76: خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا ۚ حَسُنَتْ مُسْتَقَرًّۭا وَمُقَامًۭا
- 25:77: قُلْ مَا يَعْبَؤُا۟ بِكُمْ رَبِّى لَوْلَا دُعَآؤُكُمْ ۖ فَقَدْ كَذَّبْتُمْ فَسَوْفَ يَكُونُ لِزَامًۢا

# Notes

## 25:63
[MAWARDI-FULL:v4p154/r8] verses 25:63 | Their response may be to say peace be upon you. «قالوا وعليك السلام , قاله الضحاك»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v4p154/r9] verses 25:63 | Their response may be a request for peaceful conduct. «أنه طلب المسالمة , قاله ابن بحر»
[MUJAHID:v2p455#3/r1] verses 25:63 | Walking humbly on earth means walking with calm and solemnity. «عن مجاهد في قوله ﴿ يمشون على الأرض هونا ﴾ قال يعني بالسكينة والوقار»
[MUQATIL:25:61-66/r3] verses 25:63 | The servants of the Merciful walk gently and moderately; when ignorant people insult them over Islam, they answer kindly. «{ وعباد الرحمن الذين يمشون على الأرض هونا } يعنى حلما فى اقتصاد، { وإذا خاطبهم الجاهلون } يعنى السفهاء»
[NAHHAS:v3p166/r1] verses 25:63 | Al-Akhfash treats ‘servants’ as a subject with an omitted predicate, an analysis the grammarians found difficult. «حتى قال الأخفش هو مبتدأ بلا خبر يذهب إلى أنه محذوف»
[NAHHAS:v3p166/r2] verses 25:63 | Abū Isḥāq parses ‘servants’ as the subject, ‘those who walk humbly’ as its description, the next ‘those’ as coordinated, and ‘those will be rewarded’ as the predicate; al-Naḥḥās prefers this over al-Akhfash’s account. «قال عباد مرفوع بالابتداء و ( الذين يمشون على الأرض هونا ) من صفتهم»
[NAHHAS:v3p166/r3] verses 25:63 | Another possible parse makes ‘those who walk on the earth humbly’ itself the predicate of ‘servants.’ «قال ويجوز أن يكون الخبر ( الذين يمشون على الأرض )»
[NAHHAS:v3p166/r4] verses 25:63 | ‘Peace’ is a verbal noun; its meaning was explained earlier in the work. «( قالوا سلاما ) مصدر وقد ذكرنا معناه»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p175#3/r1] verses 25:63 | Servants of the Merciful is the subject whose predicate may be those who walk, or the phrase may describe those who are rewarded; this affiliation distinguishes and honors God's allies after His enemies were described. «مبتدأ خبره ﴿ الذين يمشون ﴾ أو أولئك يجزون والذين يمشون وما بعدهما صفة والإضافة إلى الرحمن للتخصيص والتفضيل وصف أولياءه بعدما وصف أعداءه»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p175#3/r2] verses 25:63 | Hawn can be a circumstantial expression or an adjective of walking, meaning gentle, calm, dignified, humble movement without play, swagger, or pride. «حال أو صفة للمشى أى هينين أو مشيا هينا»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p175#3/r3] verses 25:63 | They do not stamp their feet or strike their sandals in boastful display; some scholars therefore disliked riding through markets. «فلا يضربون بأقدامهم ولا يخفقون بنعالهم أشرا وبطرا ولذا كره بعض العلماء الركوب فى الأسواق»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p175#3/r4] verses 25:63 | When fools address them with what they dislike, they answer with sound words that spare them injury and slander. «أى السفهاء بما يكرهون ﴿ قالوا سلاما ﴾ سدادا من القول يسلمون فيه من الإيذاء والافك»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p175#3/r5] verses 25:63 | Another interpretation is that they take leave, leave the foolish people, and do not meet their foolishness with more foolishness. «أو تسلما منكم نتارككم ولا تجاهلكم فاقيم السلام مقام التسلم»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p175#3/r6] verses 25:63 | One view says the verse was abrogated by the fighting verse. «وقيل نسختها آية القتال»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p175#3/r7] verses 25:63 | Abrogation is unnecessary because overlooking fools is commendable in law and good manners. «ولا حاجة إلى ذلك فالاغضاء عن السفهاء مستحسن شرعا ومروءة»
[QASHANI:25:61-63/r4] verses 25:63 | The servants of the Merciful receive this name’s outpouring because of their broad readiness; inner tranquility restrains them from rash bodily movement. «المخصوصون بقبول فيض هذا الاسم لسعة الاستعداد»
[QASHANI:25:61-63/r5] verses 25:63 | When fools address them, they answer peacefully without confrontation: mercy and heart-light keep them beyond the ego’s insolence and injury. «يسلمون مقالهم ولا يعارضونهم لامتلائهم بالرحمة وبعد حالهم عن ظهور النفس»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r1] verses 25:63 | The verse links to the prohibition on walking arrogantly in 31:18. «ولا تمش في الأرض مرحا إن الله لا يحب كل مختال فخور»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r10] verses 25:63 | Some hypocrites who feigned religious adherence copied only the outward slow gait; a satirical couplet says they all walk slowly while hunting prey. «كلهم يمشي رويد كلهم يطلب صيد»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r11] verses 25:63 | In his own verse he contrasts humility amid high rank with inner purity, observing that people’s quietness often comes from arrogance. «تواضعت في العلياء والأصل كابر وحزت قصاب السبق بالهون في الأمر»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r2] verses 25:63 | He explains their walk as obedience, good conduct, and humility. «وقال ابن عباس: بالطاعة والمعروف والتواضع»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r3] verses 25:63 | They are forbearing: when ignorant people treat them ignorantly, they do not answer in kind. «الحسن حلماء إن جهل عليهم لم يجهلوا»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r4] verses 25:63 | Another explanation is that they do not behave arrogantly toward people. «وقيل: لا يتكبرون على الناس»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r5] verses 25:63 | He treats these interpretations as close and gathers them under knowledge of God, fear of Him, and awareness of His judgments and punishment. «قلت: وهذه كلها معان متقاربة، ويجمعها العلم بالله والخوف منه»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r6] verses 25:63 | One group attaches هَوْنًا directly to walking, taking the verse to describe a gentle gait alone. «وذهبت فرقة إلى أن { هونا } مرتبط بقوله: { يمشون على الأرض }»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r7] verses 25:63 | He allows that their character should match their gait, but rejects gait alone as the meaning: even a sly wolf may walk slowly. «ويشبه أن يتأول هذا على أن تكون أخلاق ذلك الماشي هونا مناسبة لمشيه»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r8] verses 25:63 | The Prophet himself walked with a purposeful sway, as if descending a slope, and was the foremost model for the community. «وقد كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يتكفأ في مشيه كأنما ينحط في صبب»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#2/r9] verses 25:63 | The instruction to walk slowly when pursuing an aim concerns the resolve within oneself, not gait alone. «من مشى منكم في طمع فليمش رويدا»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r1] verses 25:63 | He says سلاما is not a greeting of peace but a request for disengagement, meaning ‘I am clear of you’; grammatically it may be governed by قالوا or be a verbal noun, as Sibawayh holds. «قال النحاس: ليس { سلاما } من التسليم إنما هو من التسلم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r10] verses 25:63 | He says Sibawayh was wrong and expressed himself badly. «محمد بن يزيد: أخطأ سيبويه في هذا وأساء العبارة»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r11] verses 25:63 | He says Muslims were neither ordered nor forbidden to greet polytheists then; they were ordered to pardon and graciously avoid them, and the Prophet visited their gatherings without compromising. «ابن العربي: لم يؤمر المسلمون يومئذ أن يسلموا على المشركين ولا نهوا عن ذلك»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r12] verses 25:63 | He finds Ibn al-Arabi’s position more consistent with the Sunnah and rejects the need to claim abrogation; he notes scholarly disagreement about greeting unbelievers in Surat Maryam. «قلت: هذا القول أشبه بدلائل السنة»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r13] verses 25:63 | In al-Khalil’s anecdote, an Arab explains that Abu Rabi‘ah’s command استووا meant ‘go up’ to the roof. «وقد ذكر النضر بن شميل قال حدثني الخليل قال: أتيت أبا ربيعة الأعرابي»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r2] verses 25:63 | He allows سلاما to be an accusative verbal noun. «ويجوز أن يكون مصدرا وهذا قول سيبويه»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r3] verses 25:63 | He says قالوا governs سلاما because the sense is that they said this expression. «قال ابن عطية: والذي أقوله: أن { قالوا } هو العامل»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r4] verses 25:63 | He interprets سلاما as sound or right speech: they gently deflect an ignorant person with words. «وقال مجاهد: معنى { سلاما } سدادا»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r5] verses 25:63 | Another group says the person addressed should utter the actual word سلاما, meaning ‘we depart in peace’; a verb from the same root governs it. «وقالت فرقة: ينبغي للمخاطب أن يقول للجاهل سلاما بهذا اللفظ»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r6] verses 25:63 | Some say the Meccan verse was abrogated for unbelievers by the sword verse, while its etiquette remains for Muslims. «هذه الآية كانت قبل آية السيف»
[QURTUBI-FULL:25:63#3/r7] verses 25:63 | He argues Muslims were not then commanded to greet polytheists; سلاما means seeking safety and neutrality, with neither good nor evil between the groups. «قال سيبويه: لم يؤمر المسلمون يومئذ أن يسلموا على المشركين»
