# Notes

## 1:1
[RAZI-FULL:v1p168/r4] verses 1:1 | Since Muawiya was powerful, the Companions’ public protest shows that aloud basmala recitation was established among them. «كان كالأمر المتقرر عند كل الصحابة من المهاجرين والأنصار»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p168/r5] verses 1:1 | Al-Bayhaqi transmits Abu Hurayra’s report that the Prophet recited the basmala aloud, and reports the same practice from Umar, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, and Ibn al-Zubayr. «كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يجهر في الصلاة ببسم الله الرحمن الرحيم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p168/r6] verses 1:1 | Razi says Ali’s loud recitation is established by mass transmission; he cites the report asking God to keep truth with Ali wherever he turns. «كان يجهر بالتسمية فقد ثبت بالتواتر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r1] verses 1:1 | Conflicting and unstable Anas reports leave the evidence balanced; possible Umayyad pressure against Ali’s practice may explain Anas’s hesitation, and Ali’s lifelong practice should prevail. «عظم فيها الخبط والاضطراب فبقيت متعارضة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r2] verses 1:1 | If reports conflict, Razi prefers Ali, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, and Abu Hurayra over Anas and Ibn al-Mughaffal because they were more knowledgeable and closer to the Prophet. «كانوا اكثر علما وقربا من رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Razi invokes Abu Hanifa’s refusal of solitary reports that oppose analogy: the evident rational case favors openly reciting a phrase that honors God. «خبر الواحد إذا ورد على خلاف القياس لم يقبل»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r4] verses 1:1 | Ali, Ibn Abbas, and Ibn Umar were more learned and eminent than Anas and Ibn al-Mughaffal; their likely proximity, the Prophet’s moderate voice, and gradual increase in volume explain who heard the basmala. «كانوا أعلى حالا في العلم والشرف وعلو الدرجة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r5] verses 1:1 | Al-Shafi‘i interprets Anas’s phrase that the Prophet began with al-hamdu lillah as saying al-Fatiha, named by its opening, came before other suras. «أنه كان قدم هذه السورة في القراءة على غيرها من السور»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r6] verses 1:1 | Ibn al-Mughaffal’s denial of loud recitation can mean only that the Prophet did not raise his voice excessively, as instructed by the Qur’an; an affirmative report outweighs a negative one. «عدم المبالغة في رفع الصوت كما قال تعالى»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r7] verses 1:1 | The verses about inward remembrance concern simple recollection; reciting God’s revealed words as worship is a different act for which audible reading is preferable. «نحمل ذلك على مجرد الذكر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r8] verses 1:1 | The Shi‘a hold that the basmala should be recited aloud in both loud and quiet prayers; most jurists disagree. «السنة هي الجهر بالتسمية سواء كانت في الصلاة الجهرية أو السرية»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169#2/r9] verses 1:1 | Those denying that the basmala is part of each sura offer two explanations for its codex placement: it separates suras, or it is Qur’an as an independent verse revealed at each opening or once for insertion throughout. «اختلفوا في سبب إثباتها في المصحف في أول كل سورة وفيه قولان»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p169/r1] verses 1:1 | Abu Hamid al-Isfarayini groups six Anas reports: three support the Hanafi position by omitting the basmala, while three contrary reports recount the protest against Muawiya, loud recitation, or Anas’s uncertainty. «روي عن أنس في هذا الباب ست روايات أما الحنفية فقد رووا عنه ثلاث روايات»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r1] verses 1:1 | Umm Salama reports that the Prophet counted the basmala as a verse marking the separation between suras. «كان يعد بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم آية فاصلة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r2] verses 1:1 | Amr ibn Dinar rebukes al-Fadl al-Raqashi for claiming the basmala is not Qur’an, since it marks the end of one sura and start of another. «علم أن تلك السورة قد ختمت وفتح غيرها»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r3] verses 1:1 | Ibn al-Mubarak says omitting the basmala omits 113 verses; similar statements are attributed to Ibn Umar and Abu Hurayra. «فقد ترك مائة وثلاث عشرة آية»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r4] verses 1:1 | For those who do not count the basmala as a verse of al-Fatiha, Abu Hanifa and several other jurists require it quietly; Malik forbids it in obligatory prayer but permits a choice in voluntary prayer. «يقرأ التسمية سرا وقال مالك : لا ينبغي أن يقرأها في المكتوبة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r5] verses 1:1 | Al-Shafi‘i’s position entails reading the basmala in every rak‘a; Abu Hanifa is reported both to require it before al-Fatiha in every rak‘a and to require it only at the start of the prayer. «مذهب الشافعي يقتضي وجوب قراءتها في كل الركعات»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r6] verses 1:1 | Al-Shafi‘i prefers repeating the basmala before every sura, citing the report that an important matter begun without it is cut off. «كل أمر ذي بال لا يبدأ فيه ببسم الله فهو أبتر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r7] verses 1:1 | Razi’s school holds that a menstruating woman or a person in major ritual impurity may not recite the basmala. «والصحيح عندنا أنه لا يجوز»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p170/r8] verses 1:1 | Scholars agree that invoking God in ablution is recommended, and most do not require it because the ablution verse omits it. «تسمية الله على الوضوء مندوبة وعامة العلماء على أنها غير واجبة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r1] verses 1:1 | Literalists require the basmala in ablution, while Ishaq invalidates ablution for deliberate omission but excuses forgetfulness. «قال أهل الظاهر إنها واجبة فلو تركها عمدا أو سهوا»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r2] verses 1:1 | Whether meat from an animal slaughtered without the basmala is lawful is a famous dispute, with Qur’anic commands both to invoke God over offerings and not eat what was not named over. «متروك التسمية عند التذكية هل يحل أكله أم لا»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r3] verses 1:1 | Scholars recommend beginning every activity with the basmala, from sleep and waking to worship, entering and leaving home, eating, drinking, giving, and receiving; they also recommend it at birth, death, burial, resurrection, and the gathering. «يستحب أن لا يشرع في عمل من الأعمال وإلا ويقول بسم الله»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r4] verses 1:1 | The basmala at the gathering is said to distance the fire from the speaker by its blessing. «فتتباعد عنه النار ببركة قوله بسم الله»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r5] verses 1:1 | Razi rejects translated Qur’an as sufficient for prayer for both able and unable reciters; Abu Hanifa allows it for either, while Abu Yusuf and Muhammad allow it only for the unable. «ترجمة القرآن لا تكفي في صحة الصلاة لا في حق من يحسن القراءة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r6] verses 1:1 | Razi calls Abu Hanifa’s view far from sound; al-Samarqandi and al-Dabusi explicitly abandoned it. «مذهب أبي حنيفة في هذه المسألة بعيد جدا»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r7] verses 1:1 | The Prophet prayed using the revealed Arabic Qur’an throughout his life, so believers must follow that practice; Razi notes the irony that Abu Hanifa treats habitual head-wiping as a condition but not habitual Arabic recitation. «إنما صلى بالقرآن المنزل من عند الله تعالى باللفظ العربي وواظب عليه طول عمره»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p171/r8] verses 1:1 | The rightly guided caliphs prayed with Arabic Qur’an, so their practice binds believers under commands to follow Abu Bakr and Umar and the caliphs’ Sunna. «أن الخلفاء الراشدين صلوا بالقرآن العربي فوجب أن يجب علينا ذلك»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172#2/r1] verses 1:1 | Ibn al-Mundhir transmits Abu Hurayra’s report that prayer is invalid without reciting the Opening; a Persian rendering is either called Qur’an, which Razi deems ignorant and contrary to consensus, or not Qur’an. «لا تجزي صلاة لا يقرأ فيها بفاتحة الكتاب»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172#2/r2] verses 1:1 | When a man could not recite Qur’an, the Prophet taught him glorification, praise, and takbir instead of a translation, showing the alternative for inability. «إني لا أستطيع أن أحفظ القرآن كما يحسن في الصلاة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172#2/r3] verses 1:1 | If Qur’an could be translated as Qur’an, Christians could claim its opening was copied from the Gospel’s matching formula; no one makes that claim. «أول الإنجيل هو قوله بسم إلاها رحمانا ومرحيانا»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172#2/r4] verses 1:1 | A Persian rendering of the cave passage or insults and requests becomes ordinary human speech, barred in prayer; the Arabic original remains distinct through its miraculous arrangement. «من جنس كلام الناس لفظا ومعنى فوجب أن لا تجوز الصلاة به»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172/r1] verses 1:1 | The Prophet and Companions recited only Arabic in prayer; the saved group follows their practice. Muslims throughout Islamic lands agree on Arabic recitation, and departing from believers’ way is condemned. «وجميع أصحابه كانوا متفقين على القراءة في الصلاة بهذا القرآن العربي»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p172/r2] verses 1:1 | Prayer requires Qur’an recitation, and Persian words are not Qur’an: revelation is in clear Arabic, messengers speak their people’s language, and the challenge to produce its like would be contradicted if a Persian rendering counted. «إن الكلام المرتب بالفارسية ليس بقرآن لوجوه»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r1] verses 1:1 | If every language’s translation were Qur’an, revelation would have more than the seven canonical forms, contradicting the report that it came in seven forms. «أنزل القرآن على سبعة أحرف كلها شاف كاف»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r2] verses 1:1 | The Hanafi principle would also validate prayer with matching verses from Torah, Gospel, or ordinary writings, an outcome Razi says is alien to Islam. «الصلاة صحيحة بقراءة الإنجيل والتوراة وبقراءة زيد وإنسان»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p173/r3] verses 1:1 | A translation of al-Fatiha reduces it to generic praise found in any sermon; allowing this would make prayer valid with all sermons, an unacceptable result. «لا خطبة إلا وقد حصل فيها هذا القدر فوجب أن يقال الصلاة صحيحة»
