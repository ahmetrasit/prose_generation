[ALUSI-FULL:v15p191#3/r1] verses 17:110 | He says the ideal direct answer to the Jews would have been that Allah is preferred because it gathers every perfection, unlike Rahman; if أو means permission, the verse instead directly permits the polytheists to combine names. «إنما رجحنا الله على الرحمن في الذكر لأنه جامع لجميع صفات الكمال»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p191#3/r2] verses 17:110 | He argues that the fronted predicate فله الأسماء الحسنى means all names belong to God alone, answering the polytheist claim of multiple gods; since using both names is possible, he takes أو as permission rather than exclusive choice. «ومنع الأجوبية أيضا الجلبي بأن تقديم الخبر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p191#3/r3] verses 17:110 | He says any restriction focuses on the names' beauty rather than ownership, choice remains possible, and reason permits combining nonconflicting names; he sides with denying that the clause is the conditional answer and with reading أو as permission. «والظاهر أن الحق مع مانع الأجوبية والقائل بالإباحة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p191#3/r4] verses 17:110 | He reads الدعاء as calling or invoking; al-Zamakhshari instead reads it as naming, with an omitted object and perhaps an omitted ب. The author reports objections about duplicate meanings or coordinating one divine referent with itself, and counters that the wording may name the names and that their distinct concepts suffice. «والدعاء على ما اختاره أبو حيان وجماعة بمعنى النداء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p191#3/r5] verses 17:110 | He sees al-Zamakhshari's naming interpretation as favoring the polytheist occasion; if the verse addresses polytheists it names God, while on a Jewish occasion it invokes God. «وجعل الطيبي لذلك تفسير الزمخشري إياه بالتسمية مؤذنا بميله إلى أنه رد على المشركين»
