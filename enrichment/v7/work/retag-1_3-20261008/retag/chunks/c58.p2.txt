[RAZI-FULL:v15p14#2/r1] verses 7:155 | Ikhtāra derives from khayr, choosing the best; its original ikhtayara shifts yā to alif after a vowel, making active and passive mukhtār identical in form. «الاختيار : افتعال من لفظ الخير يقال : اختار الشيء إذا أخذ خيره وخياره ، وأصل اختار : اختير»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p14#2/r2] verses 7:155 | Al-Razi argues voluntary action depends on judging it better than its alternative: neutral capacities cannot select one side without a preference, and judging one side beneficial creates that preference. «وما دام يبقى على هذا الاستواء امتنع أن يصير مصدرا لأحد الجانبين دون الثاني ، وإلا لزم رجحان الممكن من غير مرجح»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p14#2/r3] verses 7:155 | The objection that people knowingly commit suicide is answered by saying they expect death to remove a worse harm; the lesser harm is good relative to the greater one. «إن الإنسان لا يقدم على قتل نفسه إلا إذا اعتقد أنه بسبب ذلك القتل يتخلص عن ضرر أعظم من ذلك القتل»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p14#2/r4] verses 7:155 | A group of grammarians reads the verse as Moses choosing seventy from among his people, with min omitted and the verb directly taking the object. «قال جماعة النحويين : معناه واختار موسى من قومه سبعين . فحذفت كلمة ( من ) ووصل الفعل»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p14#2/r5] verses 7:155 | Abu Ali explains that a verb may take its second object through a preposition, then expand by omitting that preposition, as with choosing men and asking forgiveness for a sin. «قال أبو علي والأصل في هذا الباب أن من الأفعال ما يتعدى إلى المفعول الثاني بحرف واحد ، ثم يتسع فيحذف حرف الجر»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p14#2/r6] verses 7:155 | Al-Farazdaq’s verse uses ikhtāra al-rijāla directly to show the construction with min omitted. «وأنشدوا قول الفرزدق : ومنا الذي اختار الرجال سماحة … وجودا إذا هب الرياح الزعازع»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p15/r1] verses 7:155 | He notes that Arabic permits commanding someone to do good with either a bi-marked complement or a direct object; an unnamed poet witnesses the direct construction. «أمرت زيدا بالخير وأمرت زيدا الخير قال الشاعر :»

[RAZI-FULL:v15p15/r3] verses 7:155 | One account says Moses selected twelve tribes, six from each, totaling seventy-two; two stayed after a dispute when Moses promised them the same reward, and Caleb and Joshua remained. «ذكروا أن موسى عليه السلام اختار من قومه اثني عشر سبطا من كل سبط ستة»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p15/r4] verses 7:155 | Another account says Moses found only sixty elders, so God directed him to choose ten young men who became elders; he ordered fasting, purification, and clean clothes before the rendezvous. «وروى أنه لم يجد إلا ستين شيخا ، فأوحى الله إليه أن يختار من الشبان عشرة»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p15/r5] verses 7:155 | The first interpretation identifies this as the rendezvous for speech and the request to see God: the seventy went to Sinai, heard God address Moses, then requested a vision and were struck by the thunderbolt called the quake here. «إنه عليه السلام خرج بهؤلاء السبعين إلى طور سيناء»
