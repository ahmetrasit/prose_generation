# Notes

## 1:1
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r1] verses 1:1 | Medina, Basra, and Syria's readers and jurists, with Abu Hanifa, hold that the basmala is not a verse of al-Fatiha or other surahs; it separates surahs and is blessed at the opening, so they do not recite it aloud. «هو مذهب أبي حنيفة ومن تابعه رحمهم الله ولذا لا يجهر بها عندهم في الصلاة»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r10] verses 1:1 | The ba is kasra-marked because it is invariably a preposition governing genitive; the noun's initial hamza is added only when it begins speech, while medial pronunciation drops it. «وبنيت الباء على الكسر لأنها تلازم الحرفية والجر فكسرت لتشابه حركتها عملها»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r11] verses 1:1 | The noun ism has a prosthetic hamza for initial pronunciation, can be shortened to sim, and derives from deleted-final form samw, “elevation”: naming raises and publicizes the named one's mention. «واشتقاقه من السمو وهو الرفعة لأن التسمية تنويه بالمسمى وإشادة بذكره»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r12] verses 1:1 | Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz instructed his scribe to lengthen the ba, make the sin strokes clear, and round the mim; the written alif is omitted here through frequent use. «وقال عمر بن عبد العزيز لكاتبه طول الباء واظهر السينات ودور الميم»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r13] verses 1:1 | Allah is uniquely the name of the true God, not an attribute; the source derives it from al-ilah with hamza loss, while Khalil, al-Zajjaj, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan, and al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl deny derivation and other accounts link it to bewilderment or worship. «والله أصله الإله ونظيره الناس أصله الأناس حذفت الهمزة وعوض منها حرف التعريف»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r2] verses 1:1 | Meccan and Kufan readers, with al-Shafi'i and his companions, count it as a verse of al-Fatiha and every surah and recite it aloud; they cite its inclusion in the early codices despite the instruction to exclude non-Qur'anic material. «وقراء مكة والكوفة على أنها آية من الفاتحة ومن كل سورة وعليه الشافعى وأصحابه»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Ibn Abbas says that whoever omits the basmala has omitted 114 verses from God's Book. «وعن ابن عباس رضى الله عنهما من تركها فقد ترك مائة وأربع عشرة آية من كتاب الله»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r4] verses 1:1 | A Prophetic report presents al-Fatiha as divided between God and His servant: God answers the praise, glorification, and magnification, shares the response to worship and aid, and grants the servant's request in the closing supplication. «حديث أبي هريرة قال سمعت النبي عليه السلام يقول قال الله تعالى قسمت الصلاة أي الفاتحة بينى وبين عبدى نصفين»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r5] verses 1:1, 1:2 | The report begins God's response when the servant says “Praise belongs to God,” which indicates the basmala is not part of al-Fatiha; if it is outside al-Fatiha, consensus excludes it from other surahs too. «فالابتداء بقوله الحمد لله دليل على أن التسمية ليست من الفاتحة»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r6] verses 1:1 | In the Hanafi account the basmala remains a Qur'anic verse revealed to separate surahs; this does not contradict its exclusion from the surahs themselves. «التسمية آية من القرآن انزلت للفصل بين السور عندنا ذكره فخر الإسلام في المبسوط»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r7] verses 1:1 | The initial ba attaches to an omitted verb such as “I read” or “I recite”; the act named is understood from the basmala that precedes it, as in a traveler saying it on departure or arrival. «وتعلقت الباء بمحذوف تقديره بسم الله اقرأ أو أتلو لأن الذى يتلو التسمية مقروء»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r8] verses 1:1 | The omitted verb follows the basmala because the act and its object matter most; placing the divine name first gives it exclusive priority over the polytheists' invocations of al-Lat and al-Uzza. «وكانوا يبدءون بأسماء آلهتم فيقولون باسم اللات وباسم العزى»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p4#2/r9] verses 1:1 | In “Read in the name of your Lord,” the verb is brought forward because reading was the central command of the first-revealed surah; “read” may also mean perform reading absolutely, or take the following phrase as its object. «وكان الأمر بالقراءة اهم فكان تقديم الفعل اوقع ويجوز أن يحمل اقرأ على معنى افعل القراءة»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r1] verses 1:1 | The lām of Allah is generally emphatic after fatḥa or ḍamma and light after kasra; some readers lighten it in every position, others always emphasize it, while the first pattern is the majority practice. «وتفخم لامه إذا كان قبلها فتحة أو ضمة وترقق إذا كان قبلها كسرة ومنهم من يرققها بكل حال»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r2] verses 1:1 | Rahman and Rahim are both derived from mercy, but Rahman is more emphatic because it has two augmentative patterns versus one in Rahim; added form signals added meaning. «وفي الرحمن من المبالغة ما ليس في الرحيم لأن في الرحيم زيادة واحدة وفى الرحمن زيادتين وزيادة اللفظ تدل على زيادة المعنى»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r3] verses 1:1 | Rahman describes mercy encompassing all creation, believer and unbeliever, especially in this world; Rahim describes mercy particular to believers in the hereafter, explaining the supplicatory usage and the order of the names. «ولذا جاء في الدعاء يارحمن الدنيا لأنه يعم المؤمن والكافر ورحيم الآخرة لأنه يخص المؤمن»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r4] verses 1:1 | Rahman is restricted as a name to God but universal in meaning, while Rahim can describe others and is particular in its referent; Rahman is nevertheless placed first despite its greater force. «وقالوا الرحمن خاص تسمية لأنه لا يوصف به غيره»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r5] verses 1:1 | A hostile poet applied “Rahman” to Musaylima as a taunt in unbelief, not as a genuine divine description. «وأما قول الشاعر في مسيلمة وأنت غيث الورى لا زلت رحمانا فباب من تعنتهم في كفرهم»
[NASAFI-FULL:v1p6/r6] verses 1:1 | Grammarians differ on whether Rahman declines: those requiring the absence of a faʿlān feminine form leave it indeclinable, while those requiring a faʿlā feminine form decline it; the first analysis is preferable. «ورحمن غير منصرف عند من زعم أن الشرط انتفاء فعلانه إذ ليس له فعلانة»
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r1] verses 1:1 | The nineteenth letter signifies the human world as a distinct realm because humanity’s nobility and comprehensiveness gather the whole cosmos, as Gabriel is distinguished among angels. «والتسعة عشر إشارة إليها مع العالم الإنساني، فإنه وإن كان داخلا»
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r2] verses 1:1 | Three hidden alifs complete the twenty-two letters and signify the divine world in respect of essence, attributes, and acts: three in detailed consideration, one in realization. «والألفات الثلاثة المحتجبة التي هي تتمة الاثنين والعشرين عند الانفصال»
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r3] verses 1:1 | The three written alifs signify those worlds appearing in the supreme human manifestation while the divine world remains veiled. «والثلاثة المكتوبة إشارة إلى ظهور تلك العوالم على المظهر الأعظمي الإنساني ولاحتجاب العالم الإلهي»
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r4] verses 1:1 | Asked where the alif of the basmala went, the Messenger replies that Satan stole it; he orders the bāʾ lengthened in its place. «سئل رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن ألف الباء من أين ذهبت؟ قال صلى الله عليه وسلم: " سرقها الشيطان "»
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r5] verses 1:1 | Lengthening bāʾ compensates for the hidden alif: divinity is concealed in diffusive mercy and appears in human form, knowable only to its people, hence the indefinite form. «وأمر بتطويل باء بسم الله تعويضا عن ألفها إشارة إلى احتجاب ألوهية الإلهية في صورة الرحمة الانتشارية»
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r6] verses 1:1 | A hadith says God created Adam in His image; the author explains a hierarchy of veils from essence by attributes, from attributes by acts, and from acts by beings and effects. «إن الله تعالى خلق آدم على صورته»
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r7] verses 1:1 | Disclosure of acts beyond the veil of created effects yields trust; disclosure of attributes beyond acts yields contentment and surrender; disclosure of essence beyond attributes yields annihilation in unity and absolute unification. «فمن تجلت عليه الأفعال بارتفاع حجب الأكوان توكل، ومن تجلت عليه الصفات بارتفاع حجب الأفعال رضي وسلم»
[QASHANI:1:1#2/r8] verses 1:1 | Unification of acts precedes unification of attributes, which precedes unification of essence; the three are reflected in the Prophet’s prostration supplication seeking refuge in pardon, pleasure, and God Himself. «فتوحيد الأفعال مقدم على توحيد الصفات وهو على توحيد الذات وإلى الثلاثة أشار صلوات الله عليه في سجوده»
[QASHANI:1:1/r1] verses 1:1 | Names are the determinate forms by which divine attributes and essence are known: their identities signify attributes and essence, existence signifies His face, and their individuation His unity. «اسم الشيء ما يعرف به»
[QASHANI:1:1/r2] verses 1:1 | Allah names the divine essence absolutely, without reference either to its being qualified by attributes or to its lack of attributes. «اسم للذات الإلهية من حيث هي هي على الإطلاق»
[QASHANI:1:1/r3] verses 1:1 | The All-Merciful grants existence and perfection universally according to wisdom and each recipient’s capacity at the beginning. «هو المفيض للوجود والكمال على الكل بحسب ما تقتضي الحكمة»
[QASHANI:1:1/r4] verses 1:1 | The Especially Merciful grants the human kind its particular spiritual perfection at the end; the perfected human form gathers universal and special mercy and manifests the divine essence and all attributes. «هو المفيض للكمال المعنوي المخصوص بالنوع الإنساني بحسب النهاية»
[QASHANI:1:1/r5] verses 1:1 | The Prophet says he was given comprehensive speech and sent to complete noble character; existent things are realities, while noble character’s states and sources of action are gathered in the universal human being. «أوتيت جوامع الكلم، وبعثت لأتمم مكارم الأخلاق»
