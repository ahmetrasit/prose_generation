 verses 1:1 | Scholars have three views on whether a follower must recite: that the follower must recite the Fatiha just like the imam. «أحدها : أنه تجب عليه قراءتها ، كما تجب على إمامه»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r53] verses 1:1 | A second view says a follower recites nothing, whether prayer is audible or silent, based on “one with an imam has the imam's recitation”; the marfuʿ routes are weak, and Malik transmits it as Jabir's own statement. «من كان له إمام فقراءة الإمام له قراءة » ولكن في إسناده ضعف»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r54] verses 1:1 | A third view requires the follower's recitation in silent prayer but not audible prayer, combining the general requirement with the command to listen when the imam recites. «أنه تجب القراءة على المأموم في السرية»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r55] verses 1:1 | Al-Bazzar reports that reciting the Fatiha and Surat al-Ikhlas before sleep protects from everything except death. «فقد أمنت من كل شيء إلا الموت»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r6] verses 1:1 | Al-Hamd is another name for the surah. «ويقال لها : الحمد»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r7] verses 1:1 | It is also called al-Salah because the divine hadith names recitation of al-Hamd as the servant's praise, and the surah is a condition of prayer. «فسميت الفاتحة : صلاة ؛ لأنها شرط فيها»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r8] verses 1:1 | A report from Abu Saʿid calls the Fatiha a healing from every poison. «فاتحة الكتاب شفاء من كل سم»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r9] verses 1:1 | The surah is called a ruqya because the Prophet affirmed its use in healing a stung man and asked how the companion knew it was a ruqya. «فقال له رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم : «وما يدريك أنها رقية»
[IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB:v1p38/r1] verses 1:1 | The basmala is an ellipsis meaning “I begin in the name of God” or “I began in the name of God.” «{بسم الله} اختصار كأنه قال: أبدأ باسم الله. أو بدأت باسم الله.»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r1] verses 1:1 | The name al-mathānī is explained by the Fātiḥa having been revealed twice. «لأنها نزلت مرتين»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r10] verses 1:1, 1:3 | Al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm derive from mercy, whose basis is benefaction to the needy; both are intensive forms, with faʿlān more emphatic than faʿīl because it departs further from the base pattern. «والرحمن الرحيم: اسمان مشتقان من الرحمة، وأصل الرحمة: النعمة»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r2] verses 1:1 | Ubayy reports that the Prophet called the Fātiḥa the unmatched Mother of the Qur’an and seven oft-repeated verses, divided between God and servant, with the servant receiving what he asks. «وعن أبى بن كعب، قال: قرأت على رسول الله - صلى الله عليه وسلم - فاتحة الكتاب»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r3] verses 1:1 | Ism derives from sumu, elevation, rather than simah, mark: its plural is asmāʾ, its diminutive sumayy, and words losing an initial radical do not take hamzat al-waṣl in this way. «الأول أصح؛ لأنهم جمعوه على أسماء، وقالوا في تصغيره سمي»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r4] verses 1:1 | The proposed root of ism is elevation (also linked with samāʾ), a mark, or the verb meaning to rise and become manifest as a sign of the underlying meaning. «وقيل: من السمة، والأول أصح»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r5] verses 1:1 | Ism can weigh fiʿl with either ḍamma or kasra, but not faʿl with fatḥa, since its regular plural would be afʿul rather than afʿāl. «يجوز فعل، وفعل بضم الفاء وكسرها، ولا يجوز فعل بفتح الفاء»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r6] verses 1:1 | One derivation takes Allah from ilāh, dropping the hamza and making the alif-lām an obligatory substitute so the name becomes a proper name. «هذا مذهب سيبويه، وقيل: أصله لاه، ألحقت بها الألف واللام»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r7] verses 1:1 | Al-Khalīl and Abū ʿAlī regard Allah as an un-derived name; not every word must be derived, or derivation would regress without end. «هذا مذهب الخليل وأبي علي. وقيل: إنه مشتق»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r8] verses 1:1 | A group including al-Naḍr b. Shumayl derives Allah from taʾalluh, worship; Ibn ʿAbbās read ilāhatak as your worship, and a poetic line uses taʾalluh for devotion. «وقرأ ابن عباس: وإلهتك، أي عبادتك، قال الشاعر: سبحن واسترجعن من تأله»
[JISHUMI:v1p200/r9] verses 1:1 | Other derivations connect Allah with seeking refuge in someone, bewilderment (Abū ʿAmr), finding repose with someone (al-Mubarrad), or concealment from lāha. «وقيل: هو مشتق من الوله، وهو التحير، يقال: أله يأله، إذا تحير عن أبي عمرو»
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r1] verses 1:1 | Abū ʿAmr explains Allah as the one whose essential greatness leaves minds bewildered. «معناه: أنه تتحير العقول في كنه عظمته، كما يقال للمكتوب: كتاب، عن أبي عمرو بن العلاء»
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r10] verses 1:1, 1:3 | A further distinction assigns al-Raḥmān to creation and al-Raḥīm to provision. «وقيل: الرحمن بالخلق، والرحيم بالرزق»
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r11] verses 1:1, 1:3 | The pairing intensifies the attribution of mercy and teaches that all blessings come from God. «للمبالغة بصفته بالرحمة؛ ليعلم أن النعم كلها منه»
[JISHUMI:v1p203/r12] verses 1:1, 1:3 | Because Arabs commonly knew Allah but not al-Raḥmān, the names are paired to show that Allah, al-Raḥmān, and al-Raḥīm all name and describe Him. «لأن العرب كثير في لغتهم لفظ، (الله)، ولم يعرفوا الرحمن، فجمع بينهما»
