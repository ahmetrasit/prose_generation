[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r8] verses 1:1 | The great commentators say ba here means either attachment and accompaniment or seeking aid; the former makes the basmala circumstantial, the latter an indirect object. «E‘âzım-ı müfessirîn diyorlar ki: “Bâ”nın buradaki ma‘nâ-yı ilsâkı»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r9] verses 1:1 | Abduh says customary in the name of means acting for an authority, by its command and on its behalf; the Muslim's basmala can express beginning an act for Allah and by His command. «Şeyh Abduh merhum Fâtiha tefsîrinde zikre şâyan bir mâna daha kaydetmiştir»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r1] verses 1:1 | The basmala in 27:30 is plainly part of that verse and certainly Qurʾān by explicit, unanimously established transmission; surah-opening basmalas raise a separate question. «Sûre-i Neml’in bu âyetinin bir cüz’ü olduğu kemâl-i sarâhatle mâlumdur»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r10] verses 1:1 | The Shāfiʿī argument concludes that even if the basmala is not a whole verse of al-Fātiḥa, it is part of a verse, making its recitation obligatory and audible in prayer. «Fâtiha’dan bir âyet değilse cüz’-i âyettir. Ve binâen‘aleyh namazda kıraati farzdır ve cehr olunur»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r11] verses 1:1 | Like al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal has two transmitted positions, reflecting uncertainty between the two hadith reports. «Ahmed b. Hanbel hazretlerinden de bu iki hadis beyninde tereddüdle iki rivayet mevcuddur»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r12] verses 1:1 | Mālik holds that opening basmalas are neither part of al-Fātiḥa or other surahs nor a separate Qurʾānic portion; they are written for separation and blessing and are not recited in prayer. «besmelelerin ne Fâtiha ve ne sâir sûrelerden, ne de bütün Kur’an’dan bir cüz’-i mahsus olmadığına»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r13] verses 1:1 | Mālik bases his view on the need for Qurʾānic status to be explicit and mutawātir at every locus and on the practice of Medina’s people. «ehl-i Medine’nin teâmülüne istinad ederek sûre evvellerindeki besmelelerin»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r14] verses 1:1 | The sound Ḥanafī position reconciles the other arguments: each opening basmala is an independent Qurʾānic verse, revealed to separate surahs and for blessing, and belongs to none of them. «Evâil-i süverdeki besmele başlı başına bir âyet-i münferide olarak Kur’an’dandır»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r15] verses 1:1 | Because the basmala is independent rather than part of al-Fātiḥa, reciting it in every prayer is not obligatory like al-Fātiḥa, though beginning each recitation and important undertaking with it is sunnah. «her kıraatin ve her emr-i mühimmin bidâyetinde okunması sünnettir»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r16] verses 1:1 | Ḥanafīs say it at the start of each prayer unit’s recitation, not in the middle, and recite it silently even in audible prayers so it will not appear part of the surah. «namazın her rekatında, kıraatin evvelinde okuruz, ortasında okumayız»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r17] verses 1:1 | Outwardly the basmala has four words, but in reality and legal reckoning it has seven because bāʾ and the two definite articles count as words, while bāʾ also implies a verb and its agent. «Besmele zâhirde بِسْمِ، اللّٰهِّٰ، الرَّحْمٰنِ، الرَّح۪يمِ dört kelimedir. Hakikaten ve hükmen ise yedi kelimedir»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r18] verses 1:1 | In the basmala’s special orthography the alif of ism is omitted and the opening bāʾ is lengthened; frequent use explains this abbreviation, while the writing also hints at the force of connection. «besmeleye mahsus olarak hemze hazf olunup söylendiği gibi yazılır ve ‘ivaz olarak ب’nin başı uzatılır»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r19] verses 1:1 | Certain hadith allusions read the letters bāʾ, sīn, and mīm as signs of God’s splendor, radiance, and sovereignty. «بَاء بَهَاءُ اللّٰهِّٰ، سِين سَنَاءُ اللّٰهِّٰ، مِيم مَمْلَكَةُ اللّٰهِّٰ gibi telmihler»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r2] verses 1:1 | Whether the basmala written before surahs is a verse within them, a verse-part, or an independent Qurʾānic unit is a scholarly issue tied to faith, prayer, and recitation. «bu besmelenin ta‘yîn-i Kur’ânîsi tefsirde ve ilm-i usûlde bir mes’ele-i ilmiye teşkil etmiştir»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r20] verses 1:1 | The Qurʾānic wisdom concerns the relation of God to the world, especially people and their acts; the basmala’s bāʾ expresses this relation of divinity and servitude. «alâkasında hulâsa olunan ve evvelen Fâtiha’da, sâniyen bütün Kur’ân’da ‘ale’t-tedrîc tafsil edilen bu nisbet tamamen besmeledeki»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r21] verses 1:1 | The basic meaning of bāʾ is attachment, with senses such as accompaniment, assistance, connection, and oath; commentators read it here as accompaniment or assistance, with an omitted verb meaning begin, read, or the like. «besmelede müfessirîn yalnız musâhabet veya isti‘âne mânalarından birini gösterirler»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r3] verses 1:1 | These early authorities hold that every surah-opening basmala is one verse of that surah, yielding 113 such verses in the Qurʾān. «Saîd b. Cübeyr, Zührî, Atâ, İbn Mübârek hazerâtı besmelenin başında bulunduğu her sûreden birer âyet»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r4] verses 1:1 | In his later position al-Shāfiʿī and his followers adopt the view that each surah-opening basmala is a verse. «Kavl-i cedîdinde İmam Şâfiî hazretleri ve ashâbı bu mezheb üzerindedirler»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r5] verses 1:1 | On this view the basmala is al-Fātiḥa’s first verse, while anʿamta ʿalayhim is not a verse ending, so they recite the basmala aloud in prayer. «O hâlde Fâtiha’nın yedi âyetinden birincisi besmeledir»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r6] verses 1:1 | Their argument is that the early community wrote the basmalas in the muṣḥafs while preserving the Qurʾān’s purity and excluding even Āmīn. «selef bu besmeleleri mushaflarda tesbit etmişler, bununla beraber Kur’ân’ın tecrîdini tavsiye eylemişler»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r7] verses 1:1 | A report from Ibn ʿAbbās says that omitting the basmala omits 114 verses from God’s Book. «Besmele’yi terk eden Kitâbullah’dan yüz on dört âyet terk etmiş olur»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r8] verses 1:1 | A report from Abū Hurayra says the Prophet counted al-Fātiḥa as seven verses and named the basmala as its first. «Fâtihatü’l-Kitâb yedi âyettir, bunların evveli بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ»
[ELMALILI:1:1/r9] verses 1:1 | A report from Umm Salama says the Prophet counted the basmala together with al-ḥamdu lillāh as one verse. «Resûlullah Fâtiha’yı okudu ve بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ’i bir âyet saydı»
[FARRA:v1p1#2/r1] verses 1:1 | Reciters and codex scribes omit the alif in the familiar basmala because its meaning is obvious and Arabic usage favors brevity in repeated, familiar expressions. «اجتماع القراء وكتاب المصاحف على حذف الألف من «بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم»»
[FARRA:v1p1#2/r2] verses 1:1 | They retain the alif in “fa-sabbih bi-ismi rabbika” because this name is not used with the preposition as frequently as Allah’s name. «قوله: «فسبح باسم ربك» لأنها لا تلزم هذا الاسم، ولا تكثر معه ككثرتها مع الله»
[FARRA:v1p1#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Do not omit the alif of ism with a name other than Allah or with a preposition other than bi; lām and kāf do not follow this spelling convention. «إلى علم ذلك. فلا تحذفن ألف «اسم» إذا أضفته إلى غير الله»
[FARRA:v1p1#2/r4] verses 1:1 | Arabic speakers shorten frequent expressions even more, as in “aysh ʿindak” and the altered form of shayʾ. «قولهم: أيش عندك فحذفوا إعراب «أي» وإحدى ياءيه، وحذفت الهمزة من «شيء»»
[FARRA:v1p1#2/r5] verses 1:1 | The claim that alif was dropped because bāʾ cannot be paused on is disproved by other non-pauseable letters that Arabs still write in similar forms. «فقد كتبت العرب في المصاحف واضرب لهم مثلا بالألف والواو لا يسكت عليها في كثير من أشباهه.»

[IBNABIHATIM:v1p25/r2] verses 1:1 | Al-Dahhak assigns the letters of bism to God's splendor, sublime glory, and dominion, and glosses Allah as God of creation. «عن الضحاك في قوله : بسم الله قال : الباء من بهاء الله والسين من سناء الله والميم من ملك الله»
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p25/r3] verses 1:1 | Jabir ibn Zayd says Allah is the Greatest Name because every Qur'anic sura begins with it before any other name. «عن أبي الشعثاء جابر بن زيد في قوله : بسم الله قال : اسم الله الاعظم هو الله»
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p25/r4] verses 1:1 | Ibn Abbas explains al-Rahman as the Arabic intensive form of mercy and says it is an Arabic expression. «عن الضحاك عن ابن عباس قال : اول ما انزل جبريل على محمد - صلى الله عليه وسلم - قال له جبريل قل يا محمد»
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p25/r5] verses 1:1 | The Prophet calls the basmala one of God's names and compares its nearness to Allah's name with the closeness of the eye's black and white. «عن طاووس ، عن ابن عباس ان عثمان بن عفان سال رسول الله - صلى الله عليه وسلم - عن بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم فقال : هو اسم من اسماء الله»
[IBNABIHATIM:v1p25/r6] verses 1:1 | Ibn Abbas glosses al-Rahim as gentle and caring toward those God wills to show mercy, and severe toward those He wills to chastise. «عن الضحاك عن ابن عباس قال : اول ما نزل جبريل على النبي - صلى الله عليه وسلم قال له جبريل : قل»
