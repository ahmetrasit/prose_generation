[BIQAI-FULL:v1p27/r2] verses 1:1 | Al-Harali divides the three domains of well-being into action and restraint, yielding six letters; God then grants Muhammad a seventh, inclusive letter, so the Qur'an descends in seven letters. «فلما استوفى الستة وهبه ربه حرفا جامعا سابعا فردا لا زوج له ن فتم إنزاله على سبعة أحرف .»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p27/r3] verses 1:1 | The worldly pair is halal and haram: lawful things cultivate body and soul, forbidden things must be purified away; the pair originates in the Torah and is completed in the Qur'an. «فأدنى تلك الحروف هو حرف إصلاح الدنيا ، فلها حرفان ، أحدهما : حرف الحرام»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p27/r4] verses 1:1 | The afterlife pair is warning and command; avoiding prohibitions purifies and obeying commands advances the next life, even when it sacrifices temporary worldly benefits for enduring whole good. «ثم يلي هذين حرفا صلاح المعاد : أحدهما حرف الزجر والنهي التي لا تصلح الآخرة إلا بالتطهير منه»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p27/r5] verses 1:1 | The religious pair is clear guidance about heart, character and bodily duties, and ambiguous matters beyond limited reason that require declaring God incomparable; five letters are for practice and the sixth for restraint. «ثم يلي هذين حرفا صلاح الدين : أحدهما حرف المحكم الذي بان للعبد فيه خطاب ربه»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p27/r6] verses 1:1 | The six letters are shared with prior scriptures but perfected in the Qur'an; its unique seventh is the inclusive letter of praise, gifted directly to Muhammad and embodied in his heart, recitation and character. «فهذه الحروف الستة يشترك فيها القرآن مع سائر الكتب ويزيد عليها تمامها وبركة جمعها ، ويختص القرآن بالحرف السابع الجامع»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p28#s002/r1] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7, 2:1 | Al-Harali maps al-Fatiha's verses to the seven letters: praise, lawful and forbidden, command and prohibition, clear and ambiguous, then the corresponding petitions; its opening gathers all seven. «فالآية الأولى تشتمل على حرف الحمد السابع»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p28/r1] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | Because praise is the seventh inclusive letter, the Fatiha begins with it and gathers the seven universals diffused through the Qur'an, as the Qur'an gathers earlier scriptures. «ولما كان حرف الحمد هو سابعها الجامع افتتح الله به سبحانه وتعالى الفاتحة أم القرآن وأم الكتاب»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p28/r2] verses 1:1, 1:2 | The first verse contains praise's seventh letter; the second contains the lawful and forbidden letters that Rahmaniyyah establishes for worldly life, allowing enjoyment and removing either prohibition's burden or sin. «فالآية الأولى تشتمل على حرف الحمد السابع ، والثانية تشتمل على حرفي الحلال والحرام»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p28/r7] verses 1:1 | The Qur'an begins with the sixth, ambiguous letter so confessing ignorance and believing without grasping it invites divine gift; this trains compliance with the rest without claiming mastery. «ولما ابتئت الفاتحة أم القرآن بالسابع الجامع الموهوب ابتدئ القرآن بالحرف السادس المعجوز عنه»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p28/r8] verses 1:1 | After the ambiguous opening, the Qur'an presents the clear letter in the traits of believers; observance from faith is clear guidance, while external compliance without faith remains mere command. «ثم ولى السادس المفتتح به القرآن الخامس المحكم كم وجه في قوله سبحانه وتعالى»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p29/r1] verses 1:1 | The Qur'an's recited order differs from revelation order: the first revelation to the Prophet was the clear command to read, while the first public warning was the command to rise and warn of the afterlife. «وأما تنزيله يفي ترتيب البيان فإن أ , ل ما نزل على النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم هو حرف المحكم»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p29/r2] verses 1:1 | The warning came first for the community because afterlife reform is most important and also sets worldly affairs right; the Prophet rendered warning and prohibition with one sense, though the first addresses instinct and the second understanding. «فابتدأ سبحانه وتعالى ترتيل الأمة بإصلاح المعاد الأهم لأن عليه يصلح أمر الدنيا»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p29/r3] verses 1:1 | Al-Qadi Abu Bakr argues that the disconnected letters challenge opponents to use the remaining half of the alphabet to produce a rival discourse; al-Zarkashi transmits the claim. «نقل ذلك الزركشي في البرهان عن القاضي أبي بكر قال : وقد علم ذلك بعض أرباب الحقائق»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p29/r4] verses 1:1 | Abu Bakr says every book has an essence and the Qur'an's essence lies in the opening letters of its surahs; Ali likewise calls the alphabetic openings the Qur'an's selected core. «وعن أبي بكر رضي الله تعالى عنه : في كل كتا بسر وسر الله في القرآن أوائل السور»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p29/r5] verses 1:1 | Ibn al-Qayyim reads Alif-Lam-Mim as the three articulation zones in order, symbolizing creation, worldly life and its laws, and resurrection; the Qur'an gathers what all humanity needs. «وأبدى الإمام شمس الدين ابن فقيم الجوزيه الدمشقي الحنبلي يفي كتاب به كالتذكرة»
