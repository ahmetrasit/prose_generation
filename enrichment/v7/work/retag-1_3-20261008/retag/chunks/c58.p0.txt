# Notes

## 7:155
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r1] verses 7:155 | An interpretation paraphrases the trial as God destroying whom He wills and rescuing whom He wills; Ibn Abbas is cited for the wording “whom He wills.” «وقيل: تهلك من تشاء، وقيل: أراد بها من يشاء، عن ابن عباس. وتقديره: تهلك من تشاء وتنجي من تشاء،»
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r2] verses 7:155 | Because the trial can cause the guidance of the guided and misguidance of the astray, it is valid to attribute it to God. «وقيل: لما كانت المحنة كالسبب في هداية من اهتدى، وضلال من ضل جاز أن تضاف إليه»
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r3] verses 7:155 | “You are our guardian” is glossed either as our supporter or as our owner and caretaker. «أنت ولينا قيل: ناصرنا، وقيل: مالكنا والمتولي لأمورنا»
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r4] verses 7:155 | Moses asks forgiveness and mercy, calling God the best of forgivers. «فاغفر لنا وارحمنا وأنت خير الغافرين.»
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r5] verses 7:155 | The selection was by revelation, as suggested by the report that Moses deferred two men. «الآية تدل على أن ذلك الاختيار كان عن وحي؛ ولذلك أخر رجلين على ما روي في الخبر.»
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r6] verses 7:155 | Neither Moses nor the seventy asked to see God; the verse attributes the act to the foolish people, and a report tying “and me” to Moses' killing of the Egyptian is grossly ignorant. «وتدل على أن سؤال الرؤية لم يكن من موسى ولا من السبعين؛ لذلك قال: (أتهلكنا بما فعل السفهاء منا) أضافه إلى غيرهم،»
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r7] verses 7:155 | The Egyptian's killing was either permitted or a minor sin, so Moses' destruction could not have been because of it; he spoke to appeal for the requested vision. «لأن ذلك القتل إما أنه وقع مباحا أو صغيرا، فلا يجوز أن يقع الهلاك لأجله، وإنما قال ذلك انقطاعا إليه - تعالى، واستعطافا لمسألة الرؤية.»
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r8] verses 7:155 | Calling God “our guardian” shows that people must turn to Him in moments of distress. «ويدل قوله: أنت ولينا على وجوب الانقطاع إليه - تعالى - عند الشدائد.»
[JISHUMI:v4p2735/r9] verses 7:155 | Calling God the best of forgivers implies others may forgive, though only God is described this way without qualification. «وتدل على أن غيره قد يغفر حتى يصح قوله: خير الغافرين، فأما عند الإطلاق فيوصف به القديم سبحانه.»
[JISHUMI:v4p2736/r1] verses 7:155 | Some say forgiveness by someone other than God functions like pardon, such as releasing debts and rights. «مجرى العفو، كالإبراء عن الديون والحقوق وغيرها.»
[JISHUMI:v4p2736/r2] verses 7:155 | The seventy's tremor is variously understood as a trial or a punishment. «وتدل على أن تلك الرجفة كانت امتحانا، ومنهم من قال: كانت عقوبة.»
[KASHSHAF-FULL:v2p156/r22] verses 7:155 | An unnamed poet's verse urges the sinner to repent and prostrate, illustrating hadnā as turning back to God. «يا راكب الذنب هدهد … واسجد كأنك هدهد»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r1] verses 7:155 | Moses chose seventy men from Israel’s twelve tribes to accompany him on the second visit to Sinai. «on iki oymağından (sıbt) seçtiği yetmiş kişiyi yanına alarak»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r10] verses 7:155 | The word süfehāʾ, rendered “mindless,” derives from safah, which can mean ignorance, foolishness, irrationality, wastefulness, barbarity, or insolence; safīh contrasts with mature and rational people, including the legally mature rashīd. «süfehâ’ kelimesi, “cahillik, ahmaklık, akılsızlık, beyinsizlik, malı boş yere harcama veya barbarlık, küstahlık” gibi anlamlara gelen sefeh kelimesinden sefîhin çoğuludur.»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r11] verses 7:155 | In context, “mindless” describes calf-worshippers whose conduct displayed not only foolishness but also insolence, coarseness, and barbarity. «buzağı heykeline taparak ahmak ve akılsız oldukları gibi tutum ve davranışlarıyla aynı zamanda küstah, hoyrat ve barbar olduklarını da ortaya koyanlar»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r12] verses 7:155 | Moses’ plea implies that other Israelites besides him did not worship the calf. «İsrâiloğulları arasında, kendisiyle birlikte, buzağıya tapmayan daha başka kimselerin de bulunduğu anlaşılmaktadır.»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r13] verses 7:155 | Moses may have pleaded this because he thought those others had not tried hard enough to prevent the wrongdoing. «bunların söz konusu kötülüğü önlemek hususunda yeterince çaba harcamadıklarını düşündüğü için»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r14] verses 7:155 | Because the calf episode and its consequences occurred under divine decree, Moses understood it as God’s test, accepted that both misguidance and guidance come from God, and prayed for mercy, forgiveness, and good in this life and the next. «Bu altın buzağı olayı ve ona bağlı gelişmelerin hepsi temelde Allah’ın takdiriyle vuku bulduğu için Hz. Mûsâ, “Bu iş senin imtihanından başka bir şey değildir” diyerek olayı ilâhî bir imtihan olarak algılamış»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r2] verses 7:155 | The second visit was to seek pardon and pray because the people had worshipped the golden calf. «kavminin altın buzağıya tapmalarından dolayı af dilemeleri, dua ve niyazda bulunmaları için»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r3] verses 7:155 | The Torah describes two appointed meetings; Moses took seventy Israelite elders to the first, but does not say whether he took seventy on the second. «Tevrat’ta Mûsâ’nın ikinci buluşmada yanına yine yetmiş kişi alıp almadığına ilişkin bilgi yoktur.»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r4] verses 7:155 | Ibn Ashur treats the second meeting as completing the first, so taking seventy there is compatible with the Torah; the Qur’an states this explicitly. «İbn Âşûr, ikinci buluşmayı ilkinin tamamlayıcısı gibi düşünerek, Mûsâ’nın bu buluşmaya da yetmiş kişi götürmesinin Tevrat’a göre mümkün olduğunu ifade eder; Kur’an’da ise bu husus sarahatle belirtilmiştir.»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r5] verses 7:155 | The Qur’an says Moses collapsed when the mountain shook at the first meeting, but does not say whether the seventy were there or affected; it places their ordeal on the second visit. «ilk buluşmasında, dağın sallanmasıyla kendinden geçip yere yığıldığı bildirilirken, söz konusu yetmiş kişinin orada bulunup bulunmadığından»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r6] verses 7:155 | Ibn Ashur harmonizes the accounts by placing the seventy with Moses during the first visit as well. «İbn Âşûr, yine Tevrat ile Kur’an’daki bilgileri birleştirerek, yetmiş kişilik topluluğun ilk ziyarette de Mûsâ’nın yanında bulunduğunu»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r7] verses 7:155 | Razi transmits exegetes’ account that a cloud covered Sinai, Moses and the seventy entered and prostrated, heard divine commands, then the group demanded to see God before believing (2:55), whereupon the quake struck. «Fahreddin er-Râzî, ikinci ziyaret esnasında söz konusu yetmiş kişinin müthiş bir sarsıntıyla bayılıp düşmelerinin sebebine ilişkin olarak müfessirlerden şöyle bir bilgi aktarır»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r8] verses 7:155 | The Torah partly recounts this event in its account of the first meeting (Exodus 24:15–18). «Bu olay kısmen Tevrat’ta da anlatılmakta, ancak orada olay ilk ziyaretin anlatıldığı bölümde geçmektedir»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:155/r9] verses 7:155 | Moses likely thought the seventy, along with the people, had grievously sinned by worshipping the calf, and that his own request to see God at Sinai had also been a fault. «muhtemelen kavmiyle birlikte yanındaki yetmiş kişinin de daha önce buzağı heykeline tapmak suretiyle ağır bir suç işlediklerini»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r1] verses 7:155 | The wording omits “from,” and means Moses chose seventy men from his people. «وفي الكلام محذوف وتقديره : واختار موسى من قومه سبعين رجلا»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r10] verses 7:155 | Alternatively, it denies that they should be punished for the fools' deeds, since only wrongdoers are punished. «أنه سؤال نفي , وتقديره : إنك ل تعذب إلا مذبنا فكيف تهلكنا بما فعل السفهاء منا»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r11] verses 7:155 | A report says the seventy died as a trial rather than final death, distinguishing obedient people from sinners; Moses swooned, then God revived the dead. «لا موت فناء ولكن موت ابتلاء ليثبت به من أطاع وينتقم به ممن عصى وأخذت موسى غشية»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r12] verses 7:155 | Qatada interprets the fitna as punishment. «أن المراد بالفتنة العذاب , قاله قتادة»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r13] verses 7:155 | Alternatively, fitna means trial and testing. «أن المراد بها الابتلاء والاختبار»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r2] verses 7:155 | The appointment may be the earlier time associated with the request to see God. «أنه الميقات المذكور في سؤال الرؤية»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r3] verses 7:155 | Alternatively, it may be a different appointment for repentance from worship of the calf. «أنه ميقات غير الأول وهو ميقات التوبة من عبادة العجل»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r4] verses 7:155 | Al-Kalbi glosses the seizure as an earthquake. «أنها الزلزلة , قاله الكلبي»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r5] verses 7:155 | Mujahid says the seizure was death: the men died and were then brought back to life. «أنه الموت . قال مجاهد : ماتوا ثم أحياهم»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r6] verses 7:155 | Al-Farra says it was a fire that burned them; Moses thought they had died, though they had not. «أنها نار أحرقتهم فظن موسى أنهم قد هلكوا ولم يهلكوا , قاله الفراء»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r7] verses 7:155 | Ibn Ishaq says the men were seized because they asked to see God. «لأنهم سألوا الرؤية , قاله ابن إسحاق»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v2p265/r8] verses 7:155 | Ibn Abbas says they were seized because they had not forbidden worship of the calf. «لأنهم لم ينهوا عن عبادة العجل قاله ابن عباس»
