# Notes

## 18:81
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r11] verses 18:81 | The child would be more connected to kin and dutiful to the parents. «وهو قول ابن عباس قال: (أوصل للرحم، وأبر بوالديه)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r12] verses 18:81 | He glosses أقرب رحما as greater affection. «وروى عنه سعيد بن جبير: (وأقرب مودة)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r13] verses 18:81 | Most exegetes make the replacement the more dutiful child; Ibn Jurayj and al-Farrāʾ instead mean the parents would show the replacement greater compassion. The author rejects al-Zajjaj's added kinship comparison because siblings share kinship equally. «وهم؛ لأن الأولاد لصلب الوالدين سواء في القرابة»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r2] verses 18:81 | خيرا منه زكاة means better than him in religion. «قال ابن عباس في رواية سعيد بن جبير: (خيرا منه دينا)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r3] verses 18:81 | He likewise interprets زكاة as religion. «وهو قول قتادة»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r4] verses 18:81 | They gloss the replacement as better in righteousness; زكاة means righteousness. «الكلبي، والفراء: (خيرا منه صلاحا)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r5] verses 18:81 | He interprets زكاة as Islam, agreeing with the religion gloss. «تفسير ابن جريج الزكاة في هذه الآية: (بالإسلام)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r6] verses 18:81 | رحم and its variant forms mean affection and mercy; poetry illustrates the usage. «قاله أبو عبيدة، والزجاج»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r7] verses 18:81 | His line on رحم witnesses the sense of affection and compassion. «ولم تعوج رحم من تعوجا»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r8] verses 18:81 | His line asking God to bestow رحم on Idris and curse Iblis uses رحم for mercy. «يا منزل الرحم على إدريس ... ومنزل اللعن علي إبليس»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p120/r9] verses 18:81 | رحم can also mean kinship, as in saying one person is closer in kinship to another. «ويستعمل الرحم بمعنى القرابة لا بمعنى الشفقة»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p122/r1] verses 18:81 | A Prophetic report says the father went to the mother and she conceived, giving birth to a child better in purity and closer in compassion. «قال النبي -صلى الله عليه وسلم- في حديث أبي بن كعب»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p122/r2] verses 18:81 | A girl was born and later gave birth to a prophet. «وقال سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس: (ولدت جارية فولدت نبيا)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p122/r3] verses 18:81 | He says the replacement was a child in the mother's womb. «وروى عكرمة عنه في قوله: {خيرا منه زكاة وأقرب رحما} قال: (ولدا كان في بطن أمه)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p122/r4] verses 18:81 | He says the replacement was a girl. «وقال مجاهد: (كان ذلك الولد جارية)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p122/r5] verses 18:81 | He alone among the exegetes says God replaced the boy with a Muslim boy, while the slain boy was an unbeliever. «إلا ابن جريج، فإنه قال: (أبدلهما الله غلاما مسلما، وكان المقتول كافرا)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p122/r6] verses 18:81 | God replaced him with a girl who gave birth to seventy prophets; al-Bāqir also holds this. «وقال ابن عباس في رواية عطاء: (أبدلهما الله به جارية ولدت سبعين نبيا)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p122/r7] verses 18:81 | The reports reconcile if the seventy prophets descended from the girl and she herself bore only one prophet. «أن السبعين كانوا من نسلها ولم تلد لبطنها إلا نبيا»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p122/r8] verses 18:81 | He tells his son the father rejoiced at the boy's birth and grieved at his death; had he lived he would have ruined them, so God's disliked decree may be better than one's preferred choice. «قال مطرف (¬4) في هذه الآية: (أيم الله لقد كان أبوه فرحا بمولده»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:18:80-83/r2] verses 18:81 | God would replace him with a purer child, more dutiful to his parents and closer in kinship. «{ فأردنا أن يبدلهما ربهما خيرا منه زكاة } صلاحا { وأقرب رحما } وأبر بوالديه وأوصل للرحم.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p199/r9] verses 18:81 | Better in purity means better in piety. «قال: {فأردنا أن يبدلهما ربهما خيرا منه زكاة} [الكهف: 81] في التقوى.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p200/r1] verses 18:81 | Al-Ḥasan explains “closer in raḥm” as more dutiful or righteous. «{وأقرب رحما} [الكهف: 81] يعني: برا في قول الحسن.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p200/r2] verses 18:81 | Qatāda explains “closer in raḥm” as better in good. «يعني: برا في قول الحسن.
وقال قتادة: وأقرب خيرا.»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p305#2/r1] verses 18:81 | Fa-aradnā means God willed it; plural self-reference is common in divine speech. «بمعنى أراد الله - جل وعز - لأن لفظ الإخبار عن الله كذا أكثر من أن
يحصى.»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p305#2/r2] verses 18:81 | Raḥman means affection, closeness in kinship, compassion, or mercy; a poem supports the word raḥm in the sense of tenderness. «أي أقرب عطفا وأمس بالقرابة، والرحم والرحم في اللغة العطف
والرحمة»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p305#2/r3] verses 18:81 | An unidentified poem uses raḥm in a context of tenderness and kindness; the witnessed word is raḥm. «قال الشاعر:
وكيف بظلم جارية. . . ومنها اللين والرحم»

## 18:82
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p407#4/r1] verses 18:82 | Mujahid says the treasure beneath the wall consisted of written sheets of knowledge. «عن مجاهد في قوله تعالى ﴿ وكان تحته كنز لهما ﴾ قال صحف من علم»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p407/r1] verses 18:82 | The treasure beneath the wall was wealth belonging to the two boys. «﴿ وأما الجدار فكان لغلامين يتيمين في المدينة وكان تحته كنز لهما ﴾ قال مال لهما»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p407/r2] verses 18:82 | Qatada says treasure was lawful for earlier communities, while spoils were forbidden to them; the rules are reversed for this community. «عن قتادة أحل الكنز لمن كان قبلنا وحرم علينا وحرمت الغنيمة على من كان قبلنا وأحلت لنا»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p212/r6] verses 18:82 | The beneficial wall outcome is attributed to God as Lord, while the defect is not, since mentioning damage would be inappropriate in relation to God. «وفي قوله: فأراد ربك أن يبلغا لما في ذكر العيب»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p215/r11] verses 18:82 | The boys’ orphan status indicates they were young; a hadith says orphanhood ends at puberty, and here the word expresses compassion. A report gives their names and those of their parents. «ووصف الغلامين باليتم يدل على أنهما كانا صغيرين. وفي الحديث»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p215/r12] verses 18:82 | Ikrima and Qatada say the treasure was a large amount of buried gold and silver. «والظاهر في الكنز أنه مال مدفون جسيم ذهب وفضة قاله عكرمة وقتادة.»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p215/r13] verses 18:82 | Ibn Abbas and Ibn Jubayr say the treasure was knowledge in a buried codex; another report says it was a gold tablet. «وقال ابن عباس وابن جبير: كان علما في مصحف مدفونة: وقيل: لوح من ذهب»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p216/r1] verses 18:82 | The apparent father is the boys’ nearest biological father; other reports place him seven or ten generations back. The boys were preserved through his righteousness. «والظاهر أن أباهما هو الأقرب إليهما الذي ولدهما دنية»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p216/r2] verses 18:82 | A hadith says God preserves a righteous man through his descendants. «وفي الحديث: «إن الله يحفظ الرجل الصالح في ذريته»»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p216/r3] verses 18:82 | Raḥmatan is an object of purpose; al-Zamakhshari allows a verbal noun meaning God had mercy on them, while Abu al-Baqaʾ calls the alternative parses strained. «وانتصب رحمة على المفعول له وأجاز الزمخشري أن ينصب»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p216/r4] verses 18:82 | Khidr says he acted by God’s command rather than his own reasoning, indicating that he was a prophet receiving revelation. «وما فعلته أي وما فعلت ما رأيت من خرق السفينة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p216/r5] verses 18:82 | Ibn al-Sikkit lists four forms of “I can”; Abu Hayyan explains the dropped element in istaṭāʿ as the inflectional tāʾ, replaced by ṭāʾ. «قال ابن السكيت: يقال ما أستطيع وما أسطيع وما أستتيع وأستيع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r1] verses 18:82 | The two orphan boys were probably young, since human orphanhood ends at puberty; Ibn ʿAṭiyya's suggestion that they were adults described as orphans compassionately is very unlikely. «لأن يتم بني آدم بموت الأب وفي الحديث لا يتم بعد بلوغ»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r2] verses 18:82 | Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows that the boys were adults, with orphan used compassionately for their former state; the author considers this remote. «وقال ابن عطية : يحتمل أنهما كانا بالغين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r3] verses 18:82 | The city is the earlier town; calling it a city here foregrounds the orphans and their righteous local father, unlike the earlier narrative's focus. «هي القرية المذكورة فيما سبق ولعل التعبير عنها بالمدينة هنا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r4] verses 18:82 | They identify the buried treasure as gold and silver; the author cites a report from Abū al-Dardāʾ transmitted by al-Bukhārī, al-Tirmidhī, and al-Ḥākim, who authenticated it. «مال مدفون من ذهب وفضة كما أخرجه البخاري في تاريخه والترمذي»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r5] verses 18:82 | Al-Rāghib defines kanz as gathering wealth together and preserving it, from the expression for packing dates into a container. «قال الراغب : الكنز جعل المال بعضه على بعض وحفظه وأصله من كنزت التمر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r6] verses 18:82 | Because the treasure belonged to the boys, it was likely inherited from their righteous father; blameworthy treasure is wealth from which due rights are withheld, not every stored asset. «بأن الظاهر أن الكانز له أبوهما لاقتضاء ( لهما ) له إذا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r7] verses 18:82 | A report says treasure was permitted to earlier communities while spoils were forbidden, and the reverse applies to this community; laws can differ by community. «أحلت لهم الكنوز وحرمت عليهم الغنائم وأحلت لنا الغنائم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r8] verses 18:82 | Ibn ʿAbbās says the treasure was not gold or silver but written leaves of knowledge; the same interpretation is related from Ibn Jubayr. «عن ابن عباس أنه قال : ما كان ذهبا ولا فضة ولكن كان صحف علم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p12#2/r9] verses 18:82 | A raised report from ʿAlī, a report from Abū Dha
