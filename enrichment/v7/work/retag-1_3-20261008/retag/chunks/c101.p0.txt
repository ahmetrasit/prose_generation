# Notes

## 21:107
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p387/r1] verses 21:107 | Aḥmad ibn Ṣāliḥ hopes the preceding report about the Prophet’s response to Quraysh is authentic. «وقال أحمد بن صالح: أرجو أن يكون الحديث صحيحا»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p387/r2] verses 21:107 | Salman tells Hudhayfa that the Prophet explained that any insult or curse he uttered in anger should become a prayer for its recipient on Resurrection Day, since he was a human who became angry but was sent as mercy. «أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم] (4) خطب فقال: "أيما رجل من أمتي سببته [سبة] (5) في غضبي»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p387/r3] verses 21:107 | Ibn Abbas says believers receive mercy in both lives, while those who reject God and His Messenger are spared the devastating punishments that struck earlier nations; Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim transmit this wording. «عن ابن عباس في قوله: {وما أرسلناك إلا رحمة للعالمين} قال: من آمن بالله واليوم الآخر، كتب له الرحمة»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p387/r4] verses 21:107 | A second Ibn Abbas wording says Muhammad’s followers receive mercy in both lives, while nonfollowers are spared the earlier nations’ punishments of being swallowed by the earth or pelted with stones. «عن ابن عباس: {وما أرسلناك إلا رحمة للعالمين} قال: من تبعه كان له رحمة في الدنيا والآخرة، ومن لم يتبعه»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p160/r1] verses 21:107 | God sent the Prophet as mercy to deliver glad tidings and warnings; his duty is proclamation, and he should tell mockers that he brings mercy rather than punishment and does not know its timing. «God has sent the Prophet (sws) as a mercy for mankind to deliver glad tidings and warnings to people.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p92/r3] verses 21:92, 21:93, 21:94, 21:95, 21:96, 21:97, 21:98, 21:99, 21:100, 21:101, 21:102, 21:103, 21:104, 21:105, 21:106, 21:107, 21:108, 21:109, 21:110, 21:111, 21:112 | The conclusion reassures the Prophet and companions that all prophets taught one message; they should persist and leave those who divided it to God, since late faith will not help. «all prophets of God have taught the same message they are preaching»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-5-english:p92/r4] verses 21:92, 21:93, 21:94, 21:95, 21:96, 21:97, 21:98, 21:99, 21:100, 21:101, 21:102, 21:103, 21:104, 21:105, 21:106, 21:107, 21:108, 21:109, 21:110, 21:111, 21:112 | Only God's pious servants attain the Hereafter's success; judgement is near, and the Prophet ends by supplicating God to judge between him and his foes. «a decisive declaration is made from the tongue of the Prophet (sws) that the time of judgement is at hand»
[JISHUMI:v7p4900/r12] verses 21:107 | Raḥmatan is accusative as a verbal noun, meaning “except that He shows them mercy.” «(رحمة) نصب على المصدر تقديره: إلا أن يرحمهم رحمة.»
[JISHUMI:v7p4900/r7] verses 21:107 | Hafs reads qāla rabb with an alif as quoted speech; the other readers use qul as a command. «قرأ حفص عن عاصم: قال رب بالألف على الحكاية، وقرأ الباقون: قل بغير ألف على الأمر.»
[JISHUMI:v7p4903/r11] verses 21:107 | If God sent the messenger so people would disbelieve, his mission would not be mercy to everyone. «أنه لو أرسل الرسول ليكفروا لم يكن رحمة للجميع.»
[JISHUMI:v7p4903/r12] verses 21:107 | If God created people’s acts, sending the messenger would have no purpose. «ومنها: أن الأفعال لو كانت خلقا له لم يكن للبعثة معنى.»
[JISHUMI:v7p4903/r7] verses 21:107 | The statement that Muhammad was sent as a mercy to all worlds means he was sent to everyone. «يدل قوله: (رحمة للعالمين) أنه مبعوث إلى الكافة.»
[JISHUMI:v7p4903/r8] verses 21:107 | The revealed laws are a blessing and grace for believers and unbelievers alike; unbelief comes from the unbeliever himself, contrary to the determinists. «وتدل على أن شرائعه نعمة ولطف للجميع مؤمنهم وكافرهم، وأن الكافر من جهة نفسه أتي خلاف ما تقوله المجبرة»

[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p474/r12] verses 21:107 | The Prophet's mercy may be guidance to obedience to God and deserving His reward. «الهداية إلى طاعة الله واستحقاق ثوابه»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p474/r13] verses 21:107 | Alternatively, the mercy is that the punishment of annihilation was lifted from those addressed. «ما رفع عنهم من عذاب الاستئصال»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p474/r14] verses 21:107 | If mercy means guidance, the worlds addressed may be restricted to those who believe. «من آمن منهم , فيكون على الخصوص في المؤمنين إذا قيل إن الرحمة الهداية»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p474/r15] verses 21:107 | If mercy means the lifting of annihilation punishment, the worlds addressed include both believers and unbelievers. «الجميع , فيكون على العموم في المؤمنين والكافرين إذا قيل إن الرحمة ما رفع عنهم من عذاب الاستئصال»
[MUJAHID:v1p417#3/r1] verses 21:107 | Ibn ʿAbbas says the Prophet's mission was mercy: believers receive mercy in this life and the next, while unbelievers are spared the worldly punishments that struck earlier peoples, such as being swallowed by the earth or pelted. «عن ابن عباس في قوله ﴿ وما أرسلناك إلا رحمة للعالمين ﴾ قال من آمن بالله ورسوله تمت له الرحمة في الدنيا والآخرة»
[MUQATIL:21:107-109/r1] verses 21:107 | The worlds to whom Muhammad was sent as mercy are jinn and humans. «{ ومآ أرسلناك } يا محمد { إلا رحمة للعالمين } [آية: 107] يعنى الجن والإنس،»
[MUQATIL:21:107-109/r2] verses 21:107 | Following Muhammad's religion brings mercy; even those who do not follow him were spared calamity while he lived among them. «فمن تبع محمدا صلى الله عليه وسلم على دينه، فهو له رحمة كقوله سبحان: لعيسى ابن مريم صلى الله عليه:»
[NAHHAS:v3p82/r5] verses 21:107 | Ibn ʿAbbas says Muhammad was mercy for all people: believers gained happiness, while unbelievers were spared the destruction that struck earlier nations. «عن ابن عباس قال كان محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم رحمة لجميع الناس فمن آمن به وصدق به سعد»
[NAHHAS:v3p82/r6] verses 21:107 | Those who did not believe were still spared the landslides and drowning that afflicted earlier peoples. «ومن لم يؤمن به سلم مما لحق الأمم من الخسف والغرق»
