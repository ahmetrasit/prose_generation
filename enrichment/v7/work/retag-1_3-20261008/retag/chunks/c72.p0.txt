# Notes

## 17:110
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p197/r16] verses 17:110 | The Breath of the Merciful manifests divine words in the void where the world appears; humans invoke God through the forms of names made from their breath and letter-combinations, serving as translators of the divine names. «لأن الرحمن هو المنعوت بالنفس وبالنفس ظهرت الكلمات»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p198/r1] verses 17:110 | The forms arising from the Merciful's breath have meanings behind them, like spirits behind letters; these meanings are divine-name realities beyond qualification and are related to the humanly uttered forms as meanings to letters. «وخلف تلك الصور المعاني التي هي كالأرواح للأسماء الإلهية التي يذكر الحق بها نفسه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p198/r2] verses 17:110 | The two permitted invocations are either the forms of merciful breath present in cosmic aspirations within our spirits, or the names articulated by our own breath as translation; one may attend to the meaning of Allah or the form of al-Rahman. «فإن شئنا دعوناه بصور الأسماء النفسية الرحمانية وهي الهمم الكونية التي في أرواحنا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p198/r3] verses 17:110 | The author says this difficult account does not make the verse's invocation a simple supplication in the earlier sense. «وهو كلام يعسر فهمه إلا على من شاء الله تعالى بيد أنه ليس فيه حمل الدعاء على ما سمعت»
[ASAD-NOTES:17:109#n132/r1] verses 17:110 | Al-asma al-husna means the most perfect or goodly names. «the most perfect [or "most goodly"] names»
[ASAD-NOTES:17:109#n132/r2] verses 17:110 | Ar-Rahman is exclusive to God and denotes the unconditional, all-embracing exercise of grace and mercy. «denoting the unconditional, all-embracing quality and exercise of grace and mercy, and is applied exclusively to God»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p137/r1] verses 17:110 | Ibn Abbas says the verse followed the Prophet's tearful night prostration in Mecca, when he invoked Allah and al-Rahman and Abu Jahl accused him of calling on two gods; the two names belong to one God. «قال ابن عباس: سجد رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم بمكة ذات ليلة فجعل يبكي»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p137/r2] verses 17:110 | The particle mā is extra in the construction, which means whichever of these two names, or any of God's names, you invoke, His are the beautiful names. «{أيا ما تدعوا} "ما" صلة معناه: أيا ما تدعوا من هذين الاسمين»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p137/r3] verses 17:110 | Allah and al-Rahman are two names for the same God, not two deities. «فأنزل الله تعالى هذه الآية (3) . ومعناه: أنهما اسمان لواحد.»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p138/r1] verses 17:110 | The same chain's added wording explains the middle course as letting companions hear the recitation without excessive loudness. «وبهذا الإسناد عن محمد بن إسماعيل قال: حدثنا مسدد عن هشيم»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p138/r2] verses 17:110 | The middle course means letting companions hear the recitation without raising it so much that the Qur'an's opponents hear and abuse it. «أسمعهم ولا تجهر حتى يأخذوا عنك القرآن»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p138/r3] verses 17:110 | They say the verse concerns supplication; Aisha specifically reports that it was revealed about prayerful invocation. «وقال قوم: الآية في الدعاء وهو قول عائشة رضي الله عنها والنخعي»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p138/r4] verses 17:110 | He says Bedouins of Banu Tamim loudly asked the Prophet for wealth and children after prayer, so the verse prohibited raising the voice in recitation or supplication. «وقال عبد الله بن شداد: كان أعراب من بني تميم»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p138/r5] verses 17:110 | Mukhāfata means lowering the voice or silence, and the commanded course is between loudness and concealment. «والمخافتة: خفض الصوت والسكوت "وابتغ بين ذلك سبيلا" أي: بين الجهر»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p138/r6] verses 17:110 | Abū Qatada reports the Prophet asking Abū Bakr to raise his quiet recitation a little and ʿUmar to lower his loud recitation a little: one said he was speaking to his Lord, the other waking sleepers and driving Satan away. «عن أبي قتادة أن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال لأبي بكر: "مررت بك وأنت تقرأ وأنت تخفض»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:17:109-110/r10] verses 17:110 | Another interpretation prohibits making every prayer entirely loud or quiet, prescribing quiet recitation by day and loud recitation by night. «لا تجهر بصلاتك كلها ولا تخافت بها بأسرها»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:17:109-110/r4] verses 17:110 | The occasion may be that polytheists heard the Prophet say Allah and Rahman, then accused him of invoking another god; alternatively Jews said he rarely mentioned Rahman despite its prominence in the Torah. «نزلت حين سمع المشركون رسول الله يقول»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:17:109-110/r5] verses 17:110 | One explanation makes the names equal in goodness of invocation and success in attaining the aim; Bayḍāwī prefers it in light of the following clause. The other equates their reference to one divine essence despite differing aspects of designation. «وعلى الثاني أنهما سيان في حسن الإطلاق والإفضاء إلى المقصود وهو أجود»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:17:109-110/r6] verses 17:110 | Here calling means naming: the first object is omitted, aw offers a choice, ayya’s tanwīn replaces its genitive complement, and ma reinforces its indefiniteness. «والدعاء في الآية بمعنى التسمية وهو يتعدى إلى مفعولين حذف أولهما»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:17:109-110/r7] verses 17:110 | The pronoun refers to the named entity, not the name; the wording replaces an underlying statement that either call is good, emphasizing His beautiful names and their majestic and gracious attributes. «والضمير في { فله } للمسمى لأن التسمية له لا للاسم»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:17:109-110/r8] verses 17:110 | Do not recite loudly enough for polytheists to hear and revile the prayer, or so softly that fellow believers cannot hear; choose a middle course, since moderation is loved. «بقراءة صلاتك حتى تسمع المشركين، فإن ذلك يحملهم على السب واللغو فيها»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:17:109-110/r9] verses 17:110 | Abu Bakr whispered to privately address God who knew his need; Umar spoke aloud to repel Satan and wake sleepers, so the Prophet told Abu Bakr to raise his voice and Umar to lower his. «روي أن أبا بكر رضي الله عنه كان يخفت ويقول: أناجي ربي وقد علم حاجتي، وعمر رضي الله عنه كان يجهر ويقول أطرد الشيطان»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p437/r1] verses 17:110 | Since learned believers abandoned inherited religions for the Qur'an, anyone with humanity should always call upon its revealer, not only in distress; prayer is especially apt in prostration with tears and humility. «موجبا لكل من له أدنى إنسانية أن يؤمن به ويقبل عليه ويدعو من أنزله»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p437/r2] verses 17:110 | The Prophet says a servant is nearest to his Lord while prostrating, supporting supplication during prostration. «أقرب ما يكون العبد من ربه وهو ساجد»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p437/r3] verses 17:110 | The imagined question from the Arab opponents is how to pray and which name to use; the all-encompassing name Allah signifies majesty, while al-Raḥmān suits this surah's repeated, worldly and otherworldly mercy. «ولما كان الجلالة هو الاسم الجامع لجميع معاني الأسماء الحسنى»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p437/r4] verses 17:110 | Either name may be invoked because both name the same God: Allah expresses intrinsic majesty and al-Raḥmān beauty and beneficence. «فاهتفوا بهذا الاسم الدال على الجلال»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p438/r1] verses 17:110 | Both names refer to the one God, who alone owns all beautiful names denoting praise, glory, sanctification, and exaltation; the repeated pronoun is an emphatic rhetorical reuse. «هذان الاسمان وغيرهما مما ورد عن النبي»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p438/r2] verses 17:110 | Al-Ḥarālī distinguishes al-Raḥmān as God's encompassing mercy in creation from al-Raḥīm as mercy applied to particular people in ways suited to them. «الرحمانية استغراق الخلق بالرحمة في إنشائهم , والرحيمية إجراء الخلق»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p438/r3] verses 17:110 | Al-Ḥarālī says no creature embodies al-Raḥmān's full, all-encompassing meaning, so this name belongs to God alone and its tawḥīd follows tawḥīd of the divine name; he links it to the names tradition from Abū Hurayra. «فلم يجر بحق على أحد منهم , وإنما يوجد فيهم حظ خاص من معناه»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p438/r4] verses 17:110 | Supplication is good at all times and especially in prayer; the prior references to dawn recitation and listening to Qur'an could suggest loud reading, so the verse sets its volume. «أو سمى القراءة صلاة لأنها شرط فيها»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p438/r5] verses 17:110 | Do not recite so loudly that hostile Quraysh hear and insult the Qur'an, God, and Muhammad, or so softly that companions behind cannot learn; use a middle course. «فإن المخالفين قد عرف عنادهم فلا يؤمن سبهم للقرآن»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p438/r6] verses 17:110 | Ibn ʿAbbās reports from the Prophet's hidden Meccan period: he recited loudly with companions, pagans heard and insulted, so God ordered a volume that companions could hear without alerting them. «روى البخاري في التفسير عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما في هذه الآية»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p438/r7] verses 17:110 | ʿĀʾisha reports that the verse was revealed concerning supplication; the author allows that it may have had multiple occasions of revelation. «وروى البخاري عن عائشة رضي الله عنها أن هذه الآية نزلت في الدعاء»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p438/r8] verses 17:110 | 'Prayer' names recitation here by referring to the whole for a part; earlier the part, dawn recitation, named the whole prayer because recitation was the focus. «أطلق هنا اسم الكل على الجزء إشارة إلى أن المقصود الصلاة»
