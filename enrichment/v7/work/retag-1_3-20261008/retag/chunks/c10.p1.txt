[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r174] verses 1:1 | ʿAta al-Khurasani explains that Rahim follows Rahman to prevent anyone claiming the exclusive title Rahman, since only God is described as both. «جيء بلفظ الرحيم ليقطع التوهم بذلك ، فإنه لا يوصف بالرحمن الرحيم إلا الله تعالى»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r175] verses 1:1 | Quraysh's denial of Rahman at Hudaybiyya and in 25:60 was stubbornness: pre-Islamic poems already used Rahman for God. «والظاهر أن إنكارهم هذا إنما هو جحود وعناد وتعنت في كفرهم»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r176] verses 1:1 | Salama ibn Jundub's pre-Islamic verse calls God Rahman, showing the name was already known among Arabs. «وما يشأ الرحمن يعقد ويطلق»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r177] verses 1:1 | Ibn ʿAbbas calls Rahman an intensive Arabic form of mercy and glosses the two names as gentle to whom God wills mercy and severe toward whom He wills to chastise. «عن الضحاك ،   عن عبد الله بن عباس ،   قال : الرحمن : الفعلان من الرحمة»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r178] verses 1:1 | Hasan says Rahman is a protected name that people cannot assume for themselves. «عن الحسن ، قال : الرحمن اسم ممنوع»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r179] verses 1:1 | Umm Salama's report says the Prophet paused between the basmala, al-Hamd, Rahman al-Rahim, and Malik yawm al-din; some Kufans pause at the basmala while most connect it to al-Hamd. «حديث أم سلمة أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم كان يقطع قرآنه حرفًا حرفًا»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r18] verses 1:1 | There is no disagreement that the surah has seven verses; reports of eight from ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd and six from Husayn al-Juʿfi are aberrant. «وهي سبع آيات بلا خلاف»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r180] verses 1:1 | Al-Kisaʾi says some Arabs connect the basmala to al-Hamd by transferring the vowel of the connecting hamza to the mīm; Ibn ʿAtiyya says he knew of no transmitted canonical reading for it. «وحكى الكسائي عن بعض العرب أنها تقرأ بفتح الميم وصلة الهمزة»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r19] verses 1:1 | Scholars differ over whether the basmala is an independent opening verse, part of a verse, or not counted as part of the Fatiha. «هل هي آية مستقلة من أولها»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r2] verses 1:1 | The majority call it Umm al-Kitab; Anas is cited for this name. «ويقال لها أيضا : أم الكتاب عند الجمهور ، ذكره أنس»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r20] verses 1:1 | The surah has twenty-five words and one hundred thirteen letters. «كلماتها خمس وعشرون كلمة ، وحروفها مائة وثلاثة عشر حرفًا»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r21] verses 1:1 | Al-Bukhari explains Umm al-Kutub by its being written first in Qur'anic codices and recited first in prayer. «وسميت أم الكتب ، أنه يبدأ بكتابتها في المصاحف ، ويبدأ بقراءتها في الصلاة»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r22] verses 1:1 | Another explanation of Umm al-Kitab is that the meanings of the whole Qur'an return to what the Fatiha contains. «سميت بذلك لرجوع معاني القرآن كله»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r23] verses 1:1 | Ibn Jarir says Arabs call a comprehensive or leading thing with dependent parts an umm, such as the head's covering or an army's banner. «والعرب تسمي كل جامع أمر»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r24] verses 1:1 | Ibn Jarir cites Dhu al-Rumma's verse calling a spear an umm, a focal point followed in affairs. «على رأسه أم لنا نقتدي به»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r25] verses 1:1 | Mecca is called Umm al-Qura because it precedes and gathers the other settlements, or because the earth was spread from it. «وسميت مكة : أم القرى لتقدمها أمام جميعها وجمعها ما سواها ، وقيل : لأن الأرض دحيت منها»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r26] verses 1:1 | The Fatiha opens recitation and was placed first when the Companions wrote the master codex. «ويقال لها أيضًا : الفاتحة ؛ لأنها تفتتح بها القراءة ، وافتتحت الصحابة بها كتابة المصحف الإمام»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r27] verses 1:1 | The title al-Sabʿ al-Mathani is explained by its repetition in every prayer unit, though Ibn Kathir notes another meaning will follow. «قالوا : لأنها تثنى في الصلاة ، فتقرأ في كل ركعة ، وإن كان للمثاني معنى آخر»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r28] verses 1:1 | Reports from Abu Hurayra identify Umm al-Qur'an as the Fatiha and the seven oft-repeated; al-Daraqutni says its transmitters are trustworthy. «هي أم القرآن ، وهي فاتحة الكتاب ، وهي السبع المثاني»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r29] verses 1:1 | Al-Bayhaqi reports that ʿAli, Ibn ʿAbbas, and Abu Hurayra interpret the seven oft-repeated in 15:87 as the Fatiha, with the basmala its seventh verse. «وروى البيهقي عن علي{[31]} وابن عباس{[32]} وأبي هريرة{[33]} أنهم»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r3] verses 1:1 | Hasan and Ibn Sirin dislike the name Umm al-Kitab, taking it to refer to the Preserved Tablet; Hasan also calls the clear verses Umm al-Kitab. «والحسن وابن سيرين كرها تسميتها بذلك ، قال الحسن وابن سيرين : إنما ذلك اللوح المحفوظ»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r30] verses 1:1 | Ibn Masʿud explains that he omitted the Fatiha from his codex because he would have written it at the start of every surah where it is recited in prayer; Muslims' memorization sufficed. «لو كتبتها لكتبتها في أول كل سورة»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r31] verses 1:1 | One view makes the Fatiha the first revelation, based on a report in al-Bayhaqi and listed by al-Baqillani among three views. «وقد قيل : إن الفاتحة أول شيء نزل من القرآن ، كما ورد في حديث رواه البيهقي»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r32] verses 1:1 | A second view makes “O you wrapped up” the first revelation, based on Jabir's sound hadith. «وقيل : { يَا أَيُّهَا الْمُدَّثِّرُ } كما في حديث جابر في الصحيح»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r33] verses 1:1 | Ibn Kathir says the correct view is that “Read in the name of your Lord” was first revealed. «وقيل : { اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ } [ العلق : 1 ] وهذا هو الصحيح»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r34] verses 1:1 | Abu Saʿid ibn al-Muʿalla reports that the Prophet promised to teach him the Qur'an's greatest surah and identified it as al-Hamd, the seven oft-repeated and the great Qur'an. «الحمد لله رب العالمين هي : السبع المثاني والقرآن العظيم الذي أوتيته»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r35] verses 1:1 | Ibn Kathir distinguishes the Muwatta's Abu Saʿid, a Successor and client of Khuzāʿa, from the Companion Abu Saʿid ibn al-Muʿalla; the former report appears disconnected unless he heard it from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb. «فأبو سعيد هذا ليس بأبي سعيد بن المعلى ، كما اعتقده ابن الأثير»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r36] verses 1:1 | A report through Abu Hurayra from Ubayy repeats that the Fatiha has no equal in the Torah, Gospel, Psalms, or Qur'an; al-Tirmidhi calls it hasan sahih. «إني لأرجو ألا أخرج من هذا الباب حتى تعلمها»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r37] verses 1:1 | Another report from Ubayy calls Umm al-Qur'an the seven oft-repeated, divided between God and His servant; al-Tirmidhi grades it hasan gharib. «ما أنزل الله في التوراة ولا في الإنجيل مثل أم القرآن ، وهي السبع المثاني ، وهي مقسومة بيني وبين عبدي»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r38] verses 1:1 | The report that the Prophet called the Fatiha the best surah has a good chain according to Ibn Kathir; the Companion's identity is uncertain between al-ʿAbdi and al-Ansari al-Bayyadi. «هذا إسناد جيد ،   وابن عقيل تحتج{[60]} به الأئمة الكبار ،   وعبد الله بن جابر هذا هو الصحابي»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r39] verses 1:1 | These scholars infer that some verses and surahs are superior to others from the superiority reports. «واستدلوا بهذا الحديث وأمثاله على تفاضل بعض الآيات والسور»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r4] verses 1:1 | Hasan also objects to calling the surah Umm al-Qur'an. «وقال الحسن : الآيات المحكمات : هن أم الكتاب»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r40] verses 1:1 | Another group denies ranking Qur'anic passages, since all are God's speech and ranking could imply deficiency in what is less preferred; a report from Malik agrees. «وذهبت طائفة أخرى إلى أنه لا تفاضل في ذلك ؛ لأن الجميع كلام الله ، ولئلا يوهم التفضيل نقص المفضل عليه»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r41] verses 1:1 | In a report judged by the Prophet, a companion recited the Fatiha as ruqya over a stung man who recovered; the Prophet approved the reward of thirty sheep and asked how he knew it was ruqya. «فبرأ ، فأمر له بثلاثين شاة ، وسقانا لبنا»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r42] verses 1:1 | Ibn ʿAbbas reports that Gabriel announced two lights given to Muhammad alone: the Fatiha and the closing verses of al-Baqara; each letter brings a response. «أبشر بنورين قد أوتيتهما لم يؤتهما نبي قبلك : فاتحة الكتاب ، وخواتيم سورة البقرة»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r43] verses 1:1 | The Prophet says prayer without Umm al-Qur'an is deficient, and the divine hadith answers the servant's praise verse by verse as the Fatiha is divided between God and servant. «فهي خِداج - ثلاثًا - غير تمام»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r46] verses 1:1 | Abu Hanifa and his followers hold that Fatiha is not specifically required in prayer; any Qur'an passage suffices, citing 73:20 and “recite what is easy.” «فعند أبي حنيفة ومن وافقه من أصحابه وغيرهم أنها لا تتعين»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r47] verses 1:1 | The other major view requires the Fatiha in prayer, using the deficient-prayer hadith and reports that prayer does not count without it. «والقول الثاني : أنه تتعين قراءة الفاتحة في الصلاة ، ولا تجزئ الصلاة بدونها»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r48] verses 1:1 | Al-Shafiʿi and a group require the Fatiha in every prayer unit; others require it in most units, while Hasan and most Basrans require it in one unit. «أنه تجب قراءتها في كل ركعة»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r49] verses 1:1 | Hasan and most Basrans require the Fatiha in only one unit, relying on the unrestricted wording, “There is no prayer without Fatiha.” «وقال الحسن وأكثر البصريين : إنما تجب قراءتها في ركعة واحدة من الصلوات»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r5] verses 1:1 | In a hadith graded sound by al-Tirmidhi, the Prophet calls it Umm al-Qur'an, Umm al-Kitab, the seven oft-repeated, and the great Qur'an. «عن أبي هريرة قال : قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم : «الحمد لله أم القرآن وأم الكتاب والسبع المثاني والقرآن العظيم»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r50] verses 1:1 | Abu Hanifa, al-Thawri, and al-Awzaʿi again hold that another Qur'anic passage can replace the Fatiha, citing 73:20. «وقال أبو حنيفة وأصحابه والثوري والأوزاعي : لا تتعين»
[IBNKATHIR:1:1/r51] verses 1:1 | A report that each prayer unit needs al-Hamd and another surah is questionable in authenticity. «وفي صحة هذا نظر»
