# Notes

## 7:155
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p71#3/r6] verses 7:155 | In the first-meeting account, Moses selected six from each of twelve tribes, making seventy-two; two disputed who should stay, so Caleb and Joshua stayed, receiving the reward of those who went. «اختار لذلك من اثني عشر سبطا من كل سبط ستة حتى تتاموا إثنين وسبعين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p71#3/r7] verses 7:155 | Another report says only sixty elders were found, so God told Moses to choose ten young men, who awoke as elders. «وروي أنه لم يصب إلا ستين شيخا فأوحى الله تعالى أن يختار من الشبان عشرة فاختارهم فأصبحوا شيوخا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p71#3/r8] verses 7:155 | A further account says they were over twenty and no older than forty; Moses ordered them to fast and purify themselves and their clothes before leading them to Sinai. «وقيل : كانوا أبناء ماعدا العشرين ولم يتجاوزوا الأربعين فذهب عنهم الجهل والصبا فأمرهم موسى عليه السلام أن يصوموا ويتطهروا ويطهروا ثيابهم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p71#3/r9] verses 7:155 | At Sinai a cloud covered the mountain; Moses entered and called the group forward, they prostrated inside the cloud and heard God command and forbid Moses, then asked to see God when the cloud cleared. «فلما دنا من الجبل وقع عليه عمود الغمام حتى تغشى الجبل كله ودنا موسى ودخل فيه وقال للقوم : ادنوا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73#2/r1] verses 7:155 | Israelites demanded that Moses prove his claim by letting them see God openly; God then ordered him to select seventy for the appointed meeting. «عن الفضل بن عيسى بن أخي الرقاشي أن بني إسرائيل قالوا ذات يوم لموسى عليه السلام ألست ابن عمنا ومنا وتزعم أنك كلمت رب العزة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73#2/r2] verses 7:155 | The account implies this was not the first appointed meeting; al-Suddi and al-Hasan also allow that the meeting was a different one, so al-Suddi is not alone. «ظاهر في أن هذا الميقات ليس هو الأول نعم إنه مخالف لما روي عن السدي لكنهما متفقان على القول بالغيرية ويوافق السدي في ذلك الحسن أيضا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73#2/r3] verses 7:155 | Alusi rejects the claim that al-Suddi's account conflicts with what Muhyi al-Sunna transmitted. «وما ذكره من مخالفة كلام السدي لما نقله محيي السنة في حيز المنع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73#2/r4] verses 7:155 | Some exegetes plausibly say Moses selected the seventy twice. «والقول بأن الإختيار كان مرتين غير بعيد وبه قال بعضهم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73#2/r5] verses 7:155 | In al-Suddi's account, after the calf episode and repentance, God ordered Moses to bring Israelites to apologize for calf worship and then choose seventy, favoring the first branch of al-Qutb's proposed alternatives. «انطلق موسى إلى ربه فكلمه فلما كلمه قال»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73#2/r6] verses 7:155 | The demand to apologize after repentance could seek greater divine approval and mercy, emphasize the crime's enormity and need for further pardon, or have preceded the self-killing; their later realization of its gravity explains the order. «والقول بأنه لا معنى للاعتذار بعد قتل أنفسهم ونزول التوبة أجيب عنه بأن المعنى يحتمل أن يكون طلبا لزيادة الرضى واستنزال مزيد الرحمة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73#2/r7] verses 7:155 | The seizure may have been a thunderbolt or the mountain's quake; most say all seventy died and God revived them, while others say they fainted and recovered, for their demand to see God, to show God's majesty, or as discipline. «أي الصاعقة أو رجفة الجبل فصعقوا منها والكثير على أنهم ماتوا جميعا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73/r1] verses 7:155 | Al-Qutb weakens al-Suddi's account: if the apology journey followed the self-killing and forgiveness, there was no need to apologize; if it came before the killing, it is strange that the apology led to their deaths. «وذكر القطب في توهين ما نقل عن السدي بأن الخروج للإعتذار إن كان بعد قتل أنفسهم ونزول التوبة فلا معنى للإعتذار»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73/r2] verses 7:155 | Al-Qutb says Quranic stories recur across surahs, each presenting part of the story to renew reflection, so portions of a story could likewise recur at different points in one surah. «ولا ريب أن قصة واحدة تتكرر في القرآن يذكر في سورة بعضها وفي أخرى بعض أخر وليس ذلك إلا لتكرار إعتبار المعتبرين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73/r3] verses 7:155 | Alusi says the first-appointment view is not baseless and is held by many; his discussion of the episode in al-Baqara appears to support it. «وأنا أقول : إن القول أن هذا الميقات هو الميقات الأول ليس بعاطل من القول وبه قال جمع كما أشرنا إليه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73/r4] verses 7:155 | Despite that support, Alusi concludes that this passage's wording appears to require a different appointment, as most exegetes hold; al-Kashf's response does not make unity the apparent reading. «إلا أن الإنصاف أن ظاهر النظم هنا يقتضي أنه غيره وما ذكره صاحب الكشف لايقتضي أنه ظاهر في خلافه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73/r5] verses 7:155 | Mujahid reports that Moses took the seventy to pray for relief from affliction, but their prayer was not answered, so Moses realized they had committed a sin like their people's. «أخرج عبد بن حميد من طريق أبي سعد عن مجاهد أن موسى عليه السلام خرج بالسبعين من قومه يدعون الله تعالى ويسألونه أن يكشف عنهم البلاء فلم يستجب لهم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p73/r6] verses 7:155 | Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi explains through Abu Sa'd that their prayer went unanswered because they had failed to forbid the wrong and command right conduct. «قال أبو سعد : فحدثني محمد بن كعب القرظي أنه لم يستجب لهم من أجل أنهم لم ينهوهم عن المنكر ولو يأمروهم بالمعروف»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r1] verses 7:155 | The Israelites' demand to see God was apparently made at the meeting place, not after their return; this makes it unlikely to be Moses' first meeting, since his request for vision followed God's speech without a break. «لن نؤمن الخ صدر منهم في ذلك المكان لا بعد الرجوع كما قيل»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r10] verses 7:155 | A report says Moses asked who put spirit into the calf; God answered that He did, and explained that He saw the people's inclination in their hearts and made the calf's effect easy for them. «أخرج ابن أبي حاتم عن راشد بن سعد أن الله تعالى لما قال لموسى عليه السلام إن قومك اتخذوا عجلا جسدا له خوار»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r11] verses 7:155 | Alusi suggests the report may allude to an eternal predisposition that was not itself created. «ولعل هذا إشارة إلى الإستعداد الأزلي الغير المجعول»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r12] verses 7:155 | These authorities take the pronoun in “it is only Your trial” to refer to the quake, meaning the hardship of enduring God's imposed worship and trial. «الضمير راجع على الرجفة أي ما هي إلا تشديدك التعبد والتكلف علينا بالصبر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r13] verses 7:155 | Another interpretation makes the pronoun refer to Moses' request to see God, although that request was not stated in this immediate passage. «الضمير لمسئلة الإراءة وإن لم تذكر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r14] verses 7:155 | The next clause may explain the trial or function as a circumstantial clause; it means God misguides through overstepping or following illusions and guides by strengthening faith. «استئناف مبين لحكم الفتنة وقيل : حال»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r15] verses 7:155 | Other readings of the clause say the quake strikes whomever God wills and is diverted from whomever He wills, or that patience and acceptance earn reward and Paradise while rejecting the trial forfeits them; Alusi finds this latter account weak. «المعنى تصيب بهذه الرجفة من تشاء وتصرفها عمن تشاء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r16] verses 7:155 | Calling God their protector means He alone manages their worldly and afterlife affairs; Moses asks forgiveness for what would incur punishment and mercy through worldly and afterlife blessings. «أي أنت القائم بإمورنا الدنيوية والآخروية لا غيرك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r17] verses 7:155 | The connective makes the plea follow from God's guardianship, which entails averting harm and bringing benefit; forgiveness precedes mercy because removing wrong comes before adorning with good, and Moses may include himself in the plea. «والفاء لترتيب الدعاء على ماقبله من الولاية لأن من شأن من يلي الأمور ويقوم بها دفع الضر وجلب النفع وقدم طلب المغفرة على طلب الرحمة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r18] verses 7:155 | Alusi rejects the claim that Moses sought forgiveness for the boldness of calling the trial God's, since the flow does not suggest God counted it as a sin. «جرأة عظيمة فطلب من الله تعالى غفرانها والتجاوز عنها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r2] verses 7:155 | Ali reports that, after Aaron died, Israelites accused Moses of killing him; seventy were chosen, asked to become prophets, were seized, and returned revived as prophets. «لما حضر أجل هارون أوحى الله تعالى إلى موسى عليه السلام أن انطلق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r3] verses 7:155 | Alusi considers Ali's account unlikely to be sound because reports converge against it and the verses' apparent meaning resists it. «لايكاد يصح فيما أرى لتظافر الآثار بخلافه وإباء ظواهر الآيات عنه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r4] verses 7:155 | Moses' appeal that God could have destroyed them earlier recalls God's prior mercy in sparing them, including from Pharaoh and drowning, and seeks renewed mercy. «عرض للعفو السابق لاستجلاب العفو اللاحق يعني أنك قدرت على إهلاكهم قبل ذلك بحمل فرعون على إهلاكهم وبإغراقهم في البحر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r5] verses 7:155 | Other explanations of “before” place it when they failed to stop calf worship, when Moses requested vision, or when he killed the Egyptian; another says he wished all to perish rather than witness their disobedience, which Alusi finds doubtful. «حين فرطوا في النهي عن عبادة العجل وما فارقوا عبدته»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p74/r6] verses 7:155 | Moses' inclusion of himself is humility and submission; he may mean that God could have destroyed him too when he requested vision or killed the Egyptian. «تسليما منه وتواضعا وقيل : أراد بقوله»
