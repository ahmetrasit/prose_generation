- 18:64: قَالَ ذَٰلِكَ مَا كُنَّا نَبْغِ ۚ فَٱرْتَدَّا عَلَىٰٓ ءَاثَارِهِمَا قَصَصًۭا
- 18:65: فَوَجَدَا عَبْدًۭا مِّنْ عِبَادِنَآ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُ رَحْمَةًۭ مِّنْ عِندِنَا وَعَلَّمْنَٰهُ مِن لَّدُنَّا عِلْمًۭا
- 18:66: قَالَ لَهُۥ مُوسَىٰ هَلْ أَتَّبِعُكَ عَلَىٰٓ أَن تُعَلِّمَنِ مِمَّا عُلِّمْتَ رُشْدًۭا
- 18:71: فَٱنطَلَقَا حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا رَكِبَا فِى ٱلسَّفِينَةِ خَرَقَهَا ۖ قَالَ أَخَرَقْتَهَا لِتُغْرِقَ أَهْلَهَا لَقَدْ جِئْتَ شَيْـًٔا إِمْرًۭا
- 18:72: قَالَ أَلَمْ أَقُلْ إِنَّكَ لَن تَسْتَطِيعَ مَعِىَ صَبْرًۭا
- 18:73: قَالَ لَا تُؤَاخِذْنِى بِمَا نَسِيتُ وَلَا تُرْهِقْنِى مِنْ أَمْرِى عُسْرًۭا
- 18:74: فَٱنطَلَقَا حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا لَقِيَا غُلَٰمًۭا فَقَتَلَهُۥ قَالَ أَقَتَلْتَ نَفْسًۭا زَكِيَّةًۢ بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍۢ لَّقَدْ جِئْتَ شَيْـًۭٔا نُّكْرًۭا
- 18:75: ۞ قَالَ أَلَمْ أَقُل لَّكَ إِنَّكَ لَن تَسْتَطِيعَ مَعِىَ صَبْرًۭا
- 18:76: قَالَ إِن سَأَلْتُكَ عَن شَىْءٍۭ بَعْدَهَا فَلَا تُصَٰحِبْنِى ۖ قَدْ بَلَغْتَ مِن لَّدُنِّى عُذْرًۭا
- 18:77: فَٱنطَلَقَا حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ أَتَيَآ أَهْلَ قَرْيَةٍ ٱسْتَطْعَمَآ أَهْلَهَا فَأَبَوْا۟ أَن يُضَيِّفُوهُمَا فَوَجَدَا فِيهَا جِدَارًۭا يُرِيدُ أَن يَنقَضَّ فَأَقَامَهُۥ ۖ قَالَ لَوْ شِئْتَ لَتَّخَذْتَ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًۭا
- 18:78: قَالَ هَٰذَا فِرَاقُ بَيْنِى وَبَيْنِكَ ۚ سَأُنَبِّئُكَ بِتَأْوِيلِ مَا لَمْ تَسْتَطِع عَّلَيْهِ صَبْرًا

# Notes

## 18:65
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r50] verses 18:65 | In some accounts Khiḍr uncovered his face and asked whether there was peace in Moses’ land and who Moses was. «Khiḍr removed the part of the robe covering his face and asked, “Is there peace in your land? Who are you?”»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r51] verses 18:65 | Another account says Khiḍr recognized Moses immediately and greeted him as Israel’s prophet. «Khiḍr immediately recognized Moses and greeted him as the “prophet of the Children of Israel”»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r52] verses 18:65 | Some accounts place Khiḍr’s life in the eras of Afarīdūn, Dhu’l-Qarnayn, and Moses. «Some consider Khiḍr to have been alive at the time of the legendary Persian hero and king Afarīdūn»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r53] verses 18:65 | Khiḍr remains a Sufi figure whom some believe guides or initiates people through dreams and visions. «some of whom consider him to be an eternal and mysterious mystical master who still guides or even initiates certain people»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r54] verses 18:65 | Many interpret the mercy granted to Khiḍr as prophethood. «many understand as a reference to prophethood»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r55] verses 18:65 | Another interpretation makes the mercy a spiritual perfection that frees Khiḍr from base matter and grants perpetual life. «a kind of spiritual perfection that allows him to remain completely unencumbered by base matter»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r56] verses 18:65 | Knowledge from God’s Presence is widely understood as knowledge of the Unseen, which belongs only to God. «which is widely understood to mean knowledge of the Unseen, which lies only with God»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r57] verses 18:65 | The knowledge is inner and universal truth disclosed directly, without human teacher or effort, through divine inspiration. «without the intermediary of a human teacher (K, R, Su)—acquired through Divine inspiration rather than through human effort»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r58] verses 18:65 | Al-Ghazzālī and other Sufis use ʿilm ladunī for knowledge of unveiling, ʿilm al-mukāshafah. «“knowledge from God’s Presence”(ʿilm ladunī; R)»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r59] verses 18:65 | Junayd calls Khiḍr’s knowledge an unveiling of lights from the recesses of the Unseen. «as the “unveiling of lights from the recesses of the Unseen”(Su)»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r60] verses 18:65, 18:10 | Khiḍr’s paired gift of mercy and knowledge echoes the youths’ prayer for mercy from God’s Presence. «echoes the supplication of the youths of the cave in v. 10, where they pray to God seeking mercy from Thy Presence»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r61] verses 18:65, 18:60 | Khiḍr says God gave Moses knowledge withheld from him, while Moses had knowledge Khiḍr lacked; they are two seas of knowledge. «Khiḍr told Moses that God had given him knowledge that He had not given to Moses»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r62] verses 18:65 | Al-Sulamī says Moses surpassed Khiḍr in what he received from God, while Khiḍr surpassed Moses in other knowledge. «although Moses was more knowledgeable than Khiḍr in what Moses had taken from God, Khiḍr was more knowledgeable in other ways»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r63] verses 18:65 | Al-Sulamī contrasts Moses’ subsistence in truth with Khiḍr’s annihilation in it, Sufi stations called baqāʾ and fanāʾ. «although Moses “subsisted” in the truth, Khiḍr was annihilated in it»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r64] verses 18:65 | Khiḍr compares both men’s knowledge to a sparrow’s tiny sip beside God’s knowledge. «My knowledge or your knowledge, compared with the Knowledge of God, is naught but the small amount of water»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r7] verses 18:60, 18:65 | The story can symbolize the difference between exoteric knowledge from scripture or reason and esoteric knowledge received directly from God. «a symbolic account of the difference between formal and exoteric knowledge based upon revealed scripture or reason»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r8] verses 18:60, 18:65 | The story also symbolizes spiritual mastery, discipleship, and levels of the human soul and its training. «a symbolic account of spiritual mastery and discipleship and of the different levels of the human soul»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r1] verses 18:65 | Servanthood lies beyond actions and states, yet exists beneath hidden realities. «قال الجنيد رحمه الله : العبودية خارجة من الأفعال والأحوال ولكنها موجودة تحت الخفيات»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r10] verses 18:65 | Inferential knowledge requires effort and intermediaries, while ladunni knowledge comes without effort or mediation. «قال الهيثم : علم الاستنباط بكلفة ووسائط ، وعلم اللدني بلا كلفة ولا واسطة»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r11] verses 18:65 | Ladunni knowledge is firmly established revelation without doubt or contradiction and an unveiling of hidden realities; it comes to the servant who abandons wrongdoing, movements, and desires, standing before God without claim or will. «قال الجنيد رحمه الله : العلم اللدني ما كان محكما على رسوله من غير ظن فيه ولا خلاف واقع»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r2] verses 18:65 | God sent Moses to Khidr to reveal his true need and prevent self-assurance: Khidr witnessed the King's lights, while Moses witnessed intermediaries. «قال الواسطي رحمه الله : إضافات من أراد أن ينسى النعوت لا تصل إليه بالعبادات والإشارات ألجأ موسى إلى الخضر»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r3] verses 18:65 | The Prophet instructed Ibn Abbas to direct requests to God. «قال النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لابن عباس : ' إذا سألت فاسأل الله '»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r4] verses 18:65 | Khidr taught Moses that asking people is ultimately asking God, so he should not resent the villagers' refusal to host them. «فأخبر الخضر موسى أن السؤال من الناس هو السؤال من الله ، فقال : لا تغضب من المنع»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r5] verses 18:65 | Divinely bestowed knowledge governs people according to the points at which they receive success or abandonment. «قال ذو النون : العلم اللدنى هو الذي يحكم على الخلق بمواقع التوفيق والخذلان»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r6] verses 18:65 | God made knowledge widely available through many distinct paths and proofs; their signs guide each group, while divinely bestowed knowledge returns those who stray to the clear path. «سمعت ذا النون رحمه الله يقول : إن الله بسط العلم ولم يقبضه ودعا الخلق إليه من طرق كثيرة»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r7] verses 18:65 | Ladunni knowledge comes without mediation or instruction in letters, being cast to the recipient through witnessing spirits. «قال ابن عطاء : في قوله : ﴿ وعلمناه من لدنا علما ﴾ قال بلا واسطة المكشوف ، ولا بتلقين الحروف»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r8] verses 18:65 | Ladunni knowledge reaches one's perception completely without an intermediary. «قال فارس : العلم اللدني ما وقع على حسه بالاستيفاء بلا واسطة»
[SULAMI:v1p413#2/r9] verses 18:65 | Ladunni knowledge is inspiration by which the Real anchors the inner secrets so they cannot turn away. «قال الحسين : العلم اللدني إلهام أخلد الحق الأسرار فلم يملكها الانصراف»
[TABATABAI:18:60-82#3/r2] verses 18:65 | A report from Ibn Abbas and Abu Hurayra says Khidr was named for praying on white furwa that turned green. «عن ابن عباس وأبي هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم قال إنما سمي الخضر خضرا»
[TABATABAI:18:65#2/r7] verses 18:65 | Every blessing is divine mercy; outward blessings operate through ordinary causes, while inward gifts such as prophecy and divine friendship are bestowed without them. «ومنها ما لا يتوسط فيه شيء منها كالنعم الباطنيه من النبوة والولاية»
[TABATABAI:18:65-68/r1] verses 18:65 | The majestic plural and angels’ role in revelation support interpreting “mercy from Us” as prophethood rather than divine friendship, which belongs exclusively to God. «وكون النبوة مما للملائكة الكرام فيه عمل كالوحي ونحوه يؤيد أن يكون المراد»
[TABATABAI:18:65-68/r2] verses 18:65 | Knowledge “from Our presence” is gifted rather than acquired through sense, thought, or ordinary causes, and later verses identify it as knowledge of events’ interpretation. «فهو علم وهبي غير اكتسابي يختص به أولياءه وآخر الآيات يدل على أنه كان علما بتأويل الحوادث»
[TABATABAI:18:65/r1] verses 18:65 | The Moses and learned-servant story is a fourth reminder: outward worldly success has a hidden interpretation under divine authority
