[ALUSI-FULL:v1p53/r5] verses 1:1 | Sunni scholars establish the eternal divine-name usage from transmitted evidence, while shifting disputes over created names toward the underlying question of divine speech. «وأهل السنة لما رأوا أن نزاعهم لهم في القسم الأول من الإطلاق الثاني يعود إلى النزاع في منشئه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p54/r1] verses 1:1 | Calligraphers say the ba is lengthened to represent the omitted alif, compensate for it, and begin scripture with a majestic letter; a report attributes penmanship instructions to the Prophet addressing Muʿawiya. «وقال الرسميون طولت لتدل على الألف المحذوفة ولتكون عوضا عنها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p54/r2] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi regards some further calligraphic explanations as opaque or fanciful rather than demonstrative. «ولبعضهم في التعليل ما أدعى أنه ليس من عمل الأفهام بل مبذولات الإلهام»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p54/r3] verses 1:1 | The lexicons differ between deriving Allah from al-ilāh and ilāh; al-Alusi says the hamza was most likely dropped without a regular cause and replaced by the article. «أصله الأعلالي إله كما في الصحاح أو الإله كما في الكشاف»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p54/r4] verses 1:1 | Al-Sayyid al-Sanad treats both forms as names of the divine essence, with al-ilāh usable of another before deletion but Allah exclusive afterward. «هما علم لذاته إلا أنه قبل الحذف قد يطلق على غيره تعالى وبعده لا يطلق على غيره»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p54/r5] verses 1:1 | Al-Saʿd distinguishes al-ilāh as the general concept of the deity worshiped in truth from Allah as the proper name of God. «إن الإله اسم لمفهوم كلي هو المعبود بحق والله علم لذاته تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p54/r6] verses 1:1 | Al-Raḍī says both forms are exclusive to God, though before assimilation the word is an established common proper name and afterward a fully specific proper name. «هما قبل الإدغام وبعده مختصان بذاته تعالى لا يطلقان على غيره أصلا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p54/r7] verses 1:1 | Ibn Malik argues Allah was formed with the article from the outset, not from al-ilāh, because the forms differ in both morphology and meaning. «أن الله من الأعلام التي قارن وضعها أل وليس أصله الإله»

[ALUSI-FULL:v1p55/r4] verses 1:1 | Reported derivations connect ilāh with worship, bewilderment at God’s essence, refuge sought from God, or a young animal’s attachment to its mother; al-Alusi discusses problems with each. «واشتقاقه من أله كعبد إلاهة كعبادة وألوهة كعبودة وألوهية كعبودية»

[ALUSI-FULL:v1p56/r1] verses 1:1 | A mystical derivation takes the name from the pronoun of the absent, then adds the lām of ownership and lengthens the sound; removal of letters is read as leaving li-llah, lahu, and huwa. «وقيل أصله الكناية لأنها للغائب وهو سبحانه الغائب عن أن تدركه الأبصار»
