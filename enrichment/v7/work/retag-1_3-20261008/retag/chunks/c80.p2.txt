[ALUSI-FULL:v15p337/r2] verses 18:71, 18:74 | A non-Prophetic report via Shu'ayb ibn al-Habhab says Khidr was visible only to those God allowed to see him, so the crew saw only Moses and could not stop the damage or killing. «من طريق حماد بن زيد عن شعيب بن الحبحاب أنه قال : كان الخضر عبدا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r1] verses 18:74 | Moses accepted the excuse, and the two left the ship walking along the shore, as in the sound report; another narration says they passed through a village. «الفاء فصيحة أي فقبل عذره فخرجا من السفينة فانطلقا يمشيان على الساحل كما في الصحيح»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r2] verses 18:74 | Reports name the boy Jaysur, also transmitted with ḥāʾ, or Janabtur; one report says he played among ten boys and was the best-looking and cleanest. «يزعمون كما قال البخاري أن اسمه جيسور بالجيم وروي بالحاء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r3] verses 18:74 | Accounts give several methods of killing: pulling off the boy's head, laying him down and slitting his throat, striking his head against a wall, crushing it with a stone, kicking him, or removing his navel. «أخرج البخاري في رواية أنه عليه السلام أخذ برأسه من أعلاه فاقتلعه بيده»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r4] verses 18:74 | Al-Alusi says the reports of striking, laying down and slitting, and then pulling off the head might be combined sequentially, but considers such harmonization remote. «وجمع بين الروايات الثلاثة الأول بأنه ضرب رأسه بالجدار أولا ثم أضجعه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r5] verses 18:74 | The majority take the boy as prepubescent because that is the common literal sense of ghulām; another report from Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz says he was twenty. «والظاهر أن الغلام لم يكن بالغا لأنه حقيقة الغلام بالشائعة في الاستعمال»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r6] verses 18:74 | Another view takes ghulām literally as a sexually mature youth and child usage as figurative; Layla al-Akhyaliyya and another poet use it of young men. «وقيل هو حقيقة في البالغ لأن أصله من الاغتلام وهو شدة الشبق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r7] verses 18:74 | Moses' description of the boy as pure supports childhood because adults rarely remain sinless; Ibn Abbas' Prophetic report glosses zakiyya as 'young,' while the adult view says Moses simply had seen no sin from him. «وقد جاء في حديث عن ابن جبير عن ابن عباس مرفوعا تفسير زكية بصغيرة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r8] verses 18:74 | Those arguing that the boy was an adult cite 'without a soul' as evidence of retaliation, reasoning that a child could not be subject to qisas. «واستدل على كونه بالغا بقوله تعالى ﴿ بغير نفس ﴾ أي بغير حق قصاص لك عليها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p338#2/r9] verses 18:74 | They answer the argument that a child cannot be killed in retaliation: the phrase means killing without right, or singles out retaliation because it suits the context; some scholars say Mosaic law once imposed retaliation for minors. «وأجاب النووي والكرماني بأن المراد التنبيه على أنه قتله بغير حق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339#2/r1] verses 18:74 | Ibn Mardawayh reports from Ubayy ibn Ka'b that Moses was terribly startled when Khidr killed the boy and said he had committed an abhorrent deed. «وقد أخرج ابن مردويه عن أبي كعب أن الخضر عليه السلام لما قتل الغلام ذعر موسى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339#2/r2] verses 18:74 | The Imam defines munkar as what reason rejects and souls recoil from, and considers it more condemnatory than imr; al-Raghib instead defines it as a perilous, difficult unknown. «قال الإمام المنكر ما أنكرته العقول ونفرت عنه النفوس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339#2/r3] verses 18:74 | Al-Tibi takes munkar as less severe than imr because the wording moves from the harsher event to the milder one, though al-Kashf says munkar is stronger in diction and in substance. «واختار الطيبي أنه دون الأمر وقال إن الذي يقتضيه النظم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339#2/r4] verses 18:74 | Al-Kashf argues that killing directly is more abhorrent than indirectly endangering ship passengers; it rejects a descending-severity inference because the episodes are narrated in chronological order. «الظاهر أبلغية النكر أما بحسب اللفظ فظاهر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339#2/r5] verses 18:74 | Qatada is reported to hold that munkar is the more emphatic term. «وروي القول بالابلغية عن قتادة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339#2/r6] verses 18:74 | A narrative says the boy's left shoulder was exposed and bore a writing that he would never believe in God; al-Alusi gives it as a report, not an authenticated account. «حكاه القرطبي عن صاحب العرس والعرائس أن موسى عليه السلام حين قال للخضر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339#2/r7] verses 18:74 | The syntax foregrounds the murder because it is the more heinous act and Moses' objection is especially apt; this explanation assumes the answer to the conditional is the main clause. «إنما غير النظم إلى ما ترى لأن القتل أقبح والاعتراض عليه أدخل»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339/r1] verses 18:74 | These authorities read zākiya with a light yāʾ and an alif after zāy; Zayd ibn Ali, al-Hasan, al-Jahdari, Ibn Amir and the Kufans read the intensive zakiyya without alif. «وقرأ ابن عباس والأعرج وأبو جعفر وشيبة وابن محيصن وحميد والزهري ونافع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339/r2] verses 18:74 | Abu Hayyan says the intensive adjective zakiyya, formed from an active participle, conveys a stronger, settled quality. «كما قال أبو حيان يدل على المبالغة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339/r3] verses 18:74 | Abu Amr distinguishes zākiya as never having sinned from zakiyya as having sinned and then been forgiven; al-Alusi finds this distinction unconvincing. «وفرق أبو عمرو بين زاكية وزكية بأن زاكية بالألف هي التي لم تذنب قط»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339/r4] verses 18:74 | Al-Alusi sees no basis for Abu Amr's distinction: zakā denotes growth and figuratively moral purity; he suggests the alif form may be intransitive and the other passive, making the former stronger for an unpubescent boy. «وتعقب بأنه فرق غير ظاهر لأن أصل معنى الزكاة النمو والزيادة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p339/r5] verses 18:74 | Either reading intensifies the horror of the act by describing the victim as pure. «وأياما كان فوصف النفس بذلك لزيادة تفظيع ما فعل»
