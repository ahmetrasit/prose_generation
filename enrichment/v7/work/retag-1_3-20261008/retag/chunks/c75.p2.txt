[TABRISI:17:109-110/r6] verses 17:110 | Names of divine essence include Living, Knowing, and Powerful; names for good acts include Creator, Provider, Just, and Merciful; al-Ṣamad describes servants turning to God for needs. «فأما أسماؤه المنبئة عن صفات ذاته فهو القادر العالم الحي السميع»
[TABRISI:17:109-110/r7] verses 17:110 | The verse establishes one God despite varied names and attributes, recommends invoking beautiful names before petitions, and supports that He does no evil or injustice. «بين سبحانه في هذه الآية أنه شيء واحد وإن اختلفت أسماؤه وصفاته»
[TABRISI:17:109-110/r8] verses 17:110 | Al-Ḥasan reads the command as avoiding public recitation around those who would harm the Prophet, while not suppressing it around those seeking to hear. «لا تجهر بإشاعة صلاتك عند من يؤذيك ولا تخافت بها عند من يلتمسها منك»
[TABRISI:17:109-110/r9] verses 17:110 | A report says pagans heard the Prophet’s loud prayer, abused him, and caused harm, so he was told not to recite aloud; this was early in Mecca. «كان إذا صلى فجهر في صلاته تسمع له المشركون فشتموه وآذوه»
[TABRISI:17:110-111/r1] verses 17:110 | Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ, and Makḥūl, with a similar report from Ibn ʿAbbās, take ṣalāt to mean supplication: neither call loudly nor whisper. «أن معناه لا تجهر بدعائك ولا تخافت بها ولكن بين ذلك»
[TABRISI:17:110-111/r2] verses 17:110 | Abū Muslim says not to make all prayer loud or quiet: recite night prayers aloud and daytime prayers quietly. «بأن تجهر بصلاة الليل وتخافت بصلاة النهار عن أبي مسلم»
[TABRISI:17:110-111/r3] verses 17:110 | Al-Jubbāʾī says not to recite so loudly as to distract nearby worshippers or so softly that one cannot hear oneself. «لا تجهر جهرا يشغل به من يصلي بقربك ولا تخافت بها»
[TABRISI:17:110-111/r4] verses 17:110 | A report from Abū ʿAbd Allāh defines loudness as forceful voice and quietness as inaudibility to one’s own ears, recommending a middle recitation. «الجهر بها رفع الصوت شديدا والمخافتة ما لم تسمع أذنيك»
[TABRISI:17:110-111/r5] verses 17:110 | The wording says between “that” in the singular because the reference is to the act, as in 2:68. «ولم يقل بين ذينك لأنه أراد به الفعل»
[THALABI:v6p141#2/r1] verses 17:110 | At Abu Sufyan’s house near Safa, Abu Jahl told Muhammad not to fabricate about God; when Muhammad lowered his voice, Abu Jahl boasted he had silenced him, prompting the revelation. «وقال مقاتل: كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يصلي في دار أبي سفيان بن حرب عند الصفا،»
[THALABI:v6p141#2/r10] verses 17:110 | Khafata can describe a dead person when the body has cooled; the prescribed course lies between loudness and whispering. «ومنه يقال للميت إذا برد خفت. وابتغ بين ذلك أي بين الجهر والإخفات سبيلا»
[THALABI:v6p141#2/r2] verses 17:110 | Abu Bakr whispered to privately address God, while Umar recited loudly to drive away Satan and awaken the Lord; the verse made Abu Bakr raise his voice slightly and Umar lower his. «وروى [علقمة] عن ابن سيرين في هذه الآية قال: كان أبو بكر»
[THALABI:v6p141#2/r3] verses 17:110 | She says the verse concerned the tashahhud, when a Bedouin recited it loudly. «وقالت عائشة رضي (رضي الله عنه) : نزلت هذه الآية في التشهد،»
[THALABI:v6p141#2/r4] verses 17:110 | Do not use prayer publicly for show, nor neglect it in private. «وقال الحسن: [لا تراء] بصلاتك في العلانية ولا [تسئها] في السر.»
[THALABI:v6p141#2/r5] verses 17:110 | The command means neither pray ostentatiously before people nor abandon prayer out of fear of them. «الوالبي عن ابن عباس: لا تصل مرائيا الناس، ولا تدعها مخافة الناس،»
[THALABI:v6p141#2/r6] verses 17:110 | People of Scripture whispered so softly no letter was heard; God forbids shouting like them or whispering like them, and Gabriel taught the middle way in prayer. «ابن زيد: كان أهل الكتاب يخافتون في الصلاة، لم يجهر أحدهم بالحرف»
