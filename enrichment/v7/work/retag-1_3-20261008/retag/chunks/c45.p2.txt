[RAZI-FULL:v12p137#2/r2] verses 6:12 | Four explanations are given for “to the Day”: the preposition is extra, means “in,” conceals the gathering place, or describes successive generations continuing until the Day. «أن كلمة { إلى } في قوله { إلى يوم القيامة } فيها أقوال : الأول : أنها صلة»
[RAZI-FULL:v12p137#2/r3] verses 6:12 | Al-Akhfash parses “those who lost themselves” as an accusative appositive to the pronoun in “gather you,” meaning God gathers these idolaters. «وهو قول الأخفش»
[RAZI-FULL:v12p137#2/r4] verses 6:12 | Al-Zajjaj parses the phrase as a nominative subject with “they do not believe” as its predicate; the fa marks a condition-result relation, as in the example about the one who honors me. «وهو قول الزجاج ، أن قوله { الذين خسروا أنفسهم } رفع بالابتداء»

[RAZI-FULL:v12p137/r1] verses 6:12 | The Prophet reports that after completing creation God wrote that His mercy preceded His wrath. «عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه قال : ( لما فرغ الله من الخلق كتب كتابا أن رحمتي سبقت غضبي )»
[RAZI-FULL:v12p137/r2] verses 6:12 | Razi answers the objection that precedence would make divine will temporal by saying the report means precedence in abundance, not in time. «المراد بهذا السبق سبق الكثرة لا سبق الزمان»
[RAZI-FULL:v12p137/r3] verses 6:12 | Salman reports that God created one hundred mercies, kept ninety-nine, and distributed one among creatures, through which they show compassion; at the end the mercy is reserved for the pious. «وعن سلمان أنه تعالى لما خلق السماء والأرض خلق مائة رحمة»
[RAZI-FULL:v12p137/r4] verses 6:12 | The lām in “He will surely gather you” is the lām of an implicit oath: “By God, He will surely gather you.” «الأول : ( اللام ) في قوله { ليجمعنكم } لام قسم مضمر»
[RAZI-FULL:v12p137/r5] verses 6:12 | One view makes the gathering clause independent: mercy is respite in this life, while gathering means later resurrection and accounting for every deed. «فقال بعضهم أنه كلام مبتدأ ،»
[RAZI-FULL:v12p137/r6] verses 6:12 | Another view connects the clause to the preceding statement: God wrote mercy upon Himself and wrote that He would gather people on Judgment Day. «والقول الثاني : أنع متعلق بما قبله والتقدير : كتب ربكم على نفسه الرحمة .»
[RAZI-FULL:v12p137/r7] verses 6:12 | A third reading treats the gathering as an explanation of mercy, since fear of Judgment Day prevents social disorder and is itself a great worldly mercy. «وقيل : أنه لما قال : { كتب ربكم على نفسه الرحمة } فكأنه قيل : وما تلك الرحمة ؟»
