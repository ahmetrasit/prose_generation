# Verses
- 4:1: يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا رِجَالًۭا كَثِيرًۭا وَنِسَآءًۭ ۚ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ٱلَّذِى تَسَآءَلُونَ بِهِۦ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَلَيْكُمْ رَقِيبًۭا

# Notes

## 4:1
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r1] verses 4:1 | The indefinite “men” and “women” conveys abundance, and “many” modifies “men,” reinforcing that abundance; the author rejects as strained taking it as an adjective for an omitted verbal noun. «والتلوين في ( رجالا ونساءا ) للتكثير و ( كثيرا ) نعت ل ( رجالا ) مؤكد لما أفاده التنكير والإفراد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r10] verses 4:1 | Using the majestic name “Allah” instead of a pronoun moves from the attribute of lordship to all attributes of perfection, reinforcing the command to fear Him as creator and deserving of every perfection. «ووضع الأسم الجليل موضع الضمير للإشارة إلى جميع صفات الكمال ترقيا بعد صفة الربوبية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r2] verses 4:1 | Abū al-Baqāʾ analyzes “many” as an adjective for an omitted verbal noun, meaning “a great propagation”; the author calls the alternative adjective analysis contrived. «ونقل أبو البقاء أنه نعت المصدر محذوف أي بثا ( كثيرا )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r3] verses 4:1 | “Men and women” means males and females of any age, not only adults; using the gendered terms may intensify the sense of great numbers by portraying each propagated individual as a source of another. «وليس المراد بالرجال والنساء البالغين والبالغات بل الذكور والإناث مطلقا تجوزا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r4] verses 4:1 | Al-Khaṭīb explains why abundance is stated only for men: wisdom requires women to be more numerous, since a man may have more than one wife whereas a woman cannot have multiple husbands. «إذ للرجل أن يزيد في عصمته على واحدة بخلاف المرأة قاله الخطيب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r5] verses 4:1 | Some infer from the verse that every originated thing must arise from prior matter and that creation from absolute nonbeing is impossible; the author rejects this generalization because the verse only identifies the origins of humanity and its mate. «واحتج بعضهم بالآية على أن الحادث لا يحدث إلا عن مادة سابقة وأن خلق الشيء عن العدم المحض والنفي الصرف محال»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r6] verses 4:1 | A case in which matter is used for a particular wise purpose does not establish that all creation requires matter; this verse says only that humans and their mate were created from the stated origins. «وأجيب بأنه لا يلزم من إحداث شيء في صورة واحدة من المادة لحكمة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r7] verses 4:1 | A reading with “creating” and “spreading” treats the words as coordinated clauses with an omitted subject, since they continue the relative clause; the author contrasts this with coordination of a participle after a finite verb. «وقريءوخالق وباثعلى حذف المبتدأ لأنه صلة لعطفه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r8] verses 4:1 | “Fear God” repeats and confirms the opening command. The author takes both addresses as directed to the Prophet's original audience; alternatives make both addresses specific to Arabs or the second alone specific to them, but he finds that scope-shift incoherent. «( وأتقوا الله الذي تساءلون به ( تكرير للأمر الأول وتأكيد له والمخاطب من بعث إليهم صلى الله عليه وسلم أيضا كما مر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p183/r9] verses 4:1 | Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to identify the addressees in both commands as Arabs because they habitually invoked one another by God. «وقيل : المخاطب هنا وهناك هم العرب كما روى عن إبن عباس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p184/r1] verses 4:1 | Ḥamza received this reading through recognized Qurʾān transmitters, was a trusted and learned imam, and was among the seven whose readings leading authorities judged mutawātir; other early authorities also read it, so attacking him is gravely improper. «وقد قال الإمام أبو حنيفة والثوري ويحيى بن آدم في حقه غلب حمزة الناس على القراءة والفرائض»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p184/r2] verses 4:1 | The author reports that Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Qatāda, Mujāhid, and others also read the genitive form. «بل قرأ به جماعة من غير السبعة كإبن مسعود وإبن عباس وإبراهيم النخعي والحسن البصري وقتادة ومجاهد وغيرهم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p184/r3] verses 4:1 | The objection that a genitive attachment cannot follow a preposition-bound pronoun merely reflects Basran grammar; the author rejects binding readers to that school and cites Abū Ḥayyān, the Kūfans, and Ibn Mālik for its grammatical legitimacy in Arabic prose and verse. «وماذكر من إمتناع العطف على الضمير المجرور هو مذهب البصريين ولسنا متعبدين بإتباعهم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p184/r4] verses 4:1 | Mentioning kinship does aid the command to fear God: if taqwā means care for human rights, maintaining family ties is among them; if it is general, kinship is included within it. «لأن التقوى إن أريد بها تقوى خاصة وهي التي في حقوق العباد التي من جملتها صلة الرحم فالتساؤل بالأرحام مما يقتضيه بلا ريب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p184/r5] verses 4:1 | Some answer the objection based on the prohibition of swearing by other than God by limiting that prohibition to oaths made with a belief that fulfillment is obligatory; they cite the report “He prospers, by his father, if truthful.” «فقد قيل في جوابها : لا نسلم أن الحلف بغير الله تعالى مطلقا منهى عنه بل المنهي عنه ما كان مع إعتقاد وجوب البر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p184/r6] verses 4:1 | Some distinguish a plea asking another “by kinship” as mere entreaty from swearing “by kinship, I will do it”; they say the former is not covered by the prohibition, and reject the claim that the verse merely reports a pre-Islamic practice. «وقد ذكر بعضهم أن قول الشخص لآخر : أسألك بالرحم أن تفعل كذا ليس الغرض منه سوى الإستعطاف»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p184/r7] verses 4:1 | Ibn Jinnī offers a further grammatical analysis of Ḥamza's reading based on an omitted element that context makes understood; the next passage develops the proposal. «وقد خرج إبن جني هذه القراءة على تخريج آخر فقال في الخصائص : باب في أن المحذوف إذا دلت الدلالة عليه كان في حكم الملفوظ به»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r1] verses 4:1 | Following Ibn Jinnī's principle that a contextually evident omission counts as spoken, the author illustrates an omitted “rubb” in a line about a house trace and omitted “bi-khayr” in a greeting; on this analysis Ḥamza's reading omits a preposition already mentioned. «أي رب رسم دار وكان رؤبو»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r10] verses 4:1 | Mujāhid explains “watchful over you” as “preserving you”; al-Rāghib derives the expression from watching in the sense of guarding. «إن الله كان عليكم رقيبا 1 أي حفيظا قاله مجاهد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r11] verses 4:1 | The word may also mean one who observes or knows: Ibn Zayd interprets it as “knowing,” by analogy with a high lookout from which a place is observed; grammatically it is an active participle. «ومن هنا فسره إبن زيد بالعالم وعلى كل فهو فعيل بمعنى فاعل»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r12] verses 4:1 | The closing clause explains why the command and compliance are obligatory; naming God explicitly reinforces that rationale, and fronting the prepositional phrase maintains the verse's rhyme. «والجملة في موضع التعليل للأمر ووجوب الإمتثال وإظهار الأسم الجليل لتأكيده وتقديم الجار لرعاية الفواصل»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r2] verses 4:1 | Al-Zamakhsharī also uses omission of the previously mentioned preposition to explain this reading; the author reports al-Kashf's judgment that this is closer for many Basrans because preposition omission is established in comparable expressions. «وفي شرح المفصل أن الباء في هذه القراءة محذوفة لتقدم ذكرها وقد مشى على ذلك أيضا الزمخشري في أحاجيه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r3] verses 4:1 | Another analysis makes the conjunction an oath, as in “Fear God, for by God He watches over me”; omitting the explanatory fāʾ leaves the stronger independent resumption, a good analysis according to the author. «ونقل عن بعضهم أن الواو للقسم على نحوأتق الله تعالى فوالله إنه مطلع عليكوترك الفاء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r4] verses 4:1 | Ibn Zayd reads “kinship” in the nominative as a subject with an omitted predicate: kinship is likewise to be guarded against severance, or is likewise invoked in mutual requests. «وقرأ إبن زيد ( والأرحام ) بالرفع على أنه مبتدأ محذوف الخبر أي ( والأرحام ) كذلك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r5] verses 4:1 | Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies “worthy of being maintained” as the omitted predicate, while Ibn Jinnī says “among what you must maintain and protect”; the author finds the clause parenthetical on this reading and the coordination somewhat obscure. «وقدره إبن عطية أهل لأن توصلو»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r6] verses 4:1 | Abū Hurayra reports that the Prophet said the womb sought refuge from severance after creation, and God promised to join whoever maintains kinship and sever whoever cuts it; al-Bukhārī and Muslim transmit it. «عن أبي هريرة قال : قال رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم : إن الله تعالى خلق الخلق حتى إذا فرغ منهم قامت الرحم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r7] verses 4:1 | Al-Bazzār transmits with a good chain a report portraying kinship as clinging to the Throne and praying for connection or severance; God answers that He is the Merciful and reciprocates, since He derived kinship from His name. «وأخرج البزار بإسناد حسن الرحم حجنة متمسكة بالعرش تكلم بلسان زلق اللهم صل من وصلني»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r8] verses 4:1 | A report that Aḥmad transmits with a sound chain calls unjust aggression a form of usury, describes kinship as a branch of the All-Merciful, and says God bars Paradise to whoever severs it. «وأخرج الإمام أحمد بإسناد صحيح إن من أربى الربا الإستطالة بغير حق وإن هذه الرحم شجنة من الرحمن»
[ALUSI-FULL:v4p185/r9] verses 4:1 | Kinship covers relatives connected by lineage, however distant, and can refer particularly to maternal relatives; restricting the duty of maintaining it to relatives through the mother's womb is unacceptable because commands to treat relatives well are general. «والمراد بالرحم الأقارب ويقع على كل من يجمع بينك وبينه نسب وإن بعد»
