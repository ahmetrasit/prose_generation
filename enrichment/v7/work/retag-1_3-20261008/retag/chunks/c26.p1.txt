[RAZI-FULL:v1p153/r3] verses 1:2, 1:3 | Since beneficial ease and safety predominate, the agent is merciful and beneficent and deserves praise; these familiar facts make the reason for praising God apparent to every rational person. «ومن كان غالب أفعاله راحة وخيرا وكرامة وسلامة كان رحيما محسنا»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p155/r4] verses 1:3 | The cosmos is ordered with optimal perfection, and even atoms testify to God's mercy, favor, and beneficence, leading the servant to call Him Merciful and Compassionate. «منظومة على الوصف الأتقن والترتيب الأقوم والكمال الأعلى والمنهج الأسنى»

[RAZI-FULL:v1p189#2/r2] verses 1:3 | Ibrāhīm ibn Adham recounts a raven stealing a loaf and carrying it to a bound man, illustrating providential care. «حكي عن إبراهيم بن أدهم أنه قال كنت ضيفا لبعض القوم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p189#2/r3] verses 1:3 | Dhū al-Nūn recounts a scorpion riding a frog across the Nile, then killing a snake that threatened a sleeping youth, illustrating unseen protection. «وروي عن ذي النون أنه قال : كنت في البيت إذ وقعت ولولة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p189#2/r4] verses 1:3 | A raven chick emerges naked and vulnerable; gnats feed it until it grows feathers and its mother returns, prompting the Arab prayer ‘Provider of the nestling in its nest’ and showing universal mercy. «يا رزاق النعاب في عشه»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p189#2/r5] verses 1:3 | Events may look merciful while harming in reality, like a parent neglecting a child, or appear painful while benefiting, like schooling a child or amputating a diseased hand. «فالأبله يغتر بالظواهر والعاقل ينظر في السرائر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p189#2/r6] verses 1:3 | Trials and pain are ultimately wisdom and mercy: abandoning great good to avoid slight evil would be a greater evil, so the wise judge realities rather than appearances. «إن ترك الخير الكثير لأجل الشر القليل شر كثير»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p189#2/r7] verses 1:3 | Religious duties purify spirits from bodily attachments, while Hell redirects the wicked toward righteous action and toward God. «المقصود من التكاليف تطهير الأرواح عن العلائق الجسدانية»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p189#2/r8] verses 1:3 | Moses judged by outward appearances and objected to the boat, boy, and wall, while al-Khiḍr acted on hidden realities; disliked events may conceal divine wisdom and mercy. «فاستنكر تخريق السفينة وقتل الغلام وعمارة الجدار المائل»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p189#2/r9] verses 1:3 | Al-Raḥmān is exclusive to God while al-Raḥīm can describe God or others; the lower name follows because people may hesitate to ask a great Lord for small needs. «الرحمن : اسم خاص بالله والرحيم : ينطلق عليه وعلى غيره»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p190/r1] verses 1:3 | Adding al-Raḥīm invites requests for even ordinary necessities such as a sandal strap or cooking salt; a report says God told Moses to ask Him for salt and fodder. «يا موسى سلني عن ملح قدرك وعلف شاتك»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p190/r2] verses 1:3 | Mary was saved from slander by one divine mercy; worshippers recite the two mercy names 34 times daily in prayer, so al-Rāzī hopes for their rescue from punishment. «فلما صار ذكر الرحمة مرة واحدة سببا لخلاص مريم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p190/r3] verses 1:3 | God is al-Raḥmān because He creates what humans cannot, transforming impure semen into a beautiful form; He is al-Raḥīm because He returns imperfect obedience as a perfect Garden. «أنا رحمن لأنك تسلم إلي نطفة مذرة فأسلمها إليك صورة حسنة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p190/r4] verses 1:3 | A story tells of a dying youth unable to utter the testimony because his mother, whom he had injured, refused to forgive him; when she relents at the threat of burning, his speech returns. «اعتقل لسانه عن شهادة أن لا إله إلا الله»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p190/r5] verses 1:3 | The mother’s limited mercy would not permit burning her son after carrying and nursing him, so God’s boundless mercy should not burn a believer who professed faith for seventy years. «فالرحمن الرحيم الذي لم يتضرر بجنايات عبيده»

[RAZI-FULL:v1p191/r2] verses 1:3 | God tells the Prophet to leave the servants to Him: the Prophet’s single mercy is finite, but God’s mercy is infinite and absorbs every creature’s finite sins. «فرحمتي لا نهاية لها ومعصيتهم متناهية»
