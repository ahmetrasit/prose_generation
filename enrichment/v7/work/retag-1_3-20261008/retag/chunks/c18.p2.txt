[RAZI-FULL:v1p177/r9] verses 1:1 | Abu Sa‘id reports that the Messenger commanded recitation of al-Fatiha in every rak‘a, obligatory or voluntary. «أمرنا رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم أن نقرأ فاتحة الكتاب»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p178/r1] verses 1:1 | Rifa‘a ibn Malik reports the Prophet instructing an uninformed worshipper to praise and magnify God if he has memorized no Qur’an; for one unable to recite even Arabic remembrance, Razi recommends invoking God in any language one can manage. «وإن لم يكن معه شيء من القرآن فليحمد الله وليكبر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p178/r2] verses 1:1 | For the remaining case of knowing no al-Fatiha, Qur’an, or Arabic remembrance, Razi requires whatever remembrance of God is possible in any language, following the command to do as much as one can. «بأي لسان قدر عليه تمسكا بقوله عليه الصلاة والسلام»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p178/r3] verses 1:1 | Reports say Ibn Mas‘ud denied that al-Fatiha and the two protective suras belonged to the Qur’an, creating a dilemma for the status of transmission and his own knowledge. «كان ينكر كون سورة الفاتحة من القرآن وكان ينكر كون المعوذتين من القرآن»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p178/r4] verses 1:1 | If transmission was mass-transmitted in the Companions’ time, Ibn Mas‘ud’s denial would imply disbelief or deficient reason; if not, Qur’an’s transmission would lack certain proof, so Razi judges the attribution false. «نقل هذا المذهب عن ابن مسعود نقل كاذب باطل»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p214#2/r5] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | The seven Fatiha verses remove seven vices: praise dispels desire, Lord of the worlds greed, Judgment Day anger, worship and aid pride and vanity, guidance caprice, the favored path unbelief and doubt, and the final phrase innovation. «فثبت أن هذه الآيات السبع دافعة لتلك الأخلاق القبيحة السبعة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p215#2/r1] verses 1:1 | The Qur'an uses human creation to prove the Creator, as in the command to worship the Lord who created people and the first revelation about creating humanity from a clot. «استدل بخلق الإنسان على وجود الصانع تعالى»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p215#2/r2] verses 1:1 | The proof from creation is also a blessing: it teaches the servant that God exists and gives a great benefit, so it is both evidence and grace. «فهذه الحالة من حيث أنها تعرف العبد وجود الإله دليل ومن حيث أنها نفع عظيم وصل من الله إلى العبد إنعام»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p215#2/r3] verses 1:1 | The varied organs formed from similar semen show that a knowing, powerful Creator deliberately shapes them for human interests through mercy, making Him worthy of praise. «تولد الأعضاء المختلفة الطبائع والصور والأشكال من النطفة المتشابهة الأجزاء»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p215#2/r4] verses 1:1 | “Praise belongs to God” indicates the Creator's existence, knowledge, power, mercy, perfect wisdom, and entitlement to praise and glorification. «يدل على وجود الصانع وعلى علمه وقدرته ورحمته وكمال حكمته»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p215/r1] verses 1:1, 1:7 | Al-Fatiha gathers what people need to know about the origin, the middle of servitude, and the return; Razi begins its proof of the Creator from human creation, citing Abraham and Moses. «في تقرير أن سورة الفاتحة جامعة لكل ما يحتاج الإنسان إليه في معرفة المبدأ والوسط والمعاد»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p215/r2] verses 1:1 | The Qur'an repeatedly establishes the Creator by reflecting on human creation: Abraham says his Lord gives life and death and created him, and Moses says his Lord gave each thing its form and guided it. «ألا ترى أن إبراهيم عليه السلام قال»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r1] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | Razi supports obligatory Fatiha recitation because the Prophet consistently recited it and commands require following him. «صلوا كما رأتيموني أصلي . وثانيها»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r10] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | The transmitted report itself says that prayer is not established without the Fatiha. «أن هذا الخبر الذي رويناه يدل على أن عند فقدان الفاتحة لا تحصل الصلاة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r2] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | The rightly guided caliphs also consistently recited it, and the Prophet commanded Muslims to follow their sunna. «أن الخلفاء الراشدين واظبوا على قراءتها»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r3] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | Muslims everywhere recite it in prayer, and the Qur'an commands following the path of believers. «أن جميع المسلمين شرقا وغربا لا يصلون إلا بقراءة الفاتحة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r4] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | The prophetic statement “no prayer without the Opening of the Book” directly supports the requirement. «لا صلاة إلا بفاتحة الكتاب»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r5] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | The command to recite what is easy from the Qur'an is obligatory; because reciting other passages is not, the obligatory recitation must be the Fatiha. «وظاهره الوجوب فكانت قراءة ما تيسر من القرآن واجبة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r6] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | Reciting the Fatiha is the safer course, following the prophetic instruction to leave what causes doubt for what does not. «أن قراءة الفاتحة أحوط فوجب المصير إليها»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r7] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | The Prophet's constant practice makes abandoning his command forbidden, and Muslims agree that the Fatiha is better and more complete than another recitation. «وسابعها : أن الرسول عليه السلام واظب على قراءتها فوجب أن يكون العدول عنه محرما»
