[RAZI-FULL:v1p162#2/r2] verses 1:1 | Razi argues that if reading the basmala at the start of al-Fatiha is obligatory, it must be one of its verses; Quran 96:1 commands reading in God's name and the letter has meaning. «بيان الأول قوله تعالى : ﴿ اقرأ باسم ربك ﴾ [ العلق : 1 ] ولا يجوز أن يقال : الباء صلة زائدة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p162#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Because the basmala is written in Quranic script, while headings and division marks were kept out to prevent mixing non-Quranic text, its inscription marks it as Quran. «التسمية مكتوبة بخط القرآن وكل ما ليس من القرآن فإنه غير مكتوب بخط القرآن»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p162#2/r4] verses 1:1 | Since Muslims agree that everything between the two covers is God's speech and the basmala is there, Razi counts it as divine speech; he notes this does not refute Abu Bakr al-Razi's view that it is Quran only as a surah divider. «أجمع المسلمون على أن ما بين الدفتين كلام الله والتسمية موجودة بين الدفتين»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p162#2/r5] verses 1:1, 1:7 | The seven-verse count supports al-Shafi'i's division, with the basmala as one verse and the final phrase as one; Abu Hanifa denies the basmala verse and splits that final phrase, a weak view for Razi. «وسنبين في مسألة مفردة أن قول أبي حنيفة مرجوح ضعيف»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p162#2/r6] verses 1:1 | Because Abu Hanifa agrees the basmala is preferable and the Prophet likely recited it, following him makes it obligatory; with no accepted distinction, it must be part of al-Fatiha. «وإذا ثبت وجوب قراءتها ثبت أنها من السورة لأنه لا قائل بالفرق»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p162/r1] verses 1:1 | A report from Abu Huraira says the Prophet told believers not to omit the basmala when reciting the Mother of the Book because it is one of its verses. «أنه قال إذا : قرأتم أم القرآن فلا تدعوا بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم فإنها إحدى آياتها»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p162/r2] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | In the divided-prayer report, God calls the basmala glorification, al-Hamd praise, the two mercy names extolling, the Master of Judgment entrustment, and the remaining response a sharing and a request granted. «فإذا قال العبد بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم قال الله سبحانه مجدني عبدي وإذا قال الحمد لله رب العالمين»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p162/r3] verses 1:1 | Another report says the Prophet rebuked a worshipper who began al-Fatiha without the basmala: omitting a verse cuts the prayer, which is invalid without the whole surah. «يا رجل قطعت على نفسك الصلاة أما علمت أن بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم من الحمد»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p163/r1] verses 1:1 | The Prophet's saying that an important matter begun without God's name is cut off implies that prayer, the greatest act after faith, is gravely deficient without the basmala; such acknowledged deficiency entails invalidity. «كل أمر ذي بال لا يبدأ فيه باسم الله فهو أبتر أو أجذم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p163/r2] verses 1:1 | The severity of abtar is shown by Quran 108:3, where God calls the Prophet's enemy cut off; thus prayer without the basmala is gravely defective and invalid. «فلزم أن يقال : الصلاة الخالية عن قراءة بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم تكون في غاية النقصان والخلل»

Ka'b names the basmala as the greatest ayah and the Prophet confirms him, Razi argues it must be a complete ayah somewhere other than its partial appearance in Solomon's letter, namely at al-Fatiha's start. «ما روي أن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال لأبي بن كعب : ما اعظم آية في كتاب الله تعالى»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p164/r2] verses 1:1 | When Muawiya omitted the basmala aloud in prayer, the Migrants and Helpers rebuked him and he repeated the prayer; Razi takes this as Companion consensus that it is Quran and part of al-Fatiha, and that aloud recitation is preferable. «فلما قضى صلاته ناداه المهاجرون والأنصار من كل ناحية أنسيت ؟ أين بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم حين استفتحت القرآن»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p164/r3] verses 1:1 | Razi argues that Noah began boarding the Ark in God's name and Solomon's letter began with the basmala; following earlier prophets and then the Prophet makes its recitation obligatory for Muslims and part of al-Fatiha. «أن نوحا عليه السلام لما أراد ركوب السفينة قال : ﴿ اركبوا فيها بسم الله مجراها ومرساها ﴾»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p164/r4] verses 1:1 | Razi rejects the suggestion that Solomon put his own name before God's: the hoopoe laid the letter on the queen, who first identified its sender as Solomon and only after opening it read the basmala. «فلما فتحت الكتاب رأت التسمية مكتوبة فقالت : وإنه بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم»
