# Notes

## 39:6
[TABRISI:39:6-10/r11] verses 39:6 | Eve was made from the surplus of Adam's clay; another view says she came from one of his ribs. «يعني حواء أي من فضل طينته. وقيل: من ضلع من أضلاعه»
[TABRISI:39:6-10/r12] verses 39:6 | One explanation takes “then” as ordinary sequence in the wording even though the second event actually preceded the first; a poem supplies a parallel. «أنه عطف يوجب أن الكلام الثاني بعد الأول ويجري مجرى قول القائل»
[TABRISI:39:6-10/r13] verses 39:6 | A second explanation coordinates to the meaning: God created one soul alone and then made its spouse from it. «أنه معطوف على معنى واحد فكأنه قال خلقكم من نفس واحدة أوجدها وحدها»
[TABRISI:39:6-10/r14] verses 39:6 | A third view says descendants were created as tiny beings from Adam's back before Eve was made from his rib; the author calls this weak. «وثالثها: أنه خلق الذرية في ظهر آدم وأخرجها من ظهره كالذر»
[TABRISI:39:6-10/r6] verses 39:6 | In “that is God, your Lord,” “that” is a subject, “God” an explanatory apposition, and “your Lord” a substitute or predicate. «ذلكم مبتدأ والله عطف بيان وربكم بدل من لفظة الله»
[TABRISI:39:6-10/r7] verses 39:6 | “To Him belongs sovereignty” may be circumstantial, with sovereignty raised by the adverb, or may be a second predicate. «له الملك يرتفع الملك بالظرف والظرف مع ما ارتفع به في موضع الحال»
[TABRISI:39:6-10/r8] verses 39:6 | The clause “there is no deity but Him” may be circumstantial, meaning uniquely one, or another predicate. «جاز أن يكون في موضع الحال أي متوحدا بالوحدانية وجاز أن يكون خبرا آخر»
[TABRISI:39:6-10/r9] verses 39:6 | “How are you turned away?” is parsed as a circumstantial accusative or verbal noun and means “how.” «فأنى تصرفون أنى في موضع نصب على الحال أو على المصدر»
[THALABI:v8p221#3/r6] verses 39:6 | The eight pairs of livestock are kinds and individual animals; the creation in the womb proceeds through sperm, clinging clot, then lump of flesh. «أصناف وأفراد، تفسيرها في سورة الأنعام»
[THALABI:v8p221#3/r7] verses 39:6 | Ibn Zayd takes the successive creation in the womb to mean creation after the first creation in Adam's back. «معناه يخلقكم في بطون أمهاتكم من بعد الخلق الأول الذي خلقكم في ظهر آدم»
[THALABI:v8p221#3/r8] verses 39:6 | The three darknesses are the abdomen, womb, and placenta. «في ظلمات ثلاث يعني البطن والرحم والمشيمة»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:39:6-7/r2] verses 39:6 | Humanity was created from Adam and his spouse Eve; livestock were sent down as eight pairs, explained in Surat al-An'am. «خلقكم من نفس واحدة } يعني: آدم عليه السلام { ثم جعل منها زوجها } حواء»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:39:6-7/r3] verses 39:6 | Human creation proceeds through sperm, then a clinging clot, then a lump, in three darknesses: the abdomen, womb, and placenta; this should turn people to God's worship. «أي: نطفة، ثم علقة، ثم مضغة { في ظلمات ثلاث } ظلمة البطن، وظلمة الرحم، وظلمة المشيمة»
[ZAJJAJ:v4p344/r4] verses 39:6 | ثم marks sequence: the one soul is Adam and his spouse Eve, made from him before the rest of humankind. «والنفس الواحدة يعني بها آدم - صلى الله عليه وسلم - وزوجها حواء.»
[ZAJJAJ:v4p345/r1] verses 39:6 | The eight livestock pairs are male and female camels, cattle, sheep, and goats; each member of a pair is itself called a زوج. «من الإبل ذكرا وأنثى، ومن البقر ذكرا وأنثى ومن الضأن كذلك
ومن المعز ذكرا وأنثى»
[ZAJJAJ:v4p345/r2] verses 39:6 | Creation proceeds from a drop, clot, morsel, bones, flesh covering the bones, then formation and the breathing of the spirit. «نطفا ثم علقا ثم مضغا ثم عظاما ثم تكسى العظام لحما، ثم تصور
وتنفخ فيها الروح»
[ZAJJAJ:v4p345/r3] verses 39:6 | The three darknesses are the belly, womb, and placenta; another view identifies them as the loins, womb, and belly. «في البطن، والرحم، والمشيمة.
وقد قيل في الأصلاب والرحم والبطن»
[ZAJJAJ:v4p345/r4] verses 39:6 | The one who manages creation in this way is God, unlike anything else. «المعنى الذي دبر الخلق هذا التدبير ليس كمثله شيء»
[ZAJJAJ:v4p345/r5] verses 39:6 | فأنى تصرفون asks how people can turn from the path of truth after this evidence for divine oneness, like the expression فأنى تؤفكون. «فمن أين تصرفون عن طريق الحق، مثل: (فأنى تؤفكون)
أي فكيف تعدلون عن الحق بعد هذا البيان الذي يدل على صحة التوحيد»

## 40:7
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v3p178#7/r1] verses 40:7 | Qatadah quotes Muṭarrif ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Shikhkhīr: angels are God’s most sincere advisers to His servants, while devils are the most deceitful. «قال مطرف بن عبد الله بن الشخير وجدنا أنصح عباد الله لعباد الله الملائكة ووجدنا أغش عباد الله لعباد الله الشياطين»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v3p178#7/r2] verses 40:7 | Qatadah says the believers’ repentance here is repentance from shirk. «عن قتادة في قوله تعالى ﴿ فاغفر للذين تابوا ﴾ قال تابوا من الشرك»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v3p178#7/r3] verses 40:7 | Qatadah explains following God’s path as obeying Him. «عن قتادة في قوله تعالى ﴿ فاغفر للذين تابوا ﴾ قال تابوا من الشرك ﴿ تابوا واتبعوا ﴾ أي طاعتك»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p237/r1] verses 40:7 | After the disbelievers' dispute, the passage turns to the obedience of God's chosen creatures: the throne bearers and angels around it. «لما ذكر جدال الكفار في آيات الله وعصيانهم، ذكر طاعة هؤلاء المصطفين من خلقه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p237/r2] verses 40:7 | Reports about the angels' immense bodies, the throne's nature, and seventy different veils need sound transmission; God's power could encompass non-corporeal accounts, but the author requires reliable evidence. «لكنه يحتاج إلى نقل صحيح»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p237/r3] verses 40:7 | Ibn Abbas and a group read العرش with a damma, treating it as a plural like سقف or as a dialectal form. «وابن عباس وفرقة: بضمها، كأنه جمع عرش»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p237/r4] verses 40:7 | The angels' glorification declares God free of every defect, while praise recognizes him as the unrestricted giver of blessings; glorification signals awe and praise signals honor. «يسبحون بحمد ربهم: أي ينزهونه عن جميع النقائص»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p237/r5] verses 40:7 | The author links the phrase to God's name combining majesty and honor, to angels around the throne praising him, and to the angels' words in the creation account. «فهو قريب من قوله: تبارك اسم ربك ذي الجلال والإكرام»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p237/r6] verses 40:7 | The angels' belief means affirming God's existence and the exalted attributes he ascribes to himself; their glorification already entails belief. «ويؤمنون: أي ويصدقون بوجوده تعالى وبما وصف به نفسه من صفاته العلا»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p237/r7] verses 40:7 | Al-Zamakhshari says mentioning the angels' belief highlights faith's honor and encourages it, just as scripture describes prophets as righteous and follows good deeds with faith. «قال الزمخشري: فإن قلت: ما فائدة قوله: ويؤمنون به»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p237/r8] verses 40:7 | Al-Zamakhshari also argues that if God were embodied as corporealists claim, the angels would see him and would not be described as believing; their faith, like human faith, is by reasoning about the unseen. «في أن إيمان الجميع بطريق النظر والاستدلال لا غير»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r1] verses 40:7 | The sequence links the angels' belief to their prayer for believers: shared faith is the strongest cause of counsel and compassion despite different species and distant places. «وفيه تنبيه على أن الاشتراك في الإيمان يجب أن يكون أدعى شيء إلى النصيحة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r2] verses 40:7 | There is no natural kinship between angels and humans or between heaven and earth, yet shared faith creates real affinity that moves throne-side angels to seek forgiveness for earthly people. «حتى استغفر من حول العرش لمن فوق الأرض»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r3] verses 40:7 | The author praises al-Zamakhshari's discussion but questions his claim that all faith is reached by rational inference alone. «إلا أن قوله: إن إيمان الجميع بطريق النظر والاستدلال لا غير فيه نظر»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r4] verses 40:7 | The angels' prayer specifically for believers narrows the more general wording that they seek forgiveness for those on earth. «تخصيص لعموم قوله: ويستغفرون لمن في الأرض»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r5] verses 40:7 | Mutarrif ibn al-Shikhkhir says angels are the most sincere of creatures toward people and devils the most deceitful, citing this verse. «وقال مطرف بن الشخير: وجدنا أنصح العباد للعباد الملائكة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r6] verses 40:7 | The author corrects Mutarrif's comparison by adding prophets to the most sincere servants of people. «وينبغي أن يقال: أنصح العباد للعباد الأنبياء والملائكة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r7] verses 40:7 | The angels' words ربنا وسعت كل شيء may be an explanatory clause for their seeking forgiveness or a circumstantial clause; calling God Rabb expresses the servant's appeal to the one who nurtured and cared for him. «واحتمل هذا المحذوف بيانا ليستغفرون، فيكون في محل رفع»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r8] verses 40:7 | رحمة وعلما are accusative specifications; the underlying sense is that God's mercy and knowledge encompass everything, expressed as though they were his very essence. «وانتصب رحمة وعلما على التمييز، والأصل: وسعت رحمتك كل شيء»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p238/r9] verses 40:7 | Mercy precedes knowledge because the angels invoke it to seek God's favor and the forgiveness they request. «وقدم الرحمة، لأنهم بها يستمطرون إحسانه»
[AKHFASH:v2p498#2/r3] verses 40:7 | رحمة وعلما are accusative explanatory terms after وسعت takes كل شيء as its object, like لك مثله عبدا, whose pronoun occupies the verb's object slot. «وقال {وسعت كل شيء رحمة وعلما} فانتصابه كانتصاب "لك مثله عبدا"»
