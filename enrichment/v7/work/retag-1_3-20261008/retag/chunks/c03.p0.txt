# Verses
- 1:1: بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

# Notes

## 1:1
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p48/r1] verses 1:1 | Al-Farra, with a report from Ibn Abbas, prefers an imperative addressed to people to begin in God's name, because God placed the basmala first to urge this practice. «جعل المقدر فعل أمر متوجه إلى العباد ليتحد قائل الملفوظ والمقدر وأختاره الفراء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p48/r2] verses 1:1 | Grammarians generally estimate a broad verb such as I begin; Alusi gives three reasons: applicability to any basmala, direct fulfillment of its purpose, and alignment with the hadith about matters begun without God's name. «وذهب النحويون إلى تقديره عاما نحو أبتديء وأيد بوجوه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p48/r3] verses 1:1 | Alusi prefers Ibn Jarir's specific recitation verb because it makes the whole recitation occur with God's assistance, whereas beginning only links the formula to the initial moment. «وما ذهب إليه الإمام أمس وأخص بالمقصود وأتم شمولا فإنه يقتضي أن القراءة واقعة بكمالها مقرونة بالتسمية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p48/r4] verses 1:1 | He says grammarians' use of general being or stability as examples does not prove a general verb belongs here, since those are illustrative examples where no specific governor is intended. «فليس بجيد لأنهم فعلوه تمثيلا حيث لا يقصدون عاملا بعينه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p48/r5] verses 1:1 | Alusi says beginning here is specifically the beginning of recitation, not a generic action; the basmala itself already makes the reciter a beginner, so no hidden act is needed to establish that. «لأن المراد به إبتداء القراءة وهو أخص من القراءة لصدقها على قراءة الأول والوسط والآخر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p48/r6] verses 1:1 | Alusi says the formula itself begins the work without an elided verb; the hadith does not require every important act to contain an explicit or implied verb. «ولم يرد الحديث بأن كل أمر ذي بال لم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p49/r1] verses 1:1 | Alusi says the dispute is between a general verb like begin and a specific verb like recite, so arguments against recite alone do not settle which estimate is stronger. «وأيضا البحث إنما هو في ترجيح تقدير الفعل العام كأبدأ أو أشرع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p49/r2] verses 1:1 | Some estimate a noun such as my beginning, but this requires an additional omitted predicate; the noun's static meaning also competes with the contextually apt ongoing aspect of a present-tense verb. «وذهب البعض إلى تقدير إبتدائي مثلا وفيه زيادة إضمار لوجوب إضمار الخبر حينئذ»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p49/r3] verses 1:1 | Ibn Arabi interprets the phrase esoterically as the predicate of an omitted subject, the world's beginning and manifestation through divine names, and Alusi says objecting to him is unfair. «وجعل الشيخ الأكبر قدس سره هذا الجار خبر مبتدأ مضمر هو إبتداء العالم وظهوره»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p49/r4] verses 1:1 | Many prefer estimating the verb after the basmala because God's name precedes the act in essence; Alusi links this to an a priori proof and cites a gnostic who sees God before every thing. «قال بعض العارفين ما رأيت شيئا إلا ورأيت الله قبله»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p49/r5] verses 1:1 | Alusi says preposing God's name can sever claims of self-power and makes sense for the messenger's mission, while the first revelation preposes the verb because its context was prophethood rather than disputation or public preaching. «وظهر سر تقديم الفعل في أول آية نزلت إذ المقام إذ ذاك مقام نبوة ولا رد ولا تبليغ فيها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p49/r6] verses 1:1 | Alusi prefers estimating a preceding verb: putting it after ba, ism, Allah, or the attributes would split grammatical units or make mercy appear an exclusive cause of recitation. «وعندي هنا يقدر مقدما وبه قال الأكثرون وإن تقديره مؤخرا مؤخر عن ساحة التحقيق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p49/r7] verses 1:1 | With a preceding verb, the formula still implies restriction: one reads with God's help or blessing because He is the Merciful, excluding those outside that stated cause in this rhetorical context. «أقرأ مستعينا أو متبركا بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم لأنه الرحمن الرحيم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p49/r8] verses 1:1 | Alusi offers a mystical reading: mentally the created act precedes its omission in speech, while only God's pre-eternal name appears, hinting at the fixed archetypes in divine knowledge and God's absolute priority. «رمز خفي إلى تقديم الأعيان الثابتة في العلم وإن لم يكن على وجود الله تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p50/r1] verses 1:1 | Some gnostics say they see God in everything without believing in indwelling; Alusi calls this a higher rank than seeing God before things and mentions four spiritual ranks. «قال ما رأيت شيئا إلا ورأيت الله تعالى فيه ولا حلول»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p50/r2] verses 1:1 | Scholars use mustaqirr and laghw differently for an adverbial phrase: by whether its governor is expressed, whether it is a general implied verb, or whether its governor is outside or understood from the phrase. «والظرف مستقر عند بعض ولغو عند آخرين وقد أختلف في تفسيرهما»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p50/r3] verses 1:1 | Since the terminology is conventional, Alusi adopts the distinction that makes this phrase mustaqirr regardless of the estimated verb. «وحيث لا مشاحة فيه إختار الأول فيكون الظرف هنا مستقرا كيفما قدر العامل»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p50/r4] verses 1:1 | Grammarians explain the ba's kasra as a compromise: particles tend toward light, often-used fatha, but ba's particle status favors kasra and its genitive effect matches that vowel. «إلا أنها من حيث كونها كلمات برأسها مظنة للإبتداء وهو بالساكن متعذر أو متعسر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p50/r5] verses 1:1 | The explanation distinguishes ba from conjunction waw and comparative kaf, which have only one relevant feature, while oath waw and ta borrow their genitive function from the original ba. «ولا نقض بواو العطف اللازمة للحرفية ولا بكاف التشبيه اللازمة للجر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p50/r6] verses 1:1 | Alusi says these grammatical rationales were devised after the fact merely to suggest a fitting pattern, so they cannot bear forceful scrutiny. «وهذه علل نحوية مستخرجة بعد الوقوع لإبداء مناسبة فلا تتحمل مناقشة لضعفها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51#2/r1] verses 1:1 | The basmala subtly honors Muhammad through the description of him as compassionate to believers; this early allusion anticipates his later praise and invites attention to God’s mercy. «ناسب ظهور الكسر فيما يشير إلى مرتبته»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51#2/r2] verses 1:1 | Every surah except Baraʾa opens with an allusion to Muhammad; Baraʾa omits it because divine majesty dominates, though the Muhammadan allusion is not wholly absent. «وما من سورة إلا أفتتحها الرب بالرمز إلى حاله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم تعظيما له»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Basran grammarians count ism among nouns whose first letter is quiescent, requiring a helping hamza when pronounced initially. «الأسم عند البصريين من الأسماء العشرة التي بنيت أوائلها على السكون»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51#2/r4] verses 1:1 | The Basran derivation traces ism to sumuw, elevation: its referent rises from obscurity to visibility; the waw was dropped for ease and frequent use. «وأصله سمو حذفت الواو تخفيفا لكثرة الاستعمال ولتعاقب الحركات»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51#2/r5] verses 1:1 | The Kufans derive ism from wasm, a mark identifying its referent; al-Alusi rejects the derivation because its compensatory hamza and morphology lack a sound parallel. «وقال الكوفيون هو من السمة لأنه علامة على مسماه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51#2/r6] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi favors the first derivation, from elevation, citing lexical testimony; he also notes competing forms of ism, including an eighteen-form enumeration. «فلذا قيل لا حذف ولا تعويض وإنما قلبت الواو همزة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51#2/r7] verses 1:1 | Ashʿarites hold that a name is identical with its named referent, while Muʿtazilites hold that it is distinct; al-Alusi says the debate’s exact subject remained unsettled. «الأشاعرة على الأول والمعتزلة على الثاني وقد تحير نحارير الفضلاء في تحرير محل البحث»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51/r1] verses 1:1 | A symbolic interpreter says the kasra of the basmala's ba teaches that divine knowledge is reached through humility and servitude; Ibn al-Farid's verse makes lowering a cause of elevation by God's gift, not human stratagem. «كسرت الباء في البسملة تعليما للتوصل إلى الله تعالى والتعلق بأسمائه بكسر الجناب والخضوع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51/r2] verses 1:1 | Ibn al-Farid's poem says that a lowered point of ba can raise the seeker beyond what effort could attain, provided one recognizes one's own humility and dependence. «ولو كنت لي من نقطة الباء خفضة رفعت إلى ما لم تنله بحيلة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51/r3] verses 1:1 | Alusi limits this symbolism to the ba of the basmala and says it cannot be applied to every genitive ba. «وهو أمر مخصوص بباء البسملة لا يمكن أن يجري في باء الجر مطلقا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51/r4] verses 1:1 | Alusi's own symbolism makes ba the second rank after the simple alif, a sign of true existence; he recalls al-Shibli saying he was the point beneath ba and quotes Ibn Arabi on its secret of servitude. «وعندي في سر ذلك أن الباء هي المرتبة الثانية بالنسبة إلى الألف البسيطة المجردة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51/r5] verses 1:1 | Because mercy precedes wrath, Alusi reads the genitive ba as a sign of divine beauty and mercy, with its kasra symbolizing humility and preparing the way for effusion. «وللأولى السبق كما يشير إليه حديث سبقت رحمتي غضبي وباء الجر إشارة إليها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51/r6] verses 1:1 | Beginning with ba hastens glad tidings and signifies that mercy is central; a hadith says no one enters Paradise by deeds alone, but only through God's mercy, even the Prophet. «وفي الإبتداء بها هنا تعجيل للبشارة ورمز إلى أن المدار هو الرحمة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51/r7] verses 1:1 | Alusi links the basmala's ba to the Muhammadan reality and first determination, saying divine effusion came through the Prophet and citing reports that his light was created first and the cosmos for his sake. «وأما إشارة إلى الحقيقة المحمدية والتعين الأول المشار إليه بقوله صلى الله علي»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p51/r8] verses 1:1 | The Prophet's predominating quality was mercy, especially toward the believing community, as the Qur'an calls him a mercy to the worlds. «ولكون الغالب عليه عليه الصلاة والسلام صفة الرحمة لا سيما على مؤمني الأمة»
