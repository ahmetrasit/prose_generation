# Verses
- 1:1: بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

# Notes

## 1:1
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r20] verses 1:1 | Rahman and Rahim are not repeated merely for emphasis: each has its own foundational meaning and distinct mode of intensification. «“Rahmân” ile “Rahîm” başka başka birer ma‘nâ-yı rahmeti hâiz»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r21] verses 1:1 | Rahman is more emphatic in encompassing every creature, while Rahim is more emphatic in actual beneficence, which also operates through servants. «Bir cihetten rahmet-i Rahmân eblağdır, çünkü her mahlûka muzâf olur. Diğer cihetten rahmet-i Rahîm eblağdır»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r22] verses 1:1 | Some commentaries associate Rahman's mercy with great blessings and Rahim's with the subtleties of blessings. «Bazı tefsirlerde de buna işâreten “rahmet-i Rahmân celâil-i ni‘am, rahmet-i Rahîm ise dekāik-i ni‘am ile alâkadardır” derler»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r23] verses 1:1 | The grammatical pattern distinguishes them: Rahman's subject is restricted while its object is general; Rahim's subject is general while its object is restricted. «“Rahmân”ın mevsûfu hâs, müte‘allakı âmdır. “Rahîm”in mevsûfu âm, müte‘allakı hâstır»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r24] verses 1:1 | Together these attributes replace pessimism with boundless optimism, the spirit of faith; the first benefit received from the basmala is this hope, affirmed by 28:83. «Rahmân-ı Rahîm’i münkir olan kâfir istediği kadar bedbin olsun, fakat mü’minin bedbinliğine hiçbir sebep yoktur»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r3] verses 1:1 | Primordial creation is compelled and bestowed: stones, trees, and humans have their kinds through this unchosen mercy. «Taşın taş, ağacın ağaç, insanın insan olması böyle bir rahmet-i cebriyenin eseridir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r4] verses 1:1 | All realms and beings, from obedient and believing to rebellious and idolatrous, are immersed in Rahman's mercy and freed from fear in that respect. «âlemlerin hepsi rahmet-i Rahmân’a müstağraktır ve bu haysiyetle mehâfetten âzâdedir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r5] verses 1:1 | If this universal grace were all, distinctions between knowledge and ignorance, life and death, effort and idleness, obedience and disobedience, and faith and unbelief would disappear. «Lâkin bu kadarla kalsa idi, ilim ile cehlin, hayât ile mevtin, sa‘y ile atâletin, itaat ile isyanın»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r6] verses 1:1 | Without a further mercy enabling agency, people would be determinists and compelled beings, unable to act on what they know or desire. «hep tabîî olurduk, tabi‘iyyûndan, cebriyyûndan olurduk»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r7] verses 1:1 | God's wisdom requires that He display His own will and create creatures who possess will, rather than make all action compulsory. «Cenâb-ı Allah’ın kendi irade ve ihtiyârını göstermesi ve onun eseri olarak sâhib-i irâde mahlûkāt halk etmesi»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r8] verses 1:1 | God advances creatures according to good choices and grants enjoyment of mercy, while bad choices bring deprivation, pain, and punishment; this harmonizes their wills with His. «hüsn-i irade ve ihtiyarlarına göre terakki ettirerek rahmetinden mütena‘‘im ve müstefid ve bilakis sû’-i irade ve kesblerine göre mahrûmiyetle, elem ve ‘ikāb ile mu‘âteb»
[ELMALILI:1:1#7/r9] verses 1:1 | Rahman is the instinctive grace seen in mother birds beating their wings over their young. «Ana kuşlar eser-i rahmâniyet olan sevk-i fıtrî ile yavrularının başında kanat çırpar»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r1] verses 1:1 | The basmala has an apparent structure of one genitive relation, two adjectival relations, and an initial attachment; its hidden relation makes it a complete, concise statement. «Besmele zâhirde bir nisbet-i izâfiye ile iki nisbet-i vasfiyye ve başında bir nisbet-i ta‘alluktan mürekkeb»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r10] verses 1:1 | This fronting makes the beginning exclusive to Allah's name, rejecting the pagan practice of starting with Lat or Uzza and expressing tawhid. «Arap müşrikleri de sözlerine veya işlerine بِاسْمِ اللَاَّتِ بِاسْمِ الْعُزَّی gibi putlarından birinin ismiyle başlarlardı»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r11] verses 1:1 | Turkish word order cannot readily preserve the Arabic exclusivity and the direct joining of the spoken formula to the act; an implied construction can show tawhid but is awkward and loses concision. «Bunda ise fi‘l-i kavlînin, fi‘l-i hakīkīye mukāreneti temin edilmemiş olur»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r12] verses 1:1 | He proposes eight possible Turkish renderings, using both lamiyya and bayaniyya forms such as Allah's name with the two attributes or Allah, Rahman, Rahim as one name. «7. Allâh-ı Rahmân-i Rahîm ismiyle»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r2] verses 1:1 | Ism Allah may be read as a genitive meaning either Allah's name or the name Allah. «beyâniye olabilir ki evvelkinde “Allah’ın ismi”, ikincide “Allah ismi” demek olur»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r3] verses 1:1 | On the lamiyya reading the adjectives can be construed before or after the genitive; on the bayaniyya reading the whole epitheted name is cited as one name and then explains ism. «“Rahmân ve Rahîm sıfatlarıyla muttasıf bulunan Cenâb-ı Allah’ın ismiyle”»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r4] verses 1:1 | The bayaniyya analysis treats the genitive inflection as distributed over three words to stress the divine proper name, a necessary inflection rather than ordinary narration grammar. «ki buna “i‘râb-ı zarûrî” nâmı verilir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r5] verses 1:1 | Most commentators prefer the lamiyya construction because its parsing is explicit and the meanings of the names and attributes are directly intended. «Müfessirînin çoğu evvelkini tercih ediyorlar. Çünkü onda hem i‘râb zâhir ve hem esmâ ve sıfâtın mânası bizzat maksud olur»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r6] verses 1:1 | Some prefer the bayaniyya construction, citing Labid's line using ism al-salam; the Mevlid author also reflects it with Allah adı. «Bazıları ise Lebîd’in السَّلَاَمِ عَلَيْكُمَا إِلَى الْحَوْلِ ثُمَّ اسْمُ mısrâ‘ındaki اِسْمُ السَّلَاَمِ gibi beyâniyeyi tercih etmişlerdir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r7] verses 1:1 | The omitted verb is the action the speaker is about to undertake, such as reading, writing, eating, or beginning, and the opening ba attaches the name to it. «fiil besmele diyen kimsenin teşebbüs edeceği»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r8] verses 1:1 | The verb and subject are omitted to let anyone connect the basmala to any intended act; ba and the speaker's situation identify the ellipsis, as in Turkish formulas like devletle selâmetle. «cümlenin erkânı olan fiil ve fâil hazf edilmişlerdir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#8/r9] verses 1:1 | Although Arabic normally delays a complement, placing the ba phrase first gives emphasis and exclusivity; compared with 96:1, basmala foregrounds Allah's name rather than the act of reading. «böyle hakkı muahhar olan tetimmenin takdîmi bir ihtimam ve ihtisas ifade eder»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r1] verses 1:1 | Combining Allah with Rahman and Rahim as one name foregrounds Allah but still fails to connect the act directly with mercy. «doğrudan doğru Allah ismi mebde’ yapılmış olacak ve ma‘amâfîh vasl-ı rahmet yine temin edilemeyecektir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r10] verses 1:1 | Elmalılı says Abduh's deputyship reading amounts to acting as God's representative and self-effacement, appropriate only to special stations such as prophethood, sainthood, rule, and authority. «ancak risalet, velâyet, hâkimiyet, tasarruf gibi makāmât-ı mahsûsada cârî olur»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r11] verses 1:1 | That meaning cannot apply generally: saying one eats in God's name is improper, though saying one eats food God bestowed would be sound; worship is for God but not done as His deputy. «Çünkü ibadet Allah’a olursa da Allah nâmına niyâbeten değil»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r12] verses 1:1 | Reports that the basmala is the key to every book and every important act imply a general meaning for lawful acts; one of the commentators' attachment or seeking-aid senses supplies it. «bize besmelenin meşrû olan her işimizin küşâdında miftah olabilecek bir ma‘nâ-yı şâmilini ihtar etmektedir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r13] verses 1:1 | Abduh's explanation is sound for the Quran's revelation, recitation, and writing because of the basmala's position at their beginnings, and it matters in special settings too. «Kur’ân’ın, süver-i Kur’ân’ın nüzûlü, kıraati, kitabeti itibariyle evvelinde bulunması haysiyetinden besmelenin mânasında Şeyh Abduh’un îzâhı doğru olduğu gibi»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r2] verses 1:1 | Removing the participle still leaves Allah's proper name insufficiently foregrounded and separates it from the mercy attributes; listing three names may imply three divine essences, though singular ismiyle helps avert that implication. «“ismiyle” denilip, “isimleriyle” denilmemesi bu îhâmı def‘e kâfi»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r3] verses 1:1 | The basmala's words, composition, rhetoric, sound, and even its sweet pattern of letters cannot be fully carried into Turkish, so it should be recited in Arabic and understood through explanation. «ne müfredâtını ve ne terkiblerini tamamen terceme mümkin olmayan ve hele vücûh-ı belâgati, âheng-i beyânı hiçbir suretle kābil-i nakl olmayan»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r4] verses 1:1 | He rejects corrupting the tawhid key with a rendering like esirgeciyi bağışlayıcı tanrı adıyla. «bir miftâh-ı kül ve bir âyet-i tevhîd olan»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r5] verses 1:1 | The basmala's interpretive pivot is ba, which connects Allah's name, the first source and ultimate object of existence and progress, to the intended act. «olan nokta baştaki با harfidir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#9/r6] verses 1:1 | Human acts are not their own complete cause: will is a moment in the stream of existence, and success depends on the outpouring between the First Cause and human will, first Rahmani and finally Rahimi. «muvaffakiyetlerimiz, illet-i ûlâ ile iradelerimiz arasındaki nisbetin feyzine tâbi‘dir ki bu feyz ibtidâen Rahmânî, intihâen Rahîmîdir»
