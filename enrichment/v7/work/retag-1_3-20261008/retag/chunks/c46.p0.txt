# Notes

## 6:12
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r10] verses 6:12 | The Shaykh says a partitive بدل would need an omitted pronoun, while treating the losers as the sole addressees makes it a whole-for-whole بدل. «قال الشيخ: " هذا الرد ليس بجيد لأنه إذا جعلنا " ليجمعنكم " صالحا لخطاب جميع الناس كان " الذين " بدل بعض،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r11] verses 6:12 | The author rejects the contradiction charge: a partitive بدل can restrict a grammatically general term, just as adjectives, limits, and conditions can. «قلت: ما أبعده عن التناقض، لأن بدل البعض من الكل من جملة المخصصات كالتخصص بالصفة والغاية والشرط،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r12] verses 6:12 | Apparent generality can be narrowed by the speaker’s intent, as in ordering the killing of the polytheists of a named tribe. «فإذا ورد: " اقتلوا المشركين بني فلان " مثلا فالمشركون صالح لكل مشرك من حيث اللفظ،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r2] verses 6:12 | An alternative makes فيه refer to the gathering implied by ليجمعنكم, answering those who deny resurrection. «وقيل: يعود على الجمع المدلول عليه بالفعل لأنه رد على منكري الحشر.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r3] verses 6:12 | Al-Zamakhshari parses الذين خسروا as accusative under an implied أذم or أريد; the author finds أريد unobvious. «أحدها: أنه منصوب بإضمار " أذم " وقدره الزمخشري ب أريد، وليس بظاهر.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r4] verses 6:12 | Al-Zajjaj parses الذين as a subject whose predicate is فهم لا يؤمنون; ف appears because the subject carries a conditional sense. «قاله الزجاج كأنه قيل: من يخسر نفسه فهو لا يؤمن.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r5] verses 6:12 | On al-Zajjaj’s parse, the sense is that whoever loses himself does not believe. «كأنه قيل: من يخسر نفسه فهو لا يؤمن.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r6] verses 6:12 | Two more proposed parses make الذين a genitive adjective for المكذبين or a بدل from them; the author calls both remote. «الثالث: أنه مجرور على أنه نعت للمكذبين. الرابع: أنه بدل منهم، وهذان الوجهان بعيدان.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r7] verses 6:12 | Another analysis makes الذين an accusative بدل from the second-person suffix, a disputed construction that al-Akhfash allows. «ومذهب الأخفش جوازه، وقد ذكرنا دليل الجمهور ودلائله وما أجيب عنها فأغنى عن إعادتها.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r8] verses 6:12 | Al-Mubarrad rejects بدل from a second-person pronoun, citing مررت بك زيد, which the author says simply assumes the point at issue. «ورد المبرد عليه مذهبه بأنه البدل من ضمير الخطاب لا يجوز»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#3/r9] verses 6:12 | Ibn Atiyya argues this example differs because the بدل adds information: gathering addresses all people, then identifies the losers as the threatened subset. «ورد ابن عطية رده فقال: " ما في الآية مخالف للمثال؛لأن الفائدة في البدل مترتبة من الثاني،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#4/r1] verses 6:12 | Al-Zamakhshari’s sixth parse raises الذين خسروا for blame, or makes it accusative for blame with an implied أريد. «السادس: أنه مرفوع على الذم، قاله الزمخشري،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#4/r2] verses 6:12 | He supplies an omitted أنتم to connect the raised clause to ليجمعنكم, while خسروا follows the relative pronoun’s person. «قلت: إنما قدر المبتدأ " أنتم " ليرتبط مع قوله " ليجمعنكم " وقوله " خسروا أنفسهم " من مراعاة الموصول،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12#4/r3] verses 6:12 | Alternatively, أنتم الذين خسروا أنفسهم is possible by matching the addressed pronoun, just as أنت الذي قعد or قعدت. «ولو قال: " أنتم الذين خسروا أنفسهم " مراعاة للخطاب لجاز، تقول: أنت الذي قعد، وإن شئت: قعدت.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r1] verses 6:12 | لمن is a fronted predicate because it contains an interrogative; ما is the subject meaning that which is in the heavens and earth. «قوله تعالى: { لمن ما في السموات }: " لمن " خبر مقدم واجب التقديم؛ لاشتماله على ما له صدر الكلام»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r10] verses 6:12 | Ibn Atiyya objects to an emphatic نون in a plain affirmative clause, allowing it only in commands, prohibitions, and oath answers. «ورد ابن عطية هذا بأنه يلزم دخول نون التوكيد في الإيجاب قال: " وإنما تدخل على الأمر والنهي وجواب القسم ".»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r11] verses 6:12 | The Shaykh rejects Ibn Atiyya’s restriction on where emphatic نون may occur. «ورد الشيخ حصر ابن عطية ورد نون التوكيد فيما ذكر. وهو صحيح،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r12] verses 6:12 | The Shaykh also rejects ليجمعنكم as a بدل because an oath response alone has no syntactic position. «ورد كون " ليجمعنكم " بدلا من الرحمة بوجه آخر، وهو أن " ليجمعنكم " جواب قسم،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r13] verses 6:12 | Makkī combines the apposition and oath analyses, treating ليجمعنكم as a نصب بدل from الرحمة and the response to an oath implicit in كتب. «قلت: وقد خلط مكي المذهبين وجعلهما مذهبا واحدا فقال: " ليجمعنكم " في موضع نصب على البدل من " الرحمة " واللام لام القسم.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r14] verses 6:12 | The author sketches a reply to the Shaykh: the implied oath clause and its answer together form a بدل of mercy, with the oath omitted. «وقد يظهر جواب عما أورده الشيخ على غير مكي، وذلك أنهم جعلوا " ليجمعنكم " بدلا من " الرحمة " ، يعني هي وقسيمها المحذوف،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r15] verses 6:12 | The author supports this reading by noting that grammarians may name an oath clause while omitting its answer, or name the answer while omitting the oath. «وكما يقولون جملة القسم ويستغنون به عن ذكرهم جملة الجواب كذلك يستغنون بالجواب عن ذكر القسم»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r2] verses 6:12 | One explanation says the Prophet is told to answer first, though the question is aimed at others, so he leads the acknowledgement. «قيل: إنما أمره أن يجيب وإن كان المقصود أن يجيب غيره؛ ليكون أول من بادر الاعتراف بذلك،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r3] verses 6:12 | Al-Jurjani imagines the questioners asked whose it is, and قل لله answers that unspoken question. «ذكره الجرجاني. فعلى هذا قوله: " قل لله " جواب للسؤال المضمر الصادر من جهة الكفار،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r4] verses 6:12 | The author finds that explanation remote: the question was rebuke, since the disbelievers already knew it belonged to God. «وهذا بعيد، لأنهم لم يكونوا يشكون في أنه لله، وإنما هذا سؤال تبكيت وتوبيخ،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r5] verses 6:12 | لله is the predicate of an omitted subject such as هو or ذلك. «وقوله " لله " خبر مبتدأ محذوف، أي هو أو ذلك لله.»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r6] verses 6:12 | كتب على نفسه means God decreed or imposed mercy on Himself as a gracious favor, not as an owed right. «أي قضى وأوجب إيجاب تفضل لا أنه مستحق عليه تعالى»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r7] verses 6:12 | An alternative takes كتب as an oath; then ليجمعنكم is its answer and there is no pause after الرحمة. «وقيل: معناه القسم، وعلى هذا فقوله: { ليجمعنكم } جوابه؛»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r8] verses 6:12 | Al-Zajjaj makes ليجمعنكم a grammatical بدل from الرحمة, interpreting the gathering promise as mercy through extended life, provision, and respite. «قال الزجاج: " إن الجملة من قوله: { ليجمعنكم } في محل النصب على أنها بدل من " الرحمة " ،»
[SAMIN-DURR:6:12/r9] verses 6:12 | Al-Farra gives two options: end the sentence at الرحمة, or treat ليجمعنكم as a بدل, like the construction in 6:54. «وقد ذكر الفراء هذين الوجهين: أعني أن الجملة تمت عند قوله " الرحمة " أو أن " ليجمعنكم " بدل منها»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r1] verses 6:12 | Q says the Prophet is to remind disbelievers of God’s sovereignty and power to destroy them by asking who owns the heavens and earth. «The Prophet is here instructed to remind the disbelievers of God’s Sovereignty and His ability to destroy them at will»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r10] verses 6:12 | Z suggests God’s self-obligated Mercy may more generally mean He undertook to guide creatures to recognize His Oneness. «His having undertaken to guide His creatures to an understanding of His Oneness (Z)»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r11] verses 6:12 | Among God’s Quranic Attributes, Mercy alone is said to be prescribed for Himself, giving it unique and supreme status. «“Mercy” is the only one that He is said to have»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r12] verses 6:12 | That God prescribes Mercy indicates it is intrinsic to Him, since only essential mercifulness could make Mercy a principle of His acts. «Mercy is an intrinsic or essential quality of God, not merely a way of relating to His creatures»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r13] verses 6:12 | God’s Mercy continues in the Hereafter, while earthly respite and guidance eventually end when He gathers everyone for Resurrection. «Although God’s Mercy continues into the Hereafter, the period of respite and guidance for human beings on earth will eventually come to an end»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r14] verses 6:12 | The Resurrection Day is repeatedly described as a day without doubt. «Here and elsewhere, this day is described as one in which there is no doubt»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r15] verses 6:12 | Z says those who lost their souls through associating equals with God, rejecting His signs, and mocking the Prophet’s message do not believe because of these ruinous beliefs and acts. «their lack of faith can be seen as a result of these ruinous actions and beliefs (Z)»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r2] verses 6:12 | The question may be rhetorical for Makkans: some acknowledged Allah as Creator despite mocking the Prophet, though not in the Abrahamic sense. «The question may have been rhetorical for the idolatrous Makkans, since despite their mocking of the Prophet and the warning he brought, some did acknowledge Allāh as the Creator»
[STUDYQURAN:6:12/r4] verses 6:12 | God’s self-prescription of Mercy means that, while absolutely free, He has obligated Himself to act mercifully toward creatures. «although God remains absolutely free, He has nonetheless obligated Himself to act with mercy toward His creatures.»
