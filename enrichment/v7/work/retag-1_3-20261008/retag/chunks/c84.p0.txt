- 18:81: فَأَرَدْنَآ أَن يُبْدِلَهُمَا رَبُّهُمَا خَيْرًۭا مِّنْهُ زَكَوٰةًۭ وَأَقْرَبَ رُحْمًۭا

# Notes

## 18:80
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:80-81/r1] verses 18:80 | The boy's believing parents faced the risk that he would overwhelm them with tyranny and disbelief. «فخشينا أن يرهقهما } أن يغشيهما»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:80-81/r2] verses 18:80 | One explanation is that his disobedience would make his parents ungrateful for God's favor. «لنعمتهما بعقوقه فيلحقهما شرا»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:80-81/r3] verses 18:80 | Alternatively, his tyranny and unbelief would coexist with their faith in one household. «أو يقرن بإيمانهما طغيانه وكفره يجتمع في بيت واحد مؤمنان وطاغ كافر»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:80-81/r4] verses 18:80 | Alternatively, his misguidance and support for his wrongdoing might cause his parents to apostatize. «أو يعديهما بعليه فيرتدا بإضلاله، أو بممالأته على طغيانه وكفره حياله»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:80-81/r5] verses 18:80 | Khidr feared this because God had informed him of it. «وإنما خشي ذلك لأن الله تعالى أعلمه»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:80-81/r6] verses 18:80 | Ibn Abbas replied to Najda the Haruri's question about killing a boy despite the prophetic ban on killing children: one may kill if one knows the child's condition as Moses' learned servant did. «أن نجدة الحروري كتب إليه كيف قتله وقد نهى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن قتل الولدان، فكتب إليه»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:80-81/r7] verses 18:80 | A reading says your Lord feared; the expression means He disliked it, as someone fears a bad consequence. «وقرىء «فخاف ربك» أي فكره كراهة من خاف سوء عاقبته»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:80-81/r8] verses 18:80 | The wording we feared may quote God's speech. «ويجوز أن يكون قوله { فخشينا } حكاية قول الله عز وجل»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p496/r1] verses 18:80 | The author foregrounds the boat owners’ poverty because compassion for them is the stronger of the two motives for damaging the vessel. «إشارة إلى أن أقوى السببين الحاملين على فعله الرأفة بالمساكين»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p496/r2] verses 18:80 | Khidr says the boy’s parents were believers and that the boy himself was innately disposed to unbelief, as the hadith from Ubayy will explain. «على الكفر - كما يأتي في حديث أبي رضي الله عنه»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p496/r3] verses 18:80 | If the boy grew up, his attachment or harsh boldness might overwhelm his parents with tyranny and disbelief, damaging their worldly life or religious faith. «إن كبر بمحبتهما له أو بجراءته وقساوته»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p496/r4] verses 18:80 | A report from Ibn ʿAbbās through Ubayy says the boy Khidr killed was created a disbeliever and would have driven his parents into tyranny and disbelief if he lived. «عن ابن عباس عن أبي بن كعب رضي الله عنه أن النبي صلى الله عليه وعلى آله وسلم قال»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p496/r5] verses 18:80 | The author reads the report about what they would have done as indicating that hypothetical punishment applies to innate disposition, not a merely contingent condition. «إنما يكون على ما كان جبلة وطبعا»
[BURSEVI:v5p219#2/r1] verses 18:80 | The slain boy was Jaisur; his parents were named Kazbara and Suhwa in al-Ta‘rif. Their faith meant affirming God’s oneness. «وهو جيسور {فكان أبواه} اسم أبيه كازبرا واسم أمه سهوى»
[BURSEVI:v5p219#2/r2] verses 18:80 | Khashiya here means fearing that the boy would overwhelm his parents with misguidance and disbelief; the Sheikh explains they might follow him through their love and abandon faith. «أي : يكلفهما {طغيانا} ضلالة {وكفرا} ويتبعان له»
[BURSEVI:v5p219#2/r3] verses 18:80 | Khidr feared this because God informed him the child was created with an unbelieving disposition. «لأن الله أعلمه بحال الولد أنه طبع أي : خلق كافرا»
[BURSEVI:v5p219#3/r1] verses 18:80 | Killing an innocent is forbidden by outward law, but inwardly permitted to someone granted disclosure of outcomes when the child’s life would corrupt his parents’ faith and complete his own misery. «قتل النفس الزكية بلا جرم منها محظور في ظاهر الشرع وإن كان فيه مصلحة لغيره»
[DURR-FULL:v5p410/r6] verses 18:79, 18:80 | Ibn Abbas read that a king ahead seized every sound ship by force, and that the boy was an unbeliever while his parents were believers. «وكان ابن عباس يقرأ وكان أمامهم ملك يأخذ كل سفينة صالحة غصبا وكان يقرأ وأما الغلام فكان كافرا»
[DURR-FULL:v5p412/r5] verses 18:80, 18:81 | The unbelieving boy was loved by his believing parents; that love might have led them to follow his tyranny and unbelief, so a better child replaced him. «(فكان أبواه مؤمنين) وكان كافرا {فخشينا أن يرهقهما طغيانا وكفرا} أي يحملهما حبه»
[DURR-FULL:v5p414/r1] verses 18:80, 18:81 | The boy's parents loved him; keeping him alive could have drawn them into tyranny and unbelief, and the father later fathered a more pious, affectionate replacement child. «وأما الغلام فإنه كان طبع يوم طبع كافرا وكان قد ألقي عليه محبة من أبويه ولو عصياه شيئا لأرهقهما»
[DURR-FULL:v5p415/r4] verses 18:80, 18:81, 18:82 | Khidr says he acted by his Lord's command, not on his own; Ibn Abbas says the treasure under the orphans' wall was knowledge. «وما فعلته عن أمري} أي ما فعلته عن نفسي {ذلك تأويل ما لم تسطع عليه صبرا} فكان ابن عباس يقول: ما كان الكنز إلا علما»
[DURR-FULL:v5p416/r5] verses 18:75, 18:76, 18:77, 18:78, 18:79, 18:80, 18:81, 18:82 | Moses was ashamed after the boy's death; the report repeats the hospitality, wall, boat, boy, and orphans' treasure, including Ubayy's reading that the king seized sound ships. «قال ابن عباس: فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: فاستحى نبي الله موسى عند ذلك فقال: {إن سألتك عن شيء بعدها فلا تصاحبني»
[DURR-FULL:v5p417#2/r4] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:81, 18:82 | Khidr explains that the damaged boat escaped confiscation, the unbelieving boy would have burdened his parents, and the wall protected two orphans' treasure. «قال: {أما السفينة فكانت لمساكين يعملون في البحر} الآية»
[DURR-FULL:v5p426/r1] verses 18:80 | The Prophet says the boy was destined to be an unbeliever from birth and, had he lived, would have driven his believing parents into transgression and disbelief. «الغلام الذي قتله الخضر طبع يوم طبع كافرا ولو أدرك لأرهق أبويه طغيانا وكفرا»
[DURR-FULL:v5p426/r2] verses 18:80 | A second report says the boy killed by Khidr was destined to be an unbeliever. «الغلام الذي قتله الخضر طبع كافرا»

[DURR-FULL:v5p428/r3] verses 18:80 | Matar says that if the boy had remained alive, he would have caused his parents' ruin and destruction. «لو بقي كان فيه بوارهما واستئصالهما»
[DURR-FULL:v5p428/r4] verses 18:80 | Parents rejoice at a child's birth and grieve at his death, yet this boy's survival would have destroyed them; God's decree for a believer is better than the believer's own choice, even when painful. «فرحا به يوم ولد وحزنا عليه يوم قتل ولو عاش لكان فيه هلاكهما»
