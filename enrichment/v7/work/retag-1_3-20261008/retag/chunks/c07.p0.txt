# Verses
- 1:1: بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

# Notes

## 1:1
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r16] verses 1:1 | The basmala summarizes the Quran's subject and aim; al-Fatiha and the successive surahs unfold its inexhaustible detail and wise ordering, beyond merely measurable length or poetic feeling. «biz bunda ilm-i Kur’ân’ın mevzû‘unu, gayesini icmâlen bulacağız ve evvelâ Fâtiha’da sâniyen peyderpey sûreleriyle bütün Kur’an’da bunun»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r17] verses 1:1 | The Quran is not a chance or merely poetic anthology but a coherent true discourse like a single comprehensive sentence, beginning with basmala and ending with al-Nas. «Kur’ân’ın sûreleri, âyetleri keyfemettefak bir tesadüfün veya sade bir hiss-i şâirânenin sevkiyle serilivermiş bir dîvân-ı mahlût değil»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r18] verses 1:1 | The Quran's whole discourse links Allah to all creatures, especially humans, and their return relation to Him; recitation permutations at completion, transmitted from Ali, are called hal-murtahil and signify no total break in existence. «ki bu bize vücudda inkıtâ‘-ı tâm olmadığını ifade eder»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r2] verses 1:1 | When writing a mushaf, writing the basmala at each surah opening except Bara'a is obligatory. «Mushaf yazarken Berâe’den mâ‘adâ sûre başlarında بِسْمِ اللّٰهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ yazmak farzdır»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r3] verses 1:1 | For slaughter or shooting game, saying Bismillah alone is obligatory; deliberate omission makes the animal unlawful, but forgetfulness is excused. «Hayvan zebh ederken veya ava silah atarken sadece بسم اللّٰه demek de farzdır. Kasden terk edilirse o hayvan yenmez»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r4] verses 1:1 | In slaughter and similar settings, omitting Rahman and Rahim is sunnah. «Bunda ve bu gibi bazı mevâki‘de الرَّحْمٰن الرَّحِيم denilmemek sünnettir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r5] verses 1:1 | Outside prayer, beginning a surah recitation with isti'adha and basmala is sunnah according to the majority, while some imams such as Ata consider it obligatory. «demek cumhûra göre sünnet, ezcümle Atâ gibi bazı e’immeye göre vâcibdir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r6] verses 1:1 | There is no basmala at Bara'a's opening; in the Asim reading, reciting it between two surahs is recommended, and starting midway or at the end also recommends isti'adha and basmala. «Yalnız Berâe’de besmele denmez. Sûreteyn beyninde iki kıraat vardır. Bizim Âsım kıraatinde okumak mendubdur»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r7] verses 1:1 | In prayer before al-Fatiha, Hanafis recommend silently reciting isti'adha and basmala. «namazda biz Hanefîlere göre Fâtiha’dan evvel sırren eûzü-besmele sünnet»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r8] verses 1:1 | Shafi'is require basmala in prayer, either silently or aloud. «Şâfiiyye’ye göre sırren veya cehren besmele farz»
[ELMALILI:1:1#10/r9] verses 1:1 | Malikis recommend omitting basmala in prayer. «Mâlikîlere göre okunmaması mendubdur»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r1] verses 1:1 | Astronomy studies universal gravitation as one unified subject, yet it does not encompass all existence or even all scientific fields. «Bugün biliyoruz ki bütün bir ilm-i hey’et bir câzibe kanununun ifadesidir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r10] verses 1:1 | To live the universal attraction, readers should seek refuge from distraction and satanic whispering, then take the basmala as key and begin al-Fatiha in gratitude. «diyelim ve o câzibe-i külliyeyi yaşamak için»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r2] verses 1:1 | Gravity does not cover particle attraction, chemical cohesion, organic nourishment and reproduction, affective attraction and aversion, sensory images, rational truth, spiritual will, or social bonds. «onda ne ecsâmın zerrâtı veya eczâ-i ferdiyesi arasındaki câzibe-i zerreviye veya cüz’-i ferdiyeyi, temâsük ve telâsuk gibi alâka-i kimyeviyeyi»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r3] verses 1:1 | The Quran's subject unifies every physical and spiritual kind of attraction under absolute lordship and God's relation to the world, especially humanity; gravity is only one partial sign. «rubûbiyet kanununu, Allah ile âlem ve bilhassa insanlar beynindeki nisbet ve izâfet-i mutlakanın»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r4] verses 1:1 | Quranic composition unites scientific order and aesthetic beauty, surpassing the system of sciences in firmness and literature in variety and resemblance. «Nazm-ı Kur’an nesak-ı ilmî ile nesak-ı bedî‘înin vahdetgâhıdır»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r5] verses 1:1 | The Quran's integrated order supports its divine origin: if it came from other than God, much inconsistency would be found, as 4:82 says. «أَفَلَا يَتَدَبَّرُونَ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ وَلَوْ كَانَ مِنْ عِندِ غَيْرِ ٱللَّهِ لَوَجَدُوا۟ فِيهِ ٱخْتِلَٰفًا كَثِيرًا»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r6] verses 1:1 | Human science has not measured even the order of the three-body system, so it cannot certify the absence of scientific, logical, or aesthetic flaws in cosmic order. «yalnız ecsâm-ı selâse nazariyesi dâhilindeki nizam ve nisbetini bile henüz ölçmemiş olan fennimiz»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r7] verses 1:1 | Nature's order is both manifest and hidden; the Quran's surah and verse order is similarly, and even more, manifest and hidden, manifest through perfection and hidden through inexhaustibility. «Fıtratın bu nizâmı, nazarımızda nasıl böyle hem zâhir ve hem bâtın ise, Kur’ân’ın sûreleri ve âyetleri beynindeki nizâm-ı ilmî ve bedî‘î dahi onun gibi ve hatta ondan ziyade zâhir ve ondan ziyade bâtındır»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r8] verses 1:1 | A poet says the cosmos' maiden thought has been torn open while the Quran's meaning still remains veiled in its purity. «Bikr-i fikri kâinâtın çâk çâk oldu fakat Perde-i ‘ismette kaldı ma‘nî-i Kur’ân henüz»
[ELMALILI:1:1#11/r9] verses 1:1 | People often sacrifice aesthetic relations when thinking logically and logical relations when thinking aesthetically; although they cannot chart all Quranic relations, they can experience their perfection by living it. «bu tenâsübü bütün kadrosuyla bir cetvel çizer gibi fikren ölçemiyor isek o fıtratı yaşarken bu zevk-i kemâli vecd ile duyabiliriz»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r1] verses 1:1 | An ism is originally a sign that brings something to mind and conventionally is a word denoting a meaning understood on its own; the denoted meaning or its instance is the musammā. «bir mânaya delâlet eden kelime»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r10] verses 1:1 | Trying to make the encompassing divine reality into a finite object of vision is impossible: imposing a bounded endpoint on it would cut off consciousness and existence rather than yield clear vision. «muhîti muhâta kalb etmeye çalışmak gibi bir nokta-i imtinâ‘vardır»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r11] verses 1:1 | Those who cannot humble their pride before God’s infinite manifestation and say they searched for God but did not find Him proclaim failure in the name of science and philosophy. «fen ve felsefe nâmına hüsranlarını ilân etmiş olurlar»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r12] verses 1:1 | Sensing God requires the heart to discern Him and apprehend the realized relation between them. «Allah’ı sezmek için kalb ile aynı temyîz ve aralarındaki nisbet-i tahakkuku idrak edebilmelidir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r2] verses 1:1 | The preferred derivation traces ism to the root سمو, though derivation from وسم is also possible; its plurals asmāʾ and asāmī are ordinary Arabic loanwords in Turkish. «Mezheb-i muhtâra göre aslı سمو maddesidir, وسم’den olması da mümkindir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Names include both proper and common nouns and names of entities and attributes; the distinctions matter when discussing God’s beautiful names. «ism-i hâs veya ‘alem, ism-i cins veya âm diye taksim olunduğu gibi ism-i zât veya ism-i sıfat diye de tefrik olunur»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r4] verses 1:1 | Turkish isim, ad, and nâm overlap but are not interchangeable in every idiom: one may say doing something in someone’s name, while some contexts favor isim for a person. «lisânımızda biz bunları ince farklarla kullanırız»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r5] verses 1:1 | Allah is the proper personal name of the true object of worship; through this name and the divine attributes the Qurʾān introduces God and our relation to Him. «Ma‘bûd-ı Hakk’ın ism-i hâssıdır. Daha doğrusu ism-i zâttır ve ism-i ‘alemdir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r6] verses 1:1 | The divine name is the highest organizing principle: without unity in existence, truth, and causality, the sciences remain fragmented and cannot establish even a partial truth. «Vücûd, hak, nisbet-i illiyet, bütün fenlere hâkim olan mebâdî-i tasavvuriye ve tasdîkiyedir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r7] verses 1:1 | The necessary existent is the first origin and ultimate cause of both knowledge and existence; nothing that acts upon or subdues a person can be conceived without God behind it. «ki onun arkasında Allah bulunmasın»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r8] verses 1:1 | Every person has an innate, simple, unbounded inner assent to God that is more truthful and powerful than finite senses, consciousness, reason, and thought because it encompasses them. «bütün ilimlerimizin kökü olan bu hiss-i bâtın, mütenâhî hislerimizin, şuurlarımızın, akıllarımızın, fikirlerimizin hepsinden daha hak, hepsinden daha kuvvetlidir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#2/r9] verses 1:1 | People’s forgetfulness of this primordial awareness is a source of error; the universe is full of divine signs and the Qurʾān recalls them with miraculous eloquence, hence its name al-Dhikr. «bize bu âyâtı bir belâgat-i i‘câz ile ihtar ve tezkir ettiği için»
[ELMALILI:1:1#3/r1] verses 1:1 | Allah denotes the divine essence as a special name or proper name: it can signify a mental concept that points to God or name the uniquely specified reality itself. «Hak teâlâ’nın zâtına delâlet eden bir ism-i hâs veya bir ism-i ‘alemîdir»
[ELMALILI:1:1#3/r2] verses 1:1 | If a name is established before proving its referent, it is a proper name rather than a direct proper designation; once established after proof, it directly names the reality, illustrated by the name Ülker for blind and sighted people. «ism-i hâssı olursa da ism-i ‘alemi olmaz. Fakat isbattan sonra vaz‘edilmiş ise bizzat ism-i ‘alem olur»
