[ALUSI-FULL:v17p104#2/r9] verses 21:107 | A report from Ibn Abbas says the Prophet’s mercy protected disbelievers from punishment such as being swallowed, transformed, stoned, or exterminated; it also protected angels from a trial like that of Harut and Marut. «وأخرج ذلك الطبراني والبيهقي وجماعة عن ابن عباس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r1] verses 21:107 | The Prophet is mercy to all as the intermediary of divine effusion to contingent beings according to their capacities; a report says his light was the first creation. «وكونه صلى الله عليه وسلم رحمة للجميع باعتبار أنه عليه الصلاة والسلام واسطة الفيض الإلهي على الممكنات على حسب القوابل ولذا كان نوره صلى الله عليه وسلم أول المخلوقات»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r10] verses 21:107 | The phrase li-l-ʿālamīn most naturally modifies raḥmatan; Ibn ʿAtiyya also allows it to modify arsalanāka, though al-Baḥr says that attachment is normally disallowed after an exceptive particle unless the verb is open-ended. «ثم لا يخفى أن تعلق ( للعالمين ) برحمة هو الظاهر + وقال ابن عطية : يحتمل أن يتعلق بأرسلناك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r2] verses 21:107 | A report addressed to Jabir says the first thing God created was the light of the Prophet; another says God gives and the Prophet distributes. «ففي الخبر أول ما خلق الله تعالى نور نبيك يا جابر وجاء الله تعالى المعطي وأنا القاسم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r3] verses 21:107 | Ibn al-Qayyim argues that without prophethood the world would lack useful knowledge, righteous action, social and political order; good comes from prophetic traces, evil from their obscuration, and the world is a body sustained by prophecy as its soul. «أنه لو لا النبوات لم يكن في العالم علم نافع البتة ولا عمل صالح ولا صلاح في معيضه ولا قوام لمملكة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r4] verses 21:107 | If this account is accepted, Muhammad’s being the most complete prophet and his message the most excellent helps explain his being mercy to the worlds, though Al-Alusi says the argument remains open to discussion. «وإذا سلم هذا علم منه بواسطة كونه صلى الله عليه وسلم أكمل النبيين وما جاء به أجل مما جاؤا به عليهم السلام وإن لم يكون في الأصول اختلاف وجه كونه عليه الصلاة والسلام أرسل رحمة للعالمين أيضا لكن لا يخلو ذلك عن بحث»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r5] verses 21:107 | Al-Alusi rejects the claim that the worlds here means believers alone. «وزعم بعضهم أن العالمين هنا خاص بالمؤمنين وليس بشيء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r6] verses 21:107 | Al-Alusi’s chosen view is that the Prophet was sent as mercy to every individual among angels, humans, and jinn, whether believer or disbeliever; mercy varies, and apparent evils are either mercy on closer inspection or not intended primarily. «والذي اختاره أنه صلى الله عليه وسلم إنما بعث رحمة لكل فرد فرد من العالمين ملائكتهم وأنسهم وجنهم ولا فرق بين المؤمن والكافر من الإنس والجن في ذلك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r7] verses 21:107 | Muslim reports from Abu Hurayra that when asked to invoke against polytheists, the Prophet replied he was sent as mercy, not as a curser, supporting mercy even toward disbelievers. «ما أخرجه مسلم عن أبي هريرة قال : قيل يا رسول الله ادع على المشركين قال : إني لم أبعث لعانا وإنما بعثت رحمة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r8] verses 21:107 | A report in al-Bayhaqi’s Dalāʾil says the Prophet is a mercy bestowed as a gift; Al-Alusi says this supports taking raḥmatan as a circumstantial accusative. «كقوله صلى الله عليه وسلم الذي أخرجه البيهقي في الدلائل عن أبي هريرة إنما أنا رحمة مهداة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p105/r9] verses 21:107 | The causal reading does not conflict with the Ashʿari denial that God’s acts have purposes: Maturidis and Hanbalis disagree, and Al-Alusi says the usual reconciliation applies and the objection is only imagined. «ولا يشين احتمال التعليل ما ذهب إليه الأشاعرة من عدم تعليل أفعاله عز وجل فإن الماتريدية وكذا الحنابلة ذهبوا إلى خلافه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p109/r10] verses 21:107 | Most Sufis understand ‘the worlds’ as all creatures: the Prophet is mercy to each, though their shares differ, and all owe their existence to him. «أكثر الصوفية قدست أسرارهم على أن المراد من العالمين جميع الخلق وهو صلى الله عليه وسلم رحمة لكل منهم إلا أن الحظوظ متفاوتة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v17p109/r11] verses 21:107 | Al-Nabulsi explicitly says the whole universe was created from the Prophet’s light; the line about the partridge’s departure points to believers and unbelievers sharing that origin. «وقد صرح بذلك الشيخ عبد الغني النابلسي قدس سره في قوله تعالى وقد تقدم غير مرة»
[ASAD-NOTES:21:105#n102/r1] verses 21:107 | Asad takes the verse’s scope as all mankind and links this universal principle to 7:158. «I.e., towards all mankind. For an elucidation of this fundamental principle underlying the message of the Qur'an»
[ASAD-NOTES:21:105#n102/r2] verses 21:107 | The Qur’an addresses people regardless of descent, race, or cultural environment. «its appeal to all mankind irrespective of descent, race or cultural environment»
[ASAD-NOTES:21:105#n102/r3] verses 21:107 | Its appeal rests exclusively on human reason and therefore asks no one to accept dogma through blind faith. «it appeals exclusively to man's reason and, hence, does not postulate any dogma that could be accepted on the basis of blind faith alone»
