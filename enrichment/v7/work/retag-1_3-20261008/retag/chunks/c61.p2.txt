[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r12] verses 7:156 | The shift from present-tense punishment to past-tense mercy suggests mercy is intrinsic while punishment arises from people's sins; divine will also applies to mercy, though it is not stated explicitly. «وفي نسبة الإصابة إلى العذاب بصيغة المضارع ونسبة السعة إلى الرحمة بصيغة الماضي إيذان بأن الرحمة مقتضى الذات وأما العذاب فمقتضى معاصي العباد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r13] verses 7:156 | God will specifically inscribe mercy for those who fear disbelief and sins, whether from the outset or after falling into them, and who pay the zakat required of them. «أي الكفر والمعاصي إما إبتداءا أو بعد الملابسة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r14] verses 7:156 | A reported gloss takes piety to mean obeying God and His Messenger, but Alusi says the apparent sense is otherwise. «وقيل المعنى يطيعون الله ورسوله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم والظاهر خلافه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r15] verses 7:156 | Zakat is singled out because it was difficult for Moses' people, who especially loved worldly wealth; prayer is omitted because piety already encompasses every duty and avoidance of every prohibition. «وتخصيص إيتاء الزكاة بالذكر مع اقتضاء التقوى له للتعريض لقوم موسى عليه السلام لأن ذلك كان شاقا عليهم لمزيد حبهم للدنيا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r16] verses 7:156 | Belief in God's signs means believing continually in all the signs; repeating the relative pronoun emphasizes this completeness and hints at those who accept some signs but reject others, like Moses' people. «والذين هم بآياتنا ( كلها كما يفيده الجمع المضاف يؤمنون»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r2] verses 7:156 | Another gloss takes “hudnā” to mean that the people inclined or turned toward God. «معناه مال وقرأ زيد بن علي»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r3] verses 7:156 | Zayd ibn Ali reads the word with kasra on the hāʾ, deriving it from a verb meaning to move or incline. «وقرأ زيد بن علي رضي الله تعالى عنهما ( هدنا ) بكسر الهاء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r4] verses 7:156 | Abu Wajrah rejects the damma reading as unknown in Arabic and says only the kasra form meaning “we inclined” is used; Alusi says mass transmission refutes him. «وأخرج ابن المنذر وغيره عن أبي وجرة السعدي أنه أنكر الضم وقال : والله لاأعلمه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r5] verses 7:156 | With the kasra reading, the verb may be active or passive, meaning either “we moved ourselves” or “we moved others”; both voices are also allowed on the common reading in a dialectal usage. «وجوز على هذه القراءة أن يكون الفعل مبنيا للفاعل والمفعول بمعنى حركنا أنفسنا أو حركنا غيرنا وكذا على قراءة الجماعة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r6] verses 7:156 | Al-Samin objects to al-Zamakhshari's allowance of both voices: when the form is ambiguous, a vowel should distinguish them; Alusi notes Sibawayh allowed all three forms for “qīla” without qualification. «وتعقبه السمين بأنه متى حصل الإلتباس وجب أن يؤتي بحركة تزيله»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r7] verses 7:156 | Sibawayh allows the three vocalizations in forms such as “qīla” without qualification. «إلا أن سيبويه جوز في نحو قيل الأوجه الثلاثة من غير إحتراز»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r8] verses 7:156 | The repentance clause explains the plea for forgiveness and mercy; the emphatic particle in “we have turned” displays eagerness for its substance. «والجملة تعليل لطلب المغفرة والرحمة وتصديرها بحرف التحقيق لأظهار كمال النشاط والرغبة في مضمونها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p76/r9] verses 7:156 | “He said” begins an explanatory resumption; God's punishment reaches whomever He wills to punish, with no outside party determining it. «إستئناف بياني كأنه قيل : فماذا قال الله تعالى له بعد دعائه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p77/r1] verses 7:156 | Al-Tibi compares the answer to God's reply to Abraham about his descendants: the covenant does not reach wrongdoers; al-Hasan and Qatada also report that mercy covers righteous and wicked people in this world but belongs to the pious in the afterlife. «ومن ذريتي قال لاينال عهدي الظالمين ) وأيد هذا التقرير بما روي عن الحسن وقتادة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p77/r2] verses 7:156 | Alusi objects that Moses' prayer did not restrict divine mercy or ask to exclude anyone, so al-Tibi's charge of constricting what is expansive is misplaced. «وما ذكره من حديث التحجر في القلب منه شيء فإن الظاهر أن ما في دعاء موسى عليه السلام ليس منه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p77/r3] verses 7:156 | Moses asked most emphatically for forgiveness, mercy, and good in both lives for himself and his people as repentant returners, not for them regardless of their state. «إن موسى عليه السلام إنما طلب على أبلغ وجه المغفرة والرحمة الدنيوية والآخروية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p77/r4] verses 7:156 | Al-Zuhri reports that after the self-killing, God told Moses that those killed were alive and sustained with Him and that the survivors' repentance had been accepted, making Moses and Israel rejoice. «فقد جاء عن الزهري أن الله تعالى أوحى إلى موسى بعد أن كان ما كان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p77/r5] verses 7:156 | Whether the repentance was through self-killing or another form, the promised acceptance of repentance supports the likelihood that the repentant people received afterlife good; many forgiven people receive it. «وإن كانت غيرها فمن المعلوم أن التوبة تقبل بمقتضى الوعد المحتوم وخير من قبلت توبته في الآخرة كثير»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p77/r6] verses 7:156 | Their worldly good is also affirmed in God's reminder of His favor and preference for the Children of Israel. «وأما خير الدنيا فقد نطقت الآيات بأن القوم غرقى فيه ويكفي في ذلك قوله تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v9p78/r1] verses 7:156 | Alusi offers another reading: God sees Moses' anxiety and first calms him by saying that punishment strikes only whom God wills, not necessarily the now-repentant people for whom Moses fears. «أنه سبحانه لما رأى من موسى عليه السلام شدة القلق والاضطراب»
