# Notes

## 1:1
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p29/r7] verses 1:1 | Al-Harali says these signs are names when pronounced as alif, lam and mim; they are called letters only as word-parts without separate meaning, following al-Khalil's account of their names. «فينبغي أن يقال فيها أسماء وإن كانمت غير معلومة الدلالة كحروف ألف باء تاء فإنها كلها أسماء على ما فهمه الخليل»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p31/r1] verses 1:1 | Al-Harali says the alphabetic openings are the roots of God's first speech, translated into Arabic words and composed as the clear Qur'an; in heavenly scripture they signify the whole before its detailed unfolding. «وأما حقيقتها فهي جوامع أصلها في ذمر أول من كلام الله تعالى فنزلت إلى الكلم العربية»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p31/r2] verses 1:1 | Their comprehensive signs span existence from its hidden center to visible and audible revelation, and they did not appear in earlier books because creation was then incomplete; the final, complete Book fulfills their promised appearance. «ولأجل ما اقتضته من الجمع لم تنزل يفي كتاب متقدم لأن كتا بكل وقت مطابق بحال الكون فيه»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p31/r3] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | Baqara answers the Fatiha's plea for guidance by directing worshippers to the Book, describing the guided and denied groups as patterns to follow or avoid; its pious are the straight path and its others the path of ruin. «وأما منسبة ما بعد ذلك للفاتحة فهو أنه لما أخبلا سبحانه وتعالى أن عباده المخلصين سألوا في الفاتحة»
[BIQAI-FULL:v1p31/r4] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | Alternatively, the surah's purpose is solely to describe the Book; it establishes the truth of its meaning, exposes disbelief and hypocrisy to show that inward faith matters, then proves its inimitable form and authority for teaching law. «وإن شئت قلت : مقصود هذه السورة وصف الكتاب فقط وما عدا ذلك فتوابع ولوازم»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r1] verses 1:1 | The Basmala was probably prefixed to all suras from the middle Meccan period onward, retrospectively including suras already proclaimed. «Die Invokationsformel, mit dem Akronym »Basmala« benannt, wird – wohl ab mittelmekkanischer Zeit – allen Suren vorangestellt»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r10] verses 1:1 | The Basmala’s merciful divine attribute reflects the emotional dimension of divine and exemplary human conduct newly foregrounded in middle Meccan theology. «Das mit dem Gottesnamen ar-Raḥmān und der Basmala insgesamt herausgehobene Attribut Gottes als »barmherzig« trägt der in Mittelmekka neu in den Vordergrund gestellten emotionalen Dimension»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r11] verses 1:1 | Counting the Basmala as a verse derives from Q 15:87’s seven repeated elements; seven is a round number below ten, so the identification does not require exactly seven verses. «Die Zahl Sieben ist hier jedoch, wie Rubin (1993) gezeigt hat, nicht als präzise numerische Angabe zu verstehen»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r12] verses 1:1 | Rubin interprets seven in Q 15:87 as a round number under ten rather than an exact count. «wie Rubin (1993) gezeigt hat, nicht als präzise numerische Angabe zu verstehen»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r2] verses 1:1 | Its use resembles the Christian Trinitarian formula placed before public and official messages, reformulated here as a statement about one God. «Ihr Gebrauch entspricht dem der Trinitätsformel »Im Namen des Vaters und des Sohnes und des Heiligen Geistes« in christlichen Texten»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r3] verses 1:1 | The only occurrence inside a sura is in Solomon’s letter to the Queen of Sheba, raising the possibility that suras were treated as written documents from then on. «Innerhalb eines Surentexts begegnet die Basmala einzig in der spätmittelmekkanischen Sure Q 27:30»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r4] verses 1:1 | Al-Raḥmān marks a middle Meccan usage that replaced the previously frequent rabb/rabbuka in a named group of suras. «Auf die mittelmekkanische Herkunft der Basmala verweist vor allem der Gottesname ar-Raḥmān»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r5] verses 1:1 | Ammann reads the introduction of al-Raḥmān as softening frequent earlier threats of hell and emphasizing assurance of salvation. «Ludwig Ammann (2001:38) sieht in der Einführung von ar-raḥmān ein Mittel der Milderung der vorher frequenten Höllendrohungen»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r6] verses 1:1 | Here al-Raḥmān joins Allāh, a divine name used only occasionally earlier and common from late Meccan times. «In Q 1:1 verbindet er sich mit dem ab Spätmekka üblichen Gottesnamen Allāh»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r7] verses 1:1 | The Jewish liturgical and epigraphic name ha-raḥamān probably entered north Arabian liturgical language through South Arabia. «Der auch in zentralen jüdischen liturgischen und in epigraphischen Texten geläufige Gottesname (hebr. ha-raḥamān) ist mit hoher Wahrscheinlichkeit über Südarabien»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r8] verses 1:1 | The semantically related raḥīm renders the encountered name again in ordinary Arabic. «Die etymologisch und semantisch eng verwandte Prädikation raḥīm ›übersetzt‹ den vorgefundenen Namen noch einmal in das gebräuchliche Arabisch»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:1:1:kommentar/r9] verses 1:1 | The name’s dominance is not explained by its genealogy alone; liturgical experience likely shaped the focus on divine mercy, perhaps echoing kyrie eleēson and Christian salvation history. «so möchte man auch hier einen Anstoß aus liturgischer Richtung vermuten»

[DURR-FULL:v1p19#2/r2] verses 1:1 | In a weak report, the Prophet promised Burayda a unique revealed verse or surah and identified the basmala when asked how to begin Quran recitation in prayer. «بسند ضعيف عن بريدة قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم لا أخرج من المسجد حتى أخبرك بآية»

[DURR-FULL:v1p19#2/r5] verses 1:1 | Ibn Abbas says the basmala was revealed only to the Prophet, apart from its revelation to Solomon. «عن ابن عباس قال: أغفل الناس آية من كتاب الله لم تنزل على أحد سوى النبي»
[DURR-FULL:v1p19#2/r6] verses 1:1 | In a weak report, Gabriel began delivering revelation to the Prophet with the basmala. «بسند ضعيف عن ابن عمر أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم قال كان جبريل إذا جاءني بالوحي»
[DURR-FULL:v1p19#2/r7] verses 1:1 | Ibn Umar says the basmala was revealed in every surah. «عن ابن عمر قال: نزلت {بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم} في كل سورة»
[DURR-FULL:v1p20/r1] verses 1:1 | The Prophet did not know where one surah ended until the basmala came; another wording says he then knew the next surah had begun. «عن ابن عباس قال: كان النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
