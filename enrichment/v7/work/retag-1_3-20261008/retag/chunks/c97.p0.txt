# Verses
- 20:5: ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ ٱسْتَوَىٰ
- 20:108: يَوْمَئِذٍۢ يَتَّبِعُونَ ٱلدَّاعِىَ لَا عِوَجَ لَهُۥ ۖ وَخَشَعَتِ ٱلْأَصْوَاتُ لِلرَّحْمَٰنِ فَلَا تَسْمَعُ إِلَّا هَمْسًۭا

# Notes

## 20:5
[TABATABAI:20:1-8#9/r7] verses 20:5 | A report says a desert Arab asked the Prophet for rain, claiming intercession through God to him; the Prophet rebuked the request, glorified God, and described God above the throne and heavens, while the throne creaks like a new saddle under a rider. «عن جبير بن محمد بن جبير بن مطعم عن أبيه عن جده قال أتى رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم اعرابي فقال يا رسول الله جهدت الأنفس»
[TABATABAI:20:1-8/r1] verses 20:5 | Settling over the Throne is a metaphor for encompassing sovereignty and taking charge of affairs; for Allah it means His dominion appears across creation through management and ordering. It entails rule over every thing, great or small, and establishes Him as the sole Lord, since a lord owns and manages. «الاستواء على العرش كناية عن الاحتواء على الملك والأخذ بزمام تدبير الأمور»
[TABATABAI:20:1-8/r2] verses 20:5 | The verses following the Throne statement mention Allah’s ownership and knowledge as an argument explaining that sovereignty. «ولذلك عقب حديث الاستواء على العرش بحديث ملكه لكل شي وعلمه بكل شي وذلك في معنى التعليل والاحتجاج»
[TABATABAI:20:1-8/r3] verses 20:5 | Al-Raḥmān is an intensive form of mercy, which means effusion through creating and managing; its sense of abundance makes it especially apt with the Throne statement. «وهو مبالغة من الرحمة التي هي الإفاضة بالإيجاد والتدبير وهو يفيد الكثرة أنسب»
[TABATABAI:20:1-8/r4] verses 20:5 | The syntax makes al-Raḥmān the subject, istawā its predicate, and ʿalā al-ʿarsh dependent on istawā; other verses use the same Throne formulation. «أن { الرحمن } مبتدأ خبره { استوى } و { على العرش } متعلق بقوله { استوى }»
[TABATABAI:20:1-8/r5] verses 20:5 | He rejects an alternative parse that makes al-Raḥmān the subject with ʿalā al-ʿarsh as predicate, and treats istawā as a verb whose subject is what is in the heavens and whose object is all things belonging to Allah and obeying His will. «أن قوله { الرحمن على العرش } مبتدأ وخبر»
[TABATABAI:20:1-8/r6] verses 20:5 | He refers readers to his extended discussion of the Throne in his commentary on 7:54 and says a later report section will add material relevant here. «وقد أشبعنا الكلام في معنى العرش في ذيل الآية 54 من سورة الأعراف»
[TABATABAI:20:8#2/r8] verses 20:5 | The statement that the All-Merciful settled over the Throne begins a four-verse exposition of lordship, the core aim of the call and reminder. «استئناف يذكر فيه مسأله توحيد الربوبية التي هي مخ الغرض من الدعوة والتذكرة وذلك في أربع آيات»
[TABRISI:20:2-8/r10] verses 20:5 | The commentary refers readers to earlier discussions of istiwāʾ in al-Baqarah and al-Aʿrāf. «وقد سبق القول في معنى الاستواء في سورة البقرة والأعراف»
[TABRISI:20:2-8/r8] verses 20:5 | The phrase identifies the creator as the Merciful: the name clarifies who created the earth and heavens. «أي هو الرحمن لأنه لما قال ممن خلق»
[TABRISI:20:2-8/r9] verses 20:5 | Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā defines istiwāʾ as turning toward something, as if God turned toward creating the Throne and intended it. «أحمد بن يحيى: الاستواء الإقبال على الشيء»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p354#2/r1] verses 20:5 | The omitted subject in al-Mubarrad's parsing is illustrated by the phrase ففي النار, meaning in the Fire. «المعنى ففي النار»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p354#2/r2] verses 20:5 | Many exegetes interpret استوى as استولى, meaning took possession or prevailed. «قال كثير من أهل التأويل: استوى معناه استولى»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p354#2/r3] verses 20:5 | Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā was asked about the verse and said istiwāʾ means turning toward something. «قال: (سئل أحمد بن يحيى عن قول الله تعالى: {الرحمن على العرش استوى} فقال: الاستواء الإقبال على الشيء)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p354#2/r4] verses 20:5 | Al-Akhfash, like Abū ʿUbayda, interprets استوى as ascending or rising above, as in the idiom of mounting an animal or roof. «وقال الأخفش: (استوى أي علا تقول: استويت فوق الدابة وعلى ظهر البيت أي علوته)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p354#2/r5] verses 20:5 | On the rising interpretation, God is above the Throne by His power and might; the Throne is singled out because it is the greatest creation. «أنه عز وجل علا على العرش بقدرته وقوته، وخص العرش بالذكر؛ لأنه أعظم المخلوقات»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p354#2/r6] verses 20:5 | The leading early authorities named here avoided discussion of this verse and similar verses; the author calls that their way. «وعامة السلف أعرضوا عن الكلام في هذه الآية ونظائرها وذلك طريقة مالك، والشافعي، والأوزاعي، والثوري»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p354/r1] verses 20:5 | Al-Akhfash reads الرحمن as an explanatory statement: He is the All-Merciful. «قال الأخفش: (هو الرحمن)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p354/r2] verses 20:5 | Al-Mubarrad parses الرحمن as the predicate of an omitted subject: after the mention of the One who created, the sense is He is the All-Merciful. «قال المبرد: ({الرحمن} مرفوع على خبر المبتدأ المضمر)»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:20:3-5/r5] verses 20:5 | One gloss of istawā is that the Merciful attended to His creation, as in 2:29; the source also glosses it as istawlā, meaning prevailed. «استوى } [أي: أقبل على خلقه، كقوله:
ثم استوى إلى السماء
مع أنه أعظم المخلوقات]، أي: استولى.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p252/r4] verses 20:5 | A prophetic report describes five-hundred-year distances and thicknesses between the heavens and earth layers, with the seventh heaven two-heaven spans below the Throne. «عن الحسن قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: " بين هذه السماء وبين التي فوقها مسيرة خمس مائة سنة»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p252/r5] verses 20:5 | The Prophet says he was permitted to describe a Throne-bearing angel whose feet reach the lowest earth, whose horn carries the Throne, and whose bird-flight span from ear to shoulder is seven hundred years. «عن محمد بن المنكدر قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: " أذن لي أن أحدث عن ملك من حملة العرش، رجلاه في الأرض السفلى»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p252/r6] verses 20:5 | Yahya says he was informed that the angel's name is Zarufil. «قال يحيى: بلغني أن اسمه زروفيل.»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p350/r2] verses 20:5 | The nominative al-Raḥmān is preferred; genitive is also possible as an appositive to man, ‘from the Creator of earth and heavens, the Merciful,’ followed by the statement that He established Himself over the Throne. «ويجوز الخفض على البدل من " من " المعنى تنزيلا من خالق الأرض والسماوات الرحمن،»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p350/r3] verses 20:5 | Though some gloss istawā as ‘took control,’ the linguistic evidence supports the sense conveyed by istawā itself. «والذي يدل عليه استوى في اللغة على ما فعله من معنى الاستواء.»

## 20:108
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v3p19#2/r1] verses 20:108 | Al-Thawrī glosses the whisper heard on that Day as the sound of footsteps. «عبد الرزاق قال نا الثوري في قوله ﴿ فلا تسمع إلا همسا ﴾ قال صوت الأقدام»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p372/r4] verses 20:108 | Abu Ubayda defines همس as a faint sound; another view identifies it with footsteps, supported by a poetic image of quiet walking. «الهمس: الصوت الخفي قاله أبو عبيدة. وقيل: وطء الأقدام»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p372/r5] verses 20:108 | The word also describes a lion's stealthy footfall or the sound of chewing food. «ويقال للأسد الهموس لخفاء وطئه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r1] verses 20:108 | The caller is apparently God's herald summoning creatures to assembly, identified as Israfil standing on the Jerusalem rock and calling scattered bones and bodies to the Merciful's judgment. «نحو قوله مهطعين إلى الداع وهو إسرافيل يقوم على صخرة بيت المقدس»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r2] verses 20:108 | Muhammad ibn Kaʿb says people assemble in darkness after the sky is folded and stars scattered, then a herald calls them and they die once. «وقال محمد بن كعب: يجمعون في ظلمة قد طويت السماء وانتثرت النجوم»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r3] verses 20:108 | Ali ibn Isa identifies the caller as the Messenger, whose invitation people had swerved from; following him then will not benefit them. «وقال علي بن عيسى الداعي هنا الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم الذي كان يدعوهم إلى الله»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r4] verses 20:108 | The pronoun in لا عوج له most naturally refers to the caller: his summons reaches everyone without favoring one group. «والظاهر أن الضمير في له عائد على الداعي نفى عنه العوج أي لا عوج لدعائه يسمع جميعهم»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r5] verses 20:108 | Another parsing reverses the expression to mean they do not turn aside from the caller but approach and follow his voice. «وقيل: هو على القلب أي لا عوج لهم عنه بل يأتون مقبلين إليه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r6] verses 20:108 | Al-Zamakhshari explains the phrase as meaning no summoned person can turn away from the caller. «وقال الزمخشري: أي لا يعوج له مدعو بل يستوون إليه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r7] verses 20:108 | Ibn Atiyya allows that the phrase means the caller's message is true and will occur, or that no one can avoid following it. «وقال ابن عطية يحتمل أن يريد به الإخبار أي لا شك فيه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r8] verses 20:108 | خَشَع means lowering and humility; applied to voices it is metaphorical quietness caused by awe of God's manifest power, or the phrase may elide the people who own those voices. «والخشوع التطامن والتواضع وهو في الأصوات استعارة بمعنى الخفاء»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p383#2/r9] verses 20:108 | همس may mean the faint whispering people exchange, or instead the sound of footsteps while speech falls silent. «ويحتمل أن يريد بالهمس المسموع تخافتهم بينهم وكلامهم السر»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p384/r1] verses 20:108 | Al-Zamakhshari glosses همسا as a faint click, comparing it with the phonetic category of whispered consonants, or with camels' soft hoofbeats. «وقال الزمخشري: إلا همسا وهو الركز الخفي ومنه الحروف المهموسة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p384/r2] verses 20:108 | Ibn Abbas, Ikrima, and Ibn Jubayr gloss همس as footsteps; al-Farraʾ and al-Zajjaj prefer this sense. «وعن ابن عباس وعكرمة وابن جبير: الهمس وطء الأقدام»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p384/r3] verses 20:108 | Ibn Abbas also explains همس as moving the lips without uttering words. «وعن ابن عباس أيضا تحريك الشفاه بغير نطق»
