# Notes

## 7:57
[BURSEVI:v3p136#3/r4] verses 7:57 | The verse uses “mercy” to mean rain, so a saying about fleeing from mercy refers to fleeing from rain. «وفي الآية إطلاق الرحمة على المطر ، فقول : من قال إني أفر من الرحمة محمول على المطر»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#3/r5] verses 7:57 | The phrase “until it carries” marks the endpoint of the statement that God sends the winds. «{حتى إذآ أقلت} غاية لقوله : يرسل»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#4/r1] verses 7:57 | Aqallat means the wind carried the cloud easily; carrying something with ease makes it seem light, hence its derivation from the sense of fewness. «أي : حملته ورفعته باليسر والسهولة بأن وجدته خفيفا قليلا يقال أقللت كذا ، أي : حملته بالسهولة»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#4/r2] verses 7:57 | “Heavy” describes clouds laden with water; sahāb is a collective noun that can denote one cloud or many, so the adjective is plural in sense. «جمع ثقيل أي بالماء جمعه مع كونه وصفا للسحاب ، لأن السحاب اسم جنس»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#4/r3] verses 7:57 | Sahāb means clouds moving through the sky. «والسحاب : هو الغيم الجاري في السماء»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#4/r4] verses 7:57 | Sūqnāhu derives from driving; the pronoun refers to the clouds, singular in form to match the word’s grammatical form. «من السوق والضمير للسحاب والإفراد باعتبار اللفظ والمعنى بالفارسية»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#4/r5] verses 7:57 | A dead land is a place without vegetation; balad can mean any inhabited or uninhabited location, while balda is a part of it and bilād its plural. «{لبلد ميت} أي : لإحياء بلد لا نبات فيه والبلد يطلق على كل موضع»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r1] verses 7:57 | The pronoun refers to the land, and the bāʾ marks water adhering to or being sent upon it. «{فأنزلنا به المآء} أي : بالبلد والباء للإلصاق أي التصق إنزال الماء»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r2] verses 7:57 | The next bāʾ means “by means of” that water; “all fruits” refers to every kind, with universality understood in the customary sense. «{فأخرجنا به} أي : بسبب ذلك الماء {من كل الثمرات} أي : من كل أنواعها»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r3] verses 7:57 | Resurrection is likened either to producing fruits or to reviving dead land: God will return souls to gathered bodily matter and restore powers and senses. «الإشارة فيه إلى إخراج الثمرات أو إلى إحياء البلد الميت أي كما نحييه بإحداث القوة النباتية فيه»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r4] verses 7:57 | The form tadhakkarūn drops one of two tāʾs and means “you remember,” teaching that the One who can do this can raise the dead without doubt. «{لعلكم تذكرون} بطرح إحدى التاءين ، أي تتذكرون فتعلمون»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r5] verses 7:57 | Ibn Abbas and Abu Hurayra report that rain falls for forty days between the two blasts, bodies grow from graves as plants grow from water, receive souls, then awaken at the blast of resurrection. «قال ابن عباس وأبو هريرة : إذا مات الناس كلهم في النفخة الأولى مطرت السماء أربعين يوما»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r6] verses 7:57 | In the inward reading, winds are care, cloud is guidance, water is love, and its fruits are visions, unveilings, and spiritual perfections; God raises dead hearts from the graves of breasts. «والإشارة في الآية : أن الرياح رياح العناية ، والسحاب سحاب الهداية ، والماء ماء المحبة»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r7] verses 7:57 | The lesson recalls the life of intimacy and nearness in the holy precincts. «أي موتى القلوب من قبور الصدور لعلكم تذكرون ، أي : تذكرون أيام حياتكم»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r8] verses 7:57 | Primordial divine care is the foundation and reaches servants in public and private; a story begins of a saint sent among polytheists, captured, and serving at a church faithfully. «واعلم : أن العمدة هي العناية الأزلية ، وهي تصل إلى العباد في الخلا والملا حكي أنه قيل»
[BURSEVI:v3p136#5/r9] verses 7:57 | The story continues that the saint was sold to a church servant, served honestly, then appeared to a sultan who had emptied the church to pray and answered that he was a Muslim like him. «فأسره مشرك وباعه لخادم كنيسة فخدم فيها زمانا بالصدق ، فجاء السلطان يوما إلى الكنيسة»
[DURR-FULL:v3p477/r3] verses 7:57 | Asim recited the plural الرياح and read بشرا with a light bāʾ. «عن عاصم أنه قرأ {وهو الذي يرسل الرياح} على الجماع (بشرا) خفيفة بالباء»
[DURR-FULL:v3p477/r4] verses 7:57 | Al-Suddi describes winds drawing clouds from between the horizons, spreading them across the sky, then rain flowing onto them and falling from them. «عن السدي في الآية قال: إن الله يرسل الريح فتأتي بالسحاب من بين الخافقين - طرف السماء والأرض من حيث يلتقيان -»
[DURR-FULL:v3p477/r5] verses 7:57 | The winds as glad tidings before God's mercy means that people rejoice at them. «عن ابن عباس في قوله {بشرا بين يدي رحمته} قال: يستبشر بها الناس»
[DURR-FULL:v3p477/r6] verses 7:57 | Abdullah ibn al-Yamani recited بشرا as a form derived from the sense of bearers of glad tidings. «عن عبد الله بن اليماني أنه كان يقرأها {بشرا} من قبل مبشرات»
[DURR-FULL:v3p477/r7] verses 7:57 | Al-Suddi identifies God's mercy in the phrase before it as rain, and compares bringing out the dead to resurrection and plants emerging through water. «عن السدي في قوله {بين يدي رحمته} قال: هو المطر وفي قوله {كذلك نخرج الموتى} قال: وكذلك تخرجون»
[DURR-FULL:v3p477/r8] verses 7:57 | Mujahid says God will raise the dead by rain: earth splits, each soul falls to its body, and the dead are revived as the earth is revived. «عن مجاهد في قوله {كذلك نخرج الموتى} قال: إذا أراد الله أن يخرج الموتى تمطر الماء حتى تشقق الأرض»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r1] verses 7:57 | One benefit of wind is to gather and spread clouds, carrying them overhead to places in need of rain. «bulutları derleyip toplamak ve yaymak ve yağmura muhtaç olan yerlere götürmek»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r10] verses 7:57 | He calls it a miracle whose truth human science recognized only after a thousand years. «Bir mucize ki fenn-i beşer bunun sıdkına bin seneden sonra vâkıf olabilmiştir.»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r11] verses 7:57 | God gives wind its force but does not leave it inert under fixed nature; the wording turns to God's will and sovereign command. «Allah rüzgâra o kuvveti vermekle beraber onu onda atâlet kāidesi üzere bir tabiat istivâsıyla bırakmıyor.»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r12] verses 7:57 | At the moment the winds lift the heavy clouds, God says that He drives the cloud toward a dead land. «Biz o sehâbı ölü bir belde için sevk ederiz de»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r13] verses 7:57 | God sends water down to that land and brings out every kind of fruit through it. «o suyu o beldeye indiririz {فَأَخْرَجْنَا بِهِۦ مِن كُلِّ ٱلثَّمَرَٰتِ} ve o su ile her türlü meyveleri çıkarırız»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r14] verses 7:57 | The revival of a dead land demonstrates that the Lord who acts by motion and will can also raise the dead from their graves. «ölmüş bir beldeyi yeniden ihyâ etmeye kādir bulunan Rabbinizin ölüleri diriltebileceğinde şüphe yoktur.»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r2] verses 7:57 | The verse stresses the striking fact that light air lifts heavy clouds, contrary to ordinary expectations about weight. «hava hafif, bulutlar ise ağırdır. Hafifin ağırı kaldırması ise hilâf-ı tabiattır.»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r3] verses 7:57 | Clouds are not merely water vapor: recent discovery showed them to consist of aggregated liquid water droplets, which are heavier than air. «hâlinde olduğu ahîren fark edilmiş ve suyun ise havadan sakil olduğu mâlum bulunmuş»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r4] verses 7:57 | An initial theory held that hollow, soap-bubble-like droplets contained trapped air hotter than the outside air and therefore floated. «o su habbecikleri sabun köpüğü gibi mücevvef ve içlerinde hava mahbus olup bu hava hâriçteki havadan fazla bir derece-i harârette bulunduğundan hafif olarak üzerinde yüzüyor” denilmiş»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r5] verses 7:57 | He says the hollow-bubble theory was refuted in favor of full droplets, each heavier than an equal volume of air. «sonra bu nazariye cerh olunarak sehâbı terkib eden su habbeciklerinin içi boş değil, dolgun bulunduğu»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r6] verses 7:57 | The accepted account holds that wind motion keeps the heavier droplets suspended and moving in the air, like grains of sand driven upward. «her biri kendi hacmindeki havadan sakil olduğu hâlde aşağıdan rüzgârın tahrîkiyle kum tanelerinin yukarı çıkması gibi»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r7] verses 7:57 | The two theories compare buoyant light objects such as balloons and ships with heavy objects such as birds and airplanes staying aloft; the phrase about heavy clouds points to the latter. «Evvelkine göre balon ve gemi gibi hafifin sakil üzerinde duruşu, ikinciye göre de kuş ve tayyâre gibi sakīlin hafif üzerinde duruşudur»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r8] verses 7:57 | Even if warm air makes the water droplets into tiny bubbles, it is more striking that moving air lifts heavy clouds while they retain their weight. «daha ziyade câlib-i dikkat bir hâdisedir ki, bunda hareketin ehemmiyeti»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58#2/r9] verses 7:57 | The verse's account of moving winds lifting heavy clouds is, for Elmalılı, a scientific miracle and decisive evidence for Muhammad's prophethood. «risâlet-i Muhammediyenin delâil-i kat‘iyyesinden birisi olan bir mu‘cize-i ilmiye var demektir.»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58/r1] verses 7:57 | Elmalılı renders the winds as heralds sent before divine mercy. «rahmetinin önünde rüzgârları müjdeci yollar.»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58/r10] verses 7:57 | Kaʿb is reported to say that if God withheld the wind for three days, most of the earth would stink. «Hazret-i Ka‘b’dan da menkuldür ki Allah üç gün rüzgârı hapsetse ekser arz kokardı.»
