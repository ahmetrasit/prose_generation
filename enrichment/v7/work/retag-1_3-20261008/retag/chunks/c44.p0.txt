# Verses
- 6:12: قُل لِّمَن مَّا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ۖ قُل لِّلَّهِ ۚ كَتَبَ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ ٱلرَّحْمَةَ ۚ لَيَجْمَعَنَّكُمْ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ ۚ ٱلَّذِينَ خَسِرُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ فَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ
- 6:13: ۞ وَلَهُۥ مَا سَكَنَ فِى ٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ

# Notes

## 6:12
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r12] verses 6:12 | A Qur’an reciter should recite well and beautify the voice; reports say beautiful voices increase the Qur’an’s beauty. «حسنوا القرآن بأصواتكم فإن الصوت الحسن يزيد القرآن حسنا»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r13] verses 6:12 | “Singing” the Qur’an may mean being self-sufficient or melodious chanting; the latter is closer to the linguists’ usage. «قيل : أراد بالتغني الاستغناء ، وقيل الترنم وترديد الألحان ، وهو أقرب عند أهل اللغة»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r14] verses 6:12 | Al-Marghinani is reported to say that calling a contemporary Qur’an reciter “well done” during recitation is unbelief, as cited in Taj al-Shariʿa’s Sharh al-Hidaya. «ويحكى عن ظهير الدين المرغيناني أنه قال : من قال لمقرىء زماننا أحسنت عند قراءته يكفر»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r2] verses 6:12 | Shibli advised his companions to cling to God and leave everything else. «وشبلى بابعض أصحاب خود ميكفت كه عليك بالله ودع ما سواء»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r3] verses 6:12 | The heart is scattered by attachment to many things, so turn it toward the One and detach from all else. «دلرا بيكي سار وبكسل زهمه»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r4] verses 6:12 | By allusion, the verse teaches seekers of God to sever attachment to everything besides Him, which is play and diversion. «فالآية بإشارتها تدل على أن من أراد الوصول إلى الله تعالى فلينقطع عما سواه فإنه لعب ولهو»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r5] verses 6:12 | Ahmad ibn Hanbal says he saw God in a dream and asked the best means by which seekers draw near; God answered, “My speech.” «قال أحمد بن حنبل : رأيت رب العزة في المنام فقلت يا رب ما أفضل ما تقرب به المتقربون إليك؟ قال كلامي يا أحمد»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r6] verses 6:12 | Understanding the Qur’an or not, looking at the written Qur’an is worship with a reward of its own beyond recitation. «والنظر إلى المصحف عبادة برأسه وله أجر على حدته ما عدا أجر القراءة»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r7] verses 6:12 | Hamid ibn al-Aʿraj says that when someone completes the Qur’an and supplicates, four thousand angels say amen and continue praying for and seeking forgiveness for him. «وعن حميد بن الأعرج قال : من قرأ القرآن وختمه ، ثم دعا أمن على دعائه أربعة آلاف ملك»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r8] verses 6:12 | The wise should complete the Qur’an during long summer days or winter nights to gain the angels’ prayers; the best people learn and teach it. «فعلى العاقل أن يجتهد حتى يختم القرآن في أوائل الأيام الصيفية والليالي الشتائية ليستزيد»
[BURSEVI:v3p51/r9] verses 6:12 | Following the Prophet and earlier prophets means teaching for nearness to God without seeking compensation, reward, or thanks. «وينبغي أن يقتدي برسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم فلا يطلب عوضا ولا يقصد جزاء ولا شكورا»
[DURR-FULL:v3p252/r2] verses 6:12 | He says the Torah describes one hundred mercies created before humanity: one placed among creatures and ninety-nine reserved, then reunited on Judgment Day, when God’s mercy is greater and wider. «عن سلمان في قوله {كتب على نفسه الرحمة} قال: إنا نجده في التوراة عطيفتين أن الله خلق السموات والأرض ثم جعل مائة رحمة»
[DURR-FULL:v3p252/r3] verses 6:12 | The Prophet says God created one hundred mercies, placed one among creation and reserved ninety-nine for Judgment Day, when the hundredth completes them. «عن سلمان عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال خلق الله يوم خلق السموات والأرض مائة رحمة منها رحمة يتراحم بها الخلق وتسع وتسعون ليوم القيامة»
[DURR-FULL:v3p252/r4] verses 6:12 | The Prophet says God wrote above the Throne, after completing creation, that His mercy preceded His wrath. «عن أبي هريرة قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم لما قضى الله الخلق كتب كتابا فوضعه عنده فوق العرش: ان رحمتي سبقت غضبي»
[DURR-FULL:v3p252/r5] verses 6:12 | A report authenticated by al-Tirmidhi says God wrote upon Himself that His mercy overcomes His wrath. «عن أبي هريرة قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم لما خلق الله الخلق كتب كتابا بيده على نفسه: إن رحمتي تغلب غضبي»
[DURR-FULL:v3p252/r6] verses 6:12 | After judging creation, God will take one or two handfuls from Hell of people with no good deeds, marked as those God freed, after declaring His mercy preceded His wrath. «عن ابن عباس قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم إذا فرغ الله من القضاء بين الخلق أخرج كتابا من تحت العرش: إن رحمتي سبقت غضبي»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r1] verses 6:12 | True knowledge begins by grasping causes, and inquiry begins by seeking the cause behind what is observed. «hakīkat-i ilmin başı idrâk-i illet ve taharriyât-ı ilmiyenin başı da taharrî-i illettir.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r10] verses 6:12 | In God's relation to creation and lordship, mercy is His primary and expected influence; wrath is secondary, arising from created beings' mutual wrongdoing and distance from divine morality. «Allah tarafından evvelen ve bizzat gelen ve muntazar olan hükm ü te’sîr rahmetten ibarettir.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r11] verses 6:12 | Even secondary wrath serves mercy: pain is not intended for itself but distinguishes and preserves pleasure and leads from fleeting delights to lasting ones. «Elemin halkı maksûdun li-zâtihi değil, lezzete ta‘ayyün vermek, onun hükmünü muhafaza etmek ve lezâiz-i fâniyeden lezâiz-i bâkıyeye geçmek içindir.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r12] verses 6:12 | By mercy, God initially created people with sound nature, reason, choice, and freedom, guided them to knowledge and unity through signs, messengers, and books, and invited them to mercy while warning them against punishment. «insanları da ibtidâen fıtrat-ı selîme üzere halk etmiş, akl ü ihtiyar ve hürriyet vermiş ve enfüs ü âfâkta nasb-ı âyât ve irsâl-i rusül ve inzâl-i kütüb ile marifet ve tevhîdine hidâyet eylemiş»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r13] verses 6:12 | Because He prescribed mercy, God does not hasten punishment for those who misuse freedom, reject signs and books, deny and mock messengers, and adopt conduct opposed to divine morality; He accepts repentance, or they would already have perished with earlier peoples. «ukūbeti ta‘cil etmez, tevbe ve ilticâyı kabul eder, yoksa sizi de çoktan o mahv u münkariz olanların yanına gönderirdi.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r14] verses 6:12 | Merciful respite cannot continue forever: it has an appointed term, after which God will answer defiant acts with wrath and sustain the order of mercy forever. «bu imhâl, nâmütenâhî gidemez, elbette bunun bir ecel-i müsemmâsı vardır.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r15] verses 6:12 | God will gather the deniers, together with earlier deniers, in graves and at the gathering place; He will grant eternal mercy to those who follow His approval and punish the others for polytheism and sin. «kabirlerde ve mahşer yerinde- toplayacak da rızâsına doğru gidenleri rahmet-i ebediyesiyle bekâm ederken sizin de şirk ü mâsiyetlerinizin cezâsını verecektir.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r16] verses 6:12 | There is no doubt either about this gathering or about the coming of the Day of Resurrection. «Bunda, bu cem‘de ve bu kıyamet gününün geleceğinde hiç şekk ü şüphe yoktur.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r17] verses 6:12 | Those who lose themselves waste their original nature, sound reason, and special capacity through misuse; they then disbelieve the truth and wrong themselves. «Nefislerini ziyan edenler, yani kendilerinin rahmet-i ilâhiyeden sermayeleri olan fıtrat-ı asliyeyi, akl-ı selîmi, isti‘dâd-ı mahsûsu sû’-i isti‘mâl ile zâyi‘edenlerdir»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r18] verses 6:12 | Present appearances can reverse with time: what seems sweet today may be bitter tomorrow, and today's pain may become tomorrow's sweetness. «Bugün tatlı görünenler yarın acı, bugün acı gelenler[^5] yarın tatlı olabilir.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r2] verses 6:12 | A well-defined question is half of knowledge: first identify what one seeks, then frame the question clearly. «Fi’l-vâki‘tasvîr-i suâl, yani vaz‘-ı mes’ele ilmin nısfıdır denilir.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r3] verses 6:12 | The commanded question and answer are divine inspiration and inner signs, showing that God governs the human inner life. «O “bunu sor” ve arkasından da “şu cevâbı ver” diye emrediyor; bu suretle bunlarda Allah’a delâlet eden âyât-ı enfüsiye oluyorlar.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r4] verses 6:12 | Observing changes in the heavens and earth makes their need for an owner and administrator felt, so the answer that they belong to God is self-evident. «semâvât u arzın tehavvülât ve teessürâtını görmek ve bunların bir mâlik-i müdebbire muhtaç olduğunu hissetmek Allah’ın varlığını duymak demektir.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r5] verses 6:12 | Since God is the universal cause, creator, owner, and ruler, answering “to God” is an analytically self-evident proposition. «Ve zaten Allah demek bunların illet-i küllü, hâlıkı, mâliki, hâkimi demektir.»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r6] verses 6:12, 6:13 | The heavens and earth point to spatial beings and events, while the following verse points to temporal beings and events; together they establish God's mastery of both space and time and the core of proofs for the Creator and resurrection. «sonra وَلَهُۥ مَا سَكَنَ فِى ٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ âyetiyle zaman ve hâdisât-ı zamâniye irâe olunarak»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r7] verses 6:12 | The sura's opening statements about creating the heavens and earth and making darkness and light summarize spatial and temporal perspectives; creation and ordering signal causality, which these verses unfold. «sûrenin evvelindeki خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ وَجَعَلَ ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ وَٱلنُّورَ fıkraları da mekânî ve zamânî bu iki nokta-i nazarı icmâl etmiş»
[ELMALILI:6:12-18/r8] verses 6:12 | After identifying God as the cause of things, reflection should infer and witness His essential and moral attributes through successive questions and answers, moving from the beginning toward resurrection over time. «Allah teâlâ’nın sıfât-ı zâtiye ve mâneviyesini istidlâl ve istişhad ederek anlamaya»
