[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r15] verses 7:156 | A report says Iblis reached for the promise when he heard mercy encompassed everything, then despaired after hearing it was written for the Godfearing; the full description excludes Jews and Christians and points to Muhammad's community. «لما سمع إبليس ورحمتي وسعت كل شيء تطاول لها إبليس فلما سمع فسأكتبها»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r16] verses 7:156 | Exegetes say Moses's delegation was offered practices that Israel found too difficult: prayer across the earth and ritual purity, inner tranquility, and access to scripture; they requested these be confined to the ark and church, so the promise was assigned to Muhammad's community. «عرض الله هذه الخلال على قوم موسى فلم يتحملوها ولما انطلق وفد بني إسرائيل»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r2] verses 7:156 | Some identify the punishment here with the quake and its people; the statement means no one may object when God wills to punish. «وقيل العذاب هنا هو الرجفة ومن أشاء أصحابها والمعنى أنه لا اعتراض عليه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r3] verses 7:156 | Other interpretations take “whom I will” as those God will not pardon, any creatures He chooses, disbelievers, or a choice about hastening versus delaying punishment rather than abandoning it. «وقيل: من أشاء أن لا أعفو عنه، وقيل: من أشاء من خلقي كما أصبت به قومك»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r4] verses 7:156 | Al-Zamakhshari says God punishes whoever wisdom requires Him to punish, since forgiving such a person would cause corruption; Abu Hayyan calls this the Mu'tazilite method. «وقال الزمخشري: ممن أشاء من وجب علي في الحكمة تعذيبه ولم يكن في العفو عنه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r5] verses 7:156 | Ibn Abbas says God afflicts whom He wills even for a small sin. «وقال ابن عباس أصيب من أشاء على الذنب اليسير»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r6] verses 7:156 | Ibn Abbas explains the all-encompassing mercy as extending to believers' sins. «وقال أيضا وسعت كل شيء من ذنوب المؤمنين»

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r8] verses 7:156 | Ibn Zayd interprets the mercy as repentance offered universally. «وقال ابن زيد: هي التوبة على العموم»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r9] verses 7:156 | Al-Hasan says mercy is universal provision in this world and specific to believers in the afterlife. «وقال الحسن: هي في الدنيا بالرزق عامة وفي الآخرة بالمؤمنين خاصة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p192/r1] verses 7:156 | Nawf al-Bikali says Moses asked God to make his delegation belong to Muhammad's community; Nawf tells the audience to praise God that Israel's delegation was made for them. «وقال نوف البكالي: أن موسى عليه السلام قال: يا رب جعلت وفادتي لأمة محمد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p192/r2] verses 7:156 | Ibn Abbas and another group interpret those who fear as avoiding idolatry; Abu Hayyan says restricting it to shirk yields the Murji'ite view because the verse also requires deeds through zakat. «قال ابن عباس وفرقة: الشرك، وقالت فرقة: المعاصي فمن قال الشرك لا غير خرج»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p192/r3] verses 7:156 | Another group takes those who fear to mean those who avoid sins; Abu Hayyan says making all sins categorically excluded leads to the Mu'tazilite view. «وقالت فرقة: المعاصي فمن قال الشرك لا غير خرج إلى قول المرجئة ويرد عليه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p192/r4] verses 7:156 | Ibn Atiyya prefers a general meaning: it does not require avoiding every sin, since sins fall under God's will; taqwa is a protective barrier, and the lofty rank spurs listeners onward. «قال ابن عطية: والصواب أن تكون اللفظة عامة ولكن لا نقول لا بد اتقاء المعاصي»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p192/r5] verses 7:156 | The apparent meaning of zakat is almsgiving from wealth, as Ibn Abbas says; another report from him takes it as purifying deeds, and al-Hasan as sincerity in deeds. «الظاهر أنها زكاة المال وبه قال ابن عباس وروي عنه: ويؤتون الأعمال التي يزكون»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p192/r6] verses 7:156 | Abu Hayyan classifies obligations as refraining and acting; action concerns wealth or the person, and personal action includes knowledge and deeds, signaled respectively by taqwa, zakat, and belief in God's signs. «ولما كانت التكاليف ترجع إلى قسمين تروك وأفعال والأفعال قسمان راجعة إلى المال»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p192/r7] verses 7:156 | Exegetes understand the people described by these qualities as Muhammad's community. «وفهم المفسرون من قوله الذين يتقون إلى آخر الأوصاف إن المتصفين بذلك هم أمة محمد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p192/r8] verses 7:156 | Another possible construction distinguishes periods: the Godfearing who give zakat are those who did so before the Messenger, while believers in God's signs are those after his mission; the signs are the Qur'an, the inimitable Book. «ويحتمل أن يكون من باب التغاير بين المعطوف والمعطوف عليه فيكون قوله للذين يتقون»
