# Notes

## 30:50
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v18p78#2/r2] verses 30:50 | The reading آثار is plural; a singular reading is explained by its annexation to a singular, while the plural is also apt because God's mercy can mean many mercies, as in 14:34 and 16:18. «وتقرأ (آثار) على الجمع (¬5)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v18p78#2/r3] verses 30:50 | Muqatil says the traces of God's mercy are plants, the effect of rain; rain is God's mercy and favor to creation. «قال مقاتل: {آثار رحمت الله} يعني: النبت، وهو أثر المطر»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v18p78#2/r4] verses 30:50 | God revives the earth by making it grow after it had no plants. «وقوله: {كيف يحي الأرض بعد موتها} أي: كيف يجعلها تنبت بعد أن لم يكن فيها نبت»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v18p78#2/r5] verses 30:50 | The God who does this will raise the dead in the afterlife, so people must not deny resurrection; He can do everything, including life and death. «(إن ذلك) الذي فعل ما ترون؛ وهو الله تعالى: {لمحي الموتى} في الآخرة، فلا تكذبوا بالبعث»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:30:50-52/r1] verses 30:50 | The traces of God's mercy are the effects of rain: it makes the dry earth grow again, showing that God can resurrect the dead. «يعني: آثار المطر الذي هو رحمة الله تعالى»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v2p665/r4] verses 30:50 | The آثار of God's mercy to inspect are the rain. «قال: {فانظر إلى آثار رحمة الله} [الروم: 50] ، يعني: المطر.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v2p666/r1] verses 30:50 | The earth's revival means the plants God grows by means of rain. «كيف يحيي الأرض بعد موتها} [الروم: 50] ، يعني: النبات الذي أنبته الله تبارك وتعالى بذلك المطر.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v2p666/r2] verses 30:50 | The One who grew the plants by rain can raise creation on the Day of Resurrection. «أي: فالذي أنبت هذا النبات، يريد المطر، قادر على أن يبعث الخلق يوم القيامة.»
[ZAJJAJ:v4p190/r1] verses 30:50 | The reading alternates between singular أثر رحمت الله and plural آثار رحمت الله; the plural means traces of rain, which is a mercy from God. «يعني آثار المطر الذي هو رحمة من الله»
[ZAJJAJ:v4p190/r2] verses 30:50 | Reviving the earth means making it grow; this is analogous to raising the dead, and ذلك refers to God. «ذلك إشارة إلى الله عز وجل»

## 31:14
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v3p106#3/r1] verses 31:14 | Qatada explains the mother’s hardship during pregnancy as hardship upon hardship. «عن قتادة في قوله ﴿ حملته أمه وهنا على وهن ﴾ قال جهدا على جهد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r10] verses 31:14 | The first explanations parse the phrase as a circumstantial accusative; an alternative makes it the child’s condition through successive developmental stages. «وانتصب على هذه الأقوال على الحال»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r11] verses 31:14 | Isa al-Thaqafi and Abu Amr in one transmission read both occurrences of “wahn” with an open hāʾ; most readers use a quiescent hāʾ. «وقرأ عيسى الثقفي، وأبو عمرو في رواية: وهنا على وهن، بفتح الهاء فيهما»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r12] verses 31:14 | The open-vowel form may resemble alternate pronunciations of “hair,” or may be the regular accusative verbal noun of “to weaken.” «فاحتمل أن يكون كالشعر والشعر، واحتمل أن يكون مصدر وهن بكسر الهاء»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r13] verses 31:14 | Most readers use “his weaning”; al-Hasan, Abu Rajaʾ, Qatada, al-Jahdari, and Yaʿqub read “his separation,” meaning weaning at the end of two years. «وقرأوا: وفصاله. وقرأ الحسن، وأبو رجاء، وقتادة، والجحدري، ويعقوب: وفصله، ومعناه الفطام»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r14] verses 31:14 | Exegetes agree on two years as the nursing period for rulings and maintenance, but disagree about the period that establishes milk kinship. «وأجمعوا على اعتبار العامين في مدة الرضاع في باب الأحكام والنفقات»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r15] verses 31:14 | Al-Nahhas prefers reading “that you give thanks to Me” as explanatory rather than infinitival. «وقال النحاس: الأجود أن تكون مفسرة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r16] verses 31:14 | Thank God for the gift of faith and parents for the gift of upbringing; “to Me is the return” warns during the exhortation. «لي: أي على نعمة الإيمان. ولوالديك: على نعمة التربية إلي المصير: توعد أثناء الوصية»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r2] verses 31:14 | The parent command follows Luqman’s warning against shirk as a further call to obey God, then explains why parents deserve kindness. «ووصينا الإنسان بوالديه: لما بين لقمان لابنه أن الشرك ظلم ونهاه عنه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r3] verses 31:14 | One view makes the parent command part of Luqman’s advice, which God reports; another makes it God’s words to Luqman. «فهو من كلام لقمان مما وصى به ابنه، أخبر الله عنه بذلك. وقيل: هو من كلام الله»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r4] verses 31:14 | A third view treats the verse as an insertion reinforcing a child’s obedience to a parent in obeying God. «وقيل: هذه الآية اعتراض بين أثناء وصيته للقمان»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r5] verses 31:14 | Al-Qurtubi says the correct view is that this verse and 29:8 were revealed concerning Saʿd ibn Abi Waqqas, as a group of exegetes also held. «وقال القرطبي: والصحيح أن هذه الآية وآية العنكبوت نزلتا في سعد بن أبي وقاص»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r6] verses 31:14 | A hadith gives the mother three shares of filial kindness before the father receives one, because the mother bears pregnancy, birth, nursing, and upbringing. «في الحديث الأمر ببر الأم ثلاث مرات، ثم ذكر الأب»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r7] verses 31:14 | Ibn Abbas explains “weakness upon weakness” as successive hardship and successive stages of creation. «وهنا على وهن، قال ابن عباس: شدة بعد شدة، وخلقا بعد خلق»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r8] verses 31:14 | Al-Dahhak explains “weakness upon weakness” as weakness repeatedly increasing. «وقال الضحاك: ضعفا بعد ضعف»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v8p413/r9] verses 31:14 | Qatada explains it as effort upon effort: the weakness of pregnancy, labor, and postpartum recovery. «وقال قتادة: جهدا على جهد، يعني: ضعف الحمل، وضعف الطلق، وضعف النفاس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p85/r1] verses 31:14 | The parent command is an interjected divine statement reinforcing Luqman's prohibition of shirk; it is not something God said to Luqman. «كلام مستأنف إعترض به على نهج الإستطراد في أثناء وصية لقمان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p85/r2] verses 31:14 | An alternative makes God say this to Luqman as a further command to be grateful and care for parents; al-Bahr instead attributes the advice to Luqman and says God reports it, but the author dismisses both alternatives. «وقيل : هو من كلامه تعالى قاله جل وعلا له»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p85/r3] verses 31:14 | The command means that God enjoined people to care for their parents. «والمعنى وأمرنا الإنسان برعاية والديه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p85/r4] verses 31:14 | Wahnan ala wahn may mean the mother grows increasingly weak as pregnancy progresses toward labor, or it may refer to successive weakness from pregnancy, labor, and postpartum recovery. «فالمراد تضعف ضعفا متزايدا بإزدياد ثقل الحمل إلى مدة الطلق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p85/r5] verses 31:14 | Grammatically, wahn may be a circumstantial verbal noun with an implied possessor, an intensified noun used as a circumstantial, or an absolute object of an omitted verb; in the first constructions the clause also describes the mother. «والمصدر حال من ( أمه ) بتقدير مضاف أي ذات وهن»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p85/r6] verses 31:14 | The circumstance may instead describe the child, which accords with Mujahid's report that the child was weak atop the mother's weakness; this avoids the objection that the child's weakness should decrease rather than increase. «ما أخرجه إبن جرير وإبن أبي حاتم عن مجاهد أنه قال : ( وهنا ) الولد ( على وهن )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p85/r7] verses 31:14 | Isa al-Thaqafi and one transmission from Abu Amr read both instances of wahn with a fatḥa on hāʾ. «وقرأ عيسى الثقفي وأبو عمرو في رواية ( وهنا على وهن ) بفتح الهاء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p85/r8] verses 31:14 | The vocalization may reflect the regular Kufa pattern of moving a guttural consonant's vowel, or be the verbal noun of wahina/yawhanu; al-Qamus does not confine either of the two noun forms to a single verb pattern. «فإن مصدره جاء كذلك وهذا كما يقال تعب يتعب تعبا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86#2/r1] verses 31:14 | The poem argues that a mother's pregnancy, sleepless care, feeding, and self-sacrifice make her right immense and repayment small. «لأمك حق لو علمت كبير كثيرك ياهذا لديه يسير»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86#2/r2] verses 31:14 | One view defines commanded gratitude as obedience and pleasing acts: prayer and fasting toward God, and kinship and dutifulness toward parents. «فقيل هو الطاعة وفعل ما يرضي كالصلاة والصيام بالنسبة إليه تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86#2/r3] verses 31:14 | Sufyan says the five daily prayers express gratitude to God, while supplicating for one's parents after them expresses gratitude to them. «عن سفيان بن عيينة من صلى الصلوات الخمس فقد شكر الله تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86#2/r4] verses 31:14 | Alusi treats Sufyan's statement as an illustration of some forms of gratitude, not an exhaustive definition. «ولعل هذا بيان لبعض أفراد الشكر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86#2/r5] verses 31:14 | The return to God explains why the command must be obeyed: people return to Him, and He repays their disobedience. «إلي المصير 41 تعليل لوجوب الامتثال بالأمر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r1] verses 31:14 | Fiṣāl means weaning and ending suckling; the reading faṣl is broader, while fiṣāl is more apt for nursing despite sharing the same root, as al-Tibi notes. «وفصاله أي فطامه وترك إرضاعه وقرأ الحسن وأبو رجاء وقتادة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r10] verses 31:14 | An Arab is said to have carried his mother to pilgrimage on his back while chanting that he carries his mother, who had carried him. «وعن بعض العرب أنه حمل أمه إلى الحج على ظهره»
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r2] verses 31:14 | Al-Hasan, Abu Raja, Qatada, al-Jahdari, and Yaqub read faṣl in place of fiṣāl. «وقرأ الحسن وأبو رجاء وقتادة والجحدري ويعقوب ( وفصله )»
