# Notes

## 7:155
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p387/r6] verses 7:155 | Ibn Yasar and al-Suddi say the quake came after the people requested to see God openly (4:153). «قال ابن يسار، والسدي: (إنما أخذتهم الرجفة لأنهم قالوا: {أرنا الله جهرة})»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p387/r7] verses 7:155 | Another report from Ibn Abbas says they prayed for a privilege never given to anyone before or after them, which God disliked and answered with the quake. «وقال ابن عباس: (إنهم قالوا في دعائهم: اللهم أعطنا ما لم تعطه أحدا قبلنا»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p387/r8] verses 7:155 | Abu al-Jawza transmitting from Ibn Abbas says the quake came because the seventy had failed to forbid calf worship. «وروى أبو الجوزاء (¬6) عنه قال: (إنما أخذتهم الرجفة لأنهم كانوا [لم] (¬7) ينهوا عن عبادة العجل)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p387/r9] verses 7:155 | Qatada, Ibn Jurayj, and al-Qurazi likewise say the seventy did not leave their people during calf worship or enjoin right and forbid wrong. «ونحو ذلك قال قتادة وابن جريج (¬9) والقرظي (¬10) قالوا: (إنهم لم # يزايلوا قومهم حين عبدوا العجل،»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p389/r1] verses 7:155 | Al-Zajjaj interprets Moses as saying that God could have killed them before testing them with the event that caused the quake. «قال الزجاج: (أي: لو شئت أمتهم من قبل أن تبتليهم بما أوجب عليهم الرجفة)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p389/r2] verses 7:155 | Al-Suddi says Moses feared returning to Israel with its best men dead and no one to corroborate him; God then revived them. «وقال السدي: (قال موسى: يا رب كيف أرجع إلى بني إسرائيل وقد أهلكت خيارهم،»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p389/r3] verses 7:155 | Moses feared Israel would suspect him and not believe the seventy had died, so he asked why God had not destroyed them all, including him, before the meeting when Israel could witness it. «أن موسى خاف أن يتهمه بنو إسرائيل على السبعين إذا عاد إليهم»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p389/r4] verses 7:155 | Al-Farra, al-Kalbi, and others say Moses thought the quake killed them for the calf worship, but the actual cause was their demand to see God (4:153). «قال الفراء: (ظن موسى # أنهم أهلكوا باتخاذ [أصحابه] (¬1) العجل،»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p389/r5] verses 7:155 | Ibn al-Anbari rejects the idea that Moses thought God punished people for others' sins; he reads the question as a rhetorical denial, 'You would not destroy us for the fools' deed,' and says Moses did not know the quake's actual cause. «ابن الأنباري (¬7) , [و] (¬8) على هذا أنكر موسى»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p389/r6] verses 7:155 | Al-Mubarrad reads the question as an appeal meaning, 'Do not destroy us.' «قال المبرد: (هذا استفهام استعطاف أي: لا تهلكنا)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p391/r1] verses 7:155 | The pronoun 'it' refers back to the trial: the fools' lapse was God's testing, by which He misled some and guided others; this supports Moses's plea not to destroy them for their act. «إن تلك الفتنة] (¬1) التي وقع فيها السفهاء لم تكن {إلا فتنتك} أي: اختبارك، وابتلاؤك،»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p391/r2] verses 7:155 | The author calls the verse manifest proof against the Qadariyya, leaving them no excuse. «وهذه الآية من الحجج الظاهرة على القدرية التي لا يبقى لهم معها عذر»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:155/r1] verses 7:155 | God ordered Moses to bring Israelites to apologize for worshipping the calf, so Moses selected seventy men for the appointed meeting. «أمره الله تعالى أن يأتيه في ناس من بي إسرائيل يعتذرون إليه من عبادة العجل»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:155/r2] verses 7:155 | After hearing God's speech, the seventy asked Moses to show them God openly; the violent shaking killed them all. «فلما سمعوا كلام الله قالوا لموسى: أرنا الله جهرة فأخذتهم { الرجفة } وهي الحركة الشديدة، فماتوا جميعا»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:155/r3] verses 7:155 | Moses says God could have destroyed them and him before the journey, when Israel would have witnessed it and not suspected him; he initially thought they had died because their companions worshipped the calf. «وكان بنو إسرائيل يعاينون ذلك ولا يتهمونني، ظن أنهم أهلكوا باتخاذ أصحابهم العجل»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:155/r4] verses 7:155 | Despite Moses' question about the foolish ones' deed, the seventy were actually destroyed because they asked to see God. «وإنما أهلكوا لمسألتهم الرؤية»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:155/r5] verses 7:155 | The fitna was God's test and trial: He let some be led astray and others remain protected, explaining the statement that He misguides and guides whom He wills. «أي: تلك الفتنة التي وقع فيها السفهاء لم تكن إلا فتنتك، أي: اختبارك وابتلاؤك أضللت بها قوما فافتتنوا، وعصمت آخرين وهذا معنى قوله»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p379/r10] verses 7:155 | Arabic permits both choosing from among the men and taking a direct object after the verb. «يقال اخترت من الرجال زيد واخترت الرجال زيدا»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p379/r11] verses 7:155 | Zajjaj cites an unnamed poet's phrase iḫtāra al-rijāla to support the construction without min. «ومنا الذي اختار الرجال سماحة. . .»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p379/r8] verses 7:155 | Moses selected six men from each of twelve tribes, totaling seventy-two, then reduced the group by two. «فبلغوا اثنين وسبعين رجلا فخفف منهم رجلين»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p379/r9] verses 7:155 | Iḫtāra qawmahu means iḫtāra min qawmihi: the preposition min is omitted and the verb takes a direct object. «اختار من قومه فحذفت " من " ووصل الفعل فنصب»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p380/r1] verses 7:155 | Al-rajfa is intense movement or a severe earthquake. «وهي الحركة الشديدة والزلزلة الشديدة»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p380/r2] verses 7:155 | A report says the mountain shook them and they died. «يقال إنه رجف بهم الجبل فماتوا»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p380/r3] verses 7:155 | Moses means that God could have killed them before bringing on the event that required the quake. «أي لو شئت أمتهم من قبل أن تأتيهم بما أوجب عليهم الرجفة»

## 7:156
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p238/r1] verses 7:156 | Nauf reports that the Israelites rejected promises of inward sakinah, memorized Torah, and prayer anywhere; Moses asks to join the later ummah, is told he cannot reach it, and the community of Moses is affirmed as a just, guided group. «أخبرني يحيى بن أبي كثير عن نوف البكالي قال لما انطلق موسى بوفد بني إسرائيل فناجاه ربه قال إني أجعل السكينة في قلوبهم»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p239/r1] verses 7:156 | Qatada says “we have turned to You” means “we have repented to You.” «عن قتادة في قوله تعالى ﴿ إنا هدنا إليك ﴾ قال تبنا إليك»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p243/r1] verses 7:156 | Al-Hasan and Qatada say God’s mercy encompasses the righteous and wicked in worldly life, but at the Resurrection it is reserved especially for the Godfearing. «عن الحسن وقتادة في قوله تعالى ﴿ ورحمتي وسعت كل شيء ﴾ قال وسعت في الدنيا البر والفاجر وهي يوم القيامة للذين اتقوا خاصة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p190/r10] verses 7:156 | Zayd ibn Ali and Abu Wajza read هدنا with kasra, from “to move”: the pronoun may be active, meaning they moved themselves to obedience, or passive, meaning they were moved and inclined toward God; the damma reading allows both. «وقرأ زيد بن علي وأبو وجزة هدنا بكسر الهاء من هاد يهيد إذا حرك»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p190/r11] verses 7:156 | The request coheres with the preceding confession: God is their Lord and patron, while they are repentant, submissive servants seeking forgiveness, mercy, and a written good. «وتضمنت هذه الجمل كونه تعالى هو ربهم ووليهم وأنهم تائبون عبيد له خاضعون»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p190/r5] verses 7:156 | The requested worldly good may be a good outcome, pleasant life, or righteous deed earning praise; the afterlife good includes paradise, vision, and reward, and best means any good favor or obedience, with paradise the highest afterlife good. «أي وأثبت لنا عاقبة وحياة طيبة أو عملا صالحا يستعقب ثناء حسنا في الدنيا»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p190/r6] verses 7:156 | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ibn Jubayr, Abu al-Aliya, Qatada, Dahhak, and al-Suddi explain هدنا as “we repented” or “returned to You.” «قاله ابن عباس ومجاهد وابن جبير وأبو العالية وقتادة والضحاك والسدي: من هاد يهود»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p190/r7] verses 7:156 | Ibn Bahr explains the phrase as approaching God through repentance. «وقال ابن بحر: تقربنا بالتوبة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p190/r8] verses 7:156 | Another explanation takes هدنا to mean that they inclined toward God. «وقيل: ملنا. ومنه قول الشاعر»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p190/r9] verses 7:156 | A poem about Salma and her neighbors uses هد for being inclined toward someone. «قد علمت سلمى وجاراتها ... أني من الله لها هد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r1] verses 7:156 | The statement about punishment and mercy apparently begins a new report; the punishment may include the people seized by the quake. «الظاهر أنه استئناف إخبار عن عذابه ورحمته ويندرج في قوله: أصيب به من أشاء أصحاب الرجفة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r10] verses 7:156 | Al-Zamakhshari says every Muslim, disbeliever, obedient person, and sinner lives within God's blessings; Abu Hayyan sees this as expanding al-Hasan's view of universal worldly provision. «وأما رحمتي فمن حالها وصفتها أنها واسعة كل شيء ما من مسلم ولا كافر»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r11] verses 7:156 | Zayd ibn Ali, al-Hasan, Tawus, and Amr ibn Fa'id are reported to read من أساء (“whoever does wrong”) instead of من أشاء. «وقرأ زيد بن علي والحسن وطاوس وعمرو بن فائد من أساء من الإساءة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p191/r12] verses 7:156 | Abu Amr al-Dani says this reading is not soundly transmitted from al-Hasan, Tawus, or Amr ibn Fa'id; Sufyan ibn Uyayna once read and liked it, but Abd al-Rahman al-Muqri rebuked him, and Sufyan said he had not recognized the innovators' argument. «وقال أبو عمرو الداني: لا تصح هذه القراءة عن الحسن وطاوس وعمرو بن فائد»
