# Notes

## 18:74
[RAZI-FULL:v21p132#2/r3] verses 18:71, 18:74 | Others rank امر as more severe because a damaged boat might kill many, while the boy's death took one life, and امر means a great calamity. «قال : لأن خرق السفينة يؤدي إلى إتلاف نفوس كثيرة وهذ القتل ليس إلا إتلاف شخص واحد»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p132/r1] verses 18:74 | غلام can mean an adolescent, as a poem contrasts an old man with a young man and its root relates to youthful sexual desire, or simply a small child; the Quran leaves his setting, faith, age, and manner of killing unspecified. «وليس في القرآن كيف لقياه هل كان يلعب مع جمع من الغلمان الصبيان أو كان منفردا»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p132/r2] verses 18:74 | Although the word suits a child, بغير نفس more readily suggests an adult because a child is not subject to killing; the verse gives no method of killing. «إلا أن قوله : { بغير نفس } أليق بالبالغ منه بالصبي»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p132/r3] verses 18:74 | They read زاكية with alif; the others read زكية without it. Al-Kisai treats the forms as synonymous for purity, while Abu ʿAmr says زاكية is one who never sinned and زكية one who sinned then repented. «قرأ نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو زاكية بالألف والباقون زكية بغير ألف»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p132/r4] verses 18:74 | Moses appears to think killing is lawful only as retaliation for another life, though other grounds can make blood lawful; retaliation is simply the strongest and clearest ground. «وجوابه أن السبب الأقوى هو ذلك»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p61/r5] verses 18:71, 18:74 | According to the exegetes, the definite ship was a passing boat the pair had not found earlier; its crew recognized Khidr and carried them without payment, while the boy was an unspecified child met on the road. «حسب التفاسير أن الخضر وموسى - عليه السلام - لم يجدا سفينة لما جاءا إلى الساحل ثم جاءت سفينة مارّة»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p68/r2] verses 18:71, 18:74 | Damaging a plank in the ship to preserve it is less shocking than killing a boy without an apparent cause, so the milder word imr suits the ship and the harsher nukr suits the killing. «خرق السفينة ونزع لوح خشب منها دون قتل الغلام شناعة فإنه إنما خرق السفينة لتبقى لمالكيها»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p68/r3] verses 18:71, 18:74 | Qatadah says nukr is more severe than imr. «وعن قتادة: النُكر أشدّ من الإمر»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p68/r4] verses 18:71, 18:74 | Each description fits its act; the two words would not be apt if exchanged. «فجاء كل على ما يلائم ولم يكن ليحسن مجيء أحد الوصفين في موضع الآخر»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p68/r5] verses 18:71, 18:74 | The difference between imr and nukr also contributes to the verse-endings. «وهذا الاختلاف يدخل في فواصل الآي في القرآن الكريم»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74#2/r1] verses 18:74 | Bighayri nafs may modify qatalta, be a circumstantial phrase describing killer or victim, or describe an omitted verbal noun; Abu al-Baqāʾ’s circumstantial analysis is judged very remote. «قوله: { بغير نفس } فيه ثلاثة أوجه، أحدها: انها متعلقة ب " قتلت ".»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74#2/r2] verses 18:74 | These readers pronounce nukran with two dammas; the rest use one damma followed by sukūn, treated as dialectal variants or as a base form and its derivative. «قوله: " نكرا " قرأ نافع وأبو بكر وابن ذكوان بضمتين»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74#2/r3] verses 18:74 | Shayʾan can mean the verbal action, “a grievous coming,” or the object, “a reprehensible deed.” «و " شيئا ": يجوز أن يراد به المصدر، أي: مجيئا نكرا، وأن»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74#2/r4] verses 18:74 | One view makes imran more severe than nukran because damaging a ship endangers many lives, unlike killing one person. «وهل النكر أبلغ من الإمر أو بالعكس. فقيل: الإمر أبلغ؛ لأن قتل أنفس»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74#2/r5] verses 18:74 | The opposing view makes nukran more severe because killing is irreversible, whereas ship damage can be repaired. «بخلاف خرق السفينة فإنه يمكن تداركه، ولذلك قال: " ألم أقل لك "»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74/r1] verses 18:74 | These readers use zākiya with an alif and a light yāʾ, analyzing it as the ordinary active participle; the others use zakiyya without alif and with doubled yāʾ for emphasis. «قوله: { زاكية }: قرأ " زاكية " بألف وتخفيف الياء نافع»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74/r2] verses 18:74 | The remaining readers use zakiyya, a faʿīla form conveying intensification. «وبدون الألف وتشديد الياء الباقون.»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74/r3] verses 18:74 | A ghulām is normally a boy who has not reached puberty, but may refer figuratively to a grown man by reference to his earlier age; Layla’s verse uses ghulām for a man handling a spear. «فقيل: مجازا باعتبار ما كان. ومنه قول ليلى:»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74/r4] verses 18:74 | Another poet also uses ghulām for a grown man, including one who is not a poet when verbally challenged. «وقال آخر:»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74/r5] verses 18:74 | An alternative takes ghulām literally from iġlām, associated with coming first or reaching maturity, which applies to a postpubescent person. «وقيل: بل هو حقيقة لأنه من الإغلام وهو السبق، وذلك إنما يكون»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74/r6] verses 18:74 | Al-Zamakhshari explains the conjunctions: ship damage is the conditional result, while killing is included in the condition after the boy’s encounter and qāla aqtalta is its result. «جعل " خرقها " جزاء للشرط، وجعل " قتله " من جملة الشرط»
[SAMIN-DURR:18:74/r7] verses 18:74 | Al-Zamakhshari says the ship was not damaged immediately upon boarding, while the killing immediately followed meeting the boy, explaining the different conjunctions. «لأن الخرق لم يتعقب الركوب، وقد تعقب القتل لقاء الغلام "»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r85] verses 18:74 | Khiḍr reportedly killed the boy by striking him in the head with a stone. «reportedly by striking him in the head with a stone»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r86] verses 18:74, 5:32 | Moses views the boy as innocent, sinless, and unlawfully killed, contrary to Mosaic law; he considers this worse than damaging the ship. «a young, innocent boy who had slain no other soul or even committed a major sin»
[STUDYQURAN:18:60/r87] verses 18:74 | Mystically, the slain boy symbolizes the evil-inclined soul overcome by conquering anger and other passions. «the slain boy represents the soul that inclines toward evil and is “slain” when one overcomes the anger»
[TABATABAI:18:72-77/r10] verses 18:71, 18:74 | Nukr is what people find abhorrent; killing is more appalling than damaging a boat, which only indirectly risks lives. «وهو أفظع وأفجع عند الناس من الخرق الذي يستوجب عادة هلاك النفوس»
[TABATABAI:18:72-77/r3] verses 18:74 | The boat exit is omitted. “He killed him” joins the conditional sequence, while “he said” answers “when,” so the passage foregrounds Moses’ objection rather than the killing. «فخرجا من السفينة وانطلقا»
[TABATABAI:18:72-77/r4] verses 18:74, 18:77 | The syntax of the boy and wall episodes matches: the acts are part of the conditional sequence and Moses’ objection is its focus; unlike the boat episode, where damaging the ship itself answers “when.” «وبذلك يظهر أن العمدة في الكلام ذكر اعتراض موسى لا ذكر القتل»
[TABATABAI:18:72-77/r5] verses 18:71, 18:74, 18:77 | The verses tell one story of Moses’ three successive objections, not three separate stories of Khidr’s actions each followed by an objection. «فالقصة قصة اعتراضاته فهي واحدة لا قصة أعمال هذا واعتراضات ذاك»
[TABATABAI:18:72-77/r6] verses 18:74 | Zakiyya means pure from sins; Moses’ question objects to killing someone who appeared innocent. «والمراد طهارتها من الذنوب لعدم البلوغ كما يشعر به قوله»
[TABATABAI:18:72-77/r7] verses 18:74 | Some infer from “without a life” that the boy was an adult, since a minor cannot commit murder requiring retaliation. «وربما استفيد من قوله { بغير نفس } أنه كان شابا بالغا»
[TABATABAI:18:72-77/r8] verses 18:74 | Ghulam can refer to an adult or a minor, so the word alone does not prove he was underage; Moses means no offense appeared to warrant death. «ولا دلالة في إطلاق الغلام عليه على عدم بلوغه لأن الغلام يطلق على البالغ وغيره»
[TABATABAI:18:72-77/r9] verses 18:74 | “Without a life” means without a retaliatory killing that could legally justify his death; no such offense was apparent to Moses. «أي بغير قتل منها لنفس قتلا مجوزا لقتلها قصاصا وقودا»
[THALABI:v6p184/r1] verses 18:74 | Al-Khidr found boys playing, selected a handsome bright-faced boy, laid him down, and cut his throat; Ibn Abbas says he had not reached puberty. «قال سعيد بن جبير: وجد الخضر غلمانا يلعبون، وأخذ غلاما ظريفا»
