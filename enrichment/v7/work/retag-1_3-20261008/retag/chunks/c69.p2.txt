es 9:128 | ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Yaḥyā proposes an order emphasizing that the Messenger is from the people, caring for believers, and compassionate. «وقال عبد العزيز بن يحيى: نظم الآية لقد جاءكم رسول من أنفسكم عزيز حريص بالمؤمنين رؤوف رحيم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129#2/r6] verses 9:128 | He says the Prophet is occupied with his people's affairs and will intercede for them; they need not fear their hardships if they keep his Sunnah, since he wants their entry into Paradise. «عزيز عليه ما عنتم لا يهمه إلا شأنكم، وهو القائم بالشفاعة لكم فلا تهتموا بما عنتم ما أقمتم على سنته فإنه لا يرضيه إلا دخولكم الجنة»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129#3/r1] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Ubayy ibn Kaʿb called the two closing verses of the surah the Quran's most recent covenant with God. «وحكى النقاش عن أبي بن كعب أنه قال: أقرب القرآن عهدا بالله تعالى هاتان الآيتان»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129#3/r2] verses 9:128 | Yūsuf ibn Mihrān reports Ibn ʿAbbās as saying these words were the last part of the Quran revealed; al-Māwardī also mentions this report. «وروى يوسف بن مهران عن ابن عباس أن آخر ما نزل من القرآن { لقد جآءكم رسول من أنفسكم } وهذه الآية ذكره الماوردي»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129#3/r3] verses 9:128 | Qurṭubī says Ibn ʿAbbās had another report, discussed under al-Baqara, and regards that conflicting report as more sound. «وقد ذكرنا عن ابن عباس خلافه على ما ذكرناه في البقرة، وهو أصح»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129#3/r4] verses 9:128 | Muqātil says the verse was revealed in Mecca, but Qurṭubī finds this unlikely because the surah is Medinan. «وقال مقاتل: تقدم نزولها بمكة. وهذا فيه بعد لأن السورة مدنية»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129#3/r5] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Yaḥyā ibn Jaʿda reports that ʿUmar normally required two witnesses for a Quranic verse, but accepted these two verses from an Anṣārī without asking for proof. «وقال يحيى بن جعدة: كان عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه لا يثبت آية في المصحف حتى يشهد عليها رجلان فجاءه رجل من الأنصار بالآيتين»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129#3/r6] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Qurṭubī's scholars identify the Anṣārī as Khuzayma ibn Thābit; ʿUmar accepted his single testimony because the verse's accurate description of the Prophet independently confirmed it. «قال علماؤنا: الرجل هو خزيمة بن ثابت، وإنما أثبتهما عمر رضي الله عنه بشهادته وحده لقيام الدليل على صحتها في صفة النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129#3/r7] verses 9:128, 9:129 | This case differs from al-Aḥzāb 33:23: that verse required testimony from both Zayd and Khuzayma because both had heard it from the Prophet. «فإن تلك ثبتت بشهادة زيد وخزيمة لسماعهما إياها من النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r1] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Ubayy ibn Kab says these verses were nearest in time to the Quran’s descent from heaven; Said ibn Jubayr instead says the last revelation was the verse about returning to God. «هاتان الآيتان في قول أبي أقرب القرآن بالسماء عهدا»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r10] verses 9:128 | Ibn Qusayt al-Makki reads from yourselves with a fatḥa on fāʾ, deriving it from excellence; the reading is also reported from the Prophet and Fatima and means a most distinguished messenger. «وقرأ عبد الله بن قسيط المكي من «أنفسكم» بفتح الفاء»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r11] verses 9:128 | Another interpretation takes from yourselves to mean from among the most obedient of you. «وقيل: من أنفسكم أي أكثركم طاعة.»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r12] verses 9:128 | It is grievous to the Prophet when his people are burdened; anat means hardship, as in a difficult and destructive steep slope. «قوله تعالى: { عزيز عليه ما عنتم } أي يعز عليه مشقتكم.»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r13] verses 9:128 | Ibn al-Anbari says the root of taʿannut is imposing severity: to make someone suffer is to constrain them with something hard to perform. «وقال ابن الأنباري: أصل التعنت التشديد»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r14] verses 9:128 | Al-Qurtubi first treats ma as a verbal noun clause with a fronted predicate, then prefers the alternative that makes the phrase the subject of grievous and grievous an adjective of the Messenger. ««عزيز» صفة للرسول، وهو أصوب.»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r15] verses 9:128 | Al-Farra says the praise terms could also be read in the accusative as circumstantial descriptions. «قال الفراء: ولو قرىء عزيزا عليه ما عنتم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r16] verses 9:128 | Al-Nahhas calls this the best meaning consistent with Arabic usage, transmitting al-Khuraybi’s explanation that the Prophet is grieved if people enter Hell and keen for them to enter Paradise. «وأحسن ما قيل في معناه مما يوافق كلام العرب»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r2] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Al-Qurtubi reconciles the statements by suggesting Ubayy meant these verses were nearest to heaven after the verse about returning to God. «فيحتمل أن يكون قول أبي: أقرب القرآن بالسماء عهدا»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r3] verses 9:128 | The majority says the address is to Arabs, recalling the favor that a messenger came in their language, was understood by them, and gave them lasting honor. «والخطاب للعرب في قول الجمهور، وهذا على جهة تعديد النعمة عليهم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r4] verses 9:128 | Al-Zajjaj says the address is to all people and means that a human messenger came to them; al-Qurtubi first calls the Arab reading more correct. «وقال الزجاج: هي مخاطبة لجميع العالم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r5] verses 9:128 | Ibn Abbas says every Arab tribe had a share in the Prophet’s birth, so the address means an Ismaelite messenger came to the Arabs. «قال ابن عباس: ما من قبيلة من العرب إلا ولدت النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
