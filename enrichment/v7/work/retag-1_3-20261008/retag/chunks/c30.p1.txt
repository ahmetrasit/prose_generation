[ABDURRAZZAQ:v1p145/r2] verses 4:1 | Qatada reports hearing that the Prophet said to fear God and maintain ties of kinship. «عن قتادة قال بلغني أن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال اتقوا الله وصلوا الأرحام»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p488/r1] verses 4:1 | Raqīb is an intensive form from raqaba: to watch an affair closely to ascertain its true state, with an associated sense of guarding; the passage lists watcher, arrow, snake, lookout, and waiting as related usages. «الرقيب: فعيل للمبالغة من رقب يرقب رقبا ورقوبا ورقبانا»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p488/r2] verses 4:1 | Abu Dawud’s cited verse uses الرقباء in describing watchers positioned in ambush, as lexical evidence for the watcher sense. «وقال أبو داود: كمقاعد الرقباء للضرباء أيديهم نواهد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p492/r2] verses 4:1 | Most scholars consider the surah Medinan, with a reported exception for the verse about returning trusts; al-Nahhas calls it Meccan, and al-Naqqash says it descended at the Migration, though all agree it includes Medinan material. «الجمهور على أن هذه السورة مدنية»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p492/r3] verses 4:1 | Al-Bukhari identifies the inheritance question about collateral relatives as the last verse revealed. «وفي البخاري: آخر آية نزلت يستفتونك، قل الله يفتيكم في الكلالة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p492/r4] verses 4:1 | The opening follows the prior surah’s account of communities and God’s promise not to waste anyone’s work by returning to the common human origin, encouraging concord, affection, and mutual care. «ومناسبة هذه السورة لما قبلها أنه تعالى لما ذكر أحوال المشركين»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p492/r5] verses 4:1 | The single-origin reminder argues for piety: the One able to create humanity in this remarkable way deserves to be obeyed and feared. «ومن كان قادرا على مثل هذا الإيجاد الغريب الصنع»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p492/r6] verses 4:1 | Mentioning one human origin appeals to natural affinity among kindred species and encourages people to unite in piety. «ونبه بقوله: من نفس واحدة، على ما هو مركوز في الطباع»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p492/r7] verses 4:1 | Al-nās is general because of its definite article, and both the call to piety and its rationale apply generally. «والظاهر في الناس: العموم، لأن الألف واللام فيه تفيده»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p492/r8] verses 4:1 | An alternative takes “people” as the Meccans, based on Arabs’ custom of pleading by God and kinship. «وقيل: المراد بالناس أهل مكة»

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p493/r1] verses 4:1 | When a people-address includes disbelievers, the Qur'an often follows with proofs of divine unity and lordship; when it addresses believers, it often follows with reminders of blessings they already recognize. «إذا كان الخطاب والنداء بيا أيها الناس وكان للكفرة فقط»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v3p493/r10] verses 4:1 | Al-Asamm says reason alone cannot establish that humanity was created from one self; that claim rests on transmitted revelation. «وقال الأصم: لا يدل العقل على أن الخلق مخلوقين من نفس واحدة»
