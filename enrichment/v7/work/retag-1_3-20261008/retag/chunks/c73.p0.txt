# Verses
- 17:110: قُلِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ أَوِ ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ ۖ أَيًّۭا مَّا تَدْعُوا۟ فَلَهُ ٱلْأَسْمَآءُ ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ ۚ وَلَا تَجْهَرْ بِصَلَاتِكَ وَلَا تُخَافِتْ بِهَا وَٱبْتَغِ بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ سَبِيلًۭا
- 17:111: وَقُلِ ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِى لَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًۭا وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ شَرِيكٌۭ فِى ٱلْمُلْكِ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ وَلِىٌّۭ مِّنَ ٱلذُّلِّ ۖ وَكَبِّرْهُ تَكْبِيرًۢا

# Notes

## 17:110
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p235/r5] verses 17:110 | Ay is originally interrogative; extra mā makes it conditional, so tadʿū is jussive and the answer begins with fa. The beautiful names permit invocation by any name because the referent is one. «فإذا اقترنت بها ( ما ) الزائدة أفادت الشرط»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p237/r1] verses 17:110 | “Call upon Allah or al-Raḥmān” can mean mention either name in prayer; alternatively, it means name your Lord by either, with the first object omitted. «ومعنى { ادعوا الله أو ادعوا الرحمن } ادعوا هذا الاسم»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p237/r2] verses 17:110 | The prayer-volume instruction connects to the naming command and supports the occasion involving the Prophet's audible invocation of al-Raḥmān. «بسبب جهر النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم في دعائه باسم الرحمن»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p237/r3] verses 17:110 | Early authorities explained ṣalāt here as either supplication or formal prayer; those choosing formal prayer mean its recitation, which can be loud or quiet. «فسرها السلف هنا بالمعنيين»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p237/r4] verses 17:110 | In either interpretation, the Prophet audibly invoked al-Raḥmān; some polytheists asked who it was or accused him of invoking two gods, while others insulted the Qur'an, Messenger, or al-Raḥmān as another lord. «وعلى كلا الاحتمالين فقد جهر النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم بذكر الرحمن»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p237/r5] verses 17:110 | Opponents may have read public recitation as provocation; excessive loudness could inflame them and harden disbelief when the aim was to soften hearts. «فأمره الله تعالى بأن لا يجهر بصلاته هذا الجهر تجنبا»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p237/r6] verses 17:110 | The ban targets excessive loudness; the ban on whispering prevents secret or wholly silent prayer so potential converts can hear it. «والمقصود من الكلام النهي عن شدة الجهر»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r10] verses 17:110 | Ibn ʿAbbās also takes ṣalāt as Qur'an recitation in prayer; loud recitation brought abuse, so the Prophet was told to use moderate volume audible to companions but not provoking polytheists. «والسبب أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم جهر بالقرآن فسمعه المشركون»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r11] verses 17:110 | Ibn Sīrīn says the verse was revealed about Bedouins reciting tashahhud aloud. «قال ابن سيرين: كان الأعراب يجهرون بتشهدهم، فنزلت الآية»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r12] verses 17:110 | Abū Bakr recited quietly to privately supplicate; ʿUmar aloud to repel Satan and wake sleepers. Later Abū Bakr was told to raise his voice and ʿUmar lower his. «فلما نزلت هذه الآية، قيل لأبي بكر: ارفع أنت قليلا»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r13] verses 17:110 | ʿĀʾisha also takes ṣalāt to mean tashahhud. «الصلاة» يراد بها في هذه الآية التشهد»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r14] verses 17:110 | They say not to overdo loudness or ruin prayer by whispering, but to follow a middle way. «ولا تحسن صلاتك في الجهر ولا تسئها في السر»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r15] verses 17:110 | Ibn Zayd says the verse rejects alternating loud and quiet practices of Gospel and Torah communities. «النهي عما يفعله أهل الإنجيل والتوراة من رفع الصوت»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r16] verses 17:110 | Another report says do not recite aloud in daytime prayers or whisper in night prayers, but follow the prescribed way. «ولا تجهر بصلاة النهار ولا تخافت بصلاة الليل»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r17] verses 17:110 | Ibn Masʿūd says one who can make his ears hear has not whispered; the author calls it the basic linguistic sense, though the Abū Bakr report complicates it. «وقال عبد الله بن مسعود لم يخافت من أسمع أذنيه»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r3] verses 17:110 | Ibn ʿAbbās says polytheists heard Muhammad invoke Allah and al-Raḥmān and objected that he had ordered one god but prayed to two. «سبب نزول هذه الآية أن المشركين سمعوا رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r4] verses 17:110 | Makḥūl says a polytheist heard the Prophet invoke al-Raḥmān and al-Raḥīm, mistook the name for a man in Yamāma, prompting the clarification. «وقال مكحول: تهجد رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم ليلة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r5] verses 17:110 | Ṭalḥa reads ayyan mā tadʿū fa-lahu al-asmāʾ, meaning God has all other beautiful names. «وقرأ طلحة بن مصرف «أيا ما تدعوا فله الأسماء»»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r6] verses 17:110 | Divine names express best attributes and require revealed authorization, not independent reasoning; a report lists ninety-nine names. «وهي بتوقيف، لا يصح وضع اسم الله بنظر إلا بتوقيف»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r7] verses 17:110 | Whichever name one invokes, it is the one God who possesses all beautiful names. «أي الأسماء تدعوا به فأنت مصيب له الأسماء الحسنى»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r8] verses 17:110 | Absolute whispering is inaudible even to speaker, but here the command means lowering the voice without total silence. «هذه هي حقيقته، ولكنه في الآية عبارة عن خفض الصوت»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p491#3/r9] verses 17:110 | They understand ṣalāt as supplication. «فقال ابن عباس وعائشة وجماعة: هي الدعاء»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r1] verses 17:110 | If salāt means ritual prayer, Ibn Abbas gives a reading-based interpretation: do not recite too loudly or too softly; calling recitation prayer has two grammatical explanations, including that it is part of prayer. «لاتجهر بقراءتك ولا تخافت بها فكأنه نهي عن شدة الجهر بالقراءة وشدة المخافتة»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r10] verses 17:110 | Ibn al-Saʾib says it was abrogated by the command to proclaim openly in 15:94. «نسخت بقوله فاصدع بما تؤمر»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r11] verses 17:110 | The author considers abrogation here unlikely on closer examination. «وعلى التحقيق وجود النسخ هاهنا بعيد»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r2] verses 17:110 | Ibn Abbas also interprets the command as forbidding ostentatious prayer and abandoning prayer from fear of people. «لا تصل مراءاة للناس ولا تدعها مخافة الناس قاله ابن عباس ايضا»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r3] verses 17:110 | A report from Aisha and Ibn Sirin says not to raise the voice in the prayer’s tashahhud. «لا تجهر بالتشهد في صلاتك روي عن عائشة في رواية وبه قال ابن سيرين»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r4] verses 17:110 | Ikrima interprets the command as avoiding both conspicuous performance and excessive concealment of prayer. «لا تجهر بفعل صلاتك ظاهرا ولا تخافت بها شديد الاستتار قاله عكرمة»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r5] verses 17:110 | Al-Hasan says not to make prayer’s public form good while making its inward reality bad. «لا تحسن علانيتها وتسيء سريرتها قاله الحسن»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r6] verses 17:110 | Al-Qadi Abu Yaʿla says to recite aloud in night prayer and quietly in day prayer, as commanded. «فاجهر في صلاة الليل وخافت في صلاة النهار على ما أمرناك به ذكره القاضي أبو يعلى»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r7] verses 17:110 | A second broad interpretation takes salāt here to mean supplication. «أن المراد بالصلاة الدعاء وهو قول عائشة وأبي هريرة ومجاهد»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r8] verses 17:110 | Mukhāfata means concealment or quietness; seek a middle course between loudness and quietness. «المخافتة الإخفاء يقال صوت خفيت وابتغ بين ذلك سبيلا أي اسلك بين الجهر والمخافتة طريقا»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p100/r9] verses 17:110 | Ibn Abbas reports that the command was abrogated by the instruction to remember God inwardly and quietly in 7:205. «نسخت هذه الآية بقوله واذكر ربك في نفسك تضرعا وخفية ودون الجهر من القول»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p98#3/r1] verses 17:110 | When the Prophet invoked the names al-Rahman and al-Rahim while prostrating at night in Mecca, polytheists thought he called two gods, identifying al-Rahman with Musaylima; this occasion prompted the verse. «فجعل يقول في سجوده يا رحمن يا رحيم فقال المشركون كان محمد يدعو إلها واحدا فهو الآن يدعو إلهين»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p98#3/r2] verses 17:110 | Maymun ibn Mihran says Arabs questioned the unfamiliar name al-Rahman after the Prophet began writing basmala al-Rahman al-Rahim following 27:30. «هذا الرحيم نعرفه فما الرحمن فنزلت هذه الاية قاله ميمون بن مهران»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p98#3/r3] verses 17:110 | Al-Dahhak says People of the Book asked why the Prophet mentioned al-Rahman so little when the Torah mentions it often. «إنك لتقل ذكر الرحمن وقد أكثر الله في التوراة هذا الاسم فنزلت هذه الآية»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p98#3/r4] verses 17:110 | The Prophet first recited aloud in Mecca; after the polytheists cursed the Quran and its messenger, he lowered his voice, but then companions could not hear him. «كان يرفع صوته بالقرآن بمكة فيسب المشركون القرآن»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p98#3/r5] verses 17:110 | Aisha reports that the verse was revealed when a Bedouin raised his voice during the tashahhud. «أن الأعرابي كان يجهر في التشهد ويرفع صوته فنزلت هذه الآية هذا قول عائشة»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p98#3/r6] verses 17:110 | Muqatil says that at dawn prayer by al-Safa, Abu Jahl challenged Muhammad’s recitation; when the Prophet lowered his voice, Abu Jahl boasted that he had silenced him. «فجهر بالقرآن في صلاة الغداة فقال أبو جهل لاتفتر على الله فخفض النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم صوته»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p98#3/r7] verses 17:110 | Invoking Allah or al-Rahman means choosing either name for the same God; any of God’s names may be invoked. «إن شئتم فقولوا يا ألله وإن شئتم فقولوا يارحمن فانهما يرجعان إلى واحد»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p98#3/r8] verses 17:110 | Al-Farra says mā in ayyāmā may be an extra particle, as in 23:40, or mean whichever, repeated because the two names have different forms. «وما قد تكون صلة كقوله عما قليل ليصبحن نادمين المؤمنون 40 وتكون في معنى أي معادة»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p128#2/r1] verses 17:110 | Against polytheists who reject God's attribute of mercy and the name al-Rahman, the verse says either name invokes the same God, who possesses the most beautiful names. «أي: لا فرق بين دعائكم له باسم "الله" أو باسم (3) " الرحمن "، فإنه ذو الأسماء الحسنى،»
