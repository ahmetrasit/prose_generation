110 | A literal interpretation of ritual prayer says do not pray for show or abandon prayer out of embarrassment; a similar interpretation is transmitted from Ibn Abbas. «فقد أخرج ابن عساكر عن الحسن أنه قال : المعنى لا تصل الصلاة رياء ولا تدعها حياء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r13] verses 17:110 | Most commentators prefer Ibn Abbas's first explanation: the command concerns the volume of Qur'anic recitation in prayer. «والأكثرون على التفسير المروي عنه أولا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r14] verses 17:110 | Whispering means speech so quiet that the speaker cannot hear it; Ibn Masud says that anyone who makes his own ears hear has not whispered. «ومن هنا قال ابن مسعود»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r15] verses 17:110 | Calling moderation a path evokes a way toward which people direct themselves and by which capable travelers reach their goal; Abd Allah ibn al-Shikhkhir and Abu Qilaba transmit that the best affairs are middle ones. «والتعبير عن الأمر الوسط بالسبيل باعتبار أنه أمر يتوجه إليه المتوجهون ويؤمه المقتدرون»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r16] verses 17:110 | Most commentators consider the verse still operative; one view calls it abrogated based on an Ibn Abbas report that the Meccan moderation rule lapsed after the Hijra. «والآية على ما يقتضيه كلام الأكثرين محكمة وقيل منسوخة بناء على ما أخرجه ابن مردويه وابن أبي حاتم عن ابن عباس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r17] verses 17:110 | A proposed abrogation by the command to call upon God humbly and privately is dismissed as untenable. «وقيل هي منسوخة بقوله تعالى ( ادعوا ربكم تضرعا وخفية ) وهو كما ترى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r18] verses 17:110 | The rulings on reciting louder than needed and whispering in the defined sense are treated in fiqh works, to which readers are referred. «حكم رفع الصوت بالقراءة فوق الحاجة وحكم المخافتة بالمعنى الذي سمعته المسطوران في كتب الفقه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r19] verses 17:110 | Abu Razin transmits a codex reading attributed to Abd Allah: “Do not whisper your voice nor raise it.” «ابن أبي داود في المصاحف عن أبي رزين قال قرأ عبد الله ( ولا تخافت بصوتك ولا تعال به»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r2] verses 17:110 | In Mecca, while the Prophet was concealed, he recited aloud in prayer; polytheists cursed the Qur'an, its sender, and its bearer, so God instructed him to moderate his voice while remaining audible to companions. «أخرج أحمد والبخاري ومسلم والترمذي والنسائي وابن حبان وغيرهم عن ابن عباس قال : نزلت ورسول الله»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r3] verses 17:110 | Here prayer can stand metonymically for its recitation, one of its parts, or the phrase can mean the recitation of your prayer; recitation includes the basmala and other verses. «وظاهره أن المراد بالصلاة القراءة التي هي أحد أجزائها مجازا ويجوز أن يكون الكلام على تقدير مضاف»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r4] verses 17:110 | A report specifies the basmala: the Prophet raised his voice with it, and polytheists associated al-Rahman with Musaylima, then mocked with clapping and whistling; this reading especially fits the preceding verse. «أخرج ابن أبي شيبة في المصنف عن سعيد
قال : كان النبي»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r5] verses 17:110 | Abu Bakr prayed at night very quietly and Umar very loudly; after they reported their disagreement, the Prophet told Abu Bakr to raise his voice somewhat and Umar to lower his. «ابن أبي حاتم عن الربيع قال : كان أبو بكر إذا صلى من الليل خفض صوته جدا وكان عمر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r6] verses 17:110 | Asked why he prayed quietly, Abu Bakr said he was confiding in his Lord, who already knew his need. «قيل لأبي بكر : لم تصنع هذا فقال : أناجي ربي وقد عرف حاجتي»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r7] verses 17:110 | Asked why he prayed loudly, Umar said the sound drove Satan away and awakened a sleepy person. «وقيل لعمر : لم تصنع هذا قال : اطرد الشيطان وأوقظ الوسنان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r8] verses 17:110 | Another explanation says not to make every prayer loud or every prayer quiet, but to recite aloud in some prayers such as Maghrib and Isha and quietly in others. «عن ابن عباس أن المعنى لا تجهر بصلاتك كلها ولا تخافت بها كلها وابتغ بين ذلك سبيلا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p194/r9] verses 17:110 | A report in the two Ṣaḥīḥ collections interprets prayer here as supplication; Mujahid and Ibn Abbas are also reported to hold this, with people loudly saying prayers such as “Have mercy on me.” «أخرج الشيخان وغيرهما عن عائشة قالت : إنما نزلت هذه الآية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p197/r13] verses 17:110 | Esoterically, calling on Allah signifies annihilation in the divine essence, while calling on al-Rahman signifies annihilation in the attribute of mercy, the mother of attributes and the means by which God shows mercy to creatures. «قيل دعاء الله بالفناء في الذات ودعاء الرحمن بالفناء في الصفة وصفة الرحمانية هي أم الصفات»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p197/r14] verses 17:110 | Whichever of these two stations is sought, the beautiful names belong to God rather than the annihilated servant: the servant is absent in essence-annihilation, and the name al-Rahman is unique to God in attribute-annihilation. «أيا ما طلبت من هذين المقامين ( فله ) تعالى في هذين المقامين ( الأسماء الحسنى ) لا لك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p197/r15] verses 17:110 | In the Futuhat, divine names belong both to Allah and al-Rahman, but Allah is invoked by a name's meaning and al-Rahman by its form; both forms ultimately signify the same meaning. «وفي الفتوحات المكية أنه تعالى جعل الأسماء الحسنى لله كما هي للرحمن»
