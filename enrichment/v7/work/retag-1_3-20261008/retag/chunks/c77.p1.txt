[ALUSI-FULL:v15p327/r5] verses 18:65 | Shafi'i, al-Muzani, and al-Sayrafi accept istishab to affirm or deny a state previously established without contrary probability; Hanafis and theologians use it only defensively, not to prove Khidr's current life. «وأما عندنا وكذا عند المتكلمين فهو من الحجج القاصرة التي لا تصلح للإثبالت وإنما تصلح للدفع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p327/r6] verses 18:65 | The missing person inherits under the affirmative view but not under the Hanafi approach, since inheritance is an affirmative ruling; non-inheritance can instead be maintained defensively. «فالمفقود يرث عنده لا عندنا لأن الإرث من باب الإثبات فلا يثبت به»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p327/r7] verses 18:65 | Ordinary custom makes survival through so many ages improbable, defeating istishab's condition that no contrary probability arise. «فإن العادة قاضية بعدم بقاء الآدمي تلك المدة المديدة والأحقاب العديدة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p328/r1] verses 18:65 | Custom is meaningful evidence: without it, a miracle's breach of custom could not produce belief or compel following; even if custom does not prove death, it creates a probability against istishab. «إن العادة دليل معتبر ولولا ذلك لم يؤثر خرق العادة بالمعجزة في وجوب الاعتقاد والاتباع»
