# Verses
- 7:57: وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى يُرْسِلُ ٱلرِّيَٰحَ بُشْرًۢا بَيْنَ يَدَىْ رَحْمَتِهِۦ ۖ حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ أَقَلَّتْ سَحَابًۭا ثِقَالًۭا سُقْنَٰهُ لِبَلَدٍۢ مَّيِّتٍۢ فَأَنزَلْنَا بِهِ ٱلْمَآءَ فَأَخْرَجْنَا بِهِۦ مِن كُلِّ ٱلثَّمَرَٰتِ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ نُخْرِجُ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ
- 7:155: وَٱخْتَارَ مُوسَىٰ قَوْمَهُۥ سَبْعِينَ رَجُلًۭا لِّمِيقَٰتِنَا ۖ فَلَمَّآ أَخَذَتْهُمُ ٱلرَّجْفَةُ قَالَ رَبِّ لَوْ شِئْتَ أَهْلَكْتَهُم مِّن قَبْلُ وَإِيَّٰىَ ۖ أَتُهْلِكُنَا بِمَا فَعَلَ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ مِنَّآ ۖ إِنْ هِىَ إِلَّا فِتْنَتُكَ تُضِلُّ بِهَا مَن تَشَآءُ وَتَهْدِى مَن تَشَآءُ ۖ أَنتَ وَلِيُّنَا فَٱغْفِرْ لَنَا وَٱرْحَمْنَا ۖ وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ ٱلْغَٰفِرِينَ

# Notes

## 7:57
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p188/r4] verses 7:57 | Ibn ʿAbbās says “before His mercy” means before the rain. «قال ابن عباس: (يريد: بين يدي المطر)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p188/r5] verses 7:57 | Al-Kalbī glosses it as ahead of His rain. «وقال الكلبي: (قدام مطره)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p188/r6] verses 7:57 | Abū Isḥāq takes the phrase as before the rain, which is God’s mercy. «وقال أبو إسحاق: (أي: بين يدي المطر الذي هو رحمته)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p188/r7] verses 7:57 | Abū Bakr explains “the hands of” metaphorically as precedence: Arabs say trials are before the Hour, and winds precede and signal rain. «اليدان تستعملهما العرب في المجاز على معنى التقدمة»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p189/r1] verses 7:57 | Aqalla means to carry, as does istaqalla; the winds carry clouds heavy with water, according to the exegetes. «يقال: أقل فلان الشيء أي (¬5) حمله، وكذلك استقله»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p189/r2] verses 7:57 | Sahāb is grammatically masculine although it is a collective form of sahāba, like tamr/tamra and jawz/jawza; this explains the masculine pronoun. «والسحاب لفظه مذكر وإن كان جمع سحابة، لذلك ذكر الكناية»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p189/r3] verses 7:57 | Ibn ʿAbbās says a dead land is one without vegetation. «قال ابن عباس: (يريد: ليس فيه نبات)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p189/r4] verses 7:57 | Al-Kalbī says it is a place where nothing grows. «وقال الكلبي: (إلى مكان لا ينبت)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p189/r5] verses 7:57 | Abū Bakr explains the cloud is driven to a place needing rain because rain has ceased there; the lām can mean “toward” or “for the sake of.” «وقال أبو بكر: (أي: سقنا السحاب لبلد وإلى بلد محتاج إلى المطر»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p189/r6] verses 7:57 | A balad is any part of earth, settled or unsettled, empty or inhabited; its subdivision is a baldah, plural bilād, and wilderness can also be called baldah. Al-Aʿshā’s line uses بلد for a desolate wilderness. «وأما البلد فكل موضع من الأرض عامر أو غير عامر خال أو مسكون فهو بلد»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p191/r1] verses 7:57 | Al-Zajjāj and Ibn al-Anbārī say “We sent down water by it” can mean by the land or by the cloud, since cloud is the instrument for sending down water. «قال الزجاج وابن الأنباري (¬2): (جائز أن يكون فأنزلنا بالبلد الماء»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p191/r2] verses 7:57 | The apparent referent of the pronoun in “by it We brought forth” is water, because fruit is brought out by water. «الظاهر أن الكناية تعود إلى الماء؛ لأن إخراج الثمرات كان بالماء»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p191/r3] verses 7:57 | Al-Zajjāj also permits taking the pronoun as the land, since the passage does not specify one particular land. «وقال الزجاج: (وجائز أن يكون فأخرجنا بالبلد من كل الثمرات»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p191/r4] verses 7:57 | The resurrection of the dead is likened to bringing forth plants from the dead land. «وقوله تعالى: {كذلك نخرج الموتى} أي: مثل ذلك الإخراج الذي أشرنا إليه»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p191/r5] verses 7:57 | Abū Bakr makes the analogy explicit: reviving the dead after they have become remains in the soil is like reviving earth with vegetation. «وقال أبو بكر: (أي: نحيي الموتى مثل ذلك الإحياء الذي وصفناه في البلد الميت»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p192/r1] verses 7:57 | Ibn ʿAbbās glosses “so that you may remember” as “so that you may take admonition.” «قال ابن عباس: (يريد: كي تتعظوا)»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v9p192/r2] verses 7:57 | Al-Zajjāj says the evidence should lead people to infer God’s oneness and resurrection; laʿalla expresses hope from the human addressees’ perspective, since God knows whether they will heed it and people speak according to what they know and hope from others. «وقال الزجاج: (أي: لعلكم بما بينا (¬2) لكم تستدلون على توحيد الله»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:56-57/r4] verses 7:57 | The phrase describing winds as nushuran means pleasant and gentle, from nashr in the sense of a pleasant scent. «طيبة لينة، من النشر وهو الرائحة الطيبة.»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:56-57/r5] verses 7:57 | Another interpretation takes the winds as scattered in every direction, meaning dispersed. «وقيل: متفرقة في كل جانب، بمعنى المنتشرة»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:56-57/r6] verses 7:57 | Before God's mercy means before the rain; the winds lift clouds heavy with water and drive them to a land without vegetation. «قدام مطره { حتى إذا أقلت } أي: حملت هذه الرياح { سحابا ثقالا } بما فيها من الماء»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:56-57/r7] verses 7:57 | God sends down water in the dead land and brings out every kind of fruit through that water. «فأنزلنا به } بذلك البلد { الماء فأخرجنا»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:7:56-57/r8] verses 7:57 | Bringing the dead to life resembles reviving the dead land; the sign should prompt reflection on God's oneness and power to resurrect. «نحيي الموتى مثل ذلك الإحياء الذي وصفناه في البلد الميت»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r1] verses 7:57 | The word has readings with nūn bearing ḍamma or fatḥa; ʿĀṣim reads bushrā with yāʾ. «و (نشرا) أيضا بضم النون وفتحها»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r2] verses 7:57 | On the nushuran reading, the phrase means that God spreads out the winds. «وهو الذي ينشر الرياح منشرة نشرا»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r3] verses 7:57 | Nushuran can also be a plural of nushūr or nashr. «ومن قال نشرا فهو جمع نشور ونشر»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r4] verses 7:57 | Bushran is a plural of bashīra or bashr, as in this verse's description of the winds. «ومن قرأ بشرا فهو جمع بشيرة وبشر»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r5] verses 7:57 | Between God's mercy means before the rain, which is itself mercy. «أي بين يدي المطر الذي هو رحمة»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r6] verses 7:57 | The wind is the implied subject of carrying the clouds; aqalla means to carry something. «حتى إذا أقلت الريح سحابا، يقال: أقل فلان الشيء إذا هو حمله»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r7] verses 7:57 | Clouds is the plural of cloud, and thiqālan means heavy with water. «والسحاب جمع سحابة. (ثقالا) أي ثقالا بالماء»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r8] verses 7:57 | The two forms may both describe barren land as dead. «سقناه لبلد ميت). وميت جميعا»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p345/r9] verses 7:57 | He allows water to be sent down by the cloud, but prefers that the fruit be brought forth by the water; it could also be by the land, since no one land is singled out. «الأحسن - والله أعلم - فأخرجنا بالماء من كل الثمرات»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p346/r1] verses 7:57 | The resurrection of the dead is like the preceding bringing forth of fruit. «أي مثل ذلك الإخراج الذي أشرنا إليه نخرج الموتى»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p346/r2] verses 7:57 | Laʿalla expresses expectation; people are addressed according to their knowledge and what one person hopes from another, while God knows whether they will remember. «لعل ترج، وإنما خوطب العباد على قدر علمهم، وما يرجوه بعضهم من بعض، والله يعلم أي تذكرون أم لا»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p346/r3] verses 7:57 | The signs are presented so that people infer God's oneness and His resurrection of the dead. «أي لعلكم بما بيناه لكم تستدلون على توحيد الله وأنه يبعث الموتى»

## 7:155
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p186#2/r1] verses 7:155 | اختار derives from خير and means choosing or selecting. «اختار افتعل من الخير وهو التخير والانتقاء»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p186#2/r2] verses 7:155 | اختار belongs to a small set of verbs that can take two objects, one directly and one through a preposition that may be omitted; he lists seven examples. «وهي مقصورة على السماع وهي اختار واستغفر وأمر وكنى ودعا وزوج وصدق»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p186#2/r3] verses 7:155 | The cited poet's line اخترتك الناس witnesses the omitted من: “I chose you from among the people.” «قال الشاعر: اخترتك الناس إذ رثت خلائقهم ... واعتل من كان يرجى»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p186#2/r4] verses 7:155 | On Abu Hayyan's parsing, seventy is the first object and the people are the second; alternatively, treating the people as first and seventy as a partitive apposition requires omitted elements and is strained. «وسبعين هو المفعول الأول، وقومه هو المفعول الثاني»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p186#2/r5] verses 7:155 | Commentators disagree whether this appointment was the Sinai meeting and Torah revelation or a different occasion. «واختلفوا في هذا الميقات أهو ميقات المناجاة ونزول التوراة أو غيره»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p186#2/r6] verses 7:155 | Nawf al-Bikali, also transmitted by Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas, identifies it with the first Sinai appointment; Moses chose six from each tribe, making seventy-two, and two stayed behind after a dispute. «فقال نوف البكالي ورواه أبو صالح عن ابن عباس: وهو الأول بين فيه بعض ما جرى»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p186#2/r7] verses 7:155 | In this account Kalib ibn Yufanna and Joshua ibn Nun stayed behind after Moses promised a reward to those who remained; he took the seventy to Sinai after ordering fasting and purification. «فقال: من قعد فله أجر من حضر فقعد كالب بن يوقنا ويوشع بن نون واستصحب السبعين»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p186#2/r8] verses 7:155 | At Sinai, a cloud enveloped the mountain, Moses entered and called the group forward; they prostrated, heard God's commands, then asked to see Him openly when the cloud lifted. «وقع عليه عمود الغمام حتى تغشى الجبل كله ودنا موسى ودخل فيه وقام للقوم: ادنوا»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p187/r1] verses 7:155 | Al-Zamakhshari connects this to Moses asking to see God in 7:143 so the witnesses would hear God's refusal themselves; the mountain shook and they fainted. «قال الزمخشري: فقال رب أرني أنظر إليك يريد أن يسمعوا الرد والإنكار من جهته»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p187/r2] verses 7:155 | Wahb says the Israelites asked Moses to bring witnesses to hear God speak; God ordered seventy of their best, with Harun accompanying Moses and Joshua left as deputy, but they demanded a visible sight and quaked. «فقال وهب بن منبه: قال بنو إسرائيل لموسى إن طائفة تزعم أن الله لا يكلمك»
