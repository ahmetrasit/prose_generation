# Notes

## 18:80
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p333/r6] verses 18:80 | One interpretation is that Khidr knew the unbelieving boy would make his parents tyrannical and unbelieving; Ubayy's reading explicitly calls the boy an unbeliever. «قال قتادة : وفي قراءة أبي { وأما الغلام فكان كافرا وكان أبواه مؤمنين } فعبر عن العلم بالخشية»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p333/r7] verses 18:80 | Another interpretation is that the Lord feared the boy would impose tyranny and unbelief on his parents; Ubayy's reading says 'your Lord feared,' with fear figurative because it cannot literally apply to God. «قال مقاتل : في قراءة أبي { فخاف ربك } والخوف ها هنا استعارة لانتفائه عن الله تعالى»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p333/r8] verses 18:80 | A third interpretation is that Khidr disliked the boy burdening his parents with sinful oppression through his tyranny and unbelief. «وكره الخضر أن يرهق الغلام أبويه بطغيانه وكفره إثما وظلما»
[MAWARDI-FULL:v3p333/r9] verses 18:80 | Yurhiqhumā may mean that the boy would enslave or capture his parents. «أحدهما : يكفلهما , قاله ابن زيد .»
[MUQATIL:18:80-81/r1] verses 18:80 | The believing parents' son was an unbelieving highway robber and tale-bearer who relied on their defense; they thought him innocent. «وكان الغلام كافرا، يقطع الطريق، ويحدث الحديث، ويلجأ إليهما»
[MUQATIL:18:80-81/r2] verses 18:80 | Muqatil glosses khashina as knew, comparing 4:128 and 4:35; the boy's tyranny means wrongdoing that would overwhelm his parents. «يعني فعلمنا، كقوله سبحانه»
[MUQATIL:18:80-81/r3] verses 18:80 | Ubayy b. Kaʿb's reading is fa-khāfa rabbuka, meaning your Lord knew. «وفي قراءة أبي بن كعب: فخاف ربك، يعني فعلم ربك»
[NAHHAS:v2p469/r1] verses 18:80 | Sībawayh permits مؤمنان outside Qur’anic recitation by treating ‘their parents were believers’ as a subject-predicate clause inside the predicate of كان. «ويجوز عند سيبويه في غير القرآن مؤمنان على أن نضمر في كان»
[NAHHAS:v2p469/r2] verses 18:80 | Sībawayh cites the report that every child is born upon the natural disposition until the parents make him Jewish or Christian as a grammatical parallel. «وحكى سيبويه ' كل مولود يولد على الفطرة حتى يكون أبواه هما اللذان يهودانه وينصرانه ' ^ ( فخشينا»
[NAHHAS:v2p469/r3] verses 18:80 | يرهقهما طغيانا وكفرا means the boy would overstep what is right; God knew what he would become if left alive and commanded what was most beneficial. «^ ( فخشينا أن يرهقهما طغيانا وكفرا ) ^ أي تجاوزا فيما لا يجب .»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p23#3/r1] verses 18:80 | The boy was named al-Husayn. «وكان اسمه الحسين»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p23#3/r2] verses 18:80 | Al-Khidr feared the boy would oppress his believing parents, repay their care with disobedience and mistreatment, bring them trouble, or lead them astray until they renounced faith. «فخفنا أن يغشى الوالدين المؤمنين طغيانا عليهما وكفرا لنعمتهما بعقوقه وسوء صنيعه»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p23#3/r3] verses 18:80 | This is al-Khidr’s speech; he feared the child’s future because God informed him of the boy’s condition and disclosed the matter’s secret. «وهو من كلام الخضر وإنما خشي الخضر منه ذلك لانه تعالى اعلمه بحاله واطلعه على سر أمره»
[NASAFI-FULL:v3p23#3/r4] verses 18:80 | If the words are God’s speech, fakhashīnā means We knew that, if the boy lived, he would cause his parents’ disbelief. «فمعنى فخشينا فعلمنا ان عاش ان يصير سببا لكفر والديه»
[QASHANI:18:79-81/r3] verses 18:80 | The boy's parents are spirit and bodily nature, faithful through obedience; they fear the boy's self-assertion and ingratitude would corrupt their servitude and veil them. «مقرين بالتوحيد لانقيادهما في سلك طاعة الله»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:77-78#8/r10] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | Ibn Abbas says Moses' remarks on the ship and boy were for God, while his wall comment sought something worldly and caused the parting. «وكان قول موسى في السفينة والغلام لله، وكان قوله في الجدار لنفسه»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:77-78#8/r15] verses 18:80 | The objection to killing the boy is answered by recalling Moses' blow that killed the Egyptian. «أين إنكارك هذا من وكزك القبطي وقضائك عليه»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:79#2/r11] verses 18:79, 18:80 | Al-Bukhari names the king Hadad ibn Baddad and the slain boy Jaysur, with manuscript variants Haysur and Haysun. «وذكر البخاري اسم الملك الآخذ لكل سفينة غصبا فقال: هو هدد بن بدد والغلام المقتول اسمه جيسور»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:79-82#2/r6] verses 18:80, 18:81 | In “we intended,” Khidr associates killing with himself and replacement with God; maturity means completeness of body and reason. «وقال في الغلام: «فأردنا» فكأنه أضاف القتل إلى نفسه، والتبديل إلى الله تعالى»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:80-81/r1] verses 18:80 | A sound hadith says the boy was created a disbeliever, supporting the apparent inference he was not an adult; it could instead describe him while grown. «أنه طبع يوم طبع كافرا»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:80-81/r2] verses 18:80 | Most commentators take “we feared” as Khidr's own speech, and the context supports this; God had permitted his judgment to kill in this circumstance. «وهو الذي يشهد له سياق الكلام، وهو قول كثير من المفسرين»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:80-81/r3] verses 18:80 | Al-Tabari and Ibn Abbas take “we feared” as God's speech conveyed by Khidr, meaning “we knew.” «قال الطبري: معناه فعلمنا وكذا قال ابن عباس أي فعلمنا»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:80-81/r4] verses 18:80 | Ubayy's reading replaces “we feared” with “your Lord knew.” «وحكي أن أبيا قرأ «فعلم ربك»»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:80-81/r5] verses 18:80 | Another view takes khashiya to mean aversion or dislike rather than fear. «وقيل: الخشية بمعنى الكراهة»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:80-81/r6] verses 18:80 | Ibn Atiyya calls the interpretation a metaphor from the perspective of human listeners who would fear the boy's effect on his parents; Ibn Masud's reading supports this. «قال ابن عطية: والأظهر عندي في توجيه هذا التأويل وإن كان اللفظ يدافعه أنها استعارة»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:80-81/r7] verses 18:80 | Expressions of hope and fear attributed to God reflect the hearers' perspective, not divine uncertainty. «إنما هو بحسبكم أيها المخاطبون»
[QURTUBI-FULL:18:80-81/r8] verses 18:80 | Yurhiq means to burden or compel them; parental love would make them follow the boy and stray into his religion. ««يرهقهما» يجشمهما ويكلفهما والمعنى أن يلقيهما حبه في اتباعه»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p135/r1] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | The Prophet is quoted as saying that judges decide by outward evidence while God knows inner realities. «نحن نحكم بالظاهر والله يتولى السرائر»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p135/r2] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | The three incidents apply one rule: bear the lesser harm to prevent the greater harm. «عند تعارض الضررين يجب تحمل الأدنى لدفع الأعلى»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p135/r3] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | Moses judged by outwardly known law, while the unnamed sage knew inward realities; in this knowledge the sage surpassed Moses. «مرتبة موسى عليه السلام في معرفة الشرائع والأحكام بناء الأمر على الظواهر»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p136/r1] verses 18:80 | The boy's continued life would harm his parents' faith and worldly life; revelation showed the harm from killing him was lesser. «بقاء ذلك الغلام حيا كان مفسدة للوالدين في دينهم وفي دنياهم»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p136/r3] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | The sage knew inward realities and based true rulings on them, whereas Moses' rulings followed outward appearances. «كان مخصوصا بالوقوف على بواطن الأشياء وبالأطلاع على حقائقها كما هي عليها في أنفسها»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p136/r4] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | Although Moses could not learn the sage's inward knowledge as ordinary instruction, the encounter taught him that spiritual knowledge completes legal knowledge and comes through purification of the heart. «وأما العلم ببواطن الأشياء فإنما يمكن تحصيله بناء على تصفية الباطن وتجريد النفس»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p137#2/r13] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | The changes from I wanted, to we wanted, to your Lord willed assign the boat's damage to the sage, the grave killing to a majestic plural, and care for the orphans to God. «ولما ذكر رعاية مصالح اليتيمين لأجل صلاح أبيهما أضافه إلى الله تعالى»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p137/r1] verses 18:80 | One report says the boy was an adult highway robber whose parents defended and excused his wrongdoing, risking their own sin and perhaps disbelief. «قيل : إن ذلك الغلام كان بالغا وكان يقطع الطريق»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p137/r2] verses 18:80 | Another report says the boy was still a child, but God knew he would commit those harms as an adult. «وقيل : إنه كان صبيا إلا أن الله تعالى علم منه أنه لو صار بالغا»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p137/r3] verses 18:80 | Here khashiya means fear or a strong presumption; the sage's action was licensed by God's knowledge and revelation. «الخشية بمعنى الخوف وغلبة الظن»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p137/r4] verses 18:80 | One reading of yurhiqahumā is that the boy would drive his parents toward transgression by making them defend or follow his evil acts. «أن يكون المراد أن ذلك الغلام يحمل أبويه على الطغيان والكفر»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p137/r5] verses 18:80 | A second reading is that the boy himself treated his parents with the conduct of tyrants and disbelievers. «أن يكون المعنى أن ذلك الولد كان يعاشرهما معاشرة الطغاة الكفار»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p137/r6] verses 18:80 | Killing on the basis of such a suspicion is permissible only when revelation confirms it. «إذا تأكد ذلك الظن بوحي الله جاز»
