[ALUSI-FULL:v16p10#2/r1] verses 18:80 | The boy's parents means his father and mother, with masculine plural formed by grammatical predominance; their names are reported as Azīr and Sahwāʾ. «أي أبوه وأمه ففيه تغليب واسم الأب على ما في الإتقان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p10#2/r2] verses 18:80 | Ubayy's codex and Ibn ʿAbbās's reading explicitly call the boy an unbeliever; some infer he was adult, since a child is not literally described as believing or disbelieving. «وفي مصحف أبي وقراءة ابن عباس ( وأما الغلام فكان كافرا وكان أبواه )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p10#2/r3] verses 18:80 | Al-Nawawī answers that this reading is anomalous and not proof, or that the boy was named for what he would become had he lived. «أن القراءة شاذة لا حجة فيها الثاني أنه سماه بما يؤل إليه لو عاش»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p10#2/r4] verses 18:80 | A report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim says the boy was stamped an unbeliever at birth; the author remains cautious about related reports and says they do not undermine the sound view that children are in Paradise. «وفي صحيح مسلم أن الغلام طبع يوم طبع كافرا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p10#2/r5] verses 18:80 | They read the parents as nominative dual muʾminān; grammarians explain this through an implicit pronoun of circumstance, a dialect form, or the boy as subject of kāna. «وقرأ أبو سعيد الخدري والجحدري ( فكان أبواه مؤمنان )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p11#2/r1] verses 18:80 | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says fear, hope, and similar expressions in God's speech address human listeners, since God is exalted above such states. «وقال ابن عطية : إن الخوف والخشية كالترجي بلعل ونحوها الواقع في كلامه تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p11/r1] verses 18:80 | Khashiya means intense fear that the boy, if he lived, would engulf his believing parents in transgression against divine limits and unbelief. «فخفنا خوفا شديدا أن يغشى الوالدين المؤمنين لو بقي حيا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p11/r2] verses 18:80 | Ibn Jubayr says parental love might lead them to follow the boy into unbelief; tyranny before unbelief intensifies the warning or depicts faith first stained and then removed. «بأن يحملهما حبه على متابعته كما روى عن ابن جبير»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p11/r3] verses 18:80 | Some commentators on al-Bukhārī take khashiya as knowledge: the boy would grow up inviting his parents to unbelief, and their love would make them comply. «وفسر بعض شراح البخاري الخشية بالعلم فقال : أي علمنا أنه لو أدرك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p11/r4] verses 18:80 | Another interpretation is that the boy would overwhelm his parents through disobedience and ingratitude for their care, bringing them harm and distress. «أن يغشيهما طغيانا عليهما وكفرا لنعمتهما عليه من تربيتهما إياه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p11/r5] verses 18:80 | A further view takes the phrase to mean that the boy's tyranny and unbelief would coexist with his parents' faith under one roof. «ويقرن بإيمانهما طغيانه وكفره فيجتمع في بيت واحد مؤمنان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p11/r6] verses 18:80 | Reports say the boy committed wrongdoing or highway robbery and his parents protected him; adults are inferred, but the author favors those who say such conduct is implausible for a child. «وفي بعض الآثار أن الغلام كان يفسد وفي رواية يقطع الطريق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p11/r7] verses 18:80 | The author prefers that Khidr himself answered Moses; al-Zamakhsharī allows that the words narrate God's statement, with khashiya figuratively meaning dislike, but this is less apparent. «والظاهر أن هذا من كلام الخضر عليه السلام»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p21#2/r1] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | If a corrupt judge would seize a ward's property unless paid, the guardian must offer the smallest amount that prevents its loss; the guardian's account is accepted later. «فإنه يجب عليه أن يدفع إليه شيئا ويتحرى في أقل ما يمكن إرضاؤه به»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p21#2/r2] verses 18:80 | The narrative supports traveling for knowledge, honoring teachers, avoiding premature objections, interpreting puzzling conduct charitably, keeping promises, apologizing, and a teacher explaining why a student cannot yet be taught. «من استحباب الرحلة للعلم وفضل طلبه واستحباب استعمال الأدب مع العالم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p21#2/r3] verses 18:80 | It permits taking a servant and provisions on a journey without compromising trust in God; forgetfulness may be attributed figuratively to Satan out of reverence and is not blameworthy. «وعلى جواز اتخاذ الخادم في السفر وحمل الزاد فيه وأنه لا ينافي التوكل»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p21#2/r4] verses 18:80 | The story supports stating intentions with God's will, setting conditions between leader and follower, and treating three as a meaningful count in repetition. «وتقديم المشيئة في الأمر واشتراط المتبوع على التابع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p21/r1] verses 18:79, 18:80, 18:82 | The story cannot establish that inner knowledge conflicts with law. The boy's killing was a special rule for Khidr; if he was a saint rather than prophet, another prophet may have conveyed the command. «إن قصة الخضر عليه السلام لا تصلح حجة لمن يزعم المخالفة بين العلمين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p21/r2] verses 18:80 | Al-Subkī says Khidr received revelation to act according to inward reality in this exceptional case; Islamic law never permits killing a child, especially one born to believing parents, for a future offense. «أوحي إليه أن يعمل بالباطن وخلاف الظاهر الموافق للحكمة»
[ASAD-NOTES:18:80#n78/r1] verses 18:80 | Taj al-'Arus notes that khashiya can mean having reason to fear and thus knowing that something bad will happen. «khashiya sometimes denotes "he had reason to fear" and, consequently, "he knew", i.e., that something bad would happen (Taj al-'Arus»
[ASAD-NOTES:18:80#n78/r2] verses 18:80 | Asad infers that the sage's "fear" meant positive knowledge, probably from mystic insight, as he later says he did not act on his own authority. «the sage's expression of "fear" was synonymous with positive "knowledge" gained through outward evidence or through mystic insight»

[BAGHAWI-FULL:v5p194#2/r7] verses 18:80 | Baghawī explains فخشينا as فَعَلِمنا and notes Ibn ʿAbbās's reading that the boy was an unbeliever while his parents were believers. «وفي قراءة ابن عباس: "وأما الغلام فكان كافرا وكان أبواه مؤمنين فخشينا"»
