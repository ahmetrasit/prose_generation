# Verses
- 1:1: بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ
- 1:2: ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ
- 1:7: صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ

# Notes

## 1:1
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p60/r4] verses 1:1 | Al-Sukuti calls interpreting mercy as metaphor a Muʿtazilite tendency and says early Sunni authorities affirmed the revealed attribute without recasting it. «بأن جعل الرحمة مجازا نزغة اعتزالية قد حفظ الله تعالى منها سلف المسلمين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p60/r5] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi says one cannot treat mercy as metaphor while affirming life, knowledge, will, power, speech, hearing, and sight literally on no principled distinction. «فأما أن يقال بارتكاب المجاز فيها كلها إذا نسبت إليه عز شأنه أو بتركه كذلك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r1] verses 1:1 | Al-Raghib says faʿil marks frequent action while faʿlan marks action that is frequent and recurrent; some use this to call Rahman stronger. «قال الراغب أن فعيلا لمن كثر منه الفعل وفعلان لمن كثر منه وتكرر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r2] verses 1:1 | Ibn al-Mubarak explains Rahman as the giver when asked and Rahim as the one who becomes angry when not asked. «وقول إبن المبارك الرحمن إذا سئل أعطى والرحيم إذا لم يسئل غضب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r3] verses 1:1 | Al-Juwayni treats Rahman and Rahim as equal in force, citing a report that God is the Merciful of this world and the next and their two mercies. «وقيل هما سواء لظاهر الحديث الذي أخرجه الحاكم في المستدرك مرفوعا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r4] verses 1:1 | Another distinction makes Rahman the attribute of mercy itself and Rahim the relation of that mercy to those who receive it. «وفرق بعضهم بينهما بأن الرحمن دال على الصفة القائمة به تعالى والرحيم دال على تعلقها بالمرحوم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r5] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi says greater word length does not establish greater meaning in every case; the rule is general, and examples such as hadhir and hadhir show exceptions. «وما ذكر من قولهم لأن زيادة البناء تدل على زيادة المعنى قاعدة أغلبية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r6] verses 1:1 | The puzzle about divine intensification concerns relations and recipients rather than an increase in God’s attribute; theologians answer differently depending on whether mercy is an essential or active attribute. «وأجيب بأن المراد الأكثرية في التعلقات والمتعلقات لا في الصفة نفسها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r7] verses 1:1 | The view assigning worldly mercy to believers and unbelievers but afterlife mercy only to believers falters because afterlife blessings are vastly more numerous and unbelievers may receive some relief. «فهذا الوجه مخدوش على الحالين على أن في اختصاص رحمة الآخرة بالمؤمنين مقالا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r8] verses 1:1 | Reports cited include the Prophet’s intercession for all people at the praised station and reduced punishment for some damned people in the afterlife. «إذ قد ورد في الصحيح شفاعته صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم لعامة الناس من هول الموقف»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p61/r9] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi argues that treating unbelievers’ worldly benefits as merely apparent would also defeat the claim that worldly mercy embraces both believers and unbelievers. «فيبطل حينئذ دعوى شمول الرحمة المؤمن والكافر في الدنيا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p62/r1] verses 1:1 | Even a partial reduction of punishment counts as mercy in some respect, since people in Hell ask its keepers for relief and any lesser evil is easier than a greater one. «وقال الذين في انار لخزنة جهنم أدعوا ربكم يخفف عنا يوما من العذاب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p62/r2] verses 1:1 | The claim that Rahman covers both worlds while Rahim covers lesser worldly blessings lacks support and conflicts with the transmitted wording naming both mercies in both worlds. «إلا أنه يرد عليه كسابقه أن الأثر لا يعرف والمعروف المرفوع رحمن الدنيا والآخرة ورحيمهما»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p62/r3] verses 1:1 | Ibn Hisham says the ordering question is misplaced: Rahman is a substantivized appositive, not an adjective, and Rahim modifies it rather than Allah. «وذكر ابن هشام أنه غير متجه لأن هذا خارج عن كلام العرب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p62/r4] verses 1:1 | Ibn Khuruf calls Rahman a dominant adjective, but its rare use as an indefinite or annexed form makes it more noun-like. «وقال ابن خروف هو صفة غالبة ولم يقع تابعا إلا لله تعالى في البسملة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p62/r5] verses 1:1 | The scholar of al-Kashf says the sequence follows a descending order: Allah names the essence, Rahman universal mercy and existence, and Rahim each being’s particular share and perfections. «أن الله اسم للذات الإلهية باعتبار أن الكل منه وإليه وجودا ورتبة وماهية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p62/r6] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi objects that assigning general existence to Rahman and individualized existence to Rahim is arbitrary, since every existent thing is already a particular share. «وهل يوجد في الخارج من النوع إلا الحصص الأفرادية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p63/r1] verses 1:1 | Al-Zamakhshari and Abd al-Qahir say rhyme or verse endings should follow meanings and coherent ordering; sound alone cannot justify moving words, especially when the pattern is inconsistent. «فمجرد المحافظة على الرؤوس لا يصير نكتة للتقديم إلا بعد أن يثبت أن المعاني»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p63/r2] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi offers a mystical reading of the order: Rahim points to Muhammad, whose completion of the basmala mirrors the completion of the created world. «وعندي من باب الإشارة أن تأخير الرحيم لأنه صفة محمد صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p63/r3] verses 1:1 | In this symbolism Adam bears the names while Muhammad bears their meanings; Adam’s names are words, whereas Muhammad received comprehensive speech. «قال تعالى ( وعلم آدم الأسماء كلها )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p63/r4] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi contrasts Companions, who experienced the divine essence, with later believers who know the names, and cites a report promising the latter fiftyfold reward. «فإنهم حصلوا الذات وحصلنا الأسماء ولما راعينا الاسم مراعاتهم الذات ضوعف لنا الأجر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p63/r5] verses 1:1 | He further says the Prophet longs for later believers who are his brothers and would rejoice to meet one of them. «فنحن الإخوان وهم الأصحاب وهو صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم إلينا بالأشواق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64#2/r1] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi says Arabs’ question about Rahman reflected fear that they were being directed to another deity like themselves, not evidence that the word was non-Arabic. «ولعل سبب ذلك توهمهم التعدد وأنهم خافوا أن يكون المعبود الذي يدلهم عليهم من جنسهم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64#2/r2] verses 1:1 | Ibn al-Hajib gives competing conditions for declension of faʿlan forms used as names or adjectives, which leaves Rahman disputed while Sukran and Nadman are clearer. «النون والألف إذا كانا في اسم فشرطه العلمية وفي صفة فانتفاء فعلانة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Al-Zamakhshari, al-Radi, Ibn Malik, and al-Bayḍawi prefer leaving Rahman indeclinable by analogy with the common faʿlan pattern whose feminine is faʿla. «وأختار الزمشخري والشيخ الرضي وإبن مالك وأستظهره البيضاوي عدم الصرف»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64#2/r4] verses 1:1 | Because the rule’s original noun pattern and the more frequent adjective pattern conflict, al-Saʿd allows both declension and indeclinability rather than neglecting either principle. «ولما رأى السعد أن هذه المسألة مما تعارض فيها الأصل والغالب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64#2/r5] verses 1:1 | Since Rahman is heard only definite, annexed, or vocative, rare poetic evidence cannot settle its declension; Rahim does not share this ambiguity. «وحيث لم يسمع هذا الاسم إلا مضافا أو معرفا بأل أو منادى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64#2/r6] verses 1:1 | The basmala begins God’s speech because it summarizes what follows and is a magnificent verse whose benefits are immense; investigating it requires a wide range of disciplines. «وإنما جعل الله البسملة مبدأ كلامه لوجهين أما الأول فلأنها إجمال ما بعدها وهي آية عظيمة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64#2/r7] verses 1:1 | Al-Alusi illustrates the range of required learning, from grammar, morphology, rhetoric and language to theology, law, hadith, philosophy, letters, natural sciences, and ethics. «أحتاج إل ى علم النحو وإذا أراد أن يبحث عنأصول كلماتها كيف كانت وكيف آلت»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64/r1] verses 1:1 | The pairing of Allah and Rahman is especially close, supporting the appositive reading and symbolizing the divine essence; al-Alusi links it to the report that Adam was created in God’s image. «قل أدعوا الله أو أدعوا الرحمن أيا ما تدعوا فله الأسماء الحسنى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64/r2] verses 1:1 | A mystical letter reading assigns hidden and manifest alifs in Allah and Rahman to essence and knowledge, with other letters indicating divine attributes; life is their necessary condition. «في الله وفي الرحمن ألفين ألف الذات وألف العلم والأولى في كل خفية والثانية ظاهرة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64/r3] verses 1:1 | Some mystics see even Satan as sharing universal mercy because an alif appears fractionally in its name; Rahim differs by having only the alif of knowledge. «أن الألف الخفية هنا ظهرت من حيث الجزئية من هذا اللفظ في الشيطان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64/r4] verses 1:1 | Rahim symbolizes Muhammad in his rank as messenger to all people, with its alif representing knowledge and divine support for his universal mission. «ولما كان هذا الأسم مشيرا إلى سيدنا محمد صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم بإعتبار رتبته»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p64/r5] verses 1:1 | The question whether Rahman is Arabic is longstanding; al-Alusi begins to explain the Arabs’ response by their fear of multiple gods rather than foreign origin. «فقد طال النزاع في تحقيق لفظ الرحمن كما طال في تحقيق لفظ الله»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p65/r1] verses 1:1 | The basmala’s hidden indications are inexhaustible; it is the Qur’an’s title and a gateway to secrets beyond the formal scholars’ semantic discussions. «معرفة ما خفى على أرباب الرسوم من الإشارات فليضرع إلى ربه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p65/r2] verses 1:1 | The opening and ending letters, alif forms, and nine groups of triads invite mystical reflection: articulation points, omitted letters, written/spoken forms, vowel states, word classes, genitives, divine names, agency, and connection patterns. «إن أردت أن تمتحن ذهنك في بعض أسرارها فتأمل سر إفتتاحها وإختتامها بحرفين»
