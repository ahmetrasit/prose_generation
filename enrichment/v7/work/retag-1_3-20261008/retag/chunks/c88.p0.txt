# Notes

## 18:82
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p19/r9] verses 18:82 | Al-Ghazālī says one who claims a spiritual state exempts him from prayer or permits wine should be killed; the author says there is no doubt about eternal punishment because this is apostasy. «وفي التحفة لابن حجر قال الغزالي : من زعم أن له مع الله تعالى حالا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p20/r1] verses 18:82 | A story has Ḥabīb al-ʿAjamī find al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī waiting for a boat; Ḥabīb asks whether al-Ḥasan lacks certainty, and al-Ḥasan asks whether he lacks knowledge. «إن الحسن البصري وقف على شط نهر ينتظر سفينة فجاء حبيب العجمي»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p20/r2] verses 18:82 | Al-Ḥasan is superior in the story because he combines knowledge and direct vision, seeing that divine power operates through wise natural causes; Ḥabīb's ecstatic state overlooks those causes. «أن الفضل للحسن فإنه كان جامعا بين علم اليقين وعين اليقين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p20/r3] verses 18:82 | Sirhindī compares the end of Uways, the master of the Successors, with the beginning of Waḥshī, Ḥamza's killer, after conversion, to explain the rarity of miracles among Companions. «مع قول الإمام الرباني : إن نهاية أويس سيد التابعين بداية وحشي قاتل حمزة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p20/r4] verses 18:82 | The highest perfected person is the Prophet, who continually rises through descent and descends through ascent; he is the fullest union of spiritual reality and revealed law. «وليس ذلك إلا رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم ولولا ذلك ما أمد العالم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p20/r5] verses 18:82 | Al-Ghazālī divides afterlife knowledge into unveiling and practice; unveiling is a light in a purified heart clarifying knowledge of God, His attributes, acts, and wisdom. «أما علم المكاشفة فهو علم الباطن وهو غاية العلوم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p20/r6] verses 18:82 | The author rejects al-Bulqīnī's claim that unveiling conflicts with outward knowledge, since prophets rank above the truthful and the two forms coexist; he cites the hierarchy in 4:69. «ولعمري لقد أخطأ فيما قال وبالحق تعرف الرجال»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p21#2/r5] verses 18:79, 18:82 | It supports riding boats, judging by outward appearances until contrary evidence appears, doing good even to scoundrels, taking payment for work, and burying property for safekeeping. «وعلى جواز ركوب السفينة وفيه الحكم بالظاهر حتى يتبين خلافه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p21/r3] verses 18:82 | Raising the wall without wages is unobjectionable kindness, even if the beneficiaries were wrongdoers, and reflects noble character. «وأما إقامة الجدار بلا أجر فلا إشكال فيها لأنها إحسان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r1] verses 18:82 | Some spiritual knowledges belong only to prophets; a Miʿraj report says God gave Muhammad knowledge of earlier and later peoples, secret knowledge only he could bear, chosen knowledge, the Qur'an, and commands to convey publicly. «وذكر بعض العارفين أن من العلوم ما لا يعلمه إلا النبي»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r10] verses 18:82 | Moses proposed a wage to spare them from begging the vile villagers; this reflects attention to means, a trait of the perfected, and signals that Moses was more complete than Khidr. «أراد دفع ما أحوجهما من السؤال من أولئك اللئام وفيه نظر إلى الأسباب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r11] verses 18:82 | Al-Nasrabadhi says Khidr ended the companionship after Moses reached the limit of discipline but not Khidr's knowledge, lest further questions expose Moses. «وقال النصر أبادي : لما علم الخضر بلوغ موسى إلى منتهى التأديب وقصور علمه عن علمه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r2] verses 18:82 | God also possesses knowledge He has reserved for Himself and revealed to none of creation. «ولله تعالى علم استأثر به عز وجل لم يطلع عليه أحدا من خلقه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r3] verses 18:82 | Moses' request to learn guidance from Khidr occurred as a divine trial, as discussed earlier. «قاله عن ابتلاء إلهي كما قدمنا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r4] verses 18:82 | Faris says Moses knew more of what he received from God, while Khidr knew more of what came to Moses. «وقال فارس كما في أسرار القرآن : إن موسى عليه السلام كان أعلم من الخضر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r5] verses 18:82 | Faris also contrasts Moses as remaining through God with Khidr as annihilated through God. «وقال أيضا : إن موسى كان باقيا بالحق والخضر كان فانيا بالحق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r6] verses 18:82 | One view says Khidr feared accompanying Moses because Moses was the most honored person of his time and intensely quick-tempered. «قيل : علم الخضر أن موسى عليه السلام أكرم الخلق على الله تعالى في زمانه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r7] verses 18:82 | Some say Khidr made Moses despair of himself so that companionship with Khidr would not distract him from companionship with God. «وقال بعضهم : آيسه من نفسه لئلا يشغله صحبته عن صحبة الحق»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r8] verses 18:82 | Some say Moses would have been granted patience had he spoken like the sacrificial son; that wording more humbly entrusts success to God and calls himself one among the patient. «قال بعضهم : لو قال كما قال الذبيح عليه السلام : ( ستجدني إن شاء الله من الصابرين )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p22#2/r9] verses 18:82 | Asking the village for food involves humbling oneself but does not contradict reliance on God or earning a livelihood. «سلكا طريق السؤال الذي يتعلق بذل النفس في الطريقة وهو لا ينافي التوكل وكذا الكسب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r1] verses 18:82 | Khidr feared Moses would ask about divine secrets of attributes and essence, which Khidr could not answer, so he said what he said. «وقيل : خاف أن يسأله عن أسرار العلوم الربانية الصفاتية الذاتية فيعجز عن جوابه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r2] verses 18:82 | The boy was exceptionally handsome and deeply loved by his parents, so Khidr feared he would become a trial for them. «قيل : كان حسن الوجه جدا وكان محبوبا في الغاية لوالديه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r3] verses 18:82 | The apparent predestination problem is answered by saying God decreed that the parents remain faithful if the boy were killed, and the killing preserved that outcome. «بأن المقدر بقاؤهما على الإيمان إن قتل وقتله ليبقيا على ذلك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r4] verses 18:82 | Another answer is that a decree may change in its relation, without change in God's attribute itself; the discussion is linked to 13:39. «موقيل إن المقدر قد يغير ولا يلزم من ذلك سوى التغير في تعلق صفته تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r5] verses 18:82 | To the objection that guidance could remove the danger without killing, the answer is that the boy seemed unready for it, making that alternative contrary to wisdom. «وأجيب بأن الظاهر أنه غير مستعد لذلك فهو مناف للحكمة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r6] verses 18:82 | Khidr saw that Moses was about to contest the matter, so he closed off debate by saying he acted only on God's command, who is not questioned for what He commands and does. «فتخلص من ذلك بقوله ( وما فعلته عن أمري ) أي بل فعلته بأمر الله عز وجل ولا يسئل سبحانه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r7] verses 18:82 | Khidr's bird-pecking metaphor was meant to shut down objections; knowledge of such matters may be among what God alone reserves. «حين نقر العصفور في البحر سد لباب المناقشة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r8] verses 18:82 | An esoteric reading maps the story onto spiritual psychology: seas and rock to master, disciple, and self; fish to a heart salted by worldly love; ship to law; boy to the commanding soul; village and wall to body, faculties, and the barrier to intelligible realities; treasure to intellectual and practical perfection guided by a shaykh. «وأول بعضهم مجمع البحرين بمجمع ولاية الشيخ وولاية المريد والصخرة بالنفس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p23/r9] verses 18:82 | The cited allegory is the interpretation chosen by al-Nisaburi; another interpreter offered an even stranger one. «وهذا ما اختاره النيسابوري واختار غيره تأويلا آخر هو أدهى منه»
[ASAD-NOTES:18:82#n79/r1] verses 18:82 | The buried treasure was presumably an inheritance; had the wall fallen, avaricious villagers might have taken it, as their refusal of hospitality showed their character. «left to them as an inheritance. Presumably that treasure would have been exposed to view if the wall had been allowed to tumble down»
[ASAD-NOTES:18:82#n80/r1] verses 18:82 | Khidr acted through mystic insight into reality behind appearances, as a conscious part of God's plan; this explains the plural in 18:80–81 and attribution of the act to God's will. «the mystic insight which revealed to him the reality behind the outward appearance of things and made him a conscious particle in God's unfathomable plan»
[ASAD-NOTES:18:intro/r3] verses 18:60, 18:82 | The Moses and unnamed sage story shifts spiritual awakening to intellectual search for ultimate truth, showing that appearance and reality differ and require mystic insight to discern. «Appearance and reality are shown to be intrinsically different»
