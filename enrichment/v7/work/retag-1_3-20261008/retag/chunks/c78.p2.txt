[ELMALILI:18:71-74#3/r6] verses 18:65 | Ledunni knowledge cannot be gained through intellectual effort; it is a pure divine gift and direct disclosure, with reality impressed upon the soul rather than inferred from effects. «taraf-ı Hak’tan mevhibe-i mahza olan bir kuvve-i kudsiyenin tecellîsidir.»
[ELMALILI:18:71-74#3/r7] verses 18:65 | In Turkish, ‘the ledunniyat of a matter’ means its hidden intricacies and secrets; the story encourages seeking knowledge and travel while showing this special knowledge cannot be acquired by effort. «bâtınındaki gavâmız ve esrârı mânasında müte‘âreftir.»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p369/r1] verses 18:65 | من marks source: mercy and knowledge come from the honored nearness of God; Khidr's authority or prophethood and rare knowledge are gifts reserved for the chosen. «و ( من ) ابتدائية ، أي آتيناه رحمة صدرت من مكان القرب ، أي الشرف»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v15p369/r2] verses 18:65 | Changing من عندنا to من لدنا is stylistic variation that avoids repeating the same expression. «والمخالفة بين من عندنا } وبين { من لدنا } للتفنن تفاديا من إعادة الكلمة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p529#2/r3] verses 18:65 | The servant is Khidr according to the majority and hadith; an uncredited view that he was someone else is rejected. «و «العبد» هو الخضر في قول الجمهور بمقتضى الأحاديث، وخالف من لا يعتد بقوله»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p529#2/r4] verses 18:65 | Most say Khidr was a prophet; some call him a righteous servant, but the verse supports prophethood because his inward actions required revelation. «والخضر نبي عند الجمهور، وقيل هو عبد صالح غير نبي، والآية تشهد بنبوته»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p529#2/r5] verses 18:65 | A report has Moses find Khidr lying under his garment; after greetings and questions, each describes knowledge God taught him that the other lacks. «وروي في الحديث أن موسى عليه السلام وجد الخضر مسجى في ثوبه مستلقيا على الأرض»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p529#2/r6] verses 18:65 | Khidr knew divinely revealed inner realities, not outward legal rulings; Moses knew law and judged by apparent words and actions. «كان علم الخضر معرفة بواطن قد أوحيت إليه لا تعطي ظواهر الأحكام أفعاله بحسبها.»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p529#2/r7] verses 18:65 | A Prophetic report says Khidr was named after sitting on dry vegetation that sprang green beneath him; “mercy” here means prophethood. «وسمي الخضر خضرا لأنه جلس على فروة يابسة فاهتزت تحته خضراء، روي ذلك عن النبي»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p529#2/r8] verses 18:65 | The Bukhari report says Moses claimed no one was more learned, then was directed to Khidr; al-Tabari gives another account where Moses asks who is most learned and is told to add others’ knowledge to his own. «أن سبب هذه القصة أن موسى عليه السلام، قيل له تعلم أحدا أعلم منك، قال: لا،»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p529#2/r9] verses 18:65 | Most read min ladunna with doubled nun; Abu ʿAmr lightens it and rounds dal, which Abu Hatim calls two dialects. «وقرأ الجمهور «من لدنا» بتشديد النون وقرأ أبو عمرو من «لدنا» بضم الدال وتخفيف النون،»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p161/r3] verses 18:65, 18:66, 18:67, 18:68, 18:69, 18:70 | Moses met Khidr, requested instruction, and was warned that their unequal knowledge would make patience difficult; Moses promised patience and obedience. «فاذا هو مسجى بثوب فسلم عليه موسى فقال الخضر وأنى بأرضك السلام من أنت قال أنا موسى»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p166#2/r9] verses 18:63, 18:65 | ʿAtiyya reports Ibn Abbas saying Moses returned to the rock, followed the fish across the sea to an island, and met Khidr there. «وروى عطية عن ابن عباس قال رجع موسى إلى الصخرة فوجد الحوت»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r1] verses 18:65 | The servant they found is Khidr. «قوله تعالى فوجدا عبدا من عبادنا يعني الخضر»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r10] verses 18:65 | Ibn al-Anbari says the mercy was tenderness toward those deserving it; Abu Sulayman al-Dimashqi says it was blessing. «والثاني الرقة والحنو على من يستحقه ذكره ابن الأنباري والثالث النعمة قاله أبو سليمان الدمشقي»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r11] verses 18:65 | Ibn Abbas glosses knowledge bestowed from God as knowledge of the unseen. «قال ابن عباس أعطاه علما من علم الغيب»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r2] verses 18:65 | Wahb and Muqatil identify Khidr as al-Yasaʿ; Ibn al-Munadi gives two other names, Khidr son of ʿAmya and Armaya son of Halfaya. «أحدها اليسع قاله وهب ومقاتل والثاني الخضر بن عاميا والثالث أرميا بن حلفيا»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r3] verses 18:65 | ʿAli ibn Ahmad al-Naysaburi names him Bilya son of Malkan. «والرابع بليا بن ملكان ذكره علي بن أحمد النيسابوري»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r4] verses 18:65 | A report from Abu Hurayra tracing to the Prophet says Khidr sat on a white fur and it turned green; fur here means dry ground. «أنه جلس في فروة بيضاء فاخضرت رواه أبو هريرة عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r5] verses 18:65 | ʿIkrima says the area around Khidr turned green whenever he sat; Mujahid says this occurred when he prayed. «والثاني أنه كان إذا جلس اخضر ما حوله قاله عكرمة»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r6] verses 18:65 | Scholars disagree whether Khidr was a prophet or a righteous servant; many held prophethood, while some said he was only a righteous servant. «وهل كان الخضر نبيا أم لا فيه قولان ذكرهما أبو بكر بن الأنباري»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r7] verses 18:65 | On whether Khidr remained alive, al-Mawardi transmits two views; al-Hasan held that he died, as did Ibn al-Munadi. «واختلف العلماء هل هو باق إلى يومنا هذا على قولين حكاهما الماوردي وكان الحسن يذهب إلى أنه مات»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r8] verses 18:65 | Al-Bukhari rejected Khidr’s continued life, arguing that the Prophet said no one alive then would remain after a hundred years. «كيف يكون ذلك وقد قال النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لا يبقى على رأس مائة سنة ممن هو اليوم»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p167#2/r9] verses 18:65 | Muqatil says the mercy granted to Khidr was prophethood. «أحدها أنها النبوة قاله مقاتل»
