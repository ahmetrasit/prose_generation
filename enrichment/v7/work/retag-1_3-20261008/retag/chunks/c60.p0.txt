# Notes

## 7:155
[TAB-FULL:v10p477/r7] verses 7:155 | Moses calls God their helper, asks Him to cover their sins by withholding punishment, and asks for His kindness through mercy. «وقوله: {أنت ولينا} [الأعراف: 155] يقول: أنت ناصرنا. {فاغفر لنا} [الأعراف: 155] يقول: فاستر علينا ذنوبنا بتركك عقابنا عليها.»
[TAB-FULL:v10p477/r8] verses 7:155 | Khayr al-ghāfirīn means God is best at pardoning wrongdoing and covering sins. «خير من صفح عن جرم وستر على ذنب»
[TABATABAI:7:155#2/r1] verses 7:155 | The passage focuses on the people's wrongdoing, punishment, and Moses' prayer; the wording does not decide whether this is part of the preceding account. «وعلى أي حال العناية في هذه القصة ببيان ظلمهم ونزول العذاب عليهم ودعاء موسى لهم»
[TABATABAI:7:155#2/r2] verses 7:155 | An objection says the account must be separate because interrupting one story with another and then returning would be stylistically improper. «وما قيل إن ظاهر الحال أن تكون هذه القصة مغايرة للمتقدمة إذ لا يليق بالفصاحة ذكر بعض القصة ثم النقل إلى أخرى»
[TABATABAI:7:155#2/r3] verses 7:155 | The style objection fails because the Qur'an can move between episodes for its guidance and wisdom; it is not a storybook required to finish one narrative before another. «وليس القرآن كتاب قصة حتى يعاب بالانتقال عن قصة قبل تمامها، وإنما هو كتاب هداية ودلالة وحكمة»
[TABATABAI:7:155#2/r4] verses 7:155 | He distinguishes three accounts: the people's request to see God and the thunderbolt, Moses' own meeting and collapse, and the seventy's meeting and seizure. «الحق أنها قصص ثلاث قصة سؤالهم الرؤية ونزول الصاعقة، وقصة ميقات موسى وصعقته، وقصة ميقات السبعين وأخذ الرجفة»
[TABATABAI:7:155#2/r5] verses 7:155 | One account says that after the calf episode Moses took seventy to Sinai to apologize; they demanded sight, suffered a near-fatal bodily quake, and recovered after his prayer. «ولذلك ذكر بعضهم أن هذا الميقات غير الميقات الأول»
[TABATABAI:7:155#2/r6] verses 7:155 | Another account says that after Aaron died, Israelites accused Moses of killing him; Aaron cleared Moses at his grave, then the seventy asked to become prophets and were struck down. «وذكر بعض آخر أن هارون لما مات اتهم بنو إسرائيل موسى في أمره»
[TABATABAI:7:155#2/r7] verses 7:155 | A third account identifies the seventy with those who asked Moses to see God; they were struck dead and then revived by his prayer, in a separate episode. «وذكر آخرون أن بني إسرائيل سألوا موسى الرؤية فاختار منهم السبعين فجاؤوا إلى الطور»
[TABATABAI:7:155#2/r8] verses 7:155 | The proposed accounts, especially the first two, lack Qur'anic evidence and a reliable supporting report; the author's earlier reading best fits the context. «لا دليل عليه من لفظ القرآن، ولا يؤيده أثر معتبر»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r1] verses 7:155 | An utterance can count as a deed subject to punishment: 52:16 includes disbelief, lies, and falsehood among what people do. «فإنه شامل لقول كلمة الكفر والكذب والافتراء ونحو ذلك بلا ريب»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r10] verses 7:155 | He does not explicitly ask for the seventy's revival out of humility, but implies it in the plea for forgiveness and mercy; 2:56 reports their revival, which the author attributes to Moses' intercession. «وحاجته هذه مندرجة في قوله { فاغفر لنا وارحمنا } لا محالة»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r2] verses 7:155 | The punishment followed the insolence, defiance, and contempt implied by their request, not merely its verbal form. «والظاهر أنهم عذبوا بما كان يستلزمه قولهم من سوء الأدب والعناد والاستهانة بمقام ربهم»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r3] verses 7:155 | Moses wanted God to revive the seventy so Israel would not accuse him and abandon his mission. «أن يسأل ربه أن يحييهم خوفا من أن يتهمه بنو إسرائيل فيخرجوا به عن الدين»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r4] verses 7:155 | Because the scene is dominated by grave sin and divine anger, Moses does not ask directly; he gradually prepares the supplication by praising God and invoking mercy. «أخذ يمهد الكلام رويدا ويسترحم ربه بجمل من الثناء حتى يهيج الرحمة على الغضب»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r5] verses 7:155 | Moses' hypothetical that God could have destroyed them earlier means the people's deaths would not have implicated him if God had chosen that course. «لم يتجه من قومي إلي تهمة في هلاكهم»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r6] verses 7:155 | By asking whether all are to be destroyed for the fools' act, Moses appeals to the principle that God does not punish an entire people for a few offenders. «ليس من شأن رحمته وسنة ربوبيته أن يؤاخذ قوما بفعل سفهائهم»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r7] verses 7:155 | The trial is a general test through which some go astray and others find guidance; God does not destroy everyone who fails it, since mercy precedes wrath. «هذا المورد أحد موارد امتحانك وابتلائك العام الذي تبتلي به عبادك وتجريه عليهم ليضل من ضل ويهتدي من اهتدى»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r8] verses 7:155 | Moses also recalls God's promise to support him in his mission and that the deaths would expose him to accusation by his people. «وأنت الذي أرسلتني إلى قومي ووعدتني أن تنصرني في نجاح دعوتي، وهلاك هؤلاء المصعوقين يجلب علي التهمة من قومي»
[TABATABAI:7:155#3/r9] verses 7:155 | Moses frames his request between God's special guardianship and His being the best forgiver, then asks forgiveness and mercy as the best thing a servant can seek. «وبدأه بقوله { أنت ولينا } وختمه بقوله { وأنت خير الغافرين }»
[TABATABAI:7:155-160#2/r1] verses 7:155 | The phrase about the trial belongs to Moses' speech about the destruction of the seventy; it is unrelated to the calf episode unless Moses repeated it there. «وذلك أن الآية أعني قوله { إن هي إلا فتنتك } من كلامه عليه السلام في قصة هلاك السبعين»
[TABATABAI:7:155-160/r1] verses 7:155 | The account also includes divine speech, the Torah's descent, and some Israelites' transformation into despised apes; fuller details of Moses' story are deferred to the commentary on Hud. «ويمكنك أن تضيف إليها التكليم ونزول التوراة، ومسخ بعضهم قردة خاسئين.»
[TABATABAI:7:155-160/r2] verses 7:155 | An account says Moses pleaded for his seventy companions after the quake; God offered better replacements, but Moses said he recognized them and knew their scent. «عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام قال قلت له إن عبد الله بن عجلان قال في مرضه الذي مات فيه»
[TABATABAI:7:155-160/r3] verses 7:155 | The report's analogy is that God replaced Abdullah ibn Ajlan's companions with better ones, as He had done for Moses. «المراد أن الله بدل له بعبد الله بن عجلان أصحابا هم خير منه كما فعل بموسى»
[TABATABAI:7:155-160/r4] verses 7:155 | Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the report unusual and inconsistent with the Qur'an's apparent meaning. «والخبر غريب في بابه ولا يوافق ظاهر الكتاب»
[TABATABAI:7:155-160/r5] verses 7:155 | A report argues that human voters may choose a corrupt leader unknowingly; Moses' selection of hypocrites shows that only God, who knows hidden hearts, can choose an Imam. «عن القائم عليه السلام قال قلت فأخبرني يا مولاي عن العلة التي تمنع القوم من اختيار إمام لأنفسهم»
[TABATABAI:7:155-160/r6] verses 7:155 | The report's Qur'anic citation is a paraphrased composite of the Moses narrative in al-Aʿrāf and al-Nisāʾ. «الآية فيها منقولة بالمعنى بمعنى أنها ملفقة من آيات القصة في سورتي الأعراف والنساء»
[TABATABAI:7:155-160/r7] verses 7:155 | Nawf reports that God promised Moses' chosen seventy that the earth would be a mosque and purifier, His tranquility would be in their homes, and all of them could recite the Torah from memory. «عن نوف الحميري قال لما اختار موسى قومه سبعين رجلا لميقات ربه قال الله لموسى»
[TABATABAI:7:155/r1] verses 7:155 | Moses selected seventy from his people; grammatically, قومَه is accusative because من has been omitted before it. «اختار من قومه فالقوم منصوب بنزع الخافض»
[TABATABAI:7:155/r2] verses 7:155 | God had appointed a meeting attended by seventy men Moses chose, indicating an important purpose that the verse itself leaves unspecified. «أن الله سبحانه عين لهم ميقاتا فحضره منهم سبعون رجلا اختارهم موسى من القوم»
[TABATABAI:7:155/r3] verses 7:155 | The quake killed the seventy: Moses says God could have destroyed them earlier, which supports the inference that the seizure was fatal. «فيظهر من هنا أن الرجفة أهلكتهم»
[TABATABAI:7:155/r4] verses 7:155 | He connects this event with the public request to see God and the thunderbolt in 2:55 and 4:153. «ويتأيد بذلك أن هذه القصة هي التي يشير سبحانه إليها بقوله وإذ قلتم يا موسى لن نؤمن لك حتى نرى الله جهرة»
[TABATABAI:7:155/r5] verses 7:155 | The quake is the thunderbolt's seizure, not a bodily tremor; the Qur'an uses similar language for the punishment of Thamūd. «المراد بالرجفة التي أخذتهم في الميقات رجفة الصاعقة لا رجفة في أبدانهم»
[TABATABAI:7:155/r6] verses 7:155 | The reference in 4:153 suggests their request to see God was tied to receiving the Torah, while the calf episode followed afterward. «وفي آية النساء المنقولة آنفا إشعار بأن سؤالهم الرؤية كان مربوطا بنزول الكتاب»
[TABATABAI:7:155/r7] verses 7:155 | The chosen men likely accepted Moses' mission but conditioned their belief in the Torah's divine origin on seeing God. «هؤلاء المختارين كانوا مؤمنين بأصل دعوة موسى، وإنما أرادوا»
[TABATABAI:7:155/r8] verses 7:155 | He treats the seventy's episode as part of the meeting for the Torah: they went to Sinai, demanded sight, died by the thunderbolt, and were revived through Moses' prayer; Moses then met God, and the calf episode came after the people's departure. «أن هذه القصة جزء من قصة الميقات ونزول التوراة»
