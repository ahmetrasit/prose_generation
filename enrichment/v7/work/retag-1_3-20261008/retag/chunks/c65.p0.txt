## 7:156
[RAZI-FULL:v15p18#2/r2] verses 7:156 | Razi says write for us means ordain or make binding for us; asking for good in both worlds parallels the believers' prayer in 2:201. «وقوله : { واكتب } أي وجب لنا والكتابة تذكر بمعنى الإيجاب»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p18#2/r3] verses 7:156 | God's guardianship gives a reason to ask for benefits, displaying His grace and divinity; the servants' repentance, humility, and submission give a second reason. «كونه تعالى وليا للعبد يناسب أن يطلب العبد منه دفع المضار وتحصيل المنافع»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p18#2/r4] verses 7:156 | Commentators explain hudna ilayka as we repented and returned to You; al-Layth says al-hawd means repentance. «قال المفسرون : { هدنا } أي تبنا ورجعنا إليك»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p18#2/r5] verses 7:156 | The prayer rests on two grounds: God's lordly might and guardianship, and the servants' lowly state as repentant, submissive worshippers. «عهد عزة الربوبية . والثاني : عهد ذلة العبودية»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p18#2/r6] verses 7:156 | God says He punishes whom He wills without anyone's objection, because all belongs to Him and no one may object to an owner's disposal of exclusive property. «إني أعذب من أشاء وليس لأحد علي اعتراض لأن الكل ملكي»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p18#2/r7] verses 7:156 | Al-Hasan reads man asaa, from wrongdoing; al-Shafi'i preferred this reading. «وقرأ الحسن { من * أساء } من الإساءة ، واختار الشافعي هذه القراءة»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p18#2/r8] verses 7:156 | One view says God's mercy encompasses everyone in this world but is reserved for believers in the afterlife, as the following conditions specify. «قيل المراد من قوله : { ورحمتي وسعت كل شيء }»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p18#2/r9] verses 7:156 | Another view says existence itself is good, so every existent has received at least the mercy of being brought into existence. «قيل : الوجود خير من العدم ، وعلى هذا التقدير فلا موجود»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p19/r1] verses 7:156 | Razi classifies God's commands into prohibitions and required actions; the prohibitions require avoidance and are signaled by the description of those who are mindful. «اعلم أن جميع تكاليف الله محصورة في نوعين»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p19/r2] verses 7:156 | Among positive duties, the duty involving a person's wealth is zakat, signaled by the phrase about giving zakat. «أما القسم الأول : فهو الزكاة وإليه الإشارة بقوله : { ويؤتون الزكواة }»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p19/r3] verses 7:156 | Duties involving the person include knowledge and action: knowledge is recognition, while action includes verbal confession and bodily practice such as prayer; belief in God's signs points to this whole group. «وأما القسم الثاني : فيدخل فيه ما يجب على الإنسان علما وعملا أما العلم فالمعرفة»
[RAZI-FULL:v15p19/r4] verses 7:156 | Razi compares this summary of obligations with the opening of al-Baqara, which joins belief, prayer, and spending. «ونظيره قوله تعالى في أول سورة البقرة : { هدى للمتقين * الذين يؤمنون بالغيب»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156#2/r1] verses 7:156 | عذابي أصيب is parsed as subject and predicate. «قوله: { عذابي أصيب } مبتدأ وخبره.»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156#2/r2] verses 7:156 | The common reading has من أشاء with شīn; Zayd ibn ʿAlī, Ṭāwūs, and ʿAmr ibn Fāʾid read أساء with sīn, from الإساءة. «والعامة على " من أشاء " بالشين المعجمة. وقرأ زيد بن علي وطاوس وعمرو بن فائد: " أساء "»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156#2/r3] verses 7:156 | Al-Dānī says this reading is not soundly transmitted from al-Ḥasan or Ṭāwūs and disparages ʿAmr ibn Fāʾid. «قال الداني: " لا تصح هذه القراءة عن الحسن ولا عن طاوس، وعمرو بن فائد رجل سوء،»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156#2/r4] verses 7:156 | When Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna once read and approved the sīn variant, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Muqriʾ rebuked him because Muʿtazilites used it to argue that human action is created by the human; Sufyān said he had not noticed the implication. «وقرأها يوما سفيان بن عيينة واستحسنها، فقام إليه عبد الرحمن المقرئ فصاح به وأسمعه فقال سفيان:»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156/r1] verses 7:156 | The standard ḍamma reading هُدنا may derive from هاد يهود, meaning we inclined; a poem addresses Salmā and calls the speaker هائد. «العامة على ضم الهاء، من هاد يهود بمعنى مال، قال:»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156/r2] verses 7:156 | The standard form may instead mean we repented, as in a poem whose speaker says he has repented of what he did; colloquial wordplay also associates the form with repentance. «إني امرؤ مما جنيت هائد»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156/r3] verses 7:156 | Zayd ibn ʿAlī and Abū Wajza read هِدنا with a kasra, from هاد يهيد, meaning to move or shake. «وقرأ زيد بن علي وأبو وجزة " هدنا " بكسر [الهاء] من هاد يهيد أي حرك.»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156/r4] verses 7:156 | Al-Zamakhsharī allows the two vowelings as either active or passive, meaning we inclined or were inclined, and moved ourselves or were moved; the author objects that ambiguous forms should receive a disambiguating vowel, although Sībawayh permits three readings in قيل and بيع. «وقد أجاز الزمخشري في هدنا وهدنا بالضم والكسر أن يكون الفعل مبنيا للفاعل أو للمفعول»
[SAMIN-DURR:7:156/r5] verses 7:156 | هي has an understood antecedent from context, either the fitna itself or, according to another view, the request to see God in 7:143. «و " هي " ضمير يفسره سياق الكلام إذ التقدير: إن فتنتهم إلا فتنتك.»
