# Notes

## 18:65
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p322/r3] verses 18:65 | Ibn Asakir reports that Khidr and Ilyas fast Ramadan in Jerusalem, perform pilgrimage yearly, and drink Zamzam sufficient for a year. «أن إلياس والخضر يصومان شهر رمضان في بيت المقدس ويحجان في كل سنة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p322/r4] verses 18:65 | A marfu report from Ibn Abbas says Khidr and Ilyas meet annually during pilgrimage, shave each other's heads, and part after reciting a formula of reliance on God. «يلتقي الخضر وإلياس كل عام في الموسم فيحلق كل واحد منهما رأس صاحبه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p322/r5] verses 18:65 | A report says a mysterious man delayed Umar's funeral prayer, praised the deceased, vanished after burial, and left a cubit-long footprint; identifying him as Khidr is an inference, not explicit in the report. «والاستدلال بهذا مبني على أنه عنى بالمحدث عنه الخضر عليه السلام»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p322/r6] verses 18:65 | Many reports establish at most that Khidr lived in Muhammad's era, which rebuts those denying life then but does not prove he is alive now; the author says no group accepts the former while denying the latter. «وإن لم يدل على أنه حي اليوم بل يدل على أنه كان حيا في زمنه صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p322/r7] verses 18:65 | Stories of pious Successors and Sufis meeting Khidr across the ages are too numerous and famous to count, though hadith scholars deny that he narrates from the Prophet. «وحكايات الصالحين من التابعين والصوفية في الاجتماع به والأخذ عنه في سائر الأعصار أكثر من أن تحصر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p322/r8] verses 18:65 | Unlike hadith scholars, Shaykh Ala al-Din claims to receive Prophetic hadith directly from Khidr without an intermediary. «فقد ادعى الشيخ علاء الدين استفادة الأحاديث النبوية عنه بلا واسطة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p323/r1] verses 18:65 | Al-Suhrawardi says Khidr narrated three hundred hadiths to them that he heard directly from the Prophet. «أن الخضر عليه السلام حدثنا بثلثمائة حديث سمعه من النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم شفاها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p323/r2] verses 18:65 | Those affirming Khidr's present life use continuity of a previously established state until contrary proof appears. «بالاستصحاب فإنه قد تحققت من قبل بالدليل فتبقى على ذلك إلى أن يقوم الدليل على خلافها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p323/r3] verses 18:65 | They answer the hundred-year hadith by saying it excludes Khidr only after a century, or that he was then over water; they also read its scope as people ordinarily seen, excluding angels and Satan. «لا يوجب نفي حياته في زمانه صلى الله عليه وسلم وإنما يوجب لظاهره نفيها بعد مائة سنة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p323/r4] verses 18:65 | The hadith's apparent scope includes anyone ordinarily dwelling on earth, including someone at sea; excluding sea-dwellers would weaken its generality and would not decisively refute later long-lived claimants such as Ratan. «على ظهر الأرض من هو من أهل الأرض ومتوطن فيها عرفا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p323/r5] verses 18:65 | The suggestion that Khidr was in the air when the hadith was spoken is weak; if alive, he should ordinarily be visible, and an exception needs proof. «يرد على الجواب الثاني أن الخضر لو كان موجودا لكان ممن يشاهده الناس كما هو الأمر المعتاد في البشر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p323/r6] verses 18:65 | They answer Ibn Taymiyya's objection that Khidr must have met the Prophet by citing believers such as Uways al-Qarni and Najashi who did not meet him; they suggest Khidr may have come secretly. «فهذا خير التابعين أويس القرني رضي الله تعالى عنه لم يتيسر له الإتيان والمرافقة في الجهاد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p323/r7] verses 18:65 | Ibn al-Mubarak recounts a battlefield encounter in which a handsome, fragrant man helped revive his dead horse; the story continues in the next segment. «فوقع فرسي ميتا فرأيت رجلا حسن الوجه طيب الرائحة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324#2/r1] verses 18:65 | Al-Raghib says Arabic uses 'everlasting' for things that decay slowly, such as stone hearths; the author calls the response strong because Noah also lived long. «كل ما يتباطأ عنه التغير والفساد تصفه العرب بالخلود كقولهم للأثافي خوالد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324#2/r2] verses 18:65 | Defenders say extraordinary age is no objection if Khidr's life is miraculous; early peoples were not always gigantic, and Khidr might have been granted Gabriel-like shape-changing power, though the author notes these replies are contestable. «فبأن البعد العادي لا يضر القائل بتعميره هذه المدة المديدة لأن ذلك عنده من خرق العادات»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324#2/r3] verses 18:65 | The author objects that reports of tiny Gog and Magog concern the end times and do not establish ancient stature; shape-changing for saints is disputed, and if granted to anyone the Prophet could have used it during the Hijra. «وفي ثبوته للأولياء خلاف كثير من المحدثين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324#2/r4] verses 18:65 | Defenders say failure to report Khidr on Noah's Ark does not prove he was absent: he may have boarded unseen or avoided the water by becoming enormous or taking to the air; the author presents these as speculative counterarguments. «لا يلزم من عدم نقل كونه في السفينة إن قلنا بأنه عليه السلام كان قبل نوح عليه السلام عدم وجوده»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324#2/r5] verses 18:65 | A report attributed to al-Alami says Khidr guides lost sailors and Ilyas guides lost travelers in mountains, then both guard Gog and Magog's wall at night; a similar Anas report has a weak chain. «أن الخضر يدور في البحار يهدي من ضل فيها وإلياس يدور في الجبال يهدي من ضل فيها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324#2/r6] verses 18:65 | Against the claim that only Noah's descendants survived the flood, defenders question whether all others died, read 'his descendants' as survival by reproduction rather than exclusivity, and note the devil was also aboard; the pre-Noah chronology is weak. «لا نسلم الاتفاق على أنه مات كل أهل السفينة ولم يبق بعد الخروج منها غير نسل نوح عليه السلام»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324#2/r7] verses 18:65 | Defenders say Khidr's long life need not appear in the Quran: Noah's lifespan consoles Muhammad about rejection by his people, while Khidr's story would not serve that purpose; mentioning Noah also implies longer life is possible. «وإنما ذكر سبحانه نوحا عليه السلام تسلية لنبينا صلى الله عليه وسلم بما لاقى من قومه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324#2/r8] verses 18:65 | The explicit mention of Noah's long life may imply that a longer life is possible, but it proves possibility rather than Khidr's actual survival. «من ذكر طول عمر نوح عليه السلام تصريحا يفهم تجويز عمر أطول من ذلك تلويحا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324/r1] verses 18:65 | The Ibn al-Mubarak account explicitly places Khidr in a battle, and the author says the Prophet's Badr prayer meant Islam would cease to be publicly dominant if the company died, not that no believer existed elsewhere. «فهذا صريح في أنه قد يحضر بعض المعارك وأما قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم اللهم إن تهلك هذه العصابة لا تعبد في الأرض»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324/r2] verses 18:65 | The author rejects comparing Khidr to Uways or Najashi: all prophets followed Muhammad during the Ascension, and Khidr had no apparent barrier to meeting him; equating their status risks unbelief. «ومتى زعم أحد أن نسبته إلى نبينا صلى الله عليه وسلم كنسبته إلى موسى عليه السلام فليجدد إسلامه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324/r3] verses 18:65 | The claim that Khidr secretly learned from the Prophet for a divine wisdom lacks evidence; if this were so, he could have visited once or twice as Gabriel appeared in Dihya's form. «على أنه لو كان كذلك لذكره صلى الله عليه وسلم ولو مرة وأين الدليل على الذكر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324/r4] verses 18:65 | The author dismisses the Ibn al-Mubarak story as unproven and implausible, saying Khidr's presence at the Prophet's command to Sa'd would matter more than appearing to Ibn al-Mubarak. «وأما ما روي عن ابن المبارك فلا نسلم ثبوته عنه وأنت إذا أمعنت النظر في ألفاظ القصة استبعدت صحتها»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324/r5] verses 18:65 | The Badr hadith can support Khidr's absence; interpreting it to mean only that no one would visibly worship God fails because it repeats the unsupported claim that Khidr was unseen. «فالاستدلال بالحديث على عدم وجود الخضر عليه السلام له وجه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p324/r6] verses 18:65 | They answer the verse denying immortality by saying they do not claim Khidr is eternal: some say he will fight the Antichrist, die when the Quran is lifted, or die near the end times. «بأن المراد من الخلد الدوام الأبدي والقائلون بوجوده اليوم لا يقولون بتأبيده»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p326#2/r1] verses 18:65 | The authentic report explains the name Khidr by greenery springing up where he sat; it does not make this a permanent sign, and the cubit-foot report is not authenticated. «أنه إنما سمي الخضر لأنه جلس على فروة بيضاء فإذا هي تهتز من خلفه خضراء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p326#2/r2] verses 18:65 | Changing appearances and miracles cannot prove a claimant is Khidr: saints may perform wonders, speak metaphorically as 'Khidr,' or use identity language as Sufis do; good opinion cannot yield certainty. «وظهور الخوارق مشترك بينه وبين غيره من أولياء الأمة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p326#2/r3] verses 18:65 | The author rejects the claim that Khidr meets ignorant lawbreakers, attributing such stories to liars; the stronger claim is that he meets major ascetics who observe religious limits. «فالكاذبون الدجالون يكذبون على الله تعالى وعلى رسوله صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p326#2/r4] verses 18:65 | Ali al-Khawwas reportedly asked Khidr to accompany him on pilgrimage, but Khidr refused because reliance on his presence might weaken al-Khawwas's trust in God. «وسئل عن سبب إبائه فقال : خفت من النقص في توكلي حيث اعتمد على وجوده معي»
