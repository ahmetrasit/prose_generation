# Notes

## 20:5
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p158/r5] verses 20:5 | Asked how God istawa, Rabi'a replied that the meaning is known but its modality is incomprehensible; God sent the message, the Messenger conveyed it, and believers must submit. «ومن طريق ربيعة بن عبد الرحمن أنه سئل كيف استوى على العرش فقال : الأستواء غير مجهول والكيف غير معقول»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p158/r6] verses 20:5 | Some identify the salaf's position as consigning meaning after rejecting resemblance; Alusi objects that many texts and reports make it unlikely that believers were addressed about unknowable meanings. «وقيل : أن السلف بعد نفى ما يتوهم من التشبيه يقولون : لا ندرى ما معنى ذلك والله تعالى أعلم بمراده»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p158/r7] verses 20:5 | Alusi invokes reports suggesting that the original hearers understood the meaning of such expressions, as an objection to the claim that their meaning was unknowable. «وأيضا قد ورد في الأخبار ما يدل على فهم المخاطب المعنى من مثل ذلك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r1] verses 20:5 | Aisha reports that the Prophet said God laughs at His servants' despair while mercy is near; she inferred divine approval, and he confirmed that He indeed laughs. No grading is stated. «عن عائشة رضى الله تعالى عنها قالت : سمعت رسول الله ( صلى الله عليه وسلم ) يقول : أن الله تعالى يضحك من ياس عباده»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r10] verses 20:5 | Alusi suggests some Sufis who affirm apparent wording without implications may lack the special unveiling or inspiration described, whose bestowal belongs to God. «ولعل من يقول باجراء المتشابهات على ظواهرها مع نفى اللوازم كمذهب السلف الأول من الصوفيه طائفة لم يحصل لهم ما حصل لهاتين الطائفين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r11] verses 20:5 | Al-Laqqani says salaf and khalaf agree on rejecting impossible literal implications and affirming the texts from God, but differ over whether to specify a valid meaning; salaf interpretation is general and khalaf interpretation detailed. «قال اللقانى : اجمع الخلف ويعبر عنه بالمؤوله والسلف ويعبر عنهم بالمفوضة على تنزيهه تعالى عن المعنى المحاد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r2] verses 20:5 | Alusi uses Aisha's response to show that she understood divine laughter as conveying a good outcome or approval, rather than an unintelligible expression. «فلا يعد منا خيرا إذا ضحك فانها رضى الله تعالى عنها لو لم تفهم من ضحكه تعالى معنى لم تقل ما قالت»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r3] verses 20:5 | Mujahid, as cited in Sahih al-Bukhari, explains istawa as rising above the Throne. «ففى صحيح البخارى قال مجاهد : استوى على العرش علا على العرش»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r4] verses 20:5 | Abu al-Aliya explains istawa as rising. «وقال أبو العالية : استوى على العرش ارتفع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r5] verses 20:5 | Alusi distinguishes two salaf approaches: one specifies the apparent meaning after removing its implications, while another accepts that and other non-impossible meanings but leaves God's intended meaning unspecified out of caution. «أن السلف قسمان قسم منهم بعد أن نفوا التشبيه عينوا المعنى الظاهر المعرى عن اللوازم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r6] verses 20:5 | Later theologians often hold that the apparent sense is not intended and identify another meaning, usually figurative but sometimes literal; they may consign the meaning when several sound options lack decisive evidence. «وذهبت طائفة من المنزهين عن التشبيه والتجسيم إلى أنه ليس المراد الظواهر مع نفى اللوازم بل المراد معنى معين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r7] verses 20:5 | According to al-Kurani, the Sufi approach applies ambiguous texts according to their apparent wording while denying implications and affirming God's incomparability; Ibn Arabi's statements on this topic are inconsistent. «ومذهب الصوفية على ما ذكرة الشيخ إبراهيم الكورانى وغيره اجراء المتشابهات على ظواهرها مع نفى اللوازم والتنزيه بليس كمثله شئ»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r8] verses 20:5 | Ibn Arabi describes an elite Sufi group that first empties the heart of reasoning, then seeks divine instruction through remembrance, reverence, presence, and readiness for unveiling. «وطائفة من المنزهة أيضا وهى العالية وهم اصحابنا فرغوا قلوبهم من الفكر والنظر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p159/r9] verses 20:5 | Ibn Arabi says those receiving direct unveiling learn the transcendent meanings at once, varying with context; another group receives inspiration or writing and recognizes it through signs known only to them. «فعندما توجهت قلوبهم وهممهم إلى الله عز وجل ولجأت اليه سبحانه وتعالى والقت عنها ما استمسك به الغير»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p160/r1] verses 20:5 | Alusi's teacher Ala al-Din says the consigners have one interpretation, while those who interpret have two. «وكان شيخنا العلامة علاء الدين يقول : ما عليه المفوضة تأويل واحد وما عليه المؤوله تأويلان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p160/r2] verses 20:5 | One analysis says affirming the apparent wording while removing its implications is itself interpretation, since the wording's ordinary appearance entails those implications and evidence redirects it. «واماما عليه القائلون بالظواهر مع نفي اللوازم وظاهر الالفاظ انفسها تقتضيها ففيه أخراج اللفظ عما يقتضيه الظاهر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p160/r3] verses 20:5 | Another analysis denies that this is interpretation because the wording, when related to God, does not entail creaturely implications; Sunnis similarly affirm seeing God in the afterlife without worldly conditions of sight. «وقيل : لا تأويل فيه لأنه يعتبرون اللفظ من حيث نسبتعم اليه عز شأنه وهو من هذه الحيثية لا يقتضى اللوازم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p160/r4] verses 20:5 | A scholar defines interpretation as explanation: everyone who explains has interpreted, while one who does not explain has not; thus only consigners avoid interpretation. «وقال بعض الفضلاء : كل من فسر فقد أول وكل من لم يفسر لم يؤول»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p160/r5] verses 20:5 | Alusi favors interpretation over affirming apparent senses while denying implications for statements such as God's address to jinn and humans and the exclamation of sorrow over servants. «وانا اميل إلى التأويل وعدم القول بالظواهر مع نفى اللوازم في بعض ما ينسب إلى الله تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p160/r6] verses 20:5 | If sound, the Black Stone report should be understood as a simile because the contextual clue is clear; Alusi rejects treating the Stone as an attribute of God, as some do with the hand. «وكذا قوله ( صلى الله عليه وسلم ) أن صح : الحجر الاسود يمين الله في ارضه فمن قبله أو صافحه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p160/r7] verses 20:5 | Alusi sees a close connection between affirming apparent wording while denying implications and the qualified Sufi doctrine of the unity of being. «وارى من يقول بالظواهر ونفى اللوازم في الجميع بينه وبين القول بوحدة الوجود على الوجه الذي قاله محققو الصوفية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p160/r8] verses 20:5 | Alusi favors a domed Throne because of a sound hadith, while saying rational evidence more strongly supports its spherical form; proponents reconcile the reports accordingly. «واميل أيضا إلى القول بتقبيب العرش لصحة الحديث في ذلك والأقرب إلى الدليل العقلى القول بكريته»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p161/r1] verses 20:5 | Ibn Arabi describes the Throne as having four corners and faces, multiple supports, a treasury of mercy, and surrounding blindness; Alusi says most details lack known scriptural support and some cannot be taken literally. «وقال الشيخ الأكبر محي الدين قدس سره في الباب الحادى والسبعين والثلاثمائة من الفتوحات : أنه ذو اركان اربعة ووجه اربعة هي قوائمه الاصليه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p161/r2] verses 20:5 | Alusi says the apparent view is that the Throne is one, although some Sufis hold that it is multiple. «والظاهر أن العرش واحد وقال من قال من الصوفية بتعدده»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p161/r3] verses 20:5 | Associating the istiwa with the name al-Rahman increases hope in God, whose mercy encompasses everything. «ولا يخفى ما في نسبة الأستواء اليه تعالى بعنوان الرحمانية مما يزيد قوة الرجاء به جل وعلا»
[ASAD-NOTES:20:1#n3/r1] verses 20:5 | Asad renders the metaphorical al-'arsh as "the throne of His almightiness", referring to his explanation at 7:54, note 43. «For my rendering of the metaphorical term al-'arsh as "the throne of His almightiness"»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:20:5-6/r1] verses 20:5, 20:6 | God's dominion over all realms and what lies beneath the earth displays the perfection of His power and will; the following mention of His knowledge follows because power depends on will, which is inseparable from knowledge. «ليدل بذلك على كمال قدرته وإرادته»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:20:8/r4] verses 20:5 | One reading declines “al-Rahman” as an adjective of “the One who created,” making “established over the Throne” a predicate with an omitted subject; if nominative as praise, it too has an omitted subject, or may be a second predicate. «وقرىء { الرحمن } على الجر صفة لمن خلق»
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p8#2/r10] verses 20:5 | The hadith in Muslim that hearts are between two of God's fingers means God has immense, effortless power to turn hearts as He wills, not a literal finger. «عن عبد الله بن عمرو رضي الله عنهما ' القلوب بين إصبعين»
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p8#2/r11] verses 20:5 | Al-Ghazālī is cited for taking the fingers in the hadith figuratively: the image conveys God's power over hearts, not a physical finger. «نبه على ذلك حجة الإسلام الغزالي»
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p8#2/r12] verses 20:5 | Al-Zamakhsharī likewise treats so-and-so's open hand as a metaphor for generosity, even where there is no literal hand or extension. «ومنه أخذ الزمخشري أن يد فلان مبسوطة كناية عن جواد وإن لم يكن هناك يد»
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p8#2/r8] verses 20:5 | Al-Raḥmān names the giver of universal blessings to obedient and disobedient alike; the verse establishes kingship, since power alone does not make one a king. «الوصف المفيض للنعم العامة للطائع والعاصي»
[BIQAI-FULL:v5p8#2/r9] verses 20:5 | Istawā means God undertook governance of creation alone, using the familiar image of a king sitting upright on a throne; it entails neither an actual seat nor physical location. «أي أخذ في تدبير ذلك منفردا»
