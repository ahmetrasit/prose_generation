# Notes

## 9:128
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r6] verses 9:128 | Although he calls the Arab reading more correct, al-Qurtubi says the universal reading makes the proof stronger because the messenger is human like his audience and can be understood and followed. «والقول الثاني أوكد للحجة أي هو بشر مثلكم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r7] verses 9:128 | From among yourselves praises the Prophet’s lineage as belonging to the core and purest Arab line. «قوله تعالى: { من أنفسكم } يقتضي مدحا لنسب النبي»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r8] verses 9:128 | Wathila reports the Prophet saying God chose Kinana from Ishmael’s descendants, Quraysh from Kinana, Hashim from Quraysh, and the Prophet from Hashim. «وفي صحيح مسلم عن واثلة بن الأسقع قال: سمعت رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:9:128-129/r9] verses 9:128 | A report from the Prophet says his ancestry came through lawful marriage, never fornication; al-Qurtubi explains there was no illicit conception back to Adam. «وروي عنه صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه قال:
" إني من نكاح ولست من سفاح "»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r1] verses 9:128 | The fourth explanation says the Prophet’s kinship highlights his purity: his people know his truthfulness, trustworthiness, chastity, and protection of them from harm while bringing them good; such a messenger is a great blessing. «هو من عشيرتكم تعرفونه بالصدق والأمانة والعفاف والصيانة»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r2] verses 9:128 | A reported reading understands min anfusikum as ‘from among your noblest and best’; it is attributed to the Prophet, Fatima, and Aisha. «أي من أشرفكم وأفضلكم ، وقيل : هي قراءة رسول الله وفاطمة وعائشة»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r3] verses 9:128 | ʿAzīz means prevailing and severe, and ʿizza means overcoming strength; when hardship overcomes someone, its force shows that the person could not ward it off. «اعلم أن العزيز هو الغالب الشديد ، والعزة هي الغلبة والشدة .»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r4] verses 9:128 | The expression ʿazza ʿalayya (‘this was hard on me’) reflects the idea that a hardship prevailed over the person who could not repel it. «فلهذا السبب إذا اشتد على الإنسان شيء قال : عز علي هذا»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r5] verses 9:128 | ʿAnt means falling into hardship and distress from which one cannot escape; the commentary compares the usage in 4:25 and 2:220. «عنت الرجل يعنت عنتا إذا وقع في مشقة وشدة لا يمكنه الخروج منها»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r6] verses 9:128 | Al-Farrāʾ parses mā in mā ʿanittum as nominative, meaning ‘your hardship’; the Prophet is pained by their misfortune, especially the punishment of God that he was sent to avert. «قال الفراء : { ما } في قوله : { ما عنتم } في موضع رفع»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r7] verses 9:128 | The Prophet’s eagerness concerns conveying worldly and otherworldly good to people, not their persons as such; this makes the clause complement rather than repeat his pain at their harms. «والحرص يمتنع أن يكون متعلقا بذواتهم ، بل المراد حريص على إيصال الخيرات»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r8] verses 9:128 | Al-Farrāʾ glosses ḥarīṣ as ‘stingy’ with regard to believers entering Hell, but al-Rāzī rejects this as an interpretation that leaves the phrase without benefit. «قال الفراء : الحريص الشحيح ، ومعناه : أنه شحيح عليكم أن تدخلوا النار»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p187/r9] verses 9:128 | Ibn ʿAbbās says that God named the Prophet with two of His own names, Raʾūf and Raḥīm. «قال ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما : سماه الله تعالى باسمين من أسمائه .»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p188/r1] verses 9:128 | The demanding duties resemble a skilled physician’s treatment or a compassionate father’s care: they aim to save people from everlasting punishment and secure everlasting reward. «قلنا : قد ضربنا لهذا المعنى مثل الطبيب الحاذق والأب المشفق ، والمعنى :»
[RAZI-FULL:v16p188/r2] verses 9:128 | Placing ‘to believers’ before ‘compassionate, merciful’ restricts the Prophet’s compassion to believers; this balances the surah’s severity toward unbelievers and hypocrites. «يفيد الحصر بمعنى أنه لا رأفة ولا رحمة له إلا بالمؤمنين»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r1] verses 9:128 | من أنفسكم adjectivally modifies رسول and means he is from the heart of the Arabs. «صفة لرسول، أي: من صميم العرب»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r10] verses 9:128 | If this analysis is nevertheless weakened into competition, the second verb governs because making the first govern would require an omitted object in the second. «أنه متى أعمل الأول أضمر في الثاني من غير حذف»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r2] verses 9:128 | A reading with fatḥa on fāʾ, attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās and several others, means from your noblest or most choice people; it is also attributed to the Prophet, Fāṭima, and ʿĀʾisha. «أي: من أشرفكم، من النفاسة»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r3] verses 9:128 | One analysis makes عزيز an adjective of رسول; ما is either a verbal noun or relative pronoun and is the subject of عزيز, meaning your hardship grieves him. «صفة لرسول، وفيه أنه تقدم غير الوصف الصريح»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r4] verses 9:128 | The poet's example يسر المرء ما ذهب الليالي supports reading the ما-clause as the subject of عزيز, like the nights' passing being what gladdens a person. «2557 يسر المرء ما ذهب الليالي»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r5] verses 9:128 | Another parse makes عزيز a fronted predicate and ما عنتم a delayed subject, with the whole clause describing the Messenger. «ويجوز أن يكون " عزيز " خبرا مقدما»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r6] verses 9:128 | Al-Ḥawfī proposes عزيز as subject and ما عنتم as its predicate, despite the indefinite subject's following prepositional complement. «وجوز الحوفي أن يكون " عزيز " مبتدأ»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r7] verses 9:128 | The author prefers عزيز as an adjective of رسول because حريص is also adjectival; positing an omitted هو before حريص is unnecessary. «صفة لرسول؛ لقوله بعد ذلك " حريص " فلم يجعل خبرا لغيره»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r8] verses 9:128 | بالمؤمنين attaches to رؤوف. «و " بالمؤمنين " متعلق برؤوف»
[SAMIN-DURR:9:128/r9] verses 9:128 | The construction is not syntactic competition between two governors because that requires the shared complement to follow both; some grammarians nevertheless allow examples such as ضربت وشتمته. «ولا يجوز أن تكون المسألة من التنازع لأن من شرطه تأخر المعمول عن العاملين»

[SULAMI:v1p292#6/r3] verses 9:128 | Al-Husayn says the Messenger is highest in spirit and aspiration, choosing the Real above both worlds, with a heart that never swerved from accord with Him. «قال الحسين : من أجلاكم نفسا وأعلاكم همة ، جاء بالكونين عوضا عن الحق»
[SULAMI:v1p292#6/r4] verses 9:128 | Ibn Ata says the Prophet's self is in accord with God rather than like created selves; prophetic lights sanctify him, and witnessing realities establish him at the nearest locus and highest station. «قال ابن عطاء : نفسه موافقة لا نفس الخلق ، خلقه ومباينة لها حقيقة فإنها نفس مقدسة بأنوار النبوة»
