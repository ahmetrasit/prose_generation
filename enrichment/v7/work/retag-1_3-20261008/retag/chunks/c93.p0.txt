# Notes

## 19:45
[QURTUBI-FULL:19:41-46/r7] verses 19:45 | A threat from the Merciful would touch Abraham’s father if he died persisting in his present state; akhafu may mean I know, or express fear that he die an unbeliever. «أي إن مت على ما أنت عليه. ويكون «أخاف» بمعنى أعلم»
[QURTUBI-FULL:19:41-46/r8] verses 19:45 | Being Satan’s ally means becoming his companion in the Fire. «فتكون للشيطان وليا } أي قرينا في النار»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p193/r1] verses 19:45 | Al-Farra’ says “I fear” means “I know.” «قال الفراء : معنى أخاف أعلم»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p193/r2] verses 19:45 | Most interpreters take Abraham’s fear literally; certainty that his father would die an unbeliever is unproven, since he could still believe or persist, so fear is appropriate. «والأكثرون على أنه محمول على ظاهره»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p193/r3] verses 19:45 | A person is only called afraid for another when that person’s possible harm would cause pain in the one who fears, as with fear for one’s child. «من يظن وصول الضرر إلى غيره فإنه لا يسمى خائفا إلا إذا كان بحيث يلزم»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p193/r4] verses 19:45 | “Satan’s ally” may mean that, after deserving punishment, the father will accompany Satan in Hell; alliance is then used figuratively for its result, although scripture says worldly friends become enemies. «أنه إذا استوجب عذاب الله كان مع الشيطان في النار والولاية سبب للمعية»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p193/r5] verses 19:45 | A second reading takes the punishment as God’s abandonment, after which the father becomes Satan’s ally. «أن يحمل العذاب على الخذلان أي إني أخاف أن يمسك خذلان الله»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p193/r6] verses 19:45 | A third reading takes “ally” as one who follows after Satan, like rain that comes in succession. «المطر الذي يأتي تاليا وليا»
[RAZI-FULL:v21p193/r7] verses 19:45 | Satanic alliance is worse than punishment because God’s pleasure exceeds even the reward of Paradise, making its opposite worse than punishment. «رضوان الله تعالى أعظم من الثواب»
[STUDYQURAN:19:2/r3] verses 19:2, 19:18, 19:26, 19:44, 19:45, 19:58, 19:61 | The opening mention of mercy fits the sūrah’s repeated use of al-Raḥmān, from the same root. «namely, that God is referred to throughout as al-Raḥmān, “the Compassionate”»
[STUDYQURAN:19:45/r1] verses 19:45 | Walī means friend or protector and denotes close allegiance, support, or love. «Friend here translates walī, which can also mean “protector”»
[STUDYQURAN:19:45/r2] verses 19:45 | The contrast with God's friends, especially in Sufi usage, frames saints as channels of divine grace and Satan's friends as channels of temptation. «serve as channels of His Grace and Favor in this world»
[STUDYQURAN:19:45/r3] verses 19:45 | Becoming Satan's associate entails separation from or repudiation by God; some commentators say Abraham fears this, while others say he knows it will happen. «When one becomes a friend or associate of Satan, one is dissociated from or repudiated by God»
[STUDYQURAN:19:45/r4] verses 19:45 | The feared friendship may mean that Abraham's father will share Satan's company in Hell; it is not literal friendship, since Satan is humanity's enemy and abandons followers in the Hereafter. «his father will end up as a companion or cohabitant with Satan in Hellfire»
[STUDYQURAN:19:45/r5] verses 19:45 | The repeated address O my father signals love and earnest filial concern, rather than disrespect. «the words he speaks are spoken out of love and earnest filial concern»
[TAB-FULL:v15p551#2/r1] verses 19:45 | Al-Ṭabarī says Abraham's “I fear” means “I know”; he knows his father's idolatry will bring punishment and Satan's patronage. «الموضع بمعنى العلم، كما الخشية بمعنى العلم،»
[TAB-FULL:v15p551#2/r2] verses 19:45 | He compares this use with “we feared” meaning “we knew” in the account of the two boys. «الموضع بمعنى العلم، كما الخشية بمعنى العلم، في قوله: {فخشينا أن يرهقهما طغيانا وكفرا} [الكهف: 80]»
[TABATABAI:19:44-47/r5] verses 19:45 | The feared punishment is divine abandonment or withholding of mercy; the alternative that it specifically means punishment in the afterlife does not fit the context. «وما ذكره بعضهم أن المراد به العذاب الأخروي لا يساعد عليه السياق»
[TABRISI:19:45-48/r1] verses 19:45 | Ibrahim fears that persistent disbelief will bring his father punishment from God. «أي يصبك عذاب من جهة الله سبحانه لإصرارك على الكفر»
[TABRISI:19:45-48/r2] verses 19:45 | Being Satan's ally means being left to Satan, who cannot help the father. «أي فتكون موكولا إلى الشيطان وهو لا يغني عنك شيئا عن الجبائي»
[TABRISI:19:45-48/r3] verses 19:45 | Alternatively, the father follows Satan in curse and abandonment; a follower is one who comes after another. «وقيل: معناه فتكون لاحقا بالشيطان باللعن والخذلان»
[TABRISI:19:45-48/r4] verses 19:45 | Another view makes him Satan's companion in Hell. «وقيل: فتكون له قرينا في النار»
[TABRISI:19:45-48/r5] verses 19:45 | The wording may mean Satan would support him; the point is to shame him and deter allegiance, not promise real help. «ولم يقل فيكون الشيطان وليك لأنه أبلغ في الفضيحة وإنما أراد زجره عن موالاة الشيطان»
[TABRISI:19:45-48/r6] verses 19:45 | The author's community holds that the man called Ibrahim's father was his maternal grandfather; his biological father was Tārūkh, since the Prophet's forebears were monotheists. «الذي يقوله أصحابنا أن هذا الخطاب من إبراهيم ع إنما توجه إلى من سماه الله أبا له»
[TABRISI:19:45-48/r7] verses 19:45 | They argue that all the Prophet's ancestors were Muslim monotheists, citing the report of pure loins and purified wombs. «لإجماع الطائفة على أن آباء نبينا صلى الله عليه وسلم إلى آدم ع كلهم مسلمون موحدون»
[TABRISI:19:45-48/r8] verses 19:45 | They add that a disbeliever is not described as pure, citing the statement that polytheists are impure. «والكافر غير موصوف بالطهارة لقوله تعالى: إنما المشركون نجس»
[THALABI:v6p216#2/r4] verses 19:45 | Here “I fear” is explained as “I know”; related constructions use the same verb for fearing spouses will fail to uphold God’s limits. «إني أخاف أعلم أن يمسك يصيبك عذاب من الرحمن لقوله: إلا أن يخافا وقوله فإن خفتم»
[THALABI:v6p216#2/r5] verses 19:45 | Another explanation is that Abraham feared a worldly punishment would fall on his father, leaving him Satan’s companion in Hell. «وقيل: معناه إني أخاف أن ينزل عليك عذابا في الدنيا فتكون للشيطان وليا قرينا في النار»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p255/r1] verses 19:45 | Abraham fears that his father will suffer God's punishment by obeying Satan and become Satan's companion in Hell. «أخشى أن يصيبك عذاب الله بطاعتك الشيطان»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v14p255/r2] verses 19:45 | He says أخاف can mean I know, comparing it to خشينا in the story of Moses and the servant, where it means we knew. «الفراء: ({أخاف} أي: أعلم، كقوله: {فخشينا أن يرهقهما} [الكهف: 80]، أي: فعلمنا)»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:19:44-46/r2] verses 19:45 | If Abraham's father dies in his current state, punishment may strike him and make him Satan's companion in the Fire. «يا أبت إني أخاف } إن مت على ما أنت عليه أن يصيبك { عذاب من الرحمن فتكون للشيطان وليا } قرينا في النار.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p226/r7] verses 19:45 | Abraham says repentance remains acceptable until punishment descends; he hoped his father would repent, but that hope ended when he died in unbelief. «وما لم ينزل العذاب فتوبتك مقبولة إن تبت. وقد كان إبراهيم يرجو أن يتوب. فلما مات على الكفر ذهب ذلك الرجاء»

## 19:93
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p303/r3] verses 19:93 | Abu Hayyan prefers taking من after كل as a relative pronoun meaning all who are in the heavens and earth. «والأولى جعلها موصولة لأن كونها موصوفة بالنسبة إلى الموصولة قليل»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p303/r4] verses 19:93 | Al-Zamakhshari instead calls من a descriptive indefinite after كل, by analogy with its occurrence after رب. «وقال الزمخشري: من موصوفة لأنها وقعت بعد كل نكرة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p303/r5] verses 19:93 | These readers recite إلا آت with nunation and الرحمن in the accusative; the majority read it in a genitive construction, and آتي is the predicate of كل with عبدا circumstantial. «وقرأ عبد الله وابن الزبير وأبو حيوة وطلحة وأبو بحرية وابن أبي عبلة ويعقوب إلا آت بالتنوين»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p303/r6] verses 19:93 | Repeating الرحمن emphasizes that no one else deserves this name, since the origins and branches of blessings come from Him. «وتكرر لفظ الرحمن تنبيها على أنه لا يستحق هذا الاسم غيره»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p303/r7] verses 19:93 | The beings claimed as deities, including angels, Jesus, and Ezra, all come to the Merciful as obedient servants; alleged filial status cannot confer divinity. «ومن في السموات والأرض يشمل من اتخذوه معبودا من الملائكة وعيسى وعزيرا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v16p142/r1] verses 19:93 | All beings, including angels and humans and jinn, are God's owned servants, submitting to His decree; their coming is figurative submission. «أي ما منهم أحد من الملائكة والثقلين»
