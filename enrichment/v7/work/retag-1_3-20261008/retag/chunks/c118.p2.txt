[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p185/r7] verses 39:6 | Ibn Zayd says successive creation refers to descendants, from Adam and then from their fathers. «قال ابن زيد: خلقا من بعد خلق: آخر من ظهر آدم وظهور الآباء.»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p185/r8] verses 39:6 | These authorities interpret successive creation as stages such as the morsel, clot, and other embryonic forms. «وقال عكرمة ومجاهد والسدي: رتبا خلقا من بعد خلق على المضغة والعلقة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v9p185/r9] verses 39:6 | Al-Zamakhshari enumerates the sequence as a sound animal after bones covered with flesh, bare bones, morsels, clots, and drops. «حيوانا سويا، من بعد عظام مكسوة لحما، من بعد عظام عارية، من بعد مضغ»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r1] verses 39:6 | The creation of humanity is an independent proof of God's oneness and dominion, especially relevant to the lower world; humanity is mentioned first because reason and the divine trust make its creation especially remarkable. «الخ دليل آخر على الوحدة والقهر وترك عطفه على ( خلق السماوات ) للإيذان باستقلاله في الدلالة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r10] verses 39:6 | Interpretations that take the pairs as the causes of livestock's sustenance, or make their descent mean their production through heavenly causes, are dismissed as unconvincing. «وقيل يراد بالأزواج أسباب تعيشها أو يجعل الإنزال مجازا عن إحداث ذلك بأسباب سماوية وهو كما ترى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r11] verses 39:6 | A report that God literally created livestock in Paradise and sent them down is mentioned, but al-Ālūsī sees no sound basis for it. «وقيل الكلام على ظاهره والله تعالى خلق الأنعام في الجنة ثم أنزلها منها ولا أرى لهذا الخبر صحة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r12] verses 39:6 | The livestock are camels, cattle, sheep, and goats; the eight pairs are the male and female of each kind. «والأنعام الإبل والبقر والضأن والمعز وكانت ثمانية أزواج لأن كلا منها ذكر وأنثى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r13] verses 39:6 | The adverbial phrase is placed before the explicit object to foreground what precedes and create anticipation for what follows. «وتقديم الظرفين على المفعول الصريح لما مر مرارا من الإعتناء بما قدم والتشويق إلى ما أخر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r2] verses 39:6 | An anonymous saying calls a human a small body containing the greater world, underscoring why human creation is singled out. «حتى قيل : وتزعم أنك جرم صغير وفيك انطوى العالم الأكبر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r3] verses 39:6 | The one soul is Adam, and its spouse is Eve; she was created from his lowest left rib, whether from part or all of it, with a replacement created for Adam. «والمراد بالنفس آدم عليه السلام وقوله تعالى : ( ثم جعل منها زوجها أي حواء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r4] verses 39:6 | Grammatically, “then He made from it its mate” may continue the description of the single soul, or coordinate with “one,” originally a derived noun that can take a verb as its coordinate. «على معنى أنها خلقت من بعضها أو خلقت منها كلها وخلق الله تعالى لآدم مكانها عطف على محذوف»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r5] verses 39:6 | A further grammatical option coordinates the clause with “your creation”; on this reading, making Eve is a distinct sign more amazing than creating Adam, which explains the verb “made,” and “then” marks rank rather than time. «أو على ( خلقكم ) لتفاوت ما بينهما في الدلالة فإنهما وإن كانتا آيتين دالتين على ما مر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r6] verses 39:6 | One view says Adam's descendants were first drawn from his back as particles, so “created you from it” refers to their extraction and “then” is temporal; al-Ālūsī accepts this only if soundly transmitted as Prophetic or equivalent. «وقيل إنه تعالى أخرج ذرية آدم عليه السلام من ظهره كالذر ثم خلق منه حواء»
