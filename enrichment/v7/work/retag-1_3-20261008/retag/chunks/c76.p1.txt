[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p168/r6] verses 17:110 | Mujahid interprets prayer here as supplication and petition. «عاصم بن حكيم أن مجاهدا قال: {ولا تجهر بصلاتك ولا تخافت بها} [الإسراء: 110] في الدعاء والمسألة.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p168/r7] verses 17:110 | Ibn Abbas says some prayers are recited silently and others aloud, so each should be recited according to its prescribed mode. «ابن لهيعة، عن عبد الله بن هبيرة، أن ابن عباس كان يقول: إن من الصلاة سرا، ومنها جهرا، فلا تجهر فيما تسر فيه، ولا تسر فيما تجهر فيه،»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p168/r8] verses 17:110 | Yahya adopts Ibn Abbas's explanation: recite aloud in prayers prescribed aloud and quietly in prayers prescribed quietly. «قال يحيى: هي على هذا التفسير: أي تجهر فيما يجهر فيه وتسر فيما يسر فيه.»
[YAHYA-SALLAM:v1p168/r9] verses 17:110 | In a report from Zayd ibn Aslam, the Prophet approved Abu Bakr's quiet prayer, Umar's loud prayer, and Bilal's switching surahs, then directed each to moderate or complete his recitation; the verse was revealed. «عثمان، عن زيد بن أسلم، أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم سمع أبا بكر وهو يصلي من الليل وهو يخفي صوته، وسمع عمر وهو يجهر صوته،»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p264/r4] verses 17:110 | When Arabs heard the name al-Raḥmān, they thought the call invoked two beings; al-Raḥmān is also a name found in earlier revealed scriptures. «لما سمعت العرب ذكر الرحمن قالت: أتدعونا إلى اثنين إلى الله وإلى
الرحمن. واسم الرحمن في الكتب الأول المنزلة على الأنبياء.»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p264/r5] verses 17:110 | Calling God or al-Raḥmān addresses the same one; ayyan mā means whichever of God’s beautiful names you invoke. «فأعلمهم الله أن دعاءهم الرحمن ودعاءهم الله يرجعان إلى شيء واحد فقال: (أيا ما تدعوا) المعنى أي أسماء الله تدعوا (فله الأسماء الحسنى).»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p264/r6] verses 17:110 | Mukhāfata means quiet speech and jahr means raising the voice; the Prophet was told not to expose Qur’anic recitation to the polytheists’ abuse, nor to make it inaudible to companions praying behind him. «فأمره الله - جل وعز - ألا يعرض القرآن لسبهم، وألا يخافت بها مخافتة لا يسمعها من يصلي خلفه من أصحابه.»
[ZAJJAJ:v3p264/r7] verses 17:110 | Seek a course between loud recitation and quiet recitation. «أي اسلك طريقا بين الجهر والمخافتة.»

## 18:65
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r10] verses 18:65 | Al-Mursi argues Khidr is not alive; otherwise he would have had to come to Muhammad, believe in him, and follow him, citing a report about Moses and Jesus following Muhammad if alive. «وقال شرف الدين أبو عبد الله محمد بن أبي الفضل المرسي: أما خضر»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r11] verses 18:65 | Abu Hayyan notes the cited hadith may refer only to Moses, while Muslims hold Jesus is alive and will descend. «انتهى هكذا أوراد لحديث ومذهب المسلمين أن عيسى حي وأنه ينزل»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r2] verses 18:65 | Calling him a servant from God’s servants is an honorific addition expressing special distinction. «عبدا من عبادنا هذه إضافة تشريف واختصاص،»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r3] verses 18:65 | They found the servant at the rock where the fish was lost, lying under his garment; Moses greets him and they discuss their different divinely given knowledge. «وجداه عند الصخرة التي فقد الحوت عندها وهو مسجى في ثوبه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r4] verses 18:65 | The majority identify him as al-Khidr; other reports name al-Yasaʿ, Ilyas, Khidrun son of Qabil, or Bilya ibn Malkan. «والجمهور على أنه الخضر وخالف من لا يعتد بخلافه فزعم»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r5] verses 18:65 | The majority say Khidr was a prophet taught revealed inner realities, while Moses knew legal rulings and outward judgments. «والجمهور على أن الخضر نبي وكان علمه معرفة بواطن قد أوحيت إليه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r6] verses 18:65 | A hadith says Khidr was named after sitting on a worn hide that turned green beneath him. «وفي الحديث سمي خضرا لأنه جلس على فروة بالية فاهتزت تحته خضراء.»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r7] verses 18:65 | Other reports explain his name by the ground turning green when he prayed or by him sitting on elevated white or hard ground that turned green. «وقيل: كان إذا صلى اخضر ما حوله. وقيل: جلس على فروة بيضاء»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r8] verses 18:65 | Reports say his mother was Roman and his father Persian, or make him a king’s son who refused succession and fled to sea islands. «وقيل: كانت أمه رومية وأبوه فارسي. وقيل: كان ابن ملك من الملوك»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p203#2/r9] verses 18:65 | The majority say Khidr died. «والجمهور على أنه مات. وقال شرف الدين أبو عبد الله»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p204/r1] verses 18:65 | The mercy God gave Khidr is interpreted as revelation and prophethood, or as provision. «والرحمة التي آتاه الله إياها هي الوحي والنبوة. وقيل: الرزق.»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p204/r2] verses 18:65 | Knowledge from God means knowledge reserved to Him, specifically information about the unseen. «وعلمناه من لدنا علما أي من عندنا أي مما يختص بنا من العلم»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p204/r3] verses 18:65 | Abu Zayd transmits from Abu Amr a light-nūn reading of min ladunā, a dialect form of ladun, said to be its original form. «وقرأ أبو زيد عن أبي عمرو من لدنا بتخفيف النون وهي لغة في لدن»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p204/r4] verses 18:65 | Abu Hayyan criticizes people claiming private inspired knowledge that certifies companions for Paradise or identifies someone as Khidr. «قيل: وقد أولع كثير ممن ينتمي إلى الصلاح بادعاء هذا العلم»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p204/r5] verses 18:65 | Ibn Daqiq al-Idd recounted a teacher’s claim to have seen Khidr; when asked how he knew the man was Khidr, the teacher fell silent. «وكان قاضي القضاة أبو الفتح محمد بن علي بن مطيع القشيري المعروف»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p204/r6] verses 18:65 | Some say Khidriyya is a rank saints inherit in Khidr’s footsteps; Abdul Wahid al-Abbasi’s Hanbali companions believed he met Khidr. «وبعضهم يزعم أن الخضرية رتبة يتولاها بعض الصالحين على قدم الخضر»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p216/r6] verses 18:65, 18:66, 18:82 | Al-Tahrir wa al-Tahbir condemns using Moses’ encounter with Khidr to claim Khidr or a saint is superior to a prophet, calling the argument self-delusion. «وفي كتاب التحرير والتحبير ما نصه: تعلق بعض الجهال بما جرى»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p216/r7] verses 18:65, 18:66, 18:82 | Ibn al-Arabi claimed saints outrank prophets because they take directly from God while prophets are mediated; Abu Hayyan denounces this and the related claims as heresy and extremist unity doctrine. «وهكذا سمعنا من يحكي هذه المقالة عن بعض الضالين المضلين»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r1] verses 18:65 | The author considers the account of an impossible deep-sea dive likely false and says extravagant sea stories may be told without limit. «وأظنك لا تشك بكذب هذا الخبر وإن قيل حدث عن البحر ولا حرج»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r10] verses 18:65 | Ibrāhīm al-Ḥarbī rejects claims of al-Khiḍr’s life as unsupported assertions about an absent person introduced by Satan; other authorities also report his death. «وسئل إبراهيم الحربي عن بقائه فقال : من أحال على غائب لم يتصف منه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r2] verses 18:65 | Sound Bukhārī reports say Moses and Joshua returned to the rock and found a man covered by a garment; Muslim says they found al-Khiḍr praying on a green mat at sea. «وصح من حديث البخاري وغيره أنهما رجعا إلى الصخرة وإذا رجل مسجي بثوب»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r3] verses 18:65 | The sound account has Moses greet al-Khiḍr, identify himself as Israel’s Moses, and be recognized; another version has al-Khiḍr greet him as prophet of Israel. «وصح أنهما لما انتهيا إليه سلم موسى فقال الخضر : وأنى بأرضك السلام»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r4] verses 18:65 | Al-Khiḍr asks whether the Torah suffices; Moses says revelation comes to him and God sent him to follow and learn from al-Khiḍr. «ثم قال : يا موسى أما يكفيك أن التوراة بيدك وأن»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r5] verses 18:65 | Tanwīn in عبدا magnifies the servant; annexing “our servants” honors him and marks special association with God. «والتنوين في ( عبدا ) للتفخيم والإضافة في ( عبادنا ) للتشريف»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r6] verses 18:65 | رحمة means, by views, lawful provision and comfort, seclusion and independence, or long healthy life; most say revelation and prophethood, which the author prefers. «قيل المراد بها الرزق الحلال والعيش الرغد وقيل العزلة عن الناس»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r7] verses 18:65 | Most say al-Khiḍr was a prophet but not messenger; others say messenger, while al-Qushayrī and others say saint. The author prefers prophethood. «فالجمهور على أنه عليه السلام نبي وليس برسول وقيل هو رسول»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r8] verses 18:65 | Al-Bukhārī rejects al-Khiḍr’s continued life by citing the Prophet’s statement that no one alive then would remain a hundred years later; Muslim transmits this from Jābir. «وسئل البخاري عنه وعن إلياس عليهما السلام هل هما حيان فقال : كيف يكون هذا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319#2/r9] verses 18:65 | Ibn Taymiyya argues that if al-Khiḍr were alive he would have joined the Prophet, fought and learned from him; he asks where al-Khiḍr was at Badr. «وسئل عنه شيخ الإسلام ابن تيمية فقال : لو كان الخضر حيا لوجب عليه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319/r1] verses 18:65 | Most identify the servant as al-Khiḍr; other views name al-Yasaʿ, Elias, or an angel, the last called false. Sound reports establish al-Khiḍr. «الجمهور على أنه الخضر بفتح الخاء وقد تكسر وكسر الضاد»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319/r2] verses 18:65 | A Bukhārī report says he was named Khiḍr because he sat on white fur that stirred and turned green; other reports explain the name by green surroundings or grass beneath him. «عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم لأنه جلس على فروة بيضاءفإذا هي تهتز»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319/r3] verses 18:65 | Another says the name reflects his distinction and beauty, but al-Nawawī prefers the first explanation. «والصواب كما قال النووي الأول»
[ALUSI-FULL:v15p319/r4] verses 18:65 | His kunya is Abū al-ʿAbbās; proposed names include Bilyā, Iblīyā, ʿĀmir, and Aḥmad, while Ibn Diḥya and Ibn al-Jawzī reject some identifications. «وكنيته أبو العباس واسمه بليا بموحدة مفتوحة»
