# Verses
- 3:7: هُوَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَنزَلَ عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ مِنْهُ ءَايَٰتٌۭ مُّحْكَمَٰتٌ هُنَّ أُمُّ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ وَأُخَرُ مُتَشَٰبِهَٰتٌۭ ۖ فَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ فِى قُلُوبِهِمْ زَيْغٌۭ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ مَا تَشَٰبَهَ مِنْهُ ٱبْتِغَآءَ ٱلْفِتْنَةِ وَٱبْتِغَآءَ تَأْوِيلِهِۦ ۗ وَمَا يَعْلَمُ تَأْوِيلَهُۥٓ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ ۗ وَٱلرَّٰسِخُونَ فِى ٱلْعِلْمِ يَقُولُونَ ءَامَنَّا بِهِۦ كُلٌّۭ مِّنْ عِندِ رَبِّنَا ۗ وَمَا يَذَّكَّرُ إِلَّآ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ

# Notes

## 18:65
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p175/r1] verses 18:60, 18:61, 18:62, 18:63, 18:64, 18:65 | Ibn ʿAbbās calls Nauf al-Bikālī an enemy of God and rejects his claim that Moses' companion of al-Khiḍr was not the Moses of the Israelites; the sound prophetic report identifies them as the same Moses. «قال ابن عباس: كذب عدو الله، حدثنا أبي بن كعب، رضي الله عنه، أنه سمع رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يقول»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p175/r2] verses 18:60, 18:61, 18:62, 18:63, 18:64, 18:65 | The Prophet relates that Moses claimed to be the most knowledgeable, was reproved for not referring knowledge to God, then learned of a more knowledgeable servant and received the fish sign for finding him. «إن موسى قام خطيبا في بني إسرائيل فسئل: أي الناس أعلم؟ قال: أنا»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p175/r5] verses 18:64, 18:65 | After retracing their path, Moses found al-Khiḍr covered in a garment; al-Khiḍr questioned his greeting, recognized Moses of Israel, and heard his request to be taught rightly guided knowledge. «فإذا رجل مسجى بثوب، فسلم عليه موسى، فقال الخضر: وأنى بأرضك السلام»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p175/r6] verses 18:65 | Al-Khiḍr explains that each has knowledge God taught him which the other lacks; Moses promises patience by God's will, and al-Khiḍr instructs him not to ask until given an explanation. «إني على علم من علم الله علمنيه، لا تعلمه أنت، وأنت على علم من علم الله علمكه الله لا أعلمه»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p176/r1] verses 18:60, 18:65 | People who recognized al-Khiḍr carried Moses and him on a ship without fare; al-Khiḍr removed a plank, and Moses objected that this could drown its passengers, then asked pardon for his forgetfulness. «فعرفوا الخضر، فحملوهم (2) بغير نول، فلما ركبا في السفينة»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p176/r2] verses 18:60, 18:65 | The Prophet identifies the ship incident as Moses' first lapse, due to forgetfulness; a bird's sip from the sea illustrates how little Moses' and al-Khiḍr's knowledge is beside God's. «كانت الأولى من موسى نسيانا»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p176/r3] verses 18:60, 18:65 | Al-Khiḍr kills a boy whom Moses protests was innocent; al-Khiḍr calls this harder to bear, and Moses promises to stop accompanying him if he asks again. «فأخذ الخضر رأسه [بيده] (7) فاقتلعه بيده فقتله»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p176/r4] verses 18:60, 18:65 | After the inhospitable village refuses them food or lodging, al-Khiḍr straightens a collapsing wall; Moses suggests taking payment, and al-Khiḍr declares their separation and promises an explanation. «فقال موسى: قوم أتيناهم فلم يطعمونا ولم يضيفونا، {لو شئت لاتخذت عليه أجرا»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p176/r5] verses 18:60, 18:65 | The Prophet wished Moses had remained patient so God could have told more of the two men's story. «وددنا أن موسى كان صبر حتى يقص الله علينا من خبرهما»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p176/r8] verses 18:61, 18:65 | A second Bukhārī version says a spring of life stood at the rock and revived the fish; it also expands the bird analogy to all creatures' knowledge compared with God's knowledge. «وفي أصل الصخرة عين يقال لها: الحياة، لا يصيب من مائها شيء إلا حيي»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p177/r5] verses 18:64, 18:65 | Khidr lay covered on a green mat by the sea; Moses greeted him and sought instruction, while Khidr noted their distinct knowledge and compared it to a bird's beakful from the sea beside God's knowledge. «قال سعيد بن جبير: مسجى بثوب، قد جعل طرفه تحت رجليه، وطرفه تحت رأسه، فسلم عليه موسى،»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p177/r6] verses 18:64, 18:65 | Khidr compares their knowledge with a bird's beakful from the sea beside God's knowledge and says Moses has knowledge Khidr should not learn, just as Khidr has knowledge Moses should not learn. «يا موسى، إن لي علما لا ينبغي لك أن تعلمه، وإن لك علما لا ينبغي لي أن أعلمه.»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p178/r3] verses 18:61, 18:64, 18:65 | Moses and his attendant retraced the fish's path to the rock, where they found a man wrapped in a cloak; he identified himself as the learned servant and welcomed Moses. «قال ابن عباس: فظهر موسى على الصخرة حتى إذا انتهيا إليها، فإذا رجل متلفف في كساء له،»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p178/r4] verses 18:65, 18:68 | Khidr explains that Moses knows the outward justice he sees but not the hidden knowledge disclosed to Khidr, so cannot endure the journey. «أي: إنما تعرف ظاهر ما ترى من العدل، ولم تحط من علم الغيب بما أعلم.»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p179/r3] verses 18:64, 18:65 | Moses greeted Khidr, who returned the greeting, wondered at its use in that land, and asked who Moses was before welcoming him. «فقال الخضر: وعليك السلام، وأنى يكون السلام بهذه (24) الأرض؟ ومن أنت؟ قال: أنا موسى.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p474/r5] verses 18:60, 18:65 | Moses’ educational journey illustrates divine wisdom more forcefully than abstract explanation; God sent him to a specially taught servant to learn submission to divine decisions. «The way these secrets of wisdom become evident through incidents is much more powerful than mere statements.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p474/r6] verses 18:60, 18:65 | A narrative says Moses claimed to be the most knowledgeable person, so God sent him to someone more knowledgeable as a reprimand. «our exegetes have relied on a strange narrative while explaining this incident. According to this narrative, once Moses (sws) audaciously claimed that at that time he was the most knowledgeable person»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p475/r1] verses 18:60, 18:65 | The claim that Moses’ statement was arrogant and earned reprimand is baseless: prophets declare knowledge from God, and this account’s real purpose is his education in divine wisdom. «arrogance or he be regarded worthy of reprimand for saying this. So why should Moses (sws) deserve this treatment? Nevertheless, in my opinion this occasion of revelation is totally baseless»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p478/r5] verses 18:65 | The Qur’an leaves the specially blessed, knowledgeable servant unnamed, describing only his attributes; some narratives identify him as Khidr. «The Qur’ān has not named this individual. It has only mentioned some particular attributes he possessed. In some narratives, he has been»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p479/r1] verses 18:65 | Because he sees no reason to reject the narratives, the author adopts the name Khidr for the unnamed servant. «Since I do not find any reason to reject these narratives, I shall adopt this name.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p479/r2] verses 18:65, 18:66 | The author considers Khidr a prophet: first, a messenger such as Moses would not appropriately be sent to a non-prophet for instruction. «a celebrated prophet in fact messenger as Moses (sws) was sent to him for instruction and to gain knowledge.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p479/r3] verses 18:65 | Khidr’s absence from the Qur’an and Torah by name is no objection, since neither scripture names every prophet; each prophet has a particular superiority, and Moses learning from Khidr does not establish Khidr’s absolute superiority. «has not mentioned many prophets. Same is the case with the Torah too.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p479/r4] verses 18:65 | A second reason Khidr was a prophet is that his Qur’anic attributes and his claim that his acts were not his own will indicate divine revelation and knowledge of God’s secrets. «All these aspects show that he was a prophet of God who received His revelations. He also had the distinction that God had revealed some of His secrets to him.»
[JISHUMI:v6p4455/r1] verses 18:60, 18:65 | One report says a man asked whether anyone was more learned than Moses; Moses said no, so God sent Gabriel to tell him of the learned servant. «لا، فعندها بعث الله جبريل، وأخبر بحديث هذا العالم.»
[JISHUMI:v6p4455/r2] verses 18:65 | Another report says Moses asked about God’s most beloved and learned servant; God described knowledge-seeking, then directed him to al-Khidr, with the fish as a sign. «وقيل: قال موسى: يا وب أي عبادك أحب إليك؟ قال: الذي يذكرني ولا ينساني، قال: فأي عبادك أعلم؟»
[JISHUMI:v6p4455/r4] verses 18:60, 18:65 | The episode is a great miracle for Moses. «وتدل على معجزة عظيمة لموسى - عليه السلام -.»
