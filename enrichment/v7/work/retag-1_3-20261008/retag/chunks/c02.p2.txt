[ALUSI-FULL:v1p46/r7] verses 1:1 | Razi's sixth argument says the basmala implies beginning with God's aid, reminds people that obedience depends on God's power, and makes audible recitation an act of commanding right. «متعلقة بفعل مضمر نحو بإعانة بسم الله أشرعوا ولا شك أن إستماع»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47#2/r1] verses 1:1 | Alusi argues that polytheists seek help from their gods as intermediaries, making the instrument analogy more apt for their practice than the accompaniment analogy. «فلأن المشركين إلى الإستعانة بآلهتهم أقرب إذ هم وسائطهم في التقرب إليه تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47#2/r2] verses 1:1 | An instrument must persist through every part of an action, but accompaniment need not; the report about God's naming better suits assistance because it teaches reliance on God's power. «فلأن الآلة لا بد من وجودها في كل جزء إلى آخر الفعل وإلا لم يتم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47#2/r3] verses 1:1 | Blessing does not uniquely follow from accompaniment: if inferred from context it can equally attend instrumental reliance, and Alusi denies that blessing is the inherent meaning of accompaniment. «فلأنه إن أراد أن معنى المصاحبة التبرك فظاهر البطلان»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47#2/r4] verses 1:1 | Alusi denies that God's name cannot be an instrument if the basmala is part of a sura; opening a work does not prevent a phrase from being its part. «فلأن ما يفتتح به الشيء لا مانع من كونه جزءا فالفاتحة مفتتح القرآن وجزؤه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47#2/r5] verses 1:1 | He reads the hadith about God's name preventing harm as invoking God's aid and protection; even alternative readings about accompanying benefit are only supporting indications, and many indications also favor assistance. «فلأن معنى الحديث أفعل كذا مستعينا بإسم الله الذي لا يضرني»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47#2/r6] verses 1:1 | Alusi speculates that al-Zamakhshari may avoid assistance because Mu'tazili doctrine gives human beings independent agency; he promises to address that theology later. «ويمكن على بعد أن يكون عدم إختيار الزمخشري لها لنزعات الشيطان الإعتزالية من إستقلال العبد بفعله»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47#2/r7] verses 1:1 | Ibn Jarir supplies the omitted verb as I recite, since what is recited is the object; Alusi says each speaker supplies the specific action they are beginning, a natural convention that permits basmala to begin the full act. «فذهب الإمام إبن جرير إلى تقديره أتلو لأن تاليه متلو»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47/r1] verses 1:1 | Alusi lists proposed meanings of ba in the basmala: seeking aid, accompaniment, attachment, superiority, redundancy, and oath; he rejects the last four. «فالباء إما للإستعانة أو المصاحبة أو الألصاق أو الأستعلاء أو زائدة أو قسمية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47/r2] verses 1:1 | Al-Baydawi's wording favors construing ba as seeking God's assistance, which makes His name seem essential to the act. «فالذي يشعر به كلام البيضاوي أرجحية الأول وأيد بأن جعله للإستعانة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47/r3] verses 1:1 | Al-Zamakhshari's wording favors accompaniment: this usage is more common, conveys blessing and reverence, rebuts idolaters' invocation, and implies God's name accompanies every part of the act. «وما يدل عليه كلام الزمخشري أرجحية الثاني وأيد بأن باء المصاحبة أكثر في الإستعمال»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47/r4] verses 1:1 | Al-Zamakhshari also adduces the report that God's naming is in every Muslim's heart whether or not it is spoken, and the hadith about God's name protecting from harm. «ويناسبه ما روى في الحديث تسمية الله تعالى في قلب كل مسلم يسمى أو لم يسم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p47/r5] verses 1:1 | Alusi prefers assistance as nearly necessary: it expresses humility and dependence, rejects independent human power, opens mercy, and fits the Fatiha's appeal for help and the command to read in God's name. «أن الإستعانة أولى بل يكاد أن تكون متعينة إذ فيها من الأدب والإستكانة»
