[ELMALILI:90:12-20/r2] verses 90:13 | “Neck” is a part-for-whole metaphor for a person, especially a slave; releasing a neck means freeing an enslaved person, with freeing a slave as the simplest example. «“rakabe” boyun demek olup zikr-i cüz irâde-i kül tarîkiyle mecaz olarak zat ve şahıs mânasında müte‘âreftir»
[ELMALILI:90:12-20/r3] verses 90:13 | A Prophetic report says whoever frees a believing slave has each of his own limbs freed from Hell in return for the corresponding limb, down to the private parts; it is transmitted in major hadith collections. «Buhârî, Müslim, Tirmizî ve sâirede Ebû Hüreyre radıyallahu anh’tan mervîdir»
[ELMALILI:90:12-20/r4] verses 90:13 | A Bedouin asked for a deed leading to Paradise; the Prophet distinguished freeing a person by oneself from helping to manumit a slave, saying the latter is broader and may be shared. «İmam Ahmed ve İbn Hibban ve İbn Merdûye ve Beyhakī, Berâ’ radıyallahu anh’tan rivayet eylemişlerdir»
[ELMALILI:90:12-20/r5] verses 90:13 | If freeing one person is such a virtue, freeing an entire people or community is an even greater act of courage. «Bir ferdi hürriyete kavuşturmak böyle büyük bir fazilet ve kahramanlık olunca bir kavmin, bir cemaatin hürriyetini kurtarmak»
[ELMALILI:90:12-20/r6] verses 90:13 | A second meaning is rescuing oneself from Hell through righteous deeds, since each person is held by what he has earned; this requires struggle against the commanding soul and Satan. «her nefsin halâsı kazancına bağlı olduğu için insanın a‘mâl-i sâliha kesbiyle kendisini cehennem azâbından kurtarması demektir»
[ELMALILI:90:12-20/r7] verses 90:13 | The failure of the proud person to overcome the ascent reflects his failure to struggle against his lower desires and Satan’s temptations. «nefs-i emmârenin azgın hevâlarıyla Şeytan’ın iğvâlarına karşı mücahede etmekle olur»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p413/r1] verses 90:13, 90:14 | These readers use the verbal forms فك رقبة or أطعم, with رقبة in the accusative; Abū ʿAmr's transmitters report minor differences over the two forms and their attachment. «قرأ ابن كثير وأبو عمرو والكسائي: فك رقبة أو أطعم»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p413/r2] verses 90:13, 90:14 | These readers use the verbal noun إطعام alongside فك رقبة. «وقرأ عاصم وابن عامر ونافع وحمزة: فك رقبة أو إطعام»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p413/r5] verses 90:11, 90:13, 90:14 | Abū ʿUbaydah says the person failed to cross the path in worldly life, then takes freeing a captive or feeding as its explanation. «أبو عبيدة: فلا اقتحم العقبة: فلم يقتحم العقبة في الدنيا»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p413/r6] verses 90:11, 90:13, 90:14 | With the verbal-noun reading, an omitted اقتحام is required: otherwise the sentence equates the path itself with the event of freeing a captive, unlike the intended explanatory relation. «لا بد من تقدير هذا المحذوف لأنه لا يخلو من أن تقدر»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v6p414/r4] verses 90:11, 90:13, 90:14 | The verbal reading may explain the challenge through its actions; an explanatory clause need not copy the nominal-predicate construction in other examples, and meaning can link the clauses. «ومن قال: فك رقبة أو أطعم في يوم ذي مسغبة [البلد/ 13، 14]، فإنه يجوز»
[FARRA:v3p264/r1] verses 90:11, 90:13, 90:14, 90:17 | Arabic usually repeats lā across coordinated statements; here one lā covers the three parts of the steep path: freeing a slave or feeding during famine, then believing. «ولم يضم إلى قوله: [فلا أقتحم] كلام آخر فيه (لا) لأن العرب لا تكاد تفرد (لا) في الكلام»
[FARRA:v3p264/r2] verses 90:13, 90:14 | The common reading coordinates the verbal noun “freeing a slave” with “feeding.” «وقد قرأ العوام: «فك رقبة (13) أو إطعام » (14)»
[FARRA:v3p264/r3] verses 90:13, 90:14 | Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib read the nouns as a coordinated pair, فك رقبة أو إطعام. «وقرأ الحسن البصري: «فك رقبة» وكذلك علي بن أبي طالب»
[FARRA:v3p265/r1] verses 90:13, 90:14 | The reading فك رقبة أو إطعام best fits Arabic because إطعام is a noun and should coordinate with another noun; ثم كان also supports this parse. ««فك رقبة أو إطعام » وهو أشبه الوجهين بصحيح العربية لأن الإطعام: اسم، وينبغي أن يرد على الاسم اسم مثله،»
[FARRA:v3p265/r2] verses 90:13, 90:14 | The alternative reading is also possible if an omitted أن is supplied; a poem with أن in the second clause shows that it can be understood in the first. «والوجه الآخر جائز تضمر فيه (أن) ، وتلقى [138/ ب] فيكون مثل قول الشاعر :»
[FARRA:v3p265/r3] verses 90:13, 90:14 | An unnamed poet uses overt أن in the second coordinated clause, illustrating the elided أن in the earlier clause. «ألا أيها ذا الزاجري أحضر الوغى ... وأن أشهد اللذات هل أنت مخلدي»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p355/r4] verses 90:6, 90:11, 90:12, 90:13, 90:14 | Alternatively, the phrase is a rebuking question with its interrogative particle omitted: the boaster wasted wealth on gambling and wine, so why did he not spend generously on liberation and feeding during famine? «وتكون ( لا اقتحم العقبة ) استفهاما حذف منه أداته . وهو استفهام إنكار ، والمعنى : أنه يدعي إهلاك مال كثير»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p355/r5] verses 90:11, 90:13, 90:14 | The single لا can negate the past without repetition because اقتحم entails the later acts of freeing and feeding, or because the later faith clause supplies the second negation; anything that prevents ambiguity can serve similarly, as an example from al-Ḥarīrī shows. «وقد جاءت هنا نافية في غير دعاء ، ولم تتكرر استغناء عن تكريرها بكون ما بعدها وهو { اقتحم العقبة } يتضمن شيئين»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p357/r3] verses 90:13, 90:14 | These readers pronounce فك and إطعام in the nominative; the clause explains the العقبة as freeing a person or feeding, with an omitted predicate, and the hard deed metaphor needs no added implied noun. «وقرأ نافع وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة وأبو جعفر ويعقوب وخلف ، { فك رقبة } برفع { فك } وإضافته إلى { رقبة }»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p357/r4] verses 90:13, 90:14 | They read فك as an active past verb with رقبة as its object and اطعم as another past verb; this explains the preceding negation or substitutes for اقتحم العقبة. «وقرأه ابن كثير وأبو عمرو والكسائي { فك } بفتح الكاف على صيغة فعل المضي ، وبنصب { رقبة }»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p357/r5] verses 90:13 | فك is taking something from its possessor; رقبة uses the part for the whole person, especially a captive or slave whose neck is the first thing noticed because it is commonly shackled. «من إطلاق اسم الجزء على كله مثل إطلاق رأس وعينن ووجه ، وإيثار لفظ الرقبة هنا لأن المراد ذات الأسير أو العبد»
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p358/r1] verses 90:13 | Freeing is an image for releasing a person from captivity or ownership, like pulling a difficult matter from a resistant holder's grasp. «وأطلق الفك على تخليص المأخوذ في أسر أو ملك ، لمشابهة تخليص الأمر العسير بالنزع من يد القابض الممتنع .»
