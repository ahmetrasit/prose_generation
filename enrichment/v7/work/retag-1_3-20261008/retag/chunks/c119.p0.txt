# Notes

## 39:6
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r7] verses 39:6 | The passage contains three signs: Adam's creation without parents, Eve's creation from Adam's rib, and the countless number of Adam's descendants. «وقد تضمنت الآية ثلاث آيات خلق آدم عليه السلام بلا أب وأم وخلق حواء من قصيراه»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r8] verses 39:6 | The eight livestock pairs offer another proof from the lower world; “sent down” can figuratively mean God's decree and apportionment, fixed in the Preserved Tablet and conveyed by appointed angels, with the relation of descent to emergence after concealment. «استدلال بنوع آخر من العالم السفلي والإنزال مجاز عن القضاء والقسمة فإنه تعالى إذا قضى وقسم أثبت ذلك في اللوح المحفوظ»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p240/r9] verses 39:6 | Another figurative reading assigns the descent to the livestock while the actual things sent down are causes of their life, such as rain; the relation is association between them. «ويجوز أن يكون التجوز في نسبة الإنزال إلى الأنعام والمنزل حقيقة أسباب حياتها كالأمطار»
[ALUSI-FULL:v23p241/r1] verses 39:6 | The concluding question follows from the divine attributes just described: with abundant reasons to worship God, no reason to turn away, and many deterrents to worshiping another, how can people be diverted? «أي فكيف تصوفون عن عبادته تعالى مع وفور موجباتها ودواعيها وانتفاء الصارف عنها بالكلية»
[ALUSI-FULL:v24p37#2/r5] verses 39:6 | The three darknesses symbolize possibility, prime matter, and form. «قيل : يشير إلى ظلمة الإمكان وظلمة الهيولي وظلمة الصورة»
[ASAD-NOTES:39:6#n10/r1] verses 39:6 | The closing question literally asks how people are turned away, meaning turned away from the truth. «Lit., "how, then, are you turned away?" - i.e., from the truth.»
[ASAD-NOTES:39:6#n7/r1] verses 39:6 | Asad directs readers to 4:1 and its note for the parallel discussion of the first human pair. «See 4:1 and the corresponding note 1.»
[ASAD-NOTES:39:6#n8/r1] verses 39:6 | The eight pairs are male and female animals from four kinds: sheep, goats, camels, and bovines. «the male and the female of four kinds of cattle (sheep, goats, camels and bovine cattle)»
[ASAD-NOTES:39:6#n8/r2] verses 39:6 | Unlike the same cattle in 6:143–144, where they figure in baseless pre-Islamic taboos, here they are God's lawful gift. «meaningless, superstitious taboos of pre-Islamic times»
[ASAD-NOTES:39:6#n8/r3] verses 39:6 | Their mention reminds people that God supplies their sustenance and that they depend entirely on Him. «God who provides his sustenance and that, therefore, man is utterly dependent on Him»
[ASAD-NOTES:39:6#n9/r1] verses 39:6 | The three darknesses denote the womb, the membrane around the embryo, and prenatal blindness; embryonic stages are repeatedly mentioned in the Qur'an. «the darkness of the womb, the membrane enveloping the embryo, and its pre-natal blindness»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p108#2/r5] verses 39:6 | The single soul is Adam and his mate is Eve. «يعني: آدم، {ثم جعل منها زوجها} يعني حواء»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p108#2/r6] verses 39:6 | “Sending down” livestock means creating them; alternatives connect it to water producing cotton and fodder or making livestock provisions. «معنى الإنزال هاهنا: الإحداث والإنشاء، كقوله تعالى»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p108#2/r7] verses 39:6 | The eight pairs are species, explained in al-Anʿām. «ثمانية أزواج} أصناف، تفسيرها في سورة الأنعام»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p108#2/r8] verses 39:6 | Womb creation proceeds from drop to clot to lump of flesh, as in Noah 14. «نطفة ثم علقة ثم مضغة»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v7p108#2/r9] verses 39:6 | The three darknesses are the abdomen, womb, and placenta. «ظلمة البطن، وظلمة الرحم، وظلمة المشيمة»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r3] verses 39:6 | Human creation in the lower world is another, nearer and more remarkable proof: Adam without parents, Eve from his rib, then innumerable descendants from both. «استدلال آخر بما أوجده في العالم السفلي مبدوء به من خلق الإنسان»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r4] verses 39:6 | ثم may attach to an omitted description of نفس, to the sense of وحدة, or to خلقكم, marking the contrast between ordinary reproduction and the exceptional creation of Eve. «{ ثم } للعطف على محذوف هو صفة { نفس } مثل خلقها أو على معنى واحدة»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r5] verses 39:6 | One account says God drew Adam’s descendants from his back like particles, then created Eve from them. «وقيل أخرج من ظهره ذريته كالذر ثم خلق منها حواء»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r6] verses 39:6 | أنزل لكم means either decreed or apportioned livestock, with divine decisions written in heaven, or brought them about through descending causes such as starlight and rain. «{ وأنزل لكم } وقضى أو قسم لكم، فإن قضاياه وقسمه توصف بالنزول من السماء»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r7] verses 39:6 | The eight livestock pairs are male and female camels, cattle, sheep, and goats. «{ من الأنعم ثمنية أزوج } ذكر وأنثى من الإبل والبقر والضأن والمعز»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r8] verses 39:6 | The stages in wombs display extraordinary power: a formed animal follows flesh-covered bones, bare bones, morsels, clots, and drops; the three darknesses are abdomen, womb, and placenta, or loins, womb, and abdomen. «{ خلقا من بعد خلق } حيوانا سويا من بعد عظام مكسوة لحما»
[BAYDAWI-FULL:39:5-6/r9] verses 39:6 | Addressing humans either favors rational beings or singles out the intended audience; God alone owns creation and therefore alone deserves worship, so turning to idolatry is inexplicable. «إذ لا يشاركه في الخلق غيره»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p420/r7] verses 39:6 | The creation of animals and especially humans, with speech and inescapable bodily conditions, is a further sign of divine oneness and power; humanity comes from Adam, a single soul. «{ من نفس واحدة } هي آدم عليه السلام»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r1] verses 39:6 | One possible reading takes Eve's creation from Adam's side after his division; ثم marks delay in time and rank, and جعل identifies the male as her cause and material, excluding partnership. «ولما كان إيجادنا منها بعد شق الأنثى منها , قال عاطفا على ما تقديره : أوجدها من تراب»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r10] verses 39:6 | The demonstrative points to all the acts mentioned from the surah's beginning and testifies to God through every creature's speech or state; Lord means owner and nurturer through creation and provision. «الذي جميع ما ذكر من أول السورة إلى هنا أفعاله»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r11] verses 39:6 | Since a nurturer need not be a sovereign, the argument concludes that God alone is deity and uniquely one. «ولما كان المربي قد لا يكون ملكا قال نتيجة إلها , قال مثبتا له الإلهية على ما يقتضيه من الوحدانية»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r12] verses 39:6 | The final question challenges how people are diverted from exclusive devotion when all creatures are under God's command and power. «ولما تكفل هذا السياق بوجوب الإخلاص في الإقبال عليه والإعراض عما سواه , لأن الكل تحت قهره»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r13] verses 39:6 | The passive form suggests people are overpowered in their conduct because they follow ruin and abandon unmistakable proofs; sound nature and reason originally prepare them for guidance. «ونبه بالبناء للمفعول مع هذا على أنهم مقهورون في فعل ما هم عليه لأنهم تابعون للهلاك المحض»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r2] verses 39:6 | In this reading, Adam was created as the source of the descendants through whom God's perfect attributes would be manifested. «أنه ما خلق آدم عليه السلام إلا ليكون سببا لما يحدث عنه من الذرية»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r3] verses 39:6 | The preferable reading is that God determined all descendants' numbers, colors, and forms when creating Adam, including Eve's descent from him; thus all ultimately originate from Adam. «ويجوز - وهو أحسن - أن يكون المعنى لأن السياق لإحاطة العلم المدلول عليه بإنزال الكتاب»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r4] verses 39:6 | A report from Abu al-Darda says God extracted Adam's descendants from his right shoulder as tiny white ants and from his left as black as charcoal, assigning the right-hand group to Paradise and the left-hand group to Hell. «في مسند أحمد بن منيع عن أبي الدرداء رضي الله عنه عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r5] verses 39:6 | The livestock are described as sent down because their strength surpasses human strength, so their creation and domestication display divine power and human subjugation of them. «وكان سبحانه موصوفا بالعلو , وكان أكثر الأنعام أشد الإنسان , فكان تسخيره له وتذليله إنزالا عن قوته»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r6] verses 39:6 | The eight pairs are camels, cattle, sheep, and goats, each male and female; animals familiar to Arabs are listed, while Bactrian camels and buffaloes are omitted. «ولما لم يكن عند العرب البخاتي والجواميس لم يذكرها سبحانه , واقتصر على ما عندهم»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r7] verses 39:6 | A pair is one member with another needed for full benefit; unlike humans whose mate comes from the first soul, male and female livestock were each created independently, displaying God's freedom of action. «وإذا نظرت هذه العبارة مع العبارة عن خلق الإنسان فهمت أن الأنعام خلق كل ذكرها وأناثها على انفراده , لا أن أحدا منها من صاحبه»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r8] verses 39:6 | Creation in the womb unfolds in stages from mixed elements; the three darknesses can be sperm, clot, and lump, with sex becoming known when bones are clothed with flesh. «ظلمة النطفة ثم العلقة ثم المضغة , فإذا صار عظاما مكسوة لحما عرف هل هو ذكر أو أنثى»
[BIQAI-FULL:v6p421/r9] verses 39:6 | An alternate gloss takes the three darknesses as the abdomen, womb, and placenta; Ibn Abbas transmits this view, and Ibn Abi al-Dunya's Book of Contentment attributes it to Jesus. «وقيل ؛ ظلمة البطن والرحم والمشيمة - نقل ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما وعزاه ابن أبي الدنيا في كتاب القناعة إلى عيسى ابن مريم عليه السلام .»
