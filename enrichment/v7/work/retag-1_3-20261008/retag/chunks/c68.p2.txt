[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r13] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Ibn ʿAtiyya attributes the explanation to ʿAli rather than al-Mubarrad and compares it to an angry speaker who begins “as for what follows” without praise or honorific opening. «ويعزى هذا القول للمبرد وهو لعلي بن أبي طالب رضي الله عنه»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r14] verses 9:128, 9:129 | When the codex scribes differed over whether al-Anfal and al-Tawba were one surah or two, they left a separation but omitted the basmala to accommodate both positions. «فتركوا فصلا بينهما مراعاة لقول من قال هما سورتان ولم يكتبوا بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم مراعاة لقول من قال منهم هما واحدة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r15] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Ibn ʿAtiyya considers that account weak because it would mean the Qurʾan’s arrangement was left disputed in this way. «وهذا القول يضعفه النظر أن يختلف في كتاب الله هكذا»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r16] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Ubayy ibn Kaʿb says the Prophet ordered them to put the basmala at the start of each surah but gave no instruction for this one, so they did not place it. «وروي عن أبي بن كعب أنه قال: كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يأمرنا بوضع بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم في أول كل سورة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r17] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Malik says it reached him that the surah was once as long as al-Baqara, then much of it was abrogated and lifted, including the basmala; they therefore avoided putting the basmala in a place where it did not belong. «وروي عن مالك أنه قال: بلغنا أنها كانت نحو سورة البقرة ثم نسخ ورفع كثير منها وفيه البسملة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r18] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Al-Tawba was among the last revelations; an anecdote has a Bedouin infer this from its references to omissions and repudiated treaties. «وسورة براءة من آخر ما نزل على النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، وحكى عمران بن جدير أن أعرابيا سمع سورة براءة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r2] verses 9:128, 9:129 | The surah is called al-Tawba; the text associates this title with Hudhayfa and others. «فاله حذيفة وغيره، وتسمى الفاضحة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r3] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Ibn ʿAbbas reports that it is also called al-Faḍiḥa, “the exposer.” «وتسمى الفاضحة قاله ابن عباس»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r4] verses 9:128, 9:129 | It is also called al-Ḥāfira because it dug into and exposed the hypocrites’ hearts. «وتسمى الحافرة لأنها حفرت عن قلوب المنافقين»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r5] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Ibn ʿAbbas says the repeated revelations identifying people as “among them” continued until he thought no one would remain unexposed. «قال ابن عباس مازال ينزل ومنهم ومنهم حتى ظن أنه لا يبقى أحد»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r6] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Hudhayfa calls it the surah of punishment. «وقال حذيفة: هي سورة العذاب»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r7] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Ibn ʿUmar says they called it al-Mufashfisha. «قال ابن عمر كنا ندعوها المفشفشة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p3/r8] verses 9:128, 9:129 | Al-Harith ibn Yazid says it was called al-Mubaththira; other names given are al-Muthira and al-Buhuth. «قال الحارث بن يزيد: كانت تدعى المبعثرة ويقال لها المثيرة، ويقال لها البحوث»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p99#2/r10] verses 9:128 | ʿAbd Allah ibn Qusayt al-Makki reads “min anfusikum” with fāʾ fatḥa, making it mean “from among your most precious”; the reading is also transmitted from the Prophet and Fatima, with Ibn ʿAbbas reporting it from the Prophet according to Abu ʿAmr. «وقرأ عبد الله بن قسيط المكي «من أنفسكم» بفتح الفاء من النفاسة، ورويت عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم وعن فاطمة رضي الله عنها»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p99#2/r11] verses 9:128 | In “what has burdened you is grievous to him,” mā can be a verbal noun as the initial subject with ʿazīz as fronted predicate; alternatively it can be the subject of ʿazīz, which is an adjective for the Messenger. Ibn ʿAtiyya prefers the latter syntax. «ما عنتم فاعلا ب عزيز وعزيز صفة للرسول، وهذا أصوب من الأول»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p99#2/r12] verses 9:128 | ʿAnat means hardship and broadly includes what burdens people in disbelief and misguidance, as well as being killed, captured, or tested for the truth and their belief. «والعنت المشقة وهي هنا لفظة عامة أي ما شق عليكم من كفر وضلال بحسب الحق ومن قتل أو أسار وامتحان بسبب الحق»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p99#2/r13] verses 9:128 | Qatada takes the phrase specifically to mean hardship borne by the believers among the audience. «وقال قتادة: المعنى عنت مؤمنيكم»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p99#2/r6] verses 9:128 | The majority take the address as directed to the Arabs, enumerating God’s favor in sending them a messenger who spoke their language and conveyed eloquent meanings they understood, giving them enduring honor. «مخاطبة للعرب في قول الجمهور وهذا على جهة تعديد النعمة عليهم في ذلك، إذ جاء بلسانهم وبما يفهمونه من الأغراض والفصاحة»
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v3p99#2/r7] verses 9:128 | Al-Zajjaj takes the address as directed to all humanity, with the sense that a human messenger came to them; Ibn ʿAtiyya prefers the majority’s reading. «وقال الزجاج: هي مخاطبة لجميع العالم، والمعنى لقد جاءكم رسول من البشر والأول أصوب»
