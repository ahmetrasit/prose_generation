# Notes

## 9:128
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p105/r2] verses 9:128 | Al-Suddi says the phrase means he was from al-Muḥarrab of the descendants of Ishmael. «وقال السدي: من المحرب من بني إسماعيل»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p105/r3] verses 9:128 | Al-Zajjāj says the Messenger is a human like them, which makes the proof stronger because people understand someone like themselves; the author treats this at 3:164. «قال الزجاج: أي: هو بشر مثلكم فهو أوكد للحجة عليكم»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p105/r4] verses 9:128 | The Messenger finds their hardship and every harm that reaches them painful. «قال ابن عباس: يريد: يعز عليه مشقتكم وكل مضرة تصيبكم»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p105/r5] verses 9:128 | “Dear to him” means their hardship is severe for him because it is beyond his ability to remove; what burdens them is harm, and ʿanata describes severe distress with no apparent way out. «وقال أهل المعاني: معنى: {عزيز عليه} شديد عليه بامتناعه من إمكان زواله»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p105/r6] verses 9:128 | Al-Zajjāj paraphrases the phrase as “your hardship is severe for him,” meaning that you encounter difficulty and distress. «وقال الزجاج: معناه: عزيز عليه عنتكم، وهو لقاء الشدة والمشقة»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p105/r7] verses 9:128 | Al-Farrāʾ parses mā as nominative, meaning “your hardship is severe for him.” «وقال الفراء: (ما) في موضع رفع، معناه: عزيز عليه عنتكم»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p106/r1] verses 9:128 | Abū Isḥāq and al-Kalbī understand him to be keen for your faith, with an omitted genitive complement. «قال أبو إسحاق: أي: حريص على إيمانكم»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p106/r2] verses 9:128 | Al-Ḥasan takes the phrase to mean that the Messenger is eager for you to believe. «وقال الحسن: حريص عليكم أن تؤمنوا»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p106/r3] verses 9:128 | Al-Farrāʾ explains ḥarīṣ as guarding you from entering the Fire: zeal for something means guarding it lest it be lost or destroyed. «وقال الفراء: الحريص الشحيح بأن تدخلوا النار»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v11p106/r4] verses 9:128 | In a report from ʿAṭāʾ through Ibn ʿAbbās, God names the Messenger with two of His own names, raʾūf and raḥīm. «قال عطاء، عن ابن عباس: سماه الله تعالى باسمين من أسمائه»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:9:128-129/r1] verses 9:128 | The messenger is from the Arabs, descendants of Ishmael, so his people can understand him. «من العرب من بني إسماعيل ليفهموا منه»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:9:128-129/r2] verses 9:128 | The people's hardship and every harm they suffer grieve him, and he is eager for them to believe; this addresses disbelievers who did not believe him. «شديد عليه مشقتكم وكل مضرة تصيبكم»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:9:128-129/r3] verses 9:128 | Toward believers, the messenger is compassionate and merciful. «ثم ذكر أنه { بالمؤمنين رؤوف رحيم }»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p476#2/r5] verses 9:128 | The Messenger is a human like them, strengthening the proof because they can understand someone like themselves. «أي هو بشر مثلكم. أي فهو أوكد للحجة عليكم»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p476#2/r6] verses 9:128 | Alternatively, the phrase means he is an Arab like them, so they know his manner and can understand him. «وجائز أن يكون عنى به إنه عربي كما أنكم عرب»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p476#2/r7] verses 9:128 | It is hard for him when they suffer; ʿanat means encountering hardship. «أي عزيز عليه عنتكم، والعنت لقاء الشدة»
[ZAJJAJ:v2p476#2/r8] verses 9:128 | He is eager for their faith. «أي حريص على إيمانكم»

## 17:110
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p391#3/r6] verses 17:110 | Some Medinan authorities say the instruction not to recite aloud was abrogated by the command to remember God inwardly. «سماك بن الفضل عن بعض أهل المدينة في قوله تعالى ﴿ ولا تجهر بصلاتك ﴾ قال هو منسوخة»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p392#2/r1] verses 17:110 | Qatāda says the Prophet used to raise his voice in prayer and be pelted with filth, so he was told to avoid both harmful loudness and whispering and take a middle course. «عن قتادة في قوله ﴿ ولا تجهر بصلاتك ولا تخافت بها ﴾ قال كان النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم يرفع صوته في الصلاة فيرمى بالخبث»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p392#2/r2] verses 17:110 | ʿUrwa says the instruction concerns supplication. «عن هشام بن عروة عن أبيه في قوله ﴿ ولا تجهر بصلاتك ولا تخافت بها ﴾ قال في الدعاء»
[ABDURRAZZAQ:v2p392#2/r3] verses 17:110 | Al-Ḥasan warns against making one's public worship look good while one's inward state is bad. «قال معمر وكان الحسن يقول لا تحسن علانيتها وتسيء سريرتها»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r1] verses 17:110 | The reported occasions of revelation suggest الدعاء here is vocative invocation; Allah and al-Rahman are two names of one referent, one His name and the other His attribute. «والمعنى: إن دعوتم الله فهو اسمه وإن دعوتم الرحمن فهو صفته»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r10] verses 17:110 | Another Ibn Abbas view takes صلاة as Quran recitation in ritual prayer, with an understood noun because loudness and whispering concern voice. «وعن ابن عباس أيضا: هي قراءة القرآن في الصلاة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r2] verses 17:110 | He takes دعاء as naming rather than calling upon, a verb that can take two objects with one omitted when understood. «والدعاء بمعنى التسمية لا بمعنى النداء وهو يتعدى إلى مفعولين»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r3] verses 17:110 | دعوت may take a direct object and a second complement with باء, which can be omitted; a cited line from Amr's mother illustrates this usage. «ودعوت هذه من الأفعال التي تتعدى إلى اثنين ثانيهما بحرف جر»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r4] verses 17:110 | They treat Allah and al-Rahman as names of the same deity, so either name may be used in invocation. «المراد بهما اسم المسمى وأو للتخيير»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r5] verses 17:110 | أيما is conditional; its tanwin may replace an omitted genitive, while ما may instead be emphatic or itself conditional. «وأي هنا شرطية. والتنوين قيل عوض من المضاف وما زائدة مؤكدة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r6] verses 17:110 | He reads أيا من تدعو; من may be redundant by al-Kisa'i's analysis or an anomalous pairing of two conditional particles. «وقرأ طلحة بن مصرف. أيا من تدعوا»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r7] verses 17:110 | The pronoun in فله refers to the single referent behind both names, who possesses the most beautiful names. «والضمير في فله عائد على مسمى الاسمين وهو واحد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r8] verses 17:110 | A proposed pause after أي treats ما as a new universal conditional, but the author rejects it because ما does not refer to rational individuals and the conditional scope fails. «وهذا لا يصح لأن ما لا تطلق على آحاد أولي العلم»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126#2/r9] verses 17:110 | They interpret صلاة in the command about voice as supplication. «والصلاة هنا الدعاء قاله ابن عباس وعائشة وجماعة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126/r10] verses 17:110 | After being unable to answer the Quran's challenge, opponents accused the Messenger of doing what he forbade by invoking two gods; God answers that charge. «وكان عليه الصلاة والسلام قد جاءهم بتوحيد الله والرفض لآلهتهم عدلوا إلى رميه»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126/r5] verses 17:110 | Ibn Abbas reports that the Prophet prayed at night in Mecca, invoking al-Rahman and al-Rahim in prostration; pagans alleged he worshipped two gods, so the verse was revealed. «قال ابن عباس: تهجد الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم ذات ليلة بمكة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126/r6] verses 17:110 | Ibn Atiyya transmits a similar account from Makhul about the pagans hearing the Prophet invoke al-Rahman. «ونقل ابن عطية نحوا منه عن مكحول»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126/r7] verses 17:110 | A second Ibn Abbas account says pagans heard يا الله يا رحمن and again thought he invoked two gods. «وقال عن ابن عباس: سمعه المشركون يدعو يا الله يا رحمن»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126/r8] verses 17:110 | Maimun says the Prophet used باسمك اللهم until revelation of Solomon's letter, then wrote the basmala; Arabs knew al-Rahim but not al-Rahman, prompting this verse. «وقال ميمون بن مهران: كان عليه السلام يكتب: باسمك اللهم حتى نزلت إنه من سليمان»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p126/r9] verses 17:110 | Al-Dahhak says People of the Book objected that al-Rahman was common in Torah but rare in the Prophet's speech; the verse answered their denial after they failed to match the Quran. «وقال الضحاك: قال أهل الكتاب للرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم: إنك لتقل ذكر الرحمن»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p127/r1] verses 17:110 | The Prophet raised his Quran recitation and pagans insulted and disrupted it, so he was told to lower it enough not to be heard by them but loud enough for believers behind him. «وكان عليه الصلاة والسلام يرفع صوته بقراءته فيسب المشركون ويلغون»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p127/r2] verses 17:110 | The middle way means a moderate volume between loudness and whispering. «وابتغ بين ذلك أي بين الجهر والمخافتة سبيلا وسطا»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p127/r3] verses 17:110 | They give the expression a further moral sense: do not make the outward practice good while making its private side bad. «وقال ابن عباس أيضا والحسن: لا تحسن علانيتها وتسيء سريتها»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p127/r4] verses 17:110 | Aisha says صلاة here means the tashahhud. «وعن عائشة: الصلاة يراد بها هنا التشهد»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p127/r5] verses 17:110 | Ibn Sirin says the verse concerned Bedouins who said the tashahhud aloud; Abu Bakr whispered to commune with God, while Umar recited aloud to repel Satan and wake sleepers, then each was told to adjust his volume. «وقال ابن سيرين: كان الأعراب يجهرون بتشهدهم فنزلت الآية في ذلك»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p127/r6] verses 17:110 | Another Ibn Abbas gloss says not to recite loudly in daytime prayer or whisper in night prayer. «وعن ابن عباس أيضا: المعنى ولا تجهر بصلاة النهار ولا تخافت بصلاة الليل»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p127/r7] verses 17:110 | Ibn Zayd relates the verse to Torah and Gospel practice of sometimes raising the leader's voice and sometimes lowering it so followers become silent. «وقال ابن زيد: معنى الآية على ما يفعله أهل الإنجيل والتوراة»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v7p95/r23] verses 17:110 | خافت بالكلام means to whisper so softly that the speaker can barely hear
