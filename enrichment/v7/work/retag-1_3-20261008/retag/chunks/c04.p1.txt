[ALUSI-FULL:v1p65/r5] verses 1:1 | The Prophet said that an important affair begun without the basmala is cut off; al-Ālūsī cites Abū Hurayra’s report as transmitted by ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Rahāwī and gives no grading. «كل أمر ذي بال لا يبدأ فيه ببسم الله فهو أبتر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p65/r6] verses 1:1 | Bal means a state or affair of consequence that occupies the heart; another explanation likens a great matter to something possessing a heart through implied metaphor. «والبال الحال والشأن فمعنى ذي بال شريف يحتفل به ويهتم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p65/r7] verses 1:1 | One explanation says the wording honors God by beginning significant affairs with His name while easing the obligation for trivial affairs; al-Ālūsī prefers applying it to all affairs, great or small. «وفي هذا الوصف فائدتان إحداهما رعاية تعظيم أسم الله تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r1] verses 1:1 | Al-Ālūsī reads the hadith universally: every matter that comes to mind, important or trivial, should begin with the basmala, as the verse says every bird flies with its wings. «وعندي أن الأظهر جعل الوصف للتعميم كما في قوله تعالى : ( ولا طائر يطير بجناحيه )»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r2] verses 1:1 | The universal reading magnifies God and teaches disavowal of independent power: carrying a straw is like carrying a mountain unless God helps. «وحث على التبري عن الحول والقوة إلا بالله»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r3] verses 1:1 | God commands abundant remembrance, and the Prophet taught asking God for every need, down to a sandal strap; a report says God told Moses to ask even for cooking salt and sandal straps. «ليسأل أحدكم ربه حاجته كلها حتى شثع نعله»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r4] verses 1:1 | Asking for small things does not violate reverence: the Qur’an finds no disparity in creation, and a discerning believer sees no created thing as insignificant. «ما ترى في خلق الرحمن من تفاوت فأرجع البصر هل ترى من فطور»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r5] verses 1:1 | Invoking God’s name over forbidden acts is itself forbidden but, on the sound view, not unbelief; over disliked acts it is disliked, while intending contempt for God makes it unbelief. «التسمية على الحرام والمكروه مما لا ينبغي بل هي حرام في الحرام لا كفر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r6] verses 1:1 | Reports vary between beginning with praise, the basmala, or remembrance, and between descriptions such as cut off or amputated; the hadith is textually and transmission-wise unsettled, but leniency applies in merits and semantic corroboration strengthens it. «وقد أضطرب الحديث هنا فوقع في بعض الروايات لا يبدأ فيه بالحمد لله»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r7] verses 1:1 | His preferred reconciliation treats the basmala and praise versions as canceling each other’s specific qualifiers, leaving the broader command to begin with remembrance; another view allows either phrase or another remembrance. «وأستحسن فيه أن روايتي البسملة والحمد له تعارضتا فسقط قيداهما»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r8] verses 1:1 | The hadith concerns matters not recognized as complete in religious law: they may be materially finished yet deficient in meaning, since the basmala completes their meanings. «المراد من الحديث أن لا يكون معتبرا في الشرع فهو غير تام معنى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v1p66/r9] verses 1:1 | The basmala’s light is portrayed mystically as restoring sight and hearing, protecting the poisoned, and healing a demon-afflicted person when its letters are written as an incantation. «ومن مشكاة بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم تشرق على صفحات الأكوان أنوار البهاء»
[ASAD-NOTES:1:1#n1/r1] verses 1:1 | Most authorities count the opening invocation as verse 1 of Al-Fatihah; in other surahs it precedes the text without being counted. «According to most of the authorities, this invocation (which occurs at the beginning of every surah»
[ASAD-NOTES:1:1#n1/r2] verses 1:1 | Ibn al-Qayyim distinguishes rahman as God's inherent, inseparable abounding grace and rahim as that grace manifested in creation. «the one advanced by Ibn al-Qayyim (as quoted in Manar I,48)»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r1] verses 1:1 | The prefixed bi- governs what follows; its omitted complement is understood as ‘I begin’ or ‘say’ in God’s name. «الباء أداة تخفض ما بعدها مثل: من وعن، والمتعلق به الباء محذوف»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r10] verses 1:1 | Others derive Allah from ilaha, worship; Ibn Abbas’s reading of ‘your gods’ as ‘your worship’ supports the sense that God alone deserves worship. «اشتقاقه فقيل: من أله إلاهة أي عبد عبادة»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r11] verses 1:1 | Al-Mubarrad connects ilah with Arabs’ phrase for finding repose in someone; a poetic line illustrates resorting to him amid calamities. «قال المبرد: هو من قول العرب ألهت إلى فلان أي سكنت إليه»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r2] verses 1:1 | The alif is omitted from ism for ease and frequent use; al-Qutaybi explains the lengthened ba as an honorific opening, and Umar b. Abd al-Aziz ordered careful lettering. «قال القتيبي ليكون افتتاح كلام كتاب الله بحرف معظم، كان عمر بن عبد العزيز»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r3] verses 1:1 | Another explanation says the ba was lengthened to mark where the omitted alif had been; the spelling keeps the alif in ‘read in the name of your Lord.’ «وقيل: لما أسقطوا الألف ردوا طول الألف على الباء ليكون دالا على سقوط الألف»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r4] verses 1:1 | Ism can mean the named entity itself, as verses call the person Yahya by that name and call worshiped persons ‘names’; more often it means the naming. «والاسم هو المسمى وعينه وذاته قال الله تعالى»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r5] verses 1:1 | God’s naming himself in the formula teaches people how to begin recitation. «قيل هو تعليم للعباد كيف يفتتحون القراءة»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r6] verses 1:1 | Al-Mubarrad derives ism from sumuw, ‘loftiness’: the name rises above and manifests its meaning, which lies beneath it. «قال المبرد من البصريين: هو مشتق من السمو وهو العلو»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r7] verses 1:1 | Thaʿlab derives ism from wasm, a mark that indicates its meaning; al-Baghawi prefers the derivation from sumuw because of its diminutive and verb forms. «قال ثعلب من الكوفيين: هو من الوسم والسمة وهي العلامة»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r8] verses 1:1 | The derivation from sumuw is stronger: ism diminishes as sumayy, and its verb is sammaytu; a wasm derivation would yield wasīm and wasamtu. «والأول أصح لأنه يصغر على السمي ولو كان من السمة لكان يصغر على الوسيم كما يقال في الوعد وعيد»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p49#3/r9] verses 1:1 | Al-Khalil and a group treat Allah as a proper name unique to God, with no derivation, like human proper names. «قال الخليل وجماعة: هو اسم علم خاص لله عز وجل لا اشتقاق له»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r1] verses 1:1 | Ibn Abbas calls al-Rahman and al-Rahim two tender names, one more tender than the other. «قال ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما: هما اسمان رقيقان أحدهما أرق من الآخر»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r2] verses 1:1 | Some treat the two names as synonyms for merciful; al-Mubarrad describes them as favor upon favor, repeated to encourage hopeful hearts. «وقال المبرد: هو إنعام بعد إنعام، وتفضل بعد تفضل»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r3] verses 1:1 | A distinction makes al-Rahman’s mercy universal in this world, like provision for all, while al-Rahim’s mercy is particular to believers in the hereafter. «فقال: الرحمن بمعنى العموم والرحيم بمعنى الخصوص»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r4] verses 1:1 | On this distinction, people may be called merciful (rahim) but not al-Rahman; the former is general in wording and particular in meaning, the latter opposite. «فالرحمن عام المعنى خاص اللفظ، والرحيم عام اللفظ خاص المعنى»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r5] verses 1:1 | Mercy is either God’s willing good for its recipients, an attribute of essence, or withholding deserved punishment and giving unearned good, an attribute of action. «والرحمة إرادة الله تعالى الخير لأهله. وقيل هي ترك عقوبة من يستحقها وإسداء الخير إلى من لا يستحق»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r6] verses 1:1 | Medinan and Basran readers and Kufan jurists hold that the basmala is not part of the Fatiha or other surahs, but opens them for blessing. «فذهب قراء المدينة والبصرة وفقهاء الكوفة إلى أنها ليست من فاتحة الكتاب، ولا من غيرها من السور»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r7] verses 1:1 | Meccan and Kufan readers and most Hijazi jurists count the basmala as part of the Fatiha only; it is written to separate surahs. «وذهب قراء مكة والكوفة وأكثر فقهاء الحجاز إلى أنها من الفاتحة وليست من سائر السور وأنها كتبت للفصل»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r8] verses 1:1 | Al-Thawri, Ibn al-Mubarak, and al-Shafiʿi count the basmala in every surah except al-Tawba, since it is written in the codex in Quranic script. «وذهب جماعة إلى أنها من الفاتحة ومن كل سورة إلا سورة التوبة وهو قول الثوري وابن المبارك والشافعي لأنها كتبت في المصحف بخط سائر القرآن»
[BAGHAWI-FULL:v1p50/r9] verses 1:1 | Other derivations connect the divine name with refuge and bewildered longing: creatures flee to God in hardship and need as a child turns to its mother. «وقيل أصل الإله "ولاه" فأبدلت الواو بالهمزة مثل وشاح وإشاح»
