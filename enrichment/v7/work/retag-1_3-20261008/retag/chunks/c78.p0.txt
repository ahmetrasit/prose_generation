# Notes

## 18:65
[BAYDAWI-FULL:18:64-66/r8] verses 18:65 | The knowledge from God is knowledge known only through His enabling, namely knowledge of the unseen. «مما يختص بنا ولا يعلم إلا بتوفيقنا وهو علم الغيوب»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p484/r8] verses 18:60, 18:65 | The fish’s extraordinary revival serves as evidence for resurrection and marks Moses’s appointed meeting with Khidr. «مع ما فيها من الخارق الدال على البعث»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p489/r4] verses 18:65 | The servant is Khidr, named as belonging to God’s exalted presence; the majority hold that he was a prophet. «وكونه نبيا قول الجمهور»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p489/r5] verses 18:65 | Al-Ḥarālī distinguishes ʿinda for what is manifest from ladun for what is hidden; here mercy is outward signs and knowledge is a hidden gift specific to God’s presence. «وقال الأستاذ أبو الحسن الحرالي»
[BIQAI-FULL:v4p489/r6] verses 18:65 | Sufis call knowledge received through unveiling ladunnī knowledge and describe spiritual discipline as preparing the soul for divine illumination without deliberation. «فأهل التصوف سموا العلم بطريق المكاشفة العلم اللدني»
[BURSEVI:v5p205#2/r4] verses 18:65 | The indefinite servant signals exaltation, and the possessive phrase from Our servants confers honor; Moses greets Khidr, identifies himself, and says he came to learn. «وكان مسجى بثوب فسلم عليه موسى وعرفه نفسه»
[BURSEVI:v5p205#2/r5] verses 18:65 | The majority identify the servant as Khidr, whose title, according to a sound report, came from sitting on barren white ground that turned green behind him. «والجمهور على أنه الخضر بفتح الخاء المعجمة وكسر الضاد وهو لقبه»
[BURSEVI:v5p205#2/r6] verses 18:65 | Abu al-Layth relates that Khidr was a king's son who refused succession, fled his father, and escaped to the sea islands. «قال أبو الليث : إنه عليه السلام ذكر قصة الخضر»
[BURSEVI:v5p205#2/r7] verses 18:65 | Al-Suhayli's account says Khidr's father was a king, his mother a Persian princess, and he was born in a cave and nursed by a village shepherd's goat before being raised by the shepherd. «أن أباه كان ملكا وأن أمه كانت بنت فارس واسمها إلها وإنها ولدته في مغارة»
[BURSEVI:v5p205#2/r8] verses 18:65 | In that account, Khidr later came to his father's court as a scribe; his father recognized him, appointed him over the people, but Khidr eventually fled and renounced worldly life. «كان فيمن قدم عليه من الكتاب ابنه الخضر وهو لا يعرفه فلما استحسن خطه ومعرفته»
[BURSEVI:v5p207/r1] verses 18:65 | The objection that Khidr could not remain alive because no prophet follows Muhammad fails: Khidr was a prophet before Muhammad, and God preserved him for a wisdom as with Jesus. «فلا عبرة لكلامه لأنه لم يتنبأ بعده بل قبله كعيسى أبقاه الله لمعنى وحكمة»
[BURSEVI:v5p207/r2] verses 18:65 | Ibn Arabi says Khidr will appear with the Companions of the Cave when the Mahdi appears, be martyred, and rank among the best martyrs in the Mahdi's army. «أنه يظهر مع أصحاب الكهف في آخر الزمان عند ظهور المهدي ويستشهد»
[BURSEVI:v5p207/r3] verses 18:65 | Ibrahim ibn Sufyan says the man whom the Dajjal kills and revives in a report at the end of Sahih Muslim is Khidr. «قال إبراهيم بن سفيان صاحب مسلم يقال : إن هذا الرجل هو الخضر»
[BURSEVI:v5p207/r4] verses 18:65 | Ibn Abbas says Khidr and Ilyas meet each year during pilgrimage season, shave one another's heads, and recite a supplication said to protect its reader from calamities and harmful creatures. «وعن ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما يلتقي الخضر والياس في كل عام في الموسم»
[BURSEVI:v5p207/r5] verses 18:65 | Ahmad's Zuhd adds that Khidr and Ilyas fast Ramadan in Jerusalem. «وزاد أحمد في الزهد أنهما يصومان رمضان في بيت المقدس»
[BURSEVI:v5p207/r6] verses 18:65 | Ali says Khidr lives in Jerusalem between the Gate of Mercy and the Gate of the Tribes. «وعن علي رضي الله عنه مسكن الخضر بيت المقدس فيما بين باب الرحمة إلى باب الأسباط»
[BURSEVI:v5p207/r7] verses 18:65 | Al-Qashani reads Khidr as a figure for spiritual expansion and Ilyas for contraction, and raises the question whether the historical human Khidr lives on. «قال القاشاني : الخضر كناية عن البسط والياس عن القبض»
[BURSEVI:v5p208/r1] verses 18:65 | Bahr al-Ulum says all knowledge comes from God, but mediated instruction is not called ladunni; it is knowledge placed directly in the heart without an ordinary external means. «قال في "بحر العلوم" : إنما قال من لدنا مع أن العلوم كلها من لدنه»
[BURSEVI:v5p208/r2] verses 18:65 | Bahr al-Ulum gives Umar, Ali, and disciplined saints as recipients of such knowledge, then cites sayings praising the breath of lovers and the worship of the ascetic. «كما كان لعمر وعلي ولكثير من أولياء الله تعالى المرتاضين»
[BURSEVI:v5p208/r3] verses 18:65 | He says such devoted, grateful people are few, cites the few grateful servants and people's lack of knowledge, and presents the Companions as exemplary guides. «وقليل من عبادى الشكور»
[BURSEVI:v5p208/r4] verses 18:65 | The Najmiyya reading makes God's servant a free person released from servitude to other-than-God and chosen among the elect; mercy makes him receptive to divine attributes' light. «وفي "التأويلات النجمية" : {فوجدا عبدا من عبادنآ}»
[BURSEVI:v5p208/r5] verses 18:65 | In the Najmiyya reading, ladunni knowledge is knowledge of God's essence and attributes, which no one knows unless God teaches it. «وهو علم معرفة ذاته وصفاته الذي لا يعلمه أحد إلا بتعليمه إياه»
[BURSEVI:v5p208/r6] verses 18:65 | Knowledge learnable from people is not uniquely ladunni; the author cites God teaching David armor-making, which could also be learned from another teacher. «فإن علم صنعة اللبوس مما علمه الله داود عليه السلام»
[BURSEVI:v5p208/r7] verses 18:65 | Junayd defines ladunni knowledge as unambiguous disclosure of hidden realities through lights unveiled when a servant disciplines the limbs, abandons personal will, and stands before God without desire. «قال الجنيد قدس سره : العلم اللدني ما كان تحكما على الأسرار بغير ظن فيه ولا خلاف»
[BURSEVI:v5p209/r1] verses 18:65 | The author's shaykh says mercy is knowledge of worship, study, outward law, and sharia; its being from God means it comes from the level of divine attributes. «المراد بالرحمة علم العبادة والدراسة والظاهر والشريعة ولذلك عبر عنه بالرحمة»
[BURSEVI:v5p209/r2] verses 18:65 | He reads knowledge as inward allusion, inheritance, and truth, called knowledge because it is the fullest form; like spirit to body or kernel to shell, it completes rather than invalidates outward knowledge. «والمراد بالعلم علم الإشارة والوراثة والباطن والحقيقة ولذلك عبر عنه بلفظ العلم»
[BURSEVI:v5p209/r3] verses 18:65 | He says outward and inward knowledge are each perfect in themselves; apparent inferiority or superiority exists only through comparison between levels, not in their essential reality. «فكما أن الجهل والغفلة في أنفسهما محض نقصان حقيقي فكذلك العلم والمعرفة في أنفسهما محض كمال حقيقي»
