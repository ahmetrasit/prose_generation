# Notes

## 1:1
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r8] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | If the superior prayer with the Fatiha discharges the obligation, it does not follow that the inferior prayer with another passage does; the obligation remains due. «ولا يلزم من الخروج عن العهدة بالعمل الكامل الخروج عن العهدة بالعمل الناقص»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217#2/r9] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | Prayer seeks remembrance of the heart, and this concise surah gathers lordship and servitude and is equated with the mighty Qur'an; nothing else can replace it. «المقصود من الصلاة حصول ذكر القلب»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217/r1] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | In a divine report transmitted by the Prophet, God responds to each Fatiha phrase: the opening remembers Him, praise praises Him, the mercy names magnify Him, Judgment Day glorifies Him or is entrusted to Him, worship belongs to Him, aid relies on Him, and guidance is the servant's request. «فإذا قال العبد : بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم يقول الله تعالى ذكرني عبدي»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217/r2] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | Calling the Fatiha “prayer” shows that religious law serves human interests: people most need knowledge of God's lordship and servitude, which the surah's two halves provide to fulfill the covenant. «مدار الشرائع على رعاية مصالح الخلق»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p217/r3] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6 | Since God names the Fatiha “prayer,” prayer without its recitation does not occur; Razi takes this to establish that reciting it is a pillar, as his school holds. «أن عند عدم قراءة الفاتحة وجب أن لا تحصل الصلاة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p218/r1] verses 1:1 | When the servant remembers God through the opening, God remembers him in turn, as promised in the Qur'an. «لما أقدم العبد على ذكر الله لا جرم ذكره تعالى»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p218/r2] verses 1:1 | Remembrance is a high and honored form of worship: God repeatedly commands abundant remembrance and praises those who remember in every posture and when resisting Satan. «أن مقام الذكر مقام عال شريف في العبودية»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p218/r3] verses 1:1 | The report supports taking “Allah” as a proper name for God's unique essence, since a derived general noun could not identify that individual essence; “All-Merciful” and “Merciful” are universal terms. «يدل على أن قولنا الله اسم علم لذاته المخصوصة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p219/r4] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | The seven Fatiha verses correspond to the seven bodily prayer acts—standing, bowing, rising, first prostration, rising between prostrations, second prostration, and sitting—as soul to body. «أن آيات الفاتحة سبع والأعمال المحسوسة أيضا في الصلاة سبعة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p220/r1] verses 1:1 | The opening formula corresponds to standing: the connected letter bāʾ remains upright, and both the formula and standing begin the acts of prayer and other important matters. «حصلت المناسبة بين التسمية وبين القيام من هذه الوجوه»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p220/r8] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | The seven Fatiha verses are the soul of the seven prayer acts, themselves the soul of seven stages of human formation; God is the soul of souls and light of lights. «روح الأرواح ونور الأنوار هو الله تعالى»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221#2/r1] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | A prophetic report says God has seventy veils of light; if unveiled, the splendor of His face would burn everything perceived by sight, and only prophetic light reveals the number. «إن لله سبعين حجابا من النور لو كشفها لأحرقت سبحات وجهه كل ما أدرك البصر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221#2/r2] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | Prayer was given to Muhammad as a gift for his community because it joins bodily ascent through actions with spiritual ascent through remembrances. «إن تحفة أمتك الصلاة وذلك لأنها جامعة بين المعراج الجسماني وبين المعراج الروحاني»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221#2/r3] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | Before beginning the ascent, the worshipper purifies body and clothing for the holy presence and chooses which of opposing companions—angel or devil, reason or desire, truth or falsehood, and so forth—to follow, since settled companionship is hard to break. «فإنه إذا استحكمت المرافقة تعذرت المفارقة»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221#2/r4] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | Raising the hands signals leaving both worlds and directing all inner faculties toward God; “God is greatest” means He exceeds all beings, knowledge, comparison, and even the claim that He is simply greater. «فاقطع نظرك عنهما بالكلية ووجه قلبك وروحك وسرك وعقلك وفهمك»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221#2/r5] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | The opening glorification reveals the lights of divine majesty and moves from declaring God's transcendence to praising Him. «ثم قل : سبحانك اللهم وبحمدك وفي هذا المقام تجلى لك نور سبحات الجلال»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221#2/r6] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | “Blessed is Your name” unveils pre-eternity and eternity, since blessing here indicates abiding beyond extinction and nonexistence. «لأن قوله تبارك إشارة إلى الدوام المنزه عن الإفناء والإعدام»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221#2/r7] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | “Exalted is Your majesty” signifies that God's knowledge and greatness exceed every stated measure. «أعلم وأعظم من أن تكون صفات جلاله ونعوت كماله محصورة في القدر المذكور»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221#2/r8] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | “There is no god but You” means every attribute of majesty and perfection belongs only to God; ultimately reason, speech, understanding, and imagination fail before Him. «كل صفات الجلال وسمات الكمال له لا لغيره»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221/r1] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | Muhammad's ascent has a bodily journey from Mecca to Jerusalem and then the high kingdom, and a spiritual journey from the visible to the unseen and from the unseen to the unseen of the unseen. «أما ما يتعلق بعالم الأرواح فله معراجان»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221/r2] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | The spiritual ascent rises through human spirits, the spirits of the heavens, angels, throne-bearers, and pure spirits detached from bodies, whose worship and love of God exceed human comprehension. «ثم تترقى فتنتهي إلى الأرواح المقدسة عن التعلقات بالأجسام»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p221/r3] verses 1:1, 1:2, 1:3, 1:4, 1:5, 1:6, 1:7 | The ascent continues through ever-higher spiritual ranks until God, the source of all good and mercy; thus the spirit-world is the unseen and God's presence the unseen of the unseen. «فثبت أن عالم الأرواح هو عالم الغيب وحضرة جلال الربوبية هي غيب الغيب»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p89#2/r1] verses 1:1 | The omitted governor may be a noun or verb and may precede or follow the phrase, yielding four possible constructions. «هذا المضمر يحتمل أن يكون اسما وأن يكون فعلا وعلى التقديرين فيجوز أن يكون متقدما وأن يكون متأخرا»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p89#2/r2] verses 1:1 | Al-Razi prefers placing God's name first: God's existence precedes all else, so mention of Him should precede mention of other things. «وأقول : التقديم عندي أولى ويدل عليه وجوه : الأول : أنه تعالى قديم الوجود لذاته»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p89#2/r3] verses 1:1 | He also invokes God's being First and Last and the ordering of affairs before and after Him as evidence for placing His name first. «قال تعالى : ^ { هو الأول والآخر } ^ [ الحديد : 3 ] وقال : ^ { لله الأمر من قبل ومن بعد } ^ [ الروم : 4 ]»

[RAZI-FULL:v1p89#2/r5] verses 1:1 | Al-Qushayri says realized knowers see God before anything; al-Razi explains that moving from creation to Creator is an inferior proof than descending from Creator to creation. «فقال الأستاذ القشيري : المحققون فإنهم ما رأوا شيئا إلا وكانوا قد رأوا الله قبله»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p89#2/r6] verses 1:1 | The implied action's position symbolizes spiritual attention: action-first moves from one's act toward need of God, while God's name first recognizes dependence on Him before considering oneself. «قلت : وتحقيق الكلام أن الانتقال من المخلوق إلى الخالق إشارة إلى برهان الآن والنزول من الخالق إلى المخلوق برهان اللم»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p90/r1] verses 1:1 | Abu Bakr al-Razi says the omitted word is an imperative verb, inferred from the nearby command 'You alone we worship'; the implied instruction is 'Say: In God's name.' «قال الشيخ أبو بكر الرازي : نسق تلاوة القرآن يدل على أن المضمر هو الفعل وهو الأمر»
[RAZI-FULL:v1p90/r2] verses 1:1 | Al-Razi favors an omitted noun: 'In God's name is the beginning of everything' states that God is intrinsically the source and creator of events, whether or not anyone says it. «وأقول : لقائل أن يقول : بل إضمار الاسم أولى ؛ لأنا إذا قلنا : تقدير الكلام بسم الله ابتداء كل شيء»
