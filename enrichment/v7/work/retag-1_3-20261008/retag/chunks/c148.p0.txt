# Notes

## 78:38
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p147/r2] verses 78:38 | Ṣaff is a verbal noun that can refer to one or a group, like ʿadl and zawr. «والصف في الأول مصدر، فينبني عن الواحد، والجمع كالعدل والزور»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p147/r3] verses 78:38 | The apparent view of the commentators is that they stand in two rows: the Spirit in one, the angels in another. «وظاهر قول المفسرين (¬4) أنهم يقومون صفين: الروح صف، والملائكة صف»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p147/r4] verses 78:38 | Ibn Qutayba glosses the phrase as rows and notes that the day of the Eid prayer is called the “day of the row.” «وقال ابن قتيبة: صفوفا، ويقال ليوم العيد: يوم الصف»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p147/r5] verses 78:38 | The apparent referent of “they do not speak” is the angels standing in rows; they speak only with God’s permission and when permitted to say what is correct. «الظاهر أن هذا من صفة الملائكة الذين يقومون صفا.
والمعنى: أنهم لا يتكلمون إلا بإذن الله، فمن أذن له الرحمن وقال: {صوابا»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p147/r6] verses 78:38 | Ibn ʿAbbās says the correct word is the testimony that there is no god but God. «ومعنى الصواب: شهادة أن لا إله إلا الله في قول ابن عباس»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p147/r7] verses 78:38 | The commentators explain the correct word as affirming God’s oneness, rejecting associationism, and declaring God free of every false accusation. «والمفسرون قالوا: هو التوحيد، ونفي الشرك، وتنزيه الله عز وجل عن كل فرية»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p147/r8] verses 78:38 | Ṣawāb means soundness in action and speech; it is formed from aṣāba, just as jawāb derives from ajāba. «والصواب هو السداد من الفعل والقول (¬3)، يقال: فعل صوابا، وقال صوابا، وهو اسم من أصاب يصيب إصابة، كالجواب من أجاب يجيب إجابة»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p147/r9] verses 78:38 | If the prohibition on addressing God covers all creation, the exception can be the believer whom He permits to speak and who professed monotheism in worldly life. «ويجوز أن يكون الاستثناء من جميع الخلق على قول من قال: لا يملكون منه خطابا للخلق كلهم، فاستثنى من ملك منهم الخطاب»
[WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p149/r1] verses 78:38 | One interpretation attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās says those permitted to speak are those who professed monotheism and intercede for such believers; the author calls this far-fetched because the wording only supports it by strained interpretation. «وهذا معنى قول ابن عباس، يريد: يشفعون لمن قال: لا إله إلا الله، وهذا قول بعيد، واللفظ لا يدل على هذا المعنى»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:78:36-40/r3] verses 78:38 | The Spirit may be Gabriel. «قيل: هو جبريل عليه السلام.»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:78:36-40/r4] verses 78:38 | Another view says the Spirit is an angel who stands in a row. «وقيل: هو ملك يقوم صفا.»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:78:36-40/r5] verses 78:38 | Another view identifies the Spirit as a host of God, neither angels nor humans, who stand in rows. «وقيل: الروح جند من جنود الله ليسوا من الملائكة ولا من الناس يقومون { والملائكة صفا»
[WAHIDI-WAJIZ:78:36-40/r6] verses 78:38 | They speak only when the Merciful permits, and their sound word is the testimony that there is no god but God. «يعني: لا إله إلا الله.»
[ZAJJAJ:v5p275/r4] verses 78:38 | The rūḥ is a created being like humans, but is not human. «(الروح) خلق كالإنس، وليس هو إنس.»
[ZAJJAJ:v5p275/r5] verses 78:38 | Another view identifies the rūḥ as Gabriel. «وقيل: الروح جبريل عليه السلام.»

## 90:13
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p478/r4] verses 90:13 | Fakk means freeing one thing from another; a poet uses it of releasing a captive from a shackle. «الفك: تخليص الشيء من الشيء»
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p482/r10] verses 90:13 | Freeing the neck means releasing a person from captivity and bondage; a near relative combines charity with maintaining kinship ties. «وفك الرقبة: تخليصها من الأسر والرق. ذا مقربة: ليجتمع صدقة وصلة»
[AKHFASH:v2p579/r3] verses 90:13 | Al-Akhfash rejects an alternative construal and prefers the direct fakk raqaba phrase as the good reading. «وليس هذا بذاك و {فك رقبة} هو الجيد»
[AKHFASH:v2p579/r4] verses 90:13 | Some gave a different syntactic construal of fakk raqaba in relation to the obstacle and the following or feeding phrase. «وقال بعضهم {فك رقبة} وليس هذا بذاك»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#2/r1] verses 90:12, 90:13 | The question “what is the steep ascent?” magnifies the challenge; interpreting the ascent as emancipation is figurative identification, so there is no need to insert “crossing the ascent.” «تعظيم لشأن العقبة المفسرة بقوله سبحانه فك رقبة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#2/r2] verses 90:12, 90:13 | One suggestion treats the ascent itself as gratitude because of its difficulty, but Alusi finds it unconvincing. «وقال بعضهم يحتمل أن يراد بالعقبة نفس الشكر»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#2/r3] verses 90:12, 90:13 | Ibn ʿUmar reportedly calls the ascent a steep, slippery mountain in Hell; al-Hasan is reported to say the same. «عن ابن عمر أن العقبة جبل زلال في جهنم»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#2/r4] verses 90:12, 90:13 | Ibn Abbas is reported to identify the ascent as Hell, or in another report as a barrier between Paradise and Hell. «عن ابن عباس أنها النار وفي رواية عبد بن حميد عنه أنها عقبة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#2/r5] verses 90:12, 90:13 | Mujahid, al-Dahhak, and al-Kalbi identify the ascent as the bridge over Hell; it may be the same as the reported barrier between Paradise and Hell. «وعن مجاهد والضحاك والكلبي أنها الصراط»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#2/r6] verses 90:12, 90:13 | Abu Rajaʾ says he heard that the ascent takes 7,000 years to climb and 7,000 years to descend; if these reports are sound, “crossing” and an implied noun must be supplied. «عن أبي رجاء أنه قال بلغني أن العقبة التي ذكر الله تعالى»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#2/r7] verses 90:12, 90:13 | If those locations are intended, iqtihām must mean passage across them and freeing a slave or feeding must count metaphorically as the cause of passage; Alusi will accept none as the verse's exegesis without a sound Prophetic report. «أنا لا أقول بشيء من ذلك حتى تصح فيه تفسيرا للآية رواية مرفوعة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#3/r1] verses 90:13 | Fakk is a verbal noun, as is fakkāk; the usual interpretation here is freeing an enslaved person from the status of slavery. «وهو مصدر فك وكذا الفكاك بفتح الفاء كما نص عليه الفراء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#3/r2] verses 90:13 | Al-Farra identifies fakkāk as a verbal noun alongside fakk. «وكذا الفكاك بفتح الفاء كما نص عليه الفراء»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p137#3/r3] verses 90:13 | A report from al-Baraʾ begins with a Bedouin asking the Prophet for a deed that will admit him to Paradise; the Prophet advises freeing a person or contributing toward freeing a slave. «عن البراء رضي الله تعالى عنه أن أعرابيا قال يا رسول الله علمني عملا يدخلني»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p139/r1] verses 90:13, 90:14 | Ibn Kathir and the two grammarian-readers use past verbs fakka and atʿama with raqabatan in the accusative; on this reading fakk is an appositive of iqtahama, with intervening words parenthetical. «وقرأ ابن كثير والنحويان فك فعلا ماضيا رقبة بالنصب أو أطعم فعلا ماضيا»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p139/r3] verses 90:13, 90:14 | Some Successors read fakk raqabatin in construct and atʿama as a past verb, coordinated with the verbal noun by interpreting it verbally. «وقرأ بعض التابعين فك رقبة بالإضافة أو أطعم فعلا ماضيا»
[ASAD-NOTES:90:11#n7/r1] verses 90:13 | Ikrimah, as cited by Baghawi, and Razi explain freeing a neck as freeing oneself from sin's burden. «Thus lkrimah, as quoted by Baghawi; also Razi.»
[ASAD-NOTES:90:11#n7/r2] verses 90:13 | Alternatively, fakk raqabah can mean freeing a human being from bondage, including social, economic, or political subjugation and exploitation describable as slavery. «Alternatively, the phrase fakk raqabah may be rendered as "the freeing of a human being from bondage"»
