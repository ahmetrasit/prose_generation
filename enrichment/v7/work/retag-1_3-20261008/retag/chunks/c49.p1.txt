[ELMALILI:7:57-58/r13] verses 7:57 | Wind is a specific movement of air, not its natural state; physical accounts relate its formation to changing heat and cold, whose balance God directly governs. «harâret tabiatı ile burûdet tabiatı arasındaki gālibiyet ve mağlûbiyet nisbetlerinin tahvil ve tasrîfine râci‘dir»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58/r14] verses 7:57 | Both regular and irregular winds are direct divine action; those who look for God's power only in miracles wrongly think only extraordinary winds are divine acts. «Onun için kudret-i ilâhiyeyi yalnız hârikada aramak gafletinde bulunanlar, fi‘l-i ilâhî ancak fevka’l-‘âde rüzgârlardır zannederler.»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58/r15] verses 7:57 | The point is not merely that wind proves a first mover; its further features and relation to life, blessing, and rain also direct reflection. «burada mevzû‘-i bahis yalnız muharrik-i evvel delîlini teşkil eden bu tahrik değildir.»
[ELMALILI:7:57-58/r16] verses 7:57 | The wind prepares and signals the coming rain before it occurs, thereby representing a message to human reason about a later divine act. «kable’l-vukū‘iş‘âr eylemesi haysiyetine de bilhassa nazar-ı dikkat celb olunmuştur.»

[FARISI-HUJJA:v4p31#2/r6] verses 7:57 | The noun الريح derives from a form whose root waw changes in the singular because of kasra; the small plural أرواح keeps waw because no change is required. «اعلم أن الريح اسم على فعل، والعين منه واو، فانقلبت في الواحد للكسرة»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v4p31#2/r7] verses 7:57 | In the common plural رياح, waw becomes yāʾ after kasra; the following alif also favors the change because it resembles yāʾ. «وأما في الجمع الكثير فرياح، فانقلبت الواو ياء للكسرة التي قبلها»
[FARISI-HUJJA:v4p31#2/r8] verses 7:57 | A singular noun can denote a collective and take plural sense, as in الدينار والدرهم and 103:2 followed by the exception in 103:3; thus singular الريح can carry collective meaning. «وقد يجوز أن يكون (الريح) على لفظ الواحد، ويراد بها الكثرة.»
