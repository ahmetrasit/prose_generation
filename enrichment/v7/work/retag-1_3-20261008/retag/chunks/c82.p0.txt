# Notes

## 18:74
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r11] verses 18:74 | Al-Zajjaj says zakiyya means innocent, with no apparent ground for killing him. «والسادس ان الزكية البريئة التي لم يظهر ما يوجب قتلها قاله الزجاج»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r12] verses 18:74 | Abu ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAla distinguishes zākiya as never having sinned from zakiyya as one who sinned and then repented. «فروي عن أبي عمرو بن العلاء أنه قال الزاكية التي لم تذنب قط والزكية التي أذنبت ثم تابت»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r13] verses 18:74 | Abu Ubayda alternatively distinguishes zākiya as bodily purity and zakiyya as religious purity. «وروي عن أبي عبيدة أنه قال الزاكية في البدن والزكية في الدين»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r14] verses 18:74 | Bi-ghayri nafs means without having killed another person. «قوله تعالى بغير نفس أي بغير قتل نفس»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r15] verses 18:74 | Readers differ between light and doubled forms of nukran; the light form derives from the heavier one, as with other paired Arabic forms. Al-Zajjaj explains the sense as a horrid act, with an elided preposition possible; nukran is less shocking than imran because Moses found drowning a boat’s passengers worse than one killing. «النكر والنكر قال الزجاج والمعنى لقد أتيت شيئا نكرا ويجوز أن يكون معناه جئت بشيء نكر»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r2] verses 18:74 | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and most commentators say the boy had not reached puberty. «أحدهما أنه لم يكن بالغا قاله ابن عباس ومجاهد والأكثرون»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r3] verses 18:74 | A second report, cited by al-Mawardi from Ibn Abbas, says the boy was a young man who had grown a beard; it argues an unaccountable minor did not deserve execution and notes that ghulam can mean an adult. «والثاني أنه كان شابا قد قبض على لحيته حكاه الماوردي عن ابن عباس أيضا»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r4] verses 18:74 | Layla al-Akhyaliyya’s praise poem for al-Hajjaj uses ghulam of a vigorous young man, supporting the broader age sense. «قالت ليلى الأخيلية تمدح الحجاج شفاها من الداء العضال الذي بها غلام إذا هز الفتاة سقاها»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r5] verses 18:74 | The reported methods of killing are pulling off the head in the Ubayy report, breaking the neck according to Ibn Abbas, or laying the boy down and cutting him with a knife according to Saʿid ibn Jubayr. «أحدها أنه اقتلع رأسه وقد ذكرناه في حديث أبي والثاني كسر عنقه قاله ابن عباس»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r6] verses 18:74 | The Kufan readers and Ibn ʿAmir read zakiyya without alif and with doubled yāʾ; other readers use alif without doubling. Al-Kisaʾi says the forms are synonymous dialect variants. «قال الكسائي هما لغتان بمعنى واحد وهما بمنزلة القاسية والقسية»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r7] verses 18:74 | Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak gloss zakiyya as repentant; another Ibn Abbas report says it means Muslim. «أحدها أنها التائبة روي عن ابن عباس انه قال الزكية التائبة وبه قال الضحاك»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r8] verses 18:74 | Saʿid ibn Jubayr says the boy was pure in that he had not yet reached the point of committing sins. «والثالث أنها الزكية التي لم تبلغ الخطايا قاله سعيد بن جبير»
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v5p172/r9] verses 18:74 | Qatada takes zakiyya as growing or flourishing, while Ibn al-Anbari says it means sound in constitution. «والرابع أنها الزكية النامية قاله قتادة وقال ابن الأنباري القويمة في تركيبها»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p177/r9] verses 18:74 | Sa'id says Khidr chose an unbelieving, handsome boy among children at play and killed him with a knife; Ibn Abbas reads the boy as pure or Muslim, while Moses says he had not sinned. «قال يعلى: قال سعيد، وجد غلمانا يلعبون، فأخذ غلاما كافرا ظريفا فأضجعه،»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p178/r7] verses 18:74, 18:75, 18:76 | Khidr kills a handsome child with a stone blow to the head; Moses protests and promises to end their companionship if he questions again. «فإذا غلمان يلعبون خلفها، فيهم غلام ليس في الغلمان غلام أظرف منه ولا أثرى (6)»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p183#2/r1] verses 18:74 | Reports place the boy among children at play and describe him as handsome; accounts differ whether Khidr cut off his head, crushed it with a stone, or plucked it by hand. «فروي أنه احتز رأسه، وقيل: رضخه بحجر. وفي رواية: اقتطفه بيده.»
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v5p183#2/r2] verses 18:74 | Moses calls the boy pure because he was young, had not sinned, and was killed without legal basis; the deed was plainly reprehensible. «أي صغيرة لم تعمل الحنث (3) ، ولا حملت إثما بعد،»
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p395/r2] verses 18:74 | Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, and Abū ʿAmr use zākiya with alif; ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī use zakiyya without alif and with shadda. «25 واختلفوا فى قوله ﴿ أقتلت نفسا زكية ﴾ 74»
[IBNMUJAHID:v1p395/r3] verses 18:74, 18:87, 54:6 | Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī lighten nukran except at 54:6, where Ibn Kathīr also lightens; Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim and Ibn ʿĀmir use heavy forms. Ḥafṣ and Nāfiʿ have variant transmissions. «26 واختلفوا فى التخفيف والتثقيل من قوله ﴿ نكرا ﴾ 74 87»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p480/r4] verses 18:74, 18:76 | After his second outburst, Moses apologizes and says he will stop accompanying Khidr if he questions him again. «Once again Moses (sws) apologized for his outburst and said that next time he would stop accompanying him»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p482/r2] verses 18:60, 18:66, 18:71, 18:74, 18:77, 18:82 | The story’s secondary lesson is that wisdom is granted to true seekers who exert themselves, travel, yearn for knowledge, and sacrifice ego and other desires. «Those who want to acquire it must work very hard for it. It is not obtained by just sitting at home; it needs a lot of effort and struggle.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p482/r3] verses 18:71, 18:74, 18:77 | Sharī‘ah is the real guide: even a great guide such as Khidr must be challenged when an act appears to violate it; Moses accepted the acts only after learning they followed God’s directives, already disclosed to him by revelation. «our real guide is the sharī‘ah. It is our obligation that we follow it in all circumstances. If we see anything against it, we must disregard it even if it is done by as great a guide as Khiḍr.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p482/r4] verses 18:71, 18:74, 18:77 | The author rejects the view that Quṭubs and Abdāls exist in every age as authorities beyond sharī‘ah; these terms lack Qur’anic or hadith basis, and no one may claim license to violate the law. «I regard as baseless the view of all those who think that there exist some Quṭub and Abdāl in every age like Khiḍr»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-4-surah-yunus-10-surah-maryam-19:p483/r1] verses 18:74 | God’s will is known through revelation in the Book and the Messenger’s practice; an alleged Khidr who kills today would face qiṣāṣ, since no such authority exists in sharī‘ah. «directives is through His Book and the practice of His messenger.»
[JISHUMI:v6p4461/r5] verses 18:74 | Abu Ja‘far, Nafi, Ibn Kathir, Abu Amr, and Ya‘qub read زاكية with alif; the other readers omit it, and both mean pure. «قرأ أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو ويعقوب: نفسا زاكية بالألف، وقرأ الباقون بغير ألف، قال الكسائي: الزاكية والزكية لغتان ومعناهما الطاهرة،»
[JISHUMI:v6p4461/r6] verses 18:74 | Abu Amr says زاكية is one who never sinned, while زكية is one who sinned then repented. «قال أبو عمرو: والزاكية التي لم تذنب، والزكية التي أذنبت ثم تابت.»
[JISHUMI:v6p4461/r7] verses 18:74 | Some readers pronounce the kāf of نكرا with ḍamma; others leave it still. «قرأ أبو جعفر ونافع برواية ورش وقالون وابن عامر وأبو بكر عن عاصم ويعقوب: جئت شيئا نكرا بضم الكاف»
[JISHUMI:v6p4466/r10] verses 18:74 | Nukran means reprehensible, and the killing was more reprehensible than the ship incident. «منكرا، وقيل: أنكر من السفينة، عن الأصم»
[JISHUMI:v6p4466/r11] verses 18:74 | The killing was more reprehensible than the earlier incident, which he calls al-imr. «المنكر أشد من الإمر، عن قتادة»
[JISHUMI:v6p4466/r2] verses 18:74 | Khidr found boys playing, took a handsome, bright-faced boy, and slaughtered him with a knife. «مر الخضر بغلمان يلعبون، فأخذ غلاما ظريفا وضيء الوجه فذبحه بالسكين، عن سعيد بن جبير»
