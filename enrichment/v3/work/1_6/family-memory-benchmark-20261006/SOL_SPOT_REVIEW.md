# Sol high baseline: substantive spot review

Reviewed the actual frozen paragraphs 6, 9, 16, 21 and 22 against selected meal, bayani, kessaf, hadith and classical/modern coherence blocks. This assesses specificity, depth and adherence to the assignment; external attributions were not verified. The sampled blocks and all baseline outputs remain unchanged.

## Assessment

Useful literature-awareness drafts, but not consistently mini-research-paper depth. The six sampled family outputs average roughly 60–85 words per block. They often introduce several positions without developing their reasoning or the distinctions between them. Brevity alone is not a defect; the substantive question is what new external awareness the block supplies beyond the frozen paragraph.

- Kessaf p6 usefully distinguishes creation-guidance from the qibla command and identifies attributed reasons. Kessaf p16 connects continuing uprightness to the confession of faith and the no-transgression command. These are promising source-specific leads, though locations and attributions remain unverified.
- Hadith p16 introduces the report linking a confession of faith with subsequent uprightness and a remembered guidance prayer. Hadith p22 brings the mercy/deeds relationship to the gift/grace finding. These add concrete material; several other blocks merely attach a broadly related report without much interpretive depth.
- Bayani p6, p9 and p21 rely heavily on methodological affinity: what al-Khuli's or Bint al-Shati's general method would allow a reader to do. This is weaker than recalling those authors' actual treatments of the finding. Useful method descriptions should not be mistaken for a located precedent.
- Classical coherence p16 and p22 provide a coherent page/surah frame, but the specific recalled material is thin. Modern coherence p21 names the Fatiha/Baqara prayer-response relation, then largely restates connections supplied by the input.
- Meal p9 identifies the road/bridge translation issue, but the family supplies few concrete translator-specific comparisons across occurrences. No repeated translation error was recalled. Most unfamiliar roster translations have no treatment. This is a limited recall result, not a broad assessment of those translations.

## Instruction drift observed

- Meal p9 says translation choices do not prove an etymological claim; meal p21 judges loading the full homecoming synthesis into translation as improper. These are assistant verdicts about the supplied analysis, which the task excludes.
- Bayani p22 proposes testing the root connection against an ayah; kessaf p9 answers an implied claim of meaningless repetition that the frozen paragraph does not make. These drift toward reassessing the prose rather than reporting external positions.
- Many sampled blocks end with a repeated statement that an author was not recalled as making the full connection. Uncertainty matters, but routine tails take space from useful source awareness. The ledger or one concise qualification can carry these limitations.

The next prompt experiment should ask for substantive remembered positions and reasons, preserve genuine disagreements, and keep broad methodological applications visibly distinct from remembered interpretations. Increase depth where there is actual recalled material; do not meet a length target by inventing specificity. Preserve this baseline for comparison.
