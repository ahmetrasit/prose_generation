# Astra max versus Sol: four-family spot review

One fresh run per family per lane, using byte-identical frozen prose and source rosters. The substantive research brief is shared, with model/launch wording and later lanes' explicit isolation sentence differing. This review compares the supplied outputs; it performs no web search, corpus retrieval, or attribution verification. Block count, confidence, and greater length do not establish correctness. Baseline outputs are unchanged.

## Classical coherence

Astra produces nine blocks, 958 prose words, and coverage of ten paragraphs; Sol high produces five blocks and 389 words, and Sol max five blocks and 341 words. Astra develops the remembered purpose of Fâtiha, the development from worship to help to guidance, continued guidance, the favored people, religious life as a path, knowledge/action distinctions, Paradise thanksgiving, and favor. These are more substantive source-related discussions than Sol's repeated appeal to a general coherence method.

It also distinguishes the remembered source contribution from the cross-verse application being made to the frozen page. For example, the request-to-arrival connection in paragraph 21 is explicitly an application rather than a claimed Biqai Fâtiha–A'raf comparison. Twelve paragraphs remain no_recall, so this is broader but far from exhaustive coverage. Astra attributes the Fâtiha–Baqara connection to Gharnati more confidently than Sol high; this cannot be adjudicated without source checking.

## Beyânî

Astra produces thirteen blocks and 1,976 prose words, versus 22/1,804 for Sol high and 22/1,864 for Sol max. A shared block covers six connected root-image paragraphs; four other paragraphs are no_recall. Fewer blocks therefore do not mean only thirteen paragraphs were addressed.

The strongest gain is concrete remembered Bint al-Shati material: the Beled reading ties the paths and difficult ascent to moral action, the Asr reading develops belief, action, mutual counsel, and persistence, and the Duha reading connects received care and guidance to gratitude and responsibilities toward others. These are actual attributed readings of other suras, then explicitly related to the input. Sol more often invokes her general usage-comparison principle and applies it to the paragraph without a specific remembered reading.

Astra also explains a useful methodological difference: Bint al-Shati seeks contextual specificity, while Samarrai allows compatible meanings to be carried together in suitable constructions. It marks the Tekvir and Yunus recollections as uncertain instead of inventing source locations. The shared root-image block still shifts partly into prescriptions for handling the frozen prose, and the paragraph 2 ending similarly advises how to treat the other uses. This scope drift remains even with the stronger literature content.

## Hadith

Matched samples reviewed: paragraphs 2, 3, 5, 9, 16, 21, and 22. Astra supplies fuller remembered report settings, transmitter names, different wordings, and source commentary. Paragraph 2 relates a guidance prayer about disputed truth to human judgment and the judicial responsibility report; paragraph 3 adds communal perseverance in the trench narrative; paragraph 5 makes the intermediary role of the guide explicit. These connections go beyond general devotional relevance.

Paragraph 9 distinguishes the worldly path parable from the afterlife crossing, including the remembered transmission position of the thin/sharp description. Paragraph 16 differentiates confession wordings and adds Nawawi's reception through Qadi Iyad. Paragraph 21 links actual hedy practice, the house-and-banquet parable, and purification before entering Paradise; paragraph 22 adds Nawawi's reconciliation of deeds and mercy and gentle instruction. This is materially closer to a small literature review than either Sol sample, including Sol max's useful additions.

These gains also introduce more details that could be wrong. The review has not checked transmitter assignments, source locations, exact wordings, or the accuracy of the attributed commentary. Several blocks remain longer thematic applications. Their usefulness as leads is clearer than their verified fidelity.

## Meal

Matched samples reviewed: paragraphs 1, 2, 3, 7, 9, 11, 16, 21, and 22. Astra's 22 blocks and 2,873 words contain substantially more translator-specific distinctions than Sol high's 16/1,229 and Sol max's 22/1,831. Examples include TDV's showing the way versus DIB's leading, Arberry's English preposition, Asad's interpretation of the willing subject, witness versus martyr language, esenlik versus kurtuluş, and worship vocabulary. It separates choices by different publications, including works with overlapping authors, instead of treating a translator name as a single invariant wording.

Paragraph 1 supplies a remembered two-occurrence pattern for Asad at 28:56 and 10:25. Paragraph 21 attributes Mihr's narrowing to a specific spiritual process at 3:73 and 6:71 and contrasts it with Suat Yildirim's destination explanation. These are closer to the requested concrete repeated choices than Sol's general discussions of potential narrowing. The output explicitly distinguishes interpretive differences from translation errors rather than labelling unfamiliar choices mistakes.

The details remain unverified: none of these exact translator choices, paired examples, or patterns were checked against editions. The greater specificity makes the output a more useful research lead, while increasing the amount that requires checking. Some blocks also draw on explanatory tafsir rather than translation wording alone, but usually identify that layer.

## Overall reading

In the sampled outputs Astra is the strongest of these runs for substantive literature content and comparison. The advantage is not simply length: concrete source discussions and differences between authors appear where Sol often gives a generic method applied to the supplied paragraph. Sol max improves some hadith leads but does not uniformly improve over Sol high. None of this establishes a verified accuracy ranking; all lanes are learned-memory outputs, and this is one run per task rather than a repeated evaluation.
