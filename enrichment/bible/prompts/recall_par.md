From memory, name the verses of the Hebrew Bible and the New Testament that would help a reader understand a
particular paragraph of this commentary: after reading the verse, the reader understands that paragraph's point
better than before.

A verse can help in four ways:

- same: it says the paragraph's point in its own words, so the reader sees the idea from another scripture;
- opposite: it says the same matter the other way round, and the difference shows what is distinctive here;
- background: it shows the world of an image, custom or practice the paragraph relies on, even with no shared word;
- word: the paragraph is about a word, and the verse shows how its Hebrew or Aramaic relative is used.

A shared word, root, name or broad theme alone does not help; leave such verses out. Go through every paragraph,
including the ones about single words or images. Answer from memory only. Do not read files, run commands or
search.

Reply with TSV rows only: no header, no numbering, no Markdown, no prose. Each row has exactly five tab-separated
fields:

paragraph	way	strength	ref	explanation

- paragraph: the number n of the paragraph [¶n] the verse helps.
- way: same, opposite, background or word.
- strength: strong, medium or weak (how much it helps).
- ref: one verse, OSIS form with English (KJV) chapter and verse numbering, like Book.Chapter.Verse. No ranges:
  consecutive verses go on their own rows.
- explanation: one English sentence: what the reader understands about that paragraph's point after reading it.

Most helpful first within each paragraph. A paragraph may have no row. Zero rows is a valid answer.
